1.1 | Domestic violence | Does the policy include domestic violence?
Code Yes if any of the following apply:
- the policy explicitly mentions domestic violence, household violence, family violence, intimate partner violence, or equivalent terms;
- domestic violence is included in definitions of violence, victims, or protected persons;
- the policy cites domestic violence legislation or related legal frameworks in the legal foundation, "Considering", "Observing", "Bearing in Mind", or similar introductory sections;
- the policy includes domestic violence within prevention, protection, services, responsibilities, or implementation measures.
Code No only if:
- domestic violence is not mentioned anywhere in the policy; and
- there is no reference to domestic violence laws, definitions, or related concepts.
Do not require domestic violence-specific services or repeated use of the term. A reference to domestic violence in definitions or legal foundations is sufficient for Yes.
1.2 | Sexual violence | Does the policy include sexual violence?
Code Yes if any of the following apply:
- sexual violence, sexual assault, rape, sexual abuse, sexual harassment, or other forms of sexual violence are explicitly mentioned anywhere in the policy;
- sexual violence is included in the definition or classification of violence, victims, survivors, or protected persons;
- the policy lists sexual violence as a form, type, or category of violence covered by the policy;
- sexual violence is included within prevention, protection, response, referral services, institutional responsibilities, coordination mechanisms, implementation provisions, or monitoring requirements;
- the policy cites national legislation or legal frameworks addressing sexual violence (or equivalent sexual violence laws) in the legal foundation, "Considering", "Observing", "Bearing in Mind", or similar introductory legal references.
Code No only if:
- sexual violence is not explicitly mentioned anywhere in the policy; and
- the policy does not define, classify, or otherwise indicate that sexual violence is within the scope of covered violence, victims, or protection measures.
Do not require sexual violence-specific services or dedicated response measures. A reference to sexual violence in definitions, classifications, legal foundations, or general scope provisions is sufficient for a Yes.

1.5 | Economic violence | Does the policy include economic violence, economic exploitation, or domestic neglect as a form of violence against women?
Code Yes if any of the following apply:
- economic violence, economic abuse, economic exploitation, financial abuse, deprivation of economic resources, or domestic/economic neglect is explicitly identified as a form of violence, abuse, exploitation, or harm affecting women;
- the policy includes economic violence or related concepts within definitions or classifications of violence, victims, survivors, or protected persons;
- the policy identifies behaviours such as controlling access to money or property, withholding financial resources, preventing economic independence, exploitation of women's labour, or deprivation of basic economic needs as forms of violence or abuse;
- economic violence/exploitation/domestic neglect is included in prevention, protection, response, referral services, institutional responsibilities, coordination mechanisms, implementation provisions, or monitoring requirements;
- the policy cites national legislation or legal frameworks addressing economic violence, domestic violence, exploitation, or related forms of abuse in the legal foundation, "Considering", "Observing", "Bearing in Mind", or similar introductory legal references.
Code No if:
- economic violence, economic exploitation, or domestic neglect is not mentioned or included within the policy's definition or scope of violence against women; or
- the policy only mentions poverty, general social welfare problems, vulnerability, or disadvantage without linking these conditions to violence, exploitation, abuse, or neglect experienced by women.
Do not require dedicated economic violence services or measures. A reference in definitions or classifications of violence is sufficient for a Yes. However, general references to poverty, welfare needs, or social vulnerability alone are not sufficient unless they are explicitly connected to violence, exploitation, abuse, or neglect of women.
Does the policy include harmful practices affecting women and girls, including early/child marriage, female genital mutilation, honour crimes, or female infanticide?
Code Yes if any of the following apply:
- the policy explicitly mentions any harmful practice, including (but not limited to) early marriage, child marriage, female genital mutilation/cutting (FGM/C), honour crimes, honour-based violence, female infanticide, or similar practices;
- the policy includes prevention, protection, response, services, institutional responsibilities, coordination mechanisms, awareness activities, or implementation measures addressing any of these harmful practices;
- the policy identifies girls or women at risk of harmful practices, including through references to child marriage, forced marriage, traditional practices that cause harm, or gender-based customs/norms linked to violence or discrimination;
- the policy cites relevant national legislation or legal frameworks addressing harmful practices (for example, child marriage laws or anti-FGM legislation) in the legal foundation, "Considering", "Observing", "Bearing in Mind", or similar introductory legal references.
Code No only if:
- harmful practices are not mentioned anywhere in the policy; and
- the policy does not address any specific harmful practice (such as child marriage) through definitions, prevention measures, legal references, services, or implementation provisions.
Do not require the policy to use the umbrella term "harmful practices". References to any recognised harmful practice are sufficient for a Yes. In particular, explicit references to early marriage or child marriage should be coded Yes, even if other harmful practices (such as FGM, honour crimes, or female infanticide) are not mentioned.

1.8 | Human trafficking | Does the policy include human trafficking or trafficking in persons?
Code Yes if any of the following apply:
human trafficking, trafficking in persons, trafficking, or exploitation is explicitly mentioned;
victims of trafficking or trafficking for exploitation are included in definitions or target groups;
human trafficking is included as a form of violence, abuse, exploitation, or offence addressed by the policy;
the policy cites human trafficking legislation or regulations in its legal basis (considering/observing/legal foundation);
services, protection, prevention, recovery, or institutional responsibilities include trafficking victims.
Code No if the policy refers only to:
exploitation without any indication that it refers to trafficking or trafficking in persons;
general violence or victim protection with no mention of trafficking or trafficking victims.
1.9 | Sexual slavery/exploitation | Does the policy include sexual exploitation or sexual slavery?
Code Yes if any of the following apply:
- the policy explicitly mentions sexual exploitation, sexual abuse for exploitation purposes, sexual slavery, exploitation of women or children for sexual purposes, or equivalent terms;
- the policy states that women or children may be exploited sexually, including within definitions of victims, protected persons, violence, abuse, trafficking, or exploitation;
- the policy includes sexual exploitation/slavery within the scope of protection, prevention, response services, institutional responsibilities, coordination mechanisms, implementation provisions, or victim support measures;
- the policy refers to trafficking, exploitation, or related offences in a context that explicitly includes sexual exploitation or exploitation of women/children for sexual purposes;
- the policy cites national legislation or legal frameworks addressing sexual exploitation, trafficking for sexual exploitation, sexual slavery, or equivalent offences in the legal foundation, "Considering", "Observing", "Bearing in Mind", or similar introductory legal references.
Code No if:
- exploitation is mentioned only in a general sense without any reference to sexual exploitation, sexual abuse, sexual purposes, trafficking-related sexual exploitation, or sexual slavery;
- the policy only discusses economic exploitation, labour exploitation, employment exploitation, or general vulnerability without a sexual component;
- sexual exploitation/slavery is not mentioned or included anywhere in the policy.
Do not require the exact terms "sexual exploitation" or "sexual slavery". References to women or children being "sexually exploited" or "exploited sexually" are sufficient for a Yes. However, general references to "exploitation" alone are not sufficient unless the context indicates sexual exploitation or sexual exploitation-related harms.
1.10 | Technology-facilitated violence | Does the policy explicitly include technology-facilitated violence (e.g. cyber violence, cyber harassment, cyber bullying, cyber pornography, online sexual exploitation, digital abuse, or other violence committed through digital technologies)?
Code Yes if any of the following apply:
cyber violence, cyber harassment, cyber bullying, cyber pornography, online sexual exploitation, or similar technology-facilitated violence is explicitly included;
digital or online forms of violence are recognised as forms of violence against women;
technology-facilitated violence is included in definitions, lists of prohibited conduct, or protected groups;
the policy cites legislation on technology-facilitated violence as part of its legal basis.
Code No if the policy refers only to:
digital information systems, reporting platforms, hotlines, databases, websites, or communication technologies;
online service delivery or digital administration;
general references to technology or information systems without recognising technology-facilitated violence as a form of violence.
