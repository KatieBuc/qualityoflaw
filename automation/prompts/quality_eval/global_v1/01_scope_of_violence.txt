1.1 | Domestic violence | Does the national legal framework substantively address domestic violence?

Code Yes - Substantive if, in the operative provisions of any relevant instrument:
- domestic violence, household violence, family violence, intimate partner violence, or an equivalent term is defined, prohibited, criminalised, or made the basis of a protective or civil remedy;
- domestic violence is included in an operative definition of violence, victims, or protected persons that carries legal consequences or entitlements;
- the framework includes domestic violence within prevention, protection, response services, institutional responsibilities, coordination mechanisms, or monitoring obligations.

Code Yes - Referenced only if domestic violence is mentioned solely in a preamble, recital, explanatory memorandum, or as part of the cited basis for ratifying a relevant international/regional instrument, with no operative provision.

Code No if domestic violence does not appear anywhere in the framework - no operative provision, no preamble reference, no cited ratification.

Do not require a dedicated domestic-violence statute - coverage within general criminal, civil, or family law provisions is sufficient for Substantive, provided it is operative rather than aspirational. Do not infer domestic violence coverage merely because "violence against women" is addressed generally; the framework must explicitly identify domestic, household, family, or intimate-partner violence or an equivalent term.



1.2 | Sexual violence | Does the national legal framework substantively address sexual violence?

Code Yes - Substantive if, in the operative provisions of any relevant instrument:
- sexual violence, sexual assault, rape, sexual abuse, or sexual harassment is defined, prohibited, criminalised, or made the basis of a civil remedy;
- sexual violence is included in an operative definition or classification of violence, victims, survivors, or protected persons;
- the framework lists sexual violence as a covered form or category of violence with attached legal or service consequences;
- sexual violence is addressed within prevention, protection, response, referral services, institutional responsibilities, coordination mechanisms, or monitoring obligations.

Code Yes - Referenced only if sexual violence is mentioned solely in a preamble, recital, explanatory section, or as part of the cited basis for ratifying a relevant international/regional instrument, with no operative provision.

Code No if sexual violence does not appear anywhere in the framework, and no definition, classification, or scope provision indicates it is covered.

Do not require sexual-violence-specific services or a standalone response mechanism for Substantive - coverage within a general criminal or civil framework is sufficient, provided it is operative.

- Note carried from earlier discussion:- this item currently bundles sexual harassment together with sexual violence/assault/rape. If you want to track harassment (often a lower legal threshold, sometimes civil rather than criminal, and increasingly measured separately in comparative instruments like WBL) as its own indicator, split it into 1.2a; otherwise leave as-is for continuity with the regency-level data.

1.5 | Economic violence | Does the national legal framework substantively address economic violence, economic exploitation, or domestic neglect as a form of violence against women?

Code Yes - Substantive if, in the operative provisions of any relevant instrument:
- economic violence, economic abuse, economic exploitation, financial abuse, deprivation of economic resources, neglect, or domestic/economic neglect is defined, prohibited, or identified as a form of violence, abuse, or harm affecting women;
- the framework identifies controlling access to money or property, withholding financial resources, preventing economic independence, exploitation of women's labour, or deprivation of basic economic needs as forms of violence or abuse with attached legal or service consequences;
- economic violence/exploitation/neglect is addressed within prevention, protection, response, referral services, institutional responsibilities, coordination mechanisms, or monitoring obligations;
- the framework's operative definition of violence against women includes economic misery, suffering, or harm, whether occurring in public or private life.

Code Yes - Referenced only if economic violence, exploitation, or neglect is mentioned solely in a preamble, recital, explanatory section, or as part of the cited basis for ratifying a relevant international/regional instrument.

Code No if:
- economic violence, exploitation, or neglect is not mentioned or included anywhere in the framework's definition or scope of violence against women; or
- the framework only mentions poverty, general social welfare problems, vulnerability, or disadvantage without linking these to violence, exploitation, abuse, or neglect experienced by women.

Do not require dedicated economic-violence services for Substantive - inclusion in an operative definition or classification is sufficient. General references to poverty or welfare needs alone are not sufficient unless explicitly connected to violence, exploitation, abuse, or neglect of women.



1.6 | Harmful practices | Does the national legal framework substantively address harmful practices affecting women and girls, including early/child marriage, female genital mutilation, honour crimes, or female infanticide?

Code Yes - Substantive if, in the operative provisions of any relevant instrument:
- early marriage, child marriage, marriage at a child or early age, female genital mutilation/cutting (FGM/C), female circumcision, honour crimes, honour-based violence, female infanticide, or a similar practice is defined, prohibited, criminalised, or made subject to a minimum-age or protective rule;
- the framework includes prevention, protection, response, service, institutional, or awareness measures addressing any of these practices (for example, minimum-marriage-age enforcement programs);
- girls or women at risk of harmful practices are identified through operative provisions on child marriage, forced marriage, or gender-based customs linked to violence or discrimination.

Code Yes - Referenced only if a harmful practice is mentioned solely in a preamble, recital, explanatory section, or as part of the cited basis for ratifying a relevant international/regional instrument (e.g., a treaty addressing child marriage or FGM).

Code No if no harmful practice is mentioned anywhere in the framework - no operative provision, no preamble reference, no cited ratification.

Do not require the umbrella term "harmful practices" - reference to any recognised harmful practice is sufficient. In particular, an operative reference to early or child marriage should be coded Substantive even if no other harmful practice (FGM, honour crimes, female infanticide) is mentioned.



1.7 | Femicide/feminicide | Does the national legal framework substantively address femicide/feminicide?

Code Yes - Substantive if, in the operative provisions of any relevant instrument:
- femicide, feminicide, gender-motivated killing of women, or an equivalent term is defined, criminalised, or treated as an aggravating factor in homicide provisions;
- femicide is included in an operative definition or classification of violence as the most severe or lethal form of violence against women;
- femicide is addressed through prevention (risk-assessment or early-warning systems), protection, response (femicide observatories/registries), institutional responsibilities, coordination, or monitoring obligations.

Code Yes - Referenced only if femicide is mentioned solely in a preamble, recital, explanatory section, or as part of the cited basis for ratifying a relevant international/regional instrument.

Code No if femicide/feminicide does not appear anywhere in the framework - no operative provision, no preamble reference, no cited ratification.



1.8 | Human trafficking | Does the national legal framework substantively address human trafficking or trafficking in persons?

Code Yes - Substantive if, in the operative provisions of any relevant instrument:
- human trafficking, trafficking in persons, or trafficking-related exploitation is defined, prohibited, or criminalised;
- victims of trafficking are included in an operative definition or as a target group for protection or services;
- human trafficking is addressed within protection, prevention, recovery, or institutional responsibilities.

Code Yes - Referenced only if human trafficking is mentioned solely in a preamble, recital, explanatory section, or as part of the cited basis for ratifying a relevant international/regional instrument (e.g., the Palermo Protocol).

Code No if the framework refers only to:
- exploitation generally, with no indication it refers to trafficking; or
- general violence or victim protection with no mention of trafficking or trafficking victims anywhere.



1.9 | Sexual slavery/exploitation | Does the national legal framework substantively address sexual exploitation or sexual slavery?

Code Yes - Substantive if, in the operative provisions of any relevant instrument:
- sexual exploitation, sexual abuse for exploitation purposes, sexual slavery, exploitation of women for sexual purposes, forced sexual intercourse or relations for commercial or other purposes, or an equivalent term is defined, prohibited, or criminalised;
- the framework's operative provisions cover prostitution obtained through force or coercion, forced sexual labour or services, or use of a person's body for sexual purposes as a means of obtaining material or non-material gain;
- sexual exploitation/slavery is addressed within protection, prevention, response services, institutional responsibilities, coordination mechanisms, or victim support measures with a clear sexual-exploitation nexus;
- trafficking or exploitation provisions explicitly extend to sexual exploitation or exploitation of women for sexual purposes.

Code Yes - Referenced only if sexual exploitation or slavery is mentioned solely in a preamble, recital, explanatory section, or as part of the cited basis for ratifying a relevant international/regional instrument.

Code No if:
- exploitation is mentioned only in a general sense, with no reference to a sexual purpose, sexual abuse, or sexual means;
- the framework addresses only economic, labour, or employment exploitation, or general vulnerability, with no sexual component; or
- sexual exploitation/slavery is not mentioned anywhere in the framework.

Do not require the exact terms "sexual exploitation" or "sexual slavery" - references to women being "sexually exploited," "exploited sexually," or "sexually abused for commercial or non-material gain" are sufficient for Substantive. Do not Code Yes - Substantive from a general clause covering "all forms of violence and exploitation against women" alone - this must be paired with language that specifically confirms a sexual-exploitation nexus, per the note above.



1.10 | Technology-facilitated violence | Does the national legal framework substantively address technology-facilitated violence (e.g., cyber violence, cyber harassment, cyberbullying, cyber pornography, online sexual exploitation, digital abuse, or other violence committed through digital technologies)?

Code Yes - Substantive if, in the operative provisions of any relevant instrument:
- cyber violence, cyber harassment, cyberbullying, cyber pornography, online sexual exploitation, or a similar technology-facilitated form of violence is defined, prohibited, or criminalised;
- digital or online forms of violence are recognised as forms of violence against women with attached legal consequences;
- technology-facilitated violence is included in an operative definition, list of prohibited conduct, or protected-groups provision.

Code Yes - Referenced only if technology-facilitated violence is mentioned solely in a preamble, recital, explanatory section, or as part of the cited basis for ratifying a relevant international/regional instrument (e.g., the Budapest Convention, where relevant).

Code No if the framework refers only to:
- digital information systems, reporting platforms, hotlines, databases, or communication technologies used for service delivery;
- online administration or digital public services; or
- general references to technology without recognising technology-facilitated violence as a form of violence, anywhere in the framework.
