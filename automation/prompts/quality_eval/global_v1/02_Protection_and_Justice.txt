2.1 | Criminal penalty | Does the national legal framework establish criminal penalties for acts of violence against women?
 
Code Yes - Substantive if, in the operative provisions of any relevant instrument, at least one form of violence identified in the Scope of Violence domain (items 1–10) is defined as a criminal offence with an attached sanction - imprisonment, a specified fine range, or another criminal penalty - regardless of whether the offence sits in a general criminal code or dedicated VAW legislation.
 
Code Yes - Referenced only if the framework states generally that violence against women "is punishable by law" or "shall be sanctioned," without defining the offence's elements or specifying a penalty.
 
Code No if no criminal sanction attaches to any form of violence against women anywhere in the framework, or only civil/administrative remedies exist with no criminal consequence.
 
2.2 | Protection order | Does the national legal framework provide for protection or restraining orders for victims of violence against women?
 
Code Yes - Substantive if an operative provision specifies: who may apply (victim, police, or prosecutor on the victim's behalf); the issuing authority (court, police, or other designated body); the order's duration; and a defined consequence for breach (civil or criminal).
 
Code Yes - Referenced only if the framework states victims "may seek protection" or authorities "shall take necessary measures for protection," without a defined application process, issuing authority, duration, or breach consequence.
 
Code No if no protective/restraining order mechanism exists in any relevant instrument.
 

2.3 | Specialised service unit for victims of VAW in police offices | Does the national legal framework establish or mandate a specialised unit within police services for victims of violence against women?
 
Code Yes - Substantive if an operative provision designates a specific unit, desk, or division within police services (e.g., a women's/family protection unit) with a defined mandate to handle VAW cases, whether or not it further specifies staffing requirements (trained personnel, female officers, private interview space).
 
Code Yes - Referenced only if the framework states police "shall provide adequate services to women victims" or similar, without designating a specific unit, desk, or division.
 
Code No if no such unit is mentioned or mandated anywhere in the framework.
 

2.4 | Specialised court | Does the national legal framework establish or mandate a specialised court, chamber, or judicial procedure for cases of violence against women?
 
Code Yes - Substantive if an operative provision designates a specific court, chamber, judicial officer, or expedited/specialised procedure (e.g., fast-track hearings, judges with mandated VAW training) for VAW cases.
 
Code Yes - Referenced only if the framework states a general commitment to "ensure access to justice" or "expedite VAW cases," without designating a specific judicial mechanism.
 
Code No if no specialised judicial mechanism is mentioned or mandated anywhere in the framework.

2.5 | Marital rape / spousal exemption | Does the national legal framework recognise marital rape as a criminal offence or otherwise remove any spousal exemption from criminal liability for sexual violence?

Code Yes - Substantive if, in the operative provisions of any relevant instrument:
-  the framework expressly criminalises rape, sexual assault, or sexual violence within marriage or an intimate-partner relationship;
-  the framework expressly provides that marriage or spousal status does not constitute a defence, exemption, or exception to criminal liability for rape, sexual assault, or other sexual violence;
-  the framework expressly states that a spouse or intimate partner may be prosecuted for rape, sexual assault, or equivalent sexual offences against their partner;
-  the framework removes or limits any marital exemption, including by providing that the general offence of rape or sexual assault applies regardless of the victim's marital or relationship status;
-  the framework recognises withdrawal of consent, lack of consent, or absence of consent as legally relevant to sexual offences between spouses or intimate partners, where this results in criminal liability for the conduct.

Code Yes - Referenced only if marital rape, spousal exemption, or the applicability of rape/sexual-offence provisions to spouses is mentioned solely in a preamble, recital, explanatory section, commentary, or as part of the cited basis for ratifying a relevant international or regional instrument, without an operative provision establishing criminal liability or removing the exemption.

Code No if:
-  the framework expressly provides that a spouse cannot be prosecuted for rape or sexual assault against their spouse, or otherwise maintains a marital/spousal exemption from criminal liability;
-  the framework expressly excludes sexual intercourse or sexual acts between spouses from the relevant rape or sexual-offence provisions;
-  the framework does not address marital rape or the spousal exemption anywhere in the framework, and the general sexual-offence provisions do not expressly establish their application irrespective of marital or intimate-partner status; or
-  the framework addresses domestic violence or sexual violence generally but does not establish that rape or sexual assault provisions apply to spouses or remove a marital exemption.

Do not infer that marital rape is criminalised merely because rape or sexual violence is generally criminalised. Yes - Substantive requires an operative provision establishing that the offence applies within marriage/intimate partnerships or expressly removing a spousal or marital exemption. General provisions on consent, domestic violence, or sexual violence are insufficient unless their application to spouses is clear from the operative legal framework.
