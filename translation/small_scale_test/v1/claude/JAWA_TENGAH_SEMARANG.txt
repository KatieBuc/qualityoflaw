CHAPTER I
GENERAL PROVISIONS

Article 1

In this Regional Regulation, the following terms shall mean:

1. Region means Semarang Regency.
2. Regional Government means the administration of government affairs by the Regional Government and the Regional People's Representative Council according to the principles of autonomy and assistance duties, with the widest principle of autonomy within the system and principles of the Unitary State of the Republic of Indonesia as referred to in the 1945 Constitution of the Republic of Indonesia.
3. Regional Government means the Regent and regional apparatus as the element administering Regional Government.
4. The Regional People's Representative Council of Semarang Regency, hereinafter abbreviated as DPRD, means the regional people's representative body as an element administering regional government.
5. Regent means the Regent of Semarang.
6. The relevant Regional Apparatus Work Unit, hereinafter referred to as the relevant SKPD, means the Regional Apparatus Work Unit that has the main duties and functions in the relevant field.
7. Human Rights means the set of rights inherent in the nature and existence of humans as creatures of God Almighty, and constitute His gift which must be respected, upheld, and protected by the state, law, Government, and every person for the sake of honoring and protecting human dignity.
8. Women's Empowerment means every effort to improve physical, mental, spiritual, social, knowledge, and skill capabilities so that women are ready to be utilized according to their respective abilities.
9. Women's Protection means all efforts aimed at protecting women and providing a sense of security in the fulfillment of their rights by giving consistent and systematic attention aimed at achieving gender equality.
10. Discrimination Against Women means any distinction, exclusion, or restriction made on the basis of sex which has the effect or purpose of impairing or nullifying the recognition, enjoyment, or exercise by women, irrespective of their marital status, of human rights and fundamental freedoms in the political, economic, social, cultural, civil, or any other field, on a basis of equality between men and women.
11. Gender means the concept referring to the distinction of roles and responsibilities of men and women that arise from and can change due to social and cultural conditions of society.
12. Gender Equality means equal conditions for men and women to obtain opportunities and their rights as human beings in order to be able to play a role and participate in political, economic, social, cultural, defense, and security activities, and equality in enjoying the results of development.
13. Society means individuals, families, groups, and social organizations and/or community organizations.
14. Family means the smallest unit in society consisting of husband and wife, husband and wife and their children, or a father and his children, or a mother and her children.
15. Workforce means every person capable of carrying out work to produce goods and/or services either to meet their own needs or those of society.
16. Worker/Laborer means every person who works and receives wages or remuneration in another form.

CHAPTER II
SCOPE

Article 2

The scope of this Regional Regulation includes:
a. principles and objectives;
b. women's rights;
c. obligations and responsibilities of the regional government;
d. women's empowerment;
e. women's protection;
f. implementation of women's protection;
g. strategy for women's empowerment and protection;
h. mechanism for the administration of women's empowerment and protection;
i. financing;
j. guidance and supervision;
k. transitional provisions; and
l. closing provisions.

CHAPTER III
PRINCIPLES AND OBJECTIVES

Article 3

Women's Empowerment and Protection shall be implemented based on the principles of:
a. respect for human rights;
b. justice and gender equality;
c. non-discrimination; and
d. the best interests of women.

Article 4

The objectives of this Regional Regulation are:
a. to empower women, both individually and in groups and broader society, so they are able to live more independently; and
b. to prevent and address the risk of violence and vulnerability of women so that the continuity of their lives can be fulfilled in accordance with the principles of equality and justice.

CHAPTER IV
WOMEN'S RIGHTS

Article 5

(1) Every woman has the right to:
a. live and maintain her life and improve her standard of living;
b. fulfill her basic needs to grow and develop properly, build a family, and continue her lineage;
c. develop her personality, obtain an education, and improve the quality of her life so as to become a faithful, devout, responsible, noble-charactered, happy, and prosperous human being in accordance with human rights;
d. obtain justice, a sense of security, and freedom to express opinions without discrimination;
e. be involved in every stage of the development process;
f. be free from slavery or enslavement and threats;
g. obtain protection of her personal self, family, honor, dignity, and property rights;
h. obtain welfare and a decent life;
i. participate in politics;
j. perform legal acts; and
k. freely choose a partner in her marriage.

(2) Every wife, during the bond of marriage, has the same rights and responsibilities as her husband regarding all matters concerning their married life, relationships with their children, and ownership and management of joint property in accordance with applicable laws and regulations.

(3) After the dissolution of marriage, a woman has rights and responsibilities with her former husband regarding all matters concerning the children, with due regard to the best interests of the child.

CHAPTER V
OBLIGATIONS AND RESPONSIBILITIES OF THE REGIONAL GOVERNMENT

Article 6

(1) The Regional Government is obligated and responsible for women's empowerment and protection.

(2) Efforts for women's empowerment and protection as referred to in paragraph (1) include:
a. establishment of policies, programs, and activities;
b. establishment of implementation guidelines;
c. provision of services; and
d. coordination of policies, programs, and activities.

(3) Efforts for women's empowerment and protection as referred to in paragraph (2) shall be carried out in an integrated manner in accordance with applicable laws and regulations.

CHAPTER VI
WOMEN'S EMPOWERMENT

Article 7

Women's empowerment is directed at obtaining opportunities and rights as human beings in order to be able to play a role and participate in the fields of:
a. economy;
b. social and culture;
c. politics; and
d. law.

Article 8

Women's empowerment in the economic field as referred to in Article 7 letter a shall be carried out through:
a. provision of skills and job training;
b. facilitation of the formation of productive economic business groups;
c. facilitation of the strengthening and development of productive economic business groups;
d. facilitation and capital assistance; and
e. facilitation of marketing network development.

Article 9

Women's empowerment in the socio-cultural field as referred to in Article 7 letter b shall be carried out through:
a. increasing knowledge, attitude, and skills to encourage the fulfillment of tiered education according to potential to improve social status;
b. increasing awareness, willingness, and ability to address health problems through quality promotive, preventive, curative, and rehabilitative efforts, particularly in the field of reproductive health;
c. increasing awareness and knowledge about independent, healthy, and prosperous family planning, including access to consultation services and marriage registration; and
d. facilitation and efforts to preserve customs and develop science, technology, art, and culture for the advancement of women.

Article 10

(1) Women's empowerment in the political field as referred to in Article 7 letter c includes:
a. involvement of women in decision-making at various levels;
b. providing opportunities for women to hold public office;
c. participation in general elections; and
d. self-development through organizations for association, assembly, and expression of opinion.

(2) Women's empowerment in the political field as referred to in paragraph (1) shall be carried out in accordance with applicable laws and regulations.

Article 11

(1) Women's empowerment in the legal field as referred to in Article 7 letter d includes:
a. increasing awareness and knowledge in the legal field through communication, information, and education services;
b. facilitating access and legal consultation services.

(2) Women's empowerment in the legal field as referred to in paragraph (1) shall be carried out in accordance with applicable laws and regulations.

CHAPTER VII
WOMEN'S PROTECTION

Article 12

(1) Women's protection includes:
a. social protection;
b. economic protection;
c. legal protection.

(2) Social protection as referred to in paragraph (1) letter a is to guarantee the continuity of women's lives in the form of social protection to ensure the fulfillment of decent living needs.

(3) Economic protection as referred to in paragraph (1) letter b is to prevent and address the risk of vulnerability resulting from shocks and social vulnerability of a person, family, group, and/or community for the continuity of women's lives.

(4) Legal protection as referred to in paragraph (1) letter c is implemented in terms of providing protection to women who become witnesses and victims of acts of violence and discrimination.

CHAPTER VIII
IMPLEMENTATION OF WOMEN'S PROTECTION

Part One
General

Article 13

The implementation of women's protection is provided to:
a. women workers/laborers;
b. elderly women;
c. women with disabilities;
d. homeless women;
e. female domestic workers;
f. female heads of households;
g. women former inmates of correctional institutions;
h. women victims of disaster;
i. female commercial sex workers; and
j. women victims of violence.

Part Two
Women Workers/Laborers

Article 14

(1) The implementation of protection for women workers/laborers as referred to in Article 13 letter a is to provide protection from acts of economic exploitation and cruel, inhumane treatment, and to seek decent appreciation for their work.

(2) Women workers/laborers as referred to in paragraph (1) shall obtain protection regarding:
a. recognition of rights, wages, and decent working conditions;
b. health and social security;
c. access to information and legal consultation services;
d. opportunities to obtain knowledge and skills to improve status; and
e. witnesses and victims.

Part Three
Elderly Women

Article 15

(1) The implementation of protection for elderly women as referred to in Article 13 letter b is in the context of extending life expectancy so they can carry out their social functions and play an active role appropriately in their social lives.

(2) Elderly women as referred to in paragraph (1) shall obtain rights to protection regarding:
a. basic health services to maintain and improve health status, including gerontologic/geriatric services;
b. use of facilities and accessibility of public facilities and infrastructure; and
c. government administration services.

Part Four
Women with Disabilities

Article 16

(1) The implementation of protection for women with disabilities as referred to in Article 13 letter c is in the context of providing protection and prevention against forms of discrimination, including being free from cruel, inhumane treatment that demeans human dignity, and being free from exploitation.

(2) Women with disabilities as referred to in paragraph (1) shall obtain rights to protection regarding:
a. guarantee of continuity and development of personal self;
b. participation in the fields of education, health, employment, politics, sports, arts and culture, and the use of information and communication technology;
c. accessibility of information, legal assistance, mobility, and social services; and
d. early intervention efforts, including treatment and rehabilitation to improve function and capacity.

Part Five
Homeless Women

Article 17

(1) The implementation of protection for homeless women as referred to in Article 13 letter d is in the context of providing protection and prevention against forms of violence caused by the absence of shelter or permanent residence.

(2) Homeless women as referred to in paragraph (1) shall obtain rights to protection regarding:
a. decent housing;
b. health and social security;
c. population administration registration;
d. opportunities to obtain skills to improve economic status; and
e. access to information and legal consultation services.

Part Six
Female Domestic Workers

Article 18

(1) The implementation of protection for female domestic workers as referred to in Article 13 letter e is in the context of providing protection and prevention against forms of discrimination, cruel and inhumane treatment that demeans human dignity, and exploitation in employment relationships.

(2) Female domestic workers as referred to in paragraph (1) shall obtain protection regarding:
a. recognition of rights, wages, and decent working conditions;
b. health and social security;
c. access to information and legal consultation services; and
d. opportunities to obtain knowledge and skills to improve economic status.

Part Seven
Female Heads of Households

Article 19

(1) The implementation of protection for female heads of households as referred to in Article 13 letter f is in the context of providing protection and prevention against forms of discrimination, negative stigma, and economic empowerment for female heads of households.

(2) Female heads of households as referred to in paragraph (1) shall obtain rights to protection regarding:
a. acts of discrimination and efforts to weaken the function of women as economic supporters of the family;
b. strengthening of capability and recognition as head of household in their environment; and
c. accessibility to economic and financial institutions.

Part Eight
Women Former Inmates of Correctional Institutions

Article 20

(1) The implementation of protection for women former inmates of Correctional Institutions as referred to in Article 13 letter g is in the context of providing protection from obstacles to adjusting to life in society, so they can carry out a normal life.

(2) Women former inmates of Correctional Institutions as referred to in paragraph (1) shall obtain protection regarding:
a. social rehabilitation and reintegration; and
b. opportunities to obtain knowledge and skills to improve economic status.

Part Nine
Women Victims of Disaster

Article 21

(1) The implementation of protection for women victims of disaster as referred to in Article 13 letter h is in the context of providing protection during and after a disaster.

(2) Women victims of disaster as referred to in paragraph (1) shall obtain protection regarding:
a. access to decent housing;
b. health services;
c. access to information services and legal consultation; and
d. post-disaster recovery.

Part Ten
Female Commercial Sex Workers

Article 22

(1) The implementation of protection for female commercial sex workers as referred to in Article 13 letter i is in the context of providing health protection and improving life skills capability as capital to improve social status.

(2) Protection for female commercial sex workers as referred to in paragraph (1) includes:
a. providing life skills capability;
b. facilitating reproductive health services; and
c. preventing the occurrence of trafficking in persons.

Part Eleven
Women Victims of Violence

Article 23

(1) The implementation of protection for Women Victims of Violence as referred to in Article 13 letter j is in the context of providing legal protection and/or health recovery.

(2) Protection for Women Victims of Violence as referred to in paragraph (1) includes:
a. legal assistance;
b. accompaniment after the occurrence of acts of violence;
c. social rehabilitation and reintegration; and
d. health services.

CHAPTER IX
STRATEGY FOR WOMEN'S EMPOWERMENT AND PROTECTION

Article 24

(1) Women's empowerment and protection shall be carried out by integrating gender mainstreaming and gender-disaggregated data in an integrated manner based on applicable laws and regulations.

(2) In women's empowerment and protection, a Working Group and Integrated Service shall be formed, established by Regent's Decree.

(3) Women's empowerment and protection shall be coordinated by the Agency handling Women's Empowerment.

CHAPTER X
MECHANISM FOR THE ADMINISTRATION OF WOMEN'S EMPOWERMENT AND PROTECTION

Article 25

The mechanism for the administration of women's empowerment and protection shall be carried out through the stages of:
a. formulation of policy for planning programs and activities;
b. budgeting of programs and activities;
c. implementation of programs and activities; and
d. monitoring, evaluation, and reporting.

Article 26

(1) Formulation of policy for planning programs and activities as referred to in Article 25 letter a shall be carried out through:
a. mapping, collecting, processing, and presenting data;
b. coordination and synchronization of program and activity planning preparation with relevant SKPDs; and
c. establishment of programs and activities in planning documents.

(2) Formulation of policy for planning programs and activities shall be further regulated in minimum service standards and standard operating procedures.

(3) Formulation of policy for planning programs and activities for the administration of women's empowerment and protection shall be coordinated by the SKPD responsible for regional development planning.

(4) Budgeting of programs and activities as referred to in Article 25 letter b shall be carried out through general budget policy set forth in the Regional Budget (APBD).

(5) Implementation of programs and activities as referred to in Article 25 letter c shall be carried out through:
a. implementation of programs and activities set forth in SKPD budget documents; and
b. coordination and synchronization of program and activity implementation with relevant SKPDs.

(6) Implementation of monitoring, evaluation, and reporting as referred to in Article 25 letter d shall be carried out through:
a. conducting periodic monitoring and evaluation of the implementation of programs and activities that have been carried out; and
b. preparing periodic reports.

CHAPTER XI
FINANCING

Article 27

All costs for the implementation of policy for planning programs and activities related to Women's Empowerment and Protection shall be sourced from:
a. the State Revenue and Expenditure Budget;
b. the Regional Revenue and Expenditure Budget; and
c. other legitimate and non-binding sources.

CHAPTER XII
GUIDANCE AND SUPERVISION

Article 28

(1) The Regent or the SKPD responsible for women's empowerment shall conduct guidance and supervision of women's empowerment and protection.

(2) Forms of guidance as referred to in paragraph (1) are as follows:
a. providing socialization to the community and business sector regarding policies on women's empowerment and protection;
b. providing books, leaflets, brochures on women's empowerment and protection and other related issues and disseminating them to the community;
c. facilitating capacity building for institutions implementing women's empowerment and protection;
d. facilitating forums for women's participation and women's organizations;
e. facilitating rehabilitation and reintegration services for women victims of disaster and victims of violence;
f. coordinating the participation of the business sector and other institutions, including international assistance for women's empowerment and protection; and
g. giving awards to communities and the business sector, both individually and institutionally, that have carried out efforts for women's empowerment and protection.

(3) Forms of supervision as referred to in paragraph (1) may take the form of monitoring and evaluation activities of women's empowerment and protection.

CHAPTER XIII
TRANSITIONAL PROVISIONS

Article 29

At the time this Regional Regulation comes into force, all provisions of regional laws and regulations relating to the Administration of Women's Empowerment and Protection that have existed thus far shall remain in effect insofar as they do not conflict with the provisions of this Regional Regulation.

CHAPTER XIV
CLOSING PROVISIONS

Article 30

This Regional Regulation shall come into force on the date of its promulgation.

So that every person may be aware of it, it is ordered that this Regional Regulation be promulgated by placing it in the Regional Gazette of Semarang Regency.

I. GENERAL

The 1945 Constitution of the Republic of Indonesia mandates the authority of regional governments to regulate and manage their own government affairs directed at accelerating the realization of community welfare through improved services, empowerment, and community participation. The authority of the Regional Government is to make regional policies to provide services, increase participation, initiative, and community empowerment aimed at improving the welfare of the people.

The administration of regional autonomy must always be oriented towards improving community welfare while always paying attention to the interests and aspirations that grow within society. Therefore, government affairs related to basic services such as basic education, health, fulfillment of minimum living needs, and basic environmental infrastructure must be fulfilled.

Human rights are basic rights inherently attached to human beings, universal and enduring in nature, and therefore must be protected, respected, upheld, and must not be neglected, diminished, or seized by anyone. In the context of Human Rights (HAM) enforcement to guarantee justice for every individual, efforts must be made to prevent all forms of unbalanced treatment or treatment considered harmful to other parties, whether between individuals, between groups of individuals, or between individuals as well as between the government and society, including the process of marginalization of women that causes women to be vulnerable to acts of violence, discrimination, and exploitation in both the domestic and public spheres.

Women have the same and equal rights as men; however, in certain discriminatory conditions, the government needs to carry out affirmative action so that women's rights and women in vulnerable groups receive protection so they do not experience violence and can live a decent life according to the principles of humanity, equality, and justice.

Women's empowerment and women's protection are implemented in order to realize gender equality and justice. To carry out these affairs, efforts are needed to increase the capacity of women's resources and strengthen gender mainstreaming institutions through gender mainstreaming strategies and gender-disaggregated data.

The Government of Semarang Regency, as the party with the authority to regulate and protect the citizens of Semarang Regency, considers it necessary to continuously formulate regulations to provide guarantees for women's empowerment and women's protection in Semarang Regency.

This Regional Regulation is expected to be able to encourage efforts to improve the physical and mental condition of women in fulfilling their rights and life needs as part of human rights in various fields of development, to increase the commitment and effectiveness of the regional government in implementing policies, programs, and activities, and to encourage the institutional capacity of the government and society that improves the quality of women's lives.

Protection of commercial sex workers in this Regional Regulation is not intended to legalize the existence of commercial sex workers, but to provide protection to said commercial sex workers in various sectors.

Paragraph (1)
Gender Mainstreaming, hereinafter abbreviated as PUG, is a strategy built to integrate gender as an integral dimension of the planning, budgeting, implementation, monitoring, and evaluation of development policies, programs, and activities. PUG is a strategy that can reduce gender gaps to achieve gender equality by using a gender perspective in the development process; women's empowerment is an affirmative strategy to achieve gender equality aimed at increasing the fulfillment of women's rights to protection from acts of violence and discrimination.
Paragraph (2)
Integrated services may take the form of an Integrated Service Center for Women's and Children's Empowerment, hereinafter abbreviated as P2TP2A, which is a form of service vehicle for women and children in efforts to fulfill information and needs in the fields of education, health, economy, politics, law, and the fulfillment and handling of acts of violence and trafficking against women and children.
