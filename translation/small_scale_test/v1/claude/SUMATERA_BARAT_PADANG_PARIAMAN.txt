REGENT OF PADANG PARIAMAN
PROVINCE OF WEST SUMATRA
REGIONAL REGULATION OF PADANG PARIAMAN REGENCY
NUMBER 6 OF 2017
CONCERNING
WOMEN'S EMPOWERMENT AND CHILD PROTECTION
BY THE GRACE OF GOD ALMIGHTY
THE REGENT OF PADANG PARIAMAN,

Considering: a. that in order to guarantee Women's Empowerment and Child Protection in accordance with human dignity and to obtain Protection from Violence, discrimination and other violations, there is a need for legal certainty;
b. that in order to provide legal certainty in the implementation of Women's Empowerment and Child Protection and to carry out the mandatory affairs of Regional government, regulation in the form of a Regional Regulation is needed;
c. that based on the considerations referred to in points a and b, it is necessary to establish a Regional Regulation on Women's Empowerment and Child Protection;

In view of: 1. Article 18 paragraph (6) of the 1945 Constitution of the Republic of Indonesia;
2. Law Number 12 of 1956 concerning the Establishment of Autonomous Regency Regions within the Province of Central Sumatra (State Gazette of the Republic of Indonesia Year 1956 Number 25);
3. Law Number 39 of 1999 concerning Human Rights (State Gazette of the Republic of Indonesia Year 1999 Number 165, Supplement to the State Gazette Number 3886);
4. Law Number 23 of 2002 concerning Child Protection (State Gazette of the Republic of Indonesia Year 2002 Number 109, Supplement to the State Gazette of the Republic of Indonesia Number 4235) as amended by Law Number 35 of 2014 concerning Amendment to Law Number 23 of 2002 concerning Child Protection (State Gazette of the Republic of Indonesia Year 2014 Number 297, Supplement to the State Gazette of the Republic of Indonesia Number 5606);
5. Law Number 23 of 2014 concerning Regional Government (State Gazette of the Republic of Indonesia Year 2014 Number 244, Supplement to the State Gazette of the Republic of Indonesia Number 5587) as amended several times, most recently by Law Number 9 of 2015 (State Gazette of the Republic of Indonesia Year 2015 Number 58, Supplement to the State Gazette of the Republic of Indonesia Number 5679);
6. Regulation of the State Minister for Women's Empowerment and Child Protection Number 1 of 2010 concerning Minimum Service Standards for the Integrated Service Sector for Women and Children Victims of Violence;
7. Regulation of the State Minister for Women's Empowerment and Child Protection Number 11 of 2011 concerning Policy on the Development of Child-Friendly Regencies;

With the Joint Approval of
THE REGIONAL PEOPLE'S REPRESENTATIVE COUNCIL OF PADANG PARIAMAN REGENCY
and
THE REGENT OF PADANG PARIAMAN

HAS DECIDED:

To Enact: REGIONAL REGULATION CONCERNING WOMEN'S EMPOWERMENT AND CHILD PROTECTION.

CHAPTER I
GENERAL PROVISIONS

Article 1

In this Regional Regulation, the following terms shall mean:

1. Region refers to Padang Pariaman Regency.
2. Regional Government refers to the Government of Padang Pariaman Regency.
3. Regent refers to the Regent of Padang Pariaman Regency.
4. Regional Work Unit, hereinafter abbreviated as SKPD, refers to a Regional Work Unit within the Government of Padang Pariaman Regency.
5. Women's Empowerment refers to efforts at Protection, strengthening of basic rights, improvement of quality of life, and increased participation of women in development.
6. Child Protection refers to all activities to guarantee and protect Children and their rights so that they may live, grow, develop, and participate optimally in accordance with human dignity, and obtain Protection from Violence and discrimination.
7. Child refers to anyone who has not yet reached the age of 18 (eighteen) years, including a Child still in the womb and not yet married.
8. Prevention refers to direct efforts undertaken by the Regional Government, the community, and parents and/or families to prevent violations of the rights of women and Children.
9. Violence refers to any act resulting in misery or suffering, whether physical, sexual, psychological and/or domestic neglect, including threats to commit such acts, coercion, or unlawful deprivation of a person's liberty.
10. Children's Forum refers to a vehicle for child participation established with the aim of facilitating children's involvement in decision-making processes and enjoying changes related to their lives, whether directly or indirectly, conducted with the agreement and willingness of all children based on awareness and understanding, in accordance with their age and level of maturity of thought.
11. Child-Friendly Regency, hereinafter abbreviated as KLA, refers to a Regency that has a child-rights-based development system through the integration of commitments and resources of the government, community, and business world, planned comprehensively and sustainably in policies, programs, and activities to guarantee the fulfillment of children's rights.
12. Gender Mainstreaming refers to a strategy built to integrate gender as an integral dimension of the planning, formulation, implementation, monitoring, and evaluation of national development policy programs.
13. Community refers to individuals, families, groups, and social organizations and/or community organizations.
14. Women Victims of Violence refers to women who experience acts resulting in misery or suffering, whether physical, sexual, or psychological, including threats of certain acts, coercion, or arbitrary deprivation of liberty, occurring either in the public sphere or in private life.
15. Child Victims of Violence refers to Children who experience acts resulting in misery or suffering, whether physical, mental, sexual, psychological, or neglect that threatens life, body, and degrades human dignity.
16. Trafficking in persons refers to the act of recruitment, transportation, harboring, sending, transfer, or receipt of a person by means of threat of Violence, use of Violence, abduction, confinement, forgery, fraud, abuse of power or a position of vulnerability, debt bondage or the giving of payments or benefits, so as to obtain the consent of a person having control over another person, whether carried out within a State or between States, for the purpose of exploitation or resulting in the exploitation of a person.
17. Complaint handling refers to a series of actions undertaken by integrated service providers to follow up on reports of acts of Violence against women and Children, submitted by victims, families, or the community.
18. Social rehabilitation refers to services intended to restore and develop the capabilities of a person experiencing social dysfunction so that they may carry out their social functions normally.
19. Law enforcement refers to actions by officials given authority by the state to implement statutory regulations.
20. Legal aid refers to legal services provided by legal companions and advocates to assist witnesses and/or victims of Violence against women and Children in a gender-sensitive manner.
21. Repatriation refers to efforts to return women and Child victims of Violence from abroad to a point of disembarkation/entry point, or from a receiving Region to their Region of origin.
22. Social reintegration refers to efforts to reunite victims with their family, a substitute family, or the community that can provide Protection and fulfillment of the victim's needs.
23. Safe house refers to a temporary shelter intended to provide Protection and a sense of security to victims of Violence.
24. Shelter house refers to a vehicle for the development and welfare services of street Children carrying out activities of social assistance/guidance, care, treatment, education, and health, and skills guidance to ensure that Children do not engage in activities on the street so that they may grow and develop normally.
25. Spiritual guidance refers to efforts at psychological recovery through the strengthening of the religion adhered to by the victim.
26. Gender Mainstreaming refers to a strategy built to integrate gender as an integral dimension of the planning, formulation, implementation, monitoring, and evaluation of regional development policies, programs, and activities.
27. Gender responsiveness refers to consistent and systematic attention to the differences between women and men in society, accompanied by efforts to remove structural and cultural barriers to achieving gender equality.
28. Gender Sensitivity or Gender Awareness refers to the ability to identify gaps in power relations between women and men within the family and within the community, the impact of the gender-based division of labor on women and men, and the fact that the experiences, problems, needs, interests, and aspirations of women and men differ.
29. Public service provider refers to any state institution provider, corporation, independent agency established under a Law for public service activities, and other legal entity established solely for public service activities.
30. Community Organization refers to an organization established and formed by the community voluntarily based on shared aspirations, will, needs, interests, activities, and goals to participate in development for the achievement of the goals of the Unitary State of the Republic of Indonesia based on Pancasila.
31. Parent refers to a biological father and/or mother, or a stepfather and/or stepmother, or an adoptive father and/or adoptive mother.
32. Family refers to the smallest unit in society consisting of a husband and wife, or a husband, wife and their Child, or a father and his Child, or a mother and her Child, or blood relatives in a direct line upward or downward to the third degree.
33. Customs refer to enduring patterns of behavior passed down from one generation to the next as a heritage, such that they are strongly integrated with the patterns of community behavior.

Article 2

The implementation of Women's Empowerment and Child Protection shall be based on the principles of:
a. the best interests of women and Children;
b. gender justice and gender equality;
c. local wisdom;
d. law enforcement and legal certainty;
e. non-discrimination;
f. the right to life, survival, and development;
g. respect for the views of the Child; and
h. law enforcement and legal certainty.

Article 3

This Regional Regulation aims to:
a. guarantee the fulfillment of the rights of women and Children in the Region;
b. serve as a guideline for the Region in planning, policy, and strategy for Women's Empowerment and Child Protection;
c. protect and prevent women and Children from acts, decisions, and policies that violate the rights of women and Children;
d. enhance the role and value of local wisdom and the role of custom in Women's Empowerment and Child Protection.

Article 4

The scope of regulation of Women's Empowerment and Child Protection includes:
a. improvement of the quality of life of women;
b. Protection of women;
c. gender and child data systems;
d. fulfillment of children's rights;
e. Protection of Children;
f. community participation; and
g. guidance and supervision.

CHAPTER II
IMPROVEMENT OF THE QUALITY OF LIFE OF WOMEN

Part One
General

Article 5

(1) In order to improve the quality of life of women, the regional government is responsible for:
a. respecting and guaranteeing women's rights without discrimination in accordance with human dignity;
b. implementing Gender Mainstreaming in the Region;
c. forming a Gender Mainstreaming working group in the Region;
d. enhancing Women's Empowerment in roles, quality, and capability in the fields of:
1. politics;
2. law;
3. social affairs; and
4. economics.
e. strengthening and developing institutions providing Women's Empowerment services in the Region.

(2) The Regional Government's responsibility in efforts to improve the quality of life of women as referred to in paragraph (1) shall be carried out by the SKPD in accordance with its duties and functions.

Part Two
Implementation of Gender Mainstreaming in the Region

Article 6

(1) The Regent is responsible for the implementation of Gender Mainstreaming in the Region.
(2) The Regent's responsibility as referred to in paragraph (1) may be delegated to the Deputy Regent.

Article 7

In implementing Gender Mainstreaming in the Region as referred to in Article 5 paragraph (1) letter b, the Regent shall designate the SKPD responsible for Women's Empowerment as the coordinator for the implementation of Gender Mainstreaming.

Part Three
Formation of the Regional Gender Mainstreaming Working Group

Article 8

(1) In efforts to accelerate the institutionalization of Gender Mainstreaming throughout all Regional SKPDs, a Regional Gender Mainstreaming working group shall be formed.
(2) Members of the Gender Mainstreaming working group shall be the heads/leaders of all SKPDs.
(3) The Regent shall designate the head of Bappeda (Regional Development Planning Agency) as chair of the Regional Gender Mainstreaming working group, and the head of the SKPD responsible for Women's Empowerment as head of the secretariat of the Regional Gender Mainstreaming working group.
(4) The formation of the Regional Gender Mainstreaming working group shall be established by decision of the Regent.

Article 9

The Regional Gender Mainstreaming working group as referred to in Article 14 [sic] has the duty to:
a. promote and facilitate Gender Mainstreaming to each SKPD;
b. carry out socialization and advocacy of Gender Mainstreaming to sub-district heads (camat) and village heads (wali nagari);
c. draw up an annual work program;
d. encourage the realization of gender-responsive planning and budgeting;
e. draw up an annual work plan for the Gender Mainstreaming working group;
f. be responsible to the Regent through the Deputy Regent;
g. formulate policy recommendations to the Regent;
h. prepare a Regional Gender Profile;
i. monitor the implementation of Gender Mainstreaming in each agency;
j. establish a technical team to conduct analysis of the Regional budget;
k. draw up a Regional Action Plan for Regional Gender Mainstreaming; and
l. encourage the holding of elections and appointment of focal points in each SKPD.

Article 10

(1) The Technical Team referred to in Article 9 letter j shall consist of officials who understand gender-responsive budget analysis.
(2) The Regional Action Plan for Regional Gender Mainstreaming referred to in Article 9 letter k shall contain:
a. Gender Mainstreaming in regional statutory regulations;
b. Gender Mainstreaming in the regional development cycle;
c. strengthening of Gender Mainstreaming institutions in the Region; and
d. strengthening of community participation in the Region.

Article 11

(1) The Gender Mainstreaming Focal Point in each SKPD shall consist of officials and/or staff responsible for planning and/or programs.
(2) The Gender Mainstreaming Focal Point referred to in paragraph (1) has the duty to:
a. promote Gender Mainstreaming within the work unit;
b. facilitate the preparation of gender-responsive work plans and budgets for the SKPD;
c. carry out training, socialization, and advocacy of Gender Mainstreaming to all officials and staff within the SKPD;
d. report on the implementation of Gender Mainstreaming to the SKPD leadership;
e. encourage the implementation of gender analysis of policies, programs, and activities within the work unit; and
f. facilitate the preparation of gender data in each respective SKPD.
(3) The Gender Mainstreaming Focal Point referred to in paragraph (1) shall be selected and appointed by the leadership of the SKPD.

Part Four
Enhancement of the Role, Quality, and Capability of Women in the Political Field

Article 12

Efforts to enhance the role, quality, and capability of women in the political field as referred to in Article 5 paragraph (1) letter d number 1 include:
a. conducting socialization to increase women's political participation in general elections;
b. implementing programs that encourage the level of women's involvement in political processes and public office;
c. facilitating the self-development of women in organizing, forming associations, and assembling;
d. involving women in decision-making concerning women's interests.

Part Five
Enhancement of the Role, Quality, and Capability of Women in the Legal Field

Article 13

Efforts to enhance the role, quality, and capability of women in the legal field as referred to in Article 5 paragraph (1) letter d number 2 include:
a. conducting socialization to increase women's legal knowledge and awareness;
b. mapping and reviewing gender-biased policies;
c. involving women's organizations in the process of drafting and discussing Regional legal products related to women's participation.

Part Six
Enhancement of the Role, Quality, and Capability of Women in the Social Field

Article 14

Efforts to enhance the role, quality, and capability of women in the social field as referred to in Article 5 paragraph (1) letter d number 3 include:
a. improving knowledge and skills to encourage the fulfillment of education in accordance with women's potential;
b. improving and fulfilling the quality of health for women;
c. raising awareness and knowledge about independent, healthy, and prosperous family planning, including access to consultation services and marriage registration;
d. raising women's awareness of the impact of early marriage;
e. facilitating and pursuing the preservation of customs and the development of science, technology, art, and culture for the advancement of women;
f. raising women's awareness and knowledge of independent, healthy, and prosperous family planning;
g. carrying out programs to reduce the maternal mortality rate;
h. encouraging the formation of women's organizations in the Region.

Part Seven
Enhancement of the Role, Quality, and Capability of Women in the Economic Field

Article 15

Efforts to enhance the role, quality, and capability of women in the economic field as referred to in Article 5 paragraph (1) letter d number 4 include:
a. implementing programs, activities, and training to improve women's skills and expertise in order to realize improved welfare for women;
b. facilitating the strengthening of business capital for economically disadvantaged women;
c. marketing the products of business activities of economically disadvantaged women;
d. training to develop women's capabilities through the economic empowerment of pre-prosperous families;
e. comprehensive entrepreneurship training for women.

CHAPTER III
PROTECTION OF WOMEN

Part One
General

Article 16

(1) In efforts to Protect women, the Regional Government is responsible for:
a. establishing and implementing policies, programs, and activities for the Protection of women;
b. undertaking efforts to prevent acts of Violence against women, including the criminal act of trafficking in persons;
c. improving the effectiveness of services for women victims of Violence;
d. enhancing efforts to Protect female workers;
e. strengthening and developing institutions providing services for the Protection of women;
f. cooperating with the (national) Government, other Regional Governments, the private sector, the business world, and/or NGOs in efforts to prevent acts of Violence against women;
g. involving the active role of family, ninik mamak (clan elders), bundo kanduang (senior female clan figures), traditional leaders, religious leaders, the community, and institutions in the field of Protection of women.

(2) The Regional Government's responsibility in efforts to Protect women as referred to in paragraph (1) shall be carried out by the SKPD in accordance with its duties and functions and may coordinate with related agencies.

Part Two
Prevention of Acts of Violence Against Women

Article 17

In order to prevent the occurrence of acts of Violence against women, the Regional Government shall:
a. conduct socialization concerning the prevention of Violence against women by optimizing the role of customary, cultural, religious, and community organization institutions;
b. build networks and cooperation with law enforcement officials, government officials, higher education institutions, and various non-governmental organizations engaged in and/or concerned with women and children who are victims of Violence;
c. provide public facilities that are comfortable and safe for women.

Part Three
Effectiveness of Services for Women Victims of Acts of Violence

Sub-Part 1
General

Article 18

(1) Protection of women victims of acts of Violence shall be implemented through integrated services.
(2) The provision of integrated services to women victims of acts of Violence as referred to in paragraph (1) shall apply the principles of:
a. gender responsiveness;
b. non-discrimination;
c. equal and respectful relationships;
d. maintaining confidentiality;
e. providing a sense of security and comfort;
f. respecting individual differences;
g. non-judgment;
h. respecting the victim's choices and decisions;
i. sensitivity to the victim's background and condition, and use of language appropriate and understandable to the victim;
j. swiftness and simplicity;
k. empathy; and
l. fulfillment of women's rights.

Article 19

Forms of integrated services for women victims of acts of Violence include:
a. complaint handling;
b. health rehabilitation;
c. social rehabilitation;
d. law enforcement and legal aid; and
e. repatriation and social reintegration.

Sub-Part 2
Complaint Handling

Article 20

Integrated complaint handling services as referred to in Article 19 letter a shall be carried out by:
a. hospitals;
b. community health centers (puskesmas);
c. women's and children's service units;
d. trauma center protection houses;
e. women's crisis centers;
f. the marriage counseling, guidance, and preservation advisory body;
g. the police;
h. the prosecutor's office;
i. the courts;
j. the Ministry of Religious Affairs; and/or
k. legal aid institutions.

Article 21

(1) Complaints of acts of Violence against women may be made by reporters consisting of:
a. the victim;
b. family;
c. the community; and/or
d. community organizations in the field of Protection of women and Children, such as P2TP2A (Integrated Service Center for the Empowerment of Women and Children), safe houses, etc.

(2) Reporters as referred to in paragraph (1) may file complaints:
a. directly;
b. by telephone; and/or
c. by letter.

(3) In addition to complaint handling submitted by reporters, officers at the service institutions referred to in Article 20 are also authorized to handle victims of acts of Violence against women obtained through other information.
(4) Further provisions concerning procedures for complaint handling services as referred to in paragraphs (1) and (2) shall be regulated by Regent Regulation.

Sub-Part 3
Health Rehabilitation

Article 22

(1) Health rehabilitation as referred to in Article 19 letter b constitutes an effort encompassing the aspects of:
a. promotive;
b. preventive;
c. curative; and
d. rehabilitative.

(2) Health rehabilitation as referred to in paragraph (1) shall be carried out comprehensively, be accessible 24 (twenty-four) hours a day, of high quality, and conducted in accordance with health service standards.
(3) Women victims of acts of Violence have the right to free health services, including:
a. medical services;
b. medico-legal services;
c. psychosocial counseling; and
d. post-mortem/medical examination (visum).
(4) Further provisions concerning the procedures for implementing health services as referred to in paragraph (3) shall be regulated by Regent Regulation.

Sub-Part 4
Social Rehabilitation

Article 23

(1) Social rehabilitation as referred to in Article 19 letter c is intended to restore and develop the capabilities of women victims of acts of Violence who experience social dysfunction so that they may carry out their social functions in society normally.
(2) Social rehabilitation as referred to in paragraph (1) includes the following services:
a. counseling;
b. provision of safe houses; and
c. spiritual guidance.
(3) The provision of services as referred to in paragraph (2) shall be organized by:
a. the SKPD with duties and functions in the social field;
b. the SKPD responsible for Women's Empowerment, which may cooperate with integrated service centers, women's crisis centers, or existing community organizations in the social field; or
c. the Ministry of Religious Affairs.

Article 24

(1) Counseling services as referred to in Article 23 paragraph (2) letter a aim to:
a. help women victims of acts of Violence identify their problems and find effective ways to overcome them on their own;
b. empower women victims of acts of Violence to determine their own future;
c. provide moral support to women victims of acts of Violence in facing the process they are undergoing; and
d. make women victims of acts of Violence feel accepted within the community.
(2) Counseling services shall be carried out by gender-responsive social rehabilitation officers, consisting of:
a. counselors;
b. psychologists; and
c. psychiatrists.
(3) Counseling services shall be conducted in a place that ensures a sense of security, comfort, and confidentiality of information for women victims of acts of Violence.
(4) Stages of counseling services include:
a. initial counseling;
b. follow-up counseling;
c. case discussion; and
d. site visits.

Article 25

(1) The Regional Government may provide safe houses for women victims of acts of Violence.
(2) The provision of safe house services as referred to in Article 23 paragraph (2) letter b aims to protect women victims of acts of Violence from intimidation by the perpetrator or other parties.
(3) The location of safe houses as referred to in paragraph (1) must be kept confidential.
(4) Criteria for women victims of acts of Violence requiring a safe house include:
a. their life is threatened;
b. they have been rejected by family or community;
c. they require intensive services but their residence is relatively far away; and/or
d. they would be neglected if not placed in a safe house.
(5) The provision of safe house services shall be carried out by social rehabilitation officers with gender sensitivity or gender awareness, consisting of:
a. social workers/volunteers;
b. security officers; and/or
c. community leaders.
(6) Further provisions concerning the provision of safe house services shall be regulated by Regent Regulation.

Article 26

(1) Spiritual guidance as referred to in Article 23 paragraph (2) letter c aims at psychological recovery.
(2) Spiritual guidance as referred to in paragraph (1) shall be implemented based on the principles of:
a. not imposing any particular religion or belief; and
b. leaving the final decision in the hands of the victim (self-determination).
(3) Spiritual guidance shall be carried out by spiritual guidance officers.
(4) Spiritual guidance officers as referred to in paragraph (3) consist of consultants from the Marriage Counseling, Guidance, and Preservation Advisory Body (BP4), Office of Religious Affairs officers, Islamic teachers/scholars (Ustad/Ulama), priests, pastors, and other religious leaders who have gender sensitivity or gender awareness.

Sub-Part 5
Law Enforcement and Legal Aid

Article 27

(1) Law enforcement and legal aid services as referred to in Article 19 letter d constitute actions by law enforcement officials to implement statutory regulations in handling cases of acts of Violence against women, particularly to impose sanctions on perpetrators and provide Protection for witnesses and/or victims.
(2) Law enforcement and legal aid services as referred to in paragraph (1) shall be carried out by prioritizing the best interests of the victim.

Article 28

(1) Law enforcement and legal aid services are intended to provide accompaniment and Protection to women victims of acts of Violence and witnesses with a gender-responsive approach so that victims obtain justice and legal certainty.
(2) Types of legal aid services include:
a. legal aid in the process of filing reports and preparing investigation reports;
b. legal aid in the prosecution process at the prosecutor's office;
c. legal aid in the examination process at court;
d. legal aid in the process from court ruling until a final and binding legal decision is rendered; and
e. legal aid in the mediation process.
(3) The Regional Government shall provide legal aid to women victims of acts of Violence in accordance with statutory regulations.
(4) In providing legal aid as referred to in paragraph (3), the Regional Government may cooperate with legal aid institutions, women's crisis centers, advocates, and/or other gender-sensitive legal companions.

Sub-Part 6
Repatriation and Social Reintegration

Article 29

(1) Repatriation is intended to return women victims of acts of Violence to their Region of origin.
(2) The repatriation of women victims of acts of Violence shall be accompanied by social reintegration efforts.

Article 30

(1) Social reintegration shall be carried out in order to reunite women victims of acts of Violence with their family, a substitute family, or the community that can provide Protection and fulfillment of the victim's needs.
(2) Social reintegration is intended so that:
a. women victims of acts of Violence may resume their social life; and
b. women victims of acts of Violence may have a decent life.
(3) Social reintegration as referred to in paragraph (1) shall be carried out after the victim has received social rehabilitation services.

Article 31

The Regional Government shall carry out the repatriation and social reintegration of women victims of acts of Violence in accordance with statutory regulations.

Part Four
Labor Protection

Article 32

(1) Employers employing women are obligated to maintain decency, safety, and security in the workplace.
(2) Employers are obligated to place female workers in locations that are safe for women's reproductive health.
(3) Employers are obligated to fulfill the rights of female workers to receive wages in accordance with statutory regulations.
(4) Employers are obligated to provide special breastfeeding facilities in their enterprises.

Article 33

(1) Female workers/laborers under 18 (eighteen) years of age are prohibited from being employed between 11:00 PM and 7:00 AM Western Indonesia Time.
(2) Employers are prohibited from employing pregnant female workers/laborers whom, according to a doctor's statement, would face danger to the health and safety of their pregnancy.
(3) Employers employing female workers/laborers between 11:00 PM and 7:00 AM Western Indonesia Time are obligated to:
a. provide nutritious food and drink; and
b. maintain decency and security in the workplace.
(4) Employers are obligated to provide shuttle transportation for female workers/laborers departing for and returning from work between 11:00 PM and 5:00 AM Western Indonesia Time.

CHAPTER V
IMPROVEMENT OF FAMILY QUALITY

Article 34

(1) In order to improve family quality, the Regional Government is responsible for:
a. undertaking efforts to improve family quality to achieve gender equality and children's rights;
b. providing services for families in achieving gender equality and children's rights;
c. strengthening and developing institutions providing family quality improvement services in the Region.
(2) The Regional Government's responsibility in efforts to improve family quality as referred to in paragraph (1) shall be carried out by the SKPD in accordance with its duties and functions.

Article 35

The Regional Government shall undertake efforts to improve family quality as referred to in Article 34 paragraph (1) letter a, including:
a. guidance;
b. counseling;
c. supervision;
d. facilitation of family development implementation;
e. improving the quality of health for pregnant and breastfeeding mothers.

Article 36

Guidance as referred to in Article 35 letter a includes:
a. growth and development of infants and toddlers;
b. protection of adolescent reproductive health;
c. maturation of marriage age;
d. birth regulation; and
e. population management.

Article 37

Counseling as referred to in Article 35 letter b includes:
a. conducting outreach to improve the quality of life of women;
b. conducting pre-marital counseling.

Article 38

Supervision as referred to in Article 35 letter c includes:
a. evaluating data on the growth and development of infants and toddlers;
b. evaluating post-marriage data.

Article 39

The provision of services for families as referred to in Article 34 paragraph (1) letter b includes:
a. communication, information, and education;
b. contraceptive tools and medicines;
c. recording and reporting of family planning services;
d. adequate health service facilities and infrastructure;
e. breastfeeding corner rooms in every public facility.

Article 40

Communication, information, and education as referred to in Article 39 letter a constitute communication activities to increase knowledge and improve the attitudes and behavior of families and communities in the population and family planning program.

Article 41

(1) In addition to the Regional Government, the private sector (the private sector including companies providing public services) is obligated to provide breastfeeding corner rooms.
(2) Breastfeeding corner rooms as referred to in paragraph (1) shall be proper and adequate rooms.
(3) Private parties that fail to provide breastfeeding corner rooms as referred to in paragraph (2) shall be subject to administrative sanctions in the form of:
a. written warning;
b. restriction of business activities;
c. suspension of business activities;
d. administrative fines; and/or
e. revocation of permits.

CHAPTER VI
FULFILLMENT OF CHILDREN'S RIGHTS

Part One
General

Article 42

(1) In efforts to fulfill children's rights, the Regional Government is responsible for:
a. establishing and implementing policies and programs/activities for the fulfillment of children's rights;
b. respecting and guaranteeing the basic rights of every Child without distinction as to ethnicity, religion, race, group, sex, ethnic group, culture and language, legal status of the Child, birth order of the Child, and physical and/or mental condition;
c. strengthening and developing institutions providing family quality improvement services in realizing children's rights;
d. guaranteeing the Protection, care, and welfare of Children with due regard to the rights and obligations of parents, guardians, or other persons legally responsible for the Child;
e. providing facilities and infrastructure in the implementation of the fulfillment of children's rights;
f. guaranteeing the Child's right to express opinions in accordance with the Child's age and level of intelligence;
g. conducting supervision and guidance in the implementation of the fulfillment of children's rights;
h. cooperating with the private sector and/or NGOs in the implementation of Child Protection.
(2) The Regional Government's responsibility in efforts to Protect Children as referred to in paragraph (1) shall be carried out by the SKPD in accordance with its duties and functions.

Part Two
Birth Registration

Article 43

(1) The Regional Government is obligated to carry out birth registration of Children through the issuance of birth certificates.
(2) Birth certificates as referred to in paragraph (1) shall be issued free of charge.

Part Three
Education

Article 44

The Regional Government shall guarantee the implementation of the 9 (nine)-year compulsory education program for Children aged 7 (seven) to 15 (fifteen) years.

Article 45

(1) Every education unit provider is obligated to provide Children the opportunity to obtain educational services without discrimination.
(2) Every education unit provider is prohibited from expelling a Child from an educational institution unless there is a guarantee of the continuity of the Child's education.
(3) In the event a Child victim of an act of Violence drops out of school, the education unit provider is obligated to coordinate with relevant agencies.

Article 46

(1) Every Child has the right to obtain education and instruction in the development of their personality and level of intelligence in accordance with their interests and talents.
(2) Every Child has the right to obtain Protection at the education unit from sexual crimes and Violence committed by educators, education personnel, fellow students, and/or other parties.

Article 47

(1) Children with disabilities and Children with special needs shall be given equal opportunity to obtain formal, non-formal, and informal educational services, including inclusive education programs.
(2) The Regional Government shall provide educators for inclusive education at every education unit.

Part Four
Health and Social Affairs

Article 48

(1) The Regional Government is obligated to provide comprehensive health services for Children so that every Child may achieve an optimal level of health from within the womb.
(2) Comprehensive health services as referred to in paragraph (1) include:
a. promotive, preventive, curative, and rehabilitative efforts for basic health services;
b. exemption from cost burden for malnourished Children, Children with disabilities, Children with special needs, Children infected with HIV/AIDS, child laborers, Children who are victims of abuse of narcotics, alcohol, psychotropic substances, and other addictive substances, Children who are victims of Violence, and Child victims of trafficking in persons; and
(3) The Regional Government, community, families, and parents are obligated to make efforts to ensure that newborn Children are protected from diseases that threaten survival and/or cause disability.

Article 49

(1) Promotive efforts in basic health services as referred to in Article 46 [sic] paragraph (2) letter a are intended to optimally improve the level of health.
(2) Promotive efforts as referred to in paragraph (1) shall be conducted by providing socialization or outreach to the community regarding healthy lifestyle patterns, including:
a. the importance of exclusive breastfeeding for infants;
b. the importance of immunization for toddlers;
c. clean living patterns;
d. the importance of nutritious food;
e. the dangers of drugs and smoking; and
(3) In addition to the promotive efforts referred to in paragraph (2), the Regional Government may conduct other activities in organizing health services.

Article 50

(1) Preventive efforts in basic health services as referred to in Article 46 paragraph (2) letter a are intended to prevent the occurrence of disease or health problems.
(2) Efforts as referred to in paragraph (1) shall be carried out by:
a. requiring mothers to provide exclusive breastfeeding to infants up to age 6 (six) months;
b. providing free immunization and vitamins to infants and toddlers;
c. providing nutritious food to toddlers from underprivileged families;
d. optimizing the role of integrated health service posts (posyandu) for health services for mothers and toddlers;
e. conducting periodic health examinations; and
f. providing sanitation facilities and clean water at every school.

Article 51

(1) Curative efforts in basic health services as referred to in Article 46 paragraph (2) letter a are intended to prevent disease from becoming more severe through treatment.
(2) Treatment as referred to in paragraph (1) shall be implemented free of charge for Children from underprivileged families.

Article 52

(1) Rehabilitative efforts in basic health services as referred to in Article 46 paragraph (2) letter a are intended to maintain and restore condition after illness.
(2) Rehabilitative efforts as referred to in paragraph (1) shall be carried out through periodic health examinations.

Article 53

(1) The Regional Government is obligated to provide Social Welfare for:
a. Children in conflict with the law;
b. Child victims of Violence who are economically and sexually exploited, neglected Children, Child victims of abduction, and Child victims of trafficking in persons;
c. Children without parents who are cared for by parties or families who are unable to provide for them;
d. Child victims of natural disasters or social disasters; and
e. Children with disabilities.
(2) Social welfare as referred to in paragraph (1) may take the form of provision of services in:
a. health;
b. education;
c. legal aid;
d. accompaniment;
e. social, mental, and spiritual guidance;
f. social rehabilitation; and/or
g. reintegration.

Part Five
Employment

Article 54

The Regional Government shall conduct periodic supervision of every person/entity that employs Children in efforts to Protect child workers.

Article 55

Every person/entity employing a Child must observe the following requirements:
a. minimum age of 15 (fifteen) years;
b. written consent obtained from the parents/guardian of the child worker;
c. a written employment agreement between the employer and the parents/guardian of the child worker, ratified by the competent authority;
d. not employing the Child at night;
e. working hours of no more than 3 (three) hours per day.

Article 56

In the event a Child is employed together with adult workers, the Child's workplace must be separated from the workplace of adult workers.

Article 57

(1) Every person/entity is prohibited from employing or involving Children in the worst forms of work.
(2) The worst forms of work referred to in paragraph (1) include:
a. all work in the form of slavery or similar practices;
b. all work that utilizes, provides, or offers Children for prostitution, the production of pornography, pornographic performances, or gambling;
c. all work that utilizes, provides, or involves Children in the production and trafficking of liquor, narcotics, psychotropic substances, and other addictive substances; and/or
d. all work that endangers the health, safety, or morals of the Child.

Part Six
Facilitation of the Child Justice System

Article 58

(1) Facilitation of the child justice system includes criminal and civil justice.
(2) Facilitation of the criminal justice system as referred to in paragraph (1) includes:
a. provision of a Child companion or volunteer during examination by investigators;
b. provision of legal aid and psychological services;
c. involving local police members/investigators in efforts to prevent criminal acts committed by Children or where Children are victims;
d. realizing a restorative justice process for Children in conflict with the law; and
e. rehabilitation and social reintegration services.
(3) The fulfillment of children's rights in the civil justice system as referred to in paragraph (1) includes the provision of consultation services and legal aid throughout the judicial process.
(4) In facilitating the child justice system as referred to in paragraphs (1) and (2), the competent SKPD is the SKPD responsible for the Protection of women and Children.

Part Seven
Children's Forum

Article 59

(1) In organizing the fulfillment of children's rights to express opinions and participate, the Regional Government shall facilitate the formation of a Children's Forum.
(2) The Children's Forum referred to in paragraph (1) constitutes a representation of Children in the Region, whether by geographic domicile, social-cultural group, or educational background of Children.
(3) In every formulation of policy related to Children, the Regional Government is obligated to take into account and accommodate the opinions of Children expressed through the Child Participation Forum.
(4) Further provisions concerning the formation of the Children's Forum referred to in paragraphs (1) through (3) shall be established by Decision of the Regent.

Part Eight
Child-Friendly Regency

Article 60

(1) In order to fulfill children's rights in an integrated, systematic, and sustainable manner, the Regional Government shall establish a KLA Development policy.
(2) The KLA Development policy shall contain:
a. the KLA concept concerning objectives, strategy, and the role of relevant parties;
b. children's rights; and
c. the KLA development approach.

Article 61

(1) The KLA development policy shall be directed toward the fulfillment of children's rights, divided into 5 (five) groups, including:
a. civil rights and freedoms;
b. family environment and alternative care;
c. basic health and welfare;
d. education, use of leisure time, and cultural activities; and
e. special Protection.
(2) Further provisions concerning the KLA development policy referred to in paragraph (1) shall be regulated by Regent Regulation.

Article 62

(1) In order to make the implementation of KLA policy in the Region effective, a KLA Task Force shall be formed.
(2) The formation and membership structure of the KLA Task Force shall be established by Decision of the Regent.
(3) The KLA Task Force shall be based at the Office of Women's Empowerment and Family Planning.
(4) The KLA Task Force referred to in paragraph (1) has the principal duties to:
a. coordinate the implementation of KLA policy and development;
b. carry out socialization, advocacy, and communication, information, and education on KLA policy;
c. collect baseline data for KLA development;
d. analyze needs based on baseline data;
e. disseminate baseline data;
f. determine the focus and priority of programs in realizing KLA, adjusted to regional potential;
g. draw up a 5 (five)-year Regional Action Plan for Child-Friendly Regency and working mechanisms; and
h. conduct monitoring, evaluation, and reporting at least once a year.

Article 63

In carrying out its duties, the KLA Task Force may coordinate and cooperate with PPT (Integrated Service) institutions and other institutions providing Child Protection services.

CHAPTER VII
CHILD PROTECTION

Article 64

The Regional Government, in providing Protection to Children, shall:
a. implement and support national policy in the organization of Child Protection in the Region;
b. guarantee that Children may exercise their right to express opinions in accordance with the Child's age and level of intelligence;
c. carry out the prevention of Violence against Children by optimizing the role of customary institutions;
d. conduct socialization, seminars, and outreach concerning the Protection and rights of Children;
e. develop a child-friendly school model;
f. provide public facilities that are child-friendly and safe;
g. encourage community concern for the importance of Protecting Children.

Article 65

Service efforts undertaken by the Regional Government for child victims of acts of Violence as referred to in Articles 42 through 64 shall apply mutatis mutandis to service efforts for Child victims of acts of Violence.

CHAPTER VIII
PARTICIPATION OF THE COMMUNITY, AND PARENTS AND/OR FAMILY

Part One
Community

Article 66

(1) Community participation in efforts to Protect women and Children includes:
a. participating in prevention and providing support for the development of programs to prevent violations of the rights of women and Children;
b. providing information and/or reporting the occurrence of violations of the rights of women and Children to the competent authority; and
c. monitoring the process of law enforcement in cases of violations of the rights of women and Children.
(2) Ninik mamak (clan elders), alim ulama (religious scholars), cadiak pandai (wise community figures), and bundo kanduang (senior female clan figures) are responsible for protecting and preventing violations of the rights of women and Children.
(3) Community participation as referred to in paragraphs (1) and (2) shall be based on statutory regulations and the local wisdom values of the local community.

Article 67

(1) In order to prevent violations of women's rights, the community shall:
a. monitor various conditions occurring in its environment that may give rise to violations of women's rights;
b. increase awareness of violations of women's rights;
c. strengthen the role of community organizations, religious organizations, and family environments;
d. supervise the implementation of Women's Empowerment and Child Protection organized by the Regional Government through mechanisms for conveying aspirations to the Regent or the regional people's representative council.
(2) In order to prevent violations of children's rights, the community shall:
a. monitor various conditions occurring in its environment that give rise to violations of children's rights;
b. increase awareness of violations of children's rights;
c. strengthen the role of community organizations, religious organizations, and family environments; and
d. prevent the occurrence of early-age marriage.

Part Two
Parents and/or Family

Article 68

The family, in efforts to Protect women's rights, plays a role in:
a. protecting women from conflict and domestic Violence that degrades women's dignity;
b. providing equal opportunity for women to develop themselves in the fields of education, social affairs, economy, politics, and culture; and
c. preventing violations of women's rights within the household.

Article 69

(1) Parents, in efforts to Protect children's rights, play a role in:
a. raising, caring for, educating, and protecting the Child;
b. fostering the Child's development in accordance with their ability, talent, and interest;
c. providing formal and non-formal education;
d. providing character education and instilling moral values in the Child;
e. guiding the Child to behave in accordance with religion, ethics, morals, and customs; and
f. preventing the occurrence of early-age marriage.
(2) In the event a parent is absent, or their whereabouts are unknown, or for some other reason they are unable to fulfill their obligations and responsibilities, the obligations and responsibilities referred to in paragraph (1) may transfer to the family.

CHAPTER IX
GUIDANCE AND SUPERVISION

Article 70

(1) The Regional Government is obligated to conduct guidance and supervision of the implementation of Protection for women and Children who are victims of Violence.
(2) Guidance as referred to in paragraph (1) includes:
a. guidelines and fulfillment standards;
b. technical guidance and training;
c. provision of facilities;
d. monitoring; and
e. evaluation.
(3) Guidance as referred to in paragraph (2) is intended to realize the objectives of Protection for women and Children who are victims of Violence in accordance with minimum service standards.

Article 71

(1) Supervision of the implementation of Child Protection as regulated in this Regional Regulation shall be carried out by the Regional Government Supervisory Agency of Padang Pariaman Regency and P2TP2A.
(2) In conducting supervision, P2TP2A is authorized to recommend cases to law enforcement officials, government agencies, and other community institutions as regulated under the Law.
(3) Recommendations submitted by P2TP2A must be followed up by law enforcement officials, government agencies, community institutions, and other relevant individuals.

CHAPTER X
FINANCING

Article 72

Financing for the implementation of programs and activities for Women's Empowerment and Child Protection shall originate from:
a. the regional revenue and expenditure budget; and
b. other legitimate sources in accordance with statutory regulations.

CHAPTER XI
CLOSING PROVISIONS

Article 73

Implementing regulations for this Regional Regulation shall be established no later than 6 (six) months from the date this Regional Regulation is promulgated.

Article 74

This Regional Regulation shall come into force on the date of its promulgation. To ensure that every person is aware of it, the promulgation of this Regional Regulation shall be ordered by placing it in the Regional Gazette of Padang Pariaman Regency.
