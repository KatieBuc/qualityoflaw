REGENT OF NORTH CENTRAL TIMOR
NUSA TENGGARA TIMUR PROVINCE

REGIONAL REGULATION OF NORTH CENTRAL TIMOR REGENCY
NUMBER 3 OF 2020

CONCERNING
THE IMPLEMENTATION OF WOMEN'S EMPOWERMENT

BY THE GRACE OF THE ONE ALMIGHTY GOD

REGENT OF NORTH CENTRAL TIMOR,

Considering:
a. that every person has the same dignity and worth to live in society, nation, and state, and has the right to receive protection of human rights and basic human freedoms without discrimination as an effort to realize gender equality and justice in national and regional development;
b. that in order to realize gender equality and justice in development in North Central Timor Regency, it is necessary to increase the effectiveness and optimization of the implementation of the women's empowerment sub-affairs as an inseparable part of the functional activities of government and non-government institutions to improve the position, role, and quality of women in development in North Central Timor Regency;
c. that to ensure legal certainty and to fill the legal void in order to realize gender equality and justice in development in North Central Timor Regency, it is necessary to have regulations in a Regional Regulation concerning the implementation of women's empowerment;
d. that based on the considerations as intended in letters a, b, and c, it is necessary to establish a Regional Regulation concerning the Implementation of Women's Empowerment;

Bearing in mind:

1. Article 18 paragraph (6) of the 1945 Constitution of the Republic of Indonesia;
2. Law Number 69 of 1958 concerning the Formation of Level II Regions within the Territory of Level I Regions of Bali, West Nusa Tenggara, and East Nusa Tenggara (State Gazette of the Republic of Indonesia of 1958 Number 122, Supplement to the State Gazette of the Republic of Indonesia Number 1655);
3. Law Number 23 of 2014 concerning Regional Government (State Gazette of the Republic of Indonesia of 2014 Number 244, Supplement to the State Gazette of the Republic of Indonesia Number 5587), as last amended several times by Law Number 9 of 2015 concerning the Second Amendment to Law Number 23 of 2014 concerning Regional Government (State Gazette of the Republic of Indonesia of 2015 Number 58, Supplement to the State Gazette of the Republic of Indonesia Number 5679);

With the Joint Approval of:
REGIONAL PEOPLE'S REPRESENTATIVE COUNCIL OF NORTH CENTRAL TIMOR REGENCY and
REGENT OF NORTH CENTRAL TIMOR

HAS DECIDED:

To establish: REGIONAL REGULATION CONCERNING THE IMPLEMENTATION OF WOMEN'S EMPOWERMENT.

CHAPTER I
GENERAL PROVISIONS

Article 1
In this Regional Regulation, what is meant by:

1. Region is North Central Timor Regency.
2. Regional Government is the Government of North Central Timor Regency.
3. Regent is the Regent of North Central Timor.
4. Regional People's Representative Council, hereinafter abbreviated as DPRD, is the Regional People's Representative Council of North Central Timor Regency.
5. Regional Secretary is the Regional Secretary of North Central Timor Regency.
6. Office (Dinas) is the Regional Apparatus that carries out government affairs in the field of women's empowerment and child protection.
7. Head of Office is the Head of the Regional Apparatus that carries out government affairs in the field of women's empowerment.
8. Regional Apparatus, hereinafter abbreviated as PD, is the Regional Apparatus in North Central Timor Regency.
9. Head of Regional Apparatus is the Head of the Regional Apparatus in North Central Timor Regency.
10. Agency (Badan) is the Regional Apparatus that carries out government affairs in the field of regional development planning and research.
11. Head of Agency is the Head of the Regional Apparatus that carries out government affairs in the field of planning.
12. Women's Empowerment is any effort to improve physical, mental, spiritual, social, knowledge, and skill capabilities so that women are ready to be utilized according to their respective abilities.
13. Gender Mainstreaming, hereinafter abbreviated as PUG, is a strategy built to integrate gender into an integral dimension of planning, implementation, monitoring, and evaluation of regional development policies and programs.
14. Gender is a concept that refers to the roles and responsibilities of men and women that occur as a result of societal formation and can change due to the social and cultural circumstances of society.
15. Gender Equality is the equality of conditions for men and women to obtain opportunities and their rights as human beings so that they are able to play a role and participate in the development process.
16. Gender Justice is a process to be fair to men and women.
17. Gender Mainstreaming Working Group, hereinafter referred to as PUG Working Group (Pokja PUG), is a consultation forum for executors and drivers of gender mainstreaming from various agencies/institutions in the region.
18. Gender Mainstreaming Focal Point, hereinafter referred to as PUG Focal Point, is a PD apparatus that has the ability to carry out gender mainstreaming in their respective work units.
19. Regional Medium-Term Development Plan, hereinafter abbreviated as RPJMD, is a translation of the Regent's vision, mission, and programs whose preparation is guided by the Regional Long-Term Development Plan, which contains regional development strategies, general regional policies, regional programs, and a macroeconomic framework that includes an overview of the economy as a whole, including the direction of fiscal policy in a work plan in the form of a regulatory framework and an indicative funding framework.
20. Gender Analysis is a process built systematically to identify and understand the division of labor or roles of men and women, access and control over development resources, participation in development, and the benefits they enjoy.
21. Gender Responsive Budget is a performance-based budget allocation for certain programs or activities whose input, output, and outcome indicators can ensure the fulfillment of the aspirations and needs of women and men fairly.
22. Communication, Information, and Education, hereinafter abbreviated as KIE, is the delivery of messages in the form of information, ideas, or facts related to gender mainstreaming that the community needs to know directly or indirectly through communication channels to message recipients to get an effect, as a process of behavior change towards gender responsiveness.
23. Sub-district/Village Development Planning Meeting, hereinafter referred to as Musrembangkel, is an annual sub-district/village development planning deliberation forum that involves development actors in the Sub-district/Village.
24. Sub-district Development Planning Meeting, hereinafter referred to as Musrembangcam, is a deliberation forum between development actors to discuss and agree on handling steps for priority program activities listed in the list of proposed sub-district development planning activities integrated with the development priorities of North Central Timor Regency in the sub-district area, coordinated by the Regional Development Planning Agency and carried out by the Sub-district Head.
25. Development planning deliberation for the preparation of the Regional Medium-Term Development Plan, hereinafter referred to as Musrembang RPJMD, is a deliberation forum between stakeholders to discuss, sharpen, and agree on the draft Regional Medium-Term Development Plan.
26. Strategic Plan, hereinafter referred to as Renstra, is a five-year planning document that contains the vision, mission, goals, strategies, policies, programs, and development activities in accordance with the duties and functions of the organization as a translation of the RPJMD.
27. Work Plan, hereinafter referred to as Renja, is a one-year planning document that contains development policies, programs, and activities planned directly by the Regional Apparatus or pursued by encouraging community participation as a translation of the Renstra.
28. Regional Government Work Plan, hereinafter abbreviated as RKPD, is a one-year planning document that contains development policies, programs, and activities planned directly by the Regional Government or pursued by encouraging community participation as a translation of the Renstra.
29. Regional Action Plan, hereinafter abbreviated as RAD, is a document for planning activities prepared by a technical team that will be implemented in a certain period/time.
30. Gender Analysis Pathway, hereinafter abbreviated as GAP, is a gender analysis tool that can be used to help planners perform gender mainstreaming in the planning of development policies/programs/activities with the aim of identifying gender gaps and gender issues, as well as preparing development policies/programs/activities intended to reduce or eliminate those gender gaps.
31. Technical Team is a Team formed by the PUG Working Group with the task of conducting gender-perspective budget analysis and compiling the PUG RAD.

CHAPTER II
SCOPE

Article 2
The scope of the implementation of Women's Empowerment includes:
a. quality of life for women;
b. protection of women;
c. quality of the family; and
d. gender data system.

CHAPTER III
QUALITY OF LIFE FOR WOMEN
Part One
General

Article 3
Quality of Life for Women includes:
a. institutionalization of PUG;
b. Women's Empowerment in the political, legal, social, and economic fields in community organizations; and
c. strengthening and development of institutions providing women's empowerment services.

Part Two
Institutionalization of PUG
Paragraph 1
General

Article 4
Institutionalization of PUG includes:
a. appointment of PUG coordinator;
b. PUG Working Group;
c. PUG Focal Point;
d. Budgeting;
e. gender-based development planning;
f. monitoring and evaluation;
g. reporting; and
h. supervision.

Paragraph 2
PUG Coordinator

Article 5
(1) The Regent has the authority to appoint the Head of the PD that carries out government affairs in the field of Women's Empowerment as the coordinator for the implementation of PUG.
(2) The appointment as intended in paragraph (1) is by Regent's Decree.

Paragraph 3
PUG Working Group

Article 6
(1) The Regent has the authority to form a PUG Working Group.
(2) The formation of the PUG Working Group as intended in paragraph (1) is determined by Regent's Decree.
(3) The Regent's Decree as intended in paragraph (2) is valid for 5 (five) years.
(4) The management of the PUG Working Group consists of:
a. chairperson by the head of the agency;
b. secretariat by the head of the office;
c. members include:

1. head of PD;
2. technical team consisting of:
a) apparatus who have attended training or education on gender-perspective budget analysis, a maximum of 5 (five) people.
b) representatives of higher education elements, a maximum of 5 (five) people;
c) press representatives, a maximum of 5 (five) people; and
d) representatives of non-governmental organizations that have an orientation scope in the field of gender, a maximum of 5 (five) people.

Article 7
The duties of the PUG Working Group include:
a. promoting and facilitating dialogue between work units within the regional government;
b. developing work networks in accordance with the main duties, functions, and authorities given by the leadership in an effort to realize gender equality and justice;
c. assigning a technical team to compile the PUG RAD in order to realize gender equality and justice adjusted to the periodization of the RPJMD;
d. translating and following up on policies and implementation programs so that they are gender-responsive as implied and stated in the RPJMD;
e. coordinating the formation of a PUG Focal Point in every PD or work unit;
f. promoting PUG in each PD;
g. strengthening capacity for PUG Focal Points in preparing gender-responsive budgets by the technical team;
h. overseeing the preparation of gender-responsive planning and budgeting in their respective PDs;
i. including gender indicators in the guidelines for preparing the APBD;
j. ensuring the gender-responsive budget is a consideration in the discussion and determination of the APBD with the DPRD;
k. holding a coordination meeting every 6 (six) months to ensure the implementation of PUG in the Region;
l. creating and submitting a report on the implementation of PUG to the regent every 6 (six) months; and
m. holding a coordination meeting before the preparation of the RKPD.

Paragraph 4
PUG Focal Point

Article 8
(1) The Regional Secretary has the authority to form a PUG Focal Point within the Regional Secretariat.
(2) The formation of the PUG Focal Point as intended in paragraph (1) is determined by the Regional Secretary's Decree.
(3) The management of the PUG Focal Point as intended in paragraph (1) consists of:
a. person in charge by the regional secretary;
b. coordinator by an echelon IIB official handling government affairs in the legal field;
c. deputy coordinator by an echelon IIIA official of each section at the regional secretariat except for the echelon IIB official as intended in letter b; and
d. members by representatives of officials and/or staff from all sections at the regional secretariat.

Article 9
(1) The Head of PD is required to form a PD PUG Focal Point.
(2) The formation of the PUG Focal Point as intended in paragraph (1) is by the Head of PD's Decree.
(3) The management of the PUG Focal Point as intended in paragraph (1) consists of:
a. person in charge by the head of PD;
b. coordinator by an echelon IIIA official handling planning affairs at the PD;
c. deputy coordinator by an echelon IIIB official who has competence in PUG; and
d. members by representatives of officials or staff from fields and secretariats at the PD.

Article 10
(1) The Hospital Director has the authority to form a Hospital PUG Focal Point.
(2) The formation of the PUG Focal Point as intended in paragraph (1) is determined by the Hospital Director's decree.
(3) The management of the PUG Focal Point as intended in paragraph (1) consists of:
a. person in charge by the hospital director;
b. coordinator by an echelon IIIB official handling hospital planning;
c. deputy coordinator by an echelon IVA official who has competence in PUG; and
d. members by representatives of officials or staff in the fields or sections existing at the hospital.

Article 11
(1) The Head of the Community Health Center (Puskesmas) has the authority to form a Puskesmas PUG Focal Point.
(2) The formation of the PUG Focal Point as intended in paragraph (1) is determined by the Head of the Community Health Center's Decree.
(3) The management of the Puskesmas PUG Focal Point consists of:
a. person in charge by the Head of Puskesmas;
b. coordinator by an echelon IVB official handling Puskesmas planning; and
c. members consisting of Puskesmas staff.

Article 12
The duties of the PUG Focal Point include:
a. assisting work unit policy makers within the scope of their duties, main tasks, and functions of their agencies to take fully planned steps if there is a gender gap;
b. encouraging and assisting work units to evaluate and improve mandates, policies, programs, projects, activities, and budgets to be more gender-responsive;
c. facilitating the implementation of gender sensitivity training, gender analysis training, and developing gender work networks with other agencies or institutions or organizations and work units, both government and non-government;
d. striving for the implementation of gender analysis as one of the stages in every development process starting from the planning, implementation, monitoring, and evaluation stages;
e. translating and following up on policies and development implementation programs to be gender-responsive as implied in the PD Renstra, PD Renja, and RKPD;
f. participating in various activities organized by the PUG Working Group and/or the national PUG working group; and
g. creating periodic activity reports and reporting them to the regent through the chairperson of the PUG Working Group.

Paragraph 5
Gender-Based Regional Development Planning

Article 13
(1) The Regional Government is responsible for compiling planning for gender-perspective development policies, programs, and activities.
(2) The responsibility as intended in paragraph (1) is coordinated by the PD that carries out government affairs in the field of regional development planning, research, and development.
(3) The planning as intended in paragraph (1) includes the stages:
a. Sub-village development planning meeting (Musrenbangdus);
b. Village/Sub-district development planning meeting (Musrembangdes/kel);
c. Sub-district development planning meeting (Musrembangcam);
d. Regional level development planning meeting;
e. RPJMD preparation;
f. PD Renstra preparation;
g. PD Renja preparation; and
h. RKPD;
i. RAPBD preparation;
(4) The planning as intended in paragraph (1) is carried out through Gender analysis using the Gender Analysis Pathway method or other analysis methods.
(5) The implementation of Gender analysis on the RPJMD and PD Renstra can cooperate with Higher Education institutions or other parties that have capabilities in their fields.

Article 14
(1) The Agency is responsible for compiling gender-based development planning guidelines.
(2) The guidelines as intended in paragraph (1) must be used in facilitating:
a. Musrenbangdus;
b. Musrembangdes/kel;
c. Musrembangcam;
d. Regional level development planning meeting;
e. RPJMD preparation;
f. PD Renstra preparation; and
g. PD Renja preparation,
which are gender-responsive.
(3) The preparation of the guidelines as intended in paragraph (1) can cooperate with the PUG Working Group Technical Team.

Article 15
(1) The Gender Analysis Pathway method as intended in Article 13 paragraph (4) is used as a way to make plans in each PD.
(2) The method as intended in paragraph (1) includes:
a. stage I gender-responsive policy analysis;
b. stage II formulation of gender-responsive policy;
c. stage III gender-responsive action plan;
d. stage IV implementation of activities that have been compiled; and
e. stage V monitoring and evaluation of each stage and step taken.

Paragraph 6
Budgeting

Article 16
(1) The Regional Government is required to allocate a budget for the implementation of PUG in the Region.
(2) The implementation budget as intended in paragraph (1) is sourced from:
a. APBD; and
b. assistance from other non-binding parties, as long as it is in accordance with the provisions of the applicable laws and regulations.
(3) The budget as intended in paragraph (1) and paragraph (2) is used to finance PUG Working Group and PD PUG Focal Point activities in integrating Gender at the level of planning, preparation, implementation, monitoring, and evaluation of policies and development in the Region.

Paragraph 7
Monitoring and Evaluation

Article 17
(1) The Regent has the authority to carry out monitoring and evaluation of the implementation of PUG.
(2) The Regent may delegate the implementation of monitoring and evaluation to the PUG Working Group.
(3) Monitoring and evaluation are carried out at each PD and/or other institutions that carry out PUG efforts.
(4) Monitoring and evaluation are carried out at least every 6 (six) months.

Article 18
Monitoring of PUG implementation is carried out through:
a. monitoring the preparation of gender-responsive development planning documents at both the PD level and the regional level;
b. monitoring the PUG RAD; and
c. verification of the PUG RAD implementation report.

Article 19
Evaluation of PUG implementation is carried out through:
a. evaluation of achievements and challenges in PUG RAD implementation;
b. preparation of the PUG RAD for the next period;
c. evaluation of PUG implementation achievements and challenges every 6 (six) months; and
d. preparation of the PUG implementation plan for the next 6 (six) months.

Paragraph 8
PUG Reporting

Article 20
(1) The PUG Working Group is responsible for compiling the PUG Implementation Report every 6 (six) months and the five-year PUG RAD Implementation Report.
(2) The reporting framework as intended in paragraph (1) is guided by the reporting framework as intended in laws and regulations concerning the General Guidelines for the Implementation of PUG in the Regions.
(3) The PUG Working Group report as intended in paragraph (1) is reported to the Regent.
(4) The Regent is responsible for examining and stating approval of the PUG Working Group report as intended in paragraph (1).
(5) The Regent is responsible for sending the PUG Working Group report as intended in paragraph (1) to the Minister carrying out Internal Government affairs.

Paragraph 9
Supervision

Article 21
(1) The Regent is responsible for carrying out supervision of the implementation of PUG.
(2) Supervision as intended in paragraph (1) is in the form of:
a. gender-responsive planning and budgeting carried out by the regional government budget team;
b. coordination implementation between the PUG Working Group and the gender Focal Point; and
c. progress of PUG RAD achievement every year.
(3) The responsibility as intended in paragraph (1) can technically and operationally be carried out by the Chairperson of the PUG Working Group and the Head of the PUG Working Group Secretariat.
(4) The implementation of the responsibility as intended in paragraph (3) is reported to the Regent.

Part Three
Women's Empowerment in the Political, Legal, Socio-Cultural, and Economic Fields in Community Organizations
Paragraph 1
General

Article 22
Efforts to Empower Women in the political, legal, socio-cultural, and economic fields in community organizations include the stages of:
a. planning;
b. implementation; and
c. reporting and evaluation.

Paragraph 2
Planning

Article 23
(1) The Agency is responsible for facilitating the planning of Women's Empowerment in the political, legal, socio-cultural, and economic fields in community organizations.
(2) The planning as intended in paragraph (1) is carried out in an integrated manner in the development planning deliberation based on the gender analysis pathway instrument.

Paragraph 3
Implementation

Article 24
The implementation of Women's Empowerment in community organizations is carried out by the PD in accordance with the main duties and functions carried out according to the regional financial capacity.

Article 25
(1) The PD that carries out government affairs in the field of national unity and politics is responsible for facilitating the empowerment of women in the political field.
(2) Empowerment as intended in paragraph (1) includes:
a. carrying out education and training for women in the context of improving competence, especially in the political field; and
b. encouraging the representation of women in every community organization management.

Article 26
(1) The PD that carries out government affairs in the field of law is responsible for facilitating Women's Empowerment in the legal field.
(2) The implementation of Women's Empowerment in the legal field includes:
a. increasing awareness and knowledge in the legal field through communication, information, and education services; and
b. facilitating access and legal consultation services.

Article 27
(1) The PD that carries out government affairs in the field of social affairs is responsible for facilitating Women's Empowerment in the socio-cultural field.
(2) Women's Empowerment in the socio-cultural field through:
a. increasing knowledge, attitudes, and skills to encourage the fulfillment of education in stages according to potential to improve social status;
b. increasing awareness, willingness, and ability to overcome health problems through quality promotive, preventive, curative, and rehabilitative efforts, especially in the field of reproductive health;
c. increasing awareness and knowledge about independent, healthy, and prosperous family planning, including access to consultation and marriage registration services; and
d. facilitation and efforts to preserve customs and develop science, technology, art, and culture for the advancement of women.

Article 28
(1) The PD that carries out government affairs in the field of cooperatives, small and medium enterprises, and trade industry is responsible for facilitating Women's Empowerment in the economic field.
(2) The implementation of Women's Empowerment in the economic field is carried out through:
a. provision of skills and job training;
b. facilitation of the formation of productive economic business groups;
c. facilitation of strengthening and development of productive economic business groups;
d. facilitation and capital assistance; and
e. facilitation of marketing network development.

Paragraph 4
Reporting and Evaluation

Article 29
(1) Every PD is responsible for making a report on the implementation of Women's Empowerment activities that have been carried out to the Regent through the Office.
(2) The Office, after receiving the report submitted by the PD, evaluates the implementation of the activities.
(3) The results of the evaluation of the implementation of activities as intended in paragraph (2) are reported to the Regent.
(4) The evaluation results as intended in paragraph (3) are used for budget planning for the following year.

CHAPTER IV
PROTECTION OF WOMEN
Part One
General

Article 30
Protection of Women includes:
a. strengthening and development of institutions providing women's protection services;
b. prevention of violence against women; and
c. strengthening and development of institutions providing protection services for women victims of violence.

Part Two
Strengthening and Development of Institutions Providing Women's Protection Services
Paragraph 1
Regional Technical Implementation Unit for Women's and Child Protection

Article 31
(1) The PD that carries out government affairs in the field of institutional and organizational affairs is responsible for facilitating the formation of a regional technical implementation unit for women's and child protection.
(2) The formation of the UPTD P2TP2A as intended in paragraph (1) is regulated by a Regent's Regulation.
(3) The formation of the UPTD P2TP2A as intended in paragraph (2) is at the latest 2 (two) years after this Regional Regulation is established.

Paragraph 2
Regional Action Plan

Article 32
(1) The compilation of the RAD as intended in Article 7 letter c is carried out through the mechanism:
a. determination of the compilation team;
b. holding meetings to form working groups based on indicators;
c. each Working Group identifies goals, targets, and indicators;
d. determining priority programs and activities related to the achievement of each target; and
e. determining the budget allocation for each program activity.
(2) The RAD contains at least:
a. introduction;
b. direction of policies and achievement strategies;
c. monitoring and evaluation;
d. closing; and
e. attachment containing the RAD implementation matrix.
(3) The compilation of the RAD is carried out by paying attention to:
a. RPJMN;
b. RPJMD; and
c. other related documents.
(4) Provisions regarding the implementation of the RAD are regulated further by a Regent's Regulation.

Paragraph 3
Monitoring and Evaluation

Article 33
(1) The Regent carries out monitoring of the implementation of policies, programs, and activities for the protection of women.
(2) Monitoring as intended in paragraph (1) is intended to determine the progress and obstacles in the implementation of policies, programs, and activities for the protection of women in the Region.
(3) Monitoring as intended in paragraph (2) is carried out periodically on the PD through the Office.
(4) Monitoring is carried out starting from planning to the implementation of policies, programs, and activities for the protection of women for the current year.

Article 34
(1) Evaluation of the implementation of policies, programs, and activities for the Protection of Women is carried out at the end of each fiscal year.
(2) The results of the evaluation of the implementation of policies, programs, and activities for the Protection of women are used as input for the preparation of policies, programs, and activities for the Protection of Women for the following year.
(3) Evaluation as intended in paragraph (1) is carried out in accordance with the provisions of laws and regulations.

Part Three
Prevention of Violence Against Women

Article 35
(1) The Office is responsible for carrying out efforts to prevent violence against Women.
(2) Prevention as intended in paragraph (1) is carried out in the form of:
a. conducting counseling and socialization regarding matters related to violence against women;
b. conducting socialization on women's rights;
c. striving to increase education for women;
d. providing counseling to immediately report to local authorities if violence against women occurs;
e. empowering social community organizations and women's organizations to increase women's knowledge and insight; and
f. forming work networks in the context of efforts to prevent violence against women.

Part Four
Strengthening and Development of Institutions Providing Protection Services for Women Victims of Violence

Article 36
(1) The Office may provide assistance to Service Institutions located in the region in the form of:
a. strengthening Service Institutions; and
b. development of Service Institutions.
(2) Strengthening for service institutions as intended in paragraph (1) letter a is carried out through:
a. increasing the competence of members of the service institution; and/or
b. facilitating paralegal training periodically.
(3) Development of Service Institutions as intended in paragraph (1) letter b is carried out through:
a. providing additional facilities and infrastructure;
b. providing information technology-based service approaches; and/or
c. recruiting village cadres as assistant paralegals.

CHAPTER V
QUALITY OF THE FAMILY
Part One
General

Article 37
Quality of the Family includes:
a. improving the quality of the family in realizing gender equality; and
b. strengthening and development of institutions providing services to improve family quality in realizing gender equality.

Part Two
Improving the Quality of the Family in Realizing Gender Equality

Article 38
(1) The Office is responsible for facilitating activities to improve family quality in the Region.
(2) Improving family quality as intended in paragraph (1) is carried out through:
a. facilitating the formation of service provider institutions;
b. in coordination with the PD and/or related institutions that have main duties and functions to carry out activities:

1. improving entrepreneurial ability for women; or
2. developing job training for women.

Part Three
Strengthening and Development of Institutions Providing Services to Improve Family Quality in Realizing Gender Equality

Article 39
(1) Facilitation of the formation of service provider institutions as intended in Article 36 paragraph (2) letter a is carried out by forming a family learning center.
(2) The stages of forming a family learning center include:
a. preparing regulations;
b. preparing human resources;
c. preparing facilities and infrastructure; and
d. formation of a family learning center.
(3) The family learning center functions:
a. as a service institution in prevention and counseling;
b. as a referral liaison place as a solution for child and family problems;
c. strengthening the ability of the family to care for and protect children;
d. as a place for family learning through education for parents; and
e. as a one-stop holistic integrative family service based on child rights.

CHAPTER VI
GENDER DATA SYSTEM
Part One
General

Article 40
Gender Data System includes:
a. data collection;
b. data processing; and
c. analysis and presentation of gender data.

Part Two
Data Collection

Article 41
(1) The PD that carries out the duties of the PUG focal point is responsible for reporting Gender data and PUG activities carried out to the Regent through the Office.
(2) The report on Gender data and PUG activities as intended in paragraph (1) is carried out every 6 (six) months.

Part Three
Data Processing

Article 42
(1) The Office, after receiving the report on Gender data and PUG activities from every Gender focal point in the region, processes the data.
(2) Data processing as intended in paragraph (1) is carried out to disaggregate Gender data and PUG activities with grouping that is good and easy to analyze.

Part Four
Analysis and Presentation of Gender Data

Article 43
(1) The Office conducts analysis of the processed data.
(2) The analysis as intended in paragraph (1) is carried out comprehensively based on adequate mapping so that it is easy to understand.
(3) The adequate analysis as intended in paragraph (2) is presented into regional Gender data manually and/or electronically.

CHAPTER VII
SANCTION PROVISIONS

Article 44
PDs that do not form a PUG Focal Point as intended in Article 8, Article 9, Article 10, and Article 11 are subject to administrative sanctions in the form of:
a. oral reprimand;
b. written reprimand; or
c. statement of dissatisfaction.

CHAPTER VIII
TRANSITIONAL PROVISIONS

Article 45
At the time this Regional Regulation comes into effect, existing institutions providing women's protection services shall continue to carry out their duties until the formation of the regional technical implementation unit for women's and child protection in accordance with the provisions of this Regional Regulation.

CHAPTER IX
CLOSING PROVISIONS

Article 46
This Regional Regulation comes into effect on the date of its promulgation.
In order for everyone to know it, ordering the promulgation of this Regional Regulation by placing it in the Regional Gazette of North Central Timor Regency.
