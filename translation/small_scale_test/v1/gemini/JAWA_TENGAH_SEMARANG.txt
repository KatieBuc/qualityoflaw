REGIONAL REGULATION OF SEMARANG REGENCY
NUMBER 1 OF 2015
CONCERNING
EMPOWERMENT AND PROTECTION OF WOMEN

BY THE GRACE OF THE ONE ALMIGHTY GOD
REGENT OF SEMARANG,

Considering:
a. that the empowerment of women is carried out so that women can actualize their potential optimally to participate in development in accordance with their capacity;
b. that women as a nation's asset who play a role in the process of continuing and creating a quality generation need to receive guarantees for the fulfillment of their rights and protection from acts of violence and discrimination in the context of building society, nation, and state;
c. that based on Article 12 paragraph (2) letter b of Law Number 23 of 2014 concerning Regional Government, it is stated in essence that the empowerment of women is a mandatory affair that must be carried out by the regional government;
d. that based on Law Number 23 of 2014 concerning Regional Government, the Appendix states that the division of government affairs in the field of women's empowerment with one of the sub-affairs is the protection of women;
e. that based on the considerations as referred to in letter a, letter b, letter c, and letter d, it is necessary to establish a Regional Regulation concerning the Empowerment and Protection of Women;

To Establish: REGIONAL REGULATION CONCERNING THE EMPOWERMENT AND PROTECTION OF WOMEN

CHAPTER I
GENERAL PROVISIONS
Article 1
In this Regional Regulation, what is meant by:

1. The Region is Semarang Regency.
2. Regional Government is the administration of government affairs by the Regional Government and the Regional People's Representative Council according to the principle of autonomy and co-administration tasks with the principle of maximum autonomy within the system and principle of the Unitary State of the Republic of Indonesia as referred to in the 1945 Constitution of the Republic of Indonesia.
3. Regional Government is the Regent and regional apparatus as elements of the Regional Government administration.
4. The Regional People's Representative Council of Semarang Regency, hereinafter abbreviated as DPRD, is a regional people's representative institution as an element of the regional government administration.
5. The Regent is the Regent of Semarang.
6. Regional Apparatus Working Unit in charge, hereinafter referred to as the SKPD in charge, is a Regional Apparatus Working Unit that has main duties and functions in accordance with the related field.
7. Human Rights are a set of rights inherent in the nature and existence of humans as creatures of the One Almighty God and are His gifts that must be respected, upheld, and protected by the state, law, Government, and every person for the sake of honor and the protection of human dignity.
8. Women's Empowerment is every effort to improve physical, mental, spiritual, social, knowledge, and skill abilities so that women are ready to be utilized in accordance with their respective abilities.
9. Women's Protection is all efforts aimed at protecting women and providing a sense of security in the fulfillment of their rights by providing consistent and systematic attention aimed at achieving gender equality.
10. Discrimination Against Women is any distinction, exclusion, or restriction made on the basis of sex which has the effect or purpose of impairing or nullifying the recognition, enjoyment, or exercise by women, irrespective of their marital status, on a basis of equality of men and women, of human rights and fundamental freedoms in the political, economic, social, cultural, civil, or any other field.
11. Gender is a concept that refers to the distinction of roles and responsibilities of men and women that occurs as a result of and can be changed by the social and cultural conditions of society.
12. Gender Equality is the same condition for men and women to obtain their opportunities and rights as human beings so that they can play a role and participate in political, economic, social, cultural, defense, and security activities, and equality in enjoying the results of development.
13. Community is individuals, families, groups, and social organizations and/or community organizations.
14. Family is the smallest unit in society consisting of husband and wife, husband-wife and their children, or father and his children, or mother and her children.
15. Workforce is every person who is able to do work to produce goods and/or services, whether to meet their own needs or for the community.
16. Worker/Laborer is every person who works by receiving wages or other forms of remuneration.

CHAPTER II
SCOPE
Article 2
The scope of this Regional Regulation includes:
a. principles and objectives;
b. women's rights;
c. obligations and responsibilities of the regional government;
d. women's empowerment;
e. protection of women;
f. implementation of women's protection;
g. strategies for women's empowerment and protection;
h. mechanisms for the implementation of women's empowerment and protection;
i. financing;
j. guidance and supervision;
k. transitional provisions; and
l. closing provisions.

CHAPTER III
PRINCIPLES AND OBJECTIVES
Article 3
Empowerment and Protection of Women are carried out based on the principles of:
a. respect for human rights;
b. justice and gender equality;
c. non-discrimination; and
d. the best interests of women.

Article 4
The objective of this Regional Regulation is to:
a. empower women both individually and in groups and the wider community so that they are able to live more independently; and
b. prevent and handle the risk of violence and vulnerability of women so that their survival can be fulfilled in accordance with the principles of equality and justice.

CHAPTER IV
WOMEN'S RIGHTS
Article 5
(1) Every woman has the right to:
a. live and maintain life as well as improve her standard of living;
b. meet her basic needs to grow and develop properly, have a family, and continue her lineage;
c. develop her personality, to obtain education, and improve her quality of life so as to become a human being who is faithful, devoted, responsible, of noble character, happy, and prosperous in accordance with human rights;
d. obtain justice, a sense of security, and freedom of expression without discrimination;
e. be involved in every stage of the development process;
f. be free from slavery or servitude and threats;
g. obtain protection of her personal self, family, honor, dignity, and property rights;
h. obtain welfare and a decent life;
i. participate in politics;
j. perform legal actions; and
k. be free to choose a partner in her marriage.
(2) Every wife, during the marriage bond, has the same rights and responsibilities as her husband regarding all matters concerning her married life, relationships with her children, and ownership and management of joint assets in accordance with the prevailing laws and regulations.
(3) After the termination of a marriage, a woman has rights and responsibilities with her ex-husband regarding all matters concerning children, taking into account the best interests of the child.

CHAPTER V
OBLIGATIONS AND RESPONSIBILITIES OF THE REGIONAL GOVERNMENT
Article 6
(1) The Regional Government has the obligation and responsibility for the empowerment and protection of women.
(2) Efforts for the empowerment and protection of women as referred to in paragraph (1) include:
a. establishment of policies, programs, and activities;
b. establishment of implementation guidelines;
c. provision of services; and
d. coordination of policies, programs, and activities.
(3) Efforts for the empowerment and protection of women as referred to in paragraph (2) are carried out in an integrated manner in accordance with the prevailing laws and regulations.

CHAPTER VI
WOMEN'S EMPOWERMENT
Article 7
Women's empowerment is directed towards obtaining opportunities and rights as human beings so that they are able to play a role and participate in the fields of:
a. economy;
b. social and culture;
c. politics; and
d. law.

Article 8
Women's empowerment in the economic field as referred to in Article 7 letter a is carried out through:
a. provision of job skills and training;
b. facilitation of the formation of productive economic business groups;
c. facilitation of strengthening and development of productive economic business groups;
d. facilitation and capital assistance; and
e. facilitation of marketing network development.

Article 9
Women's empowerment in the social and cultural field as referred to in Article 7 letter b is carried out through:
a. improvement of knowledge, attitudes, and skills to encourage the fulfillment of education in stages according to potential to improve social status;
b. improvement of awareness, willingness, and ability to overcome health problems through quality promotive, preventive, curative, and rehabilitative efforts, especially in the field of reproductive health;
c. improvement of awareness and knowledge about independent, healthy, and prosperous family planning including access to consultation services and marriage registration; and
d. facilitation and efforts to preserve customs and develop science, technology, art, and culture for the advancement of women.

Article 10
(1) Women's empowerment in the political field as referred to in Article 7 letter c includes:
a. involvement of women in decision-making at various levels;
b. provision of opportunities for women to occupy public positions;
c. participation in general elections; and
d. self-development through organizations to associate, assemble, and express opinions.
(2) Women's empowerment in the political field as referred to in paragraph (1) is carried out in accordance with the prevailing laws and regulations.

Article 11
(1) Women's empowerment in the legal field as referred to in Article 7 letter d includes:
a. improvement of awareness and knowledge in the field of law through communication, information, and education services;
b. facilitation of access and legal consultation services.
(2) Women's empowerment in the legal field as referred to in paragraph (1) is carried out in accordance with the prevailing laws and regulations.

CHAPTER VII
PROTECTION OF WOMEN
Article 12
(1) Protection of women includes:
a. social protection;
b. economic protection;
c. legal protection.
(2) Social protection as referred to in paragraph (1) letter a is to ensure the survival of women in the form of social protection to ensure the fulfillment of decent living needs.
(3) Economic protection as referred to in paragraph (1) letter b is to prevent and handle the risk of vulnerability due to shocks and social vulnerability of a person, family, group, and/or community for the survival of women.
(4) Legal protection as referred to in paragraph (1) letter c is carried out in terms of providing protection to women who are witnesses and victims of acts of violence and discrimination.

CHAPTER VIII
IMPLEMENTATION OF WOMEN'S PROTECTION
Part One
General
Article 13
Implementation of women's protection is provided to:
a. female workers/laborers;
b. elderly women;
c. female persons with disabilities;
d. homeless women;
e. female domestic workers;
f. female heads of households;
g. female former inmates of correctional institutions;
h. female disaster victims;
i. female commercial sex workers; and
j. female victims of violence.

Part Two
Female Workers/Laborers
Article 14
(1) The implementation of protection for female workers/laborers as referred to in Article 13 letter a is to provide protection from acts of economic exploitation and cruel, inhumane treatment and to strive for decent recognition of work.
(2) Female workers/laborers as referred to in paragraph (1) receive protection for:
a. recognition of rights, wages, and decent working conditions;
b. health and social security;
c. access to information and legal consultation services;
d. opportunities to obtain knowledge and skills to improve status; and
e. witnesses and victims.

Part Three
Elderly Women
Article 15
(1) The implementation of protection for elderly women as referred to in Article 13 letter b is in the context of extending life expectancy so that they can carry out their social functions and play an active role appropriately in their social life.
(2) Elderly women as referred to in paragraph (1) receive rights to protection for:
a. basic health services to maintain and improve health status including gerontological/geriatric services;
b. use of facilities and accessibility of public facilities and infrastructure; and
c. government administration services.

Part Four
Female Persons with Disabilities
Article 16
(1) The implementation of protection for female persons with disabilities as referred to in Article 13 letter c is in the context of providing protection and prevention against forms of discrimination including freedom from cruel, inhumane, and degrading treatment, and freedom from exploitation.
(2) Female persons with disabilities as referred to in paragraph (1) receive rights to protection for:
a. assurance of continuity and personal self-development;
b. participation in the fields of education, health, work, politics, sports, art, and culture, as well as the utilization of information and communication technology;
c. accessibility to information, legal aid, mobility, social services; and
d. early intervention efforts including treatment and rehabilitation for the improvement of their functions and capacities.

Part Five
Homeless Women
Article 17
(1) The implementation of protection for homeless women as referred to in Article 13 letter d is in the context of providing protection and prevention against forms of violence caused by the absence of a place of shelter or permanent residence.
(2) Homeless women as referred to in paragraph (1) receive rights to protection for:
a. decent housing;
b. health and social security;
c. population administration registration;
d. opportunities to obtain skills to improve economic status; and
e. access to information and legal consultation services.

Part Six
Female Domestic Workers
Article 18
(1) The implementation of protection for female domestic workers as referred to in Article 13 letter e is in the context of providing protection and prevention against forms of discrimination, cruel, inhumane, and degrading treatment, and exploitation in employment relationships.
(2) Female domestic workers as referred to in paragraph (1) receive protection for:
a. recognition of rights, wages, and decent working conditions;
b. health and social security;
c. access to information and legal consultation services; and
d. opportunities to obtain knowledge and skills to improve economic status.

Part Seven
Female Heads of Households
Article 19
(1) The implementation of protection for female heads of households as referred to in Article 13 letter f is in the context of providing protection and prevention against forms of discrimination, negative stigma, and economic empowerment for female heads of households.
(2) Female heads of households as referred to in paragraph (1) receive rights to protection for:
a. discriminatory actions and efforts to weaken the function of women as economic supporters of the family;
b. strengthening of capabilities and recognition as head of household in their environment; and
c. accessibility to economic and financial institutions.

Part Eight
Female Former Inmates of Correctional Institutions
Article 20
(1) The implementation of protection for female former inmates of correctional institutions as referred to in Article 13 letter g is in the context of providing protection from obstacles to adjusting to life in society, so that they can carry out life normally.
(2) Female former inmates of correctional institutions as referred to in paragraph (1) receive protection for:
a. rehabilitation and social reintegration; and
b. opportunities to obtain knowledge and skills to improve economic status.

Part Nine
Female Disaster Victims
Article 21
(1) The implementation of protection for female disaster victims as referred to in Article 13 letter h is in the context of providing protection during and after a disaster.
(2) Female disaster victims as referred to in paragraph (1) receive protection for:
a. access to decent housing;
b. health services;
c. access to information and legal consultation services; and
d. post-disaster recovery.

Part Ten
Female Commercial Sex Workers
Article 22
(1) The implementation of protection for female commercial sex workers as referred to in Article 13 letter i is in the context of providing health protection and improving life skills as capital to improve social status.
(2) Protection for female commercial sex workers as referred to in paragraph (1) includes:
a. providing life skills;
b. facilitating reproductive health services; and
c. preventing human trafficking.

Part Eleven
Female Victims of Violence
Article 23
(1) The implementation of protection for female victims of violence as referred to in Article 13 letter j is in the context of providing legal protection and/or health recovery.
(2) Protection for female victims of violence as referred to in paragraph (1) includes:
a. legal aid;
b. assistance after the act of violence occurs;
c. rehabilitation and social reintegration; and
d. health services.

CHAPTER IX
STRATEGIES FOR WOMEN'S EMPOWERMENT AND PROTECTION
Article 24
(1) Empowerment and protection of women are carried out by integrating gender mainstreaming and gender-disaggregated data in an integrated manner based on the prevailing laws and regulations.
(2) In the empowerment and protection of women, an Integrated Working Group and Services are formed, which are determined by the Regent's Decree.
(3) Empowerment and protection of women are coordinated by the Agency handling Women's Empowerment.

CHAPTER X
MECHANISMS FOR THE IMPLEMENTATION OF WOMEN'S EMPOWERMENT AND PROTECTION
Article 25
The mechanism for the implementation of women's empowerment and protection is carried out through the following stages:
a. formulation of program and activity planning policies;
b. budgeting for programs and activities;
c. implementation of programs and activities; and
d. monitoring, evaluation, and reporting.

Article 26
(1) The formulation of program and activity planning policies as referred to in Article 25 letter a is carried out through:
a. mapping, collection, processing, and presentation of data;
b. coordination and synchronization of the preparation of program and activity planning with related SKPDs; and
c. determination of programs and activities in planning documents.
(2) The formulation of program and activity planning policies is further regulated in minimum service standards and standard operating procedures.
(3) The formulation of program and activity planning policies for the implementation of women's empowerment and protection is coordinated by the SKPD in charge of regional development planning.
(4) Budgeting for programs and activities as referred to in Article 25 letter b is carried out through general budget policies set forth in the APBD (Regional Budget).
(5) The implementation of programs and activities as referred to in Article 25 letter c is carried out through:
a. implementation of programs and activities contained in the SKPD budget documents; and
b. coordination and synchronization of the implementation of programs and activities with related SKPDs.
(6) The implementation of monitoring, evaluation, and reporting as referred to in Article 25 letter d is carried out through:
a. conducting periodic monitoring and evaluation of the implementation of programs and activities that have been carried out; and
b. making periodic reports.

CHAPTER XI
FINANCING
Article 27
All costs for the implementation of policies, program planning, and activities related to the Empowerment and Protection of Women are sourced from:
a. State Revenue and Expenditure Budget (APBN);
b. Regional Revenue and Expenditure Budget (APBD); and
c. Other legitimate and non-binding sources.

CHAPTER XII
GUIDANCE AND SUPERVISION
Article 28
(1) The Regent or the SKPD in charge of women's empowerment conducts guidance and supervision over the empowerment and protection of women.
(2) The forms of guidance as referred to in paragraph (1) are as follows:
a. providing socialization to the community and the business world regarding policies for women's empowerment and protection;
b. providing books, leaflets, and brochures regarding women's empowerment and protection, and other related issues, and disseminating them to the community;
c. facilitation of capacity building for institutions implementing women's empowerment and protection;
d. facilitation of women's participation forums and women's organizations;
e. facilitation of rehabilitation and reintegration services for female disaster victims and victims of violence;
f. coordinating the participation of the business world and other institutions, including international assistance for the empowerment and protection of women; and
g. giving awards to the community and the business world, both individually and institutionally, that have carried out efforts for women's empowerment and protection.
(3) The forms of supervision as referred to in paragraph (1) can be in the form of monitoring and evaluation activities on the empowerment and protection of women.

CHAPTER XIII
TRANSITIONAL PROVISIONS
Article 29
At the time this Regional Regulation comes into effect, all provisions of regional legislation related to the Implementation of Women's Empowerment and Protection that have existed so far remain in effect as long as they do not conflict with the provisions in this Regional Regulation.

CHAPTER XIV
CLOSING PROVISIONS
Article 30
This Regional Regulation comes into effect on the date of its enactment.
