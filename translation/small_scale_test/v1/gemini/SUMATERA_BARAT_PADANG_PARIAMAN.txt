REGENT OF PADANG PARIAMAN
WEST SUMATRA PROVINCE
REGIONAL REGULATION OF PADANG PARIAMAN REGENCY
NUMBER 6 OF 2017
REGARDING
WOMEN'S EMPOWERMENT AND CHILD PROTECTION

BY THE GRACE OF THE ONE ALMIGHTY GOD
THE REGENT OF PADANG PARIAMAN,

Considering: a. that to ensure Women's Empowerment and Child Protection in accordance with human dignity and to receive protection from violence, discrimination, and other violations, there is a need for legal certainty;
b. that to provide legal certainty in the implementation of Women's Empowerment and Child Protection and to carry out mandatory regional government affairs, it is necessary to have regulations in the form of a Regional Regulation;
c. that based on the considerations as referred to in letter a and letter b, it is necessary to establish a Regional Regulation on Women's Empowerment and Child Protection;

In view of: 1. Article 18 paragraph (6) of the 1945 Constitution of the Republic of Indonesia;
2. Law Number 12 of 1956 regarding the Establishment of Autonomous Regency Regions within the Central Sumatra Province Region (State Gazette of the Republic of Indonesia of 1956 Number 25);
3. Law Number 39 of 1999 regarding Human Rights (State Gazette of the Republic of Indonesia of 1999 Number 165, Supplement to the State Gazette Number 3886);
4. Law Number 23 of 2002 regarding Child Protection (State Gazette of the Republic of Indonesia of 2002 Number 109, Supplement to the State Gazette of the Republic of Indonesia Number 4235) as amended by Law Number 35 of 2014 regarding Amendments to Law Number 23 of 2002 regarding Child Protection (State Gazette of the Republic of Indonesia of 2014 Number 397, Supplement to the State Gazette of the Republic of Indonesia Number 5606);
5. Law Number 23 of 2014 regarding Regional Government (State Gazette of the Republic of Indonesia of 2014 Number 244, Supplement to the State Gazette of the Republic of Indonesia Number 5587) as amended several times, most recently by Law Number 9 of 2015 (State Gazette of the Republic of Indonesia of 2015 Number 58, Supplement to the State Gazette of the Republic of Indonesia Number 5679);
6. Regulation of the State Minister for Women's Empowerment and Child Protection Number 1 of 2010 regarding Minimum Service Standards for Integrated Services for Women and Children Victims of Violence;
7. Regulation of the State Minister for Women's Empowerment and Child Protection Number 11 of 2011 regarding the Policy on Developing Child-Friendly Regencies;

With the Joint Approval of
THE REGIONAL PEOPLE'S REPRESENTATIVE COUNCIL OF PADANG PARIAMAN REGENCY and
THE REGENT OF PADANG PARIAMAN
DECIDES:

To establish: REGIONAL REGULATION ON WOMEN'S EMPOWERMENT AND CHILD PROTECTION.

CHAPTER I
GENERAL PROVISIONS
Article 1
In this Regional Regulation, the following definitions shall apply:

1. Region is Padang Pariaman Regency.
2. Regional Government is the Government of Padang Pariaman Regency.
3. Regent is the Regent of Padang Pariaman Regency.
4. Regional Apparatus Work Unit, hereinafter abbreviated as SKPD, is a Regional Apparatus Work Unit within the Padang Pariaman Regency Government.
5. Women's Empowerment is an effort of protection, strengthening of human rights, improvement of quality of life, and increasing women's participation in development.
6. Child Protection is all activities to ensure and protect the Child and their rights so that they can live, grow, develop, and participate optimally in accordance with human dignity, and receive protection from violence and discrimination.
7. Child is someone who has not reached the age of 18 (eighteen) years, including a child still in the womb and unmarried.
8. Prevention is a direct effort made by the Regional Government, society, and parents and/or family to prevent the violation of women's and children's rights.
9. Violence is any act that results in misery or suffering, whether physical, sexual, psychological, and/or household neglect, including threats to commit such acts, coercion, or unlawful deprivation of liberty.
10. Child Forum is a forum for child participation established with the aim of facilitating children's involvement in decision-making processes and enjoying changes related to their lives, whether directly or indirectly, implemented with the approval and willingness of all children based on awareness and understanding, in accordance with age and level of maturity.
11. Child-Friendly Regency, hereinafter abbreviated as KLA, is a Regency that has a child rights-based development system through the integration of commitment and resources of the government, society, and the business world that is planned comprehensively and sustainably in policies, programs, and activities to ensure the fulfillment of child rights.
12. Gender Mainstreaming is a strategy built to integrate gender as an integral dimension of planning, drafting, implementation, monitoring, and evaluation of national development policy programs.
13. Society is individuals, families, groups, and social organizations and/or community organizations.
14. Women Victims of Violence are women who experience acts resulting in physical, sexual, or psychological misery or suffering, including threats of certain acts, coercion, or arbitrary deprivation of liberty, whether occurring in the public sphere or private life.
15. Children Victims of Violence are children who experience acts resulting in physical, mental, sexual, psychological misery or suffering, or neglect that threatens life, the body, and degrades human dignity.
16. Human trafficking is the act of recruiting, transporting, sheltering, sending, transferring, or receiving a person by threatening violence, using violence, kidnapping, confinement, forgery, fraud, abuse of power or vulnerable positions, debt bondage, or giving payment or benefits, thereby obtaining consent from the person who holds control over that other person, whether carried out within the country or between countries, for the purpose of exploitation or resulting in the person being exploited.
17. Complaint Handling is a series of actions taken by integrated service providers to follow up on reports of violence against women and children submitted by victims, families, or society.
18. Social Rehabilitation is a service aimed at restoring and developing the capabilities of a person experiencing social dysfunction so that they can carry out their social functions normally.
19. Law Enforcement is the action of state-authorized officials to carry out statutory regulations.
20. Legal Aid is legal services provided by legal companions and advocates to carry out the process of assisting witnesses and/or victims of violence against women and children that are gender-sensitive.
21. Repatriation is an effort to return women and children victims of violence from abroad to the point of disembarkation/entry point, or from the receiving region to the region of origin.
22. Social Reintegration is an effort to reunite victims with families, substitute families, or society that can provide protection and fulfill the needs of the victim.
23. Safe House is a temporary shelter aimed at providing protection and a sense of safety to victims of violence.
24. Shelter/Halfway House is a forum for the guidance and social service of street children that carries out social assistance/guidance activities, maintenance, care, education, and health, and skills guidance to ensure children do not engage in street activities so they can grow and develop normally.
25. Spiritual Guidance is an effort to restore mental health through the strengthening of the religion practiced by the victim.
26. Gender Mainstreaming is a strategy built to integrate gender as an integral dimension of planning, drafting, implementation, monitoring, and evaluation of development policies, programs, and activities in the Region.
27. Gender Responsive is consistent and systematic attention to the differences between women and men in society, accompanied by efforts to eliminate structural and cultural barriers in achieving gender equality.
28. Gender Sensitivity or Gender Awareness is the ability to identify power relation gaps between women and men in the family and in the community, the impact of gender-based division of labor on women and men, and that the experiences, problems, needs, interests, and aspirations of women and men are different.
29. Public Service Provider is every state institution, corporation, independent institution established by Law for public service activities, and other legal entities established solely for public service activities.
30. Community Organization is an organization established and formed by the community voluntarily based on similarity of aspirations, wishes, needs, interests, activities, and goals to participate in development for the achievement of the goals of the Unitary State of the Republic of Indonesia based on Pancasila.
31. Parents are biological father and/or mother, or stepfather and/or stepmother, or adoptive father and/or adoptive mother.
32. Family is the smallest unit in society consisting of husband and wife or husband, wife and children, or father and children, or mother and children, or blood relatives in a straight line up or down up to the third degree.
33. Customary Law/Traditions are rules of conduct that are permanent and passed down from one generation to another as a legacy, so their integration with the behavioral patterns of society is strong.

Article 2
The implementation of Women's Empowerment and Child Protection is based on the principles of:
a. the best interests of women and children;
b. gender justice and gender equality;
c. local wisdom;
d. law enforcement and legal certainty;
e. non-discrimination;
f. the right to life, survival, and development;
g. respect for the child's opinion; and
h. law enforcement and legal certainty.

Article 3
This Regional Regulation aims to:
a. guarantee the fulfillment of women's and children's rights in the Region;
b. serve as a guideline for the Region in planning, policies, and strategies for Women's Empowerment and Child Protection;
c. protect and prevent women and children from acts, decisions, and policies that violate the rights of women and children;
d. increase the role and value of local wisdom and the role of custom in Women's Empowerment and Child Protection.

Article 4
The scope of regulation of Women's Empowerment and Child Protection includes:
a. improvement of the quality of life of women;
b. protection of women;
c. gender and child data systems;
d. fulfillment of child rights;
e. child protection;
f. community participation; and
g. guidance and supervision.

CHAPTER II
IMPROVEMENT OF THE QUALITY OF LIFE OF WOMEN
Part One
General
Article 5
(1) In the context of improving the quality of life of women, the regional government is responsible for:
a. respecting and guaranteeing women's rights without discrimination in accordance with human dignity;
b. implementing Gender Mainstreaming in the Region;
c. establishing a Gender Mainstreaming working group (pokja) in the Region;
d. increasing Women's Empowerment in roles, quality, and ability in the fields of:

1. politics;
2. law;
3. social; and
4. economy.
e. strengthening and developing institutions providing Women's Empowerment services in the Region;
(2) The responsibility of the Regional Government in efforts to improve the quality of life of women as referred to in paragraph (1) is carried out by the SKPD in accordance with their duties and functions.

Part Two
Implementation of Gender Mainstreaming in the Region
Article 6
(1) The Regent is responsible for the implementation of Gender Mainstreaming in the Region.
(2) The responsibility of the Regent as referred to in paragraph (1) may be delegated to the Deputy Regent.

Article 7
In implementing Gender Mainstreaming in the Region as referred to in Article 5 paragraph (1) letter b, the Regent determines the SKPD that handles the task of Women's Empowerment as the coordinator for the implementation of Gender Mainstreaming.

Part Three
Establishment of a Gender Mainstreaming Working Group in the Region
Article 8
(1) In an effort to accelerate the institutionalization of Gender Mainstreaming in all Regional SKPDs, a Gender Mainstreaming working group (pokja) is formed in the Region.
(2) Members of the Gender Mainstreaming pokja are all heads/leaders of SKPDs.
(3) The Regent appoints the head of Bappeda as the chair of the Regional Gender Mainstreaming pokja and the head of the SKPD that handles the task of Women's Empowerment as the head of the Regional Gender Mainstreaming pokja secretariat.
(4) The establishment of the Regional Gender Mainstreaming pokja is determined by the Regent's decision.

Article 9
The Regional Gender Mainstreaming pokja as referred to in Article 14 has the tasks of:
a. promoting and facilitating Gender Mainstreaming to each SKPD;
b. conducting socialization and advocacy of Gender Mainstreaming to sub-district heads (camat) and village heads (wali nagari);
c. compiling an annual work program;
d. encouraging the realization of gender-responsive planning and budgeting;
e. compiling the annual work plan of the Gender Mainstreaming pokja;
f. being responsible to the Regent through the Deputy Regent;
g. formulating policy recommendations to the Regent;
h. compiling the Regional Gender profile;
i. monitoring the implementation of Gender Mainstreaming in each agency;
j. establishing a technical team to conduct analysis of the Regional budget;
k. compiling a Regional Gender Mainstreaming action plan; and
l. encouraging the implementation of the selection and appointment of a focal point in each SKPD.

Article 10
(1) The Technical Team as referred to in Article 9 letter j consists of apparatus who understand gender-responsive budget analysis.
(2) The Regional Gender Mainstreaming action plan as referred to in Article 9 letter k contains:
a. Gender Mainstreaming in regional statutory regulations;
b. Gender Mainstreaming in the development cycle in the Region;
c. strengthening of Gender Mainstreaming institutions in the Region; and
d. strengthening of community participation in the Region.

Article 11
(1) The Gender Mainstreaming Focal Point at each SKPD consists of officials and/or staff who handle planning and/or program tasks.
(2) The Gender Mainstreaming Focal Point as referred to in paragraph (1) has the tasks of:
a. promoting Gender Mainstreaming in the work unit;
b. facilitating the drafting of gender-responsive SKPD work and budgeting plans;
c. conducting training, socialization, and advocacy of Gender Mainstreaming to all officials and staff within the SKPD environment;
d. reporting the implementation of Gender Mainstreaming to the SKPD head;
e. encouraging the implementation of gender analysis on policies, programs, and activities in the work unit; and
f. facilitating the compilation of gender data at each SKPD;
(3) The Gender Mainstreaming Focal Point as referred to in paragraph (1) is selected and determined by the SKPD leader.

Part Four
Increasing the Role, Quality, and Ability of Women in the Political Field
Article 12
Efforts to increase the role, quality, and ability of women in the political field as referred to in Article 5 paragraph (1) letter d number 1 include:
a. conducting socialization to increase women's political participation in general elections;
b. implementing programs that encourage the level of women's involvement in political processes and public positions;
c. facilitating women's self-development in organizing, unionizing, and gathering;
d. involving women in decision-making that concerns women's interests.

Part Five
Increasing the Role, Quality, and Ability of Women in the Legal Field
Article 13
Efforts to increase the role, quality, and ability of women in the legal field as referred to in Article 5 paragraph (1) letter d number 2 include:
a. conducting socialization to increase women's knowledge and legal awareness;
b. conducting mapping and reviewing gender-biased policies;
c. involving women's organizations in the process of drafting and discussing Regional legal products related to women's participation.

Part Six
Increasing the Role, Quality, and Ability of Women in the Social Field
Article 14
Efforts to increase the role, quality, and ability of women in the social field as referred to in Article 5 paragraph (1) letter d number 3 include:
a. increasing knowledge and skills to encourage the fulfillment of education according to their potential;
b. increasing and fulfilling the quality of women's health;
c. increasing awareness and knowledge about independent, healthy, and prosperous family planning, including access to consultation services and marriage registration;
d. increasing women's awareness of the impact of early marriage;
e. facilitating and striving for the preservation of traditions and the development of science, technology, art, and culture for the advancement of women;
f. increasing women's awareness and knowledge about independent, healthy, and prosperous family planning;
g. conducting programs to reduce maternal mortality;
h. encouraging the formation of women's organizations in the Region.

Part Seven
Increasing the Role, Quality, and Ability of Women in the Economic Field
Article 15
Efforts to increase the role, quality, and ability of women in the economic field as referred to in Article 5 paragraph (1) letter d number 4 include:
a. implementing programs, activities, and training to improve women's skills and expertise in order to realize the improvement of women's welfare;
b. facilitating the strengthening of business capital for women who are economically weak;
c. marketing products from women's businesses who are economically weak;
d. training in developing women's abilities through empowerment of pre-prosperous family economics;
e. comprehensive entrepreneurship training for women.

CHAPTER III
PROTECTION OF WOMEN
Part One
General
Article 16
(1) The Regional Government in efforts to protect women is responsible for:
a. establishing and implementing policies, programs, and activities for women's protection;
b. taking preventive measures against acts of violence against women, including the crime of human trafficking;
c. increasing the effectiveness of services for women victims of violence;
d. increasing protection efforts for female workers;
e. strengthening and developing institutions providing women's protection services;
f. collaborating with the Government, other Regional Governments, private parties, the business world, and/or NGOs in efforts to prevent acts of violence against women;
g. involving the active role of the family, traditional leaders (ninik mamak), women's leaders (bundo kanduang), customary leaders, religious leaders, society, and institutions in the field of women's protection;
(2) The responsibility of the Regional Government in efforts to protect women as referred to in paragraph (1) is carried out by the SKPD in accordance with their duties and functions and can coordinate with related agencies.

Part Two
Prevention of Acts of Violence against Women
Article 17
In order to prevent acts of violence against women, the Regional Government:
a. conducts socialization regarding the prevention of violence against women by optimizing the role of customary, cultural, religious, and community organizations;
b. builds networks and cooperation with law enforcement officials, government apparatus, universities, and various non-governmental organizations that move and/or care about women and children victims of violence;
c. provides public facilities that are comfortable and safe for women.

Part Three
Effectiveness of Services for Women Victims of Violence
Paragraph 1
General
Article 18
(1) Protection for women victims of violence is carried out through integrated services.
(2) The provision of integrated services for women victims of violence as referred to in paragraph (1) uses the principles of:
a. gender responsive;
b. non-discrimination;
c. equal and respectful relationships;
d. maintaining confidentiality;
e. providing a sense of safety and comfort;
f. respecting individual differences;
g. not judging;
h. respecting the victim's choices and decisions;
i. being sensitive to the victim's background and condition and using language that is appropriate and understood by the victim;
j. fast and simple;
k. empathy; and
l. fulfillment of women's rights.

Article 19
Forms of integrated services for women victims of violence include:
a. complaint handling;
b. health rehabilitation;
c. social rehabilitation;
d. law enforcement and legal aid; and
e. repatriation and social reintegration.

Paragraph 2
Complaint Handling
Article 20
Integrated services for complaint handling as referred to in Article 19 letter a are carried out by:
a. hospitals;
b. community health centers (puskesmas);
c. women and children service units;
d. trauma center protection houses;
e. women's crisis centers;
f. advisory boards for marriage guidance and preservation;
g. police;
h. prosecutor's office;
i. courts;
j. ministry of religion; and/or
k. legal aid institutions.

Article 21
(1) Complaints of acts of violence against women can be made by reporters consisting of:
a. the victim;
b. the family;
c. society; and/or
d. community organizations in the field of women's and children's protection, such as: P2TP2A, Safe Houses, etc.
(2) The reporter as referred to in paragraph (1) can make complaints:
a. directly;
b. via telephone; and/or
c. via letter.
(3) In addition to complaint handling submitted by reporters, officers at the service institutions as referred to in Article 20 are also authorized to handle victims of acts of violence against women obtained through other information.
(4) Further provisions regarding the procedures for complaint handling services as referred to in paragraph (1) and paragraph (2) are regulated by a Regent's Regulation.

Paragraph 3
Health Rehabilitation
Article 22
(1) Health rehabilitation as referred to in Article 19 letter b is an effort that includes aspects of:
a. promotive;
b. preventive;
c. curative; and
d. rehabilitative.
(2) Health rehabilitation as referred to in paragraph (1) is carried out comprehensively, is accessible 24 (twenty-four) hours, is of high quality, and is carried out in accordance with health service standards.
(3) Women victims of acts of violence have the right to free health services, including:
a. medical services;
b. medicolegal services;
c. psychosocial counseling; and
d. visum (forensic examination report).
(4) Further provisions regarding the procedures for the implementation of health services as referred to in paragraph (3) are regulated by a Regent's Regulation.

Paragraph 4
Social Rehabilitation
Article 23
(1) Social rehabilitation as referred to in Article 19 letter c is aimed at restoring and developing the capabilities of women victims of acts of violence who experience social dysfunction to be able to carry out their social functions in society normally.
(2) Social rehabilitation as referred to in paragraph (1) includes services of:
a. counseling;
b. provision of a safe house; and
c. spiritual guidance.
(3) The implementation of services as referred to in paragraph (2) is carried out by:
a. SKPD that has the duties and functions in the social field;
b. SKPD that handles Women's Empowerment, and can cooperate with existing integrated service centers, women's crisis centers, or social community organizations; or
c. ministry of religion.

Article 24
(1) Counseling services as referred to in Article 23 paragraph (2) letter a aim to:
a. help women victims of acts of violence recognize their problems and find effective ways to overcome them themselves;
b. empower women victims of acts of violence to determine their future;
c. provide moral support to women victims of acts of violence in facing the process they are undergoing; and
d. make women victims of acts of violence feel accepted in the community environment.
(2) Counseling services are performed by gender-responsive social rehabilitation officers, consisting of:
a. counselors;
b. psychologists; and
c. psychiatrists.
(3) Counseling services are performed in a place that ensures a sense of safety, comfort, and confidentiality of information from women victims of acts of violence.
(4) Stages of counseling services include:
a. initial counseling;
b. follow-up counseling;
c. case discussion; and
d. field visits.

Article 25
(1) The Regional Government can provide safe houses for women victims of acts of violence.
(2) The service of providing safe houses as referred to in Article 23 paragraph (2) letter b aims to protect women victims of acts of violence from intimidation by the perpetrator or other parties.
(3) The location of the safe house as referred to in paragraph (1) must be kept confidential.
(4) Criteria for women victims of acts of violence who need a safe house include:
a. their lives are threatened;
b. receiving rejection from family or society;
c. requiring intensive services but their residence is relatively far; and/or
d. being neglected if not placed in a safe house.
(5) The implementation of safe house services is carried out by social rehabilitation officers who have gender sensitivity or gender awareness, consisting of:
a. social workers/volunteers;
b. security officers; and/or
c. community leaders.
(6) Further provisions regarding the implementation of safe house services are regulated by a Regent's Regulation.

Article 26
(1) Spiritual guidance as referred to in Article 23 paragraph (2) letter c aims to restore mental health.
(2) Spiritual guidance as referred to in paragraph (1) is implemented based on the principles of:
a. not forcing any religion or belief; and
b. leaving the final decision in the hands of the victim (self-determination).
(3) Spiritual guidance is performed by spiritual guidance officers.
(4) The spiritual guidance officers as referred to in paragraph (3) consist of consultants from the Board for Marriage Advice, Guidance, and Preservation (BP4), Office of Religious Affairs (KUA) officers, Ustad/Ulama, Pastors, Priests, and other religious leaders who have gender sensitivity or gender awareness.

Paragraph 5
Law Enforcement and Legal Aid
Article 27
(1) Law enforcement and legal aid services as referred to in Article 19 letter d are actions of law enforcement officials to carry out statutory regulations in the context of handling cases of acts of violence against women, especially to provide sanctions against perpetrators and provide protection for witnesses and/or victims.
(2) Law enforcement and legal aid services as referred to in paragraph (1) are carried out by prioritizing the best interests of the victim.

Article 28
(1) Law enforcement and legal aid services are aimed at providing assistance and protection for women victims of acts of violence and witnesses with a gender-responsive approach so that victims receive justice and legal certainty.
(2) Types of legal aid services include:
a. legal aid in the reporting process and making the investigation report (BAP);
b. legal aid in the prosecution process at the prosecutor's office;
c. legal aid in the examination process in court;
d. legal aid in the court verdict process until there is a permanent legal decision; and
e. legal aid in the mediation process.
(3) The Regional Government provides legal aid to women victims of acts of violence based on the provisions of statutory regulations.
(4) In providing legal aid as referred to in paragraph (3), the Regional Government can cooperate with legal aid institutions, women's crisis centers, advocates, and/or other gender-sensitive legal companions.

Paragraph 6
Repatriation and Social Reintegration
Article 29
(1) Repatriation is aimed at returning women victims of acts of violence to their region of origin.
(2) The repatriation of women victims of acts of violence is accompanied by social reintegration efforts.

Article 30
(1) Social reintegration is carried out in the context of reuniting women victims of acts of violence with their families, substitute families, or society that can provide protection and fulfill the needs of the victim.
(2) Social reintegration is intended so that:
a. women victims of acts of violence can resume their social life; and
b. women victims of acts of violence can have a decent life.
(3) Social reintegration as referred to in paragraph (1) is carried out after the victim has received social rehabilitation services.

Article 31
The Regional Government carries out the repatriation and social reintegration of women victims of acts of violence in accordance with statutory regulations.

Part Four
Employment Protection
Article 32
(1) Entrepreneurs who employ women are required to maintain decency, safety, and security while in the workplace.
(2) Entrepreneurs are required to place female workers in a place that is safe for women's reproductive health.
(3) Entrepreneurs are required to fulfill the rights of female workers to receive wages in accordance with the provisions of statutory regulations.
(4) Entrepreneurs are required to provide special breastfeeding facilities in their companies.

Article 33
(1) Female workers/laborers under the age of 18 (eighteen) years are prohibited from being employed between 23:00 WIB and 07:00 WIB.
(2) Entrepreneurs are prohibited from employing pregnant female workers/laborers if, according to a doctor's statement, it is dangerous for the health and safety of her pregnancy.
(3) Entrepreneurs who employ female workers/laborers between 23:00 WIB and 07:00 WIB are required to:
a. provide nutritious food and drinks; and
b. maintain decency and security while in the workplace.
(4) Entrepreneurs are required to provide shuttle transportation for female workers/laborers who leave and return to work between 23:00 WIB and 05:00 WIB.

CHAPTER V
IMPROVEMENT OF FAMILY QUALITY
Article 34
(1) In the context of improving family quality, the Regional Government is responsible for:
a. taking efforts to improve family quality to realize gender equality and child rights;
b. providing services for families in realizing gender equality and child rights;
c. strengthening and developing institutions providing family quality improvement services in the Region; and
(2) The responsibility of the Regional Government in efforts to improve family quality as referred to in paragraph (1) is carried out by the SKPD in accordance with their duties and functions.

Article 35
The Regional Government takes efforts to improve family quality as referred to in Article 34 paragraph (1) letter a, including:
a. development/guidance;
b. counseling/guidance;
c. supervision;
d. facilitation of family development implementation;
e. improving the quality of health for pregnant and breastfeeding mothers.

Article 36
Development/guidance as referred to in Article 35 letter a includes:
a. growth and development of children under five (balita);
b. protection of adolescent reproductive health;
c. maturation of marriage age;
d. birth regulation; and
e. population management.

Article 37
Counseling/guidance as referred to in Article 35 letter b includes:
a. conducting counseling on improving women's quality of life;
b. conducting pre-marital counseling.

Article 38
Supervision as referred to in Article 35 letter c includes:
a. evaluating data on the growth and development of children under five;
b. evaluating post-marriage data.

Article 39
The provision of services for families as referred to in Article 34 paragraph (1) letter b includes:
a. communication, information, and education;
b. contraceptive devices and medicines;
c. recording and reporting of family planning services;
d. adequate health service facilities and infrastructure;
e. breastfeeding corner (pojok ASI) room in every public facility.

Article 40
Communication, information, and education as referred to in Article 39 letter a are communication activities to increase knowledge and improve the attitudes and behaviors of families and society in population and family planning programs.

Article 41
(1) In addition to the Regional Government, private parties (private parties include companies/PT that provide public services) are required to provide a breastfeeding corner room.
(2) The breastfeeding corner room as referred to in paragraph (1) is a room that is decent and adequate.
(3) Private parties that do not provide a breastfeeding corner room as referred to in paragraph (2) are subject to administrative sanctions in the form of:
a. written warning;
b. business activity restriction;
c. business activity suspension;
d. administrative fine; and/or
e. license revocation.

CHAPTER VI
FULFILLMENT OF CHILD RIGHTS
Part One
General
Article 42
(1) The Regional Government in efforts to fulfill child rights is responsible for:
a. establishing and implementing policies and programs/activities for the fulfillment of child rights;
b. respecting and guaranteeing the human rights of every child without discriminating by tribe, religion, race, group, gender, ethnicity, culture and language, child's legal status, child's birth order, and physical and/or mental condition;
c. strengthening and developing institutions providing services for improving family quality in realizing child rights;
d. guaranteeing the protection, maintenance, and welfare of the child by paying attention to the rights and obligations of parents, guardians, or other people who are legally responsible for the child;
e. providing facilities and infrastructure in the implementation of the fulfillment of child rights;
f. guaranteeing the child's right to express their opinion in accordance with the child's age and level of intelligence;
g. conducting supervision and guidance in the context of implementing the fulfillment of child rights;
h. conducting cooperation with private parties and/or NGOs in the implementation of child protection.
(2) The responsibility of the Regional Government in efforts for child protection as referred to in paragraph (1) is carried out by the SKPD in accordance with their duties and functions.

Part Two
Birth Registration
Article 43
(1) The Regional Government is obligated to carry out the registration of child births with the issuance of a birth certificate.
(2) The birth certificate as referred to in paragraph (1) is provided free of charge.

Part Three
Education
Article 44
The Regional Government guarantees the implementation of a 9 (nine) year compulsory education program for children aged 7 (seven) to 15 (fifteen) years.

Article 45
(1) Every education unit provider is required to give children the opportunity to obtain educational services without discrimination.
(2) Every education unit provider is prohibited from expelling children from educational institutions unless there is a guarantee for the continuity of the child's education.
(3) In the event that a child who is a victim of an act of violence drops out of school, the education unit provider is required to coordinate with related agencies.

Article 46
(1) Every child has the right to obtain education and instruction in the context of developing their personality and level of intelligence in accordance with their interests and talents.
(2) Every child has the right to obtain protection in the education unit from sexual crimes and violence committed by educators, education personnel, fellow students, and/or other parties.

Article 47
(1) Children with disabilities and children with special needs are given the same opportunity to obtain formal, non-formal, and informal educational services, including inclusive education programs.
(2) The Regional Government provides educators for inclusive education in every education unit.

Part Four
Health and Social
Article 48
(1) The Regional Government is required to conduct comprehensive health services for children so that every child obtains an optimal degree of health since being in the womb.
(2) Comprehensive health services as referred to in paragraph (1) include:
a. promotive, preventive, curative, and rehabilitative efforts for basic health services;
b. exemption from costs for children with malnutrition, children with disabilities, children with special needs, children infected with HIV/AIDS, child laborers, children victims of abuse of narcotics, alcohol, psychotropics, and other addictive substances, children who are victims of violence, and children victims of human trafficking; and
(3) The Regional Government, society, family, and parents are required to strive so that children born avoid diseases that threaten survival and/or cause disability.

Article 49
(1) Promotive efforts of basic health services as referred to in Article 46 paragraph (2) letter a are aimed at increasing the degree of health more optimally.
(2) Promotive efforts as referred to in paragraph (1) are carried out by providing socialization or counseling to the society regarding healthy lifestyles, which include:
a. the importance of exclusive breastfeeding for babies;
b. the importance of immunization for children under five;
c. clean lifestyle;
d. the importance of nutritious food;
e. the dangers of drugs and smoking; and
(3) In addition to promotive efforts as referred to in paragraph (2), the Regional Government can carry out other activities in the context of health implementation.

Article 50
(1) Preventive efforts of basic health services as referred to in Article 46 paragraph (2) letter a are aimed at preventing the occurrence of diseases or health problems.
(2) Efforts as referred to in paragraph (1) are carried out by:
a. requiring mothers to provide exclusive breastfeeding to babies up to the age of 6 (six) months;
b. providing free immunization and vitamins to babies and children under five;
c. providing nutritious food to children under five from low-income families;
d. optimizing the role of integrated service posts (posyandu) for health services for mothers and children under five;
e. conducting health check-ups periodically; and
f. providing sanitation and clean water facilities in every school.

Article 51
(1) Curative efforts of basic health services as referred to in Article 46 paragraph (2) letter a are aimed at preventing diseases from becoming more severe through treatment.
(2) Treatment as referred to in paragraph (1) is carried out free of charge for children from low-income families.

Article 52
(1) Rehabilitative efforts of basic health services as referred to in Article 46 paragraph (2) letter a are aimed at maintaining and restoring conditions after illness.
(2) Rehabilitative efforts as referred to in paragraph (1) are carried out through periodic health check-ups.

Article 53
(1) The Regional Government is required to provide Social Welfare for:
a. children in conflict with the law;
b. children victims of violence who are economically and sexually exploited, neglected children, children victims of kidnapping, and children victims of human trafficking;
c. children who do not have parents and are cared for by incapable parties or families;
d. children victims of natural disasters or social disasters; and
e. children with disabilities;
(2) Social welfare as referred to in paragraph (1) can be in the form of providing services of:
a. health;
b. education;
c. legal aid;
d. accompaniment;
e. social, mental, and spiritual guidance;
f. social rehabilitation; and/or
g. reintegration.

Part Five
Employment
Article 54
The Regional Government conducts periodic supervision of every person/body that employs children in an effort to protect child laborers.

Article 55
Every person/body that employs a child must pay attention to the requirements of:
a. being at least 15 (fifteen) years of age;
b. having written approval from the child laborer's parents/guardian;
c. having a written employment agreement between the employer and the child laborer's parents/guardian and obtaining approval from the authorized agency;
d. not employing children at night;
e. maximum working hours of 3 (three) hours a day.

Article 56
In the event that a child is employed together with adult workers, the child's workplace must be separated from the adult workers' workplace.

Article 57
(1) Every person/body is prohibited from employing and involving children in the worst forms of labor.
(2) The worst forms of labor referred to in paragraph (1) include:
a. all work in the form of slavery or similar practices;
b. all work that utilizes, provides, or offers children for prostitution, pornography production, pornographic shows, or gambling;
c. all work that utilizes, provides, or involves children for the production and trade of alcoholic beverages, narcotics, psychotropics, and other addictive substances; and/or
d. all work that endangers the health, safety, or morals of the child.

Part Six
Facilitation of the Juvenile Justice System
Article 58
(1) Facilitation of the juvenile justice system includes criminal and civil justice.
(2) Facilitation of the criminal justice system as referred to in paragraph (1) includes:
a. provision of a child companion or volunteer during examination by investigators;
b. provision of legal aid and psychologist services;
c. involving local police members/investigators in efforts to prevent criminal acts committed by children or children as victims;
d. realizing a restorative justice process for children in conflict with the law; and
e. social rehabilitation and reintegration services;
(3) Fulfillment of child rights in the civil justice system as referred to in paragraph (1) includes the provision and delivery of consultation and legal aid services during the judicial process.
(4) In facilitating the juvenile justice system as referred to in paragraph (1) and paragraph (2), the authorized SKPD is the SKPD that handles the protection of women and children.

Part Seven
Child Forum
Article 59
(1) In organizing the fulfillment of child rights to express opinions and participate, the Regional Government facilitates the formation of a Child Forum.
(2) The Child Forum as referred to in paragraph (1) is a representation of children in the Region, both representations of the child's geographical domicile, components of the child's socio-cultural groups, and the child's educational background.
(3) In every drafting of policies related to children, the Regional Government is required to pay attention to and accommodate the opinions of children submitted through the Child Participation Forum.
(4) Further provisions regarding the formation of the Child Forum as referred to in paragraph (1) to paragraph (3) are determined by a Regent's Decision.

Part Eight
Child-Friendly Regency (KLA)
Article 60
(1) In the context of the fulfillment of child rights in an integrated, systematic, and sustainable manner, the Regional Government establishes a KLA Development policy.
(2) The KLA Development policy contains:
a. the KLA concept regarding objectives, strategies, and the roles of related parties;
b. child rights; and
c. the KLA development approach.

Article 61
(1) The KLA development policy is directed at the fulfillment of child rights, which is divided into 5 (five) groups, including:
a. civil rights and freedoms;
b. family environment and alternative care;
c. basic health and welfare;
d. education, use of leisure time, and cultural activities; and
e. special protection.
(2) Further provisions regarding the KLA development policy as referred to in paragraph (1) are regulated by a Regent's Regulation.

Article 62
(1) In the context of the effectiveness of the implementation of the KLA policy in the Region, a KLA Task Force is formed.
(2) The formation and composition of the KLA Task Force membership are determined by a Regent's Decision.
(3) The KLA Task Force is based at the Office of Women's Empowerment and Family Planning.
(4) The KLA Task Force as referred to in paragraph (1) has the main tasks of:
a. coordinating the implementation of KLA policies and development;
b. conducting socialization, advocacy, and communication, information, and education on KLA policies;
c. collecting KLA development baseline data;
d. conducting needs analysis sourced from baseline data;
e. disseminating baseline data;
f. determining the focus and priority of programs in realizing KLA, which are adjusted to the regional potential;
g. compiling a 5 (five) year Child-Friendly City regional action plan and work mechanism; and
h. conducting monitoring, evaluation, and reporting at least once (1) a year.

Article 63
In carrying out its duties, the KLA Task Force can coordinate and cooperate with PPT institutions and other institutions that perform child protection services.

CHAPTER VII
CHILD PROTECTION
Article 64
The Regional Government, in the context of providing protection to children:
a. implements and supports national policies in the implementation of child protection in the Region;
b. guarantees the child to use their right to express opinions in accordance with the child's age and level of intelligence;
c. carries out the prevention of violence against children by optimizing the role of customary institutions;
d. conducts socialization, seminars, and counseling regarding child protection and rights;
e. develops child-friendly school models;
f. provides public facilities that are friendly and safe for children;
g. encourages community concern for the importance of child protection.

Article 65
Service efforts carried out by the Regional Government for child victims of acts of violence as referred to in Article 42 to Article 64 apply mutatis mutandis to service efforts for children victims of acts of violence.

CHAPTER VIII
COMMUNITY PARTICIPATION, AND PARENTS AND/OR FAMILY
Part One
Society
Article 66
(1) Community participation in efforts to protect women and children:
a. participates in prevention and provides support for the development of programs to prevent violations of women's and children's rights;
b. provides information and/or reports the occurrence of violations of women's and children's rights to authorized parties; and
c. oversees the law enforcement process against cases of violations of women's and children's rights.
(2) Traditional leaders (ninik mamak), religious leaders (alim ulama), intellectual leaders (cadiak pandai), and women's leaders (bundo kanduang) are responsible for protecting and preventing the occurrence of violations of women's and children's rights.
(3) Community participation as referred to in paragraph (1) and paragraph (2) is based on statutory regulations and the local community's values of local wisdom.

Article 67
(1) In the context of preventing the occurrence of violations of women's rights, the society:
a. monitors various conditions occurring in its environment that can lead to violations of women's rights;
b. increases concern for violations of women's rights;
c. strengthens the role of community, religious, and family environment organizations;
d. monitors the implementation of Women's Empowerment and Child Protection carried out by the Regional Government with a mechanism for submitting aspirations to the Regent or the Regional People's Representative Council.
(2) In the context of preventing the occurrence of violations of child rights, the society:
a. monitors various conditions occurring in its environment that lead to violations of child rights;
b. increases concern for violations of child rights;
c. strengthens the role of community, religious, and family environment organizations; and
d. prevents the occurrence of marriage at an early age.

Part Two
Parents and/or Family
Article 68
The family in efforts to protect women's rights plays a role in:
a. protecting women from conflict and domestic violence that degrades women's dignity;
b. providing equal opportunities for women to develop themselves in the fields of education, social, economic, political, and culture; and
c. preventing the occurrence of violations of women's rights within the household.

Article 69
(1) Parents in efforts to protect child rights play a role in:
a. caring for, maintaining, educating, and protecting the child;
b. growing and developing the child in accordance with their abilities, talents, and interests;
c. providing formal and non-formal education;
d. providing character education and instilling moral values in the child;
e. guiding the child to behave in accordance with religion, ethics, and morals as well as traditions; and
f. preventing the occurrence of marriage at an early age.
(2) In the event that parents are not present, or their whereabouts are unknown, or due to a certain cause, cannot carry out their obligations and responsibilities, then the obligations and responsibilities as referred to in paragraph (1) can be transferred to the family.

CHAPTER IX
GUIDANCE AND SUPERVISION
Article 70
(1) The Regional Government is required to conduct guidance and supervision of the implementation of the protection of women and children victims of violence.
(2) Guidance as referred to in paragraph (1) includes:
a. guidelines and fulfillment standards;
b. technical guidance and training;
c. provision of facilities;
d. monitoring; and
e. evaluation.
(3) Guidance as referred to in paragraph (2) is in the context of realizing the objectives of protecting women and children victims of violence in accordance with minimum service standards.

Article 71
(1) Supervision of the implementation of child protection as regulated in this Regional Regulation is carried out by the Padang Pariaman Regency Regional Government Supervision Agency and P2TP2A.
(2) In carrying out supervision, P2TP2A is authorized to recommend cases to law enforcement, government agencies, and other community institutions as regulated in the Law.
(3) Recommendations submitted by P2TP2A must be followed up by law enforcement, government agencies, community institutions, and other related individuals.

CHAPTER X
FINANCING
Article 72
Financing for the implementation of Women's Empowerment and Child Protection programs and activities is sourced from:
a. the regional revenue and expenditure budget (APBD); and
b. other legitimate sources in accordance with the provisions of statutory regulations.

CHAPTER XI
CLOSING PROVISIONS
Article 73
Implementing regulations for this Regional Regulation are established no later than 6 (six) months from the date this Regional Regulation is promulgated.

Article 74
This Regional Regulation comes into force on the date of its promulgation; in order for everyone to know it, ordering the promulgation of this Regional Regulation by placing it in the Regional Gazette of Padang Pariaman Regency.
