c. that based on the considerations as referred to in letter a and letter b, it is necessary to establish a Qanun concerning Women's Protection;

CHAPTER I
GENERAL PROVISIONS
Article 1
In this Qanun, the following shall mean:

1. Region is Bireuen Regency.
2. Regency is Bireuen Regency which is part of the Aceh provincial region led by a Regent.
3. Regency Government is the Bireuen Regency Government as the administration of affairs carried out by the regency government and the Bireuen Regency House of Representatives in accordance with their respective functions and authorities.
4. Regency Government is an element of the administration of the Regency Government consisting of the Regent and regency apparatus.
5. Regent is the Head of the Bireuen Regency Government elected through a democratic process conducted based on the principles of direct, general, free, confidential, honest, and fair in accordance with statutory regulations.
6. Regency House of Representatives, hereinafter abbreviated as DPRK, is an element of the administration of Bireuen Regency whose members are elected through a general election in accordance with statutory regulations.
7. Woman is a person (human) who is at least 15 years old who menstruates, becomes pregnant, gives birth, and breastfeeds.
8. Women's Protection is all efforts aimed at protecting women and providing a sense of security in the fulfillment of their rights by providing consistent and systematic attention aimed at achieving just and equal relations with men.
9. Victim is a woman who experiences threats of violence and/or violence inside and/or outside the household and/or becomes an object of trafficking/exploitation, and/or discrimination in all its forms and aspects.
10. Threat of Violence is any act of non-physical abuse by using or not using means unlawfully that can cause danger to the body, life, liberty, and human rights of women.
11. Violence is any act against a person, especially a woman, which results in physical, psychological, sexual misery or suffering, and/or household neglect, including threats to commit acts, coercion, or unlawful deprivation of liberty, both inside and outside the scope of the household.
12. Discrimination Against Women is any distinction, exclusion, or restriction made on the basis of sex which has the effect or purpose of impairing or nullifying the recognition, enjoyment, or exercise by women of human rights and fundamental freedoms in the political, economic, social, cultural, civil, or any other field, regardless of their marital status, on the basis of equality between men and women.
13. Women's Rights are all rights owned by every woman that are part of human rights.
14. Special Protection is protection provided to women and children in emergency situations, women and children in conflict with the law, women and children from minority and isolated groups, women and children who are economically and/or sexually exploited, women and children who are trafficked, women and children who are victims of kidnapping, sale, trafficking, women and children who are victims of physical and/or mental violence.
15. Community is individuals, families, groups, and social organizations and/or community organizations.
16. Family is the smallest unit in society consisting of husband and wife, or husband-wife and their children, or father and his children, or mother and her children, or blood relatives in a direct line upward or downward up to the third degree.
17. Workforce is every person who is able to perform work to produce goods and/or services, both to meet their own needs and for the community.
18. Worker/Laborer is every person who works by receiving wages or other forms of compensation.
19. Person with a Disability is every person who experiences long-term physical, intellectual, mental, and/or sensory limitations which, in interacting with the environment, can encounter obstacles and difficulties to participate fully and effectively with other citizens based on equal rights.
20. Service is an action taken as soon as possible to the victim when seeing, hearing, and knowing that violence against the victim is about to, is, or has occurred.
21. Rehabilitation is a series of actions carried out by parties authorized in their field to restore the physical, mental, and psychosocial condition of women to their original state.

CHAPTER II
PRINCIPLES AND OBJECTIVES
Article 2
Women's protection is implemented based on the principles of:
a. respect for the values of Islamic Sharia and Aceh customs that are responsive to women;
b. legal certainty;
c. public interest;
d. respect for human rights;
e. gender justice and equality;
f. non-discrimination;
g. professionalism, accountability, and transparency; and
h. protection and fulfillment of victims' rights.

Article 3
Women's protection aims to:
a. strengthen regional policy and budget support in the context of optimizing the implementation of women's protection;
b. reaffirm the commitment of policymakers at the regency, sub-district, and village (gampong) levels in the framework of improving efforts and guarantees of protection for women;
c. improve coordination, integration, synchronization, and synergy between Regional Apparatus Working Units (SKPK) and all stakeholders, including religious organizations, professional organizations, academics, the private sector, and the wider community in efforts to improve the quality of women's protection;
d. build community knowledge and awareness to support efforts to protect women;
e. increase the institutional capacity and human resources of the Regional Technical Implementation Unit for Women's and Children's Protection (UPTD PPA);
f. provide guarantees for women to be able to fulfill their rights as human beings in all aspects of life;
g. provide a sense of security to women in all aspects of life; and
h. reduce the number of cases of violence against women.

CHAPTER III
SCOPE
Article 4
The scope of the regulation in this Qanun includes:
a. principles and objectives;
b. women's rights;
c. women's protection;
d. obligations and responsibilities of the Regency Government;
e. support of the village (gampong) government;
f. strategies and mechanisms for the implementation of women's protection;
g. community participation; and
h. awards.

CHAPTER IV
WOMEN'S RIGHTS
Part One
General
Article 5
(1) Every woman has the right to:
a. live and maintain her life, and improve her standard of welfare in her life;
b. carry out religious teachings in accordance with Islamic Sharia;
c. obtain justice, a sense of security, and freedom to express opinions without discrimination;
d. obtain protection for her personal self, family, honor, dignity, and property rights;
e. fulfill her basic needs to grow and develop properly, have a family, and continue her lineage;
f. obtain fair and equal treatment in the context of carrying out her roles and functions, both in family life, society, and participate fully in every process of making, determining, and implementing development policies;
g. obtain education and instruction at all types, levels, and paths of education, without any obstacles and pressure in the context of improving her skills and quality of life;
h. obtain complete information and knowledge for women about health, especially reproductive health; and
i. obtain special protection in the performance of her work or profession against things that threaten her safety and/or health related to reproductive functions.
(2) Every woman in her capacity as a wife, while in a marital bond, has the same rights and responsibilities as her husband regarding all matters concerning her married life, relationships with her children, and ownership and management of joint property in accordance with applicable laws and regulations.
(3) In addition to the rights as referred to in paragraph (2), a woman as a wife has the right to receive physical and spiritual support (nafkah) from her husband.
(4) After the termination of the marriage, a woman has rights and responsibilities from her ex-husband regarding all matters concerning the children, by observing the best interests of the children.

Part Two
Rights of Women with Disabilities
Article 6
Every woman with a disability has the right to obtain:
a. education in all units, paths, types, and levels of education;
b. decent work and livelihood in accordance with the type and degree of disability, education, and abilities;
c. equal treatment to play a role in development and enjoy its results;
d. accessibility in the context of her independence;
e. rehabilitation, social assistance, and maintenance of social welfare standards;
f. equal rights to develop her talents, abilities, and social life; and
g. other rights as regulated in laws and regulations.

Part Three
Rights of Women Victims of Violence
Article 7
Every woman victim of violence has the right to obtain:
a. respect for her dignity and worth as a human being;
b. health and psychological recovery from the suffering experienced by the victim;
c. determine her own decisions in handling the violence case she experienced;
d. obtain information;
e. obtain guarantees of confidentiality;
f. social rehabilitation;
g. complaint handling;
h. obtain ease in the judicial process;
i. assistance; and
j. obtain empowerment after the case is handled.

Part Four
Special Treatment
Article 8
(1) Every woman who is in a condition of disability, old age, and/or pregnancy has the right to obtain convenience and special treatment related to access to public services.
(2) Every elderly woman, person with a physical and/or mental disability, has the right to receive care, education, training, and special assistance at the expense of the Regency Government to ensure a decent life in accordance with human dignity.
(3) Every woman who is involved with drugs, both as a perpetrator and a victim, has the right to receive fair treatment both in the law enforcement process and the rehabilitation (recovery) process.

CHAPTER V
WOMEN'S PROTECTION
Part One
Implementation of Women's Protection
Article 9
Implementation of women's protection is provided to:
a. women as housewives;
b. female heads of households;
c. female domestic workers;
d. female workers/laborers;
e. women with disabilities;
f. homeless women;
g. women who are former correctional institution inmates;
h. women victims of violence;
i. women victims of conflict; and
j. elderly women.

Paragraph 1
Women as Housewives
Article 10
Implementation of protection for women as housewives as referred to in Article 9 letter a, is in the following matters:
a. physical and spiritual support (nafkah);
b. the head of the family provides special protection to the housewife during pregnancy; and
c. a housewife who has given birth must receive protection from the head of the family to reduce the burden as a housewife.

Article 11
A housewife who performs work outside the home must receive protection from the head of the family in the form of granting permission and may provide domestic workers.

Paragraph 2
Female Heads of Households
Article 12
(1) Female heads of households as referred to in Article 9 letter b, are widows left by their deceased husbands and due to divorce, and are required to receive protection.
(2) The implementation of protection for female heads of households as referred to in paragraph (1) is in the context of providing protection and prevention against forms of discrimination, negative stigma, and economic empowerment for female heads of households.
(3) Women as heads of households as referred to in paragraph (2) receive the right of protection over:
a. acts of discrimination and efforts to weaken the function of women as the economic support of the family;
b. strengthening capabilities and recognition as heads of households in their environment; and
c. accessibility to economic and financial institutions.
(4) The implementation of protection for women as heads of households whose husbands have died as referred to in paragraph (1) includes:
a. women as heads of households receive 1/8 (one-eighth) of the total remaining assets of their husbands if they have children; and
b. women as heads of households receive 1/4 (one-fourth) of the total remaining assets of their husbands if they do not have children.
(5) The implementation of protection for women as heads of households due to divorce as referred to in paragraph (1) during the iddah period, the ex-husband is responsible for providing housing and living support (nafkah).

Paragraph 3
Female Domestic Workers
Article 13
(1) The implementation of protection for female domestic workers as referred to in Article 9 letter c, is in the context of providing protection and prevention against forms of discrimination, cruel, inhumane, degrading treatment, and exploitation in employment relationships.
(2) Female domestic workers as referred to in paragraph (1) are required to receive protection from their employers in the form of:
a. recognition of rights, wages, and decent working conditions;
b. health and social security; and
c. guarantees against discrimination, inhumane, degrading treatment, and exploitation.

Paragraph 4
Female Workers/Laborers
Article 14
(1) The implementation of protection for female workers/laborers as referred to in Article 9 letter d, is to provide protection from acts of economic exploitation and cruel, inhumane treatment and to strive for decent appreciation of work.
(2) Female workers/laborers as referred to in paragraph (1) are required to receive protection from employers in the form of:
a. recognition of rights, wages, and decent working conditions;
b. health and social security; and
c. guarantees against discrimination, inhumane, degrading treatment, and exploitation.

Paragraph 5
Women with Disabilities
Article 15
(1) The implementation of protection for women with disabilities as referred to in Article 9 letter e, is in the context of providing protection and prevention against forms of discrimination, including freedom from cruel, inhumane, degrading treatment, and freedom from exploitation.
(2) Women with disabilities as referred to in paragraph (1) receive the right of protection over:
a. guarantees for personal continuity and development;
b. accessibility to information, legal aid, mobility, social services; and
c. early handling efforts, including treatment and rehabilitation, to improve their functions and capacities.

Paragraph 6
Homeless Women
Article 16
(1) The implementation of protection for homeless women as referred to in Article 9 letter f, is in the context of providing protection and prevention against forms of violence caused by the absence of a shelter or permanent residence.
(2) Homeless women as referred to in paragraph (1) receive the right of protection over:
a. decent housing;
b. health and social security;
c. population administration recording;
d. opportunities to obtain education and skills to improve economic status; and
e. access to information and legal consultation services.

Paragraph 7
Women Former Correctional Institution Inmates
Article 17
(1) The implementation of protection for women former correctional institution inmates as referred to in Article 9 letter g, is in the context of providing protection from obstacles to adjusting to life in the community, so they can live normally.
(2) Women former correctional institution inmates as referred to in paragraph (1) receive protection over:
a. social rehabilitation and reintegration; and
b. opportunities to obtain knowledge and skills to improve economic status.

Paragraph 8
Women Victims of Violence
Article 18
(1) The implementation of protection for women victims of violence as referred to in Article 9 letter h, is in the context of providing legal protection and/or health recovery.
(2) Protection for women victims of violence as referred to in paragraph (1) includes:
a. legal aid;
b. assistance after the occurrence of violence;
c. social rehabilitation and reintegration; and
d. health services.

Article 19
(1) Every victim as referred to in Article 18 has the right to receive protection, information, integrated services, continuous handling until the rehabilitation stage, and confidential handling from individuals, groups, or institutions, both the Regency Government and the community.
(2) In the event of violence, every victim has the right to receive health services and assistance, both psychologically and legally, as well as receive guarantees for their rights related to their status as women.

Paragraph 9
Women Victims of Conflict
Article 20
Implementation of protection for women victims of conflict as referred to in Article 9 letter i, is through:
a. providing a sense of security;
b. fulfilling the basic needs of women victims of conflict;
c. eliminating trauma; and
d. providing the services needed by victims.

Paragraph 10
Elderly Women
Article 21
(1) The implementation of protection for elderly women as referred to in Article 9 letter j, is in the context of carrying out their social functions and playing an active and reasonable role in their social life.
(2) Protection for elderly women as referred to in paragraph (1) is prioritized for protection from their children and heirs.
(3) If an elderly woman does not receive services from her children and heirs and/or does not have children and other heirs as referred to in paragraph (2), then the protection of the elderly woman becomes the responsibility of the Regency Government.
(4) Elderly women as referred to in paragraph (1) receive the right of protection over:
a. basic health services to maintain and improve health status, including gerontological/geriatric services;
b. use of facilities and accessibility to public infrastructure; and
c. government administration services.

CHAPTER VII
OBLIGATIONS AND RESPONSIBILITIES OF THE REGENCY GOVERNMENT
Part One
General
Article 22
(1) The Regency Government is obligated and responsible for the protection of women.
(2) Women's protection efforts as referred to in paragraph (1) include:
a. determination of policies, programs, and activities;
b. determination of implementation guidelines;
c. provision of services; and
d. coordination of policies, programs, and activities.
(3) Women's protection as referred to in paragraph (2) is carried out in an integrated manner in accordance with statutory regulations.

Article 23
(1) In implementing policies, programs, and activities for women's protection and empowerment for female victims, the Regency Government may cooperate with the community/community institutions.
(2) Cooperation as referred to in paragraph (1) is implemented in accordance with the provisions of statutory regulations.

Article 24
The Regent, in implementing policies, programs, and activities for women's protection, carries out efforts to:
a. coordinate the implementation of women's protection policies, programs, and activities among regency apparatus;
b. cooperate with Regencies/Cities in one Province, and cooperation between Regencies/Cities in other Provinces in the implementation of women's protection policies, programs, and activities in accordance with statutory provisions;
d. strengthen institutional capacity for the implementation of women's protection policies, programs, and activities;
e. facilitate women's protection services;
f. provide women's protection services;
g. implement affirmative action for women's protection; and
h. develop a women's protection data system.

Article 25
(1) The Regency Government is obligated and responsible for carrying out prevention efforts against violence against women in the form of:
a. collecting data and information on victims of violence and statutory regulations;
b. conducting education on anti-violence values against women;
c. socializing statutory regulations related to the implementation of protection for victims of violence;
d. fostering support from religious leaders and customary leaders to build community awareness of the importance of protection for women;
e. monitoring and evaluating the implementation of protection for victims of violence; and
f. preparing reports on the implementation of protection for women victims of violence.
(2) To anticipate the occurrence of violence against women, the Regency Government is obligated to provide and organize services for victims in the form of:
a. forming institutions and facilitating the implementation of integrated services for victims by involving community elements; and
b. encouraging community concern for the importance of protection for victims.
(3) To prevent the occurrence of violence against women, the Regency Government carries out the following efforts:
a. socializing regulations and policies related to violence against women to village (gampong) apparatus, customary leaders, and religious leaders;
b. providing counseling to immediately report to local authorities if violence against women occurs;
c. forming networks in efforts to prevent violence; and
d. other efforts considered strategic to prevent the occurrence of violence against women.

Article 26
(1) The regency government is obligated to provide social assistance for women who experience social problems and victims of disasters.
(2) The regency government is obligated to provide the right to social security for poor women and women with limited abilities.

Part Two
Women's Protection Institution
Article 27
(1) The Regency Government shall form a women's protection institution.
(2) The women's protection institution as referred to in paragraph (1) also handles protection for children.
(3) The women's protection institution as referred to in paragraph (2) is implemented by forming a UPTD PPA (Regional Technical Implementation Unit for Women's and Children's Protection).
(4) The Regency Government must ensure the availability of adequate facilities, infrastructure, human resources, and budget to realize the operation of UPTD PPA.
(5) Further provisions regarding UPTD PPA are regulated by Regent Regulation.

CHAPTER VIII
VILLAGE (GAMPONG) GOVERNMENT SUPPORT
Article 28
(1) The Village (Gampong) Government supports women's protection efforts by:
a. issuing village-level regulations concerning women's protection;
b. socializing regulations related to women's empowerment and protection to the community;
c. encouraging the realization of a village environment that is friendly to women;
d. building community awareness, including customary leaders and religious leaders at the village level, to care about the issue of violence against women;
e. allocating budget to support women's empowerment and protection programs; and
f. building a system for handling violence against women at the village level.
(2) The efforts as referred to in paragraph (1) letter e, above can be developed according to the financial capacity of the village, problems, and needs of women.

CHAPTER IX
STRATEGIES AND MECHANISMS FOR WOMEN'S PROTECTION
Part One
General
Article 29
(1) The Regent integrates women's protection policies, programs, and activities into the regency development planning.
(2) The integration of women's protection policies, programs, and activities must be set forth in planning and budgeting documents which include the Regional Long-Term Development Plan (RPJPD), Regional Medium-Term Development Plan (RPJMD), Strategic Plan of the Regency Apparatus Working Unit (Renstra-SKPK), Regency Government Work Plan (RKPK), and Work Plan and Budget of the Regency Apparatus Working Unit (RKA-SKPK).
(3) The Regency Apparatus Working Unit whose tasks and functions handle women's empowerment affairs facilitates the integration of women's protection policies, programs, and activities into the preparation of planning and budget documents as stated in paragraph (2).
(4) In carrying out the tasks as referred to in paragraph (3), the work unit whose tasks and functions handle women's empowerment in the region coordinates with the Regional Development Planning Agency (BAPPEDA).

Part Two
Strategy
Article 30
(1) Women's protection is implemented by integrating Gender Mainstreaming (PUG) into disaggregated data, gender data, and integrated violence data based on applicable statutory regulations.
(2) The Regency Apparatus Working Unit that handles women's empowerment and protection coordinates with other Regional Apparatus organizations in order to realize the implementation of women's protection in an integrated manner.
(3) The coordination as referred to in paragraph (2), is carried out in the framework of strengthening synergy in policy formulation, support for programs and activities, as well as monitoring and evaluation.

Part Three
Mechanism
Article 31
The mechanism for the implementation of women's protection is carried out through the stages of:
a. formulation of program and activity planning policies;
b. budgeting of programs and activities;
c. implementation of programs and activities; and
d. monitoring, evaluation, and reporting.

Article 32
(1) The formulation of program and activity planning policies as referred to in Article 31 letter a, is carried out through:
a. mapping, collecting, processing, and presenting disaggregated data;
b. coordination and synchronization of program and activity planning preparation with related SKPK; and
c. determination of programs and activities in the planning document.
(2) The formulation of program and activity planning policies is further regulated in service standards and standard operating procedures.
(3) The budgeting of programs and activities as referred to in Article 31 letter b, is carried out through general budget policies set forth in the Regency Revenue and Expenditure Budget (APBK).
(4) The implementation of programs and activities as referred to in Article 31 letter c, is carried out through:
a. implementation of programs and activities stated in the SKPK budget document; and
b. coordination and synchronization of program and activity implementation with related SKPK.
(5) The implementation of monitoring, evaluation, and reporting as referred to in Article 31 letter d, is carried out through:
a. periodic monitoring and evaluation of the implementation of programs and activities that have been carried out; and
b. making periodic reports annually which are submitted to the Regent.

CHAPTER VIII (IX)
COMMUNITY PARTICIPATION
Article 33
(1) The community may participate in the protection of women.
(2) Community participation as referred to in paragraph (1) can be carried out by individuals and/or organizations.
(3) The participation as referred to in paragraph (1) and paragraph (2) can be carried out by:
a. providing input and suggestions to the Regency Government related to women's protection policies, programs, and activities;
b. monitoring Regency Government policies regarding policies related to women's protection;
c. initiating or proposing new policies that need to be determined by the Regency Government in an effort to optimize the protection of women;
d. assisting village (gampong) officials in the context of implementing women's protection efforts as regulated in this Qanun;
e. conducting socialization on the elimination of violence against women;
f. providing information and/or reporting the existence of violence against women to law enforcement institutions or authorities; and
g. monitoring the performance of law enforcement officials in handling cases of violence against women.

Article 34
To ensure the best service for women victims of violence, the community, either individually, in groups, or as institutions, can coordinate with UPTD PPA.

Article 35
(1) The Regency Government encourages the business world to participate in the protection of women from acts of violence.
(2) Such participation is carried out for both internal and external purposes.
(3) Internal participation as referred to in paragraph (2) can be in the form of:
a. socialization to employees within the company environment regarding the dangers of acts of violence against women;
b. building an internal women's system to handle issues of violence against women; and
c. providing social assistance as a form of corporate social responsibility.
(4) External participation as referred to in paragraph (2) can be in the form of:
a. providing special opportunities for women victims of violence to work in the company;
b. creating special education and training for women victims of violence;
c. educational assistance for women victims of violence who have been removed from education; and
d. fostering and increasing the economic independence of women victims of violence.

CHAPTER IX (X)
AWARDS
Article 36
(1) The Regency Government gives awards to State Apparatus who are considered to have commitment and succeed in carrying out the duties of women's protection.
(2) The Regency Government gives awards to Community Organizations, the Private Sector, and Individuals who are considered to make innovations and succeed in supporting women's protection efforts.
(3) Awards are also given to the Village (Gampong) Government which is considered to have commitment and succeeds in supporting women's protection efforts.
(4) Further arrangements regarding the granting of awards as referred to in paragraph (1) and paragraph (2) are regulated by Regent Regulation.

CHAPTER X (XI)
FINANCING
Article 37
All costs for implementing program and activity planning policies related to women's protection are sourced from:
a. Regency Revenue and Expenditure Budget; and
b. Other sources that are legitimate and non-binding.

CHAPTER XI (XII)
GUIDANCE, MONITORING, AND EVALUATION
Article 38
(1) The Regent, through the Regional Apparatus that handles women's empowerment affairs, conducts guidance and supervision of women's protection.
(2) The form of guidance as referred to in paragraph (1) is as follows:
a. providing socialization to the community and the business world regarding women's protection policies;
b. providing books, leaflets, brochures regarding women's protection and other related issues and disseminating them to the community;
c. facilitation of capacity building for women's protection implementing institutions;
d. facilitation of women's participation forums, women's organizations, and organizations for people with disabilities;
e. facilitation of rehabilitation and reintegration services for women victims of disasters and victims of violence;
f. coordinating the role of the business world and other institutions, including international aid for women's protection; and
g. giving awards to the community and the business world, both individually and institutionally, that have carried out women's protection efforts.

Article 39
(1) To ensure synergy, continuity, and effectiveness of integrated steps in the implementation of women's protection policies, programs, and activities, the Regency Government conducts monitoring.
(2) Monitoring as referred to in paragraph (1) is intended to determine developments and obstacles in the implementation of women's protection policies, programs, and activities.
(3) Monitoring is carried out periodically through coordination and direct monitoring of regional apparatus that implement women's protection policies, programs, and activities.
(4) Monitoring is carried out from planning to the implementation of policies, programs, and activities.

Article 40
(1) Evaluation of the implementation of women's protection policies, programs, and activities is carried out at the end of each fiscal year.
(2) The results of the evaluation of the implementation of women's protection policies, programs, and activities are used as input for the preparation of women's protection policies, programs, and activities for the following year.
(3) The evaluation as referred to in paragraph (1) is carried out in accordance with the provisions of statutory regulations.

CHAPTER XII (XIII)
CLOSING PROVISIONS
Article 41
Implementing regulations of this Qanun in the form of Regent Regulations must be established no later than six (6) months from the date this Qanun is enacted.

Article 42
This Qanun shall come into force on the date of its enactment.
In order for everyone to know, it is ordered that this Qanun be enacted by placing it in the Bireuen Regency Gazette.

WOMEN'S PROTECTION

I. GENERAL

1. That Islamic Sharia gives a high and noble position to women. Women have the same position as men as servants of Allah and dignified beings. In the Qur'an and the Hadith of the Prophet Muhammad SAW, there are no teachings or activities that lead to discrimination and exploitation of women. In fact, one of the missions of the Prophet Muhammad SAW in this world is to uplift the honor and dignity of women from a very low and despicable state.
2. That Law Number 44 of 1999 concerning the Implementation of Aceh's Privileges and Law Number 11 of 2006 concerning the Government of Aceh have provided a foundation for the empowerment and protection of women, which are important activities because the condition of women so far is quite concerning in terms of education, economy, health, labor, law, and even threats of violence, exploitation, discrimination, pornography, and obscene acts are often targeted at women.
3. The obligation of the Bireuen Regency Government feels it is necessary to have a Bireuen Regency Qanun on Women's Empowerment and Protection, hoping that every woman can be empowered and receive protection without discrimination, and the Integrated Service Center for Women's and Child Empowerment (P2TP2A) that already exists in Bireuen Regency has a Juridical Foundation as one of the organizers of women's empowerment and protection in Bireuen Regency.
