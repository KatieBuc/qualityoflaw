# CHAPTER I

## GENERAL PROVISIONS

#### Article 1

In this Regional Regulation, the following terms shall mean:  
1. Regency Government is the Regent and regional apparatus as elements of regional government administration
2. Regent is the Regent of Jember
3. Child is a person who has not reached 18 years of age, including those in the womb
4. Woman is a person of female gender
5. Violence is any act that results in or may result in misery or suffering whether physical, sexual, economic, social, or psychological to the victim
6. Violence against women is any act based on gender differences that results in or may result in misery or suffering to women physically, sexually, economically, socially, psychologically, including threats of certain acts, coercion or deprivation of liberty, whether occurring in public or private life
7. Violence against children is any act that results in or may result in suffering to children physically, sexually, economically, socially and psychologically.  
8. Physical violence is any act that causes pain, injury, wounds or disability to a person's body, miscarriage, unconsciousness and or causes death.  
9. Psychological violence is an act that causes fear, loss of self-confidence, loss of ability to act, feelings of helplessness and or severe psychological suffering to a person.  
10. Sexual violence is any act in the form of sexual harassment, forced sexual intercourse, forced unnatural or unwanted sexual intercourse, forced sexual intercourse with others for commercial purposes and or certain purposes.  
11. Victim is a woman and child who experiences misery and or suffering either directly or indirectly as a result of violence.  
12. Protection of women is all activities aimed at providing a sense of security carried out by the police, prosecutor's office, courts, social institutions, or other parties who know or hear that violence against women will occur, is occurring or has occurred
13. Child protection is all activities to guarantee and protect children and their rights so they can live, grow, develop and participate optimally in accordance with human dignity and receive protection from violence and discrimination
14. Special protection is protection given to children in emergency situations, children in conflict with the law, children from minority and isolated groups, children who are exploited economically and or sexually, children who are trafficked, children who are victims of narcotics, alcohol, psychotropic and other addictive substance (NAPZA) abuse, children who are victims of kidnapping, sale, trafficking, children who are victims of physical and or mental violence, children with disabilities, and children who are victims of abuse and neglect.  
15. Service is action taken as soon as possible to victims when seeing, hearing and knowing that violence against victims will occur, is occurring or has occurred.  
16. Companion is a person or representative from an institution who has expertise in accompanying victims to conduct counseling, therapy and advocacy for strengthening and recovery of victims of violence.  
17. Judicial body is the general court that has the authority to receive, examine, and adjudicate, as well as resolve every case submitted to it, to realize law enforcement and justice.  
18. Prosecution is the action of the public prosecutor to transfer a case to the competent district court in the manner and according to the procedures regulated by law with a request that it be examined and decided by a judge in a court session.  
19. Lawsuit is a notification accompanied by a request by an interested party to a judge to take action according to law against a person who has harmed him or her in a civil manner
20. Integrated Service Center hereinafter referred to as PPT is an institution providing services to victims of violence based at the Regional General Hospital managed jointly in the form of medical services (including medico-legal), psycho-social and legal services.  
21. Safe House is a shelter for victims, during the accompaniment process, for the safety and comfort of victims from threats and dangers of perpetrators.  
22. Standard Operational Procedure hereinafter referred to as SOP is the standard operational procedure that serves as a reference for service actions established by Regent Regulation.  
23. Community is individuals, families, groups, social organizations and or community organizations.  
24. Family is the smallest unit in society consisting of husband and wife, or husband-wife and their children, or father and his children, and mother and her children.  
25. Household is family members and relatives (grandchildren, nephews/nieces, older siblings, younger siblings, grandfathers, grandmothers, cousins and so on) and non-relatives (servants, drivers and so on) who live and eat from one kitchen and reside in one house.  
26. Regional Revenue and Expenditure Budget hereinafter referred to as APBD is the APBD of Jember Regency.  
27. State Revenue and Expenditure Budget hereinafter referred to as APBN is the annual financial plan of the State Government approved by the House of Representatives.

# CHAPTER II

## PRINCIPLES, OBJECTIVES AND SERVICE PRINCIPLES

#### Article 2

The principles of protection for women and children victims of violence are:  
1. Respect and fulfillment of victims' rights  
2. Justice and gender equality  
3. Non-discrimination  
4. Best interests of victims

#### Article 3

The objective of Protection for Women and Children Victims of Violence is to provide protection and services for the best interests of women and children victims of violence that occurs in the domestic and or public sphere.

#### Article 4

The provision of services to victims is carried out with principles including free of charge, fast, safe, empathetic, non-discriminatory, easily accessible, and confidential.

# CHAPTER III

## RIGHTS AND OBLIGATIONS OF VICTIMS

#### Article 5

Every victim has the right to:  
1. Obtain protection from the community and government as well as non-governmental institutions at local, national and international levels.
2. Obtain information about the location of complaint centers.  
3. Obtain integrated services in accordance with service principles.  
4. Make legal claims and or lawsuits through judicial bodies and or courts outside the court, and may report to the International Court
5. Obtain information about laws and regulations that protect victims
6. Obtain confidential handling
7. Obtain information and be involved in every decision-making process related to accompaniment and development of case handling.  
8. Obtain guarantees of their rights related to their status as wife, mother or child, family member, household member, and community member.  
9. Obtain accompaniment at every level of examination and during the judicial process.  
10. Obtain continuous handling up to the rehabilitation stage.  
11. Obtain protection from disclosure of identity through mass media.

#### Article 6

Every victim has the obligation to:  
1. Victims are obliged not to retaliate against perpetrators of violence;  
2. Victims are obliged to become witnesses if it does not endanger themselves and if there is a security guarantee for the victim;  
3. Victims are obliged to give perpetrators the opportunity to provide compensation to victims in addition to the punishment determined by the Court;  
4. Victims are willing to receive accompaniment;  
5. Victims together with the community are obliged to prevent the increase of other victims of violence.

# CHAPTER IV

## OBLIGATIONS AND RESPONSIBILITIES

## Part One

Obligations and Responsibilities of the Regency Government

#### Article 7

1. The Regency Government has the obligation and responsibility to implement efforts to prevent the occurrence of violence, in the form of:  
a. collecting data and information about women and children who are victims of violence as well as laws and regulations;  
b. conducting education on anti-violence values towards women and children;  
c. conducting socialization of laws and regulations related to the protection of women and children victims of violence;  
d. conducting monitoring and evaluation of the protection of women and children victims of violence.  
2. The Regency Government has the obligation and responsibility to provide and organize services for victims in the form of:  
a. establishing and ensuring the operation of integrated service institutions for victims by involving community elements;  
b. facilitating the formation of service institutions;  
c. encouraging public awareness of the importance of protection for victims.  
3. The Regency Government has the obligation and responsibility to ensure the implementation of protection for victims by taking into account the rights and obligations of parents, guardians, husbands or other persons legally responsible for the victims.  
4. The Regency Government has the obligation and responsibility to supervise the implementation of services for victims, with service standards that involve the community.  
5. The Regency Government has the obligation and responsibility to provide funds for the protection of women and children victims of violence through the Regency APBD and or other state financial sources.  
6. The Regent appoints officials to carry out the obligations and responsibilities of implementing protection for women and children victims of violence.

## Part Two

Obligations and Responsibilities of the Integrated Service Center

#### Article 8

The Management of Women and Children Protection implemented by the Integrated Service Center has the obligation to organize services in accordance with the service principles as regulated in the Regional Regulation.

## Part Three

Obligations and Responsibilities of the Community

#### Article 9

1. The community has the obligation to make efforts to provide support for the development of violence prevention programs.  
2. The community has the obligation to prepare proposals regarding the formulation and policies on protection.  
3. The community has the obligation to make efforts to protect and provide moral or material support to victims.  
4. The community has the obligation to conduct supervision and reporting of acts of violence to the competent authorities.

# CHAPTER V

## IMPLEMENTATION OF PROTECTION

## Part First

Institutional

#### Article 10

1. The implementation of protection for victims is carried out in an integrated manner within the PPT framework.  
2. The Regency Integrated Service Center receives case referrals from service institutions in the Regency area.  
3. Provisions regarding the Integrated Service Center will be regulated by Regent Regulation.

## Part second

Implementation Principles

#### Article 11

1. The principle of implementing services for victims is not charged any fees whatsoever;  
2. The implementation of services for victims is carried out quickly, safely, with empathy, non-discrimination, and easily accessible;  
3. Services for victims ensure the confidentiality of everything reported.

## Part third

Forms and Service Mechanisms

#### Article 12

The forms of services for victims organized by P3A include:

1. Medical services, in the form of treatment and recovery of physical injuries aimed at restoring the physical condition of victims carried out by medical personnel and paramedics.  
2. Medicolegal services are a form of medical services for evidentiary purposes in the legal field.  
3. Psychosocial services are services provided by companions in order to restore the traumatic condition of victims, including the provision of safe houses to protect victims from various threats and intimidation for victims and provide social support so that victims have self-confidence, strength, and independence in resolving their problems.  
4. Legal services are services to assist victims in undergoing the judicial process.  
5. Economic independence services in the form of services for skills training and providing economic access so that victims can be independent.  
6. Information services (Informed Consent), related to services and assistance

#### Article 13

Service mechanisms are organized according to Standard Operational Procedure (SOP) procedures whose guidelines will be further regulated by Regent Regulation.

# CHAPTER VI

## ASSISTANCE

#### Article 14

1. Assistance includes all integrated efforts to restore and strengthen the condition of victims, carried out by the police, prosecutors, advocates and social workers.  
2. Assistance by the police, prosecutors and advocates as referred to in paragraph (1) is in accordance with applicable legislation.  
3. The duties of Social Workers as companions are:  
a. providing sufficient information to victims about their rights;  
b. providing psychosocial services to victims so that victims feel safe and comfortable;  
c. accompanying victims during the examination and medical recovery process;  
d. accompanying victims during the medicolegal process;  
e. accompanying victims during the examination process at the Police, Prosecutor's Office and court;  
f. monitoring the interests and rights of victims in the examination process at the Police, Prosecutor's Office and Court;  
g. maintaining the privacy and confidentiality of victims from all parties who have no interest, including coverage by the mass media;  
h. coordinating with other companions;  
i. providing continuous handling up to the rehabilitation stage.

# CHAPTER VII

## FUNDING

#### Article 15

1. Funding for the implementation of protection for women and children victims of violence includes:  
a. medical services, which include doctor's examinations, treatment costs, hospital costs, and medication costs;  
b. medico legal services, which include examinations for Visum et Repertum and Visum et Psikiatrikum;  
c. psychosocial services, consisting of counseling and psychological therapy as well as safe houses (shelter);  
d. legal services;  
e. economic empowerment, in the form of services for skills training and providing economic access;  
2. Funding management is carried out through technical institutions appointed by the Regency Government which is carried out transparently and accounted for in accordance with laws and regulations.  
3. Sources of funds in the implementation of protection for women and children are sourced from the APBN, Provincial APBD, Regency APBD as well as other legal and non-binding sources.

# CHAPTER VIII

## GUIDANCE AND SUPERVISION

#### Article 16

1. Guidance on the implementation of Women and Children protection is carried out by related Agencies in accordance with their respective main duties and functions.  
2. Guidance as referred to in paragraph (1) is in the form of providing guidelines that include technical implementation of Women and Children protection.  
3. Supervision of the implementation of Women and Children protection is carried out by related agencies together with the Government's internal supervisory apparatus in accordance with Laws and Regulations.

# CHAPTER IX

## SANCTION PROVISIONS

#### Article 17

1. Every person who intentionally fails to provide protection to women and children resulting in the occurrence of violence, allows violence to occur, and or fails to report and fails to provide protection to victims, shall be subject to sanctions as stipulated in the laws and regulations.
2. Appointed officials who fail to carry out their obligations and responsibilities as stipulated in Article 7 shall be subject to administrative action and or sanctions.
3. Appointed officials who fail to carry out supervision as stipulated in Article 15 shall be subject to administrative action and or sanctions.
4. Administrative action and or sanctions as referred to in paragraph (2) and paragraph (3) shall be imposed in accordance with the laws and regulations.
5. PPT managers who carry out service duties and violate service principles shall be subject to sanctions in accordance with the laws and regulations.

# CHAPTER X

## CLOSING PROVISIONS

#### Article 18

This Regional Regulation shall come into force on the date of promulgation.

##### I. GENERAL ELUCIDATION

The State has an obligation to provide a sense of security to its citizens from threats and actions that may disturb or damage psychological, physical, sexual or economic security, this is philosophically stated in the Preamble of the 1945 Constitution that the purpose of establishing the Unitary State of the Republic of Indonesia, the first is to protect the entire nation and the homeland of Indonesia.

Since the Government of Indonesia has signed the Universal Declaration of Human Rights (UDHR 1948) and ratified CEDAW (Law Number 7 Year 1948 concerning the Elimination of All Forms of Discrimination Against Women), it is obliged to comply with these provisions. Likewise in the fourth amendment of the 1945 Constitution, Chapter XA of the 1945 Constitution concerning Human Rights, states that the enforcement and fulfillment of Human Rights is the responsibility of the state, especially the Government.

Furthermore, in the National Action Plan for the Elimination of Violence Against Women Year 1999 it is stated that Women as mothers of the nation and children as successors of the nation are creatures of God Almighty and therefore must be protected and their honor, dignity and self-esteem must be safeguarded in a reasonable and proportional manner, both legally, economically, politically, socially and culturally, without distinguishing ethnicity, religion, race and class.

From the series of provisions above, the Government is responsible for taking actions both legally, politically, economically and socially to prevent, suppress, reduce and eliminate all forms of violence against women and children because it constitutes a crime against human existence.
