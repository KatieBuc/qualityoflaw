# CHAPTER II

FORMS OF VIOLENCE

#### Article 5

Forms of Violence against Women and Children, include: a. Physical violence; a0 o 0

Psychological violence;

Sexual violence;

Domestic neglect; Exploitation;

Human trafficking; and/or Other violence.

#### Article 6

(1) Physical violence as referred to in Article 5 letter a, constitutes acts that result in pain accompanied by injury, wounds or disability to the body, miscarriage, fainting and/or causing death of Women and/or Children.

(2) Physical violence as referred to in paragraph (1) results in: the victim suffering from serious illness or injury; a. death of the victim; and/or b. causing illness or impediment to C. carrying out work, position or livelihood or daily activities.

#### Article 7

Psychological violence as referred to in Article 5 letter b, constitutes acts that result in fear, loss of self-confidence, loss of ability to act, a sense of helplessness and/or psychological suffering to Women and/or Children.

#### Article 8

(1) Sexual violence as referred to in Article 5 letter c, includes: non-physical sexual harassment; physical sexual harassment;

F®m o oo contraceptive coercion; forced sterilization; forced marriage; sexual torture; sexual exploitation; sexual slavery; and electronic-based sexual violence.

(2) In addition to sexual violence as referred to in paragraph (1), sexual violence also includes: rape; a. indecent acts; b. intercourse with Children, indecent acts c. against Children, and/or sexual exploitation of Children; acts violating decency that are contrary to the will of the Victim;

e. pornography involving Children or pornography that explicitly contains Violence and sexual exploitation,

f. forced prostitution,

g. criminal acts of human trafficking aimed at sexual exploitation,

h. sexual violence within the domestic sphere; i. criminal acts of money laundering where the predicate offense constitutes sexual violence crimes; and

j. other criminal acts expressly stated as sexual violence crimes as regulated in the provisions of laws and regulations.

#### Article 9

Neglect within the household as referred to in Article 5 letter d, includes:

- a. acts of deliberately neglecting a woman within the scope of the household, whereas according to the law applicable to them or due to agreement or contract they are obligated to provide livelihood and care to the woman;
- b. acts that result in the improper fulfillment of the needs of children, whether physical, mental, spiritual or social, committed by parents, guardians, or other parties responsible for their care;
- c. acts of deliberately neglecting to care for or take care of children as appropriate, committed by parents, guardians, or any other party responsible for their care; and
- d. deliberate neglect of children who still require care by their biological mother to become Indonesian Migrant Workers abroad.

#### Article 10

(1) Exploitation as referred to in Article 5 letter e, includes:

- a. acts of economic or sexual exploitation with the intention of benefiting oneself or others;
- b. acts with or without the consent of the victim which include but are not limited to prostitution, forced labor or services, slavery or similar practices, oppression, extortion, exploitation of physical, sexual, reproductive organs, or unlawfully transferring or transplanting organs and/or body tissues or exploiting the labor or ability of a person by another party to obtain material or immaterial benefits; and
- c. acts of exploiting children in horse racing activities as jockeys by disregarding children's rights and/or with the intention of exploiting the labor or ability of children to obtain material or immaterial benefits.

#### Article 12

CONCERNING

IMPLEMENTATION OF PROTECTION FOR WOMEN AND CHILDREN FROM ACTS OF VIOLENCE

I. GENERAL

Acts of violence against women and children have shown an increasingly rising trend. Triggering factors such as economic, social, cultural and social environment are becoming more complex along with the influence of technological development. Concrete steps are needed to provide protection by all elements of Citizens as a unity of society, as well as the role of the Regional Government as a protector for its citizens with various programs to prevent acts of violence against women and children. One of the efforts to prevent violence against women is carried out through women's empowerment and optimization of existing potential, so that it is expected to be able to improve and develop themselves to play a role and be protected from potential acts of violence. This condition will strengthen the form of appreciation and respect for women while maintaining their role and nature as a woman to play a role in educating and supporting the welfare and happiness of their family.

Efforts to prevent violence against children are a mutual obligation for parents, families, communities and the private sector in a holistic and inseparable manner. Community services are the frontline when there is potential violence against children. The mechanism for preventing violence and protecting child victims of violence involves multiple sectors coordinated by the Regional Government. The said efforts are carried out with the direction of providing social rehabilitation for child victims of violence, reuniting child victims of violence with their families and/or environment, and increasing the empowerment of child victims of violence.

In connection with the foregoing, to realize the provision of protection efforts for women and children victims of violence as well as for legal certainty in the implementation of women's empowerment and protection of child victims of violence in the Regional area, it is necessary to regulate this in a Regional Regulation.
