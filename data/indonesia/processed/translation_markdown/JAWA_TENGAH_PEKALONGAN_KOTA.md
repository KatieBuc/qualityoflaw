BY THE GRACE OF ALMIGHTY GOD

MAYOR OF PEKALONGAN,

Considering : a. that all forms of violence, especially gender-based violence and violence against children are violations of human rights and crimes against human dignity as well as forms of discrimination;

b. that victims of gender-based violence and children must receive protection, both from the Regional Government, related agencies and/or the community, so that the community is protected and free from violence and/or threats of violence in the household and society;

c. that pursuant to Law Number 23 of 2002 concerning Child Protection, as amended by Law Number 35 of 2014 concerning Amendments to Law Number 23 of 2002 concerning Child Protection and Law Number 23 of 2004 concerning Elimination of Domestic Violence, the Regional Government, related agencies, and/or the community are obligated to undertake prevention, protection and recovery efforts for victims of gender-based violence and children;

d. that based on the considerations referred to in letters a, b, and c, it is necessary to stipulate a Regional Regulation concerning Implementation of Protection for Victims of Gender-Based Violence and Children;

# CHAPTER I

GENERAL PROVISIONS

#### Article 1

In this Regional Regulation, what is meant by:

- 1. Region is the City of Pekalongan.
- 2. Regional Government is the Mayor as an element of regional government administration who leads the implementation of government affairs that fall under the authority of the autonomous region. 3. Mayor is the Mayor of Pekalongan.
- 4. Regional People's Representative Council, hereinafter abbreviated as DPRD, is the regional people's representative institution of Pekalongan City which is positioned as an element of regional government administration.
- 5. Regional Apparatus Work Unit, hereinafter abbreviated as SKPD, is an auxiliary element of the Mayor and DPRD in administering Government Affairs that fall under the authority of the Region.
- 6. Child is a person who has not yet reached the age of 18 (eighteen) years, including a child still in the womb.
- 7. Prevention is activities carried out to prevent the occurrence of acts of violence against women and children. 8. Gender is a concept that refers to the roles and responsibilities of men and women that occur as a result of and can be changed by the social and cultural circumstances of society. 9. Protection is all efforts aimed at providing a sense of security and fulfilling the rights of victims carried out by family parties, integrated services, advocates, social institutions, police, prosecutors, courts, or other parties either temporarily or based on court decisions.
- 10. Implementation of Protection is a series of activities aimed at preventing the occurrence of violence, providing protection and recovery services and social reintegration, conducting coordination and cooperation, and increasing community participation carried out by the relevant SKPD.
- 11. Violence is any act that results in or causes misery and suffering whether physical, sexual, psychological including neglect, threats of certain actions, coercion or arbitrary deprivation of freedom, whether occurring in public or in private life.
- 12. Gender-Based Violence is any form of restriction, exclusion, differentiation and all forms of treatment carried out on the basis of sex and aimed at reducing, eliminating the recognition, enjoyment or use of human rights, the consequences of which are and are not limited to physical, sexual, psychological and economic violence.
- 13. Violence Against Children is any form of restriction, differentiation, exclusion and all forms of treatment carried out against children, the consequences of which are and are not limited to physical, sexual, psychological, and economic violence.
- 14. Victims of Gender-based violence are people who, because of their sex, experience physical, psychological, economic, social, sexual suffering, and other losses caused by state policies, acts of violence and/or threats of violence within the scope of the household and community.
- 15. Physical violence is any act that results in pain, injury, wounds or disability to someone's body, miscarriage or fainting and/or causing death.
- 16. Psychological violence is an act that results in fear, loss of self-confidence, loss of ability to act, distrust or severe psychological suffering to a person.
- 17. Sexual violence is any act in the form of sexual harassment, forced sexual relations, either unnaturally or unwillingly with others for commercial purposes and/or certain purposes.
- 18. Victims of acts of violence are individuals or groups of people who experience physical, mental or emotional suffering, economic loss, or experience neglect, reduction or deprivation of their basic rights, as a result of violations of human rights (HAM), or criminal acts and acts of violence committed either by state apparatus or by the state or regional government apparatus or by individuals.
- 19. Service is immediate activities and actions carried out for the strengthening and recovery of victims of violence.
- 20. Assistance is activities and actions carried out by assistants during the service process.
- 21. Assistant is a person or representative from an institution who has the expertise to provide assistance.
- 22. Institution is an agency/service/body/office within the scope of regional government and/or non-governmental organization (NGO) that provides assistance.
- 23. Repatriation is an effort to return victims of acts of violence to their families, substitute families, or communities that can provide protection and fulfillment of their needs.
- 24. Rehabilitation is the recovery of victims from psychosocial disorders and the restoration of normal social functioning, both in the family and in society.
- 25. Social reintegration is an effort to reunite victims with their families, communities, institutions, or other social environments that can provide protection.
- 26. Regional Action Plan is the foundation and guideline for relevant agencies, vertical agencies, and the community, in the context of implementing prevention and protection activities for victims of gender-based violence and children.
- 27. Safe house is temporary accommodation, provided to give protection to victims in accordance with predetermined standards.
- 28. Community is individuals, families, groups, social organizations and/or community organizations.
- 29. Family is the smallest unit in society consisting of husband and wife, or husband and wife and their children, or father and his children, or mother and her children, or blood relatives in a direct line upwards or downwards up to the third degree.
- 30. Parents are biological father and/or mother, or step father and/or step mother, or adoptive father and/or adoptive mother.

# CHAPTER II

PRINCIPLES AND OBJECTIVES

## Part One

Principles

#### Article 2

Protection of victims of gender-based violence and children is based on Pancasila and founded on the 1945 Constitution, as well as basic principles that include:

- a. non-discrimination;
- b. best interests of victims;
- c. gender justice and equality;
- d. victim protection;
- e. survival, and growth and development of children; f. respect for children's opinions;
- g. openness;
- h. integration;
- i. empowerment; and
- j. victim confidentiality.

#### Article 3

(1) The objectives of implementing protection for victims of gender-based violence and children are:

- a. to prevent all forms of gender-based violence and violence against children, occurring within the scope of the household and/or community;
- b. to provide protection;
- c. to provide legal assistance;
- d. to seek recovery and social reintegration; and/or e. to increase community participation.
  - (2) The objectives of implementing protection for victims of gender-based violence and children as referred to in paragraph (1), include aspects of:
- a. prevention;
- b. service and assistance;
- c. reunification; and
- d. empowerment.

# CHAPTER III

RIGHTS OF VICTIMS

#### Article 4

Every victim of gender-based violence and child victim of violence has the right to receive:

- a. protection of personal, family, and property security, as well as freedom from threats related to information that will be, is being, or has been provided;
- b. participation in the process of selecting and determining forms of protection and security support;
- c. information regarding case developments and court decisions; d. services that are fast, appropriate, comfortable, and according to needs; e. recovery and social reintegration;
- f. legal, psychological assistance, spiritual guidance, economic, social and interpreter.

#### Article 5

Child victims of acts of violence, in addition to receiving the rights as referred to in Article 4, also receive special rights, as follows:

- a. right to respect;
- b. right to basic services;
- c. right to equal protection;
- d. right to be free from various stigma; and
- e. right to obtain freedom
- f. right fully to survival, growth and development.

# CHAPTER IV

OBLIGATIONS AND RESPONSIBILITIES

#### Article 6

Obligations and responsibilities in the implementation of protection for victims of gender-based violence and children in the Region constitute joint responsibility between:

- a. Regional Government;
- b. community;
- c. family and/or parents.

#### Article 7

(1) Obligations and responsibilities of the Regional Government as referred to in Article 6 letter a, include:

- a. implementing protection policies against gender-based violence and children established by the government; b. establishing policies, programs, and protection activities against gender-based violence and children; c. conducting cooperation in the implementation of protection for women and children from acts of violence;
- d. providing support for facilities and infrastructure for the implementation of protection against gender-based violence and children;
- e. allocating budget for the implementation of protection against gender-based violence and children in accordance with regional financial capacity; and
- f. fostering and supervising the implementation of protection against gender-based violence and children. (2) In order to implement the obligations and responsibilities as referred to in paragraph (1), the regional government establishes programs and action activities for gender and child protection in 1 (one) Regional Action Plan as the basis for SKPD in implementing protection against gender-based violence and children.
  - (3) Further provisions regarding the Regional Action Plan as referred to in paragraph (2), shall be regulated by Mayor Regulation.

#### Article 8

(1) Obligations and responsibilities of the community as referred to in Article 6 letter b, shall be implemented in the form of community participation.

(2) Forms of community participation as referred to in paragraph (1), include:

- a. preventing the occurrence of gender-based violence and children;
- b. providing information and/or reporting gender-based violence and children to law enforcement or authorized parties; and/or
- c. participating in providing assistance, protection or handling of victims of acts of violence.
  - (3) Forms of community participation as referred to in paragraph (2), shall be implemented responsibly in accordance with the provisions of laws and regulations.

#### Article 9

Obligations and responsibilities of family and/or parents as referred to in Article 6 letter c to prevent all forms of violence and protect victims of gender-based violence and children as family members in accordance with their respective roles.

# CHAPTER V

DUTIES AND AUTHORITIES OF REGIONAL GOVERNMENT

#### Article 10

(1) The Regional Government is tasked with implementing efforts to prevent the occurrence of gender-based violence and children, in the form of: a. collecting data and information on acts of gender-based violence and children, and laws and regulations; b. conducting socialization of laws and regulations related to the implementation of protection against gender-based violence and children;

c. conducting education on anti-violence values against gender and children; and

d. conducting monitoring and evaluation of the implementation of protection against gender-based violence and children.

(2) The Regional Government in anticipating the occurrence of gender-based violence and children, has the authority to provide and organize services for victims in the form of: a. providing and facilitating the establishment of integrated services for victims of acts of violence by involving institutions and community elements;

b. encouraging community awareness of the importance of protection for victims; and

c. conducting community empowerment for the prevention of acts of violence.

d. actively conducting efforts to handle victims of gender-based violence and children.

(3) The Regional Government in implementing the obligations as referred to in paragraph (1) and paragraph (2) must pay attention to the rights and obligations of parents, guardians, husbands, wives, or other persons who are legally responsible to the victim.

# CHAPTER VI

IMPLEMENTATION OF PROTECTION

## Part One

Institution

#### Article 11

In order to provide services and protection for acts of gender-based violence and children, the Regional Government establishes the Women, Children and Adolescents Protection Agency (LP PAR) as an integrated service center for victims of gender-based violence and children.

## Part Two

Form and Mechanism of Prevention

#### Article 12

Forms of prevention of the occurrence of gender-based violence and children carried out by the Women, Children and Adolescents Protection Agency, may be implemented through:

- a. socialization activities of laws and regulations to the community; and
- b. training of members of the Women, Children and Adolescents Protection Agency related to the implementation of their duties in preventing acts of gender-based violence and children.

#### Article 13

(1) The Women, Children and Adolescents Protection Agency in implementing its duties and functions may coordinate with the Sub-district Women, Children and Adolescents Protection Agency and competent parties in making efforts to prevent the occurrence of criminal acts of gender-based violence and children.

(2) Further provisions regarding the mechanism for implementing prevention duties as referred to in paragraph (1) shall be regulated by Mayor Regulation.

## Part Three

Prevention of Acts of Violence

#### Article 14

(1) The Regional Government conducts empowerment and awareness to families, parents, and the community by providing information, guidance and/or counseling to prevent the occurrence of criminal acts of gender-based violence and children.

(2) In addition to empowerment and awareness as referred to in paragraph (1), the Regional Government makes the following efforts:

- a. increasing the number and quality of education both formal and non-formal and informal;
- b. opening accessibility to obtain education, training, funding, income enhancement and social services;
- c. opening employment opportunities;
- d. building community participation and awareness to implement prevention and protection against gender-based violence and children;
- e. building and providing a complete and easily accessible information system;
- f. building cooperation networks with law enforcement officials, government apparatus, universities and various non-governmental organizations that work and/or care about gender and children; and
- g. opening complaint posts for protection against gender-based violence and children.

#### Article 15

(1) Efforts to prevent the occurrence of gender-based violence and children as referred to in Article 14, shall be implemented by SKPD whose duties and functions are in the fields of:

- a. social;
- b. health;
- c. education;
- d. manpower;
- e. women's empowerment and child protection; f. peace and order; and
- g. planning.
  - (2) Prevention of acts of violence by SKPD as referred to in paragraph (1), shall be implemented in an integrated and sustainable manner based on the Regional Action Plan.

## Part Four

Services and Assistance

#### Article 16

Forms of services and assistance to victims of acts of gender-based violence and children organized by the Women, Children and Adolescents Protection Agency, include: a. medical;

b. medicolegal;

c. psychosocial;

d. legal;

e. economic independence

## Part Five

Principles of Services and Assistance

#### Article 17

The provision of services and assistance to victims of gender-based and child violence acts is carried out with the principles of: a. fast, safe, and empathetic;

b. guarantee of confidentiality;

c. easily accessible; and

d. no fees charged.

## Part Six

Services for Victims of Acts of Violence

#### Article 18

(1) The form of services is implemented in accordance with minimum service standards established by the government and carried out by SKPD whose duties and functions are in the fields of:

- a. social affairs;
- b. health;
- c. gender-based empowerment and child protection; d. welfare;
- e. education;
- f. population;
- g. labor.
  - (2) In carrying out the duties and functions as referred to in paragraph (1), the Regional Government cooperates with Government agencies, Provincial Government, other Regency/City Governments, and the community.

#### Article 19

The Regional Government and/or community may establish recovery houses or safe houses.

## Part Seven

Reunification

#### Article 20

(1) The form of Reunification provided to victims of gender-based and child violence is as follows:

- a. repatriation;
- b. social reintegration.
  - (2) Repatriation services as referred to in paragraph (1) letter a include:
- a. conducting coordination with relevant agencies;
- b. carrying out the victim repatriation process; and
- c. providing protection to the victim's destination. (3) Social reintegration services as referred to in paragraph (1) letter b include:
- a. assistance in the process of reuniting with the victim's family;
- b. providing information to the local community for the victim's reunification with their environment;
- c. providing assurance that the family and community are ready to accept the victim back; and
- d. implementing economic empowerment.

## Part Eight

Empowerment of Victims of Acts of Violence

#### Article 21

(1) The Regional Government is obligated to conduct empowerment of victims of acts of violence.

(2) Empowerment of victims of acts of violence as referred to in paragraph (1) is carried out by the Women, Child and Adolescent Protection Institution.

(3) In conducting empowerment of victims of acts of violence as referred to in paragraph (1), the Regional Government prepares empowerment programs in the economic field, which include:

- a. procuring the necessary needs for entrepreneurship training for victims of acts of violence, in order to improve business knowledge, attitudes, and skills;
- b. facilitating the implementation of various job training and skills training;
- c. providing assistance in developing productive economic enterprises;
- d. exploring cooperation with small, medium and large enterprises, as well as financial institutions to develop businesses of victims of acts of violence;
- e. endeavoring to facilitate the provision of capital for victims of acts of violence; and
- f. expanding information access and promoting product results of victims of acts of violence.
  - (4) In conducting empowerment of victims of acts of violence as referred to in paragraph (1), the Regional Government may establish cooperation with job training institutions organized by the private sector.

#### Article 22

(1) Empowerment of victims of acts of violence as referred to in Article 21 is carried out by providing training to improve capabilities, skills, and independence.

(2) Training as referred to in paragraph (1) is carried out through: a. on-the-job training;

b. pre-placement training; and

c. job-ready training.

#### Article 23

Further provisions regarding the establishment, duties and functions of the Women, Child and Adolescent Protection Institution and the implementation of protection for victims of gender-based and child violence acts are regulated by Mayor Regulation.

# CHAPTER VII

COOPERATION AND PARTNERSHIP

## Part One

Cooperation

#### Article 24

(1) In order to achieve the objectives of implementing protection against gender-based and child violence acts as referred to in Article 3, the Regional Government cooperates with: a. the Government;

b. the Provincial Government;

c. other regency/city Governments; and

d. the community.

(2) Cooperation as referred to in paragraph (1) includes: a. exchange of data and information;

b. rehabilitation of victims of acts of violence;

c. repatriation and social reintegration; and

d. provision of evidence and witnesses, and followed up in accordance with applicable law.

(3) Cooperation as referred to in paragraph (1) and paragraph (2) is implemented in accordance with laws and regulations.

## Part Two

Partnership

#### Article 25

(1) The Regional Government establishes partnerships with stakeholders in implementing protection against gender-based and child violence acts.

(2) Partnership as referred to in paragraph (1) is carried out through: a. notification of job opportunity information for victims of gender-based and child violence acts;

b. education and training for victims of acts of violence; c. educational assistance for victims of acts of violence who are withdrawn from their education; and

d. fostering and improving the economic independence of victims of acts of violence.

(3) Partnership as referred to in paragraph (1) and paragraph (2) is set forth in the form of an agreement.

# CHAPTER VIII

GUIDANCE AND SUPERVISION

#### Article 26

(1) The Regional Government is obligated to conduct guidance and supervision of the implementation of protection against gender-based and child violence acts.

(2) Guidance as referred to in paragraph (1) includes: a. minimum service standards (SPM);

b. technical guidance and training;

c. provision of facilities;

d. monitoring; and

e. evaluation.

(3) Supervision as referred to in paragraph (1) is carried out with the principles of:

- a. professionalism;
- b. transparency; and
- c. accountability.
  - (4) Further provisions regarding procedures for guidance and supervision by the Regional Government as referred to in paragraph (2) and paragraph (3) are regulated by Mayor Regulation.

#### Article 27

The community may conduct supervision of the implementation of protection against gender-based and child violence acts organized by the Regional Government through the mechanism of conveying aspirations to the Mayor or DPRD.

# CHAPTER IX

COMMUNITY PARTICIPATION

#### Article 28

(1) The community may participate in efforts to implement protection against gender-based and child violence acts. (2) Community participation as referred to in paragraph (1) may be carried out by individuals or organizations.

(3) Community participation in the implementation of protection for Victims of Gender-Based and Child Violence may be carried out through:

- a. providing protection for victims of gender-based and child violence;
- b. providing emergency assistance;
- c. providing advocacy to victims and/or the community regarding handling cases of gender-based and child violence;
- d. assisting the process of submitting applications for protection determination;
- e. conveying information to authorized officials related to cases of gender-based and child violence; f. fostering local wisdom with the principles of non-discrimination and the best interests of victims in handling gender-based and child violence;
- g. organizing strengthening of community groups in handling gender-based and child violence; h. disseminating information about laws and regulations related to gender-based and child violence.

# CHAPTER X

REPORTING

#### Article 29

(1) SKPD is obligated to report the implementation of protection against gender-based and child violence acts to the Mayor.

(2) Reporting as referred to in paragraph (1) is submitted in writing, including:

- a. administration;
- b. finance;
- c. services; and
- d. performance.
  - (3) Submission of written reports on the recapitulation of reported cases is carried out at least once every 3 (three) months.

# CHAPTER XI

SOURCE OF FUNDS

#### Article 30

Funds for the implementation of protection against gender-based and child violence, are sourced from:

- a. Regional Revenue and Expenditure Budget; and
- b. other lawful sources in accordance with the provisions of laws and regulations.

CLOSING PROVISIONS

I. GENERAL

The State has an obligation to provide a sense of security to its citizens from threats and actions that may disturb and damage mental, physical, sexual and economic security. This is philosophically stated in the preamble of the 1945 Constitution, that the purpose of the establishment of the Unitary State of the Republic of Indonesia is to protect the entire Nation and the entire Homeland of Indonesia. Since the Government of Indonesia has signed the Universal Declaration of Human Rights (UDHR 1948) and ratified CEDAW (Law Number 7 of 1984 concerning the Elimination of All Forms of Discrimination against Women), it is obliged to fulfill those provisions.

Human Rights state that, the enforcement and fulfillment of Human Rights is the responsibility of the State, especially the Government. Through Law Number 23 of 2002 concerning Child Protection, the government recognizes the existence of children's rights. The human rights inherent in children, include basic rights as human beings, namely the Right to Life, the Right to Growth and Development, the Right to Protection and the Right to Participation. To fulfill the Right to life, children need food in sufficient quantities, healthy and nutritious, as well as optimal access to health. Protection from violence that threatens their safety and health must be provided from an early age. Protection is needed to prevent physical, mental and sexual violence and exploitation. To fulfill the right to growth and development, children need space to play, exercise, education that is appropriate to their physical and mental development.

In the fulfillment of children's rights, every government administrator, community and parents must understand and care about children's rights. The existence of legal instruments and law enforcement officers who advocate for the interests of children is necessary for this protection effort. Laws and regulations that are based on the principle of advocating the best interests of the child (the best interest of the child) are necessary to realize legal protection. Furthermore, it is not uncommon to find cases of women and children experiencing problems of physical, psychological, sexual and social violence. For example, women and children victims of persecution, neglect, eviction and mistreatment (abuse) by parents or others. For women and children experiencing these problems, the government must provide a Safe House (children protection home) or Integrated Service Center (PPT) for women and children victims of violence. The Safe House or PPT is a safe place and friendly in nature for women and children. The PPT is equipped with companions from the elements of educators, doctors, psychologists, ulama/clergy and networked with lawyers for women and children as well as the RPK Unit at the Police.

The fate of women and children victims of violence must be given attention by the government. The high number of cases of violence against women and children is often caused by factors that develop in society, such as low levels of economy, education, environment that is in the industrial sector. Therefore, victims of such violence need to receive protection in accordance with the principles of justice, truth, legal certainty, equality and upholding human rights. For the empowerment of victims of violence, regional governments can establish an Integrated Service Center for Women and Children Empowerment (P2TP2A) as a center for the empowerment of women victims of violence that specifically has the main tasks and functions for the empowerment of women and children victims of violence. From the above framework, the Government is responsible for taking actions legally, politically, economically and socially to prevent, suppress, reduce, and eliminate all forms of violence against women and children. From these various efforts, a legal framework will be realized in the implementation of protection for victims of gender-based and child violence.

#### Article 16

In addition to victims of gender-based violence and children, the forms of services and assistance organized by LP-PAR are also provided to Children in Conflict with the Law. In legal literature, ABH states that Children in conflict with the law are children who have reached the age of 12 (twelve) years but have not reached the age of 18 (eighteen) years and are not yet married:

- a. Who are suspected, accused, indicted, or sentenced for committing a criminal offense;
- b. Who become victims of a criminal offense or who see and/or hear directly the occurrence of a criminal offense.

Children in conflict with the law can also be referred to as children who are forced to have contact with the criminal justice system because:

- a. They are suspected, indicted, or declared proven guilty of violating the law
- b. They have become victims as a result of acts of legal violation committed by a person/group of persons/institution/state against them; or
- c. They have seen, heard, felt, or known of an event of legal violation.

There are two categories of child behavior that cause them to be in conflict with the law:

- a. Status Offence, namely child delinquency behavior which if committed by an adult would not be considered a crime, for example: skipping school, running away from home, etc.
- b. Juvunile Delequency, namely child delinquency behavior which if committed by an adult would be considered a crime or criminal act, for example; robbery, rape, sexual harassment, etc. Therefore, if viewed from its scope, children in conflict with the law can be divided into:
- a. Perpetrators or suspects of criminal acts;
- b. Victims of criminal acts;
- c. Witnesses to a criminal act. As a child, the legal mechanism and sense of justice in society must be placed within a framework that constructively encourages their physical and psychological development.

The forms of services and assistance organized by LP-PAR are also provided to women in conflict with the law.

Legal protection for women's interests in playing their role in society requires the preparation of various laws and regulations, and the consistent enforcement thereof, as well as socialization activities to raise awareness of their rights and obligations. In addition, non-legal support is needed, because law is only one means of social control. Action agendas from various components of society in an integrative manner need to and must be realized immediately.

In addition, within the framework of women's empowerment and protection, there is a need for legal, psychological, social and economic assistance when women are faced with legal processes.
