# CHAPTER I

GENERAL PROVISIONS

## Part One

Definition, Meaning and Terminology

#### Article 1

In this Regional Regulation, what is meant by:

- 1. Regency is Musi Banyuasin Regency.
- 2. Regency Government is the Government of Musi Banyuasin Regency. 3. Regent is the Regent of Musi Banyuasin.
- 4. Regional Apparatus hereinafter abbreviated as PD is the Regional Apparatus within the Government of Musi Banyuasin Regency.
- 5. Office of Women Empowerment and Child Protection hereinafter abbreviated as DPPPA is the Regional Apparatus that administers Women Empowerment and Child Protection affairs in Musi Banyuasin Regency.
- 6. Protection of Women is all efforts to guarantee and protect the rights of Women so that they can live healthily and participate optimally in accordance with human dignity and honor and receive protection from neglect, violence and discrimination, whether in the form of preventive, repressive or post-remedial (assistance and treatment) protection.
- 7. Child Protection is all activities to guarantee and protect Children and their rights so that they can live, grow, develop, and participate optimally in accordance with human dignity and honor, and receive protection from violence and discrimination.
- 8. Child is a person who has not reached the age of 18 (eighteen) years, including a Child still in the womb.
- 9. Child with Disabilities is a Child who has physical, mental, intellectual, or sensory limitations for a long period of time who in interacting with the environment and community attitudes may encounter obstacles that make it difficult to participate fully and effectively based on equal rights.

I

10.Child with Excellence is a Child who has extraordinary intelligence or has potential and/or special talents not limited to intellectual abilities, but also in other fields.

11. Prevention is direct efforts carried out by the Regency Government, community and parents and/or family to prevent violations of Women's rights and Children's Rights.

12. Children's Forum is a forum for Children's participation formed with the aim of facilitating the involvement of Children in decision-making processes and enjoying changes related to their lives either directly or indirectly, which is implemented with the consent and will of all Children based on awareness and understanding, in accordance with age and level of maturity of thinking.

13. Child-Friendly Regency hereinafter abbreviated as KLA is a Regency/City that has a child rights-based development system through the integration of government, community and business commitments and resources that are planned comprehensively and sustainably in policies, programs and activities to ensure the fulfillment of Children's rights.

14. Abandoned Child is a Child whose needs are not adequately met, whether physical, mental, spiritual, or social.

15. Women victims of violence are Women who experience actions that result in misery or suffering physically, sexually or psychologically, including threats of certain actions, coercion or arbitrary deprivation of liberty, whether occurring in the public sphere or in private life.

16.Child victims of violence are Children who experience actions that result in misery or suffering physically, mentally, sexually, psychologically or neglect that threatens life, body and degrades human dignity.

17. Human trafficking is the act of recruiting, transporting, harboring, sending, transferring, or receiving a person by threat of violence, use of violence, kidnapping, confinement, forgery, fraud, abuse of power or vulnerable position, debt bondage or giving payment or benefits, so as to obtain consent from the person who holds control over that other person, whether carried out within a Country or between Countries, for the purpose of exploitation or resulting in the person being exploited.

18. Complaint handling is a series of actions taken by integrated service providers to follow up on reports of acts of violence against Women and Children, submitted by victims, families or the community.

19.Social rehabilitation is a service aimed at restoring and developing the ability of a person experiencing social dysfunction so that they can perform their social functions properly.

20. Law enforcement is the action of officials authorized by the state to implement laws and regulations. 21. Legal aid is legal services provided by legal assistants and advocates to carry out the process of accompanying witnesses and/or victims of violence against Women and Children that are gender sensitive.

22. Repatriation is an effort to return Women and Child victims of violence from abroad to the debarkation point/entry point, or from the receiving area to the area of origin.

23. Social reintegration is an effort to reunite victims with family, substitute family, or community that can provide protection and fulfillment of needs for victims.

I

24. Safe house is a temporary shelter that aims to provide protection and a sense of security to victims of violence according to established standards.

25. Shelter is a forum for guidance and welfare services for street Children that carries out social assistance/guidance activities, maintenance, care, education, and health, skills guidance in order to ensure that Children do not engage in activities on the streets so they can grow and develop properly.

I

26. Spiritual guidance is an effort to restore the psyche through strengthening the religion adhered to by the victim.

27. Gender responsive is consistent and systematic attention to the differences between Women and men in society accompanied by efforts to eliminate structural and cultural barriers in achieving gender equality.

28. Gender Sensitivity or Gender Awareness is the ability to recognize power relation gaps between Women and men in the family and in the community, the impact of the division of labor based on gender on Women and men, and the experiences, problems, needs, interests and aspirations of Women and men are different.

29. Public service provider is every state institution, corporation, independent institution formed based on Law for public service activities, and other legal entities formed solely for public service activities.

30. Community Organization is an organization established and formed by the community voluntarily based on common aspirations, will, needs, interests, activities and goals to participate in development for the achievement of the objectives of the Unitary State of the Republic of Indonesia based on Pancasila.

31. Parents are biological father and/or mother, or step father and/or mother, or adoptive father and/or mother.

32. Family is the smallest unit in society consisting of husband and wife or husband, wife and their Children, or father and his Children, or mother and her Children, or blood relatives in a direct line upward or downward up to the third degree.

33. Integrated Service Center for Women Empowerment and Child Protection, hereinafter abbreviated as P2TP2A is an integrated service center in efforts to empower Women in various fields of development, as well as protection of Women and Children from various types of discrimination and acts of violence, including human trafficking, which is formed by the government or community-based.

## Part Two

Definitions, Meanings and Terminology

#### Article 2

(1) The implementation of the Protection of Women is based on the principles of:

- a. the best interests of Women;
- b. gender justice and gender equality;
- c. local wisdom; and
- d. legal certainty.
  - (2) The implementation of the Protection of Children is based on the principles of: a. non-discrimination;
- b. the best interests of the Child;
- c. the right to live, survive, and develop; d. respect for the Child's opinion; and
- e. legal certainty.

#### Article 3

The regulation of the Protection of Women and Children aims to:

- a. serve as a guideline for the Regency Government in planning, policies and strategies for the Protection of Women and Children;
- b. protect Women and Children from actions, decisions and policies that violate the rights of Women and Children;
- c. prevent violations of the Rights of Women and Children; d. increase empowerment of Women and Children; e. enhance the role and value of local wisdom in the Protection of Women and Children; and
- f. enhance the role of Regional Agencies and non-Government entities in the Regency in efforts to Protect Women and Children.

#### Article 4

The scope of regulation for the Protection of Women and Children includes: a. prevention;

b. implementation of the Protection of the Rights of Women and Children; c. handling;

d. empowerment; and

e. guidance and supervision.

# CHAPTER II

RIGHTS OF WOMEN AND RIGHTS OF CHILDREN

## Part One

Rights of Women

Article 5

Every Woman has the right to:

- a. obtain protection from discriminatory actions;
- b. obtain protection from economic exploitation and sexual exploitation; c. obtain protection from physical, psychological, sexual violence, and neglect;
- d. obtain decent work according to her capabilities; e. obtain work leave while receiving wages/salary in accordance with laws and regulations;
- f. obtain education and instruction in all types, levels and pathways of education;

I

g. receive special protection in the performance of work or profession against matters that may threaten her safety and/or health in relation to Women's reproductive function; and

h. ownership and management of joint property in marriage in accordance with the provisions of Laws and Regulations.

## Part Two

Rights of Children

#### Article 6

(1) Every Child has the right to:

- a. be able to live, grow, develop, and participate reasonably in accordance with human dignity and worth, and receive protection from violence and discrimination;
- b. obtain a name as personal identity and citizenship status; c. worship according to their religion, think, and express themselves in accordance with their level of intelligence and age under the guidance of Parents or Guardians; d. obtain education and instruction in the context of personal development and their level of intelligence according to interests and talents and receive protection in educational institutions from sexual crimes and Violence committed by educators, educational personnel, fellow students, and/or other parties;
- e. obtain protection from being subjected to persecution, torture, or imposition of inhumane punishment;
- f. obtain freedom in accordance with law and arrest, detention, or criminal imprisonment of Children is only carried out if in accordance with applicable law and can only be carried out as a last resort;
- g. be cared for by their own Parents, unless there are reasons and/or valid legal provisions showing that the separation is in the best interests of the Child and is a last consideration;
- h. obtain protection from abuse in political activities, involvement in armed conflict, involvement in social unrest, involvement in events containing elements of Violence, involvement in warfare and, sexual crimes;
- i. participate, express opinions and think in accordance with their conscience and religion;
  - j. receive oral or written information in accordance with the stages of age and development of the Child;
- k. associate and assemble; and
  - l. rest, play, recreate, create, and produce cultural arts, obtain play facilities that meet health and safety requirements and are adapted to the age of the Child, the Child's level of ability, and their environment so as not to hinder and disturb. (2) Every child who is deprived of their freedom has the right to: a. be treated humanely; b. be placed separately from adults; c. obtain legal assistance or other assistance effectively at every stage of applicable legal remedies; and d. defend themselves and obtain justice before an objective and impartial Children's court in a closed session to the public. (3) Every child who is a victim or perpetrator of sexual violence or who is in conflict with the law has the right to confidentiality.

(4) Every Child with Disabilities in addition to receiving the rights referred to in paragraph (1) is also entitled to obtain: a. special education/special education; b. rehabilitation;

c. social assistance; and d. maintenance of social welfare standards.

(5) Every Child who has excellence has the right to receive special education.

# CHAPTER III

OBLIGATIONS AND RESPONSIBILITIES

## Part One

Regency Government

#### Article 7

(1) The Regency Government in efforts to protect Women is obligated and responsible to:

- a. guarantee Women's Rights without discrimination in accordance with the provisions of Laws and Regulations;
- b. establish and implement policy programs and activities for the protection of Women;
- c. cooperate with the community and/or Community Organizations in implementing the Protection of Women in accordance with the provisions of Laws and Regulations;
- d. conduct rehabilitation and empowerment of Women who are victims of acts of violence;
- e. provide special protection to Women victims of acts of violence and human trafficking; and
- f. take preventive action in order to prevent violations of Women's rights.
  - (2) The responsibility of the Regency Government in efforts to protect Women's Rights as referred to in paragraph (1) is carried out by DPPPA.

#### Article 8

(1) The Regency Government in its efforts to protect Children shall be obligated and responsible for:

- a. respecting the fulfillment of Children's rights without distinguishing ethnicity, religion, race, class, gender, ethnicity, culture and language, legal status, birth order, and physical and/or mental condition;
- b. providing support for facilities, infrastructure, and availability of human resources in the implementation of Child Protection;
- c. ensuring the protection, care, and welfare of Children by taking into account the rights and obligations of Parents, Guardians, or other persons who are legally responsible for the Child;
- d. ensuring that Children can exercise their right to express their opinions in accordance with the Child's age and level of intelligence;
- e. establishing and implementing policies and programs/activities for the protection of Children's rights;
- f. cooperating with the community and/or Community Organizations in the implementation of Child Protection in accordance with the provisions of Laws and Regulations;
- g. conducting rehabilitation for Children who are victims of violence;
- h. providing special protection to Children:
  - 1. in emergency situations, disaster and conflict victims;
  - 2. in conflict with the law;
  - 3. victims of violence, neglect and human trafficking; 4. from minority and isolated groups;
  - 5. who are economically and/or sexually exploited/victims of pornography;
  - 6. who are victims of narcotics, alcohol, and psychotropic substance abuse; and
  - 7. identified with HIV/AIDS;
- i. organizing inclusive education for Children with special needs;
- j. taking preventive measures in order to prevent violations against Children; and
- k. implementing and supporting National policies in the implementation of Child Protection in the Regency carried out through efforts to realize Child-Friendly Regency, Child-Friendly District and Child-Friendly Village;
  - (2) The responsibility of the Regency Government in its efforts to protect Children as referred to in paragraph (1) shall be implemented by DPPPA.

#### Article 9

(1) The Regency Government in carrying out its responsibilities, establishes programs and activities for the protection of Women and Children with the Regional Action Plan as the basis for Regional Apparatus in implementing the protection of Women and Children.

(2) Further provisions regarding the Regional Action Plan as referred to in paragraph (1) shall be regulated by Regent Regulation.

## Part Two

Society

Article 10

(1) Society shall be obligated and responsible for implementing the protection of Women and Children including:

- a. participation in prevention and providing support for the development of prevention programs for violations of the rights of Women and Children;
- b. providing information and/or reporting violations of the rights of Women and Children to the competent authorities; and
- c. supervising the law enforcement process regarding cases of violations of the rights of Women and Children.
  - (2) The responsibility of society as referred to in paragraph (1) shall be in accordance with the provisions of laws and regulations and the values of local wisdom of the local community.

## Part Three

Parents and/or Family

### Paragraph 1

Responsibility for the Protection of Women's Rights

#### Article 11

The responsibility of the family in its efforts to protect women includes: a. protecting women from conflicts and violence within the household that demean the dignity of women;

b. providing equal opportunities for women to develop themselves in the fields of:

- 1. education;
- 2. social;
- 3. economy; and
- 4. politics and culture.
  - c. preventing violations of women's rights within the household.

### Paragraph 2

Responsibility for the Protection of Children's Rights

Article 12

(1) Parents in their efforts to protect Children shall be responsible for: a. caring for, maintaining, educating, and protecting Children; b. developing Children in accordance with their abilities, talents, and interests;

c. providing formal and non-formal education;

d. guiding Children to behave in accordance with religion, ethics and morals as well as customs; and

e. preventing marriage at Child age.

(2) In the event that parents are absent, or their whereabouts are unknown, or due to any reason, are unable to carry out their obligations and responsibilities, then the obligations and responsibilities as referred to in paragraph (1) may be transferred to the family.

# CHAPTER IV

PREVENTION

## Part One

Regency Government

#### Article 13

(1) Efforts undertaken by the Regency Government in order to prevent violations of the rights of Women include:

- a. organizing socialization regarding the prevention of violence against Women by optimizing the role of customary, cultural, religious institutions and community organizations;
- b. providing guidelines on the handling of violence against Women;
- c. involving community participation in recovery and reintegration; d. developing an integrated database and information system on violence against Women;
- e. developing family empowerment programs;
- f. strengthening the function of community organizations engaged in Women's empowerment; and
- g. providing public facilities that are comfortable and safe for Women.
  - (2) Efforts undertaken by the Regency Government in order to prevent violations of Children's rights include:
- a. preventing violence against Children by optimizing the role of customary, cultural, religious institutions and community organizations; b. developing child-friendly schools;
- c. encouraging increased participation of children through the establishment of Children's Forums;
- d. providing guidelines on the handling of violence against Children;
- e. involving community participation in recovery and reintegration services for child victims of violence;
- f. developing an integrated database and information system on violence against Children;
- g. strengthening the function of community organizations engaged in Child protection; and
- h. encouraging the establishment of Child-Friendly Regency.

## Part Two

Society

#### Article 14

(1) Efforts undertaken by society in order to prevent violations of the rights of Women include:

- a. supervision of various conditions occurring in their environment that may give rise to violations of the rights of Women; b. strengthening the role of community, religious organizations and the family environment; and
- c. prevention of underage marriage/unregistered marriage. (2) Efforts undertaken in order to prevent violations of the rights of Children include:
- a. supervision of various conditions occurring in their environment that give rise to violations of the rights of Children;
- b. strengthening the role of community, religious organizations and the family environment; and
- c. preventing marriage at Child age.

## Part Three

Parents and/or Family

#### Article 15

(1) Efforts undertaken by the family in order to prevent violations of the rights of Women include:

- a. protecting women from various rights violations within the household; and
- b. encouraging the formation of a harmonious, educative and democratic family;
  - (2) Efforts undertaken by parents in order to prevent violations of the rights of Children include:
- a. providing good examples to children in accordance with religious teachings and customs;
- b. supervising children in their activities inside or outside the home;
- c. providing opportunities for children to engage in dialogue and express their opinions democratically; and
- d. protecting children from various violations of Children's rights within the household.

# CHAPTERVI

HANDLING

## Part One

Handling of Women and Children Victims of Violence

#### Article40

The implementation of handling Women and Children victims of violence includes: a. handling of complaints from victims of violence;

b. health services;

c. social rehabilitation;

d. law enforcement and legal assistance; and

e. repatriation and social reintegration.

#### Article41

I

(1) The implementation of handling Women and Children victims of violence as referred to in Article 40 is carried out through integrated services.

(2) Integrated services for Women and Children victims of violence as referred to in paragraph (1) use the principles of:

- a. gender responsiveness;
- b. non-discrimination;
- c. equal and respectful relations;
- d. maintaining confidentiality;
- e. providing a sense of security and comfort;
- f. respecting individual differences;
- g. non-judgmental;
- h. respecting the choices and decisions of victims;
- i. sensitive to the background and condition of victims and the use of appropriate language understood by victims;
- j. quick and simple;
- k. empathy;
  - l. fulfillment of the rights of Women and Children; and
- m. no charge.

#### Article42

Services for handling complaints from victims of violence against Women and Children are carried out by:

- a. hospitals;
- b. community health centers;
- c. P2TP2A;
- d. Women and Children Service Unit (UPPA);
- e. Trauma Center Shelter House (RPTC);
- f. Child Social Protection House (RPSA);
- g. Women Crisis Centre (WCC)
- h. Marriage Guidance and Preservation Advisory Board (BP4); i. Police;
- j. Prosecutor's Office;
- k. Court;
  - l. Legal Aid Institute (LBH); and m.Child Protection Institute (LPA).

#### Article43

c. Community; and/or d.community organizations in the field of protection of Women and Children.

(2) Reporters as referred to in paragraph (1) may make complaints: a. directly;

b. by telephone; and

c. by letter.

(3) In addition to handling complaints submitted by reporters, officers at service institutions as referred to in Article 42 are authorized to handle Women and Children victims of violence obtained through other information.

## Part Three

Social Rehabilitation Services for Women and Children Victims of Violence

#### Article45

(1) Social rehabilitation services for Women and Children victims of violence are aimed at restoring and developing the capacity of victims who experience social dysfunction to be able to carry out their social functions in society properly.

(2) Social rehabilitation services as referred to in paragraph (1) include: a. counseling services;

b. provision of safe houses;

c. spiritual guidance.

(3) The provision of services as referred to in paragraph (2) is organized by:

- a. Regional Apparatus having duties and functions in the social field; b. DPPPA;
- c. P2TP2A;
- d. Women Crisis Centre (WeC);
- e. community organizations in the social field; and/or
- f. religious institutions.

#### Article46

(1) Counseling services as referred to in Article 45 paragraph (2) letter a aim to:

- a. help victims identify their problems and find effective ways to overcome them themselves;
- b. empower victims to determine their future;
- c. provide moral support for victims in facing the process they are undergoing; and
- d. make victims feel accepted in the community environment. 25
  - (2) Counseling services are carried out by social rehabilitation officers who are gender responsive, consisting of:
- a. counselors;
- b. psychologists; and
- c. psychiatrists.
  - (3) Counseling services are conducted in places that guarantee a sense of security, comfort and confidentiality of information from victims.
  - (4) The stages of counseling services include:
- a. initial counseling;
- b. follow-up counseling;
- c. case discussion (Case conference); and
- d. site visits (home visit).

#### Article47

(1) The Regency Government may provide safe houses for Women and Children victims of violence.

(2) Safe houses as referred to in paragraph (1) aim to protect Women and Children victims of violence from intimidation by perpetrators or other parties. I

(3) The location of safe houses as referred to in paragraph (1) must be kept confidential.

(4) The criteria for Women and Children victims of violence requiring safe houses include:

- a. their life is threatened;
- b. rejected by family or community;
- c. require intensive services but their residence is relatively far; and/or
- d. abandoned if not placed in a safe house.
  - (5) The provision of safe house services is carried out by social I rehabilitation officers who have gender sensitivity or gender awareness, consisting of:
- a. social workers/volunteers;
- b. security officers; and
- c. community leaders.
  - (6) Further provisions regarding the provision of safe house services are regulated by Regent Regulation.

Article48

(1) Every Woman and Child victim of violence is given spiritual guidance aimed at psychological recovery.

(2) Spiritual guidance as referred to in paragraph (1) is carried out based on the principles of:

- a. not imposing any religion or belief; and
- b. leaving the final decision in the hands of the victim (self determination). (3) Spiritual guidance as referred to in paragraph (2) is carried out by spiritual guidance officers.
  - (4) Spiritual guidance officers as referred to in paragraph (3) consist of:
- a. consultants of the Marriage Counseling, Guidance and Preservation Board (BP4);
- b. Officers of the Office of Religious Affairs (KUA);
- c. Ustadz or Islamic Scholars;
- d. Priest;
- e. Minister; and
- f. other religious leaders who have gender sensitivity or gender awareness.

## Part Four

Law Enforcement and Legal Assistance Services

I,

#### Article49

(1) Law enforcement and legal assistance services constitute actions by law enforcement officers provided to Women and Children victims of violence and/or witnesses.

(2) Law enforcement and legal assistance services as referred to in paragraph (1) are carried out by prioritizing the best interests of victims.

#### Article 50

(1) Law enforcement and legal aid services as referred to in Article 49 are intended to provide assistance and protection to Women and Children victims of violence and witnesses with a gender-responsive approach so that victims obtain justice and legal rights certainty.

(2) Types of legal aid services as referred to in paragraph (1) include:

- a. legal aid in the reporting process and preparation of BAP; b. legal aid in the prosecution process at the prosecutor's office;
- c. legal aid in the examination process at court; d. legal aid in the court hearing decision process until there is a final legal decision; and
- e. legal aid in the mediation process.
  - (3) The Regency Government provides legal aid to Children and Women victims of violence free of charge.
  - (4) In providing legal aid as referred to in paragraph (3) the Regency Government may cooperate with:
- a. Legal Aid Institute (LBH);
- b. Women Crisis Center (WCC);
- c. Advocates; and/or
- d. other gender-sensitive legal assistants.

## Part Five

Repatriation and Social Reintegration Services

#### Article 51

(1) Repatriation of victims of violence is intended to return Women and Children victims of violence to their region of origin.

(2) Repatriation of Women and Children victims of violence as referred to in paragraph (1) is accompanied by social reintegration efforts.

(3) The Regency Government implements repatriation and social reintegration of Women and Children victims of violence in accordance with the provisions of Legislation.

# CHAPTER VII

WOMEN EMPOWERMENT

#### Article 52

(1) The Regency Government guarantees the implementation of empowerment for Women.

(2) The implementation of Women empowerment as referred to in paragraph (1) is supported by community participation and Community Organizations.

Article 53

(1) The Regency Government provides skills training for the capacity building of Women.

(2) In providing training as referred to in paragraph (1) the Regency Government may cooperate with training institutions organized by community organizations.

(3) The Regency Government expands access and opportunities for Women in obtaining skills and employment fields.

#### Article 54

(1) The Regency Government facilitates:

- a. provision of business capital assistance for Women with weak economies;
- b. marketing of Women's business products;
- c. implementation of community capacity development training through economic empowerment of pre-welfare families;
- d. expansion of access, information and provision of knowledge to Women regarding efforts to market products produced; and e. providing comprehensive entrepreneurship training to Women.

(2) The Regency Government in facilitating activities as referred to in paragraph (1) cooperates with the community and/or community organizations.

# CHAPTER VIII

CHILD PARTICIPATION

#### Article 55

(1) Every Child has the right to participate in expressing opinions, ideas, and thoughts related to life based on awareness and understanding, in accordance with age and level of thinking maturity.

(2) Child Participation as referred to in paragraph (1) may be conducted through the Children's Forum starting from the village level, sub-district within the regency.

(3) The Children's Forum as referred to in paragraph (2) is a representation of Children, both geographical domicile representation, socio-cultural groups and educational background of Children.

(4) The establishment of the Children's Forum as referred to in paragraph (2) is facilitated by the Regency Government. (5) The establishment of the Children's Forum as referred to in paragraph (4) is determined by Regent Decree.

#### Article 56

(1) In every formulation of policies related to Children, the Regency Government considers and accommodates Children's Opinions conveyed through the Children's Forum.

(2) The Regency Government includes representatives of the Children's Forum in the Regency Development Planning Deliberation.

# CHAPTER IX

CHILD-FRIENDLY REGENCY

#### Article 57

(1) The Regency Government conducts guidance to the Sub-district Government in developing a Child-Friendly Regency.

(2) Guidance as referred to in paragraph (1) includes: a. coordination;

b. facilitation;

c. guidance;

d. supervision;

e. consultation; and

f. education and training.

(3) Provisions regarding Child-Friendly Regency are further regulated by Regent Regulation.

# CHAPTER X

GUIDANCE AND SUPERVISION

#### Article 58

(1) The Regent conducts guidance and supervision of the implementation of Women and Child Protection.

(2) Guidance as referred to in paragraph (1) may be conducted in the form of:

- a. training for government officials and law enforcement officers on prevention and handling of violence;
- b. training for religious leaders, community leaders, traditional leaders, educators, journalists and media managers in order to prevent violence against Women and Children;
- c. training for health workers who provide health services to Women and Children victims of violence at community health centers, integrated service units, and general hospitals;
- d. training for social rehabilitation officers and spiritual guidance officers in order to protect Women and Children victims of violence; and
- f. improvement of professionalism of officers, managers of violence case data against Women and Children at community health centers, integrated service units and hospitals.
  - (3) The Regent in conducting guidance and supervision as referred to in paragraph (1) may delegate to the Head of Agency.

#### Article 59

(1) The Head of DPPPA must submit a report on the implementation of the Women and Child protection program to the Regent.

(2) The report as referred to in paragraph (1) is submitted periodically once every 3 (three) months.

#### Article 60

(1) The Regent conducts supervision and evaluation of the implementation of Women and Child Protection periodically once every 3 (three) months.

(2) The evaluation results as referred to in paragraph (1) constitute evaluation material for the Women and Child protection program policy in the Regency.

# CHAPTER XI

FINANCING

#### Article 61

Financing for the implementation of Women and Child protection is sourced from:

- a. Regional Revenue and Expenditure Budget;
- b. State Revenue and Expenditure Budget; and
- c. other legitimate sources in accordance with the provisions of Legislation.

# CHAPTER XII

CLOSING PROVISIONS

#### Article 62

Implementing regulations of this Regional Regulation are stipulated no later than 1 (one) year from the promulgation of this Regional Regulation.

#### Article 63

This Regional Regulation comes into force on the date of promulgation.
