# CHAPTER I

GENERAL PROVISIONS

#### Article 1

In this Regional Regulation, what is meant by:

- 1. Region is Bandar Lampung City.
- 2. Regional Government is the Mayor as the element of regional government administration who leads the implementation of government affairs which are the authority of the autonomous region.
- 3. Regional House of Representatives hereinafter abbreviated as DPRD is the Regional House of Representatives of Bandar Lampung City.
- 4. Mayor is the Mayor of Bandar Lampung.
- 5. Protection is all preventive actions, services and empowerment to protect and guarantee women's rights from acts of violence.
- 6. Women are human beings of female sex and persons recognized by law as women.
- 7. Violence is any act that results in misery or suffering whether physical, sexual, psychological, and/or domestic neglect including threats to commit acts, coercion, or unlawful deprivation of a person's freedom.
- 8. Trafficking in Persons is the act of recruitment, transportation, harboring, sending, transferring, or receiving a person by threat of violence, use of violence, abduction, confinement, forgery, fraud, abuse of power or vulnerable position, debt bondage or giving payment or benefits to obtain consent from the person in control of that other person, whether conducted within a country or between countries for the purpose of exploitation or resulting in a person being exploited.
- 9. Victim is a person who experiences misery and/or threats of violence resulting in suffering either physically and/or psychologically either directly or indirectly.
- 10. Prevention is the effort of regional government and society in creating conditions that prevent the occurrence of violence, mistreatment, exploitation and neglect and trafficking against women.
- 11. Service is action taken as soon as possible to victims when seeing, hearing and knowing of the impending, ongoing or occurred violence against victims.
- 12. Empowerment is an effort to strengthen women to be able to endeavor and work independently.
- 13. Regional Technical Implementation Unit for Women and Children Protection hereinafter abbreviated as UPTD PPA is a Regional Technical Implementation Unit established by the Regional Government in providing services for Women and Children who experience violence, discrimination, special protection and other problems.
- 14. Safe House is a temporary residence, used to provide protection for victims.

# CHAPTER II

PRINCIPLES AND OBJECTIVES

#### Article 2

(1) Protection of women is based on the principles of: a. respect and protection of Human Rights;

b. justice and gender equality;

c. non-discrimination;

d. best interests of women;

e. right to life, survival, and development;

f. empowerment;

g. legal certainty; and

h. participation.

(2) Protection of women aims to ensure the fulfillment of rights to be able to live, grow, develop and participate optimally in accordance with human dignity and honor and to receive protection from violence, discrimination, exploitation and trafficking in persons for the realization of quality and prosperous women.

# CHAPTER III

SCOPE

#### Article 3

The Scope of Women's Protection includes: a. Prevention;

b. Service; and

c. Empowerment.

## Part One

Prevention

#### Article 4

Every Woman has the right:

- a. to live and maintain her life and living;
- b. to obtain education and benefit from science and technology, arts and culture;
- c. to obtain health services;
- d. to form a family in a legitimate marriage bond;
- e. to develop herself;
- f. to recognition, guarantee, protection and fair legal certainty and equal treatment before the law;
- g. to work and receive fair remuneration and treatment in employment relations;
- h. to associate, assemble and express opinions;
- i. to live peacefully, safely, peacefully, happily, prosperously, physically and spiritually;
- j. to obtain social security;
- k. to embrace and worship according to her respective religion and belief;
- l. special protection in emergency conditions/situations.

#### Article 5

(1) Prevention in the framework of protection includes the fields of education, infrastructure, public services and spatial planning, governance and institutional governance, economy, employment, social and culture;

(2) Prevention efforts as referred to in paragraph (1) are implemented through:

- a. improvement of quality of life;
- b. establishing and developing protection strategies and policies; and
- c. establishing and developing institutional capacity related to women's protection.

#### Article 6

Improvement of women's quality of life as referred to in Article 5 paragraph (2) letter a can be carried out through:

- a. providing access to women to education, health services and other strategic fields;
- b. encouraging women's involvement in various development activities;
- c. providing knowledge, skills and character values, morals and family resilience; d. encouraging programs that can increase women's independence in various fields, both legal, political, social, economic, employment, cultural and other strategic fields.

#### Article 7

In establishing and developing strategies and policies for the protection of women, as referred to in Article 5 paragraph (2) letter b can be carried out through:

- a. presenting communication, information and education materials on women's protection;
- b. conducting socialization, advocacy and social campaigns in the framework of women's protection both to the community and to every element responsible in the framework of protection of women;
- c. conducting sustainable cooperation with the community in women's protection;
- d. strengthening and developing gender data systems;
- e. enhancing and involving the role and active participation of the community in women's protection;

#### Article 8

(1) In order to establish and develop institutional capacity related to women's protection, as referred to in Article 5 paragraph (2) letter c can be carried out through:

- a. strengthening coordination mechanisms and work networks of women's protection units;
- b. Regional Government establishes a task force consisting of representatives from regional government, law enforcement, community organizations, non-governmental organizations, professional organizations, and researchers/academics;
- c. increasing managerial resources through education and training for law enforcement apparatus, medical officers, psychologists, psychiatrists, and victim assistants;
- d. strengthening institutional capacity for Gender Mainstreaming;
- e. optimizing technical service units;
- f. providing adequate facilities and infrastructure in the framework of women's protection
  - (2) To implement the provisions as referred to in paragraph (1) shall be further regulated by Mayor Regulation.

## Part Two

Service

### Paragraph One

Forms of Violence

#### Article 9

Forms of violence against women include:

- a. physical violence caused by acts resulting in pain, injury, wounds or disability to a person's body, miscarriage, unconsciousness and/or causing death;
- b. psychological violence caused by acts resulting in fear, loss of self-confidence, loss of ability to act, feelings of helplessness and/or severe psychological suffering to a person;
- c. sexual violence caused by:
  - 1. sexual harassment;
  - 2. sexual exploitation;
  - 3. forced contraception;
  - 4. forced abortion;
  - 5. rape;
  - 6. forced marriage;
  - 7. forced prostitution;
  - 8. sexual slavery;
  - 9. sexual torture;
  - 10. abandonment.
- d. Abandonment caused by:
  - 1. acts resulting in the unfulfillment of women's needs properly, whether physical, mental, spiritual or social;
  - 2. acts that abandon persons within the household scope, whereas according to the law applicable to them or due to agreement or contract they are obliged to provide livelihood, care, or maintenance to such person; and/or
  - 3. acts resulting in economic dependence by restricting and/or prohibiting women from working properly inside or outside the home.
- e. Exploitation caused by:
  - 1. acts that exploit economically or sexually with the intention of benefiting oneself or others;
  - 2. acts with or without the victim's consent which include but are not limited to prostitution, forced labor or services, slavery or similar practices, oppression, extortion, utilization of physical, sexual, reproductive organs, or unlawfully transferring or transplanting organs and/or body tissues or utilizing the labor or ability of someone by another party to obtain benefits both material and immaterial; and/or
  - 3. all forms of utilization of sexual organs or other body organs of the victim to obtain benefits, and/or not limited to all prostitution and indecency activities

### Second Paragraph

Rights of Victims

#### Article 10

Every victim has the right to receive:

- a. protection from family, police, prosecution, courts, advocates, social institutions, or other parties either temporarily or based on a protection order from the court;
- b. health services in accordance with medical needs;
- c. special handling related to victim confidentiality;
- d. accompaniment by social workers and legal assistance at every level of the examination process in accordance with statutory provisions; and
- e. spiritual guidance services.

### Third Paragraph

Forms of Services

#### Article 11

(1) Handling of complaints is a series of actions carried out by integrated service providers to follow up on reports of acts of violence against women submitted by victims, families or the community.

(2) Services as referred to in paragraph (1) are in the form of:

- a. conducting interviews and observations of the victim's condition;
- b. making recommendations for follow-up services; c. coordinating and referring to follow-up services and related parties;
- d. administering the complaint process.

#### Article 12

(1) Health services are efforts that include promotive, preventive, curative and rehabilitative aspects.

(2) Services as referred to in paragraph (1) are in the form of:

- a. conducting examinations;
- b. treatment;
- c. follow-up care for victims;
- d. coordinating the implementation of health rehabilitation and reproductive health services;
- e. conducting medico-legal examinations including collection of evidence from victims and preparation of visum et repertum.
- f. conducting supporting examinations and laboratory tests on evidence.
- g. conducting consultations with specialist doctors or making referrals.
- h. preparing case reports.

#### Article 13

(1) Social rehabilitation is a service aimed at restoring and developing the ability of a person experiencing social dysfunction to be able to carry out their social functions properly.

(2) Services as referred to in paragraph (1) are in the form of:

- a. providing accompaniment during the case handling process;
- b. conducting counseling.

#### Article 14

(1) Legal aid is a legal service provided by legal companions and advocates to carry out the process of accompanying witnesses and/or victims of violence against women.

(2) Services as referred to in paragraph (1) are in the form of:

- a. Accompanying and/or defending every legal handling process;
- b. preparing reports on the progress of legal handling.

#### Article 15

(1) Repatriation is an effort to return female victims of violence from the city area to their area of origin.

(2) Services as referred to in paragraph (1) are in the form of:

- a. coordinating with relevant agencies for the repatriation of victims.
- b. preparing progress reports on the process of accompanying the repatriation of victims;
- c. conducting monitoring for at least three months after the victim is returned to their family.

#### Article 16

(1) Social reintegration is an effort to reunite victims with family, substitute family, or community that can provide protection and fulfillment of needs for victims.

(2) Services as referred to in paragraph (1) are in the form of:

- a. Economic and social empowerment;
- b. Education; and
- c. Monitoring and/or further guidance.

## Part Three

Empowerment

#### Article 17

(1) In order to improve the position, role, and quality of women, as well as efforts to realize gender equality and justice in family, community, national, and state life, women must be empowered.

(2) Women's empowerment as referred to in paragraph (1) is directed at obtaining opportunities and rights as human beings, so as to be able to play a role and participate in economic, socio-cultural, political, and legal activities.

#### Article 18

The implementation of women's empowerment in the economic field as referred to in Article 17 paragraph (2) is carried out by:

- a. providing knowledge, skills and job training;
- b. facilitating the formation of productive economic business groups;
- c. facilitating the strengthening and development of productive economic business groups;
- d. facilitating and providing capital assistance; and e. facilitating the development of marketing networks.

#### Article 19

Women's empowerment in the socio-cultural field as referred to in Article 17 paragraph (2) is carried out through:

- a. increasing knowledge, attitudes and skills to encourage the fulfillment of tiered education in accordance with potential to improve social status;
- b. increasing awareness, willingness and ability to overcome health problems through quality promotive, preventive, curative, and rehabilitative efforts, especially in the field of reproductive health.
- c. increasing awareness and knowledge about independent, healthy and prosperous family planning including access to consultation services and marriage registration; and
- d. facilitation and efforts to preserve customs and development of science, technology, arts and culture for the advancement of women.

#### Article 20

The implementation of women's empowerment in the political field as referred to in Article 17 paragraph (2) includes:

- a. involvement of women in decision-making at various levels;
- b. providing opportunities for women to hold public office;
- c. participation in general elections; and d. self-development through organizations to associate, assemble and express opinions.

#### Article 21

The implementation of women's empowerment in the legal field as referred to in Article 17 paragraph (2) includes:

- a. increasing awareness and knowledge in the legal field through communication, information and education services; and
- b. facilitating access and legal consultation services.

# CHAPTER IV

OBLIGATIONS AND RESPONSIBILITIES OF ORGANIZERS

## Part One

Regional Government

#### Article 22

(1) The Regional Government has the obligation and responsibility to:

- a. make policies that take into account the interests of women, especially with regard to the fulfillment and protection of their rights;
- b. facilitate efforts to organize services for the fulfillment of rights and provide support for facilities and infrastructure in organizing women's protection;
- c. guarantee the protection, maintenance, and welfare of women by taking into account women's rights and obligations;
- d. supervise the organization of women's protection;
- e. conduct cross-institutional coordination between government agencies as well as with the community and the Business World;
- f. conduct data collection and inventory of women's data;
- g. provide safe houses.
  - (2) The Regional Government has the authority to:
- a. prepare operational guidelines for minimum service standards for efforts to fulfill rights and protection for women;
- b. implement services for the fulfillment of rights and protection of women;
- c. conduct supervision of efforts to fulfill women's rights and women's protection services; and
- d. develop cooperation networks between government agencies as well as with Universities, the community and the Business World.

## Part Two

Community

#### Article 23

(1) The community is responsible for conducting prevention, risk reduction and case handling for women victims of violence, exploitation, and human trafficking.

(2) The community's responsibilities as referred to in paragraph (1) include:

- a. preventing acts of violence against women;
- b. providing information and/or reporting if acts of violence occur to law enforcement or the authorities;
- c. protecting victims; and
- d. providing emergency assistance.

## Part Three

Business World

#### Article 24

The business world has the obligation and responsibility to:

- a. provide contributions and guarantees in the implementation of women's protection;
- b. provide facilities and infrastructure to enhance efforts to fulfill rights and protect women; and
- c. be responsible for conducting prevention, risk reduction and case handling for women victims of violence, exploitation, and human trafficking.

# CHAPTER V

INSTITUTIONAL FRAMEWORK FOR WOMEN'S PROTECTION

## Part One

Development, Coordination, Implementation and Supervision of Women's Protection

#### Article 25

(1) The Mayor conducts development, coordination of implementation and supervision of women's protection.

(2) The Mayor may delegate development, coordination of implementation and supervision, as referred to in paragraph (1), to the Regional Government Organization that carries out the main duties and functions in the field of women's protection.

(3) The procedures and mechanisms for development, coordination, implementation and supervision shall be further regulated by Mayoral Regulation.

## Part Two

Service Organization

#### Article 26

(1) The organization of women's protection services is carried out in an integrated manner by the Regional Technical Implementation Unit for Women and Children Protection and in coordination with Regional Government Organizations and related Agencies or institutions in accordance with the field of Women's services.

(2) The implementation of service organization as referred to in paragraph (1) shall be further regulated by Mayoral Regulation.

# CHAPTER VI

EVALUATION, REPORTING

AND ACCOUNTABILITY

#### Article 27

(1) Regional Government Organizations conduct prevention services, risk reduction services and case handling services for women who are victims.

(2) Regional Government Organizations that implement the organization of women's protection are obliged to carry out evaluations and prepare accountability reports to the Mayor.

# CHAPTER VII

FINANCING

Funding for women's protection may be sourced from the State Revenue and Expenditure Budget, Regional Revenue and Expenditure Budgets for both the Province and City and/or other lawful sources that are non-binding in accordance with the provisions of laws and regulations.

# CHAPTER VIII

CRIMINAL PROVISIONS

#### Article 28

Any person or entity/institution/organization that intentionally fails to fulfill women's rights and protection may be subject to criminal sanctions in accordance with applicable laws and regulations.

# CHAPTER IX

CLOSING PROVISIONS

#### Article 29

This Regional Regulation shall come into force on the date of promulgation.
