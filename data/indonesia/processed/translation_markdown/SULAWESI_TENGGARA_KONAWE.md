CONCERNING

PROTECTION OF CHILDREN AND WOMEN VICTIMS OF VIOLENCE IN KONAWE REGENCY

BY THE GRACE OF ALMIGHTY GOD

THE REGENT OF KONAWE,

Considering : a. that every citizen has the right to be free from torture or treatment that degrades human dignity and has the right to obtain security and freedom from all forms of violence;

b. that children and women are vulnerable groups experiencing violence where all forms of violence against children and women constitute violations of human rights and crimes against human dignity so that legal protection is needed to protect their dignity and to guarantee their right to life in accordance with their nature and character without discrimination;

c. that violence against children and women in Konawe Regency shows a fairly high number and continues to increase, so that protection efforts are required;

d. that all forms of violence against children and women constitute violations of human rights so that their dignity needs to be protected and their right to life guaranteed in accordance with their nature and character without discrimination;

e. that the implementation of Protection of Children and Women Victims of Violence in Konawe

Regency needs to be supported by institutions and regulations so that its implementation can be guaranteed;

f. that based on the considerations as referred to in letters a, b, c, d and e above, it is deemed necessary to enact a

# CHAPTER I

GENERAL PROVISIONS

#### Article 1

In this Regional Regulation, the following terms are defined as: 1. Region is Konawe Regency.

2. Regional Government is the Government of Konawe Regency. 3. Regent is the Regent of Konawe.

4. Regional House of Representatives hereinafter abbreviated as DPRD is the Regional House of Representatives of Konawe Regency.

5. Protection is all efforts aimed at providing a sense of security to victims carried out by family, advocates, social institutions, police, prosecutors, courts, or other parties either temporarily or based on court determination.

6. Child is a person who has not yet reached the age of 18 (eighteen) years, including a child in the womb.

7. Woman is a person who has female genitalia, can experience menstruation, pregnancy, childbirth, breastfeeding and includes persons who are legally recognized as Women.

8. Violence is any act against a person that results in suffering or pain physically, sexually, psychologically, including threats to commit acts, coercion, or unlawful deprivation of liberty.

9. Violence against children is any act against a child that results in suffering or pain physically, psychologically, sexually, and/or neglect, including threats to commit acts, coercion, or unlawful deprivation of liberty.

10. Violence against women is any act based on gender difference that results or may result in suffering or pain to women physically, sexually, economically, socially, psychologically, including threats of certain actions, coercion or deprivation of liberty, whether occurring in public or private life.

11. Physical violence is any act that results in pain, injury, serious wounds or disability to a person's body, miscarriage or unconsciousness and/or causes death.

12. Psychological violence is an act that results in fear, loss of self-confidence, loss of ability to act, feeling of helplessness or severe psychological suffering in a person.

13. Sexual violence is any act in the form of coercion of sexual relations, sexual abuse, coercion of sexual relations in an unnatural and/or unwanted manner, coercion of sexual relations with others for commercial purposes and/or certain purposes.

14. Exploitation is an act with or without the consent of the victim which includes but is not limited to prostitution, forced labor or service, slavery or practices similar to slavery, oppression, extortion, physical exploitation, sexual, reproductive organs, or unlawfully removing or transplanting organs and/or body tissues or exploiting the labor or abilities of a person by another party to obtain material or immaterial benefits.

15. Victim is a child and woman who experiences physical suffering, psychological suffering, sexual suffering, either directly or indirectly resulting from acts of violence.

16. Community is individuals, families, groups, social organizations and/or community organizations.

17. Family is the smallest unit in society consisting of husband and wife, or husband and wife and their children, or father and his children, or mother and her children, or blood relatives in a direct line upward or downward up to the third degree.

18. Parents are biological father and/or mother, or step father and/or mother, or adoptive father and/or mother.

19. Service is immediate activities and actions carried out by Professional personnel in accordance with their respective professions in the form of counseling, therapy and advocacy for the empowerment and recovery of victims of violence.

20. Assistance is all actions in the form of counseling, psychological therapy, advocacy, and spiritual guidance, for the self-empowerment of victims of violence to resolve the problems they face.

21 . Repatriation is the effort to return child and woman victims of violence to the family, substitute family, or community that can provide protection and fulfill their needs.

22. Rehabilitation is recovery from impairment of physical, psychological, and social conditions in order to perform their role again normally both in the family and in society.

23. Social Reintegration is the effort to reunite victims with the family, or family substitute, community, institution, or other social environment that can provide protection and fulfillment of needs for victims.

24. Institution is an agency/service/body/office within the scope of regional government and/or non-governmental organization that provides assistance.

25. Integrated Service Center hereinafter referred to as PPT is a generic term that can be used by various institutions that provide services for victims of violence such as Integrated Service Center for Women and Children Empowerment, Safe House, Child Social Protection House, Trauma Center Protection House, Women's Social Protection House, Shelter House, etc.

26. Regional Action Plan is the foundation and guideline for relevant agencies, vertical agencies, and the community, in order to carry out prevention and protection activities for women and children victims of violence.

27. Safe House is a temporary residence, provided to provide protection to victims in accordance with predetermined standards.

# CHAPTER I I

PRINCIPLES AND OBJECTIVES

#### Article 2

The implementation of protection for women and children victims of violence is carried out based on the principles of:

- a. respect for human rights;
- b. justice;
- c. non-discrimination; and
- d. victim protection;

#### Article 3

(1) The purpose of implementing protection for children and women victims of violence is to:

- a. prevent violence against children and women; b. eliminate all forms of violence and exploitation against children and women;
- c. protect and provide a sense of security for children and women;
- d. provide services to children and women victims of violence, reporters, and witnesses; and
- e. facilitate and mediate domestic disputes to realize the integrity of harmonious and prosperous households.
  - (2) The purpose of implementing protection for children and women victims of violence as referred to in paragraph (1), includes the aspects of:
- a. prevention;
- h. service and assistance;
- c. reunification; and
- d. empowerment.

# CHAPTER II I

FORMS OF VIOLENCE

#### Article 4

Forms of violence against children and women include: a. physical violence;

b. psychological violence;

c. sexual violence;

d. neglect; and

e. exploitation.

#### Article 5

Physical violence as referred to in Article 4 letter a is caused by acts that result in pain, injury, serious wounds or disability to a person's body, miscarriage, unconsciousness and/or causes death.

#### Article 6

Psychological violence as referred to in Article 4 letter b is caused by acts that result in fear, loss of self-confidence, loss of ability to act, feeling of helplessness and/or severe psychological suffering in a person.

#### Article 7

Sexual violence as referred to in Article 4 letter c is caused by:

- a. acts in the form of sexual harassment;
- b. coercion of sexual relations;
- c. coercion of sexual relations in an unnatural or unwanted manner; and/or
- d. coercion of sexual relations with others for commercial purposes and or certain purposes.

#### Article 8

Neglect as referred to in Article 4 letter d is caused by:

- a. acts that result in the non-fulfillment of the needs of children properly, whether physical, mental, spiritual or social, committed by parents, guardians, or other parties responsible for care;
- b. acts of deliberately neglecting to maintain, care for, or take care of children as appropriate, committed by parents, guardians or any other party responsible for their care;
- c. acts of neglecting a person within the scope of the household, whereas according to the law applicable to them or due to an agreement or contract they are obliged to provide livelihood, care, or maintenance to that person; and/or
- d. acts that result in economic dependence by restricting and/or prohibiting proper work inside or outside the home so that the victim is under the control of that person.

#### Article 9

Exploitation as referred to in Article 4 letter e is caused by:

- a. acts that exploit economically or sexually with the intention of benefiting oneself or others; b. acts with or without the consent of the victim which include but are not limited to prostitution, forced labor or services, slavery or similar practices, oppression, extortion, physical, sexual, reproductive organ exploitation, or unlawfully removing or transplanting organs and/or body tissues or exploiting the labor or ability of a person by other parties to obtain benefits whether material or immaterial; and/or
- c. all forms of exploitation of sexual organs or other body organs of the victim to obtain benefits, including but not limited to all prostitution or sexual abuse activities.

# CHAPTER IV

RIGHTS OF VICTIMS

#### Article 10

Every victim has the right to obtain:

- a. protection;
- b. information;
- c. optimal services;
- d. continuous handling up to the rehabilitation stage; e. confidential handling;
- f. psychological and legal assistance;
- g. guarantee of rights relating to status as a family member and community member.

#### Article 11

Child victims of violence, in addition to obtaining the rights as referred to in Article 10, also receive special rights as follows: a. right to respect and full use for survival, growth and development;

b. right to basic services;

c. right to equal protection;

d. right to be free from various stigmas; and

e. right to obtain freedom.

# CHAPTER V

OBLIGATIONS AND RESPONSIBILITIES

#### Article 12

Obligations and responsibilities in providing protection to children and women victims of violence are the joint responsibility of:

- a. regional government;
- b. community;
- c. family and parents.

#### Article 13

(1) The Regional Government carries out efforts to protect children and women victims of violence through:

- a. implementing policies for the protection of children and women victims of violence adopted by the government;
- b. establishing policies, programs, and activities for the protection of children and women victims of violence;
- c. establishing PPT;
- d. ensuring the implementation of service facilities for victims;
- e. promoting effectiveness and efficiency for the victim recovery process; and
- f. promoting the creation of cooperation and coordination in victim recovery efforts.
  - (2) In order to carry out the obligations and responsibilities as referred to in paragraph (1) letter b, the regional government establishes action programs and activities for the protection of children and women in 1 (one) Regional Action Plan as a basis for regional apparatus in implementing protection for women and children victims of violence.
  - (3) The Regional Action Plan as referred to in paragraph (2), is part of the Regional Medium-Term Development Plan (RPJMD).
  - (4) The Regional Action Plan as referred to in paragraph (2), is further regulated by Regent Regulation.

#### Article 14

(1) The obligations and responsibilities of the community as referred to in Article 11 letter b, are carried out in the form of community participation.

(2) Forms of community participation as referred to in paragraph (1), include:

- a. forming family partners at the village level;
- b. forming child and women protection units within community organizations;
- c. conducting independent socialization of children's rights and women's rights;
- d. providing first aid to victims; and e. reporting to the competent authority if violence against victims occurs in their environment.
  - (3) Community participation as referred to in paragraph (1) is carried out by individuals, community social institutions, non-governmental organizations, educational institutions, religious institutions, private sector, and mass media.
  - (4) Forms of community participation as referred to in paragraph (2), are implemented responsibly in accordance with the provisions of laws and regulations.

#### Article 15

The obligations of family and parents as referred to in Article 11 letter c, who legally have full responsibility to prevent all forms of violence and protect children and women as family members.

# CHAPTER VI

IMPLEMENTATION OF PROTECTION

## Part One

Institutions

#### Article 16

(1) In order to provide services and protection to children and women from violence, the Regional Government establishes PPT as an integrated service center for children and women victims of violence.

(2) PPT as referred to in paragraph (1), consists of elements of the Health Office, Youth Education and Sports Office, Social Office, Cooperatives Industry and Trade Office, Population and Civil Registry Office, Women Empowerment and Family Planning Agency, Community Empowerment Agency, Civil Service Police Unit, Regional General Hospital, Police Resort, Non-Governmental Organizations, and Universities.

(3) PPT as referred to in paragraph (2), is established by Regent's decree.

(4) The provision of services to victims is carried out in an integrated manner by PPT.

(5) PPT as referred to in paragraph (4) may receive and send case referrals from or to other service units in a networked manner.

(6) PPT provides services and temporary protection in the form of safe houses.

(7) In the event that PPT does not yet have a safe house, then victims of violence are referred to PPT that has a safe house. (8) PPT consists of several counseling fields.

(9) The counseling fields as referred to in paragraph (8) consist of at least:

- a. legal field;
- b. health field;
- c. spiritual field; and
- d. psychology field.
  - (10) Further provisions regarding the organizational structure of PPT are regulated by Regent Regulation.

## Part Two

Forms and Mechanisms of Prevention and Services

#### Article 17

Forms of prevention of violence against children and women carried out by PPT, can be implemented through: a. Socialization activities of laws and regulations to the community relating to the protection of the rights of children and women; and

b. Training of PPT members related to the implementation of their duties in preventing violence against children and women.

#### Article 18

(1) PPT in carrying out its duties and functions may coordinate with District PPT, and competent parties in making efforts to prevent violence against children and women.

(2) Further provisions regarding the mechanism for implementing prevention duties as referred to in paragraph (1), are regulated by Regent Regulation.

#### Article 19

Forms of services for victims organized by PPT include:

- a. Medical services, in the form of treatment and recovery of physical injuries aimed at restoring the physical condition of victims carried out by medical personnel and paramedics;
- b. Medicolegal services are a form of medical services for the purposes of evidence in the legal field;
- c. Psychosocial services are services provided in order to restore the traumatic condition of victims, including the provision of safe houses to protect victims from various threats and intimidation against victims and provide social support so that victims have a sense of confidence, strength and independence in resolving their problems;
- d. Legal services to assist victims in undergoing legal processes, and;
- e. Economic independence services in the form of skills training and providing economic access so that victims can be independent.

#### Article 20

(1) In carrying out service tasks for child and women victims of violence, PPT coordinates with Sub-district PPT.

(2) The mechanism for implementing tasks as referred to in paragraph (1) is organized according to Standard Operating Procedures which are further regulated by Regent Regulation.

## Part Three

Assistance

#### Article 21

(1) Assistance is carried out by individuals and/or institutions that cooperate with PPT.

(2) The assistance mechanism as referred to in paragraph (1) is organized according to Standard Operating Procedures (SOP) which are further regulated by Regent Regulation.

## Part Four

Principles of Service and Assistance

#### Article 22

The organization of services and assistance for victims is carried out with the principles:

- a. fast, safe, and empathetic;
- b. guarantee of confidentiality;
- c. easily accessible; and
- d. no charge.

## Part Five

Services

#### Article 23

(1) Forms of services provided to child and women victims of violence are as follows:

- a. complaint services;
- b. health services;
- c. legal aid;
- d. repatriation;
- e. rehabilitation, social reintegration, and medicolegal;
- f. identification services; and
- g. psychological services.
  - (2) Forms of services as referred to in paragraph (1) are implemented in accordance with minimum service standards established by the government and carried out by regional apparatus whose tasks and functions are in the fields of:
- a. social;
- b. health;
- c. women's empowerment and child protection; and 14
- d. mental and spiritual.
  - (3) In carrying out the tasks and functions as referred to in paragraph (2), the regional government cooperates with government agencies, provincial governments, other regency/city governments, communities, families and parents.
  - (4) Further provisions regarding the procedures for services and handling of child and women victims of violence as referred to in paragraph (1), paragraph (2), and paragraph (3) are regulated by Regent Regulation.

# CHAPTER VI

GUIDANCE AND SUPERVISION

#### Article 24

(1) The Regional Government conducts guidance and supervision of the organization of protection for child and women victims of violence.

(2) Guidance as referred to in paragraph (1) includes: a. guidelines and fulfillment standards;

b. technical guidance and training;

c. provision of facilities;

d. monitoring; and

e. evaluation.

(3) Supervision as referred to in paragraph (1) is carried out with the principles:

- a. professional;
- b. transparent; and
- c. accountable.

#### Article 25

Guidance and supervision as referred to in Article 24 paragraph (2) and paragraph (3) are carried out in order to realize the objectives of organizing protection for child and women victims of violence in accordance with minimum service standards established by laws and regulations.

# CHAPTER VI I

REPORTING

#### Article 26

(1) PPT reports the implementation of organizing protection for child and women victims of violence to the Regent.

(2) Reporting as referred to in paragraph (1) is submitted in writing, including:

- a. administration;
- b. finance;
- c. services; and
- d. performance.
  - (3) Submission of written reports as referred to in paragraph (2) is carried out at least once every 3 (three) months.

# CHAPTER VII I

FUNDING

#### Article 27

Funds for organizing protection for child and women victims of violence are sourced from:

- a. Regional Revenue and Expenditure Budget; and
- b. other legal sources in accordance with the provisions of laws and regulations.

#### Article 28

(1) The Regional Government may provide financing assistance to community organizations, social organizations, or non-governmental organizations and universities that carry out protection for child and women victims of violence.

(2) Financing assistance as referred to in paragraph (1) is in accordance with regional financial capacity and is implemented in accordance with the provisions of laws and regulations in the field of regional finance.

# CHAPTER IX

CLOSING PROVISIONS

#### Article 29

This Regional Regulation comes into force on the date of promulgation. So that everyone knows it, orders the promulgation of this Regional Regulation by placing it in the Regional Gazette of Konawe Regency.
