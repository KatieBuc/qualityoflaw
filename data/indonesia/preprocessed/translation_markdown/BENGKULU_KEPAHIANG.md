# CHAPTER I

GENERAL PROVISIONS

#### Article 1

In this Regional Regulation, the following terms are defined as:

- 1. Region is the District Region of Kepahiang.
- 2. Regent is the Regent of Kepahiang.
- 3. Regional Government is the Regent as an element of the Regional Government administration who leads the implementation of government affairs that are autonomous authority.
- 4. Regional House of Representatives, hereinafter abbreviated as DPRD, is a regional people's representative institution positioned as an element of Regional Government administration.
- 5. Child is a person who has not reached 18 years of age, including a child still in the womb.
- 6. Woman is a person of female gender.
- 7. Violence is any act that results in or may result in misery or suffering, whether physical, sexual, economic, social, and psychological to the victim.
- 8. Violence against women and children is any action that results in or may result in misery or suffering of women physically, sexually, economically, socially, psychologically, including threats of certain actions, coercion or deprivation of freedom, whether occurring in public or private life.
- 9. Victim is a woman and child who experiences acts of violence occurring in the territory of Kepahiang District.
- 10. Protection of women and children is a series of fulfillment of the rights of women and child victims of violence ranging from prevention, handling, recovery and empowerment.
- 11. Service is action taken as soon as possible to the victim when seeing, hearing and knowing of the impending, ongoing or occurred violence against the victim.
- 12. Companion is a person or representative from an institution who has the expertise to accompany victims to conduct counseling, therapy and advocacy for strengthening and recovery of violence victims.
- 13. Integrated Service Center for the Empowerment of Women and Children, hereinafter abbreviated as P2TP2A, is a cooperative network that carries out the function of integrated services for the protection of women and child victims of violence.
- 14. Safe House is a residence for victims, during the process of assistance, and recovery for the safety and comfort of victims from threats and actions of perpetrators.
- 15. Standard Operating Procedures, hereinafter abbreviated as SOP, are standard steps that must be taken in protecting victims starting from complaints, identification, health and psychological recovery, legal services up to repatriation and reintegration of victims into society.
- 16. Family is the smallest unit in society consisting of husband and wife, or husband-wife and their children, or father and his children, or mother and her children, or blood relatives in a straight line upward or downward up to the third degree.
- 17. Household is husband, wife, and children, and people who have family relationships due to blood relations, marriage, breastfeeding, care, and representation, and/or domestic workers and residing in that household.
- 18. Regional Revenue and Expenditure Budget, hereinafter abbreviated as APBD, is the APBD of Kepahiang District.

# CHAPTER II

PRINCIPLES AND OBJECTIVES

#### Article 2

The principles of protection for children and women who are victims of violence are:

- a. respect and fulfillment of victims' rights as human rights; b. justice and equality;
- c. non-discrimination;
- d. rights-based handling; and
- e. best interests of the victim.

#### Article 3

The objectives of protection for women and children are:

- a. prevent acts of violence against women and children; b. eliminate all forms of violence and exploitation against women and children;
- c. protect, and provide a sense of security for women and children; d. provide services to women and children both as victims of acts of violence, as well as reporters, and witnesses;
- e. ensure handling and resolution of acts of violence against women and children in a fair and dignified manner; and
- f. carry out recovery and empowerment for women and child victims of acts of violence.

#### Article 4

The scope of protection for women and children includes prevention, service, recovery and empowerment efforts.

# CHAPTER II

RIGHTS OF WOMEN AND CHILD VICTIMS OF VIOLENCE

#### Article 5

Every woman and child victim of violence has the following rights:

- a. the right to have their dignity and worth respected as human beings; b. the right to health and psychological recovery from suffering experienced by the victim;
- c. the right to make their own decisions;
- d. the right to obtain information;
- e. the right to confidentiality of their identity;
- f. the right to complaint handling;
- g. the right to assistance and legal aid;
- h. the right to obtain facilitation in the judicial process; and i. the right to compensation;
- j. the right to social recovery;

#### Article 6

Child victims of violence, in addition to obtaining the rights as referred to in Article 5, also obtain special rights, as follows:

- a. the right to respect for survival, growth and development; b. the right to basic services;
- c. the right to equal protection;
- d. the right to be free from various stigma; and
- e. the right to obtain freedom.

# CHAPTER III

ACTS OF VIOLENCE AGAINST WOMEN AND CHILDREN

#### Article 7

Forms of violence include:

- a. physical violence;
- b. psychological violence;
- c. sexual violence;
- d. neglect;
- e. exploitation; and/or
- f. other violence.

#### Article 8

Physical violence as referred to in Article 7 letter (a) is caused by acts or physical actions that result in pain, injury, wounds or disability to a person's body, miscarriage, fainting and/or causing death.

#### Article 9

Psychological violence as referred to in Article 7 letter (b) is caused by acts that result in fear, loss of self-confidence, loss of ability to act, feelings of helplessness and/or severe psychological suffering to a person.

#### Article 10

Sexual violence as referred to in Article 7 letter (c) is caused by:

- a. acts in the form of sexual harassment;
- b. forced sexual relations;
- c. forced unnatural sexual relations; and/or d. forced sexual relations with others for commercial purposes and or certain purposes.

#### Article 11

Neglect as referred to in Article 7 letter d is caused by:

- a. acts that result in the unfulfillment of the child's needs properly, whether physical, mental, spiritual or social, committed by parents, guardians, or any other party responsible for their care;
- b. acts of intentionally neglecting to maintain, care for, or take care of the child properly, committed by parents, guardians, or any other party responsible for their care;
- c. acts that neglect a person within the scope of their household, even though according to the law applicable to them or due to agreement or contract they are obliged to provide living, care, or maintenance to that person; and
- d. acts that result in economic dependency by limiting and/or prohibiting proper work inside or outside the home so that the victim is under the control of that person.

#### Article 12

Exploitation as referred to in Article 7 letter (e) is caused by:

- a. acts that exploit economically or sexually with the intent to benefit oneself or others;
- b. acts with or without the consent of the victim that include but are not limited to prostitution, forced labor or services, slavery or similar practices, oppression, extortion, exploitation of physical, sexual, reproductive organs, or unlawfully removing or transplanting organs and/or body tissues or exploiting the labor or ability of a person by another party to gain benefits both material and immaterial; and/or
- c. all forms of exploitation of sexual organs or other body organs of the victim to gain benefits, including but not limited to all prostitution or obscenity activities

#### Article 13

Other violence as referred to in Article 7 letter (f) is caused by:

- a. threats of violence include every unlawful act in the form of speech, writing, images, symbols, or body movements, whether with or without using means that cause fear or restrict the fundamental freedom of a person; and
- b. coercion, includes a situation where a person/victim is ordered or forced to do something in such a way that the person does something contrary to their own will.

# CHAPTER III

PROTECTION OF WOMEN AND CHILDREN VICTIMS OF ACTS OF VIOLENCE Part One

Prevention

#### Article 14

(1) Efforts to prevent violence against women and children are carried out in an integrated manner by the Regional Government coordinated by the SKPD which has the main duties and coordination functions in the field of women's empowerment, family planning and child protection.

(2) Prevention efforts as referred to in paragraph (1) are carried out by:

- a. preparing Action Programs and Women and Children Empowerment activities in the Strategic Plan;
- b. forming a work network in efforts to prevent violence; c. conducting coordination, integration, and synchronization of violence prevention against women and children.
- d. establishing a violence prevention system;
- e. conducting socialization regarding laws and regulations related to the rights and protection of women and children against acts of violence;
- f. providing critical education on the rights of women and children to the community.

## Part Two

Services

#### Article 16

The provision of services to victims is carried out with the principles of:

- a. fast;
- b. safe and comfortable;
- c. empathy;
- d. non-discrimination;
- e. easily accessible;
- f. free of charge; and
- g. confidentiality guaranteed.

#### Article 17

Forms of services to victims include:

- a. complaint, consultation, and counseling services;
- b. accompaniment services;
- c. health services;
- d. social recovery services;
- e. legal services; and
- f. repatriation and reintegration services into society.

#### Article 18

Complaint, consultation, and counseling services as referred to in Article 17 letter a include:

- a. identification or initial recording of victims; and
- b. consent to action (inf ormed consent).

#### Article 19

Accompaniment services as referred to in Article 17 letter b include:

- a. accompanying victims during the examination and health recovery process;
- b. accompanying victims during the medicolegal process;
- c. accompanying victims during the examination process at the Police, Prosecutor's Office and court;
- d. monitoring the interests and rights of victims in the examination process at the Police, Prosecutor's Office and Court;
- e. maintaining the privacy and confidentiality of victims from all parties who have no interest, including news coverage by mass media;
- f. coordinating with other companions; and g. providing continuous treatment until the recovery stage.

#### Article 20

Health services as referred to in Article 17 letter c include:

- a. first aid to victims;
- b. treatment and recovery of physical injuries aimed at restoring the physical condition of victims carried out by medical and paramedical personnel; and
- c. referral to health services.

#### Article 21

Social recovery services as referred to in Article 17 letter d are services provided by companions in order to restore the traumatic condition of victims, including the provision of safe houses to protect victims from various threats and intimidation to victims and provide social support so that victims have confidence, strength, and independence in resolving their problems, by means of:

- a. providing spiritual guidance to victims; and b. psychological recovery of victims.

#### Article 22

Legal services as referred to in Article 17 letter e to assist victims in undergoing the judicial process by:

- a. providing legal consultation that includes information regarding the rights of victims and the judicial process;
- b. accompanying victims at the investigation, prosecution, and examination levels in court hearings and assisting victims to fully describe the violence they experienced;
- c. coordinating with fellow law enforcers, volunteer companions, and social workers so that the judicial process runs as it should.

#### Article 23

(1) Repatriation and reintegration services into society as referred to in Article 17 letter f aim to return victims to their families and social environment.

(2) Repatriation and reintegration services into society as referred to in paragraph (1) are carried out by the Regional Government in coordination with related government and non-government agencies and institutions.

Empowerment of Women Victims of Violence

- a. job training;
- b. productive economic enterprises and joint business groups; and c. capital assistance.

Job training as referred to in Article 24 letter a includes:

- a. skills training;
- b. field work practice; and
- c. apprenticeship.

#### Article 26

Productive economic enterprises and joint business groups as referred to in Article 24 letter b include:

- a. entrepreneurial skills training;
- b. facilitation of joint business group formation; and
- c. business implementation accompaniment.

#### Article 27

Capital assistance as referred to in Article 24 letter c includes:

- a. work facilities and infrastructure assistance; and
- b. facilitation of working capital assistance

- b. Village Government and Kutei Traditional Institution of Village/Sub-district c. community;
- d. family;
- e. parents; and
- f. related institutions both directly and indirectly.

## Part Two

Village Government and Kutei Customary Institution of Village/Sub-District Article 31

Village Government in the protection of women and children against acts of violence has the role:

- a. actively participate in preventing the occurrence of physical violence, psychological violence, sexual violence, neglect, exploitation, and/or other violence against women and children;
- b. protect victims;
- c. secure perpetrators caught red-handed and evidence before handing them over to the authorities;
- d. based on the victim's request, the Village Government hands over the perpetrator to the Indonesian National Police, if there are criminal elements; and
- e. provide emergency assistance.

#### Article 32

Village Kutei and/or Sub-District Kutei have obligations and responsibilities in the following matters:

- a. actively participate in preventing the occurrence of physical violence, psychological violence, sexual violence, neglect, exploitation, and/or other violence against women and children based on customary law provisions;
- b. resolve acts of violence against women and children if violations of customary law norms occur;
- c. participate in supervising the implementation of protection for women and children carried out by the government and other institutions.

## Part Three

Community and Family

#### Article 33

The community and family have obligations and responsibilities in the following matters:

- a. prevent the occurrence of physical violence, psychological violence, sexual violence, neglect, exploitation, and/or other violence against women and children;
- b. report if violence occurs;
- c. protect victims;
- d. provide security for perpetrators caught red-handed; and e. provide emergency assistance.
- f. participate in supervising the implementation of protection for women and children carried out by the government and other institutions.

#### Article 35

(1) In the event of being caught red-handed as referred to in Article 33 letter d, the community may secure the perpetrator before handing them over to the authorities.

(2) The obligations of the family and/or parents as referred to in Article 28 letters b and e, who legally have full responsibility to prevent all forms of violence and protect women and children as family members;

(3) The role of the community as referred to in Article 34 paragraph (1) is carried out by individuals, child protection institutions, social community institutions, non-governmental organizations, educational institutions, religious institutions, business entities and mass media.

# CHAPTER V

P2TP2A

#### Article 38

(1) The Regional Government, in order to provide services and protection to women and children victims of acts of violence, establishes the P2TP2A.

(2) The P2TP2A as referred to in paragraph (1) is a functional work unit that has the principal duties and functions of providing integrated protection to women and children from acts of violence.

#### Article 39

(1) The organizational structure of the P2TP2A formed by the Regional Government as referred to in Article 19 paragraph (1) consists of: a. general chairperson or other equivalent title;

b. secretary;

c. treasurer; and

d. executive chairperson who oversees the fields.

(2) The Fields as referred to in paragraph (1) letter d, at a minimum include:

- a. complaint service field;
- b. health service field;
- c. social recovery service field;
- d. field of repatriation and reintegration into society; and e. advocacy and legal aid service field.
  - (3) The organizational structure of the P2TP2A as referred to in paragraph (1) shall be determined by the Regent.

#### Article 40

The complaint service field as referred to in Article 39 paragraph (2) letter a has the duties:

- a. conduct interviews and observation of the victim's condition;
- b. make recommendations for follow-up services;
- c. conduct coordination and referral for follow-up services with relevant parties; and
- d. administer the complaint process.

#### Article 41

The health service field as referred to in Article 39 paragraph (2) letter b has the duties:

- a. conduct examination, treatment, and follow-up care for victims;
- b. coordinate the implementation of health recovery and medicolegal services;
- c. conduct medicolegal examination including collection of evidence from victims and preparation of visum et repertum;
- d. conduct supporting and laboratory examinations on evidence;
- e. conduct consultations with specialist doctors or make referrals; and f. prepare case reports.

#### Article 42

The social recovery service field as referred to in Article 39 paragraph (2) letter c has the duties:

- a. provide assistance during the case handling process; b. conduct counseling; and
- c. facilitate obtaining a psychological certificate for the victim.

#### Article 43

The field of repatriation and reintegration into society as referred to in Article 39 paragraph (2) letter d has the duties:

- a. coordinate with relevant agencies for the repatriation of victims; b. prepare progress reports on the repatriation assistance process and social recovery; and
- c. conduct monitoring for at least three months after the victim is returned to their family.

#### Article 44

The legal aid service field as referred to in Article 39 paragraph (2) letter e has the duties:

- a. accompany and defend every legal service process; and b. prepare progress reports on legal handling.

#### Article 45

Further provisions regarding the development, mechanisms and networked working relationships of the P2TP2A with relevant parties in handling violence against women and children shall be stipulated by Regent Regulation.

# CHAPTER VII

SAFE HOUSE

#### Article 46

(2) The management of the Safe House as referred to in paragraph (1) refers to the applicable laws and regulations.

(3) The financing of the Safe House management as referred to in paragraph (2) is charged to the APBD.

(2) The information/reporting system as referred to in paragraph (1) is carried out in accordance with the provisions of laws and regulations.

# CHAPTER IX

GUIDANCE AND SUPERVISION

#### Article 48

- a. coordination;
- b. guidance;
- c. education and training; and

  - (4) Guidance as referred to in paragraph (2) letter b includes aspects related to planning, implementation, procedures, funding, quality, control and supervision.

FUNDING

#### Article 49

(1) Funding for protection activities for victims carried out by the Regional Government is charged to the APBD and/or other legitimate sources in accordance with the provisions of laws and regulations.

(2) Every SKPD related to programs and activities for the protection of women and children against acts of violence must allocate its budget.

# CHAPTER XI

COMMUNITY PARTICIPATION

#### Article 50

(1) The community participates in the implementation of protection for victims.

(2) Community participation as referred to in paragraph (1) is in the form of: a. establishing family partners at the Kelurahan/Village level; b. establishing women and child protection units within community organizations;

c. conducting socialization of women's and children's rights independently; d. providing first aid to victims; and

e. reporting to the competent authorities if violence against victims occurs in their environment.

(3) Community participation as referred to in paragraph (1) is carried out by individuals, social community institutions, non-governmental organizations, educational institutions, religious institutions, and mass media.

I. GENERAL

Acts of violence against women and children constitute violations of human rights and therefore their dignity and honor must be protected and their right to life guaranteed in accordance with their nature and destiny without discrimination.

Sociologically, acts of violence against women and children have been increasing both in quantity and quality. This is in line with technological developments that can be imitated by perpetrators of violence at any time, so that the quality is also increasing. Culturally, the people of Kepahiang mostly work and live on plantations, so that attention and responsibility towards their children is neglected. In order to prevent and overcome violence against women and children in Kepahiang Regency so that they are protected from violence, threats of violence, torture or treatment that degrades the degree and dignity of humanity, it is necessary to provide protection for women and children victims of violence in the form of regional regulations.

Juridically, laws and regulations governing the protection of women and children victims of violence have not yet regulated protection efforts at the Regional level, so institutional support and regulations are needed that can guarantee its implementation.

This Regional Regulation regulates protection efforts for victims, particularly in terms of prevention, services and empowerment of women and children victims of violence in Kepahiang Regency.
