THE REGENT OF REJANG LEBONG,

b. that in order for efforts to prevent and mitigate violence against women and children to obtain optimal results, and to be implemented quickly, in a planned, integrated, comprehensive and organized manner by involving all relevant elements in the region, it is necessary to regulate the prevention and mitigation of violence against women and child victims of violence.

c. that based on the considerations as referred to in letter a and letter b, it is necessary to establish a Regional

Regulation concerning Prevention and Mitigation of

Violence Against Women and Children.

# CHAPTER I

GENERAL PROVISIONS

#### Article 1

In this Regional Regulation, what is meant by:

- 1. Region is Rejang Lebong Regency.
- 2. Regional Government is the administration of governmental affairs by the regional government and Regional House of Representatives according to the principles of autonomy and assistance duties with the principle of the broadest autonomy within the system and principles of the Unitary State of the Republic of Indonesia as referred to in the 1945 Constitution of the Republic of Indonesia.
- 3. Regional Government is the Regent as an element of Regional Government administration who leads the implementation of governmental affairs which are the authority of the autonomous region.
- 4. Regent is the Regent of Rejang Lebong.
- 5. Regional House of Representatives, hereinafter abbreviated as DPRD, is the Regional House of Representatives of Rejang Lebong Regency. 6. Regional Work Unit, hereinafter abbreviated as SKPD, is the Regional Work Unit of Rejang Lebong Regency.
- 7. Regional Apparatus is the auxiliary element of the Regent in the administration of regional government consisting of regional secretariat, DPRD secretariat, regional agencies, regional technical institutions, sub-districts, and urban villages.
- 8. Regional Population and Family Planning Agency, hereinafter abbreviated as BKKBD, is the Regional Population and Family Planning Agency of Rejang Lebong Regency.
- 9. Child is a person who has not reached the age of 18 years, including a child who is still in the womb.
- 10. Woman is an adult human being of female sex and a person who is recognized by law as a woman.
- 11. Prevention is an activity carried out to prevent the occurrence of acts of violence against women and children.
- 12. Protection is all service actions to guarantee and protect the rights of victims of acts of violence organized by the Integrated Service Center.
- 13. Violence is any act that results or causes suffering both physical, sexual, psychological including neglect, threats of certain actions, coercion or arbitrary deprivation of freedom, whether occurring in public or in private life.
- 14. Physical violence is any act that causes pain, injury, wounds or disability to a person's body, miscarriage or fainting and/or causes death.
- 15. Psychological violence is an act that causes fear, loss of self-confidence, loss of ability to act, feelings of helplessness or severe psychological suffering in a person.
- 16. Sexual violence is any act in the form of sexual harassment, coercion of sexual relations, whether unnatural or unwanted with other persons for commercial and/or certain purposes.
- 17. Exploitation is an action with or without the victim's consent which includes but is not limited to prostitution, forced labor or services, slavery or practices similar to slavery, oppression, extortion, physical, sexual exploitation, reproductive organs or unlawfully removing or transplanting organs and/or body tissues or exploiting the strength or abilities of a person by another party to obtain material or immaterial benefits.
- 18. Victim is a woman and child who experiences suffering and or misery either directly or indirectly as a result of violence.
- 19. Victim of acts of violence is an individual or group of people who experience physical, mental or emotional suffering, economic loss, or experience neglect, reduction or deprivation of their basic rights, as a result of human rights violations, or criminal acts as well as acts of violence committed either by state apparatus or by the state or regional government apparatus or by individuals.
- 20. Service is immediate activities and actions carried out by Professionals in accordance with their respective professions in the form of counseling, therapy and advocacy for the empowerment and recovery of victims of violence.
- 21. Assistance is activities and actions carried out by assistants during the service process.
- 22. Assistant is a person or representative from an institution who has expertise in providing assistance.
- 23. Repatriation is an effort to return victims of acts of violence to the family, substitute family, or community that can provide protection and fulfillment of their needs.
- 24. Rehabilitation is the recovery of victims from psychosocial disorders and the restoration of normal social functioning, both in the family and in society.
- 25. Social Reintegration is an effort to reunite victims with family, community, institutions, or other social environments that can provide protection.
- 26. Institution is an agency/office/body within the scope of regional government and/or non-governmental organization (NGO) that provides assistance.
- 27. Integrated Service Center for the Empowerment of Women and Children, hereinafter abbreviated as P2TP2A, is an integrated service unit established by the regional government to provide services in the context of empowerment for Women and Children victims of acts of violence.
- 28. Regional Action Plan, hereinafter abbreviated as RAD, is the foundation and guideline for related agencies, vertical agencies, and the community, in order to carry out activities for the prevention and protection of women and children victims of acts of violence.
- 29. Safe House is temporary housing, provided to give protection to victims in accordance with predetermined standards.
- 30. Community is individuals, families, groups, social organizations and/or community organizations.
- 31. Family is persons who have blood relations in direct line upwards or downwards and collateral line up to the third degree, or who have marital relations, or persons who are dependents of women and/or children.
- 32. Parents are biological father and/or mother, or step father and/or mother, or adoptive father and/or mother.
- 33. Regional Revenue and Expenditure Budget, hereinafter abbreviated as APBD, is the annual financial plan of the Regional Government which is discussed and jointly approved by the Regional Government and DPRD and established by Regional Regulation.

# CHAPTER II

PRINCIPLES, PURPOSE AND OBJECTIVES

## Part One

Principles

#### Article 2

The implementation of prevention and handling of violence against women and children, is carried out based on the principles of:

- a. respect for the rights of women and children;
- b. justice and gender equality;
- c. non-discrimination in the empowerment of women and children; d. the best interests of women and children; and e. restoration of social and economic rights for women and children who experience acts of violence or neglect.

## Part Two

Purpose

#### Article 3

The regulation of the implementation of prevention and handling of violence against women and children, is intended to provide protection and services for women and children victims of acts of violence.

## Part Three

Objectives

#### Article 4

The objectives of implementing prevention and handling of violence against women and children, include:

- a. preventing acts of violence or neglect against women and children including trafficking in persons;
- b. eliminating all forms of violence and exploitation against women and children;
- c. protecting and providing a sense of security for women and children; d. providing services to women and children victims of acts of violence, reporters, and witnesses;
- e. facilitating and conducting mediation on domestic disputes to realize the integrity of a harmonious and prosperous household; and
- f. seeking compensation for losses suffered by victims from perpetrators of violence.

# CHAPTER III

VIOLENCE

#### Article 5

(1) Forms of violence against women and children include: a. physical violence;

b. psychological violence;

c. sexual violence;

d. neglect;

e. exploitation; and/or

f. other violence.

(2) Physical violence as referred to in paragraph (1) letter a is caused by acts resulting in pain, injury, wounds or disability to a person's body, miscarriage, unconsciousness and/or causing death.

(3) Psychological violence as referred to in paragraph (1) letter b is caused by acts resulting in fear, loss of self-confidence, loss of ability to act, feelings of helplessness and/or severe psychological suffering to a person.

(4) Sexual violence as referred to in paragraph (1) letter c is caused by:

- a. acts in the form of sexual harassment;
- b. coercion of sexual relations;
- c. coercion of sexual relations in an unnatural or unwanted manner; and/or
- d. coercion of sexual relations with others for commercial purposes and/or specific purposes.
  - (5) Neglect as referred to in paragraph (1) letter d is caused by:
- a. acts resulting in the non-fulfillment of a child's reasonable needs, whether physical, mental, spiritual or social, committed by parents, guardians, or any other party responsible for their care;
- b. acts of deliberately neglecting to maintain, care for, or attend to a child as appropriate, committed by parents, guardians, or any other party responsible for their care;
- c. acts of abandoning a person within their household, when according to the law applicable to them or by consent or agreement they are obliged to provide livelihood, care, or maintenance to that person; and/or
- d. acts resulting in economic dependency by restricting and/or prohibiting proper work inside or outside the home so that the victim is under the control of that person.
  - (6) Exploitation as referred to in paragraph (1) letter e is caused by:
- a. acts of economic or sexual exploitation with the intent to benefit oneself or others; b. acts with or without the victim's consent which include but are not limited to prostitution, forced labor or service, slavery or similar practices, oppression, extortion, exploitation of physical, sexual, reproductive organs, or unlawfully removing or transplanting organs and/or body tissue or exploiting a person's labor or abilities by another party to obtain benefits both material and immaterial; and/or
- c. all forms of exploitation of sexual organs or other body organs of the victim to obtain benefits, including but not limited to all activities of prostitution or indecency.
  - (7) Other violence as referred to in paragraph (1) letter f is caused by:
- a. threats of violence include: any unlawful act in the form of speech, writing, images, symbols, or body movements, whether with or without the use of means that cause fear or restrict a person's fundamental freedom; and
- b. coercion, includes: a situation where a person/victim is ordered to do something in such a way that the person does something contrary to their own will.

# CHAPTER IV

RIGHTS OF WOMEN AND CHILDREN VICTIMS OF VIOLENCE

#### Article 6

(1) Every woman and child who becomes a victim of physical violence, exploitation or human trafficking has the right to receive protection and services.

(2) Protection and services for victims as referred to in paragraph (1) include:

- a. complaints or requests for protection from the concerned party; b. confidential handling or placement of victims in safe locations and houses whose whereabouts are kept secret to avoid intimidation and threats;
- c. medical services/health care in the form of treatment and recovery of wounds or physical condition of victims by medical and paramedical personnel;
- d. medicolegal services for evidence in the legal field; e. protection by law enforcement officers;
- f. provision of information regarding legal rights and obligations to victims or guardians of victims as well as identification of incidents;
- g. continuous handling up to the rehabilitation stage;
- h. psychological assistance to restore the traumatic condition of victims and restore the victim's self-confidence;
- i. assistance for legal processes and obtaining the right to damages or compensation;
- j. guarantee of rights related to status as a family member and community member (social reintegration); and k. economic independence services in the form of skills training and providing economic access so that victims can be independent.
  - (3) In the case where the victim is a child, in addition to receiving the rights as referred to in paragraph (2), they also receive special rights including:
- a. the right to respect and full use for survival, growth and development;
- b. the right to basic services;
- c. the right to equal protection;
- d. the right to be free from various stigmas;
- e. the right to obtain freedom; and
- f. the right to obtain compensation from the perpetrator.

# CHAPTER V

OBLIGATIONS AND RESPONSIBILITIES

#### Article 7

Obligations and responsibilities in conducting prevention and handling in the form of protection and services for women and children from acts of violence are joint responsibilities by: a. regional government;

b. community;

c. family; and

d. parents.

#### Article 8

(1) Obligations and responsibilities of Regional Government as referred to in Article 7 letter a, include:

- a. implementing obligations for protection of women and children from acts of violence established by the government;
- b. establishing policies, programs and activities for protection of women and children from acts of violence;
- c. conducting cooperation in the implementation of protection of women and children from acts of violence;
- d. providing support for facilities and infrastructure for the implementation of protection of women and children from acts of violence;
- e. providing shelters for women and children victims of violence;
- f. allocating budget for the implementation of protection of women and children from acts of violence in accordance with regional financial capacity; and
- g. fostering and supervising the implementation of protection of women and children from acts of violence.
  - (2) The implementation of obligations and responsibilities as referred to in paragraph (1), is technically carried out in an integrated manner by the relevant SKPD.

#### Article 9

(1) Obligations and responsibilities of the community as referred to in Article 7 letter b, are conducted in the form of community participation. (2) Forms of community participation as referred to in paragraph (1), include:

- a. forming family partners at the sub-district/village level by the community; b. forming women and child protection units within community organizations;
- c. conducting socialization of women's and children's rights independently; d. providing first aid to victims; and
- e. reporting to the competent authorities if violence against victims occurs in their environment.
  - (3) Community participation as referred to in paragraph (1) is carried out by individuals, social community institutions, non-governmental organizations, educational institutions, religious institutions, private sector, and mass media.
  - (4) Forms of community participation as referred to in paragraph (2), are carried out responsibly in accordance with the provisions of Laws and Regulations.

#### Article 10

The obligations and responsibilities of the family and parents towards children as referred to in Article 7 letter c and letter d are in the form of: a. caring for, maintaining, educating and protecting children; b. preventing the occurrence of marriage at an early age;

c. ensuring the continuity of children's education in accordance with the abilities, talents and interests of the child; and

d. in the event that the parents are absent or their whereabouts are unknown or due to certain reasons, cannot carry out their obligations and responsibilities, then the obligations and responsibilities as referred to in paragraph (1) may be transferred to the family as the child's guardian, which is implemented in accordance with the provisions of the prevailing laws and regulations.

# CHAPTER VI

INSTITUTIONAL ARRANGEMENTS

## Part One

Organization

#### Article 11

(1) In order to carry out prevention and mitigation activities against violence towards women and children, the regional government establishes P2TP2A as an integrated service center for women and children victims of violence.

(2) P2TP2A as referred to in paragraph (1) is established by joint initiative between the Regional Government, NGOs, Universities, professional organizations, organizations and the community.

(3) The institutional structure of P2TP2A as referred to in paragraph (2) is determined by Regent's Decree.

(4) Further provisions regarding the organization of P2TP2A as referred to in paragraph (1), are regulated by Regent's Regulation.

#### Article 12

P2TP2A establishes functional networks with:

- a. the police;
- b. the prosecutor's office;
- c. the court;
- d. hospitals/community health centers;
- e. psychiatrists; and
- f. other institutions that have similar objectives.

#### Article 13

P2TP2A as an independent institution in the region must be in coordination together with BKKBD whose scope of duties and responsibilities includes the fields of protection of women, children and gender mainstreaming.

## Part Two

Financing of P2TP2A

#### Article 14

(1) The regional government assists the operations of P2TP2A by budgeting in the APBD in accordance with the capabilities and financial condition of the region. (2) Budgeting as referred to in paragraph (1) is through BKKBD. (3) For orderly administration and use of finances, P2TP2A is obligated to prepare a work program and accountability for the use of regional finances.

#### Article 15

(1) Financing of P2TP2A in addition to being obtained from APBD funds may be through obtaining assistance receipts from individuals, agencies, or official institutions whose existence is legally recognized.

(2) P2TP2A is obligated to submit annual reports on performance and finances obtained from APBD to the Regent through BKKBD.

# CHAPTER VII

PREVENTION AND MITIGATION, SERVICES AND EMPOWERMENT

## Part One

General

#### Article 16

The Regional Government through P2TP2A, carries out prevention and mitigation efforts for victims of violence, exploitation and neglect of women and children up to the empowerment of the victims to be able to resume their lives in accordance with their dignity and honor.

## Part Two

Prevention and Mitigation of Violence, Exploitation and Neglect of Women and Children

#### Article 17

(1) The form of prevention as referred to in Article 16 is carried out through:

- a. socialization activities of legislation related to the protection of women and children to the wider community in the region;
- b. activities to disseminate the existence of P2TP2A to be known by everyone in the region through the distribution of brochures, leaflets, pamphlets or bulletin boards so that incidents of violence, exploitation and neglect can be reported as soon as possible and can be handled before reaching the worst level;
- c. training for members who are able to disseminate norms of prohibitions and sanctions to the community;
- d. opening of public complaint channels by the Regional Government or P2TP2A in every area through community networks; e. strengthening coordination channels with District, Sub-district/Village level governments;
- f. immediate handling of public complaints in all areas in the region and securing subjects of violence, exploitation, human trafficking or neglect; and/or
- g. counseling/socialization in the form of awareness raising for the wider community down to the smallest community groups (families), especially parents to protect women and children.
  - (2) The Regional Government through P2TP2A conducts data collection of areas of violence based on incidence rate figures and conducts research and assessment of the level of regional vulnerability to find solutions for reduction up to the cessation of violence, exploitation and neglect in vulnerable point areas.

## Part Three

Services for Women and Children who Experience Violence, Exploitation and Neglect

#### Article 18

The forms of services provided to women and children victims of violence, exploitation, human trafficking or neglect are implemented in accordance with the rights of victims as referred to in Article 6.

#### Article 19

The forms of services to victims organized by P2TP2A as referred to in Article 18, include:

- a. medical services, in the form of treatment and recovery of physical injuries aimed at restoring the physical condition of victims carried out by medical and paramedical personnel;
- b. medico-legal services which are a form of medical services for the purposes of evidence in the legal field;
- c. psycho-social services which are lessons provided by companions in the context of traumatic recovery of the victim's condition, including the provision of space and victim's house to protect victims from threats and intimidation against victims;
- d. legal services to assist victims in undergoing the judicial process;
- e. economic independence services in the form of services for skills training and providing economic access so that victims can be independent; and
- f. other services in accordance with the rights of women and children victims of violence.

#### Article 20

The Regional Government or P2TP2A is obligated to prepare operational standards for assistance services to victims of violence, exploitation, trafficking or neglect in every stage of fulfillment of victims' rights.

#### Article 21

The provision of assistance services to victims is carried out with the principles of:

- a. non-discrimination;
- b. fast, safe, and empathetic;
- c. coordination between government agencies;
- d. guarantee of confidentiality;
- e. easily accessible; and
- f. free of charge.

#### Article 22

Further provisions regarding the procedures for handling the protection of women and children victims of violence and operational standards as referred to in Article 18 and Article 20, are regulated by Regent's Regulation.

## Part Four

Empowerment of Women and Children Victims of Violence Article 23

(1) The Regional Government through P2TP2A is obligated to carry out empowerment in the economic field for women and children victims of violence, exploitation and/or trafficking or neglect.

(2) The forms of empowerment of victims of violence as referred to in paragraph (1), include:

- a. procuring the necessary requirements for entrepreneurship training for victims of violence, in order to improve business knowledge, attitudes, and skills;
- b. facilitating the implementation of various job training and skills training;
- c. providing assistance in developing productive economic enterprises;
- d. exploring cooperation with small, medium and large companies, as well as financial institutions to develop businesses of victims of violence;
- e. seeking to provide capital for victims of violence; and f. expanding access to information and promoting product results of victims of violence.

# CHAPTER VIII

COOPERATION AND PARTNERSHIP

## Part One

Cooperation

#### Article 24

(1) Cooperation is carried out to strengthen the role of P2TP2A in achieving the objectives of the protection of women and children in the regional area. (2) Cooperation as referred to in paragraph (1) is carried out with: a. the government;

b. the provincial government;

c. other regency/city governments;

d. non-governmental institutions; and

e. recognized international institutions.

(3) Cooperation as referred to in paragraph (2) includes: a. exchange of data and information;

b. rehabilitation of victims of violence;

c. repatriation and social reintegration; and

d. provision of evidence and witnesses, and followed up based on the provisions of laws and regulations.

(4) Cooperation as referred to in paragraph (1) and paragraph (2) shall be set forth in the form of a joint agreement.

## Part Two

Partnership

#### Article 25

(1) The Regional Government establishes partnerships with the business world to support the implementation of protection for women and children in the regional area.

(2) Partnership as referred to in paragraph (1) is carried out through: a. notification of employment opportunity information for women victims of violence;

b. education and training for women victims of violence; c. educational assistance for women and children victims of violence who have been withdrawn from their education; and

d. fostering and enhancing economic independence of women victims of violence.

(3) Partnership as referred to in paragraph (1) and paragraph (2) shall be set forth in the form of an agreement.

# CHAPTER IX

REGIONAL ACTION PLAN

#### Article 26

(1) In order to carry out obligations and responsibilities, the regional government establishes programs and action activities for the protection of women and children in 1 (one) RAD as a basis for implementing the protection of women and children from acts of violence.

(2) The RAD as referred to in paragraph (1) is part of the Regional Medium-Term Development Plan.

(3) Further provisions regarding the RAD as referred to in paragraph (1) shall be regulated by Regent Regulation.

# CHAPTER X

GUIDANCE AND SUPERVISION

#### Article 27

(1) The Regent carries out guidance and supervision of the implementation of the protection of women and children from acts of violence.

(2) Guidance as referred to in paragraph (1) includes: a. guidelines and fulfillment standards;

b. technical guidance and training;

c. provision of facilities;

d. monitoring; and

e. evaluation.

(3) Supervision as referred to in paragraph (1) is carried out with the principles of:

- a. professional;
- b. transparent; and
- c. accountable.
  - (4) Guidance and supervision as referred to in paragraph (2) and paragraph (3) are intended to realize the objectives of implementing the protection of women and children from acts of violence in accordance with minimum service standards implemented by SKPD/UKPD and the community.
  - (5) The Regent may appoint BKKBD whose scope of duties and responsibilities includes the protection of women and children to carry out guidance and supervision.

#### Article 28

The community may supervise the implementation of the protection of women and children from acts of violence organized by the regional government, through a mechanism for conveying aspirations to the Regent or DPRD.

# CHAPTER XI

REPORTING

#### Article 29

(1) P2TP2A submits reports on the implementation of prevention and protection of women and children victims of violence to the Regent through the SKPD that oversees women and children empowerment programs.

(2) Reporting as referred to in paragraph (1) includes: a. administration;

b. finance;

c. services; and

d. performance.

(3) Submission of reports referred to in paragraph (2) shall be carried out in writing and implemented at least 1 (one) time in 3 (three) months.

# CHAPTER XII

INVESTIGATION

#### Article 30

(1) In addition to general investigating officials, investigations of criminal acts of violence against women and children in this Regional Regulation may also be carried out by Civil Servant Investigators whose appointment is in accordance with laws and regulations.

(2) Civil Servant Investigators as referred to in paragraph (1) have the authority to:

- a. receive reports or complaints from someone about the existence of criminal acts of violence against women and children;
- b. take first action at that time at the scene and conduct an examination;
- c. interrogate a suspect and examine the suspect's identification;
- d. seize objects and/or documents;
- e. take fingerprints and photograph a person;
- f. summon persons to be heard and examined as suspects or witnesses;
- g. bring in experts as needed in connection with the examination of cases;
- h. terminate the investigation after receiving instructions from the General Investigator that there is insufficient evidence, or that the incident does not constitute a criminal act and subsequently through the General Investigator notify the public prosecutor, suspect or his/her family; and/or
- i. take other necessary actions for the smooth investigation of criminal acts of violence against women and children, which are legally accountable.
  - (3) Investigators as referred to in paragraph (2) shall notify the commencement of the investigation and submit the results of their investigation to the public prosecutor through the Investigator Official of the State Police of the Republic of Indonesia, in accordance with the provisions regulated in the Criminal Procedure Code.

# CHAPTER XIII

SANCTION PROVISIONS

#### Article 31

(1) Any person who deliberately fails to provide protection to women and children thereby causing acts of violence, allows acts of violence to occur, and/or fails to report and provide protection to victims, shall be subject to sanctions as stipulated in Laws and Regulations.

(2) Officials appointed to organize protection who fail to carry out their obligations and responsibilities shall be subject to sanctions in accordance with the provisions of Laws and Regulations.

(3) P2TP2A managers who carry out service and protection duties for Children and Women from acts of violence and violate service principles may be subject to sanctions in accordance with the internal mechanisms of the P2TP2A organization.

# CHAPTER XIV

SPECIAL PROVISIONS

#### Article 32

(1) Handling of legal cases in the investigation process by law enforcement officials must be handled by special investigators who have been designated according to their competence by law enforcement institutions for the field of protection of women and children.

(2) Special investigators as referred to in paragraph (1) are: a. in investigation cases for women or girls who are victims of violence must be handled by female investigators;

b. the implementation process as referred to in letter a must be in a closed room attended only by the victim's parents or guardian along with the advocacy and psychology team from P2TP2A of the same gender;

c. in investigation cases for boys who are victims of violence may be conducted by male or female investigators, preferably of the same gender and carried out as referred to in letter b; and d. Trial proceedings are not open to the public.

#### Article 33

In the case of trial proceedings related to cases of violence against children, the presence of the child does not necessarily have to be presented at the trial, except in circumstances that are very compelling while still separating the meeting between the child and the perpetrator in trial proceedings at different times.

#### Article 34

In the event the perpetrator of a criminal act is a child, the following matters apply:

- a. the legal process is carried out in a humane manner considering that the child still has hope for their future;
- b. The Investigator must be a person who has competence in special investigation for cases handling children;
- c. Investigation is conducted in a closed room and only attended by the advocacy team and the child's parent/guardian;
- d. during the investigation process, the child is not placed in general detention, but rather entrusted to their family with supervision and custody;
- e. the trial process is carried out specifically in a juvenile court room; and
- f. the sentencing process for children is placed in a special juvenile correctional institution, with a transition period that may be accompanied by their parents.

#### Article 35

News media are not permitted to expose news coverage for cases concerning child victims of sexual violence in order to protect the child's identity and their hope for the future unless the victim loses their life as a result of such violence.
