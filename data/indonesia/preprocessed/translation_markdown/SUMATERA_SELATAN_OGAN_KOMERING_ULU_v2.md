REGIONAL REGULATION

OF OGAN KOMERING ULU REGENCY

NUMBER 1 OF 2022

CONCERNING

WOMEN'S EMPOWERMENT AND CHILD PROTECTION BY THE GRACE OF ALMIGHTY GOD

REGENT OF OGAN KOMERING ULU,

Considering : a. that every woman and child has the right to survival, to grow, and to develop properly and has the right to protection from violence and discrimination as mandated in the 1945 Constitution of the Republic of Indonesia;

b. that discriminatory treatment and violence against women and children will impact the quality of human resources in the future;

c. that in order to realize the vision and mission of development in the field of women's empowerment and child protection, respect, protection, fulfillment, enforcement, and advancement of the rights of women and children, it is deemed necessary to establish policies that can ensure the implementation of women's empowerment and child protection;

d. that based on the considerations as referred to in letter a, letter b, and letter c, it is necessary to establish a Regional Regulation concerning Women's Empowerment and Child Protection;

In view of : 1. Article 18 paragraph (6) of the 1945 Constitution of the Republic of Indonesia;

# CHAPTER I

GENERAL PROVISIONS

#### Article 1

In this Regional Regulation, what is meant by:

- 1. Regency is Ogan Komering Ulu Regency.
- 2. Regency Government is the Government of Ogan Komering Ulu Regency.
- 3. Regent is the Regent of Ogan Komering Ulu.
- 4. Regional Apparatus, hereinafter abbreviated as PD, is the Regional Apparatus within the Government of Ogan Komering Ulu Regency.
- 5. Gender is a concept that refers to the differentiation of roles and responsibilities of men and women that occurs as a result of and can be changed by the social and cultural conditions of society.
- 6. Gender Equality, hereinafter abbreviated as KG, is the equality of conditions for men and women to obtain opportunities and their rights as human beings, in order to be able to play a role and participate in political, economic, socio-cultural, defense and security activities and equality in enjoying the results of development.
- 7. Gender Analysis is a process that is systematically constructed to identify and understand the division of labor or roles of men and women, access, and control over development resources, participation in the development process and the benefits they enjoy.
- 8. Gender Mainstreaming, hereinafter abbreviated as PUG, is a strategy built to integrate gender into an integral dimension of planning, formulation, implementation, monitoring, and evaluation of policies, programs and development activities in the region.
- 9. Gender responsive is a policy, program, activity, and budgeting that reduces differences, needs, experiences, and aspirations of men and women.
- 10. Gender Responsive Budget, hereinafter abbreviated as ARG, is a budget that is responsive to the needs of women and men with the aim of realizing gender equality and justice.
- 11. Women's empowerment is an effort by women to obtain access and control over economic, political, social, and cultural resources, so that women can organize themselves and increase self-confidence to be able to play a role and participate actively in solving problems, so as to be able to build capacity and self-concept.
- 12. Protection of women is all efforts aimed at protecting women and providing a sense of security in the fulfillment of their rights by providing consistent and systematic attention aimed at achieving gender equality.
- 13. Child is a person who has not reached the age of 18 (eighteen) years, including a child still in the womb.
- 14. Child Protection is all activities to guarantee and protect the Child and their rights so that they can live, grow, develop, and participate optimally in accordance with human dignity and worth, and receive protection from violence and discrimination.
- 15. Special Protection is a form of protection received by a Child in certain situations and conditions to obtain a guarantee of security against threats that endanger their body and life in their growth and development.
- 16. Violence is any unlawful act that results in suffering or distress physically, sexually, mentally, psychologically, including neglect and ill-treatment that threatens and/or endangers body and soul and degrades dignity.
- 17. Special Protection for Children is a form of protection received by children in certain situations and conditions to obtain a guarantee of security against threats that endanger their body and life in their growth and development.
- 18. Children in Emergency Situations are Children who are in an environmental situation that threatens and disturbs the life and livelihood of the Child caused by natural, non-natural, and/or social factors.
- 19. Children in Conflict with the Law are Children in conflict with the law, Children who are victims of criminal acts, and Children who are witnesses to criminal acts.
- 20. Children from Minority and Isolated Groups are Children who are left behind, foremost, outermost in an environment with different cultures, traditions, ethnicities, races, religions from other children whose numbers are far fewer than Children of other groups.
- 21. Children Who Are Economically Exploited are Children who are victims of prostitution, forced labor or services, slavery or practices similar to slavery, oppression, extortion, physical, sexual, reproductive organ exploitation, or unlawfully transferring or transplanting organs and/or body tissues or exploiting the labor or ability of a Child by another party or other similar actions to obtain material benefits.
- 22. Children Who Are Sexually Exploited are Children who are exploited for profit through sexual organs or other body organs of the Child, including but not limited to all prostitution and sexual abuse activities.
- 23. Children Who Are Victims of Narcotics, Alcohol, Psychotropic Substances, and Other Addictive Substances Abuse are Children who are persuaded, deceived, tricked, forced, and/or threatened to use narcotics, alcohol, psychotropic substances, and other addictive substances.
- 24. Children Who Are Victims of Pornography are Children who experience trauma or suffering as a result of the crime of pornography.
- 25. Human Immunodeficiency Virus, hereinafter abbreviated as HIV, is a virus that attacks the immune system and if not treated can reduce human immunity until the condition of Acquired Immuno Deficiency Syndrome occurs.
- 26. Acquired Immuno Deficiency Syndrome, hereinafter abbreviated as AIDS, is a collection of symptoms of reduced self-defense ability caused by the entry of the HIV virus into a person's body.
- 27. Children with HIV and AIDS are Children infected with HIV and/or AIDS either transmitted from parents or from other risk factors.
- 28. Child Victim of Kidnapping is a Child who is taken by someone unlawfully with the intention of placing the Child under their power or the power of another person or to place the Child in a helpless state.
- 29. Child Victim of Sale is a Child who is transferred by a person or group of people to another party for a reward or other reason.
- 30. Child Victim of Trafficking is a Child who experiences psychological, mental, physical, sexual, economic, and/or social suffering resulting from acts of recruitment, transportation, sheltering, sending, transfer, or receipt by threat of violence, use of violence, kidnapping, confinement, forgery, fraud, abuse of power or vulnerable position, debt bondage or giving payment or benefits, so as to obtain consent from the person holding control over the other person, whether carried out within the country or between countries, for the purpose of exploitation or resulting in the exploitation of the Child.
- 31. Child Victim of Physical Violence is a Child who experiences violence that causes pain, illness, or serious injury.
- 32. Child Victim of Psychological Violence is a Child who experiences fear, loss of self-confidence, loss of ability to act, a sense of helplessness, and/or severe psychological suffering.
- 33. Child Victim of Sexual Crime is a Child who experiences forced sexual intercourse, forced sexual intercourse in an unnatural and/or undesired manner, and forced sexual intercourse with others for commercial purposes and/or certain purposes.
- 34. Child Victim of Terrorism Network is a Child who experiences physical, mental suffering, and/or economic loss caused by the crime of terrorism either as a child victim, child perpetrator, child of the perpetrator, and child witness.
- 35. Child with Disabilities is a Child who experiences physical, intellectual, mental, and/or sensory limitations in the long term which in interacting with the environment may experience obstacles and difficulties to participate fully and effectively based on equal rights.
- 36. Child Victim of Maltreatment is a Child who is threatened physically and non-physically due to acts of violence, treated wrongly or improperly in the family environment or the closest social environment, so that their basic needs are not properly fulfilled either physically, spiritually, or socially.
- 37. Child Victim of Neglect is a Child who does not receive the fulfillment of needs such as but not limited to health, safety, and welfare needs from parents or other persons who have legal responsibility to care for the Child, resulting in harm in the Child's growth and development process.
- 38. Child with Deviant Social Behavior is a Child whose attitude and behavior does not consider the judgment and existence of other people in general around them, shows an irresponsible attitude and lack of remorse regarding their mistakes, and often violates the rights and norms that live in society.
- 39. Child Who Is a Victim of Stigmatization from Labeling related to Parents' Condition is a Child who is given a negative social label based on prejudice and aimed at separating, differentiating, discrediting, and ostracizing the Child with a bad stigma or view from labeling related to their parents' condition.
- 40. Substitute Family is foster parents, adoptive parents, and guardians who carry out the role and responsibility to provide alternative care for the Child.
- 41. Educational Institution is an education unit as referred to in the laws and regulations concerning the national education system.
- 42. Counseling is a process carried out in the form of interviews to help Children understand themselves better, so that Children can overcome difficulties in adjusting themselves to various roles and relationships and find the right problem solving.
- 43. Social Assistance is a dynamic interaction between social workers and Children who need Special Protection to jointly face and solve social problems encountered.
- 44. Social Rehabilitation is a process of refunctionalization and development to enable Children to be able to carry out their social functions properly in community life.
- 45. Social Reintegration is a process of preparing Children who need Special Protection to be able to return to the family and community environment.
- 46. Fulfillment of Children's Rights, hereinafter abbreviated as PHA, is a child protection strategy by integrating children's rights into every development activity from the preparation of planning, budgeting, implementation, monitoring, and evaluation of various laws and regulations, policies, programs, and activities by applying the principle of the best interests of the child.
- 47. Discrimination is any restriction, harassment, or exclusion, either directly or indirectly based on differentiation of human beings on the basis of religion, ethnicity, race, group, social status, economic status, gender, language, political beliefs, legal status of children, and physical and/or mental conditions that result in reduction, deviation, or elimination, recognition, implementation or use of human rights and fundamental freedoms in life both individually and collectively in the political, economic, legal, socio-cultural fields and other aspects of life.
- 48. Exploitation is an act with or without the consent of the victim that includes but is not limited to prostitution, forced labor or services, slavery or practices similar to slavery, oppression, extortion, physical sexual exploitation, reproductive organs or unlawfully transferring or transplanting organs and/or body tissues or exploiting the labor or ability of a person by another party to obtain benefits both material and immaterial.
- 49. Parents are biological father and/or mother, or step father and/or mother, or adoptive father and/or mother.
- 50. Guardian is a person or entity that in reality exercises parental authority as a parent to a child.
- 51. Family is the smallest unit in society consisting of husband, wife and their children, or father and his children, or mother and her children, or blood relatives in a direct line upward or downward up to the third degree.
- 52. Society is individuals, families, groups and social organizations and/or community organizations, business world.

# CHAPTER II

WOMEN'S EMPOWERMENT

## Part One

General

#### Article 2

( 1 ) Women's empowerment in the Regency is implemented through the protection of women's rights. ( 2 ) Empowerment as referred to in paragraph (1) is implemented in order to fulfill women's rights in improving the quality of women's lives.

( 3 ) Women's rights as referred to in paragraph (2) must be guaranteed by the Regency Government. ( 4 ) Women's rights as referred to in paragraph (3) include the field of education, field of health, field of employment, field of politics, field of law, field of economic culture and social.

## Part Two

Obligations of the Regency Government

#### Article 3

( 1 ) The Regency Government must guarantee the protection of women's rights.

( 2 ) Protection of women's rights as referred to in paragraph (1) is carried out by:

- a. formulation of gender-responsive regional policies;
- b. institutionalization of PUG within the Regency Government;
- c. women's empowerment in the field of education, field of health, field of employment, field of politics, field of law, field of economic culture and social in community organizations at the Regency level; and
- d. strengthening and development of institutions providing women's empowerment services at the regency level.

#### Article 4

(1) The formulation of gender-responsive Regency policies as referred to in Article 3 paragraph (2) letter a serves as a guideline in the formulation of regional development plans.

(2) The formulation of development plans as referred to in paragraph (1) includes long-term plans, medium-term plans, and regional government work plans.

(3) The long-term development plan, medium-term plan, and regional government work plan as referred to in paragraph (2) must serve as a guideline for PD in the formulation of strategic plans and work plans in their respective work environments.

(4) The formulation of development plans as referred to in paragraph (3) is carried out through gender-responsive planning through gender analysis and ARG which contains a priority scale for the implementation of women's empowerment.

(5) The formulation of the priority scale for the implementation of women's empowerment as referred to in paragraph (4) is based on the national level priority scale.

(6) The national level priority scale as referred to in paragraph (5) becomes an integral part of the regional development plan.

#### Article 5

In order to ensure the realization of the formulation of gender-responsive policies as referred to in Article 3 paragraph (2) letter a, PD that carries out governmental affairs in the field of regional development planning must:

- a. involve PD that organizes governmental affairs in the field of women's empowerment in the formulation of regional development plans; and
- b. evaluate programs and activities at the PD level that are not gender-responsive.

#### Article 6

(1) The institutionalization of PUG as referred to in Article 3 paragraph (2) letter b is implemented in all PD within the Regency Government.

(2) To accelerate the institutionalization of PUG as referred to in paragraph (1), the Regent may form a PUG Working Group at the Regency level.

(3) Members of the PUG Pokja as referred to in paragraph (2) are all Heads/Leaders of PD.

(4) Further provisions regarding the duties and procedures for membership of the PUG Pokja are regulated by Regent Regulation.

#### Article 7

( 1 ) In order to strengthen and develop PUG, the Regent may form a PUG Focal Point.

( 2 ) The PUG Focal Point as referred to in paragraph (1) is PD apparatus who have the ability to carry out gender mainstreaming in their respective work units.

( 3 ) The Focal Point as referred to in paragraph (1) is formed in each PD.

( 4 ) Further provisions regarding the duties, composition of membership and procedures for the formation of the Focal Point are implemented in accordance with the provisions of laws and regulations.

#### Article 8

Women's empowerment in the field of education, field of health, field of employment, field of politics, field of law, field of economic culture and social in community organizations at the Regency level as referred to in Article 3 paragraph (2) letter c is part of temporary special policies provided by the Regency Government.

#### Article 9

Women's empowerment in the field of education is implemented by means including:

- a. facilitating women to obtain education and teaching in all types, levels and pathways of education in accordance with predetermined requirements;
- b. education policies have encouraged the equalization, improvement and quality relevance of education for all genders; and
- c. equalization of learning opportunities for women and men.

#### Article 10

Women's empowerment in the field of health is implemented by means including:

- a. facilitating women's health through health program activities implemented by the government such as:
  - 1. Maternal mortality reduction program;
  - 2. Mother and child health program (KIA);
  - 3. Healthy environment, water sanitation program;
  - 4. Malaria eradication program;
  - 5. Pulmonary tuberculosis eradication program; 6. HIV/AIDS prevention program;
  - 7. Community nutrition improvement program;
- b. Women are given the right to receive special protection in the performance of their work or profession against matters that may threaten their safety and/or health;
- c. Women are given special rights inherent to women due to their reproductive function, guaranteed and protected by law.

#### Article 11

Women's empowerment in the field of employment is implemented by means including:

- a. equal wages for male and female workers for the same work;
- b. prohibition of discrimination against female workers;
- c. prohibition of termination for female workers who marry, are pregnant, and give birth;
- d. guidelines for employing female workers at night;
- e. providing flexibility for female workers who are breastfeeding children;
- f. prohibition of discrimination against female workers in company regulations; and
- g. regarding malnutrition in female workers.

#### Article 12

Women's empowerment in the field of politics is implemented by means including:

- a. facilitating the improvement of human resources to prospective legislative candidates at the regency level, and members of political parties/women's community organizations in order to fulfill women's representation in legislative institutions in accordance with laws and regulations;
- b. facilitating the increase of women's political participation in legislative institutions;
- c. providing facilities and infrastructure for women's community organizations or community organizations whose vision and mission is to fight for the fulfillment of rights and protection of women in the Regency in accordance with Regional Financial capacity;
- d. conducting socialization in order to increase awareness of women's rights and women's participation in the field of politics;
- e. encouraging increased involvement of women in community organizations and decision-making activities within the Regency Government; and
- f. encouraging the formation of women's organizations/forums.

#### Article 13

Women's empowerment in the field of law is implemented by means including:

- a. facilitating the improvement of knowledge and legal awareness for women; and
- b. encouraging the formation of organizations that provide free legal services for women.

#### Article 14

(1) Women's empowerment in the economic field is implemented by means including:

- a. facilitating women in efforts to improve the family economy;
- b. providing capital assistance to women, especially migrant workers, women in remote areas, women as heads of households, and women victims of domestic violence; and
- c. implementing home industry and non-agricultural industry development programs.
  - (2) The procedures for providing capital assistance as referred to in paragraph (1) letter b shall be regulated by Regent Regulation.

#### Article 15

Women's empowerment in the cultural field is implemented by means including:

- a. facilitating women in preserving regional indigenous culture;
- b. encouraging women's activities in various cultural arts activities.

#### Article 16

Women's empowerment in the social field is implemented by means including:

- a. facilitating women in increasing social activities in the community environment; and
- b. encouraging women's activities as agents of change in society.

## Part Three

Scope of Women's Empowerment

### Paragraph 1

General

#### Article 17

Women's Protection is carried out by means of: a. prevention of violence against women;

b. provision of services and further referral handling; and

c. strengthening and development of women's protection service provider institutions.

### Paragraph 2

Prevention

#### Article 18

( 1 ) Prevention of violence against women as referred to in Article 17 letter a is implemented by increasing family and community awareness.

( 2 ) Increasing family and community awareness as referred to in paragraph (1) is conducted in an integrated and continuous manner in the form of socialization, guidance and training, and/or counseling.

( 3 ) Socialization, guidance and training, and/or counseling as referred to in paragraph (2) shall be carried out by the technical Regional Apparatus in charge, institutions, organizations, mass media, business community, academics, law enforcers, the community and Regional Technical Implementation Units in accordance with their duties and functions.

#### Article 19

(1) In addition to prevention of violence against women as referred to in Article 18, efforts to prevent violence against women are carried out by enhancing women's insight, knowledge, skills, and independence.

(2) Prevention of violence against women as referred to in paragraph (1) is carried out by means of:

- a. enhancing informal education;
- b. opening accessibility to obtain education, training, funding, income enhancement, and social services; and
- c. providing access to informal employment for women.

### Paragraph 3

Provision of Services and Further Referral Handling Article 20

Provision of services and further referral handling as referred to in Article 17 letter b is intended for:

- a. women victims of domestic violence; b. women victims of human trafficking crimes; c. women in emergency situations and special conditions; and
- d. women victims of other acts of violence requiring Regency-level coordination.

#### Article 21

( 1 ) In addition to the provision of services and handling of service referrals as referred to in Article 20, the Regency Government is obligated to provide special services for:

- a. women belonging to vulnerable and/or minority groups;
- b. elderly women; and
- c. women with special needs.
  - (2) Special services as referred to in paragraph (1) are provided in the form of:
- a. facilitation in obtaining and enjoying their rights in accordance with the provisions of laws and regulations;
- b. facilitation in access to economic resources and natural resources, social security, and basic public services; and
- c. provision of specific needs for women in emergency situations and special conditions.
  - (3) Further provisions regarding special services as referred to in paragraph (2) shall be regulated by Regent Regulation.

#### Article 22

(1) In order to provide services and further referral handling for women as referred to in Article 20, the Regency Government may establish:

- a. a Regency-level Women and Children Protection (PPA) unit;
- b. a task force; and/or
- c. a task unit.
  - (2) The PPA unit, task force, and/or task unit as referred to in paragraph (1) shall be established by Regent Regulation.
  - (3) Further provisions regarding the duties, membership, procedures and/or work task unit of the PPA unit as referred to in paragraph (2) shall be regulated by Regent Regulation.

### Paragraph 4

Strengthening and Development

Women's Protection Service Provider Institutions Article 23

Strengthening of women's protection service provider institutions is carried out by:

- a. establishing a coordination forum or other forums in order to support the handling of victims of violence against women;
- b. providing service units and legal protection assistance for women victims of violence who conduct data collection related to violence against women;
- c. providing information education communication, advocacy, socialization, and technical guidance;
- d. procurement of facilities and infrastructure for women's protection and services in disaster and evacuation areas;
- e. enhancement of cross-sectoral coordination;
- f. establishment of working groups;
- g. formulation of regional action plans; and
- h. information education communication both through electronic and print media and simulations on women's protection.

# CHAPTER III

IMPLEMENTATION OF CHILD PROTECTION

## Part One

General

#### Article 24

(1) In order to implement Child Protection, the Regency Government is obligated to carry out activities:

- a. formulation of policies, programs, and regional development activities that incorporate children's rights perspectives as set forth in regional development planning documents;
- b. formulation of norms, standard procedures and assessment criteria for the fulfillment and protection of children's rights that refer to the norms, standards, procedures and criteria established by the Government;
- c. coordination of child-friendly Regency development implemented in accordance with the provisions of laws and regulations;
- d. facilitation of the implementation of regional children's forums;
- e. enhancement of community participation in the implementation and protection of children's rights;
- f. coordination and cooperation in the fulfillment and protection of Children;
- g. monitoring and evaluation of the implementation of programs for the fulfillment and protection of Children;
- h. guidance of child protection institutions; and i. providing special protection for Children.
  - (2) In addition to the activities as referred to in paragraph (1), the Regency Government may carry out supervision and facilitation of child advocacy institutions in the Regency that are involved in providing assistance to Children requiring special handling.
  - (3) Supervision and facilitation as referred to in paragraph (2) shall be implemented in accordance with the provisions of laws and regulations.
  - (4) Provision of facilitation as referred to in paragraph (3) shall be adjusted to the regional financial capacity.

#### Article 25

(1) Coordination of child-friendly regency development as referred to in Article 24 paragraph (1) letter c is implemented to encourage the acceleration of the realization of a Child-Friendly Regency.

(2) Coordination as referred to in paragraph (1) shall be implemented by related agencies in accordance with their main duties and functions.

(3) Further provisions regarding the form and procedures for coordination as referred to in paragraph (1) shall be regulated in a Regent Regulation.

#### Article 26

(1) Guidance of Child protection institutions as referred to in Article 24 paragraph (1) letter h is implemented in order to improve the effectiveness of implementing the fulfillment of children's rights.

(2) Guidance of Child protection institutions as referred to in paragraph (1) shall be carried out by the Head of the Regional Apparatus whose duties and functions administer government affairs in the field of child protection.

(3) Further provisions regarding institutional guidance and development as referred to in paragraph (1) shall be regulated in a Regent Regulation.

#### Article 27

Activities in the Implementation of Child Protection in the Regency include:

- a. fulfillment of children's rights; and
- b. special protection of children.

## Part Two

Fulfillment of Children's Rights

#### Article 28

The fulfillment of children's rights is the obligation of the Regency Government, Parents, and the community.

#### Article 29

( 1 ) The Regency Government must fulfill children's rights in the areas of:

- a. civil rights and freedoms;
- b. family environment and alternative care; c. basic health and welfare; and
- d. education, utilization of leisure time, and arts and cultural activities.

( 2 ) In fulfilling children's rights as referred to in paragraph (1) letter a, the Regency Government is obligated to:

- a. ensure that children obtain birth certificates free of charge;
- b. provide facilities and infrastructure needed by children to express themselves, think, and create; and
- c. provide facilities and infrastructure so that children can obtain the knowledge they need.
  - (3) In fulfilling children's rights as referred to in paragraph (1) letter b, the Regency Government undertakes efforts to:
- a. provide guidance for families or substitute families to fulfill the rights of children needed during their care;
- b. provide family consultation institutions for child caregivers;
- c. provide child social welfare institutions as an alternative place for child caregivers in the event that families or substitute families cannot provide proper care; and
- d. provide guidance for children as a vulnerable group.
  - (4) In fulfilling children's rights as referred to in paragraph (1) letter c, the Regency Government undertakes efforts to:
- a. reduce infant mortality rates;
- b. reduce malnutrition rates for children;
- c. provide breast milk for infants;
- d. provide immunization for children;
- e. provide reproductive health services;
- f. improve access to welfare improvement for poor families;
- g. improve access to clean water for households; h. create smoke-free zones; and
- i. child-friendly Community Health Centers.
  - (5) In fulfilling children's rights as referred to in paragraph (1) letter d, the Regency Government undertakes efforts to:
- a. provide early childhood education;
- b. provide 12 years of compulsory education;
- c. provide child-friendly schools;
- d. provide safe routes to/from school; and
- e. provide child-friendly creative and recreational activity facilities;
  - (6) Further provisions regarding the forms and procedures for fulfilling children's rights as referred to in paragraph (1) are regulated in Regent Regulations.

#### Article 30

( 1 ) Parents are obligated and responsible to: a. care for, nurture, educate, and protect children;

b. develop children according to their abilities, talents, and interests; and

c. prevent marriage at child age.

(2) In the event that parents are absent, or their whereabouts are unknown, or for any reason, cannot carry out their obligations and responsibilities, then the obligations and responsibilities as referred to in paragraph (1) may transfer to the family, which shall be implemented in accordance with the provisions of laws and regulations.

#### Article 31

( 1 ) The Regency Government is obligated and responsible to conduct prevention and handling of cases of children who are victims of neglect, violence, discrimination, exploitation, child trafficking, abuse, narcotics, psychotropics, and other addictive substances, and/or pornography.

( 2 ) The responsibilities as referred to in paragraph (1) include:

- a. respecting, fulfilling, and protecting children's rights;
- b. conducting socialization about the adverse effects of neglect, violence, discrimination, exploitation, child trafficking, abuse, narcotics, psychotropics, and other addictive substances, and/or pornography;
- c. making policies and/or policy changes regarding child protection;
- d. providing assistance;
- e. conducting training on handling cases of neglect, violence, discrimination, exploitation, child trafficking, abuse, narcotics, psychotropics, and other addictive substances, and/or pornography against children for stakeholders;
- f. assisting in the social rehabilitation and social reintegration process;
- g. providing temporary shelters for children; and
- h. conducting coordination and cooperation with stakeholders related to handling child cases.

## Part Three

Special Protection of Children

#### Article 32

(1) The Regency Government is obligated and responsible to provide special protection to child victims of violence which includes:

- a. children in emergency situations;
- b. children in conflict with the law;
- c. children from minority and isolated groups;
- d. children who are economically and/or sexually exploited;
- e. children who are victims of narcotics, alcohol, psychotropics, and addictive substance abuse;
- f. children who are victims of pornography;
- g. children with HIV/AIDS;
- h. children who are victims of abduction, sale, and/or trafficking;
- i. children who are victims of physical and/or mental violence;
- j. children who are victims of sexual crimes;
- k. children who are victims of terrorism networks;
- l. children with disabilities;
- m. children who are victims of abuse and neglect; n. children with deviant social behavior; and

### Paragraph 2

Children in Conflict with the Law

#### Article 36

(1) Special Protection for Children in Conflict with the Law as referred to in Article 32 paragraph (1) letter b is carried out through:

- a. humane treatment with attention to needs according to their age;
- b. separation from adults;
- c. provision of legal aid and other assistance effectively;
- d. implementation of recreational activities;
- e. freedom from torture, punishment, or other treatment that is cruel, inhumane, and degrading to dignity and honor;
- f. avoidance of imposition of the death penalty and/or life imprisonment;
- g. avoidance of arrest, detention or imprisonment, except as a last resort and for the shortest possible time;
- h. provision of justice before a Children's court that is objective, impartial, and in a hearing closed to the public.
- i. avoidance of publication of their identity;
- j. provision of accompaniment by parents/guardians and persons trusted by the Child;
- k. provision of social advocacy;
- l. provision of private life;
- m. provision of accessibility, especially for Children with Disabilities;
- n. provision of education;
- o. provision of health services; and
- p. provision of other rights in accordance with the provisions of laws and regulations.
  - (2) Special Protection for Children in Conflict with the Law as referred to in paragraph (1) is carried out through efforts of:
- a. prevention;
- b. administrative case settlement;
- c. rehabilitation; and
- d. social reintegration.

#### Article 37

(1) Provision of legal aid and other assistance effectively as referred to in Article 36 paragraph (1) letter c is provided in the form of:

- a. legal consultation, legal assistance, and defense in accordance with the provisions of laws and regulations;
- b. provision of language interpreters for Children in legal processes including sign language interpreters for Children with Disabilities;
- c. provision of information regarding the development of their case in accordance with the provisions of laws and regulations; and
- d. provision of legal aid and other assistance effectively in accordance with the provisions of laws and regulations.
  - (2) Implementation of recreational activities as referred to in Article 36 paragraph (1) letter d consists of daily free physical exercise activities in open spaces, daily entertainment activities, arts, or developing skills.
  - (3) Avoidance of arrest, detention, or imprisonment as referred to in Article 36 paragraph (1) letter g is carried out by taking into account the child's age, type of crime committed, threatened punishment, and criminal liability.
  - (4) Provision of justice before a Children's court that is objective, impartial, and in a hearing closed to the public as referred to in Article 36 paragraph (1) letter h is carried out by seeking a child-friendly court with infrastructure and facilities standards in accordance with the provisions of laws and regulations.
  - (5) Avoidance of publication of their identity as referred to in Article 36 paragraph (1) letter i is carried out by keeping confidential the name of the Child in Conflict with the Law, parents' names, address, face, and other things that can reveal the identity of the Child in Conflict with the Law.
  - (6) Provision of accompaniment by parents/guardians and persons trusted by the Child as referred to in Article 36 paragraph (1) letter j is carried out to help and provide support to the Child so they are ready to follow the process of investigation, prosecution, and examination in court proceedings.
  - (7) Provision of social advocacy as referred to in Article 36 paragraph (1) letter k is intended to protect and defend Children in Conflict with the Law provided in the form of awareness of rights and obligations, defense, and fulfillment of rights.
  - (8) Provision of health services as referred to in Article 36 paragraph (1) letter o is carried out in the form of promotive, preventive, curative, and rehabilitative in accordance with the provisions of laws and regulations in the field of health.

#### Article 38

(1) Preventive efforts so that children do not come into conflict with the law as referred to in Article 36 paragraph (2) letter a are carried out so that:

- a. Children do not become victims of criminal acts b. Children do not come into conflict with the Law; and c. Children do not commit criminal acts again.
  - (2) Prevention so that children do not come into conflict with the Law as referred to in paragraph (1) is carried out through:
- a. print media, electronic media, and online media;
- b. face-to-face in the form of counseling, discussions, lectures, campaigns; and
- c. outdoor media.
  - (3) Preventive efforts so that Children do not come into conflict with the law as referred to in paragraph 1 letter b are carried out through:
- a. dissemination;
- b. providing places or spaces for playing, healthy recreation, and channeling Children's creativity;
- c. providing guidance on religious values and social values;
- d. conducting supervision of the environment that will impact the occurrence of Children in conflict with the Law;
- e. improving family resilience and welfare; f. providing guidance and skills training;
- g. providing places or spaces for playing, healthy recreation, and channeling Children's creativity;
- h. providing religious guidance, social values, and morals;
- i. developing an environment that cares about Children in Conflict with the Law; and/or
- j. involving families in service programs, guidance, and mentoring.

#### Article 39

Special protection for Children in Conflict with the Law in the form of administrative case settlement, rehabilitation, and social reintegration as referred to in Article 36 paragraph (2) letters b through letter d is implemented in accordance with the provisions of laws and regulations.

#### Article 40

(1) Preventive efforts so that children do not come into conflict with the law as referred to in Article 38 paragraph (1) are carried out by the Regency Government.

(2) Special protection for Children in Conflict with the Law in the form of rehabilitation as referred to in Article 36 paragraph (2) letter c is carried out by the Regency Government in accordance with statutory provisions.

- h. avoiding blood transfusions contaminated with HIV and AIDS to Children;

- j. changing Children's behavior to avoid HIV and AIDS;
- k. keeping Children away from tattooing; and

- a. reduce or inhibit the development of HIV and AIDS virus in Children;

- b. teaching character education and good conduct; and

  - (2) Education about ideology for Child Victims of Terrorism Networks as referred to in Article 88 letter a is carried out by providing understanding about:
- a. Pancasila as the state ideology;
- b. the history, meaning, function of Pancasila as the state foundation, philosophy, way of life, and unifier of the nation; and
- c. application or actualization of Pancasila in daily life.
  - (3) Education about nationalist values as referred to in Article 88 letter a is carried out by providing understanding to:
- a. foster love for the nation and homeland;
- b. be willing to sacrifice for the nation and state;
- c. be proud of the Indonesian nation and homeland;
- d. prioritize the interests of the nation and state above other interests;
- e. eliminate extremism; and
- f. create harmonious, harmonious relationships and strengthen brotherhood.

#### Article 90

(1) Counseling about the dangers of terrorism as referred to in Article 89 letter b is carried out in the form of:

- a. religious counseling;
- b. personality counseling;
- c. family counseling; and/or
- d. community life counseling.
  - (2) Religious counseling as referred to in paragraph (1) letter a is carried out to:
- a. increase faith and devotion to God Almighty;
- b. tolerance in religious life; and
- c. reduce extreme understanding by providing deradicalization.
  - (3) Personality counseling as referred to in paragraph (1) letter b is carried out to determine the psychological condition of Child Victims of Terrorism Networks and encourage Children to be able to control themselves and express the interests and talents of Children positively.
  - (4) Family counseling as referred to in paragraph (1) letter c aims to establish good relationships between Child Victims of Terrorism Networks and their families.
  - (5) Community life counseling as referred to in paragraph (1) letter d aims to establish good relationships between Child Victims of Terrorism Networks and the Community, help each other, respect and appreciate, and not violate norms that live in the Community.

#### Article 91

Provisions regarding Social Rehabilitation as referred to in Article 81 apply mutatis mutandis to the form of Social Rehabilitation as referred to in Article 88 letter c.

#### Article 92

(1) Social Assistance for Child Victims of Terrorism Networks as referred to in Article 88 letter d is provided outside and within the child criminal justice procedure.

(2) Social Assistance outside the child criminal justice procedure as referred to in paragraph (1) is carried out by:

- a. home visits;
- b. conducting assessments;
- c. identifying needs;
- d. intervention plans;
- e. implementing interventions;
- f. connecting to institutions that handle Child Victims of Terrorism Networks; and
- g. providing strengthening to Child Victims of Terrorism Networks.

#### Article 93

Special Protection for Child Victims of Terrorism Networks is also provided in the form of:

- a. fulfillment of the rights of Child Victims of Terrorism Networks to care and psychological health recovery;
- b. medical rehabilitation;
- c. reeducation and Social Reintegration; and
- d. safety guarantees for the physical, mental, and social aspects of Child Victims of Terrorism Networks.

#### Article 94

(1) Education about education, ideology, and nationalist values as referred to in Article 89 letter a is implemented in accordance with the provisions of laws and regulations.

(2) Counseling about the dangers of terrorism as referred to in Article 91 letter b is implemented in accordance with the provisions of laws and regulations.

(3) Social Rehabilitation as referred to in Article 91 letter c and Social Assistance as referred to in Article 91 letter d are implemented in accordance with the provisions of laws and regulations.

#### Article 95

(1) Fulfillment of the rights of Child Victims of Terrorism Networks to care and psychological health recovery as referred to in Article 93 letter a is implemented in accordance with the provisions of laws and regulations.

(2) Medical rehabilitation as referred to in Article 93 letter b is implemented in accordance with the provisions of laws and regulations.

(3) Reeducation and Social Reintegration as referred to in Article 93 letter c are implemented in accordance with the provisions of laws and regulations.

(4) Safety guarantees for the physical, mental, and social aspects of Child Victims of Terrorism Networks as referred to in Article 93 letter d are implemented by the District Government in accordance with its authority.

Paragraph 12

Children with Disabilities

#### Article 96

Special Protection for Children with Disabilities as referred to in Article 32 paragraph (1) letter l is carried out through efforts:

- a. treatment in a humane manner in accordance with the dignity and rights of the Child;
- b. fulfillment of special needs;
- c. equal treatment with other Children to achieve full social integration and individual development as much as possible; and
- d. Social Assistance.

#### Article 97

Treatment in a humane manner in accordance with the dignity and rights of the Child as referred to in Article 96 letter a is carried out through:

- a. fulfillment of the rights of Children with Disabilities;
- b. protection from violence;
- c. respect for mental and physical integrity based on equality with others; and
- d. care and upbringing by the family or Substitute Family to grow and develop optimally.

#### Article 98

Fulfillment of special needs as referred to in Article 99 letter b is carried out through:

- a. physical and non-physical accessibility; and
- b. provision of required services including medicines inherent to Children with Disabilities.

#### Article 99

Equal treatment with other Children to achieve full social integration and individual development as much as possible as referred to in Article 96 letter c is carried out through:

- a. non-discriminatory treatment;
- b. involving Children with Disabilities in expressing views according to needs; and
- c. providing access for Children with Disabilities to develop themselves and utilize all abilities according to their talents and interests.

#### Article 100

Special Protection for Children with Disabilities can also be carried out through:

- a. habilitation and rehabilitation; and
- b. provision of reasonable accommodation for Children with Disabilities in accordance with the provisions of laws and regulations.

#### Article 101

The humane treatment of Children in accordance with the dignity and rights of the Child, fulfillment of special needs, equal treatment with other Children to achieve social integration as fully as possible and individual development, and Social Assistance as referred to in Article 96 shall be carried out in accordance with the provisions of laws and regulations.

### Paragraph 13

Children Victims of Mistreatment and Neglect Article 102

Special Protection for Children Victims of Mistreatment and Children Victims of Neglect as referred to in Article 32 paragraph (1) letter m shall be carried out through supervision, prevention, care, Counseling, Social Rehabilitation, and Social Assistance.

#### Article 103

Supervision as referred to in Article 102 shall be carried out by way of:

- a. mapping of Children who are vulnerable to mistreatment and neglect; and
- b. dissemination and advocacy of laws and regulations.

#### Article 104

Prevention as referred to in Article 102 shall be carried out by:

- a. providing guidance to parents about the rights of Children so that they are not mistreated and neglected;
- b. providing basic needs services;
- c. providing access to education; and
- d. providing skills training or self-employment.

#### Article 105

Care as referred to in Article 102 shall be carried out in the form of:

- a. medical rehabilitation; and/or
- b. family care or Substitute Family for optimal growth and development.

#### Article 106

The provisions regarding Social Rehabilitation as referred to in Article 83 shall apply mutatis mutandis to the form of Social Rehabilitation as referred to in Article 102.

#### Article 107

Special Protection for Children Victims of Mistreatment and Neglect as referred to in Article 102 shall be carried out by the Regency Government in accordance with its duties, functions, and authorities.

### Paragraph 14

Children with Deviant Social Behavior

#### Article 108

Special Protection for Children with Deviant Social Behavior as referred to in Article 32 paragraph (1) letter n shall be carried out through guidance on religious and social values, Counseling, Social Rehabilitation, and Social

Assistance.

#### Article 109

Guidance on religious and social values as referred to in Article 108 shall be provided in the form of:

- a. teaching to carry out religious commands in accordance with the beliefs of Children with Deviant Social Behavior;
- b. understanding to behave in accordance with decency and courtesy norms;
- c. understanding not to commit violence and damage; and
- d. increasing awareness of the surrounding environment.

#### Article 110

The provisions regarding Social Rehabilitation as referred to in Article 83 shall apply mutatis mutandis to the form of Social Rehabilitation as referred to in Article 108.

#### Article 111

Special Protection for Children as referred to in Article 108 shall be carried out by involving the role of parents, the Community, Educational Institutions, and Religious Institutions.

#### Article 112

(1) Guidance on religious and social values as referred to in Article 109 shall be carried out in accordance with the provisions of laws and regulations.

(2) Counseling, Social Rehabilitation, and Social Assistance as referred to in Article 108 shall be carried out in accordance with the provisions of laws and regulations.

### Paragraph 15

Children Who Become Victims of Stigmatization from Labeling Related to Their Parents' Condition

#### Article 113

Special Protection for Children Who Become Victims of Stigmatization from Labeling related to Their Parents' Condition as referred to in Article 32 paragraph (1) letter o shall be carried out through Counseling, Social Rehabilitation, and Social Assistance.

#### Article 114

The provisions regarding Social Rehabilitation as referred to in Article 83 shall apply mutatis mutandis to the form of Social Rehabilitation as referred to in Article 113.

#### Article 115

(1) Children who have received Special Protection as referred to in Article 113 shall be returned to their family or Community.

(2) Before being returned to the family or Community as referred to in paragraph (1), the steps that must be taken:

- a. preparing the psychological condition of Children Who Become Victims of Stigmatization from Labeling related to Their Parents' Condition to be willing to be returned to the family or Community;
- b. ensuring that the family, Substitute Family, or Community accepts and no longer labels and discriminates against Children Who Become Victims of Stigmatization from Labeling related to Their Parents' Condition;
- c. tracing other Substitute Families if there is rejection from the family or Community;
- d. ensuring that Children Who Become Victims of Stigmatization from Labeling related to Their Parents' Condition are in a safe, comfortable condition and their needs are met; and
- e. monitoring and evaluating the development of Children Who Become Victims of Stigmatization from Labeling related to Their Parents' Condition who have been returned to the family, Substitute Family, or Community.

#### Article 116

The steps of monitoring and evaluating the development of Children Who Become Victims of Stigmatization from Labeling related to Their Parents' Condition who have been returned to the family, Substitute Family, or Community as referred to in Article 115 paragraph (2) letter e shall be carried out by the Regency Government in accordance with its duties, functions, and authorities.

#### Article 117

Counseling, Social Rehabilitation, and Social Assistance as referred to in Article 113 shall be carried out by the Regency Government in accordance with its duties, functions, and authorities.

#### Article 118

To prevent the occurrence of stigmatization from labeling of Children related to their parents' condition, the Regency Government shall:

- a. provide education to the Community and play an active role in eliminating stigma against Children who are labeled related to their parents' condition; b.provide space for Children who are labeled related to their parents' condition to obtain recreational activities; and
- c. coordinate with service units that handle the protection of Children who are labeled related to their parents' condition in the event of potential violence and discrimination against Children in the Regency.

## Part Four

Service or Advanced Referral Handling for Child Victims of Violence

#### Article 119

In the context of child protection, the Regency Government shall be obligated to:

- a. provide service or advanced referral handling; and
- b. carry out strengthening and development of service provider institutions for the protection of women and children.

#### Article 120

( 1 ) The provision of service or advanced referral handling as referred to in Article 119 letter a shall be intended for child victims of violence who require Regency-level coordination.

( 2 ) The provision of service or referral handling as referred to in paragraph (1) shall be implemented through the Regency-level PPA unit.

( 3 ) Further provisions regarding the duties, membership, and formation of the PPA unit as referred to in paragraph (2) shall be regulated by Regent Regulation.

# CHAPTER IV

IMPROVEMENT OF FAMILY QUALITY

#### Article 121

Improvement of family quality shall be implemented in order to realize gender equality and the rights of the child.

#### Article 122

Improvement of family quality shall be carried out through: a. prevention of child marriage;

b. fulfillment of children's rights to exclusive breastfeeding;

c. bringing mothers closer to health services;

d. increasing family resilience.

#### Article 123

Prevention of child marriage as referred to in Article 122 letter a aims to:

- a. increase the productivity of girls; b. develop the self-potential of girls; c. prevent pregnancy at too young an age;
- d. reduce maternal and infant mortality rates;
- e. reduce poverty rates; and
- f. prevent domestic violence.

#### Article 124

The fulfillment of children's rights to exclusive breastfeeding as referred to in Article 123 letter b aims to:

- a. guarantee the right of every child to obtain exclusive breastfeeding;
- b. guarantee the health of children and optimal growth and development, especially during the golden age; and
- c. prevent malnutrition.

#### Article 125

Bringing mothers closer to health services as referred to in Article 122 letter c aims to:

- a. guarantee the health of mothers and children before, during, and after childbirth; and
- b. increase mothers' insight and knowledge about their own and their families' health.

#### Article 126

Family resilience as referred to in Article 122 letter d aims to:

- a. fulfill the rights of family members;
- b. improve the quality of life of family members; and c. improve family welfare.

#### Article 127

Further provisions regarding the form of activities and procedures for implementing family quality improvement as referred to in Article 122 shall be regulated by Regent Regulation.

# CHAPTER V

GENDER AND CHILD DATA SYSTEM

#### Article 128

(1) The Regency Government shall organize a gender and child data information system.

(2) The Regency Government shall establish a forum/working group for the gender and child data system as regulated in the Regent Regulation.

(3) In the context of forming a team, compiling collection, processing, analysis and presentation of gender and child data, the Regency Government shall coordinate with related Regional Apparatus at the regency level.

(4) The presentation of gender and child data shall be developed in the family information system and e-violence applications.

(5) The presentation and utilization of gender and child data shall be used to accelerate the implementation of Gender Mainstreaming in regional development.

(6) Further provisions regarding the implementation of the gender and child data information system as referred to in paragraph (1) shall be regulated by Regent Regulation.

# CHAPTER VI

COORDINATION AND COOPERATION

#### Article 129

(1) The Regency Government shall establish a coordination and cooperation forum in organizing the protection of women and children with:

- a. other Regency Governments; and/or
- b. other institutions/agencies.
  - (2) The coordinator for the implementation of coordination and cooperation in organizing the protection of women and children shall be coordinated by the Regional Apparatus that administers governmental affairs in the field of Child Protection.
  - (3) Further provisions regarding the establishment of forum procedures as referred to in paragraph (1) shall be regulated by Regent Regulation.

#### Article 130

In the context of coordinating the implementation of protection for women and children, the Regency Government may establish networks and cooperation with law enforcement apparatus, government apparatus, universities and various non-governmental organizations that work and/or care for women.

# CHAPTER VII

COMMUNITY PARTICIPATION

#### Article 131

(1) The community may participate in organizing the protection of women and the protection of children.

(2) Participation as referred to in paragraph (1) may be implemented in the context of prevention, handling and empowerment, and fulfillment of children's rights.

(3) The role of the community in organizing child protection as referred to in paragraph (1) shall be carried out by:

- a. providing information through socialization and education regarding children's rights and laws and regulations concerning children;
- b. providing input in the formulation of policies related to child protection;
- c. reporting to the competent authorities if violations of children's rights occur;
- d. playing an active role in the rehabilitation and social reintegration process for children;
- e. conducting monitoring, supervision and being responsible for the organization of child protection;
- f. providing facilities and infrastructure and creating a conducive atmosphere for children's growth and development;
- g. establishing a Regency regional coordination forum;
- h. playing an active role by eliminating negative labeling of Children; and
- i. providing space for children to participate and express their opinions.
  - (4) The role of community organizations and educational institutions as referred to in paragraph (2) shall be carried out by taking necessary steps in accordance with their respective duties, functions, and authorities to assist in organizing child protection and providing information.
  - (5) The role of mass media as referred to in paragraph (2) shall be carried out through the dissemination of information and educational materials that are beneficial from the social, cultural, educational, religious and child health aspects by taking into account the best interests of Children.
  - (6) The role of the business sector as referred to in paragraph (2) shall be carried out through:
- a. corporate policies with a child perspective;
- b. products intended for children must be safe for children; and
- c. contributing to the fulfillment of children's rights through corporate social responsibility.

#### Article 132

In the context of strengthening and developing services for child victims of violence, the community or social service institutions may establish special facilities.

# CHAPTER VIII

GUIDANCE AND SUPERVISION

#### Article 133

(1) The Regent shall be obliged to provide guidance on the organization of women's empowerment and child protection.

(2) The implementation of guidance as referred to in paragraph (1) shall be carried out by the Regional Apparatus that administers women's empowerment and child protection affairs.

(3) Guidance as referred to in paragraph (1) shall include:

- a. guidelines and fulfillment standards;
- b. technical guidance and training;
- c. provision of facilities;
- d. monitoring; and
- e. evaluation.
  - (4) Supervision shall be implemented in the form of:
- a. monitoring; and
- b. evaluation.
  - (5) In implementing guidance as referred to in paragraph (1), the Regional Apparatus that administers women's empowerment and child protection affairs may cooperate and coordinate with other Regional Apparatus within the Regency Government or other agencies/institutions related to the Regency.

#### Article 134

(1) The Regent shall be obliged to supervise the organization of women's empowerment and child protection.

(2) The implementation of supervision as referred to in paragraph (1) shall be carried out by the Regional Apparatus that performs supervisory functions.

#### Article 135

(1) In addition to the Regional Apparatus that administers women's empowerment and child protection affairs, to ensure the effectiveness of implementing women's empowerment and child protection in the Regency, every Regional Apparatus shall be obliged to conduct supervision in the form of monitoring and evaluation.

(2) Supervision as referred to in paragraph (1) shall be implemented in tiers in accordance with their respective authorities.

(3) The results of monitoring and evaluation as referred to in paragraph (1) shall be announced to the public.

#### Article 136

(1) Reports on the results of guidance and supervision shall be submitted to the regent.

(2) Reporting as referred to in paragraph (1) shall be implemented at least once every 6 (six) months.

(3) Reporting as referred to in paragraph (1) shall be carried out in a networked manner or between Regional Apparatus, and coordinated by the Regional Apparatus that administers women's empowerment and protection of women and children affairs.

# CHAPTER IX

FUNDING

#### Article 137

Funding for the implementation of women's empowerment and child protection shall be sourced from:

- a. regional revenue and expenditure budget; and
- b. other legitimate sources, in accordance with the provisions of laws and regulations.

# CHAPTER X

PROHIBITIONS

#### Article 138

Every education provider at the regency level is prohibited from:

- a. creating school rules that potentially violate children's rights; and/or
- b. expelling Children from educational institutions without guarantees for the continuity of Children's education.

# CHAPTER XI

ADMINISTRATIVE SANCTIONS

#### Article 139

(1) The head of an educational unit who violates the provisions as referred to in Article 138 may be subject to administrative sanctions.

(2) Sanctions as referred to in paragraph (1) may be in the form of:

- a. written warning;
- b. transfer;
- c. demotion;
- d. dismissal from position; or
- e. revocation of license.
  - (3) The imposition of sanctions as referred to in paragraph (1) shall be implemented in accordance with the provisions of laws and regulations.

# CHAPTER XII

CLOSING PROVISIONS

#### Article 140

At the time this Regional Regulation comes into force, all implementing regulations related to Women's Empowerment and Child Protection shall remain in force insofar as they are not contrary to the provisions of this Regional Regulation.

#### Article 141

At the time this Regional Regulation comes into force, Regional Regulation Number 6 of 2012 concerning the Implementation of Protection for Victims of Gender-Based Violence and Children (Regional Gazette of Ogan Komering Ulu Regency Year 2012 Number 6), is revoked and declared invalid.

#### Article 142

This Regional Regulation shall come into force on the date of promulgation.
