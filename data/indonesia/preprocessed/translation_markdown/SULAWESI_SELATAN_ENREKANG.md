REGENT OF ENREKANG.

Considering : a. that in order to guarantee and protect the rights of women and children so that they can participate optimally in accordance with human dignity and worth, and receive protection from violence, it is necessary to carry out efforts to protect women and children;

b. that violence against women and children in Enrekang Regency continues to increase and expand, causing citizens to feel unsafe in carrying out their lives, so that integrated protection efforts are required;

c. that based on the provisions in Article 12 of Law Number 23 of 2014 concerning Regional Government, women's empowerment and child protection constitute mandatory governmental affairs for regional governments;

d. that based on the considerations referred to in point a, point b, and point c, it is necessary to establish a Regional Regulation concerning Protection of Women and Children;

# CHAPTER I

GENERAL PROVISIONS

#### Article 1

In this Regional Regulation, what is meant by:

- 1. Region is Enrekang Regency.
- 2. Regional Government is the Government of Enrekang Regency.
- 3. Regent is the Regent of Enrekang.
- 4. Regional Apparatus, hereinafter abbreviated as PD, is the Regional Apparatus Organization within the Regional Government.
- 5. Woman is a person of female sex.
- 6. Child is a person who has not reached the age of 18 (eighteen) years, including a child still in the womb.
- 7. Protection of Women is all efforts made by families, regional government and others directed at women to ensure the fulfillment of women's rights as a form of respect for human rights.
- 8. Protection of Children is all activities to guarantee and protect children and their rights so that they can live, grow, develop and participate optimally in accordance with human dignity and receive protection from violence and discrimination.
- 9. Special Protection is protection given to Women and Children in conflict with the law, economic and sexual exploitation, being trafficked, becoming victims of narcotics, alcohol, psychotropic and other addictive substance abuse (drugs), victims of kidnapping, sale, victims of violence, disabilities, victims of maltreatment and/or neglect.
- 10. Prevention is direct efforts made by the Regional Government together with the community and Parents and/or families as well as private parties to prevent violations of the rights of women and children.
- 11. Women's Empowerment is every effort to improve physical, mental, spiritual, social abilities, knowledge, and skills so that women are ready to be utilized according to their respective abilities.
- 12. Discrimination is any distinction, harassment or exclusion, direct or indirect, based on human differences on the basis of religion, ethnicity, race, ethnic group, class, social status, socio-economic status, gender, language, political beliefs that result in reduction, deviation or elimination of recognition, implementation or use of human rights and basic freedoms in life, both individual and collective, in the political, economic, legal, social, cultural and other aspects of life.
- 13. Violence is any act that results in or may result in suffering or harm, whether physical, sexual, economic, social, or psychological to the victim.
- 14. Violence against women is any act based on gender differences that results in or may result in suffering or harm to women physically, sexually, economically, socially, psychologically, including threats of certain acts, coercion or arbitrary deprivation of liberty, whether occurring in public or in private life.
- 15. Violence against children is any act that results in suffering or harm to children physically, sexually, economically, socially, psychologically, including threats of certain acts, coercion or deprivation of liberty.
- 16. Domestic Violence is any act against a person, especially women, that results in suffering or harm physically, sexually, psychologically, and/or domestic neglect, including threats to commit acts, coercion, or unlawful deprivation of liberty within the scope of the household.
- 17. Trafficking of women and children, hereinafter referred to as trafficking, is the act of recruitment, transportation, harboring, dispatch, transfer, or receipt of women and/or children by threat of violence, use of violence, kidnapping, confinement, forgery, fraud, abuse of power or position of vulnerability, debt bondage or giving payment or benefits, so as to obtain consent from the person holding control over the other person, whether conducted within the country or between countries, for the purpose of exploitation or resulting in the person being exploited.
- 18. Exploitation is an act with or without the consent of the victim that includes but is not limited to prostitution, forced labor or service, slavery or practices similar to slavery, oppression, extortion, utilization of physical, sexual, reproductive organs and/or body tissue or exploiting the labor or ability of a person by another party to obtain benefits, both material and immaterial.
- 19. Person with Disability is any person who experiences physical, intellectual, mental and/or sensory limitations in the long term who, in interacting with the environment, may experience obstacles and difficulties to participate fully and effectively with other citizens based on equal rights.
- 20. Service is an action taken as soon as possible to the victim when seeing, hearing and/or knowing that violence against the victim will, is, or has occurred.
- 21. Victim is a woman and child who experiences and/or suffers directly or indirectly as a result of violence.
- 22. Children's Forum is a Children's Participation Forum established with the aim of facilitating children's involvement in the decision-making process and enjoying changes relating to their lives, either directly or indirectly, which is carried out with the consent and willingness of all children based on awareness and understanding, in accordance with age and level of thinking maturity.
- 23. Child-Friendly Regency, Sub-district, and Village is a Regency, Sub-district, and Village that has a child rights-based development system through the integration of commitments and resources of government, community and business world that are planned comprehensively and sustainably in policies, programs and activities to ensure the fulfillment of children's rights.
- 24. Safe house is a temporary shelter that aims to provide protection and a sense of security to victims of violence.
- 25. Shelter home is a facility for guidance or welfare services for street children that carries out social assistance/guidance activities, maintenance, care, education and health, skills guidance in order to ensure that children do not engage in activities on the streets so that they can grow and develop normally.
- 26. Parents are biological father and/or mother, or adoptive father and/or mother.

# CHAPTER II PRINCIPLES, OBJECTIVES AND SCOPE

#### Article 2

The implementation of the Protection of Women and Children is based on the principles of:

- a. non-discrimination;
- b. the best interests of women and children;
- c. the right to life, survival, and development;
- d. respect for the opinion of the child;
- e. legal certainty;
- f. local wisdom;
- g. justice and gender equality; and
- h. sustainability.

#### Article 3

The implementation of the Protection of Women and Children aims to:

- a. provide guidance to the regional government in planning, policies and strategies for the protection of women and children;
- b. strengthen the protective environment for women and children from all forms of violence, exploitation, maltreatment, and neglect;
- c. prevent violations of the rights of women and children;
- d. increase empowerment of women and children; and
- e. increase the role of government institutions, business entities, media and/or Non-Governmental Organizations in the region.

#### Article 4

The scope of regulation on the Protection of Women and Children includes:

- a. prevention;
- b. implementation of the protection of women and children;
- c. services for women and children victims of violence;
- d. institutions;
- e. cooperation and partnership; and
- e. guidance and supervision.

# CHAPTER III RIGHTS OF WOMEN AND CHILDREN

## Part One Rights of women

#### Article 5

Every woman has the right to:

- a. obtain protection from discriminatory acts;
- b. obtain protection from economic and sexual exploitation;
- c. obtain protection from physical, psychological, sexual violence, neglect and Trafficking;
- d. obtain decent work in accordance with her capabilities;
- e. obtain work leave while continuing to receive wages/salary in accordance with statutory regulations;
- f. obtain education and instruction in all types, levels and pathways of education; and
- g. receive special protection in the performance of work or profession against matters that may threaten her safety and/or health in connection with women's reproductive function.

## Part Two Rights of children

#### Article 6

Every child has the right to:

- a. live, grow, develop and participate reasonably in accordance with human dignity;
- b. obtain protection from discriminatory acts;
- c. obtain protection from economic and sexual exploitation;
- d. obtain protection from physical, psychological, sexual violence, neglect and Trafficking;
- e. a name as personal identity and citizenship status;
- f. obtain a birth certificate free of charge;
- g. embrace a religion and worship according to the teachings of his/her religion with the guidance of parents/guardians;
- h. not be forced to change religion by foster parents, adoptive parents and by anyone;
- i. be acknowledged and know his/her parents, be raised and cared for by his/her own parents;
- j. obtain health services and social security in accordance with physical, mental, spiritual and social needs;
- k. obtain education and instruction in accordance with his/her interests and talents;
  - 1. obtain care, education and training, self-improvement and the ability to participate in community and state life for children with special needs;
- m. express and have his/her opinion heard; and
- n. rest and utilize leisure time for self-development.

# CHAPTER IV

OBLIGATIONS AND RESPONSIBILITIES OF REGIONAL GOVERNMENT, SOCIETY, PARENTS/GUARDIANS AND/OR FAMILY

## Part One

Regional Government

#### Article 7

(1) The Regional Government in efforts to protect women and children is obliged and responsible to:

- a. guarantee the rights of women and children without discrimination in accordance with statutory regulations;
- b. establish and implement policies, programs and activities for the protection of women's rights and children's rights;
- c. provide support for facilities and infrastructure in the implementation of protection of women and children;
- d. cooperate with society and/or social organizations in the implementation of protection of women and children in accordance with statutory regulations;
- e. conduct rehabilitation for women and children who are victims of acts of violence;
- f. provide special protection to women victims of acts of violence and trafficking in women and children;
- g. provide special protection to children in emergency situations, children in conflict with the law, child victims of acts of violence and trafficking in women and children, children from minority and isolated groups, children economically and/or sexually exploited, children who are victims of narcotics, alcohol, psychotropic abuse and children with HIV/AIDS;
- h. organize inclusive education for children with Disabilities; and
- i. take preventive measures in order to prevent violations of the rights of women and children.
  - (2) The obligations and responsibilities of the Regional Government in efforts to protect women and children as referred to in paragraph (1) are carried out by PD in accordance with its duties and functions.

#### Article 8

(1) In carrying out the obligations and responsibilities as referred to in Article 7 paragraph (1), the Regional Government establishes programs and activities for the protection of women and children with a Regional Action Plan as the basis for PD in implementing the protection of women and children in accordance with its duties and functions.

(2) The Regional Action Plan as referred to in paragraph (1) is regulated by Regent Regulation.

## Part Two

Society

#### Article 9

The obligations and responsibilities of society in efforts to protect women and children:

- a. participate in prevention and provide support for the development of programs to prevent violations of the rights of women and children;
- b. provide information and/or report the occurrence of violations of the rights of women and children to the competent authorities; and
- c. supervise the law enforcement process for cases of violations of the rights of women and children.

#### Article 10

(1) Parents/guardians in efforts to protect the rights of children are obliged and responsible to:

- a. care for, maintain, educate and protect children;
- b. foster the development of children in accordance with their abilities, talents and interests;
- c. provide formal and non-formal education;
- d. guide children to behave in accordance with religion, ethics and morals as well as customs; and
- e. prevent marriage at child age.
  - (2) In the event that parents/guardians do not exist, or their whereabouts are unknown, or due to a certain reason, cannot carry out their obligations and responsibilities, then the obligations and responsibilities as referred to in paragraph (1) may be transferred to the family.

## Part Three

Parents/Guardians and/or Family

#### Article 11

The family in efforts to protect the rights of women is obliged and responsible to:

- a. protect women from conflict and domestic violence that degrades the dignity of women;
- b. provide equal opportunities for women to develop themselves in the fields of education, social, economic, political and cultural; and
- c. prevent violations of women's rights in the household.

# CHAPTER V PREVENTION

## Part One Regional Government

#### Article 12

The Regional Government conducts prevention of violations of the rights of women and children by:

- a. organizing socialization regarding the prevention of violence against women and children;
- b. providing guidelines on handling acts of violence against women and children;
- c. developing child-friendly schools;
- d. developing child-friendly health centers;
- e. encouraging increased child participation through the establishment of children's forums;
- f. developing an integrated database and information system on acts of violence against women and children;
- g. developing family empowerment programs;
- h. realizing the development of Child-Friendly Regencies, Districts, and Villages; and
- i. creating Child-Friendly Open Public Spaces (RPTRA).

## Part two

Society

#### Article 13

Society conducts prevention of violations of the rights of women and children by:

- a. monitoring various conditions occurring in their environment that may cause violations of the rights of women and children;
- b. strengthening the role of social, religious organizations and the family environment;
- c. preventing unregistered marriage and unofficial marriage;
- d. preventing marriage at Child age; and
- e. strengthening the participation of society in recovery and social reintegration.

## Part three Parents/guardians and/or Family Article 14

Parents/guardians, and/or family conduct prevention of violations of the rights of women and children by:

- a. protecting women and children from various acts of violation of the rights of women and children in the household.
- b. supervising women and children in their activities inside or outside the home; and
- c. providing opportunities for women and children to engage in dialogue and express their opinions democratically.

# CHAPTER VI IMPLEMENTATION OF PROTECTION OF WOMEN AND CHILDREN

## Part One Protection of Women

### Paragraph 1

Health

#### Article 15

(1) The Regional Government provides facilities and organizes comprehensive health efforts for women to obtain optimal health status.

(2) The provision of facilities and organization of comprehensive health efforts as referred to in paragraph (1) is supported by community participation.

### Paragraph 2

Education

#### Article 16

(1) The Regional Government guarantees the implementation of informal education for women who drop out of school.

(2) The implementation of informal education as referred to in paragraph (1) is supported by community participation and the business sector.

### Paragraph 3 Empowerment

(1) The Regional Government guarantees the implementation of empowerment for women.

(2) The implementation of women's empowerment as referred to in paragraph (1) is supported by community participation and the business sector.

### Paragraph 4

Employment

#### Article 18

(1) Protection of women workers includes:

- a. obtaining space for breastfeeding; and
- b. obtaining facilities for child care.
  - (2) Early childhood care facilities as referred to in paragraph (1) letter b managed by the community must obtain a recommendation from the Regional Government.

### Paragraph 5

Micro Economy

#### Article 19

Protection of Women's Micro Economy includes:

- a. obtaining opportunities to conduct business activities in the household environment; and
- b. obtaining economic empowerment facilities especially for women heads of households.

### Paragraph 6

Marriage

#### Article 20

(1) A woman during marriage has equal rights and responsibilities with her husband in all matters relating to her marital life, relations with her children and ownership rights and management of joint property.

(2) After a court decision regarding divorce, a woman has equal rights and responsibilities with her former husband in all matters relating to her children, taking into account the best interests of the child.

(3) After a court decision regarding divorce, a woman has equal rights with her former husband in all matters relating to joint property without reducing the rights of the child, in accordance with the provisions of laws and regulations.

## Part Two Protections of Children

### Paragraph 1

Health

#### Article 21

(1) The Regional Government provides facilities and organizes comprehensive health efforts for children to obtain optimal health status starting from the womb.

(2) The provision of facilities and organization of comprehensive health efforts as referred to in paragraph (1) is supported by community participation.

(3) Comprehensive health efforts as referred to in paragraph (1) include promotive, preventive, curative, and rehabilitative efforts for both basic health and referral.

(4) The Regional Government facilitates and provides nutritional assistance or supplementary food for improving the health of children and poor families.

(5) Comprehensive health efforts as referred to in paragraph (1) are carried out gradually in accordance with regional financial capacity.

#### Article 22

Families and parents are responsible for maintaining and caring for children from the womb.

#### Article 23

The Regional Government, business sector, community, families and parents endeavor to ensure that children born are free from disease.

### Paragraph 2

Education

#### Article 24

(1) The Regional Government guarantees the implementation of the 9 (nine) year compulsory education program for all children.

(2) The implementation of the compulsory education program as referred to in paragraph (1) is supported by community participation and the business sector.

(3) Parents and families provide the widest opportunity to children according to the child's ability to obtain education.

(4) Every organizer of an educational unit is prohibited from expelling children from educational institutions without clear reasons and through procedures in accordance with laws and regulations.

#### Article 25

Children in conflict with the law, children who experience pregnancy outside of marriage and children victims of HIV/AIDS transmission are protected in their rights to obtain education.

#### Article 26

For children aged 7 (seven) to less than 15 (fifteen) years who have not completed their formal education may pursue education through informal education units:

- a. learning group package A equivalent to Elementary School and Madrasah Ibtidaiyah; or
- b. learning group package B equivalent to Junior High School and Madrasah Tsanawiyah;

#### Article 27

(1) The Regional Government may provide scholarships and/or educational assistance for children from poor families.

(2) The implementation of scholarship programs and/or educational assistance as referred to in paragraph (1) is supported by community participation and social organizations.

#### Article 28

The Government is obliged to protect children in the school environment from acts of violence committed by teachers, school administrators or schoolmates.

#### Article 29

(1) The regional government is obliged to develop a child-friendly school model.

(2) Child-friendly schools as referred to in paragraph (1) constitute a joint effort of the Regional Government with school administrators to create a safe and comfortable environment for children at school.

(3) Children from poor families and/or persons with disabilities have the right to equal and fair treatment to obtain services and guidance at school.

#### Article 30

(1) Education organizers equip the schools they lead with gender-responsive facilities.

(2) Gender-responsive facilities as referred to in paragraph (1) include:

- a. male and female toilets/WCs are separated as well as between teachers and students;
- b. teacher desks and student desks are equipped with covers.
  - (3) Implementation of the provisions as referred to in paragraph (1) and paragraph (2) is carried out in accordance with regional financial capacity.

### Paragraph 3

Employment

#### Article 31

(1) Every person is prohibited from employing and involving children in the worst forms of work.

(2) The worst forms of work as referred to in paragraph (1) include:

- a. work in the form of slavery or the like;
- b. work that exploits, provides or offers children for prostitution, pornography production, pornographic performances or gambling.
- c. work that exploits, provides, or involves children in the production and trade of alcoholic beverages, narcotics, psychotropics, and other addictive substances; and/or
- d. work that endangers the health, safety and morals of children.

### Paragraph 4

Social

#### Article 32

(1) The Regional Government organizes care for abandoned children.

(2) Care as referred to in paragraph (1) may be carried out by Social Organizations in the social field.

#### Article 33

(1) The Regional Government organizes skills training for street children and abandoned children.

(2) Skills training as referred to in paragraph (1) is organized by Regional Apparatus in accordance with their respective duties and functions.

#### Article 34

(1) The Regional Government may provide shelters to guide street children and social homes for abandoned children.

(2) Shelters as referred to in paragraph (1) may be organized in cooperation with social organizations.

#### Article 35

The implementation of protection for women and children is carried out by cross-sectoral Regional Apparatus whose duties and functions are related to the protection of women and children.

# CHAPTER VII

SERVICES FOR WOMEN AND CHILDREN VICTIMS OF VIOLENCE

## Part One

General

#### Article 36

Forms of services provided to women and children victims of violence are as follows:

- a. complaint services;
- b. health services;
- c. social rehabilitation;
- d. legal aid, and
- e. repatriation and social reintegration.

## Part Two Complaint Services

#### Article 37

(1) The implementation of complaint services as referred to in Article 36 letter a is carried out through integrated services.

(2) Integrated services for women and children victims of violence use the principles of:

- a. fast, responsive, safe, empathetic;
- b. gender responsive;
- c. non-discrimination;
- d. easily accessible;
- e. guarantee of confidentiality, and
- f. without charging fees from victims.

#### Article 38

(1) Complaints of acts of violence against women and children may be made by reporters consisting of victims, families, communities and/or community organizations in the field of protection of women and children.

(2) Reporters as referred to in paragraph (1) may make complaints;

a. directly;

b. through telephone/short message service; or

c. through letter/electronic mail.

#### Article 39

Complaint handling services for women and children victims of violence are carried out by:

- a. Hospitals;
- b. Community Health Centers;

P2TP2A,

d. Police;

e. Office of Religious Affairs;

f. Legal Aid Institutions;

g. Child Protection Institutions;

h. Family Welfare Consultation Institutions;

i. PPA Task Force

#### Article 40

Provisions regarding the procedures for complaints as referred to in Article 38 shall be regulated by Regent Regulation

## Part three

Health Services

#### Article 41

(1) Health services as referred to in Article 36 letter b include promotive, preventive, curative and rehabilitative aspects.

(2) Health services for women and children victims of violence are carried out comprehensively, accessible 24 (twenty-four) hours, quality and carried out in accordance with health service standards.

(3) Special health services for child victims of violence must prioritize the best interests of the child.

(4) Women and children victims of violence have the right to health services, including:

- a. medical services;
- b. medicolegal services; and
- c. psychosocial counseling.
  - (5) Comprehensive health service efforts as referred to in paragraph (4) intended for victims of violence, abduction, neglect, economically exploited, sexually and trafficking of women and children are guaranteed by the Regional Government.
  - (6) Comprehensive health service efforts as referred to in paragraph (1) are carried out in stages adjusted to the regional financial capacity.

## Part four

Social Rehabilitation

#### Article 42

(1) Social Rehabilitation Services for Women and Children Victims of Violence are aimed at restoring and developing the abilities of victims who experience social dysfunction to be able to carry out their social functions in society normally.

(2) Social Rehabilitation Services as referred to in paragraph (1) include counseling services and spiritual guidance.

(3) The provision of services as referred to in paragraph (2) is organized by:

- a. Regional Apparatus having duties and functions in the social field; and
- b. service handling institutions.

#### Article 43

(1) Counseling services as referred to in Article 42 paragraph (2) aim to:

- a. assist victims regarding problems and find effective ways to overcome them themselves;
- b. empower victims to determine their future;
- c. provide moral support for victims in facing the process they undergo; and
- d. make victims feel accepted in the community environment.
  - (2) Counseling services are carried out by gender-responsive social rehabilitation officers, consisting of:
- a. Counselors;
- b. Psychologists; and
- c. Psychiatrists.
  - (3) Counseling services are carried out in places that guarantee a sense of security, comfort and confidentiality of information from victims.
  - (4) Stages of counseling services include:
- a. initial counseling; and
- b. follow-up counseling.

#### Article 44

(1) The Regional Government is responsible for facilitating the availability of safe houses for women and children victims of violence.

(2) Safe houses aim to protect women and children victims of violence from intimidation by perpetrators and other parties.

(3) The location of safe houses as referred to in paragraph (1) must be kept confidential.

(4) Criteria for women and children victims of violence who require safe houses include:

- a. their life is threatened;
- b. receiving rejection from family or community;
- c. requiring intensive services but their residence is relatively far; and/or
- d. abandoned if not placed in a safe house.

#### Article 45

(1) Women and children victims of violence are provided with spiritual guidance aimed at psychological recovery.

(2) Spiritual guidance as referred to in paragraph (1) is carried out based on the principles of:

- a. not imposing any religion and belief; and
- b. leaving the final decision in the hands of the victim/self determination
  - (3) Spiritual guidance is carried out by spiritual guidance officers
  - (4) Spiritual guidance officers as referred to in paragraph (3) consist of Consultants of the Marriage Counseling, Guidance and Preservation Agency (BP4), Officers of the Office of Religious Affairs (KUA), Islamic Teachers/Scholars, Priests, Pastors and other gender-responsive religious leaders.

## Part Five

Legal Aid

#### Article 46

(1) Legal aid as referred to in Article 36 letter d is aimed at providing assistance and protection to women and children victims of violence and witnesses with a gender-responsive approach so that victims obtain justice and certainty of their legal rights.

(2) Types of legal aid services include:

- a. legal aid in the reporting process and preparation of Official Investigation Reports;
- b. legal aid in the prosecution process at the Prosecutor's Office;
- c. legal aid in the examination process at the Court;
- d. legal aid in the court hearing decision process until there is a decision with permanent legal force;
- e. legal aid in the mediation process;
- f. legal aid in the Diversion process.
  - (3) The Regional Government provides legal aid to children and women victims of violence.
  - (4) In providing legal aid as referred to in paragraph (3) the Regional Government may cooperate with Legal Aid Institutions, Advocates and/or other gender-responsive legal assistants.

## Part six

Repatriation and Social Reintegration Services

#### Article 47

(1) Repatriation as referred to in Article 36 letter e is aimed at returning women and children victims of violence to their area of origin.

(2) Repatriation of women and children victims of violence is accompanied by social reintegration efforts.

(3) The Regional Government carries out repatriation and social reintegration of women and children victims of violence in accordance with laws and regulations.

INSTITUTIONS

(2) The implementation of Protection of Women and Children by the Regional Government as referred to in paragraph (1) is carried out by:

- a. P2TP2A, UPT
- b. LK3; and
- c. PPA Task Force.

P2TP2A UPT PPA

## Part Two

#### Article 50

(1) LK3 services as referred to in Article 48 paragraph (2) letter b, constitute services to families experiencing psychosocial problems.

(2) Types of LK3 services as referred to in paragraph (1), consist of:

- a. consultation;
- b. counseling;
- c. information;
- d. advocacy;
- e. referral; and
- f. outreach.
  - (3) The membership composition of LK3 is determined by Regent Decree.

#### Article 51

(4) The Regional Government facilitates the formation of Children's Forums at the regency, sub-district, and village/urban village levels.

(5) The Children's Forum as referred to in paragraph (1) constitutes a representation of children in the Region, both representation of children's geographical domicile, components of children's socio-cultural groups, and children's educational background.

(6) In every formulation of policies related to children, the Regional Government must consider and accommodate the opinions of children conveyed through the Children's Forum.

(7) The establishment of the Children's Forum as referred to in paragraph (1) at the regency level is stipulated by Regent's Decree.

(8) The establishment of the Children's Forum as referred to in paragraph (1) at the sub-district level is stipulated by the Sub-district Head.

(9) The establishment of the Children's Forum as referred to in paragraph (1) at the Village level is stipulated by Village Head's Decree and at the Urban Village level is stipulated by the Urban Village Head.

## Part Three

PPA Task Force

#### Article 52

(1) In order to ensure the effectiveness of implementing the Protection of Women and Children, the Regional Government establishes a Task Force.

(3) The Task Force as referred to in paragraph (1) has the duty to assist the Regional Apparatus that handles women's empowerment and child protection in addressing women's and children's issues.

(4) In carrying out the duties as referred to in paragraph (1), the task force has the following functions:

- a. conducting outreach to women and children experiencing problems;
- b. identifying the conditions and services needed by women and children experiencing problems;
- c. protecting women and children at the incident location from things that may endanger them;
- d. placing and evacuating women and children experiencing problems;
- e. making recommendations to regional women's and children's service organizations to obtain further services.
  - (5) The task force as referred to in paragraph (1) is under the coordination of the Regional Apparatus that administers affairs in the field of women's empowerment and child protection.
  - (6) The establishment of the Task Force is stipulated by Regent's Decree.
  - (7) Further provisions regarding organizational structure, procedures for establishment and work mechanisms are regulated by Regent's Regulation.

# CHAPTER IX COOPERATION AND PARTNERSHIP

## Part One

Cooperation

#### Article 53

(1) In order to achieve the objectives of the Protection of Women and Children, cooperation may be conducted with:

- a. the central government;
- b. provincial governments;
- c. other regency/city governments; and
- d. non-governmental institutions.
  - (2) Cooperation as referred to in paragraph (1) includes:
- a. delivery of data and information;
- b. rehabilitation of victims of violent acts;
- c. repatriation and social reintegration; and
- d. provision of evidence and witnesses.

## Part Two

Partnership

#### Article 54

(1) The Regional Government may establish partnerships with the business sector in protecting women and children from violent acts.

(2) Partnership as referred to in paragraph (1) is conducted through:

- a. employment opportunity information system for women victims of violent acts;
- b. education and training for women victims of violent acts;
- c. educational assistance for women and child victims of violent acts who are withdrawn from their education; and
- d. fostering and improving the economic independence of women victims of violent acts;
  - (3) Partnership as referred to in paragraph (1) and paragraph (2) is set forth in the form of a Cooperation Agreement.

# CHAPTER X GUIDANCE AND SUPERVISION Article 55

(1) The Regional Government conducts guidance and supervision of the implementation of the Protection of Women and Children carried out by the community and community institutions.

(2) Guidance as referred to in paragraph (1) includes:

- a. guidelines and fulfillment standards;
- b. technical guidance and training;
- c. provision of facilities;
- d. monitoring;
- e. evaluation; and
- f. reporting
  - (3) Supervision as referred to in paragraph (1) is conducted with the principles of:
- a. professionalism;
- b. transparency; and
- c. accountability.

# CHAPTER XI

COORDINATION AND EVALUATION

## Part One

Coordination

#### Article 56

(1) Regional Apparatus coordination includes:

- a. health service referrals to victims;
- b. legal assistance;
- c. repatriation of victims of trafficking in women and children;
- d. social reintegration; and
- e. development of forms of Protection of Women and Children.
  - (2) Development of forms of protection as referred to in paragraph (1) letter e includes:
- a. mediation process for Domestic Violence cases;
- b. assistance for people with HIV/AIDS (ODHA) and people living with HIV/AIDS (OHIDHA);
- c. assistance for victims of human trafficking;
- d. child victims and sexual exploitation or other exploitation; and
- e. other issues directly related to efforts to protect women and children.

#### Article 57

The Regional Apparatus in charge of planning affairs coordinates programs for the protection of women and children from violence, exploitation and discrimination with relevant Regional Apparatus.

## Part Two

Evaluation

#### Article 58

(1) Evaluation of the implementation of policies, programs and activities for the Protection of Women and Children is conducted at the end of each fiscal year or when necessary as needed.

(2) The results of the evaluation of the implementation of policies, programs and activities for the protection of Women and Children are used as input material for the formulation of policies, programs and activities for the protection of Women and Child victims of violence for the following year.

# CHAPTER XII

COMMUNITY AND PRIVATE SECTOR PARTICIPATION

#### Article 59

(1) The community and private sector participate in assisting efforts to protect women and children from all forms of violence, exploitation and discrimination.

(2) Forms of community and private sector participation as referred to in paragraph (1) may include:

- a. establishing family partners at the Urban Village/Village level by the community;
- b. conducting independent socialization of women's and children's rights;
- c. reporting or providing information on incidents of violence, exploitation and discrimination experienced by women and children;
- d. providing safe houses and shelters;
- e. providing legal assistance for children in conflict with the law;
- f. providing educational scholarships;
- g. providing playgrounds for children;
- h. conducting supervision of the implementation of the protection of women and children; and
- i. other participation relevant to child protection efforts.

# CHAPTER XIII FINANCING

#### Article 60

Financing for the implementation of the Protection of Women and Children is sourced from:

- a. regional revenue and expenditure budget; and
- b. other legitimate sources in accordance with the provisions of laws and regulations.

#### Article 61

(1) The Regional Government may provide financial assistance to community organizations, social organizations or non-governmental organizations that implement the Protection of Women and Children from violent acts.

(2) Financial assistance as referred to in paragraph (1) is in accordance with regional financial capacity and is implemented in accordance with the provisions of laws and regulations.

# CHAPTER XIV ADMINISTRATIVE SANCTIONS Article 62

(1) Any person or Institution that carries out the service duties of handling complaints from victims of violence against women and children who violates the service principles as referred to in Article 37 paragraph (2) shall be subject to administrative sanctions.

(2) Administrative sanctions as referred to in paragraph (1) are in the form of:

- a. verbal warning;
- b. written warning;
- c. temporary license revocation; or
- d. license revocation.
  - (3) Temporary license revocation as referred to in paragraph (2) letter c is carried out after 3 (three) consecutive written warnings with a grace period of 7 (seven) working days.
  - (4) License revocation as referred to in paragraph (2) letter c is carried out after temporary revocation with a grace period of 1 (one) month.

#### Article 63

The head of an educational unit who violates the provisions as referred to in Article 24 paragraph (4) shall be subject to employment sanctions in accordance with the provisions of laws and regulations.

# CHAPTER XV

TRANSITIONAL PROVISIONS

#### Article 64

At the time this Regional Regulation comes into effect, the existing P2TP2A shall continue to carry out the duties and functions of integrated services for women and children until the P2TP2A is established based on this Regional Regulation.

This Regional Regulation shall come into effect on the date of promulgation.
