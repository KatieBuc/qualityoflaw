CONCERNING

THE IMPLEMENTATION OF PROTECTION OF WOMEN AND CHILDREN BY THE GRACE OF ALMIGHTY GOD

THE REGENT OF TRENGGALEK,

Considering : a. that the Unitary State of the Republic of Indonesia guarantees the welfare of each of its citizens, including protection of the rights of women and the rights of children which are human rights;

b. that in order to improve the position, role, and quality of women is an effort to realize gender equality and justice in family, community, national, and state life;

c. that every child is a bud, potential, and the next generation of the nation's struggle, and is expected to be able to bear that responsibility in the future, therefore they need to be given the widest possible opportunity to grow and develop optimally, both physically, mentally and socially, and with noble character, so that protection efforts need to be made and to realize child welfare by providing guarantees for the fulfillment of their rights and treatment without discrimination;

d. that violence against women and children is a crime against humanity which is a violation of human rights;

e. that based on the considerations as referred to in letters a, b, c, and d, it is necessary to establish a Regional Regulation concerning

the Implementation of Protection of Women and Children;

# CHAPTER I

GENERAL PROVISIONS

#### Article 1

In this Regional Regulation, what is meant by: 1. Region is Trenggalek Regency.

2. Regional Government is the Government of Trenggalek Regency.

3. Regent is the Regent of Trenggalek.

4. Regional House of Representatives, hereinafter abbreviated as DPRD, is the Regional House of Representatives of Trenggalek Regency.

5. Implementation is the form of execution of protection of women and children including protection of women and children victims of violence.

6. Protection of Women is any effort made by family, government and others aimed at women to ensure the fulfillment of women's rights as a form of respect for human rights.

7. Gender is a concept that refers to the roles and responsibilities of men and women that occur as a result of and can be changed by the social and cultural conditions of society.

8. Victim is a woman and child who experiences violence and/or threat of violence.

9. Violence is any act that results in misery or suffering whether physical, sexual, psychological, and/or domestic neglect including threats to commit acts, coercion, or deprivation of a person's freedom unlawfully.

10. Child Protection is all activities to ensure and protect children and their rights so that they can live, grow, develop and participate optimally in accordance with human dignity and receive protection from violence and discrimination.

11. Child is a person who is not yet 18 (eighteen) years of age, including a child who is still in the womb.

12. Special protection is protection given to children in emergency situations, children in conflict with the law, children from minority and isolated groups, children who are exploited economically and/or sexually, children who are trafficked, children who are victims of abuse of narcotics, alcohol, psychotropic substances, and other addictive substances (drugs), children who are victims of kidnapping, sale, trafficking, children who are victims of violence both physical and/or mental, children with disabilities, and children who are victims of abuse and neglect.

13. Protection of Women and Children Victims of violence is all activities aimed at providing a sense of security carried out by the Regional Government, family, advocates, police, prosecutors, courts, social institutions, or other parties who know or hear that violence against Women and Children will or has occurred.

14. Gender mainstreaming is a strategy built to integrate gender into an integral dimension of planning, formulation, implementation, monitoring, and evaluation of national development program policies.

15. Child rights mainstreaming is a strategy to integrate child issues and rights into every stage of development which includes planning, implementation, monitoring, and evaluation of Laws and Regulations, policies, programs, activities and budgets with the principles of the best interests of the child.

16. Neglected child is a child whose needs are not properly fulfilled, whether physical, mental, spiritual, or social.

17. Child with disability is a child who experiences physical and/or mental obstacles that interfere with their growth and development properly.

18. Child with excellence is a child who has extraordinary intelligence, or has special potential and/or talent.

19. Adopted child is a child whose rights are transferred from the environment of the authority of the family of parents, legal guardian, or other person responsible for the care, education, and raising of the child, into the family environment of adoptive parents based on a court decision or determination.

20. Foster child is a child who is cared for by a person or institution, to be given guidance, maintenance, care, education, and health, because their parents or one of their parents is unable to ensure the child's proper growth and development.

21. Custody is the power of parents to care for, educate, maintain, nurture, protect, and develop children in accordance with their religion and abilities, talents, and interests.

22. Child rights are part of human rights that must be guaranteed, protected, and fulfilled by parents, family, society, government, and the state.

23. Society is individuals, families, groups, and social organizations and/or community organizations. 24. Companion is a social worker who has professional competence in their field.

25. Family is the smallest unit in society consisting of husband and wife, or husband and wife and their children, or father and their children, or mother and their children, or blood relatives in direct line upwards or downwards up to the third degree.

26. Parents are biological father and/or mother, or step father and/or mother, or adoptive father and/or mother.

27. Guardian is a person or body that in fact exercises custody as a parent to a child.

28. Every person is an individual or corporation. 29. Integrated Service Center for Women and Children (PPTPA) is a cooperative network that carries out integrated service functions for the protection of women and children victims of violence.

30. Safe House (Shelter) is a shelter for women and children including family and witnesses who are victims of violence.

31. Community Social Institution is a Non-Governmental Organization (NGO) and/or other community organization.

32. Human Trafficking is the act of recruitment, transportation, harboring, sending, transfer, or receipt of a person by threat of violence, use of violence, abduction, confinement, forgery, fraud, abuse of power or position of vulnerability, debt bondage or giving payment or benefits, so as to obtain consent from the person holding control over the other person, whether conducted within the country or between countries, for the purpose of exploitation or resulting in the person being exploited.

33. Criminal Act of Human Trafficking is any act or series of acts that meet the elements of a criminal act specified in the Law on Human Trafficking.

# CHAPTER II

PRINCIPLES AND OBJECTIVES

#### Article 2

(1) The implementation of the Protection of Women is based on Pancasila and the 1945 Constitution of the Republic of Indonesia including:

- a. respect for human rights;
- b. justice and gender equality;
- c. non-discrimination; and
- d. victim protection.
  - (2) The implementation of child protection is based on Pancasila and the 1945 Constitution of the Republic of Indonesia as well as the basic principles of the Convention on the Rights of the Child including:
- a. non-discrimination;
- b. the best interests of the child;
- c. the right to life, survival, and development; and
- d. respect for the views of the child.

#### Article 3

The objectives of implementing the protection of women are: a. to ensure the fulfillment of women's rights;

b. to protect women and prevent all forms of violence and discrimination against women; and

c. to maintain the integrity of a harmonious and prosperous household.

#### Article 4

The objectives of implementing child protection are: a. to ensure the fulfillment of children's rights so that they can live, grow, develop, and participate optimally in accordance with human dignity;

b. to protect children and prevent all forms of violence and discrimination against children; and

c. to create quality children who have noble character and are prosperous.

# CHAPTER III

PROTECTION OF WOMEN

## Part One

Rights and Obligations of Women

#### Article 5

Every Woman has the right:

- a. to live and has the right to maintain life and livelihood;
- b. to form a family and continue offspring through lawful marriage;
- c. to develop herself through fulfillment of basic needs;
- d. to obtain education and benefit from science and technology, arts and culture, in order to improve the quality of her life and for the welfare of mankind;
- e. to advance herself in fighting for her rights collectively to build society, nation and state;
- f. to recognition, guarantee, protection and fair legal certainty as well as equal treatment before the law;
- g. to work and receive fair and proper remuneration and treatment in employment relations;
- h. to obtain equal opportunity in government;
- i. to freedom of association, assembly and expression of opinion;
- j. to protection of personal self, family, honor, dignity and property under her authority, and the right to a sense of security and protection from the threat of fear to do or not do something which is a basic right;
- k. to be free from torture or treatment that degrades human dignity and the right to obtain political asylum from other countries;
- l. to live prosperously physically and spiritually, to reside and obtain a good and healthy living environment, and the right to obtain health services;
- m. to social security that enables her full development as a dignified human being;
- n. to equality of position in law and government;
- o. to decent work and livelihood for humanity; p. in the defense of the state;
- q. to communicate and obtain information to develop personal and social environment, and the right to seek, obtain, own, store, process, and convey information using all types of available channels;
- r. to embrace their respective religions and to worship according to their religion and beliefs; and
- s. to obtain education.

#### Article 6

Every person is obliged to respect the human rights of others in the orderly life of society, nation and state.

## Part Two

Regional Government Obligations

#### Article 7

Regional Government is obliged to implement efforts for the protection of women.

#### Article 8

(1) To implement the provisions as referred to in Article 7, Regional Government shall organize gender mainstreaming including:

- a. establishing the implementation of gender mainstreaming;
- b. coordinating, facilitating, and mediating gender mainstreaming;
- c. facilitating institutional strengthening and development of gender mainstreaming mechanisms in government institutions, women's study centers, research and development institutions, non-governmental institutions;
- d. implementing gender mainstreaming related to the fields of development, education, health, economy, law, Human Rights and politics, environment and socio-culture;
- e. improving the quality of life of women related to the fields of development, education, health, economy, law, Human Rights and politics, environment and socio-culture;
- f. integrating efforts to improve the quality of life of women related to the fields of development, education, health, economy, law, Human Rights and politics, environment and socio-culture;
- g. coordinating the implementation of improving the quality of life of women related to the fields of development, education, health, economy, law, Human Rights and politics, environment and socio-culture;
- h. organizing the protection of women especially protection against victims of violence, female workers, elderly women, and women with disabilities, in conflict areas and disaster-affected areas;
- i. facilitating the integration of protection of women especially protection against victims of violence, female workers, elderly women and women with disabilities, in conflict areas and disaster-affected areas;
- j. coordinating the protection of women especially protection against victims of violence, female workers, elderly women and women with disabilities;
- k. facilitating the strengthening and development of networks of community institutions or organizations and the business sector for the implementation of gender mainstreaming;
- l. developing gender-based information systems; and
- m. providing and organizing integrated or non-integrated services for women victims of violence, female workers, elderly women, and women with disabilities.
  - (2) Further provisions regarding forms of protection of women and gender mainstreaming mechanisms as referred to in paragraph (1) shall be regulated by Regent Regulation.

## Part Three

Community Participation

#### Article 9

(1) To provide protection of women as referred to in Article 8, Regional Government may involve community participation.

(2) Community participation as referred to in paragraph (1) shall be carried out by individuals, Social Community Institutions, religious institutions and/or other legitimate institutions.

# CHAPTER IV

CHILD PROTECTION

## Part One

Rights and Obligations of Children

#### Article 10

Every child has the right:

- a. to survival, growth, development and participation in a reasonable manner in accordance with human dignity and to receive protection from violence and discrimination;
- b. to a name as personal identity and citizenship status;
- c. to worship according to their religion, think, and express themselves in accordance with their level of intelligence and age, under the guidance of parents;
- d. to know their parents, be raised, and cared for by their own parents;
- e. to obtain health services and social security in accordance with physical, mental, spiritual, and social needs; f. to obtain education and teaching in the context of developing their personality and level of intelligence in accordance with their interests and talents;
- g. to express and be heard their opinions, receive, seek, and provide information in accordance with their level of intelligence and age for their self-development in accordance with the values of decency and propriety;
- h. to rest and utilize free time, socialize with peers, play, recreate, and create in accordance with their interests, talents, and level of intelligence for self-development;
- i. to receive protection from discriminatory treatment, exploitation, both economic and sexual, neglect, violence, abuse, injustice, other mistreatment, while in the care of parents, guardians, or any other party responsible for care;
- j. to be cared for by their own parents, unless there are valid reasons and/or legal rules showing that separation is in the best interests of the child and is a last consideration; and
- k. to obtain protection from abuse in political activities, involvement in armed conflicts, involvement in social unrest, involvement in events containing elements of violence and involvement in warfare.

#### Article 11

(1) Every child has the right to obtain protection from being the target of persecution, torture, or imposition of inhuman punishment.

(2) Every child has the right to obtain freedom in accordance with the law.

(3) Arrest, detention, or criminal imprisonment of children shall only be carried out if in accordance with applicable law and can only be carried out as a last resort.

#### Article 12

(1) Every child who is deprived of their freedom has the right to: a. receive humane treatment and be placed separately from adults;

b. obtain legal assistance or other assistance effectively at every stage of applicable legal proceedings; and

c. defend themselves and obtain justice before an objective and impartial juvenile court in a closed hearing.

(2) Every child who becomes a victim or perpetrator of sexual violence or who is in conflict with the law has the right to confidentiality.

#### Article 13

Every child who becomes a victim or perpetrator of a criminal offense has the right to obtain legal assistance and other assistance.

#### Article 14

(1) In the event that for any reason the parents cannot guarantee the child's growth and development, or the child is in a state of neglect, then the child has the right to be cared for or adopted as a foster child or adopted child by others in accordance with the provisions of Laws and Regulations.

(2) In the event that parents, guardians, or caregivers commit any form of treatment as referred to in Article 10 letter j, then the perpetrator shall be subject to aggravated punishment in accordance with Laws and Regulations.

#### Article 15

Specifically for children with disabilities, in addition to receiving the rights as referred to in Article 10 through Article 12 and Article 14, they also have the right to:

- a. obtain special education, while for children who have excellence also have the right to receive special education; and
- b. obtain rehabilitation, social assistance, and maintenance of social welfare standards.

#### Article 16

Every child is obligated to:

- a. respect parents, guardians, and teachers;
- b. love family, society, and care for friends; c. love the homeland, nation and state;
- d. perform worship in accordance with their religious teachings; and
- e. practice noble ethics and morals.

## Part Two

Obligations and Responsibilities

### Paragraph 1

General

#### Article 17

The Regional Government, society, family, and parents are obligated and responsible for the implementation of child protection.

### Paragraph 2

Obligations and Responsibilities

Regional Government

#### Article 18

The Regional Government is obligated in efforts to protect children.

#### Article 19

(1) To implement the provisions as referred to in Article 18, the Regional Government shall organize child rights mainstreaming including:

- a. establish the implementation of child rights mainstreaming;
- b. coordinate and facilitate child rights mainstreaming;
- c. facilitate institutional strengthening and development of child mainstreaming mechanisms in government institutions, research and development institutions, non-governmental institutions;
- d. coordinate and facilitate child-responsive programs and activities and the development of communication, information, and education on child rights mainstreaming;
- e. implement child rights mainstreaming related to the fields of development, education, health, economy, law, Human Rights and politics, environment and socio-culture;
- f. improve the quality of life of children related to the fields of development, education, health, economy, law, Human Rights and politics, environment and socio-culture;
- g. integrate development efforts related to the fields of development, education, health, economy, law, Human Rights and politics, environment and socio-culture;
- h. coordinate the implementation of improving the quality of life of children related to the fields of development, education, health, economy, law, Human Rights and politics, environment and socio-culture;
- i. provide protection for children, especially for victims of violence, children with disabilities in conflict areas and disaster-affected areas;
- j. facilitate the integration of child protection, especially protection for victims of violence, children with disabilities, in conflict areas and disaster-affected areas;
- k. coordinate child protection, especially protection for victims of violence, children with disabilities, in conflict areas and disaster-affected areas;
- l. facilitate the strengthening and development of networks of community institutions or organizations and the business sector for the implementation of child rights mainstreaming; and
- m. provide and organize integrated or non-integrated services for child victims of violence, children with disabilities, and neglected children.
  - (2) Further provisions regarding forms of child protection and mechanisms for mainstreaming child rights as referred to in paragraph (1) shall be regulated by Regent Regulation.

#### Article 20

The Regional Government is obligated and responsible to respect and guarantee the human rights of every child without distinguishing ethnicity, religion, race, class, gender, ethnicity, culture and language, legal status of the child, order of birth of the child, and physical and/or mental condition.

#### Article 21

The Regional Government is obligated and responsible to provide support for facilities and infrastructure in the implementation of child protection.

#### Article 22

(1) The Regional Government guarantees the protection, maintenance, and welfare of children with due regard to the rights and obligations of parents, guardians, or other persons who are legally responsible for the child.

(2) The Regional Government supervises the implementation of child protection.

#### Article 23

The Regional Government guarantees children to use their rights in expressing opinions in accordance with the child's age and level of intelligence.

### Paragraph 3

Obligations and Responsibilities

Society

#### Article 24

The obligations and responsibilities of society for child protection are implemented through activities of community participation in the implementation of child protection.

### Paragraph 4

Obligations and Responsibilities

Family and Parents

#### Article 25

(1) Parents are obligated and responsible to: a. care for, nurture, educate, and protect the child;

b. develop the child in accordance with their abilities, talents, and interests; and

c. prevent marriage at child age. (2) In the event that parents are absent, or their whereabouts are unknown, or for any reason, cannot carry out their obligations and responsibilities, then the obligations and responsibilities as referred to in paragraph (1) may be transferred to the family, which shall be implemented in accordance with the provisions of Laws and Regulations.

## Part Three

Child Status, Custody, Guardianship, Care and Child Adoption

#### Article 26

Matters concerning child status, custody, guardianship, care and child adoption shall be regulated in accordance with the provisions of Laws and Regulations.

## Part Four

Implementation of Protection

### Paragraph 1

Religion

#### Article 27

(1) Every child receives protection to worship according to their religion.

(2) Before the child can determine their choice, the religion embraced by the child follows the religion of their parents.

#### Article 28

(1) The Regional Government, society, family, parents, guardians, and social community institutions guarantee the protection of children in embracing their religion.

(2) Protection of children in embracing their religion as referred to in paragraph (1) includes guidance, counseling, and practice of religious teachings for children.

### Paragraph 2

Health

#### Article 29

(1) The Regional Government is obligated to provide facilities and organize comprehensive health efforts for children, so that every child obtains optimal health status from the womb.

(2) The provision of facilities and organization of comprehensive health efforts as referred to in paragraph (1) shall be supported by community participation.

(3) Comprehensive health efforts as referred to in paragraph (1) include promotive, preventive, curative, and rehabilitative efforts, both for basic health services and referrals.

(4) Comprehensive health efforts as referred to in paragraph (3) shall be organized free of charge for families who are unable to afford them.

(5) The implementation of provisions as referred to in paragraphs (1) through (4) shall be adjusted to the provisions of Laws and Regulations.

#### Article 30

(1) Parents and family are responsible for maintaining the health of children and caring for children from the womb. (2) In the event that parents and family are unable to carry out the responsibilities as referred to in paragraph (1), the Regional Government is obligated to fulfill them. (3) The obligations as referred to in paragraph (2) shall be implemented in accordance with the provisions of Laws and Regulations.

#### Article 31

The Regional Government, family, and parents are obligated to ensure that children who are born are protected from diseases that threaten survival and/or cause disability.

#### Article 32

(1) The Regional Government, family, and parents are obligated to protect children from attempts to transplant their bodily organs to other parties.

(2) The Regional Government, family, and parents are obligated to protect children from the acts of:

- a. removal of children's bodily organs and/or children's bodily tissues without regard to the health of the child;
- b. buying and selling of children's organs and/or children's bodily tissues; and c. health research that uses children as research subjects without parental consent and does not prioritize the best interests of the child.

### Paragraph 3

Education

#### Article 33

(1) The Regional Government is obligated to provide basic education for a minimum of 9 (nine) years for all children.

(2) Parents are obligated to fulfill the child's right to obtain basic education for a minimum of 9 (nine) years.

#### Article 34

The Regional Government, family, and parents are obligated to provide the widest possible opportunity to children to obtain education.

#### Article 35

Education as referred to in Article 33 is directed toward:

- a. development of the child's attitudes and personality abilities, talents, mental and physical abilities to achieve their optimal potential;
- b. development of respect for human rights and fundamental freedoms;
- c. development of respect for parents, cultural identity, their own language and values, national values where the child resides, from where the child originates, and civilizations different from their own civilization;
- d. preparation of the child for a responsible life; and
- e. development of respect and love for the environment.

#### Article 36

Children with physical and/or mental disabilities are given equal opportunity and accessibility to obtain regular education and special education.

#### Article 37

Children who have excellence are given opportunity and accessibility to obtain special education.

#### Article 38

(1) The Regional Government is responsible for providing education costs and/or free assistance or special services for children from underprivileged families, neglected children, and children residing in remote areas.

(2) The accountability of the Regional Government as referred to in paragraph (1) also includes encouraging the community to play an active role.

#### Article 39

Children in and around the school must be protected from acts of violence committed by teachers, school administrators or their peers within the relevant school, or other educational institutions.

### Paragraph 4

Social

#### Article 40

(1) The Regional Government is obligated to organize the maintenance and care of neglected children, both within institutions and outside institutions.

(2) The organization of maintenance as referred to in paragraph (1) may be carried out by community institutions. (3) To organize the maintenance and care of neglected children, government institutions and community institutions, as referred to in paragraph (2), may establish cooperation with various related parties.

(4) In the case of organizing maintenance and care as referred to in paragraph (3), its supervision is conducted by regional apparatus institutions that administer social affairs in accordance with the provisions of Laws and Regulations.

#### Article 41

(1) The Regional Government in organizing maintenance and care is obligated to strive for and assist children, so that children can:

- a. participate;
- b. freely express opinions and think in accordance with their conscience and religion;
- c. freely receive oral or written information in accordance with the child's age stage and development;
- d. freely associate and assemble;
- e. freely rest, play, recreate, create, and engage in cultural arts; and
- f. obtain play facilities that meet health and safety requirements.
  - (2) The efforts as referred to in paragraph (1) are developed and adjusted to the child's age, level of ability, and environment so as not to hinder or disrupt the child's development.

#### Article 42

In the event that a child is neglected due to a reason that their parents neglect their obligations, then the institution as referred to in Article 40, family, or authorized official may submit a request to the court to designate the child as a neglected child.

#### Article 43

(1) The court determination as referred to in Article 42 simultaneously determines the place of shelter, maintenance, and care for the neglected child concerned.

(2) The Regional Government or authorized institution is obligated to provide the place as referred to in paragraph (1).

## Part Five

Special Protection

#### Article 44

The Regional Government and other state institutions have the obligation and responsibility to provide special protection to children in emergency situations, children in conflict with the law, children from minority and isolated groups, children exploited economically and/or sexually, children who are trafficked, children who are victims of abuse of narcotics, alcohol, psychotropic substances, and other addictive substances (napza), children who are victims of kidnapping, sale and trafficking, children who are victims of violence both physical and/or mental, children with disabilities, and children who are victims of maltreatment and neglect.

#### Article 45

Children in emergency situations as referred to in Article 44 consist of:

- a. children who become refugees;
- b. children who are victims of riots;
- c. children who are victims of natural disasters; and
- d. children in armed conflict situations.

#### Article 46

Special protection for children who become refugees as referred to in Article 45 letter a is implemented in accordance with the provisions of Laws and Regulations.

#### Article 47

Special protection for children who are victims of riots, victims of disasters, and children in armed conflict situations as referred to in Article 45 letter b through letter d, is implemented through:

- a. fulfillment of basic needs consisting of food, clothing, housing, education, health, learning and recreation, security guarantees, and equal treatment; and
- b. fulfillment of special needs for children with disabilities and children experiencing psychosocial disorders.

#### Article 48

Every person is prohibited from recruiting or exploiting children for military and/or other interests and leaving children without protection of life.

#### Article 49

(1) Special protection for children in conflict with the law as referred to in Article 44 includes children in conflict with the law and child victims of criminal acts, and is the obligation and responsibility of the Regional Government and society.

(2) Special protection for children in conflict with the law as referred to in paragraph (1) is implemented through:

- a. treatment of children in a humane manner in accordance with the dignity and rights of the child;
- b. provision of special child assistance officers from an early stage; c. provision of special facilities and infrastructure;
- d. imposition of appropriate sanctions for the best interests of the child;
- e. continuous monitoring and recording of the development of children in conflict with the law; f. provision of guarantees to maintain relationships with parents or family; and
- g. protection from disclosure of identity through mass media and to avoid labeling.
  - (3) Special protection for children who are victims of criminal acts as referred to in paragraph (1) is implemented through:
- a. rehabilitation efforts, both within institutions and outside institutions;
- b. protection efforts from disclosure of identity through mass media and to avoid labeling;
- c. provision of safety guarantees for victim witnesses and expert witnesses, whether physical, mental, or social; and
- d. provision of accessibility to obtain information regarding case developments.

#### Article 50

(1) Special protection for children from minority and isolated groups as referred to in Article 44 is carried out through the provision of infrastructure and facilities to be able to enjoy their own culture, acknowledge and practice their own religious teachings, and use their own language.

(2) Every person is prohibited from obstructing children as referred to in paragraph (1) from enjoying their own culture, acknowledging and practicing their religious teachings, and using their own language without neglecting access to community and cultural development.

#### Article 51

(1) Special protection for children who are exploited economically and/or sexually as referred to in Article 44 is the obligation and responsibility of the Regional Government and society.

(2) Special protection for children who are exploited as referred to in paragraph (1) is carried out through: a. dissemination and/or socialization of provisions of Laws and Regulations relating to the protection of children who are exploited economically and/or sexually;

b. monitoring, reporting, and imposition of sanctions; and c. involvement of various Regional Government agencies, companies, labor unions, Social Community Institutions, and the community in the elimination of exploitation of children economically and/or sexually.

(3) Every person is prohibited from placing, allowing, committing, ordering to commit, or participating in the exploitation of children as referred to in paragraph (1).

#### Article 52

(1) Special protection for children who are victims of abuse of narcotics, alcohol, psychotropic substances, and other addictive substances (drugs) as referred to in Article 44, and involved in their production and distribution, is carried out through supervision, prevention, treatment, and rehabilitation efforts by the Regional Government and society.

(2) Every person is prohibited from intentionally placing, allowing, involving, ordering to involve children in the abuse, production and distribution of drugs as referred to in paragraph (1).

#### Article 53

(1) Special protection for child victims of kidnapping, sale, and trafficking of children as referred to in Article 44 is carried out through supervision, protection, prevention, treatment, and rehabilitation efforts by the Regional Government and society.

(2) Every person is prohibited from placing, allowing, committing, ordering to commit, or participating in kidnapping, sale, or trafficking as referred to in paragraph (1).

#### Article 54

(1) Special protection for child victims of violence as referred to in Article 44 includes physical, psychological, and sexual violence carried out through efforts: a dissemination and socialization of provisions of Laws

and Regulations that protect child victims of violence; and b monitoring, reporting, and imposition of sanctions. (2) Every person is prohibited from placing, allowing, committing, ordering to commit, or participating in violence as referred to in paragraph (1).

#### Article 55

(1) Special protection for children with disabilities as referred to in Article 44 is carried out through efforts:

- a. treatment of children in a humane manner in accordance with the dignity and rights of the child;
- b. fulfillment of special needs; and c. obtaining the same treatment as other children to achieve social integration as fully as possible and individual development.
  - (2) Every person is prohibited from treating children by ignoring their views in a discriminatory manner, including labeling and equalization in education for children with disabilities.

#### Article 56

(1) Special protection for child victims of abuse and neglect as referred to in Article 44 is carried out through supervision, prevention, treatment, and rehabilitation by the Regional Government and society.

(2) Every person is prohibited from placing, allowing, involving, ordering to involve children in situations of abuse and neglect as referred to in paragraph (1).

## Part Six

Community Participation

#### Article 57

(1) The community has the right to obtain the widest possible opportunity to play a role in child protection.

(2) The community role as referred to in paragraph (1) is carried out by individuals, child protection institutions, social community institutions, educational institutions, religious institutions, business entities, and mass media.

#### Article 58

The community role is implemented in accordance with the provisions of Laws and Regulations.

# CHAPTER V

PROTECTION OF VICTIMS OF VIOLENCE

## Part One

Victims' Rights

#### Article 59

Every victim has the right to:

- a. obtain protection from family, the Regional Government, police, prosecution, courts, advocates, social institutions or other parties either temporarily or based on the determination of a protection order from the Court;
- b. obtain health services in accordance with medical needs;
- c. obtain special handling related to the confidentiality of victims;
- d. obtain assistance from social workers and legal aid at every level of the examination process in accordance with the provisions of Laws and Regulations; and
- e. obtain spiritual guidance services;
- f. obtain protection from threats that endanger themselves, life and/or property whether before, during or after the case examination process from the Police;
- g. obtain health rehabilitation, social rehabilitation, repatriation and social reintegration if the person concerned experiences suffering either physically or psychologically;
- h. obtain restitution for victims of human trafficking crimes or their heirs in accordance with the provisions of Laws and Regulations; and
- i. other rights stipulated in the provisions of Laws and Regulations.

#### Article 60

Every victim has the right to report and obtain assistance both psychologically and legally and to obtain guarantees for their rights relating to their status as a wife, mother, child, family member or as a member of society.

#### Article 61

The rights as referred to in Article 59 letter c, are also given to the families of witnesses and/or victims up to the second degree, if the families of witnesses and/or victims receive threats either physically or psychologically from other persons concerning the testimony of witnesses and/or victims.

## Part Two

Obligations of Regional Government and Society

#### Article 62

The Regional Government is obligated to implement prevention of acts of violence, criminal acts of human trafficking, and protection efforts for victims.

#### Article 63

(1) To implement the provisions referred to in Article 62, the Regional Government:

- a. formulates policies on efforts to prevent violence and prevent the criminal act of trafficking in persons against women and children;
- b. formulates policies on the elimination of violence;
- c. conducts communication, information, education, socialization, and advocacy on violence against women and children and the criminal act of trafficking in persons against women and children;
- d. establishes institutions as a forum for cooperation in the protection and recovery of victims;
- e. implements mechanisms for medical services, medicolegal services, psychosocial services, legal services, assistance services, spiritual guidance services, and victim recovery services;
- f. provides facilities and infrastructure for the protection and recovery of victims;
- g. facilitates the resocialization of victims, so that victims can return to performing their social functions in society; and
- h. conducts health rehabilitation services, social rehabilitation, repatriation, and social reintegration for victims of the criminal act of trafficking in persons against women and children.
  - (2) Further provisions regarding the forms and mechanisms of victim protection as referred to in paragraph (1) shall be regulated by Regent Regulation.

#### Article 64

(1) Families and communities are obliged to prevent the occurrence of violence and the occurrence of criminal acts against women and children.

(2) Every person who hears, sees, or knows of the occurrence of violence is obliged to make efforts in accordance with their capacity to:

- a. prevent the continuation of criminal acts;
- b. provide protection to victims;
- c. provide emergency assistance; and
- d. assist in the process of filing an application for protection order.

## Part Three

Community Participation In

Victim Protection

#### Article 65

(1) The community participates in assisting prevention and handling efforts for victims of violence and criminal acts against women and children.

(2) Community participation as referred to in paragraph (1) shall be realized through actions of providing information and/or reporting the occurrence of violence and criminal acts against women and children to law enforcement or the authorities, or participating in handling victims of violence and criminal acts against women and children.

(3) To carry out the participation as referred to in paragraph (2), the community is entitled to legal protection.

(4) The Regional Government in organizing victim protection may cooperate with the community or social community organizations.

CONCERNING

IMPLEMENTATION OF PROTECTION OF WOMEN AND CHILDREN

I. GENERAL

The existence of Regional Regulations that side with vulnerable or subordinated groups, particularly women and children, becomes very necessary in connection with the many social problems that result in the deprivation of the human rights of women and children.

The human rights of women and children are part of human rights contained in the 1945 Constitution and the United Nations Convention on the Rights of the Child as well as the ratification of the convention on the elimination of all forms of discrimination against women, however the enforcement of the human rights of women and children has still not been able to encourage becoming a political commitment in the Region.

The form of implementation of protection of women carried out by the Government is to implement gender mainstreaming, namely a strategy built to integrate gender into an integral dimension of planning, formulation, implementation, monitoring and evaluation of regional development program policies within the framework of National development.

A child is a mandate as well as a gift from God Almighty, which we must always protect because in him/her is inherent dignity, honor, and rights as a human being that must be upheld. From the perspective of the life of the nation and state, children are the future of the nation and the next generation of the nation's ideals, so that every child has the right to survival, growth, and development, to participate and has the right to protection from acts of violence and discrimination as well as civil rights and freedoms.

Although Law Number 23 of 2002 concerning Child Protection has included the rights of children, the implementation of the obligations and responsibilities of parents, family, community, government, and the state to provide protection to children, a Regional Regulation is still needed as a juridical foundation for the implementation of such obligations and responsibilities in the Region. Thus, the establishment of this Regional Regulation is based on the consideration that child protection in all its aspects is part of development activities in the Region, particularly in advancing the life of the nation and state.

Parents, family, and community are responsible for safeguarding and maintaining these human rights in accordance with the obligations imposed by law. Likewise, in the context of implementing child protection, the state and Regional Government are responsible for providing facilities and accessibility for children, especially in ensuring their growth and development in an optimal and directed manner.

This Regional Regulation affirms that the accountability of parents, family, community, Regional Government and the state is a series of activities carried out continuously for the protection of children's rights. This series of activities must be sustainable and directed to ensure the growth and development of children, both physically, mentally, spiritually and socially. This action is intended to realize the best life for children who are expected to be potential successors to the nation, resilient, having nationalism imbued with noble character and Pancasila values, as well as having a strong will to maintain the unity and integrity of the nation and state.

Child protection efforts need to be implemented as early as possible, namely from the fetus in the womb until the child is 18 (eighteen) years old. Starting from the concept of holistic, comprehensive, and complete child protection, this Regional Regulation places the obligation to provide protection to children based on the following principles:

- a. non-discrimination;
- b. the best interests of the child;
- c. the right to life, survival, and development; and d. respect for the views of the child.

In conducting guidance, development and protection of children, the role of the community is needed, whether through child protection institutions, religious institutions, non-governmental organizations, community organizations, social organizations, business world, mass media, or educational institutions.

The form of implementation of child protection carried out by the Regional Government is through mainstreaming children's rights, namely integrating children's issues and rights into every stage of development which includes planning, implementation, monitoring, and evaluation of laws and regulations, policy programs and budgets with the principles of the best interests of the child.

The integrity and harmony of a happy, safe, peaceful, and tranquil household is the aspiration of every person in the household. The Republic of Indonesia is a state based on the Belief in the One and Only God as guaranteed by Article 29 of the 1945 Constitution of the Republic of Indonesia. Thus, every person within the scope of the household in carrying out their rights and obligations must be based on religion. This needs to be continuously developed in order to build household integrity.

To realize such integrity and harmony, it is highly dependent on each person within the scope of the household, especially the level of quality of behavior and self-control of each person within the scope of the household.

The integrity and harmony of the household can be disrupted if quality and self-control cannot be controlled, which ultimately can result in domestic violence resulting in insecurity or injustice against people within the scope of the household.

To prevent, protect victims, and prosecute perpetrators of domestic violence, the state and society are obliged to carry out prevention, protection, and prosecution of perpetrators in accordance with the philosophy of Pancasila and the 1945 Constitution of the Republic of Indonesia. The state views that all forms of violence, especially domestic violence, are violations of human rights and crimes against human dignity as well as a form of discrimination. The state's view is based on Article 28 of the 1945 Constitution of the Republic of Indonesia, together with its amendments. Article 28G paragraph (1) of the 1945 Constitution of the Republic of Indonesia stipulates that "Every person shall have the right to protection of his/her person, family, honor, dignity, and property under his/her control, and shall have the right to a sense of security and protection from the threat of fear to do or not do something that is a human right". Article 28H paragraph (2) of the 1945 Constitution of the Republic of Indonesia stipulates that "Every person shall have the right to obtain facilities and special treatment to obtain equal opportunities and benefits in order to achieve equality and justice".

Current developments show that acts of physical, psychological, sexual violence, and domestic neglect in reality occur so that adequate legal instruments are needed to eliminate domestic violence.

The Regional Regulation regulates the matter of prevention and protection as well as recovery for victims of violence, and regulates the obligations of law enforcement officials, health workers, social workers, assisting volunteers, or spiritual counselors to protect victims so that they are more sensitive and responsive to the interests of the household which from the outset is directed at the integrity and harmony of the household. To prevent domestic violence, the Regional Government is obliged to carry out duties in the field of women's empowerment to carry out preventive actions, among others, organizing communication, information, and education regarding the prevention of violence against women and children.

Based on these considerations, it is time to establish a Regional Regulation that is regulated comprehensively, clearly, and firmly in the implementation of protection of women and children including protection of women and children victims of violence.
