# CHAPTER I

GENERAL PROVISIONS

Article 1

In this Regional Regulation, the following terms shall mean: 1. Regency is Penukal Abab Lematang Ilir Regency. 2. Regency Government is the Regent as an element of the regency government organizer who leads the implementation of governmental affairs that are the authority of the autonomous region.

3. Regent is the Regent of Penukal Abab Lematang Ilir. 4. Regency Regional Apparatus is an auxiliary element of the Regent and the Regency Regional House of Representatives in organizing governmental affairs that are the authority of the regency.

5. Child is a person who has not yet reached the age of 18 (eighteen) years, including a child still in the womb. 6. Woman is a human being of female sex and a person who is recognized by law as a woman. 7. Fulfillment of Children's Rights is all activities to guarantee and protect children and their rights so that they can live, grow, develop and participate optimally in accordance with human dignity and receive protection from violence and discrimination. 8. Violence is any act that results in misery or suffering whether physical, sexual, psychological, and/or domestic neglect including threats to commit acts, coercion, or deprivation of a person's liberty unlawfully. 9. Victim is a woman and/or child who experiences misery and/or suffering either directly or indirectly as a result of violence that occurs in the territory of Penukal Abab Lematang Ilir Regency. 10.Protection of women is all activities aimed at providing a sense of security carried out by the police, prosecutor's office, court, social institutions, or other parties who know or hear that violence against women will or has occurred.

11.Child protection is all activities to guarantee and protect children and their rights so that they can live, grow, develop and participate optimally in accordance with human dignity and receive protection from violence and discrimination.

12.Prevention is activities carried out to prevent the occurrence of acts of violence against women and children. 13.Service is action taken as soon as possible to victims when seeing, hearing and knowing that violence against victims will, is, or has occurred. 14.Empowerment is strengthening women victims of violence to be able to strive and work independently after they are restored and provided with health and social rehabilitation services.

15.Health Rehabilitation is an effort to restore the victim's condition including physical, psychological and social health so that the victim can carry out their role again properly and reasonably both in the family and in society.

16.Social rehabilitation is services aimed at restoring and developing the abilities of a person experiencing social dysfunction so that they can carry out their social functions properly.

17.Repatriation is an effort to return victims of violence from the regional territory to their region of origin.

18.Social reintegration is an effort to reunite victims with their family, substitute family or community that can provide protection and fulfillment of needs for victims.

19.Integrated Service Center hereinafter abbreviated as PPT, is a unit that provides integrated services for victims of violence and is integrated with PPT that provides integrated services for witnesses and/or victims of criminal acts of human trafficking.

#### Article 2

Implementation of Protection for Women and Children is carried out based on the principles of:

- a. respect and protection of human rights; b. gender justice and equality;
- c. non-discrimination;
- d. best interests of the Child;
- e. right to life, survival, and development; f. Empowerment; and
- g. legal certainty.

Article 3

Protection of Women and Children aims to: a. ensure the fulfillment of the rights of Women and Children; b. maintain the integrity of the household to create a harmonious family;

c. prevent violence against Women and Children; d. protect and provide security for Women and Children;

e. provide services to Women and Children victims of violence;

f. provide protection to reporters and witnesses; and g. empower Women victims of violence.

# CHAPTER II

PROTECTION OF WOMEN AND CHILDREN

## Part One

Rights of Women

#### Article4

Every Woman has the right:

- a. to live, to maintain life and her livelihood; b. to form a family and continue offspring through legal marriage;
- c. to develop herself through fulfillment of her basic needs; d. to obtain education and benefit from science and technology, arts and culture, to improve the quality of her life and for the welfare of humankind;
- e. to advance herself in fighting for her rights collectively to build society, nation and state;
- f. to recognition, guarantee, protection and just legal certainty as well as equal treatment before the law;
- g. to work and receive fair and proper remuneration and treatment in employment relations;
- h. to obtain equal opportunity in government;
- i. to freedom of association, assembly and expression of opinion;
  - J. to protection of her person, family, honor, dignity and property under her control, and the right to security and protection from threats of fear to do or not do something which is a basic right;
- k. to be free from torture or treatment that degrades human dignity and the right to obtain political asylum from another country;
  - 1. to live in physical and spiritual prosperity, to have a place to live and obtain a good and healthy living environment, and the right to obtain health services; m.to social security that enables her full development as a human being with dignity;
- n. to equality of status in law and government;
- o. to work and a livelihood worthy of humanity;
- p. in efforts to defend the state;
- q. to communicate and obtain information to develop her personal and social environment, and the right to seek, obtain, possess, store, process, and convey information using all types of available channels;
- r. to embrace her respective religion and to worship according to her religion and belief; and
- s. to obtain education.

## Part Two

Fulfillment of Children's Rights

### Paragraph 1

Rights and Obligations of Children Article5

Every Child has the right:

- a. to birth registration;
- b. to obtain health;
- c. to obtain education; and
- d. to social welfare.

#### Article6

Every Child has the obligation to:

- a. respect parents, guardians and teachers;
- b. love family, society and care for friends; c. love the homeland, nation and state;
- d. perform worship in accordance with their religion and belief;
- e. study and develop themselves in accordance with their abilities and talents, interests; and
- f. have noble character.

### Paragraph2

Birth Registration

#### Article 7

Birth registration of children as referred to in Article 5 letter a, the Regency Government is obliged to issue a birth certificate in accordance with the provisions of Laws and Regulations.

### Paragraph 3

Health

#### Article8

(1) The implementation of Fulfillment of Children's Rights in the health sector as referred to in Article 5 letter b, the Regency Government is obliged to provide facilities and organize comprehensive health services for children.

(2) Comprehensive health services as referred to in paragraph (1) include:

- a. promotive, preventive, curative and rehabilitative efforts for basic health services; and
- b. exemption from cost burden for malnourished children, Children with disabilities, children with special needs, street children, children infected with HIV/AIDS, child laborers, children victims of narcotics, alcohol, psychotropic and other addictive substances (NAPZA) abuse, Children who are victims of violence, such as neglect, economically and sexually exploited and victims of trafficking in persons.
  - (3) Exemption from cost burden for Children as referred to in paragraph (2) letter b is for Children from poor families unless otherwise stipulated in accordance with the health financing program established by the government.

#### Article9

The Regency Government together with the community, family and parents are obliged to ensure that Children who are born are protected from diseases that threaten survival and/or cause disability.

### Paragraph 4

Education

#### Article10

(1) The implementation of Fulfillment of Children's Rights in the education sector as referred to in Article 5 letter c, the Regency Government guarantees the implementation of a 9 (nine) year compulsory education program.

(2) Every organizer of an education unit is obliged to provide opportunities for Children to obtain educational services without discrimination.

Article11 Every organizer of an education unit is prohibited from expelling Children from educational institutions unless there is a guarantee for the continuity of the Child's education.

Article12 Every organizer of an education unit is obliged to coordinate with relevant child protection agencies if they find Children dropping out of school due to being victims of acts of violence.

#### Article13

Children with disabilities and Children with special needs are given equal opportunities to obtain educational services:

- a. formal;
- b. nonformal;
- c. informal;
- d. special education; and
- e. inclusive.

#### Article14

Early Childhood Education aged 0 (zero) to 3 (three) years and 4 (four) to 6 (six) years may be organized by:

- a. Posyandu institutions;
- b. Posyandu gardens;
- c. Early Childhood Education Posts; and
- d. similar Early Childhood Education unit institutions.

### Paragraph 5

Social Welfare

#### Article15

(1) The implementation of Child Protection and Empowerment in the social welfare sector as referred to in Article 5 letter d includes:

- a. children in conflict with the law;
- b. child victims of violence who are economically and sexually exploited;
- c. neglected children;
- d. child victims of abduction and child victims of trafficking in persons;
- e. child victims of narcotics, alcohol, psychotropic and other addictive substances (NAPZA) abuse;
- f. children infected with HIV/AIDS;
- g. children who do not have parents and are cared for by other parties or families who are unable;
- h. street children;
- i. child victims of natural disasters or social disasters; j. children with physical and mental disabilities;
- k. children of migrant workers' families;
- l. children living in or around prostitution locations; and
- m. child victims of other mistreatment.
  - (2) Social welfare as referred to in paragraph (1) may be in the form of convenience to obtain access to services: a. health;
- b. education;
- c. legal aid;
- d. assistance;
- e. social, mental and spiritual guidance;
- f. social rehabilitation;
- g. empowerment;
- h. social assistance; and/or
- i. reintegration.
  - (3) Special infrastructure for persons with disabilities in public places.

#### Article 16

(1) The Regency Government shall protect the fulfillment of the rights of Child workers in the informal sector.

(2) Child workers in the informal sector as referred to in paragraph (1) include:

- a. domestic workers;
- b. shoe shiners;
- c. street vendors;
- d. scavengers;
- e. parking attendants;
- f. children working in agriculture, livestock and plantations;
- g. children working in mining; and h. other informal sector jobs that employ Children.
  - (3) Every person who employs Children in the informal sector shall observe the requirements:
- a. minimum age of 15 (fifteen) years, except for the category of Worst Forms of Child Labor minimum 18 (eighteen) years;
- b. obtain written consent from the parent/guardian of the Child worker;
- c. have a written employment agreement between the employer and the parent/guardian of the Child worker and obtain validation from the authorized agency;
- d. not be employed at night;
- e. working time of no more than 3 (three) hours per day; f. not be employed in a place or environment that may disturb the child's growth and development process, whether physical, mental, moral and intellectual or the Child's health;
- g. provide opportunities to receive education in accordance with their talents and interests;
- h. employ for light types of work; and 1. provide one day off per week.

Article17

The fulfillment of the rights of Child workers in the informal sector as referred to in Article 16 paragraph (1) includes:

- a. counseling to stakeholders about Children's rights;
- b. assistance with psychological, medical and legal services and socio-economic reintegration;
- c. family empowerment through providing training, business capital stimulus and assistance;
- d. scholarships to continue formal education to higher levels; and
- e. non-formal education and skills training for those who do not pursue formal education.

Third Part

Forms of Violence

Article18

Forms of violence against Women and Children include: a. physical violence;

b. psychological violence;

c. sexual violence;

d. neglect;

e. exploitation; and/or

f. other violence.

Fourth Part

Prevention of Violence against Women and Children Article19

(1) Efforts to prevent violence against Women and Children are carried out in an integrated manner by the Regency Government and coordinated by the technical Regional Apparatus.

(2) Prevention efforts as referred to in paragraph (1) are implemented by:

- a. conducting guidance towards harmonious household integrity;
- b. forming a network in efforts to prevent violence;
- c. conducting coordination, integration, synchronization of violence prevention based on partnership patterns;
- d. establishing a violence prevention system;
- e. conducting socialization regarding laws and regulations related to the protection of Women and Children victims of violence; and
- f. providing critical education regarding the rights of Women and Children to the community.

#### Article20

Efforts to prevent violence against Women and Children as referred to in Article 19 paragraph (1) in addition to the Regency Government are also implemented by:

- a. family and/or closest relatives;
- b. community;
- c. educational institutions; and
- d. Non-Governmental Organizations.

## Fifth Part

Handling of Women and Children Victims of Violence Article21

The implementation of Handling of Women and Children victims of violence is carried out with:

- a. speed;
- b. safety and comfort;
- c. a sense of empathy;
- d. non-discrimination;
- e. easy accessibility;
- f. no charges; and
- g. guaranteed confidentiality.

#### Article22

Forms of handling of Women and Children victims of violence as referred to in Article 21 include services:

- a. complaints, consultation, and counseling;
- b. assistance;
- c. medical rehabilitation;
- d. social rehabilitation;
- e. legal; and
- f. social reintegration.

## Sixth Part

Financing

#### Article23

Financing required for the implementation of the Protection of Women and Children is charged to:

- a. Regional Revenue and Expenditure Budget; and
- b. Other legal and non-binding sources of funds in accordance with the provisions of applicable Laws and Regulations.

# CHAPTER III

INTEGRATED SERVICE CENTER (PPT)

#### Article24

(1) The implementation of services for Women and Children victims of violence is carried out in an integrated manner by the PPT. (2) The PPT as referred to in paragraph (1) is an integrated part and constitutes one unit with the PPT that organizes integrated services for witnesses and/or victims of criminal acts of trafficking in persons. (3) The implementation of services for Women and Children victims of violence as referred to in paragraph (1) includes:

- a. providing fast and free services to victims; b. organizing protection and fulfillment of victims' rights to health rehabilitation, social rehabilitation, repatriation, social reintegration and legal assistance; c. cooperating with certain institutions in providing interpreters and volunteer assistants needed for victims;
- d. networking with government or private hospitals for treatment and health recovery of victims and cooperating with witness and victim protection institutions, social protection houses or trauma centers owned by the government, community or other institutions for victims' health recovery;
- e. providing ease, comfort and safety for victims;
- f. maintaining the confidentiality of victims; and
- g. providing fulfillment of legal assistance for victims. (4) Further provisions regarding procedures and mechanisms for implementing the PPT are regulated by Regent Regulation.

# CHAPTER IV

RIGHTS OF WOMEN AND CHILDREN VICTIMS OF VIOLENCE Article25

Every Woman and Child victim of violence has the right: a. to complaint handling;

b. to have their dignity respected as human beings; c. to receive protection from family, community, local government and/or other parties either temporarily or based on protection determination from the court; d. to health and psychological recovery from the suffering experienced by the victim;

e. to make their own decisions;

f. to special handling related to victim confidentiality;

g. to obtain information;

h. to confidentiality of their identity;

1. to social rehabilitation;

J. to assistance by social workers and legal assistance at every level of examination process in accordance with the provisions of Laws and Regulations; and

k. to spiritual guidance services.

#### Article26

Women and children victims of violence in addition to having the rights as referred to in Article 25, also receive special rights as follows:

- a. right to survival;
- b. to grow and develop;
- c. to receive equal protection; and
- d. to participate in community life.

# CHAPTER V

MONITORING AND EVALUATION

#### Article27

(1) The Regency Government conducts monitoring to ensure synergy, balance and effectiveness of integrated measures in the implementation of policies, programs, activities, Protection of Women and Children.

(2) Monitoring as referred to in paragraph (1) is implemented by the Regional Apparatus and PPT.

(3) Monitoring as referred to in paragraph (1) is to determine progress and obstacles in the implementation of policies, programs and activities for the Protection of Women and Children.

(4) Monitoring is conducted periodically through coordination and direct monitoring of the Regional Apparatus implementing policies, activities for the Protection of Women and Children.

(5) Monitoring stages as referred to in paragraph (1) include:

- a. planning
- b. policy implementation;
- c. program implementation; and
- d. Protection of Women and Children activities every year.

#### Article28

(1) Evaluation of the implementation of policies, programs and activities for the Protection of Women and Children shall be conducted at the end of each Fiscal Year or when necessary as needed.

(2) The results of the evaluation of the implementation of policies, programs and activities for the Protection of Women and Children as referred to in paragraph (1) shall be used as input for the formulation of policies, programs and activities for the following year.

(3) The evaluation as referred to in paragraph (1) shall be carried out in accordance with the provisions of laws and regulations.

GUIDANCE AND SUPERVISION

- a. provision of implementation guidelines;
- b. counseling;
- c. supervision;
- d. monitoring; and

- a. individuals;
- b. community institutions;

- e. religious institutions;

  - 1. taking other actions according to law that can be accounted for.
    - (4) Investigators as referred to in paragraph (1) shall notify the commencement of the investigation and submit the results of their investigation to the Public Prosecutor through the Investigator Official of the Police of the Republic of Indonesia, in accordance with the provisions stipulated in the Criminal Procedure Code.

CONCERNING

THE IMPLEMENTATION OF PROTECTION OF WOMEN AND CHILDREN

I. GENERAL

Violence against women and children constitutes a violation of human rights, therefore their dignity and honor must be protected and their right to life guaranteed in accordance with their nature and disposition without discrimination. The fate of women and children who are victims of violence must be given attention by the regional government. The high number of cases of violence against women and children is often caused by factors that develop in society, such as low economic levels, education, and environments located in industrial sectors. Therefore, victims of such violence need to receive protection in accordance with the principles of justice, truth, legal certainty, equality and upholding human rights.

For the empowerment of victims of violence, the regency government establishes an Integrated Service Center (PPT), which is a generic term that can be used by various institutions that provide services for victims of violence such as the Integrated Service Center for Women and Children Empowerment (P2TP2A), Safe House, Social Protection House for Children (RPSA), Trauma Center Protection House (RPTC), Social Protection House for Women (RPSW), Shelter House, etc.

The Regency Government has the obligation and responsibility to implement protection efforts for women and children through implementing protection policies for women and children applied by the government, establishing policies, programs, and protection activities for women and children, establishing PPT, ensuring the implementation of service facilities for victims, seeking effectiveness and efficiency for the victim recovery process, and seeking the creation of cooperation and coordination in the protection efforts for women and children.

#### Article 18

Point a

What is meant by "physical violence" is an act that results in pain, injury, wounds or defects to a person's body, miscarriage, fainting and/or causing death.

Point b

What is meant by "psychological violence" is an act that results in fear, loss of self-confidence, loss of ability to act, feelings of helplessness and/or severe psychological suffering to a person.

Point c

What is meant by "sexual violence" is an act in the form of sexual harassment, coercion of sexual relations, coercion of sexual relations in an unnatural or unwanted manner and/or coercion of sexual relations with others for commercial purposes and/or certain purposes.

Point d

What is meant by "neglect" is an act that results in the failure to meet the child's needs properly, whether physical, mental, spiritual or social, committed by parents, guardians, or any other party responsible for their care; an act of deliberately neglecting to maintain, care for, or take care of the child as appropriate, committed by parents, guardians, or other parties responsible for their care; an act of neglecting a person within the scope of their household, whereas according to the law applicable to them or due to agreement or contract they are obliged to provide living, care, or maintenance to that person; and/or an act that results in economic dependence by restricting and/or prohibiting proper work inside or outside the home so that the victim is under the control of that person.

Point e

What is meant by "exploitation" is an act of economic or sexual exploitation with the intention of benefiting oneself or others; an act with or without the victim's consent including prostitution, forced labor or services, slavery or similar practices, oppression, extortion, exploitation of physical, sexual, reproductive organs, or unlawfully removing or transplanting organs and/or body tissues or exploiting the labor or abilities of a person by other parties to obtain material or immaterial benefits; and/or any form of exploitation of sexual organs or other body organs of the victim to obtain benefits, including but not limited to all prostitution or indecency activities.

Point f

What is meant by "other violence" is threat of violence including: any unlawful act in the form of speech, writing, images, symbols, or body movements, whether with or without using means that create fear or restrict a person's fundamental freedom; and coercion, including: a situation where a victim/person is ordered to do something in such a way that the person does something contrary to their own will.

Point c

What is meant by "empathy" is an act of appreciating, respecting, loving, being friendly, and making happy that aims to please and calm the victim's heart.

Point d

What is meant by "non-discrimination" is handling of victims provided to all victims without distinguishing ethnicity, religion, race, class, gender, ethnicity, culture and language, legal status and physical or mental condition.

Point e

What is meant by "guarantee of confidentiality" is an effort to guarantee certainty for victims that their identity, medical treatment and legal handling will not be disclosed. Point f

What is meant by "easily accessible" is the provision of services and assistance for all people regardless of their social status, so that the services are affordable for those who are unable or relatively sufficient for those who are able.

Point g

What is meant by "not charged" is that the activities of providing services and assistance carried out by PPT are not charged to the victim.

#### Article 22

Letter a What is meant by "complaint, consultation, and counseling services" includes initial identification or recording of victims; case identification; and approval for action to be taken.

Letter b What is meant by "assistance services" includes accompanying victims during the examination and health recovery process; accompanying victims during the examination process at the police, prosecutor's office and court; monitoring the interests and rights of victims in the examination process at the police, prosecutor's office and court; maintaining the privacy and confidentiality of victims from all parties who are not concerned, including coverage by mass media; providing a sense of security to victims; and providing continuous treatment up to the rehabilitation stage.

Letter c What is meant by "medical rehabilitation services" includes first aid to victims; treatment and recovery of physical injuries aimed at restoring the physical condition of victims carried out by medical and paramedical personnel; and referral to health services. letter d What is meant by "social rehabilitation services" is services provided in order to restore the traumatic condition of victims. The form of service is carried out through social support through spiritual guidance to victims; and psychological recovery of victims. Letter e

What is meant by "legal services" is to assist victims in undergoing the judicial process by providing legal consultation that includes information regarding victims' rights and the judicial process; accompanying victims at the investigation, prosecution, and examination levels in court hearings and helping victims to fully describe the violence they have experienced; and coordinating with fellow law enforcers, accompanying volunteers, and social workers so that the judicial process runs as it should.

Letter f What is meant by "social reintegration services" is aimed at returning victims to their families and social environment. Social reintegration services are carried out by the Regency Government by coordinating with relevant agencies and institutions both government and non-government; and village community institutions and/or local community leaders.
