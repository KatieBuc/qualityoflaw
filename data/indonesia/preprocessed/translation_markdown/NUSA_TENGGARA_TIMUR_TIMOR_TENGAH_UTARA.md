REGIONAL REGULATION OF NORTH CENTRAL TIMOR REGENCY NUMBER 3 OF 2020

CONCERNING

IMPLEMENTATION OF WOMEN'S EMPOWERMENT

BY THE GRACE OF ALMIGHTY GOD

THE REGENT OF NORTH CENTRAL TIMOR,

Considering : a. that every person has equal worth and dignity to live in society, nationhood and statehood and has the right to obtain protection of human rights and fundamental human freedoms without discrimination as an effort to realize gender equality and justice in national and regional development;

b. that in order to realize gender equality and justice in development in North Central Timor Regency, it is necessary to increase the effectiveness and optimization of the implementation of the sub-affairs of women's empowerment as an inseparable part of the functional activities of governmental and non-governmental institutions in order to improve the position, role and quality of women in development in North

Central Timor Regency;

c. that to ensure legal certainty and to fill the legal vacuum in order to realize gender equality and justice in development in North Central Timor

Regency, it is necessary to have regulations in a

Regional Regulation concerning the implementation of women's empowerment;

d. that based on the considerations as referred to in letter a, letter b, and letter c, it is necessary to establish a Regional Regulation concerning the

Implementation of Women's Empowerment;

# CHAPTER I

GENERAL PROVISIONS

#### Article 1

In this Regional Regulation, the following terms mean:

- 1. Region is the North Central Timor Regency.
- 2. Regional Government is the Government of North Central Timor Regency.
- 3. Regent is the Regent of North Central Timor.
- 4. Regional House of Representatives, hereinafter abbreviated as DPRD, is the Regional House of Representatives of North Central Timor Regency.
- 5. Regional Secretary is the Regional Secretary of North Central Timor Regency.
- 6. Agency is the Regional Apparatus that administers governmental affairs in the field of women's empowerment and Child Protection.
- 7. Head of Agency is the Head of Regional Apparatus that administers governmental affairs in the field of women's empowerment.
- 8. Regional Apparatus, hereinafter abbreviated as PD, is the Regional Apparatus in North Central Timor Regency. 9. Head of Regional Apparatus is the Head of Regional Apparatus in North Central Timor Regency.
- 10. Board is the Regional Apparatus that administers governmental affairs in the field of development planning and regional research.

11.Head of Board is the Regional Apparatus that administers governmental affairs in the field of planning. 12.Women's Empowerment is every effort to improve physical, mental spiritual, social, knowledge, and skills capabilities so that women are ready to be utilized in accordance with their respective capabilities.

13.Gender Mainstreaming, hereinafter abbreviated as PUG, is a strategy built to integrate gender into an integral dimension of planning, implementation, monitoring and evaluation of regional development policies and programs.

14.Gender is a concept that refers to the roles and responsibilities of men and women that occur as a result of social construction and can change due to social and cultural circumstances of society.

15.Gender Equality is the equality of conditions for men and women to obtain opportunities and rights as human beings so they are able to play a role and participate in the development process.

16.Gender Justice is a process to be fair to men and women. 17.Gender Mainstreaming Working Group, hereinafter referred to as Pokja PUG, is a consultation forum for implementers and mobilizers of gender mainstreaming from various agencies/institutions in the region.

18.Gender Mainstreaming Focal Point, hereinafter referred to as Focal Point PUG, is a PD apparatus that has the ability to carry out gender mainstreaming in their respective work units.

19.Regional Medium-Term Development Plan, hereinafter abbreviated as RPJMD, is the elaboration of the vision, mission and programs of the Regent whose preparation is guided by the Regional Long-Term Development Plan, which contains regional development strategies, general regional policies, 3 regional programs and a macroeconomic framework that includes an overview of the economy as a whole including the direction of fiscal policy in a work plan in the form of a regulatory framework and funding framework that is indicative.

20.Gender Analysis is a process built systematically to identify and understand the division of labor or roles of men and women, access and control over development resources, participation in development and the benefits they enjoy.

21.Gender Responsive Budget is a performance-based budget allocation for certain programs or activities whose input, output, and outcome indicators can guarantee the fulfillment of the aspirations and needs of women and men fairly.

22.Communication, Information and Education, hereinafter abbreviated as KIE, is the delivery of messages in the form of information, ideas or facts related to gender mainstreaming that need to be known by the public directly or indirectly through communication channels to message recipients to obtain effects, as a process of behavioral change towards gender responsiveness.

23.Village/Sub-district Development Planning Meeting, hereinafter referred to as Musrembangkel, is an annual village/sub-district development planning meeting forum that involves development actors in the Village/Sub-district.

24.District Development Planning Meeting, hereinafter referred to as Musrembangcam, is a meeting forum among development actors to discuss and agree on priority program activity handling steps listed in the list of proposed village development activity planning that are integrated with the development priorities of the North Central Timor Regency in the district area coordinated by the Regional Development Planning Board

and implemented by the District Head.

25.Development planning meeting for the preparation of the Regional Medium-Term Development Plan, hereinafter referred to as Musrembang RPJMD, is a meeting forum among stakeholders to discuss, sharpen and agree on the draft Regional Medium-Term Development Plan.

26.Strategic Plan, hereinafter referred to as Renstra, is a five-year planning document that contains the vision, mission, objectives, strategies, policies, programs and development activities in accordance with the duties and functions of the organization as an elaboration of the RPJMD.

27.Work Plan, hereinafter referred to as Renja, is a one-year planning document that contains development policies, programs, and activities both planned directly by the Regional Apparatus and pursued by encouraging community participation as an elaboration of the Renstra.

28.Regional Government Work Plan, hereinafter abbreviated as RKPD, is a one-year planning document that contains development policies, programs, and activities both planned directly by the Regional Government and pursued by encouraging community participation as an elaboration of the Renstra.

29.Regional Action Plan, hereinafter abbreviated as RAD, is a document of activity planning prepared by a technical team to be implemented in a certain period/time.

30.Gender Analysis Pathway, hereinafter abbreviated as GAP, is a gender analysis tool that can be used to assist planners in gender mainstreaming in the planning of development policies/programs/activities with the aim of identifying gender gaps and gender issues as well as simultaneously preparing development policy/program/activity plans aimed at reducing or eliminating such gender gaps.

31.Technical Team is a Team formed by Pokja PUG with the task of conducting gender-perspective budget analysis and preparing the PUG RAD.

CHAPTER II

SCOPE

Article 2

The scope of Women's Empowerment administration includes:

- a. quality of life of women;
- b. protection of women;
- c. quality of family; and
- d. gender data system.

CHAPTER III

QUALITY OF LIFE OF WOMEN

Part One

General

Article 3

Quality of Life of Women includes:

- a. institutionalization of PUG;
- b. Women's Empowerment in the political, legal, social and economic fields in community organizations; and
- c. strengthening and developing institutions providing women's empowerment services.

Part Two

Institutionalization of PUG

Paragraph

General

Article 4

Institutionalization of PUG includes:

- a. appointment of PUG coordinator;
- b. Pokja PUG;
- c. Focal Point PUG;
- d. Budgeting;
- e. gender-based development planning;
- f. monitoring and evaluation;
- g. reporting; and
- h. supervision.

Paragraph 2 PUG Coordinator

Article 5

(1) The Regent has the authority to appoint the Head of PD that administers governmental affairs in the field of Women's Empowerment as the coordinator of PUG administration.

(2) The appointment as referred to in paragraph (1) shall be by Regent's Decree.

Paragraph 3

Pokja PUG

Article 6

(1) The Regent has the authority to establish Pokja PUG.

(2) The establishment of Pokja PUG as referred to in paragraph (1) shall be stipulated by Regent's Decree.

(3) The Regent's Decree as referred to in paragraph (2) shall be valid for 5 (five) years.

(4) The management of Pokja PUG consists of:

- a. chair by the head of board;
- b. secretariat by the head of agency;
- c. members include:
  - 1. head of PD;
  - 2. technical team consisting of:
    - a) apparatus who have attended gender-perspective budget analysis training or education of at most 5 (five) persons.
    - b) representatives of university elements of at most 5 (five) persons;
    - c) representatives of the press of at most 5 (five) persons; and
    - d) representatives of non-governmental organizations that have an orientation scope in the field of gender of at most 5 (five) persons.

#### Article 7

The duties of Pokja PUG include:

- a. promoting and facilitating dialogue among work units within the scope of regional government;
- b. developing work networks in accordance with the main duties, functions and authorities given by the leadership in efforts to realize gender equality and justice;
- c. assigning technical teams to prepare RAD PUG in order to realize gender equality and justice that is adjusted to the RPJMD periodization;
- d. elaborating and following up on policies and implementation programs to be gender responsive that are implicit and explicit in the RPJMD;
- e. coordinating the establishment of Focal Point PUG in every PD or work unit;
- f. promoting PUG in each PD;
- g. conducting capacity strengthening for Focal Point PUG in preparing gender responsive budgets by the technical team; h. overseeing the preparation of gender responsive planning and budgets in their respective PDs;
- i. including gender indicators in the guidelines for preparing the APBD; j. ensuring gender responsive budgets are a consideration in the discussion and determination of the APBD together with DPRD; k. holding coordination meetings every 6 (six) months to ensure the implementation of PUG in the Region;
- l. preparing and submitting reports on the implementation of PUG to the regent every 6 (six) months; and
- m. holding coordination meetings before the preparation of RKPD.

### Paragraph 4

Focal Point PUG

#### Article 8

(1) The Regional Secretary has the authority to establish Focal Point PUG within the Regional Secretariat.

(2) The establishment of Focal Point PUG as referred to in paragraph (1) shall be determined by Decree of the Regional Secretary.

(3) The management of Focal Point PUG as referred to in paragraph (1) consists of:

- a. person in charge by the regional secretary;
- b. coordinator by echelon IIB official in charge of government affairs in the field of law;
- c. vice coordinator by echelon IIIA official of each division in the regional secretariat except for echelon IIB official as referred to in letter b; and
- d. members by representatives of officials and/or staff from all divisions in the regional secretariat.

#### Article 9

(1) The Head of PD is obliged to establish Focal Point PUG PD.

(2) The establishment of Focal Point PUG as referred to in paragraph (1) shall be by Decree of the Head of PD.

(3) The management of Focal Point PUG as referred to in paragraph (1) consists of:

- a. person in charge by the head of PD;
- b. coordinator by echelon III A official in charge of planning affairs at the PD;
- c. Vice Coordinator by Echelon IIIB Official who has competence in PUG; and
- d. Members by representatives of Officials or staff from divisions and secretariat at the PD.

#### Article 10

(1) The Hospital Director has the authority to establish Focal Point PUG Hospital.

(2) The establishment of Focal Point PUG as referred to in paragraph (1) shall be determined by decree of the Hospital Director. (3) The management of Focal Point PUG as referred to in paragraph (1) consists of:

- a. person in charge by the hospital director;
- b. coordinator by echelon IIIB official in charge of hospital planning;
- c. vice coordinator by echelon IVA official who has competence in PUG; and
- d. members by representatives of officials or staff in divisions or sections within the hospital.

#### Article 11

(1) The Head of Community Health Center has the authority to establish Focal Point PUG Puskesmas.

(2) The establishment of Focal Point PUG as referred to in paragraph (1) shall be determined by Decree of the Head of Community Health Center.

(3) The management of Focal Point PUG puskesmas consists of:8

a. person in charge by the Head of Puskesmas;

b. coordinator by echelon IV B official in charge of Puskesmas planning; and

c. members consisting of Puskesmas staff.

#### Article 12

The duties of Focal Point PUG include:

- a. assisting policy makers of work units within the scope of duties, main duties and functions of their agencies to take planned steps fully if there are gender gaps;
- b. encouraging and assisting work units to evaluate and improve mandates, policies, programs, projects, activities and budgets to be more gender responsive;
- c. facilitating the implementation of gender sensitivity training, gender analysis training and developing gender work networks with agencies or institutions or organizations and other work units, both government and non-government;
- d. seeking the implementation of gender analysis as one of the stages in every development process starting from the planning, implementation, monitoring and evaluation stages;
- e. elaborating and following up on policies and development implementation programs to be gender responsive that are implicit in Renstra PD, Renja PD and RKPD;
- f. participating in various activities organized by Pokja PUG and/or national working group PUG; and g. preparing activity reports periodically and reporting them to the regent through the chairperson of pokja PUG.

### Paragraph 5

Gender-based Regional Development Planning Article 13

(1) The Regional Government is responsible for preparing planning of policies, programs and development activities with Gender perspective.

(2) The responsibility as referred to in paragraph (1) shall be coordinated by the PD that administers government affairs in the field of development planning, research and Regional development.

(3) The planning as referred to in paragraph (1) includes stages:

- a. Musrenbangdus;
- b. Musrembangdes/kel;
- c. Musrembangcam;
- d. Musrenbang at regional level;
- e. preparation of RPJMD;
- f. preparation of Renstra PD;
- g. preparation of Renja PD; and
- h. RKPD;
- i. preparation of RAPBD;
  - (4) The planning as referred to in paragraph (1) shall be conducted through Gender analysis using the Gender Analysis Pathway method or other analytical methods.
  - (5) The implementation of Gender analysis of RPJMD and Renstra PD may cooperate with Higher Education institutions or other parties who have capability in their field.

#### Article 14

(1) The Agency is responsible for preparing guidelines for gender-based development planning.

(2) The guidelines as referred to in paragraph (1) must be used in facilitating:

- a. Musrenbangdus;
- b. Musrembangdes/kel;
- c. Musrembangcam;
- d. Musrenbang at regional level;
- e. preparation of RPJMD;
- f. preparation of Renstra PD; and
- g. preparation of Renja PD, that is gender responsive.
  - (3) The preparation of guidelines as referred to in paragraph (1) may cooperate with the Technical Team of Pokja PUG.

#### Article 15

(1) The Gender Analysis Pathway method as referred to in Article 13 paragraph (4) shall be used as a way to make planning in every PD.

(2) The method as referred to in paragraph (1) includes: a. stage I analysis of gender responsive policies;

b. stage II formulation of gender responsive policies; c. stage III gender responsive action plan;

d. stage IV implementation of activities that have been prepared; and e. stage V monitoring and evaluation of each stage and step taken.

### Paragraph 6

Budgeting

#### Article 16

(1) The Regional Government is obliged to allocate a budget for the implementation of PUG in the Region.

(2) The implementation budget as referred to in paragraph (1) is sourced from: a.APBD; and b.assistance from other parties that is non-binding, as long as it is in accordance with the provisions of applicable laws and regulations.

(3) The budget as referred to in paragraph (1) and paragraph (2), shall be used to finance the activities of Pokja PUG and Focal Point PUG PD in integrating Gender at the level of planning, preparation, implementation, monitoring and evaluation of policies and development in the Region.

### Paragraph 7

Monitoring and Evaluation

#### Article 17

(1) The Regent has the authority to conduct monitoring and evaluation of the implementation of PUG.

(2) The Regent may delegate the implementation of monitoring and evaluation to Pokja PUG.

(3) Monitoring and evaluation shall be carried out at each PD and/or other institutions that implement PUG efforts.

(4) Monitoring and evaluation shall be carried out at least every 6 (six) months.

#### Article 18

Monitoring of PUG implementation is carried out through:

- a. monitoring the preparation of gender-responsive development planning documents at both PD and regional levels; b. monitoring RAD PUG; and
- c. verification of RAD PUG implementation Reports.

#### Article 19

Evaluation of PUG implementation is carried out through:

- a. evaluation of achievements and challenges in RAD PUG implementation; b. preparation of RAD PUG for the next period;
- c. evaluation of achievements and challenges in PUG implementation every 6 (six) months; and
- d. preparation of PUG implementation plan for the next 6 (six) months.

### Paragraph 8

PUG Reporting

#### Article 20

(1) Pokja PUG is responsible for preparing PUG Implementation Reports every 6 (six) months and five-yearly RAD PUG Implementation Reports.

(2) The reporting framework as referred to in paragraph (1) is guided by the reporting framework as referred to in the legislation regarding General Guidelines for PUG Implementation in Regions.

(3) The Pokja PUG Report as referred to in paragraph (1) shall be reported to the Regent.

(4) The Regent is responsible for examining and approving the Pokja PUG Report as referred to in paragraph (1).

(5) The Regent is responsible for sending the pokja PUG report as referred to in paragraph (1) to the Minister who administers Internal Government affairs.

### Paragraph 9

Supervision

#### Article 21

(1) The Regent is responsible for supervising the implementation of PUG.

(2) Supervision as referred to in paragraph (1) shall be in the form of: a. Gender-responsive planning and budgeting carried out by the regional government budget team;

b. implementation of coordination between Pokja PUG and Gender Focal Point; and

c. development of RAD PUG achievements every year. (3) The responsibility as referred to in paragraph (1) may be operationally and technically carried out by the Chairperson of Pokja PUG and the Head of the Pokja PUG Secretariat.

(4) The implementation of responsibilities as referred to in paragraph (3) shall be reported to the Regent.

## Part Three

Women's Empowerment in the Fields of Politics, Law, Social Culture and Economy in Community Organizations

### Paragraph 1

General

#### Article 22

Women's Empowerment efforts in the fields of politics, law, social culture and economy in community organizations include the stages:

- a. planning;
- b. implementation; and
- c. reporting and evaluation.

### Paragraph 2

Planning

#### Article 23

(1) The Agency is responsible for facilitating the planning of Women's Empowerment in the fields of politics, law, social culture and economy in community organizations.

(2) Planning as referred to in paragraph (1) shall be carried out in an integrated manner in development planning deliberations based on the gender analysis pathway instrument.

### Paragraph 3

Implementation

#### Article 24

Implementation of Women's Empowerment in community organizations shall be carried out by PD in accordance with the main tasks and functions undertaken in accordance with regional financial capacity.

#### Article 25

(1) PD that administers governmental affairs in the field of national unity and politics is responsible for facilitating women's empowerment in the field of politics.

(2) Empowerment as referred to in paragraph (1) includes: a. conducting education and training for women in order to improve competence, especially in the field of politics; and

b. encouraging women's representation in every management of community organizations.

#### Article 26

(1) PD that administers governmental affairs in the field of law is responsible for facilitating Women's Empowerment in the field of law.

(2) The implementation of Women's Empowerment in the field of law includes:

- a. increasing awareness and knowledge in the field of law through communication, information and education services; and b. facilitation of access and legal consultation services.

#### Article 27

(1) PD that administers governmental affairs in the social field is responsible for facilitating Women's Empowerment in the socio-cultural field.

(2) Women's Empowerment in the socio-cultural field through: a. improving knowledge, attitudes and skills to encourage the fulfillment of tiered education in accordance with potential to improve social status;

b. increasing awareness, willingness and ability to overcome health problems through quality promotive, preventive, curative, and rehabilitative efforts, especially in the field of reproductive health;

c. increasing awareness and knowledge about independent, healthy and prosperous family planning including access to consultation services and marriage registration; and

d. facilitation and efforts to preserve customs and develop science, technology, arts and culture for the advancement of women.

#### Article 28

(1) PD that administers governmental affairs in the field of cooperatives, small and medium enterprises and trade industry is responsible for facilitating Women's Empowerment in the economic field.

(2) The implementation of Women's Empowerment in the economic field is carried out through:

- a. provision of skills and job training; b.facilitation of the formation of productive economic business groups; c. facilitation of strengthening and development of productive economic business groups; d.facilitation and capital assistance; and
- e. facilitation of marketing network development.

### Paragraph 4

Reporting and Evaluation

#### Article 29

(1) Each PD is responsible for preparing a report on the implementation of Women's Empowerment activities that have been carried out to the Regent through the Office.

(2) The Office, after receiving the report submitted by PD, shall evaluate the implementation of activities. (3) The results of the evaluation of activity implementation as referred to in paragraph (2) shall be reported to the Regent.

(4) The evaluation results as referred to in paragraph (3) shall be used for budgeting planning for the following year.

# CHAPTER IV

WOMEN'S PROTECTION

## Part One

General

#### Article 30

Women's Protection includes:

- a. strengthening and development of women's protection service provider institutions;
- b. prevention of violence against women; and c. strengthening and development of protection service provider institutions for women victims of violence;

## Part Two

Strengthening and Development of Women's Protection Service Provider Institutions

### Paragraph 1

Regional Technical Implementation Unit for Women and Children Protection

#### Article 31

(1) PD that administers governmental affairs in the field of institutions and organizations is responsible for facilitating the formation of a regional technical implementation unit for women and children protection.

(2) The establishment of UPTD P2TP2A as referred to in paragraph (1) shall be regulated by Regent Regulation.

(3) The establishment of UPTD P2TP2A as referred to in paragraph (2) shall be no later than 2 (two) years after this Regional Regulation is enacted.

### Paragraph 2

Regional Action Plan

#### Article 32

(1) The preparation of RAD as referred to in Article 7 letter c shall be carried out through the mechanism:

- a. establishment of a drafting team;
- b. conducting meetings to form working groups based on indicators;
- c. each Pokja shall identify objectives, targets and indicators;
- d. establishing priority programs and activities related to the achievement of each target; and
- e. determining budget allocation for each activity program. (2) RAD shall contain at minimum:
- a. introduction;
- b. policy direction and achievement strategy;
- c. monitoring and evaluation;
- d. closing; and
- e. appendix containing the RAD implementation matrix. (3) The preparation of RAD shall be carried out by taking into account: a. RPJMN;
- b. RPJMD; and
- c. other related documents.
  - (4) Provisions regarding the implementation of RAD shall be further regulated by Regent Regulation.

### Paragraph 3

Monitoring and Evaluation

#### Article 33

(1) The Regent conducts monitoring of the implementation of policies, programs, and activities for the protection of women.

(2) The monitoring as referred to in paragraph (1) is intended to ascertain the progress and obstacles in the implementation of policies, programs, and activities for the Protection of women in the Region.

(3) The monitoring as referred to in paragraph (2 is conducted periodically toward PD through the Agency.

(4) Pemantuan is conducted starting from planning up to the implementation of policies, programs and activities for the Protection of women for the current year.

#### Article 34

(1) Evaluation of the implementation of policies, programs, and activities for the Protection of Women is conducted at the end of every fiscal year.

(2) The results of the evaluation of the implementation of policies, programs, and activities for the Protection of women are used as input material for the formulation of policies, programs, and activities for the Protection of Women for the following year.

(3) The evaluation as referred to in paragraph (1) is carried out in accordance with the provisions of laws and regulations.

## Part Three

Prevention of Violence Against Women

#### Article 35

(1) The Agency is responsible for implementing efforts to prevent the occurrence of Violence against Women. (2) The prevention as referred to in paragraph (1) is carried out in the form of:

- a. conducting counseling and socialization regarding matters relating to violence against women; b. conducting socialization of women's rights; c. promoting improved education for women; d. providing counseling to immediately report to local authorities in case of occurrence of Violence against women;
- e. empowering social community organizations and women's organizations to enhance the knowledge and insights of women; and
- f. establishing a network in order to prevent violence against women.

## Part Four

Strengthening and Development of Service Provider Institutions for the Protection of Women Victims of Violence

#### Article 36

(1) The Agency may provide assistance to Service Institutions located in the region in the form of:

- a. strengthening of Service Institutions; and
- b. development of Service Institutions
  - (2) Strengthening to service institutions as referred to in paragraph (1) letter a is carried out through:
- a. enhancement of the competence of service institution members; and/or b. facilitating paralegal training periodically
  - (3) Development of Service Institutions as referred to in paragraph (1) letter b is carried out through:
- a. providing additional facilities and infrastructure;
- b. providing information technology-based service approaches; and/or
- c. recruiting village cadres as paralegal assistants.

# CHAPTER V

FAMILY QUALITY

## Part One

General

#### Article 37

Family Quality includes:

- a. improvement of family quality in realizing gender equality;and
- b. strengthening and development of service provider institutions for family quality improvement in realizing gender equality.

## Part Two

Improvement of Family Quality In Realizing

Gender Equality

#### Article 38

(1) The Agency is responsible for facilitating family quality improvement activities in the Region.

(2) Family quality improvement as referred to in paragraph (1) is carried out through:

- a. facilitating the establishment of service provider institutions; b. in coordination with PD and/or related institutions which have the main duties and functions to carry out activities:

1.improvement of entrepreneurship ability for women; or

2.development of job training for women.

## Part Three

Strengthening And Development of Service Provider Institutions For Family Quality Improvement In Realizing Gender Equality

#### Article 39

(1) Facilitation of the establishment of service provider institutions as referred to in Article 36 paragraph (2) letter a is carried out by way of establishing a family learning center.

(2) The stages of establishing a family learning center include: a. preparing regulations;

b. preparing human resources;

c. preparing facilities and infrastructure; and

d. establishment of the family learning center.

(3) The family learning center functions:

- a. as a service institution in prevention and counseling; b. as a place of referral liaison as a solution for child and family problems;
- c. strengthening family capacity in caring for and protecting children;
- d. as a place of family learning through education for parents; and
- e. as a holistic integrative family one-stop service based on child rights.

# CHAPTER VI

GENDER DATA SYSTEM

General

#### Article 40

The Gender Data System includes:

- a. data collection;
- b. data processing;and
- c. analysis and presentation of gender data.

Bagain Two

Data Collection

#### Article 41

(1) PD which carries out the duties of PUG focal point is responsible for reporting Gender data and PUG activities that are carried out to the Regent through the Agency.

(2) The reporting of Gender data and PUG activities as referred to in paragraph (1) is conducted every 6 ( s i x ) m o n t h s .

Data Processing

#### Article 42

(1) The Agency after receiving the report of Gender data and PUG activities from each Gender focal point existing in the region conducts data processing.

Analysis And Presentation of Gender Data

#### Article 43

(3) The adequate analysis as referred to in paragraph (2) is presented as a regional Gender data manually and/or electronically.

# CHAPTER VII

PROVISIONS ON SANCTIONS

- a. verbal warning;
- b. written warning; or
- c. statement of dissatisfaction.

# CHAPTER VIII

IMPLEMENTATION OF WOMEN'S EMPOWERMENT

I. GENERAL

The presence of Law Number 23 of 2014 concerning Regional Government as amended several times, most recently by Law Number 9 of 2015 concerning the Second Amendment to Law Number 23 of 2014 concerning Regional Government and Law Number 33 of 2004 concerning Financial Balance Between Central Government and Regional Government constitutes an entry point for Women's Empowerment in regional development. Women's empowerment is an appropriate development strategy to realize gender equality and justice through gender-sensitive and responsive development policies and programs in the future, particularly in North Central Timor Regency.

In North Central Timor Regency there are several conditions that need to be intervened with the establishment of a Regional Regulation on Women's Empowerment. This occurs because there is much discrimination against women in developing themselves and opportunities for the fulfillment of their rights. Women are only considered as second-class citizens. Their task is to take care of the household and they do not need to work. This needs to be opposed, this stigma becomes a problem that must be resolved with empowerment programs.

The above paradigm is reflected in data on the presence of women working in Government, data shows that women are not in strategic positions to participate in making decisions or policies, especially in Government affairs through data on Civil Servants based on gender available in the field.

Based on these considerations, it is time for North Central Timor Regency to establish a Regional Regulation on Women's Empowerment in North Central Timor Regency. This Regional Regulation will regulate the implementation of gender mainstreaming clearly, firmly, in a balanced and comprehensive manner to realize gender equality and justice as well as simultaneously provide understanding about the importance of women's empowerment as a gender analysis strategy in planning, formulating, implementing, monitoring and evaluating development policies and programs.
