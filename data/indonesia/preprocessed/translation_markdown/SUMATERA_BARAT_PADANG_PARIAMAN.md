# CHAPTER I

GENERAL PROVISIONS

#### Article 1

In this Regional Regulation, what is meant by:

- 1. Region is Padang Pariaman Regency.
- 2. Regional Government is the Government of Padang Pariaman Regency. 3. Regent is the Regent of Padang Pariaman Regency.
- 4. Regional Work Unit, hereinafter abbreviated as SKPD, is the Regional Work Unit within the Government of Padang Pariaman Regency.
- 5. Women's Empowerment is an effort to protect, strengthen human rights, improve quality of life, and increase women's participation in development.
- 6. Child Protection is all activities to guarantee and protect Children and their rights so that they can live, grow, develop, and participate optimally in accordance with human dignity, as well as receive Protection from Violence and discrimination.
- 7. Child is a person who has not reached the age of 18 (eighteen) years, including a Child who is still in the womb and is not yet married. 8. Prevention is a direct effort undertaken by the Regional Government, community and parents and/or family to prevent violations of the rights of women and Children.
- 9. Violence is any act that results in suffering or hardship whether physical, sexual, psychological and/or domestic neglect including threats to commit acts, coercion, or deprivation of a person's freedom unlawfully.
- 10. Children's Forum is a platform for Children's participation established with the aim of facilitating Children's involvement in decision-making processes and enjoying changes concerning their lives either directly or indirectly, which is carried out with the approval and willingness of all Children based on awareness and understanding, in accordance with their age and level of maturity of thinking.
- 11. Child-Friendly Regency, hereinafter abbreviated as KLA, is a Regency that has a Child rights-based development system through the integration of government, community and business commitments and resources that are planned comprehensively and sustainably in policies, programs and activities to ensure the fulfillment of Children's rights.
- 12. Gender Mainstreaming is a strategy built to integrate gender into an integral dimension of planning, formulation, implementation, monitoring and evaluation of national development program policies.
- 13. Community is individuals, families, groups and social organizations and/or community organizations.
- 14. Women Victims of Violence are women who experience actions resulting in suffering or hardship physically, sexually or psychologically, including threats of certain actions, coercion or deprivation of freedom arbitrarily, whether occurring in the public sphere or in private life.
- 15. Child Victims of Violence are Children who experience actions resulting in suffering or hardship physically, mentally, sexually, psychologically or neglect that threatens life, body and degrades human dignity.
- 16. Human trafficking is the act of recruiting, transporting, harboring, sending, transferring, or receiving a person by threats of Violence, use of Violence, kidnapping, confinement, forgery, fraud, abuse of power or vulnerable position, debt bondage or giving payments or benefits, so as to obtain consent from the person who holds control over that person, whether carried out within the State or between States, for the purpose of exploitation or resulting in the person being exploited.
- 17. Complaint handling is a series of actions taken by integrated service providers to follow up on reports of acts of Violence against women and Children, submitted by victims, families or the community.
- 18. Social rehabilitation is a service aimed at restoring and developing the capacity of a person experiencing social dysfunction so that they can perform their social functions normally.
- 19. Law enforcement is action by officials authorized by the state to implement laws and regulations. 20. Legal aid is legal services provided by legal assistants and advocates to carry out the process of assisting witnesses and/or victims of Violence against women and Children in a gender-sensitive manner. 21. Repatriation is an effort to return women and Child victims of Violence from abroad to the debarkation point/entry point, or from the receiving Region to the Region of origin.
- 22. Social reintegration is an effort to reunite victims with family, substitute family, or community that can provide Protection and fulfillment of needs for victims.
- 23. Safe house is a temporary shelter that aims to provide Protection and a sense of security to victims of Violence. 24. Shelter home is a forum for guidance and welfare services for street Children that carries out activities of social assistance/guidance, maintenance, care, education, and health, skills guidance in order to ensure that Children do not engage in activities on the streets so that they can grow and develop normally.
- 25. Spiritual Guidance is an effort to restore mental well-being through the strengthening of the religion adhered to by the victim.
- 26. Gender Mainstreaming is a strategy built to integrate gender into an integral dimension of planning, formulation, implementation, monitoring, and evaluation of development policies, programs, and activities in the Region.
- 27. Gender responsive is consistent and systematic attention to the differences between women and men in society accompanied by efforts to eliminate structural and cultural barriers in achieving gender equality.
- 28. Gender Sensitivity or Gender Awareness is the ability to identify power relation gaps between women and men within the family and within the community, the impact of gender-based division of labor on women and men, and that the experiences, problems, needs, interests and aspirations of women and men are different.
- 29. Public service provider is every institution of state organizer, corporation, independent institution established based on Law for public service activities, and other legal entities established solely for public service activities.
- 30. Community Organization is an organization founded and formed by the community voluntarily based on shared aspirations, will, needs, interests, activities and objectives to participate in development for the achievement of the objectives of the Unitary State of the Republic of Indonesia based on Pancasila.
- 31. Parent is biological father and/or mother, or stepfather and/or stepmother, or adoptive father and/or mother.
- 32. Family is the smallest unit in society consisting of husband and wife or husband, wife and their Child, or father and his Child, or mother and her Child, or blood relatives in direct line upwards or downwards up to the third degree.
- 33. Customs and Traditions are enduring patterns of behavior passed down from one generation to another as heritage, so that they are strongly integrated with community behavior patterns.

#### Article 2

The implementation of Women's Empowerment and Child Protection is based on the principles of:

- a. best interests of women and Children;
- b. gender justice and gender equality;
- c. local wisdom;
- d. law enforcement and legal certainty.
- e. non-discrimination;
- f. right to life, survival, and development; g. respect for the opinion of Children; and
- h. law enforcement and legal certainty.

#### Article 3

This Regional Regulation aims to:

- a. ensure the fulfillment of the rights of women and Children in the Region b. serve as a guideline for the Region in planning, policies and strategies for Women's Empowerment and Child Protection;
- c. protect and prevent women and Children from actions, decisions and policies that violate the rights of women and Children;
- d. enhance the role and value of local wisdom as well as the role of customs in Women's Empowerment and Child Protection;

#### Article 4

The scope of regulation of Women's Empowerment and Child Protection includes:

- a. improvement of women's quality of life;
- b. Protection of women;
- c. gender and Child data system;
- d. fulfillment of Children's rights;
- e. Child Protection;
- f. community participation; and
- g. guidance and supervision.

# CHAPTER II

IMPROVEMENT OF WOMEN'S QUALITY OF LIFE

## Part One

General

#### Article 5

(1) In order to improve the quality of life of women, the regional government is responsible for:

- a. respecting and guaranteeing the rights of women without discrimination in accordance with human dignity;
- b. implementing Gender Mainstreaming in the Region;
- c. establishing a Gender Mainstreaming working group in the Region;
- d. enhancing Women's Empowerment in roles, quality and capacity in the fields of;
  - 1. politics;
  - 2. law;
  - 3. social; and
  - 4. economy.
- e. strengthening and developing institutions providing Women's Empowerment services in the Region;
  - (2) The responsibility of the Regional Government in efforts to improve the quality of life of women as referred to in paragraph (1) shall be implemented by the SKPD in accordance with its duties and functions.

## Part Two

Implementation of Gender Mainstreaming in the Region

#### Article 6

(1) The Regent is responsible for the implementation of Gender Mainstreaming in the Region.

(2) The responsibility of the Regent as referred to in paragraph (1) may be delegated to the Vice Regent.

#### Article 7

In implementing Gender Mainstreaming in the Region as referred to in Article 5 paragraph (1) letter b, the Regent shall designate the SKPD responsible for Women's Empowerment duties as the coordinator for the implementation of Gender Mainstreaming.

## Part Three

Establishment of the Gender Mainstreaming Working Group in the Region

#### Article 8

(1) In an effort to accelerate the institutionalization of Gender Mainstreaming in all Regional SKPDs, a Gender Mainstreaming working group is established in the Region. (2) Members of the Gender Mainstreaming working group are all heads/leaders of SKPDs.

(3) The Regent shall appoint the head of Bappeda as the chairperson of the Regional Gender Mainstreaming working group and the head of the SKPD responsible for Women's Empowerment duties as the head of the secretariat of the Regional Gender Mainstreaming working group.

(4) The establishment of the Regional Gender Mainstreaming working group shall be determined by a decision of the Regent.

#### Article 9

The Regional Gender Mainstreaming Working Group as referred to in Article 14 has the duties to:

- a. promote and facilitate Gender Mainstreaming to each SKPD;
- b. conduct socialization and advocacy of Gender Mainstreaming to sub-district heads and wali nagari;
- c. prepare work programs every year;
- d. encourage the realization of gender-responsive planning and budgeting;
- e. prepare the work plan of the Gender Mainstreaming working group every year; f. be responsible to the Regent through the Vice Regent;
- g. formulate policy recommendations to the Regent;
- h. prepare the Regional Gender profile;
- i. conduct monitoring of the implementation of Gender Mainstreaming in each agency;
- j. establish a technical team to conduct analysis of the Regional budget; k. prepare the regional action plan for Regional Gender Mainstreaming; and l. encourage the implementation of the selection and designation of focal points in each SKPD.

#### Article 10

(1) The Technical Team as referred to in Article 9 letter j shall consist of officials who understand gender-responsive budget analysis.

(2) The Regional Gender Mainstreaming regional action plan as referred to in Article 9 letter k shall contain:

- a. Gender Mainstreaming in laws and regulations in the Region;
- b. Gender Mainstreaming in the development cycle in the Region; c. strengthening of Gender Mainstreaming institutions in the Region; and d. strengthening of community participation in the Region.

#### Article 11

(1) The Gender Mainstreaming Focal Point in each SKPD shall consist of officials and/or staff responsible for planning and/or program duties. (2) The Gender Mainstreaming Focal Point as referred to in paragraph (1) has the duties to:

- a. promote Gender Mainstreaming in the work unit; b. facilitate the preparation of work plans and budgeting of SKPDs that are gender-responsive;
- c. conduct training, socialization, advocacy of Gender Mainstreaming to all officials and staff within the SKPD;
- d. report the implementation of Gender Mainstreaming to the SKPD leadership; e. encourage the implementation of gender analysis of policies, programs, and activities in the work unit; and
- f. facilitate the preparation of gender data in each SKPD; (3) The Gender Mainstreaming Focal Point as referred to in paragraph (1) shall be selected and designated by the SKPD leadership.

## Part Four

Enhancement of Women's Roles, Quality, and Capacity

Political Field

#### Article 12

Efforts to enhance the role, quality and capacity of women in the political field as referred to in Article 5 paragraph (1) letter d number 1 include: a. conducting socialization to increase women's political participation in general elections;

b. implementing programs that encourage the level of women's involvement in political processes and public positions;

c. facilitating women's self-development in organizing, associating and assembling;

d. involving women in decision-making concerning women's interests;

## Part Five

Enhancement of Women's Roles, Quality, and Capacity

Legal Field

#### Article 13

Efforts to enhance the role, quality and capacity of women in the legal field as referred to in Article 5 paragraph (1) letter d number 2 include: a. conducting socialization to increase women's legal knowledge and awareness;

b. conducting mapping and reviewing gender-biased policies;

c. involving women's organizations in the process of formulating and discussing Regional legal products related to women's participation;

## Part Six

Enhancement of Women's Roles, Quality, and Capacity

Social Field

#### Article 14

Efforts to enhance the role, quality and capacity of women in the social field as referred to in Article 5 paragraph (1) letter d number 3 include: a. enhancing knowledge and skills to encourage the fulfillment of education in accordance with their potential;

b. enhancing and fulfilling the quality of health for women; c. increasing awareness and knowledge about independent, healthy and prosperous family planning including access to consultation services and marriage registration;

d. increasing women's awareness of the impact of early marriage; e. facilitating and pursuing the preservation of customs and the development of science, technology, arts and culture for women's advancement; f. conducting enhancement of women's awareness and knowledge about independent, healthy and prosperous family planning;

g. conducting programs to reduce maternal mortality rates; h. encouraging the establishment of women's organizations in the Region;

## Part Seven

Enhancement of Women's Roles, Quality, and Capacity

Economic Field

#### Article 15

Efforts to enhance the role, quality and capability of women in the economic field as referred to in Article 5 paragraph (1) letter d number 4 include: a. implementing programs, activities, training for improving the skills and expertise of women in order to realize the improvement of women's welfare;

b. facilitating the strengthening of business capital for women with weak economic conditions;

c. marketing products of women's business results who have weak economic conditions; d. training for developing women's capabilities through economic empowerment of pre-prosperous families;

e. comprehensive entrepreneurship training for women.

# CHAPTER III

PROTECTION OF WOMEN

## Part One

General

#### Article 16

(1) The Regional Government in efforts to Protect women is responsible for: a. establishing and implementing policies, programs and activities for the Protection of women;

b. making efforts to prevent acts of Violence against women including criminal acts of human trafficking;

c. increasing the effectiveness of services for women victims of acts of Violence;

d. increasing efforts for Protection of women workers; e. strengthening and developing institutions providing Protection services for women;

f. conducting cooperation with the Government, other Regional Governments, private parties, business world and/or NGOs in efforts to prevent acts of Violence against women;

g. involving the active role of families, ninik mamak, bundo kanduang, customary leaders, religious leaders, society, and institutions in the field of Protection of women;

(2) The responsibility of the Regional Government in efforts to Protect women as referred to in paragraph (1) is carried out by the SKPD in accordance with its duties and functions and may coordinate with related agencies.

## Part Two

Prevention of Acts of Violence against Women

#### Article 17

In order to prevent the occurrence of acts of Violence against women, the Regional Government:

- a. conducts socialization regarding the prevention of Violence against women by optimizing the role of customary, cultural, religious institutions and community organizations;
- b. builds networks and cooperation with law enforcement apparatus, government apparatus, universities and various non-governmental organizations that work and/or care for women and children victims of Violence;
- c. provides public facilities that are comfortable and safe for women;

## Part Three

Effectiveness of Services for Women Victims of Acts of Violence

### Paragraph 1

General

#### Article 18

(1) Protection of women victims of acts of Violence is carried out through integrated services.

(2) The provision of integrated services to women victims of acts of Violence as referred to in paragraph (1) uses the principles of:

- a. gender responsive;
- b. non-discrimination;
- c. equal and respectful relations;
- d. maintaining confidentiality;
- e. providing a sense of security and comfort;
- f. respecting individual differences;
- g. non-judgmental; h. respecting the choices and decisions of victims;
- i. sensitive to the background and condition of victims and the use of appropriate language understood by victims;
- j. fast and simple;
- k. empathy; and
- l. fulfillment of women's rights.

#### Article 19

Forms of integrated services for women victims of acts of Violence include: a. complaint handling;

b. health rehabilitation;

c. social rehabilitation;

d. law enforcement and legal aid; and

e. repatriation and social reintegration.

### Paragraph 2

Complaint Handling

#### Article 20

Integrated complaint handling services as referred to in Article 19 letter a are carried out by:

- a. hospitals;
- b. community health centers;
- c. women and children service units;
- d. trauma center Protection houses;
- e. women crisis centres;
- f. advisory board for marriage guidance and preservation;
- g. police;
- h. prosecutor's office;
- i. courts;
- j. ministry of religious affairs; and/or
- k. legal aid institutions;

#### Article 21

(1) Complaints of acts of Violence against women may be made by reporters consisting of:

- a. victims,
- b. family,
- c. community; and/or
- d. community organizations in the field of Protection of women and Children, such as: P2TP2A, Rumah Aman etc.)
  - (2) Reporters as referred to in paragraph (1) may make complaints: a. directly;
- b. by telephone; and/or
- c. by letter.
  - (3) In addition to handling complaints submitted by reporters, officers at service institutions as referred to in Article 20 are also authorized to handle victims of acts of Violence against women obtained through other information.
  - (4) Further provisions regarding the procedure for complaint handling services as referred to in paragraph (1) and paragraph (2) are regulated by Regent Regulation.

### Paragraph 3

Health Rehabilitation

#### Article 22

(1) Health rehabilitation as referred to in Article 19 letter b is an effort that includes aspects of:

- a. promotive;
- b. preventive;
- c. curative; and
- d. rehabilitative.
  - (2) Health rehabilitation as referred to in paragraph (1) is conducted comprehensively, accessible 24 (twenty-four) hours, of quality and conducted in accordance with health service standards.
  - (3) Women victims of acts of Violence have the right to free health services, including:
- a. medical services;
- b. medicolegal services;
- c. psychosocial counseling; and
- d. visum.
  - (4) Further provisions regarding the procedures for implementing health services as referred to in paragraph (3) are regulated by Regent Regulation.

### Paragraph 4

Social Rehabilitation

#### Article 23

(1) Social rehabilitation as referred to in Article 19 letter c is aimed at restoring and developing the capabilities of women victims of acts of Violence who experience social dysfunction to be able to carry out their social functions in society properly.

(2) Social rehabilitation as referred to in paragraph (1) includes services: a. counseling;

b. provision of safe houses; and

c. spiritual guidance.

(3) The implementation of services as referred to in paragraph (2) is organized by:

- a. SKPD having duties and functions in the social field;
- b. SKPD in charge of Women's Empowerment, and may cooperate with integrated service centers, women crisis centres or existing community organizations in the social field; or
- c. ministry of religious affairs.

#### Article 24

(1) Counseling services as referred to in Article 23 paragraph (2) letter a aim to:

- a. help women victims of acts of Violence recognize their problems and find effective ways to overcome them themselves;
- b. empower women victims of acts of Violence to determine their future;
- c. provide moral support to women victims of acts of Violence in facing the process they are undergoing; and
- d. make women victims of acts of Violence feel accepted in the community environment.
  - (2) Counseling services are conducted by gender-responsive social rehabilitation officers, consisting of:
- a. counselors;
- b. psychologists; and
- c. psychiatrists.
  - (3) Counseling services are conducted in places that guarantee a sense of security, comfort and confidentiality of information from women victims of acts of Violence. (4) Stages of counseling services include:
- a. initial counseling;
- b. follow-up counseling;
- c. case discussions; and
- d. site visits.

#### Article 25

(1) Regional Governments may provide safe houses for women victims of acts of Violence.

(2) The provision of safe house services as referred to in Article 23 paragraph (2) letter b aims to protect women victims of acts of Violence from intimidation by perpetrators or other parties.

(3) The location of the safe house as referred to in paragraph (1) must be kept confidential. (4) The criteria for women victims of acts of Violence who require safe houses include:

- a. their lives are threatened;
- b. receiving rejection from family or community;
- c. requiring intensive services but their residence is relatively far away; and/or
- d. abandoned if not placed in a safe house.
  - (5) The provision of safe house services is carried out by social rehabilitation officers who have gender sensitivity or gender awareness, consisting of: a. social workers/volunteers;
- b. security officers; and/or
- c. community leaders.
  - (6) Further provisions regarding the provision of safe house services are regulated by Regent Regulation.

#### Article 26

(1) Spiritual guidance as referred to in Article 23 paragraph (2) letter c aims at psychological recovery.

(2) Spiritual guidance as referred to in paragraph (1) is implemented based on the principles of:

- a. not imposing any religion or belief; and
- b. leaving the final decision in the hands of the victim (self determination).
  - (3) Spiritual guidance is carried out by spiritual guidance officers. (4) Spiritual guidance officers as referred to in paragraph (3) consist of consultants from the Marriage Counseling, Guidance and Preservation Board (BP4), Religious Affairs Office Officers, Ustads/Ulemas, Priests, Pastors and other religious leaders who have gender sensitivity or gender awareness.

### Paragraph 5

Law Enforcement and Legal Assistance

#### Article 27

(1) Law enforcement and legal assistance services as referred to in Article 19 letter d constitute actions by law enforcement apparatus to implement laws and regulations in order to handle cases of acts of Violence against women, especially to impose sanctions on perpetrators and provide Protection for witnesses and/or victims.

(2) Law enforcement and legal assistance services as referred to in paragraph (1) are carried out by prioritizing the best interests of the victim.

#### Article 28

(1) Law enforcement and legal assistance services are aimed at providing accompaniment and Protection for women victims of acts of Violence and witnesses with a gender-responsive approach so that victims obtain justice and legal certainty.

(2) Types of legal assistance services include:

- a. legal assistance in the reporting process and preparation of investigation reports;
- b. legal assistance in the prosecution process at the prosecutor's office;
- c. legal assistance in the examination process at court;
- d. legal assistance in the court hearing decision process until there is a final legal decision; and
- e. legal assistance in the mediation process.
  - (3) Regional Governments provide legal assistance to women victims of acts of Violence based on the provisions of laws and regulations. (4) In providing legal assistance as referred to in paragraph (3), Regional Governments may cooperate with legal aid institutions, women crisis centers, advocates and/or other gender-sensitive legal assistants.

### Paragraph 6

Repatriation and Social Reintegration

#### Article 29

(1) Repatriation is aimed at returning women victims of acts of Violence to their Region of origin.

(2) Repatriation of women victims of acts of Violence is accompanied by social reintegration efforts.

#### Article 30

(1) Social reintegration is carried out in order to reunite women victims of acts of Violence with family, substitute family, or community that can provide Protection and fulfillment of needs for victims.

(2) Social reintegration is aimed at ensuring: a. women victims of acts of Violence can resume their social lives; and b. women victims of acts of Violence can have a decent life. (3) Social reintegration as referred to in paragraph (1) is carried out after the victim receives social rehabilitation services.

#### Article 31

Regional Governments implement repatriation and social reintegration of women victims of acts of Violence in accordance with laws and regulations.

## Part Four

Labor Protection

#### Article 32

(1) Employers who employ women must maintain decency, safety and security while at the workplace.

(2) Employers must place female workers in places that are safe for women's reproductive health.

(3) Employers must fulfill the rights of female workers to receive wages in accordance with the provisions of laws and regulations. (4) Employers must provide special breastfeeding facilities at their companies.

#### Article 33

(1) Female workers/laborers who are under 18 (eighteen) years of age are prohibited from being employed between 23.00 WIB and 07.00 WIB.

(2) Employers are prohibited from employing pregnant female workers/laborers who according to a doctor's statement are dangerous for the health and safety of their pregnancy.

(3) Employers who employ female workers/laborers between 23.00 WIB and 07.00 WIB, must:

- a. provide nutritious food and drinks; and
- b. maintain decency and security while at the workplace.
  - (4) Employers must provide shuttle transportation for female workers/laborers who depart and return from work between 23.00 WIB and 05.00 WIB.

# CHAPTER V

IMPROVEMENT OF FAMILY QUALITY

#### Article 34

(1) In order to improve family quality, Regional Governments are responsible for:

- a. making efforts to improve family quality to realize gender equality and the rights of Children;
- b. providing services for families in realizing gender equality and the rights of Children.
- c. strengthening and developing institutions providing family quality improvement services in the Region; and
  - (2) The responsibility of Regional Governments in efforts to improve family quality as referred to in paragraph (1) is implemented by SKPD in accordance with its duties and functions.

#### Article 35

Regional Governments make efforts to improve family quality as referred to in Article 34 paragraph (1) letter a including:

- a. guidance;
- b. counseling;
- c. supervision;
- d. facilitation of family development implementation;
- e. improving the health quality of pregnant and breastfeeding mothers;

#### Article 36

Guidance as referred to in Article 35 letter a, includes: a. growth and development of toddler Children;

b. Protection of adolescent reproductive health;

c. Maturation of marriage age;

d. Birth regulation; and

e. Population management.

#### Article 37

Counseling as referred to in Article 35 letter b includes:

- a. conducting outreach on improving women's quality of life; b. conducting premarital counseling;

#### Article 38.

Supervision as referred to in Article 35 letter c includes: a. Conducting evaluation of toddler children's growth and development data;

b. Conducting evaluation of post-marriage data;

#### Article 39

Provision of services for families as referred to in Article 34 paragraph (1) letter b includes:

- a. communication, information and education;
- b. contraceptive devices and medicines;
- c. recording and reporting of family planning services;
- d. adequate health service facilities and infrastructure; e. breastfeeding corner rooms in every public facility.

#### Article 40

Communication, information and education as referred to in Article 39 letter a constitute communication activities to increase knowledge and improve the attitudes and behavior of families and communities in the population and family planning Program.

#### Article 41

(1) In addition to Regional Governments, private parties (private parties include, among others, limited liability companies that provide public services) are obliged to provide breastfeeding corner rooms.

(2) The breastfeeding corner room as referred to in paragraph (1) shall be a proper and adequate room.

(3) Private parties who do not provide a breastfeeding corner room as referred to in paragraph (2) shall be subject to administrative sanctions in the form of:

- a. written warning;
- b. restriction of business activities;
- c. suspension of business activities;
- d. administrative fine; and/or
- e. revocation of permit.

# CHAPTER VI

FULFILLMENT OF CHILDREN'S RIGHTS

## Part One

General

#### Article 42

(1) Regional Governments in efforts to fulfill the rights of Children are responsible to: a. establish and implement policies and programs/activities for the fulfillment of Children's rights;

b. respect and guarantee the human rights of every Child without distinction of ethnicity, religion, race, class, gender, ethnic group, culture and language, legal status of the Child, birth order of the Child, and physical and/or mental condition;

c. strengthen and develop institutions providing family quality improvement services in realizing Children's rights;

d. guarantee the Protection, care, and welfare of Children with due regard to the rights and obligations of parents, guardians, or other persons who are legally responsible for the Child;

e. provide facilities and infrastructure in the implementation of the fulfillment of Children's rights;

f. guarantee the right of Children to express opinions in accordance with the age and level of intelligence of the Child;

g. conduct supervision and guidance in the context of implementing the fulfillment of Children's rights; h. cooperate with private parties and/or NGOs in the implementation of Child Protection;

(2) The responsibilities of Regional Governments in Child Protection efforts as referred to in paragraph (1) shall be implemented by SKPD in accordance with their duties and functions.

## Part Two

Birth Registration

#### Article 43

(1) Regional Governments are obliged to administer birth registration of Children with the issuance of birth certificates.

(2) Birth certificates as referred to in paragraph (1) shall be administered free of charge.

## Part Three

Education

#### Article 44

Regional Governments shall guarantee the implementation of the 9 (nine) year compulsory education program for Children aged 7 (seven) to 15 (fifteen) years.

#### Article 45

(1) Every education unit organizer is obliged to provide opportunities for Children to obtain education services without discrimination.

(2) Every education unit organizer is prohibited from expelling Children from educational institutions unless there is a guarantee for the continuity of the Child's education.

(3) In the event that a Child victim of acts of Violence drops out of school, the education unit organizer must coordinate with the relevant agencies.

#### Article 46

(1) Every Child has the right to obtain education and teaching in the context of developing his/her personality and level of intelligence in accordance with interests and talents.

(2) Every Child has the right to obtain Protection in education units from sexual crimes and Violence committed by educators, education personnel, fellow students, and/or other parties.

#### Article 47

(1) Children with disabilities and Children with special needs shall be given equal opportunities to obtain formal, non-formal and informal education services, including inclusive education programs.

(2) Regional governments shall provide teaching staff for inclusive education in every education unit.

## Part Four

Health and Social

#### Article 48

(1) Regional Governments are obliged to provide comprehensive health services for Children, so that every Child obtains an optimal level of health from the womb.

(2) Comprehensive health services as referred to in paragraph (1) include:

- a. promotive, preventive, curative and rehabilitative efforts for basic health services;
- b. exemption from cost burdens for Children with malnutrition, Children with disabilities, Children with special needs, Children infected with HIV/AIDS, child workers, Children who are victims of narcotics, alcohol, psychotropic and other addictive substance abuse, Children who are victims of Violence, and Children who are victims of human trafficking; and
  - (3) Regional Governments, communities, families and parents must endeavor to ensure that Children who are born are protected from diseases that threaten survival and/or cause disability.

#### Article 49

(1) Promotive efforts for basic health services as referred to in Article 46 paragraph (2) letter a are aimed at improving the level of health more optimally.

(2) Promotive efforts as referred to in paragraph (1) shall be carried out by providing socialization or counseling to the community regarding healthy lifestyles which include:

- a. the importance of exclusive breastfeeding for infants;
- b. the importance of immunization for toddlers;
- c. clean living patterns;
- d. the importance of nutritious food;
- e. the dangers of drugs and smoking; and
  - (3) In addition to promotive efforts as referred to in paragraph (2), Regional Governments may carry out other activities in the context of providing health services.

#### Article 50

(1) Preventive efforts for basic health services as referred to in Article 46 paragraph (2) letter a are aimed at preventing the occurrence of disease or health problems.

(2) Efforts as referred to in paragraph (1) shall be carried out by: a. requiring mothers to provide exclusive breastfeeding to infants up to 6 (six) months of age;

b. providing free immunization and vitamins to infants and toddlers; c. providing nutritious food to toddlers from underprivileged families; d. optimizing the role of posyandu for health services for mothers and toddlers;

e. conducting periodic health examinations; and

f. providing sanitation facilities and clean water in every school.

#### Article 51

(1) Curative efforts for basic health services as referred to in Article 46 paragraph (2) letter a are aimed at preventing disease from becoming more severe through treatment.

(2) Treatment as referred to in paragraph (1) shall be carried out free of charge for Children from underprivileged families.

#### Article 52

(1) Rehabilitative efforts for basic health services as referred to in Article 46 paragraph (2) letter a are aimed at maintaining and restoring conditions after illness.

(2) Rehabilitative efforts as referred to in paragraph (1) shall be carried out through periodic health examinations.

#### Article 53

(1) Regional Governments are obliged to provide Social Welfare for: a. Children in conflict with the law;

b. Children who are victims of Violence who are economically and sexually exploited, neglected Children, Children who are victims of kidnapping and Children who are victims of human trafficking;

c. Children who have no parents and are cared for by parties or families who are unable;

d. Children who are victims of natural disasters or social disasters; and

e. Children with disabilities;

(2) Social welfare as referred to in paragraph (1) may be in the form of providing services:

- a. health;
- b. education;
- c. legal aid;
- d. assistance;
- e. social, mental and spiritual guidance;
- f. social rehabilitation; and/or
- g. reintegration.

## Part Five

Manpower

#### Article 54

Regional Governments shall conduct periodic supervision of every person/entity that employs Children in efforts to Protect child workers.

#### Article 55

Every person/entity that employs Children must pay attention to the following requirements:

- a. at least 15 (fifteen) years of age;
- b. obtaining written consent from the parents/guardians of the child worker; c. having a written employment agreement between the employer and the parents/guardians of the child worker and obtaining approval from the authorized agency; d. not employing Children at night;
- e. working hours of no more than 3 (three) hours per day;

#### Article 56

In the event that Children are employed together with adult workers, then the workplace for Children must be separated from the workplace for adult workers.

#### Article 57

(1) Every person/entity is prohibited from employing and involving Children in the worst forms of work.

(2) The worst forms of work referred to in paragraph (1) include: a. all work in the form of slavery or similar practices; b. all work that utilizes, provides, or offers

Children for prostitution, pornography production, pornographic performances, or gambling;

c. all work that utilizes, provides, or involves Children for the production and trade of alcoholic beverages, narcotics, psychotropic substances, and other addictive substances; and/or

d. all work that endangers the health, safety, or morals of Children.

## Part Sixth

Facilitation of the Child Justice System

#### Article 58

(1) Facilitation of the Child justice system includes criminal and civil justice. (2) Facilitation of the criminal justice system as referred to in paragraph (1) includes:

- a. provision of companions or Child volunteers during examination by investigators;
- b. provision of legal assistance and psychologist services;
- c. involving members/investigators of local police in efforts to prevent criminal acts committed by Children and Children as victims;
- d. realizing restorative justice processes for Children in conflict with the law; and
- e. rehabilitation and social reintegration services;
  - (3) Fulfillment of Children's rights in the civil justice system as referred to in paragraph (1) includes the provision and delivery of consultation and legal assistance services during the judicial process.
  - (4) In facilitating the Child justice system as referred to in paragraph (1) and paragraph (2), the authorized SKPD is the SKPD responsible for the Protection of Women and Children

## Part Seventh

Child Forum

#### Article 59

(1) In implementing the fulfillment of Children's rights to express opinions and participate, the Regional Government facilitates the establishment of the Child Forum.

(2) The Child Forum as referred to in paragraph (1) is a representation of Children in the Region, both representation of Children's geographical domicile, components of Children's socio-cultural groups and Children's educational background.

(3) In every formulation of policies related to Children, the Regional Government must pay attention to and accommodate the opinions of Children submitted through the Child participation Forum.

(4) Further provisions regarding the establishment of the Child Forum as referred to in paragraph (1) through paragraph (3) shall be stipulated by Regent Decree.

## Part Eighth

Child-Friendly Regency

#### Article 60

(1) In the context of fulfilling Children's rights in an integrated, systematic and sustainable manner, the Regional Government establishes KLA Development policies. (2) KLA Development policies contain:

- a. the KLA concept concerning objectives, strategies and roles of related parties;
- b. Children's rights; and
- c. KLA development approaches.

#### Article 61

(1) KLA development policies are directed at fulfilling Children's rights divided into 5 (five) groups, including:

- a. civil rights and freedoms;
- b. family environment and alternative care;
- c. basic health and welfare;
- d. education, use of leisure time, and cultural activities; and e. Special Protection.
  - (2) Further provisions regarding KLA development policies as referred to in paragraph (1) shall be regulated by Regent Regulation.

#### Article 62

(1) In order to ensure the effectiveness of KLA policy implementation in the Region, a KLA Task Force is established.

(2) The establishment and membership composition of the KLA Task Force shall be stipulated by Regent Decree.

(3) The KLA Task Force is domiciled at the Office of Women's Empowerment and Family Planning.

(4) The KLA Task Force as referred to in paragraph (1) has the main tasks of:

- a. coordinating the implementation of KLA policies and development; b. conducting socialization, advocacy and communication of information and education on KLA policies;
- c. collecting basic data for KLA development;
- d. conducting needs analysis sourced from basic data; e. disseminating basic data;
- f. determining the focus and priority of programs in realizing KLA, adjusted to regional potential;
- g. preparing a 5 (five) year Child-Friendly City regional action plan and work mechanism; and
- h. conducting monitoring, evaluation and reporting at least once every 1 (one) year.

#### Article 63

In carrying out its duties, the KLA Task Force may coordinate and cooperate with PPT institutions and other institutions that provide Child Protection services.

# CHAPTER VII

CHILD PROTECTION

#### Article 64

The Regional Government, in order to provide protection to Children: a. implements and supports national policies in the implementation of Child Protection in the Region;

b. guarantees Children to exercise their rights in expressing opinions in accordance with the age and intelligence level of the Child;

c. ]conducts prevention of Violence against Children by optimizing the role of traditional institutions;

d. conducts socialization, seminars and education regarding Protection and rights of Children;

e. develops child-friendly school models;

f. provides public facilities that are friendly and safe for Children g. encourages public awareness of the importance of Protection for Children;

#### Article 65

Service efforts carried out by the Regional Government for child victims of acts of Violence as referred to in Article 42 through Article 64 apply mutatis mutandis to service efforts for Children who are victims of acts of Violence.

# CHAPTER VIII

PARTICIPATION OF THE COMMUNITY, AND PARENTS AND/OR FAMILY

## Part First

Community

#### Article 66

(1) The participation of the community in efforts to Protect women and Children:

- a. participates in prevention and provides support for the development of programs to prevent violations of the rights of women and Children;
- b. provides information and/or reports violations of the rights of women and Children to the authorities; and
- c. monitors the law enforcement process regarding cases of violations of the rights of women and Children.
  - (2) Ninik mamak, alim ulama and cadiak pandai as well as bundo kanduang are responsible for protecting and preventing violations of the rights of women and Children.
  - (3) Community participation as referred to in paragraph (1) and paragraph (2) is based on laws and regulations and local wisdom values of the local community.

#### Article 67

(1) In the context of preventing violations of women's rights, the community:

- a. monitors various conditions that occur in their environment that may lead to violations of women's rights; b.increases awareness of violations of women's rights; c. strengthens the role of community organizations, religious organizations and the family environment; d.conducts supervision of the implementation of Women's Empowerment and Child Protection organized by the Regional Government through mechanisms for conveying aspirations to the Regent or regional people's representative council;
  - (2) In the context of preventing violations of Children's rights, the community:
- a. monitors various conditions that occur in their environment that lead to violations of Children's rights; b.increases awareness of violations of Children's rights;
- c. strengthens the role of community organizations, religious organizations and the family environment; and prevents early marriage.

## Part Second

Parents and/or Family

#### Article 68

The Family in efforts to Protect women's rights plays a role in: a. protecting women from conflict and Violence in the household that degrades the dignity of women;

b. providing equal opportunities for women to develop themselves in the fields of education, social, economic, political and cultural; and

c. preventing violations of women's rights in the household.

#### Article 69

(1) Parents in the effort of Protection of the rights of Children have the role of: a. caring for, maintaining, educating, and protecting Children;

b. developing Children in accordance with their abilities, talents, and interests;

c. providing formal and non-formal education;

d. providing character education and instilling moral values in Children;

e. guiding Children to behave in accordance with religion, ethics and morals as well as customs; and

f. preventing the occurrence of marriage at an early age.

(2) In the event that parents are absent, or their whereabouts are unknown, or due to a certain reason, cannot carry out their obligations and responsibilities, then the obligations and responsibilities as referred to in paragraph (1) may be transferred to the family.

# CHAPTER IX

GUIDANCE AND SUPERVISION

#### Article 70

(1) Regional Government is obligated to conduct guidance and supervision of the implementation of Protection of women and Children victims of Violence. (2) Guidance as referred to in paragraph (1), includes: a. guidelines and fulfillment standards;

b. technical guidance and training;

c. provision of facilities;

d. monitoring; and

e. evaluation.

(3) Guidance as referred to in paragraph (2) is in order to realize the objectives of Protection of women and Children victims of Violence in accordance with minimum service standards.

#### Article 71

(1) Supervision of the implementation of Child Protection as regulated in this Regional Regulation is carried out by the Regional Government Supervisory Agency of Padang Pariaman Regency and P2TP2A;

(2) In carrying out supervision, P2TP2A is authorized to recommend cases to law enforcement officials, government agencies and other community institutions as regulated in the Law

(3) Recommendations submitted by P2TP2A must be followed up by law enforcement officials, government agencies, community institutions and other related individuals.

# CHAPTER X

FINANCING

#### Article 72

Financing for the implementation of Women's Empowerment and Child Protection programs and activities is sourced from:

- a. regional revenue and expenditure budget; and
- b. other lawful sources in accordance with the provisions of laws and regulations.

# CHAPTER XI

CLOSING PROVISIONS

#### Article 73

Implementing regulations for this Regional Regulation shall be stipulated no later than 6 (six) months from the promulgation of this Regional Regulation.

#### Article 74

This Regional Regulation comes into effect on the date of promulgation, so that everyone shall know of it, orders the promulgation of this Regional Regulation by its placement in the Regional Gazette of Padang Pariaman Regency.
