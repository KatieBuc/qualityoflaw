EMPOWERMENT AND PROTECTION OF WOMEN

BY THE GRACE OF GOD ALMIGHTY

REGENT OF PANGANDARAN,

Considering:
 
 
 
 
 
 
 
a. that in order to improve the position, role and quality of women is an effort to realize gender equality and justice in family life, society, nation and state;
b. that all forms of violence against women constitute a violation of human rights so that women need to be protected in their dignity and honor and guaranteed their right to life in accordance with their nature and destiny without discrimination and empowered to be able to actualize their potential optimally;
c. that to protect the community and realize equity and justice, as an obligation of the regional government to provide protection and welfare for women, a legal foundation is required in the implementation of empowerment and protection of women;
d. that based on the considerations as referred to in letter a, letter b and letter c, it is necessary to establish a Regional Regulation on Empowerment and Protection of Women.

In View of:
 
 
 
 
 
 
 
1. Article 18 paragraph (6) of the 1945 Constitution of the Republic of Indonesia;

# CHAPTER I

## GENERAL PROVISIONS

#### Article 1

In this Regional Regulation, what is meant by:  
1. Regency Area is Pangandaran Regency.  
2. Regent is the Regent of Pangandaran.  
3. Regency Regional Government is the Regent as an element of regional government administration who leads the implementation of government affairs that are the authority of the autonomous region.  
4. Regional Apparatus is an auxiliary element of the Regent and the Regional House of Representatives in the administration of government affairs that are the authority of the Region.  
5. Women are adult humans of female sex and persons recognized by law as Women.  
6. Prevention is activities carried out to prevent the occurrence of acts of violence against Women.  
7. Women's Empowerment is every effort to improve physical, mental spiritual, social abilities, knowledge and skills so that Women are ready to be utilized according to their respective abilities.  
8. Protection is all efforts aimed at providing a sense of security to victims carried out by family, advocates, social institutions, police, prosecutors, courts, or other parties either temporarily.  
9. Protection of Women is all activities aimed at providing a sense of security carried out by the Regional Government, community, and/or other parties who know or hear that violence against Women will or has occurred.  
10. Victim is a Woman who experiences misery and/or suffering either directly or indirectly as a result of violence and life vulnerability.  
11. Violence is any act that results in or may result in misery or suffering whether physical, sexual, economic, social and psychological to the victim.  
12. Victim of Violence is an individual or group of people who experience physical, mental or emotional suffering, economic loss, or experience neglect, reduction or deprivation of their basic rights, as a result of human rights violations or criminal acts as well as acts of Violence committed either by state apparatus, Regional Government apparatus, or individuals.  
13. Violence Against Women is any action that results in or may result in misery or suffering to Women physically, sexually, economically, socially, psychologically including threats of certain actions, coercion or deprivation of freedom, whether occurring in public or private life.  
14. Physical Violence is any act that causes pain, injury, wounds or disability to a person's body, miscarriage or fainting and/or causes death.  
15. Psychological Violence is an act that causes fear, loss of self-confidence, loss of ability to act, distrust or severe psychological suffering to a person.  
16. Sexual Violence is any act in the form of sexual harassment, forced sexual intercourse, whether unnatural or unwanted with another person for commercial purposes and/or certain purposes.  
17. Domestic Neglect is a person who neglects a person within their household scope, whereas according to the law applicable to them or because of an agreement or contract they are obliged to provide livelihood, care, or maintenance to that person. In addition, neglect also applies to every person who causes economic dependence by limiting and/or prohibiting decent work inside or outside the home so that the victim is under the control of that person.  
18. Trafficking in Persons is the act of recruitment, transportation, harboring, sending, transfer or receipt of a person by threat of violence, use of violence, abduction, confinement, forgery, fraud, abuse of power or vulnerable position, debt bondage or giving payment or benefits, so as to obtain consent from the person holding control over that other person, whether carried out within the country or between countries, for the purpose of exploitation or resulting in the person being exploited.  
19. Exploitation is an act that exploits economically or sexually with the intention of benefiting oneself or others, acts with or without the victim's consent which include but are not limited to prostitution, forced labor or service, slavery or similar practices, oppression, extortion, utilization of physical, sexual, reproductive organs, or unlawfully transferring or transplanting organs and/or body tissues or utilizing the labor or ability of a person by another party to obtain material or immaterial benefits and/or any form of utilization of sexual organs or other body organs of the victim to obtain benefits, including but not limited to all prostitution or indecency activities.  
20. Domestic Violence is any act against a Woman that results in misery or suffering physically, sexually, psychologically and/or Domestic Neglect including threats to commit acts, coercion or deprivation of freedom unlawfully within the domestic environment.  
21. Family is a person who has a blood relationship in a straight line upward or downward and collateral line up to the third degree, or who has a marital relationship, or a person who is a dependent of a Woman.  
22. Parents are biological father and/or mother, or stepfather and/or stepmother, or adoptive father and/or mother.  
23. Discrimination Against Women is any distinction, exclusion or restriction made on the basis of sex which has the effect or purpose of reducing or eliminating the recognition, enjoyment or exercise of human rights and fundamental freedoms in the political, economic, social, cultural, civil or any other field by Women, regardless of their marital status on the basis of equality between men and Women.  
24. All Forms of Discrimination Against Women are all forms of discrimination that include territorial dimensions, age dimensions, and special dimensions.  
25. Gender is a concept that refers to the differentiation of roles and responsibilities of men and Women that occur as a result of and can be changed by the social and cultural circumstances of society.  
26. Gender Equality is the equality of conditions for men and Women to obtain opportunities and rights as human beings so as to be able to play a role and participate in political, economic, socio-cultural, defense and security activities, and equality in enjoying the results of development.  
27. Gender Justice is a process to be fair to men and Women.  
28. Health Rehabilitation is an effort to restore the Victim's condition including physical, psychological, and sexual health so that the Victim can carry out their role again properly and naturally both in the family and in society.  
29. Social Rehabilitation is a service aimed at restoring and developing the ability of a person experiencing social dysfunction so that they can carry out their social functions normally.  
30. Repatriation is an effort to return Victims of Violence to their area of origin.  
31. Social Reintegration is an effort to reunite Victims with family, substitute family, or community that can provide Protection and fulfillment of needs for Victims.  
32. Integrated Service Center for Women and Children Empowerment hereinafter referred to as P2TP2A is an integrated service unit established by the Regional Government to carry out the functions and main tasks of integrated services for Women and child victims of violence, including complaint services, Health Rehabilitation, Social Rehabilitation, Repatriation, Social Reintegration, and legal assistance.  
33. Minimum Service Standards hereinafter abbreviated as SPM is a performance benchmark for integrated service units in providing services for handling reports, complaints, health services, Social Rehabilitation, law enforcement and legal assistance, as well as Repatriation and Social Reintegration for Women Victims of Violence.

# CHAPTER II

## WOMEN'S RIGHTS

## Part One

General

#### Article 2

Every Woman has the right:  
a. to life;  
b. to form a family and continue descendants;  
c. to self-development;  
d. to obtain justice;  
e. to personal freedom;  
f. to security;  
g. to welfare; and  
h. to participate in government.

## Part Two

Rights of Women with Disabilities

#### Article 3

Every Woman with disabilities has the right to obtain:  
a. education at all units, paths, types and levels of education;  
b. decent work and livelihood in accordance with the type and degree of disability, education and ability;  
c. equal treatment to play a role in development and enjoy its results;  
d. accessibility in the context of independence;  
e. rehabilitation, social assistance and maintenance of social welfare standards; and  
f. equal rights to develop talents, abilities and social life.

## Part Three

Rights of Women Victims of Violence

#### Article 4

Every woman victim of violence has the right:  
a. to have her dignity respected as a human being;  
b. to health rehabilitation;  
c. to determine her own decisions;  
d. to obtain information;  
e. to confidentiality;  
f. to compensation;  
g. to social rehabilitation;  
h. to complaint handling;  
i. to obtain facilities in the judicial process; and  
j. to assistance.

# CHAPTER III

## WOMEN'S EMPOWERMENT

#### Article 5

Women's empowerment is directed to obtain opportunities and rights as human beings to be able to play a role and participate in the fields of:  
a. economy;  
b. social culture;  
c. politics and government;  
d. law;  
e. education;  
f. health;  
g. employment; and  
h. social security.

## Part One

Economic Field

#### Article 6

Women's empowerment in the economic field is implemented through:  
a. provision of skills and job training;  
b. facilitation of the formation of productive economic business groups;  
c. facilitation of strengthening and development of productive economic business groups;  
d. facilitation and capital assistance; and  
e. facilitation of marketing network development.

## Part Two

Social and Cultural Field

#### Article 7

Women's empowerment in the social and cultural field is implemented through:  
a. improvement of knowledge, attitudes and skills to encourage the fulfillment of tiered education in accordance with potential to improve social status;  
b. improvement of awareness, willingness and ability to overcome health problems through quality promotive, preventive, curative, and rehabilitative efforts, especially in the field of reproductive health;  
c. improvement of awareness and knowledge about independent, healthy and prosperous family planning including access to consultation services and marriage registration; and  
d. facilitation and efforts to preserve customs and develop science, technology, arts and culture for the advancement of women.

## Part Three

Political and Governmental Field

#### Article 8

1. Women's empowerment in the political and governmental field includes:  
a. involvement of women in decision-making at various levels;  
b. provision of opportunities for women to hold public office;  
c. participation in general elections; and  
d. self-development through organizations to associate, assemble and express opinions.  
2. Women's empowerment in the political and governmental field as referred to in paragraph (1) is implemented in accordance with laws and regulations.  
3. The Regional Government provides guarantees to women to have the right to vote and/or be elected in general elections, regional head elections, village head elections and/or other political office elections based on equal rights through direct, general, free, secret, honest and fair voting according to laws and regulations.  
4. The Regional Government provides opportunities for women to be appointed as regional government officials and occupy strategic positions in regional government; and
5. The Regional Government and/or Political Parties are responsible for providing political education for women.

## Part Four

Legal Field

#### Article 9

1. Women's empowerment in the legal field includes:  
a. improvement of awareness and knowledge in the legal field through communication, information and education services; and  
b. facilitation of access and legal consultation services.
2. Women's empowerment in the legal field as referred to in paragraph (1) is implemented in accordance with laws and regulations.

## Part Five

Education Field

#### Article 10

Women's empowerment in the education field through:  
a. provision of formal and non-formal education for women in the Region.  
b. non-formal education aimed at improving women's abilities.  
c. non-formal education as referred to in letter b, includes women's empowerment education, skills education and job training.  
d. formal and non-formal education as referred to in letter a, is regulated by a separate Regional Regulation.

## Part Six

Health Field

#### Article 11

1. Women have the right to obtain health services in order to undergo reproductive life and sexual life that is healthy, safe, and free from coercion and/or violence.  
2. Health services as referred to in paragraph (1) include:  
a. health services before pregnancy, childbirth and after delivery;  
b. pregnancy regulation, contraceptive services and sexual health; and  
c. reproductive health services.  
d. health services implemented through promotive, preventive, curative and rehabilitative approaches.

## Part Seven

Employment Field

#### Article 12

1. Women have equal opportunities without discrimination to obtain employment.  
2. Employers who employ female workers must provide protection that includes welfare, safety and health.

## Part Eight

Social Security Field

#### Article 13

1. The Regional Government is responsible for providing guidance facilities for women with social problems.  
2. The Regional Government provides the right to social security for women with social problems.  
3. The responsibility for providing guidance facilities for women with social problems is under the regional apparatus organization that handles women's empowerment affairs.

# CHAPTER IV

## WOMEN'S PROTECTION

## Part One

General

#### Article 14

Implementation of women's protection is provided to:  
a. women workers/laborers;  
b. elderly women;  
c. women with disabilities;  
d. homeless women;  
e. domestic workers;  
f. women as family economic supporters;  
g. women former inmates of correctional institutions;  
h. women victims of disasters;  
i. commercial sex workers; and  
j. women victims of violence.

## Part One

Women Workers/Laborers

#### Article 15

1. Implementation of protection for women workers/laborers to provide protection from acts of economic exploitation and cruel, inhumane treatment and to seek proper appreciation for work.  
2. Women workers/laborers as referred to in paragraph (1) receive protection for:  
a. recognition of rights, wages and decent working conditions;  
b. health insurance and employment social security;  
c. access to information and legal consultation services;  
d. opportunities to obtain knowledge and skills to improve status.

## Part Two

Elderly Women

#### Article 16

1. Implementation of protection for elderly women in order to extend life expectancy so they can carry out their social functions and play an active role naturally in their social life.
2. Elderly women as referred to in paragraph (1) shall receive the right to Protection over:
a. guarantee of continuity and personal self-development;
b. participation in the fields of education, health, employment, politics, arts and culture as well as utilization of information and communication technology;
c. accessibility to information, legal aid, mobility, social services; and
d. early intervention efforts including treatment and rehabilitation to improve their function and capacity.

## Part Three

Women with Disabilities

#### Article 17

1. Implementation of Protection for women with disabilities in order to provide protection and prevention against forms of discrimination including freedom from cruel, inhumane treatment, degrading human dignity and freedom from exploitation;
2. Women with disabilities as referred to in paragraph (1) shall receive the right to protection over:
a. guarantee of continuity and personal self-development;
b. participation in the fields of education, health, employment, politics, sports, arts and culture as well as utilization of information and communication technology;
c. accessibility to information, legal aid, mobility, social services; and
d. early intervention efforts including treatment and rehabilitation to improve their function and capacity.

## Part Four

Homeless Women

#### Article 18

1. Implementation of protection for homeless women in order to provide protection and prevention against forms of violence caused by the absence of shelter or permanent residence.
2. Homeless women as referred to in paragraph (1) shall receive the right to protection over:
a. decent housing;
b. health and social security;
c. population administration registration;
d. opportunity to obtain skills to improve economic status; and
e. access to information and legal consultation services.

## Part Five

Domestic Workers

#### Article 19

1. Implementation of Protection for domestic workers in order to provide protection and prevention against forms of discrimination, cruel, inhumane treatment, degrading human dignity, and exploitation in employment relations.
2. Domestic workers as referred to in paragraph (1) shall receive protection over:
a. recognition of rights, wages, and decent working conditions;
b. health insurance and social security;
c. access to information and legal consultation services; and
d. opportunity to obtain knowledge and skills to improve economic status.

## Part Six

Women as Family Economic Supporters

#### Article 20

1. Implementation of protection for women as family economic supporters in order to provide protection and prevention against forms of discrimination, negative stigma and economic empowerment for women as family economic supporters.
2. Women as family economic supporters as referred to in paragraph (1) shall receive the right to protection over:
a. discriminatory actions and efforts to weaken the function of women as family economic supporters;
b. strengthening of capacity and recognition as family economic supporters in their environment; and
c. accessibility to economic and financial institutions.

## Part Seven

Women Former Inmates of Correctional Institutions

#### Article 21

1. Implementation of Protection for women former inmates of Correctional Institutions in order to provide protection from obstacles to adjusting to life in society, so they can carry out life normally.
2. Women former inmates of Correctional Institutions as referred to in paragraph (1) shall receive protection over:a. social rehabilitation and reintegration; and
b. opportunity to obtain knowledge and skills to improve economic status.

## Part Eight

Women Disaster Victims

#### Article 22

1. Implementation of Protection for women disaster victims in order to provide protection during disasters and post-disaster.
2. Women disaster victims as referred to in paragraph (1) shall receive protection over:
a. access to decent housing;
b. health services;
c. access to information services and legal consultation; and
d. post-disaster recovery.

## Part Nine

Women Commercial Sex Workers

#### Article 23

1. Implementation of Protection for women commercial sex workers in order to provide health protection and improvement of life skills as capital to improve social status.
2. Protection for women commercial sex workers as referred to in paragraph (1) includes:
a. providing life skills;
b. facilitating reproductive health services; and
c. preventing trafficking in persons.

## Part Ten

Women Victims of Violence

#### Article 24

1. Forms of violence against Women include:
a. physical violence;
b. psychological violence;
c. sexual violence;
d. domestic neglect;
e. trafficking in women; and
f. exploitation.
2. Implementation of protection for women victims of violence in order to provide legal protection and/or health recovery (3) Protection for women victims of violence as referred to in paragraph (2) includes:
a. legal aid;
b. assistance following the occurrence of acts of violence;
c. social rehabilitation and reintegration; and
d. health services.

# CHAPTER V

## OBLIGATIONS AND RESPONSIBILITIES

## Part One

General

#### Article 25

Empowerment and protection of women is a joint obligation and responsibility among:
a. Regional Government;
b. society;
c. family; and
d. parents.

## Part Two

Obligations and Responsibilities of Regional Government

#### Article 26

1. The Regional Government has the obligation and responsibility for the empowerment and protection of women.  
2. The efforts for empowerment and protection of women as referred to in paragraph (1), include:  
a. determination of policies, programs, and activities;  
b. determination of implementation guidelines;  
c. provision of services; and  
d. coordination of policies, programs and activities.  
3. The obligations and responsibilities of the Regional Government as referred to in Article 25 letter a include:  
a. implementing policies for the Protection of Women from all forms of discrimination and acts of violence;  
b. determining policies, programs, and activities for the Empowerment and Protection of Women that have been established;  
c. collecting data and information about Women Victims of Violence and Discrimination as well as supporting laws and regulations;  
d. conducting education on matters related to the Protection of Women Victims of Violence and Discrimination;  
e. conducting socialization of laws and regulations related to the Protection of Women Victims of Violence and Discrimination;  
f. conducting monitoring and evaluation of the implementation of Protection of Women Victims of Violence and Discrimination.  
4. To anticipate the occurrence of acts of Violence, the Regional Government is obliged to provide and organize services for Victims in the form of:  
a. establishing and facilitating the operation of integrated service institutions for Victims by involving community elements;  
b. facilitating legal aid assistance and legal services according to the needs of Victims;  
c. providing safe houses or alternative residences along with handling mechanisms, psychosocial and spiritual services;  
d. conducting continuous handling up to the rehabilitation and social reintegration stage;  
e. conducting monitoring and evaluation of the implementation of protection of Victims; and  
f. encouraging public awareness of the importance of Protection for Victims.  
5. In carrying out the obligations as referred to in paragraph (3) and paragraph (4), the Regional Government must pay attention to the rights and obligations of Parents, guardians, husbands or other persons who are legally responsible for the Victim.  
6. To carry out the obligations and responsibilities as referred to in paragraph (1) through paragraph (4), the Regional Government prepares a regional action plan for the Protection of Women Victims of Violence.  
7. Provisions regarding the regional action plan for the Protection of Women Victims of Violence as referred to in paragraph (6) are regulated by Regent Regulation.

#### Article 27

1. The Regent integrates policies, programs and activities for the empowerment and protection of women into regional development planning.  
2. The integration of programs and activities as referred to in paragraph (1) is incorporated into planning and budget documents which include:  
a. regional long-term development plan;  
b. regional medium-term development plan;  
c. strategic plan of Regional Apparatus;  
d. regional government work plan; and

4. In carrying out the activities as referred to in paragraph (3), the Regional Apparatus in charge of Women coordinates with Regional Apparatus that have direct relevance to the field of Women.

#### Article 28

1. The Regent implements policies, programs, and activities for the Protection of Women to eliminate all forms of discrimination and acts of Violence Against Women.  
2. The Regional Apparatus in charge of Women implements policies, programs, and activities for the Protection of Women that have been established and their implementation is carried out in cooperation and coordination with relevant Regional Apparatus, law enforcers, community institutions, universities, and the community.  
3. The implementation of policies, programs, and activities for the Protection of Women as referred to in paragraph (2) is in the form of:  
a. policy analysis;  
b. advocacy;  
c. coordination and communication;  
d. socialization;  
e. education;  
f. training;  
g. facilitation;  
h. provision of services; and  
i. other forms.

#### Article 29

1. The Regent in implementing policies, programs, and activities for the protection of women as referred to in Article 28 undertakes efforts to:  
a. coordinate implementation among Regional Apparatus;  
b. cooperate with the central government, provincial government, and other regency/city governments in accordance with the provisions of laws and regulations;  
c. strengthen the capacity of gender institutions;  
d. facilitate services for the Protection of Women;  
e. provide services for the Protection of Women;  
f. implement affirmative action for the Protection of Women; and  
g. prepare a data system for the Protection of Women including a data system for Violence Against Women.  
2. The implementation of policies, programs, and activities as referred to in paragraph (1) is coordinated by the Vice Regent.

## Part Three

Obligations and Responsibilities of the Community, Family, and Parents

#### Article 30

1. The obligations and responsibilities of the community as referred to in Article 25 letter b are organized in the form of community participation.  
2. Forms of community participation as referred to in paragraph (1) include:  
a. forming family partners at the sub-district and/or village level;  
b. forming women's protection units within community organizations;  
c. conducting socialization of women's rights independently;  
d. providing first aid to victims; and  
e. reporting to the competent authority if violence against victims occurs in their environment;  
3. Community participation as referred to in paragraph (1) is carried out by individuals, social community institutions, non-governmental organizations, educational institutions, religious institutions, the private sector, and mass media.  
4. Provisions regarding forms of community participation as referred to in paragraph (2) are carried out responsibly in accordance with the provisions of laws and regulations.

#### Article 31

The obligations of the family and parents as referred to in Article 25 letter c and letter d are in the form of full responsibility to prevent all forms of violence and protect women as family members.

# CHAPTER VI

## STRATEGIES FOR EMPOWERMENT AND PROTECTION OF WOMEN

#### Article 32

1. The empowerment and protection of women is implemented by integrating gender mainstreaming and gender-disaggregated data in an integrated manner based on laws and regulations.  
2. In the empowerment and protection of women, a Working Group and Integrated Services are formed as determined by Regent Decree.  
3. The empowerment and protection of women is coordinated by the Agency that handles Women's Empowerment.

# CHAPTER VII

PREVENTION, PROTECTION SERVICES, AND RECOVERY OF WOMEN VICTIMS OF VIOLENCE

## Part One

Prevention of Violence Against Women

#### Article 33

The Regional Government in preventing the occurrence of acts of violence against women:  
a. prepares and socializes matters relating to violence against women;  
b. conducts socialization of Women's rights;  
c. seeks to improve education for Women;  
d. opens skills courses for Women to support the family's economic needs;  
e. creates employment opportunities for Women;  
f. provides counseling related to Violence Against Women;  
g. empowers social community organizations and Women's organizations at the Regional, sub-district, and village levels to increase Women's knowledge and insight;  
h. builds complaint posts for the Protection of Women; and  
i. forms a network in efforts to prevent Violence Against Women.

## Part Two

Protection Services

#### Article 34

1. Protection services to women are provided in the form of:  
a. protection of the victim's personal security from physical and mental threats;  
b. health services according to medical needs;  
c. medical services for evidentiary purposes in the legal field;  
d. maintaining the confidentiality of the victim's identity;  
e. provision of testimony during court hearings without facing the suspect;  
f. spiritual guidance services; and/or  
g. protection of Women working as laborers or workers in accordance with the provisions of laws and regulations.  
2. Protection of the victim's personal security from physical and mental threats as referred to in paragraph (1) letter a shall be carried out by family, law enforcement officials, social institutions, and other parties either temporarily or based on a court Protection order.

#### Article 35

1. In providing protection services as referred to in Article 34 paragraph (1), the Regional Government shall:  
a. provide services and handling as quickly as possible to witnesses and/or victims;  
b. provide convenience, comfort, safety, and free of charge for witnesses and/or victims;  
c. maintain the confidentiality of witnesses and/or victims;  
d. guarantee justice and legal certainty for witnesses and/or Victims;  
e. provide apparatus, health workers, social workers, and spiritual counselors;  
f. create and develop a system and mechanism for cooperation of service programs involving parties that are easily accessible to victims;  
g. provide protection for companions, witnesses, family, and friends of victims;  
h. provide decent work and livelihood according to the type and degree of disability, education, and ability; and  
i. provide protection for female workers or laborers in accordance with the provisions of protection for laborers or workers in accordance with the provisions of laws and regulations.  
2. The Regional Government in providing protection services as referred to in paragraph (1) may cooperate with the central government, provincial government, other regency/city governments, the community, and/or other social institutions.  
3. Cooperation as referred to in paragraph (2) shall be set forth in a cooperation agreement.

#### Article 36

1. The provision of Women's Protection services as referred to in Article 35 consists of Victim identification, legal assistance, medical rehabilitation, psychosocial rehabilitation, Social Reintegration, Repatriation assistance, or other forms.  
2. The provision of Women's Protection services as referred to in paragraph (1) shall be provided for Women Victims of Violence including Victims of Trafficking in Persons, Women in disaster-prone areas, Women workers, elderly Women, Women with disabilities, Women Victims of sexual exploitation, Women heads of households, and other vulnerable Women groups.

#### Article 37

Any person related to the implementation of Women's Protection who knows of the occurrence of acts of Violence against Women but allows it, does not provide Protection to Victims, and/or does not report to the relevant agency may be subject to administrative sanctions in the form of:  
a. written warning;  
b. temporary suspension from activities;  
c. termination of cooperation;  
d. revocation of registration certificate;  
e. revocation of operational license;  
f. withdrawal of facilities; and  
g. administrative fine.

## Part Three

Recovery

#### Article 38

1. For the purpose of recovery, victims have the right to obtain:  
a. health services;  
b. victim assistance;  
c. counseling;  
d. spiritual guidance; and  
e. resocialization.  
2. For the purpose of recovery as referred to in paragraph (1), the Regional Government is obliged to provide:  
a. expert and professional personnel;  
b. service centers and safe houses; and  
c. other facilities and infrastructure necessary for victim recovery.  
3. Expert and professional personnel as referred to in paragraph (2) letter b consist of:  
a. health workers;  
b. social workers;  
c. volunteer companions; and/or d. spiritual counselors.  
4. Health workers as referred to in paragraph (3) letter a are obliged to examine victims in accordance with their professional standards, restore, and rehabilitate the health of victims if victims require treatment.  
5. Social workers, volunteer companions, and spiritual counselors as referred to in paragraph (3) letter b, letter c, and letter d are obliged to provide services to victims in the form of counseling to strengthen and/or provide a sense of security for victims.  
6. Violations of the provisions as referred to in paragraph (4) and paragraph (5) may be subject to administrative sanctions in the form of:  
a. written warning;  
b. temporary suspension from activities;  
c. termination of cooperation;  
d. revocation of registration certificate;  
e. revocation of operational license;  
f. withdrawal of facilities; and  
g. administrative fine.  
7. Provisions regarding the procedures for implementing administrative sanctions as referred to in paragraph (6) shall be regulated in a Regent Regulation.

# CHAPTER VIII

## INSTITUTIONS

#### Article 39

1. P2TP2A in the implementation of women's protection services.  
2. Women's protection services by P2TP2A as referred to in paragraph (1) involve elements of:  
a. health;  
b. psychology;  
c. law;  
d. clergy;  
e. social; and  
f. economy.  
3. Further provisions regarding the establishment, membership composition, duties, and functions of P2TP2A as referred to in paragraph (1) and paragraph (2) shall be stipulated by Regent Decree.

#### Article 40

1. The Regional Government shall establish a forum for the protection of women victims of violence at every sub-district level.  
2. The forum for the protection of women victims of violence as referred to in paragraph (1) has the duty to:  
a. coordinate and synchronize the handling of protection services for women victims of violence;  
b. facilitate and provide more optimal services and reach the lower level community;  
c. maintain and develop networks and referral systems; and  
d. compile a database regarding the development of protection for women victims of violence.  
3. Further provisions regarding the establishment, membership composition, duties, and functions of the Forum for the Protection of Women Victims of Violence as referred to in paragraph (1) and paragraph (2) shall be stipulated by Regent Decree.

# CHAPTER IX

## SUPERVISION, EVALUATION AND GUIDANCE

## Part One

Supervision

#### Article 41

1. The Regent shall supervise the implementation of policies, programs, and activities for the empowerment and protection of women.  
2. To ensure synergy, continuity and effectiveness in an integrated manner in the implementation of supervision as referred to in paragraph (1), the Regent shall conduct monitoring through the Regional Apparatus handling the field of Women.  
3. Monitoring as referred to in paragraph (2) shall be carried out from planning to implementation of policies, programs and activities for the empowerment and protection of women for the current year.  
4. The results of monitoring as referred to in paragraph (3) shall be reported to the Regent periodically 2 (two) times in 1 (one) year.

## Part Two

Evaluation

#### Article 42

1. The Regional Government shall evaluate the implementation of policies, programs, and activities for the protection of women at the end of each fiscal year.  
2. The results of the evaluation as referred to in paragraph (1) shall be used as input material for the formulation of policies, programs, and activities for the protection of women in the following year.  
3. Evaluation as referred to in paragraph (1) shall be implemented in accordance with the provisions of laws and regulations.

## Part Three

Guidance

#### Article 43

The Regent shall provide guidance on the implementation of policies, programs, and activities for the protection of women to relevant Regional Apparatus and sub-districts.

#### Article 44

Further provisions regarding supervision, evaluation and guidance of policies, programs and activities for the protection of women as referred to in Article 41 through Article 43 shall be regulated by Regent Regulation.

# CHAPTER X

## SOURCES OF FINANCING

#### Article 45

Financing for the implementation of protection of Women shall be sourced from:  
a. regional revenue and expenditure budget; and  
b. other lawful and non-binding sources of funds.

#### Article 46

1. The Regional Government may provide financial assistance to community organizations, social organizations, or non-governmental organizations and universities that carry out protection of women.  
2. Financial assistance as referred to in paragraph (1) shall be provided in accordance with the financial capacity of the Region and implemented in accordance with the provisions of laws and regulations in the field of regional finance.

##### I. GENERAL

Basically, Regions are established to improve public services and realize the welfare of the community, therefore in the implementation of autonomy, regions have obligations including protecting the community, maintaining unity, integrity and national harmony.  

The unequal distribution of opportunities in all areas of life including in the fields of economy, socio-culture, politics and government, law, education, health, employment and social security, which can give rise to various social problems, especially for women. The reality shows that in the Region there are still acts of violence both physically, psychologically, sexually and this constitutes a violation of human rights. Violence against women is a violation of human rights so that their dignity and honor need to be protected and their right to life guaranteed in accordance with their nature and destiny without discrimination. The fate of women victims of violence must be given attention by the regional government. The high number of cases of violence against women are often caused by factors that develop in society, for example low levels of economy, education, environments located in the industrial sector. Therefore, victims of violence such as this need to receive protection in accordance with the principles of justice, truth, legal certainty, equality and upholding human rights.  

The Regional Government is obliged and responsible to carry out efforts to protect women victims of violence through: implementing policies for the protection of women victims of violence applied by the government, establishing policies, programs, and activities for the protection of women victims of violence, establishing P2TP2A, ensuring the implementation of ease of service to victims, seeking effectiveness and efficiency for the victim recovery process, and seeking the creation of cooperation and coordination in victim recovery efforts.
