REGENT OF BANTUL

# REGIONAL REGULATION OF BANTUL REGENCY

NUMBER 15 OF 2013

CONCERNING

PROTECTION OF CHILDREN AND WOMEN VICTIMS OF VIOLENCE

BY THE GRACE OF ALMIGHTY GOD

REGENT OF BANTUL,

Considering:
 
 
 
 
 
 
 
a. that every child and woman has the right to be free from torture or treatment that degrades human dignity and has the right to obtain a sense of security, free from all forms of violence, discrimination and violations of the rights of women and children;
b. that acts of violence against children and women continue to increase, thus requiring comprehensive and maximum protection efforts, concrete actions, formal institutional support, regulations that can guarantee implementation by the Regional Government in increasing broad community participation;
c. that based on the considerations as referred to in letter a, letter b, it is necessary to establish a Regional Regulation concerning Protection of Children and Women Victims of Violence;

# CHAPTER I

## GENERAL PROVISIONS

#### Article 1

In this Regional Regulation, the following terms shall mean:

1. Government is the Central Government.
2. Region is Bantul Regency.
3. Regional Government is the Regent and Regional Apparatus as elements of regional government administration.
4. Regional House of Representatives hereinafter abbreviated as DPRD is the Regional House of Representatives of Bantul Regency as an element of Regional Government administration.
5. Regent is the Regent of Bantul.
6. Regional Apparatus Work Unit hereinafter abbreviated as SKPD is the Regional Apparatus Work Unit of Bantul Regency.
7. Woman is a person who is female by sex and a person who is legally recognized as a woman.
8. Child is a person who has not reached 18 years of age, including a child still in the womb.
9. Violence is any act that results in or may result in misery or suffering whether physical, sexual, economic, social, and psychological, threats of certain actions, coercion or arbitrary deprivation of liberty, whether occurring in public or in private life against victims.
10. Violence against women is any action that results in or may result in misery or suffering of women physically, sexually, economically, socially, psychologically, including threats of certain actions, coercion or deprivation of liberty, whether occurring in public or private life.
11. Violence against children is any action that results in or may result in suffering of children physically, psychologically, sexually, neglect, exploitation, economically, and other violence.
12. Physical Violence is any act that causes pain, injury, wounds or disability to a person's body, miscarriage, fainting and or causes death.
13. Psychological Violence is any act that causes fear, loss of self-confidence, loss of ability to act, feelings of helplessness and or severe psychological suffering to a person.
14. Sexual Violence is any act in the form of sexual harassment, forced sexual intercourse, forced unnatural or unwanted sexual intercourse, forced sexual intercourse with others for commercial purposes and or certain purposes.
15. Economic Violence is any act that neglects family members in the form of not providing a proper living, care or maintenance.
16. Exploitation of Children is any act involving children in activities that may harm their welfare and development or endanger the safety of children with the aim of enabling others to obtain economic, sexual, social, or political benefits, including if it contains restrictions or elimination of opportunities for children to obtain their rights.
17. Sexual harassment is any action with sexual nuances conveyed through physical or non-physical contact that targets sexual body parts or a person's sexuality, including by using whistles, winking, comments or sexually nuanced speech, displaying pornographic materials and sexual desires, pinching or touching body parts, movements or gestures that are sexual in nature resulting in feelings of discomfort, offense, feeling degraded in dignity, and possibly causing health and safety problems.
18. Victim is a Child and Woman who experiences misery and or suffering either directly or indirectly as a result of violence occurring in the area of Bantul Regency.
19. Prevention is a direct effort made by the Regional Government to prevent acts of violence against women and children.
20. Protection is all service actions to guarantee and protect the rights of victims of violence organized by the Integrated Service Center.
21. Protection of women is all activities aimed at providing a sense of security for women carried out by family members, the police, prosecutors, courts, social institutions, or other parties who know or hear that violence against women will or has occurred.
22. Protection of children is all activities to guarantee and protect children and their rights so that they can live, grow, develop and participate optimally in accordance with human dignity and receive protection from violence and discrimination.
23. Service is an action taken as soon as possible to victims when seeing, hearing and knowing that violence against victims will, is or has occurred.
24. Women's Empowerment is any effort to improve physical, mental, spiritual, social abilities, knowledge, and skills so that women are ready to be utilized according to their respective abilities.
25. Companion is a person or representative from an institution who has the expertise to accompany victims to conduct counseling, therapy and advocacy for strengthening and recovery of victims of violence.
26. Spiritual guidance is assistance in the form of services for spiritual strengthening so that sick people can rise to be closer to God so that a sense of calm, security arises, and mental health is achieved.
27. Judicial body is a general court that has the authority to receive, examine, and adjudicate, as well as resolve every case submitted to it, to realize law enforcement and justice.
28. Prosecution is the action of a public prosecutor to transfer a case to the competent District Court in the manner and according to the procedures regulated in the Law with a request to be examined and decided by a judge in a court session.
29. Lawsuit is a notification accompanied by a request by an interested party to a judge to take action according to law against a person who has harmed him civilly.
30. Institution is an agency/office/body/office within the scope of the Regional Government and/or Non-Governmental Organization (NGO) that provides assistance to victims of violence.
31. Integrated Service Center hereinafter abbreviated as PPT is a functional work unit that organizes integrated services for victims of violence based in hospitals, and is managed jointly in the form of medical services (including medico-legal), psychosocial and legal services.
32. Regional Action Plan is the foundation and guideline for related agencies, vertical agencies, and the community, in order to carry out activities for the prevention and protection of children and women victims of violence.
33. Safe House is a shelter for victims, during the assistance process, for the safety and comfort of victims from threats and dangers of perpetrators in accordance with predetermined standards.
34. Social Reintegration is the process of preparing the community and victims that supports the reunification of victims into the family environment, family substitutes who can provide protection and fulfillment of victims' needs.
35. Standard Operating Procedure hereinafter abbreviated as SOP is the standard steps that must be taken in protecting victims starting from complaints/identification, rehabilitation, health, social rehabilitation, legal services up to repatriation and social reintegration of witnesses and/or victims.
36. Family is the smallest unit in society consisting of husband and wife, or husband-wife and their children, or father and his children, or mother and her children, or blood relatives in a straight line upward or downward up to the third degree.
37. Household is husband, wife, and children, people who have family relationships due to blood relations, marriage, breastfeeding, care, and representation, and/or domestic workers and reside in that household.
38. Community is individuals, families, groups, social organizations and/or community organizations.
39. Regional Revenue and Expenditure Budget hereinafter abbreviated as APBD is the Regional Revenue and Expenditure Budget of Bantul Regency.
40. Gender responsive budget is a budget that provides and accommodates justice and equality for women and men to access and obtain access, as well as benefits from development programs and participate in decision-making processes and have control over resources.
41. Forum for the Protection of Victims of Violence hereinafter abbreviated as FPKK is a coordination forum for handling victims of violence against Children and Women whose implementation is carried out in a networked manner.

#### Article 2

The principles of organizing the protection of Children and Women from acts of violence are:  
a. respect for and fulfillment of the rights of victims;  
b. justice and gender equality;  
c. non-discrimination; and  
d. the best empowerment for victims.

#### Article 3

1. The objectives of protecting Children and Women from acts of violence are:  
a. to prevent and eliminate all forms of acts of violence and exploitation against children and women that occur in the family environment and society;  
b. to pursue recovery and social reintegration for children and women victims of acts of violence;  
c. to provide services to children and women victims of acts of violence, reporters, and witnesses;  
d. to carry out empowerment for women victims of violence; and  
e. to increase community participation.  
2. The organization of protection of children and women from acts of violence as referred to in paragraph (1), includes aspects of:  
a. prevention;  
b. services and assistance;  
c. social reintegration; and  
d. empowerment.

# CHAPTER II

## RIGHTS OF VICTIMS

#### Article 4

Every victim of acts of violence shall receive the following rights:  
a. the right to have their dignity respected as a human being;  
b. the right to health and psychological recovery from the suffering experienced by the victim;  
c. the right to determine their own decisions;  
d. the right to obtain information;  
e. the right to confidentiality of their identity;  
f. the right to optimal services;  
g. the right to compensation;  
h. the right to continuous handling up to the social rehabilitation stage;  
i. the right to complaint handling;  
j. the right to obtain facilities in the judicial process;  
k. the right to psychological and legal assistance; and  
l. the right to legal protection and services.

#### Article 5

Child victims of violence, in addition to receiving the rights as referred to in Article 4, shall also receive the following rights:  
a. the right to respect for survival, growth and development;  
b. the right to basic services in the field of education;  
c. the right to basic services in the field of health;  
d. the right to equal protection;  
e. the right to be free from various stigma; and  
f. the right to obtain freedom.

# CHAPTER III

## OBLIGATIONS AND RESPONSIBILITIES

#### Article 6

The obligations and responsibilities in organizing protection for children and women victims of violence are:  
a. Regional Government;  
b. society;  
c. family; and  
d. parents.

#### Article 7

1. To implement the provisions as referred to in Article 6 letter a, the Regional Government shall carry out victim protection efforts, in the form of:  
a. formulating policies on the protection of Children and Women from all forms of acts of violence and discrimination;  
b. preparing program and activity planning;  
c. providing support for facilities and infrastructure;  
d. allocating special gender-responsive budgets;  
e. establishing PPT and/or Safe Houses;  
f. establishing and facilitating FPKK; and  
g. conducting monitoring and evaluation.  
2. In the implementation of the obligations of the Regional Government as referred to in paragraph (1), the Regional Government shall prepare a Regional Action Plan as a basis for SKPD in implementing protection for victims.  
3. The Regional Action Plan as referred to in paragraph (2), constitutes part of the Regional Medium-Term Development Plan (RPJMD).  
4. Further provisions regarding the Regional Action Plan as referred to in paragraph (2), shall be further regulated by Regent Regulation.

#### Article 8

The Regional Government shall organize services for victims in the form of:  
a. facilitating the establishment of PPT;  
b. facilitating PPT facilities and infrastructure according to capacity; and  
c. facilitating FPKK as a network forum for victim handling.

#### Article 9

The Regional Government has the obligation and responsibility to:  
a. supervise the organization of services for victims in accordance with minimum service standards; and  
b. provide funds for victim protection through APBD.

#### Article 10

In implementing the obligations and responsibilities of the Regional Government as referred to in Article 7, Article 8, and Article 9, the Regent shall grant authority to the SKPD that has duties and functions in the field of women's empowerment and child protection.

#### Article 11

The obligations and responsibilities of society toward the protection of children and Women Victims of violence are:  
a. preventing the occurrence of violence against children and women;  
b. providing education on a culture of peace and gender equality;  
c. providing information and/or reporting if acts of violence against children and women occur to law enforcement officials or authorized parties;  
d. protecting victims; and  
e. providing emergency assistance.

#### Article 12

The obligations of family and/or parents as referred to in Article 6 letter c and letter d, legally have full responsibility to prevent all forms of acts of violence and protect children and women as family members.

# CHAPTER IV

## ORGANIZATION OF PROTECTION

## Part One

Institutions

#### Article 13

1. In order to provide services and protection to victims of acts of violence, the Regional Government shall establish PPT.  
2. PPT as referred to in paragraph (1) has the duties, principal tasks, and functions of providing protection to Children and Women from acts of violence, which consist of fields.  
3. PPT as referred to in paragraph (2), shall be stipulated by Regent Decree.  
4. PPT as referred to in paragraph (2) has the duty to provide services and temporary protection in the form of a safe house for victims of violence.  
5. In the event that PPT does not have a safe house (shelter) as referred to in paragraph (3), then victims of violence shall be referred to other institutions.

#### Article 14

1. The organizational structure of PPT established by the Regent as referred to in Article 12 paragraph (1) consists of:  
a. Chairperson or other equivalent designation;  
b. Secretary;  
c. Treasurer; and  
d. Executive Chairperson who oversees fields.  
2. The Fields as referred to in paragraph (1) letter d, shall at least include:  
a. complaint service field;  
b. health service field;  
c. social rehabilitation service field;  
d. repatriation and social reintegration field;  
e. legal aid service field;  
f. spiritual field; and  
g. psychology field

#### Article 15

The complaint service field as referred to in Article 14 paragraph (2) letter a has the duties of:  
a. conducting interviews and observation of the victim's condition;  
b. making recommendations for follow-up services;  
c. conducting coordination and referral to follow-up services and related parties; and  
d. conducting administration of the complaint process.

#### Article 16

The health service field as referred to in Article 14 paragraph (2) letter b has the duties of:  
a. conducting examination, treatment, and follow-up care for victims;  
b. conducting coordination of health rehabilitation implementation and medico-legal;  
c. conducting medico-legal examination including collection of evidence from victims, preparation of visum et repertum and visum et psikiatrikum;  
d. conducting supporting and laboratory examinations of evidence;  
e. conducting consultation with specialist doctors or making referrals; and  
f. preparing case reports.

#### Article 17

The social rehabilitation service field as referred to in Article 14 paragraph (2) letter c has the duties of:  
a. providing assistance during the case handling process;  
b. conducting counseling; and  
c. carrying out social functions.

#### Article 18

The repatriation and social reintegration field as referred to in Article 14 paragraph (2) letter d has the duties of:  
a. conducting coordination with relevant agencies for victim repatriation;  
b. preparing progress reports on the repatriation assistance and social rehabilitation process; and  
c. conducting monitoring for a maximum of 3 (three) months after the victim is returned to their family.

#### Article 19

The field of legal aid services as referred to in Article 14 paragraph (2) letter e has the following duties:  
a. accompanying and defending in every legal process; and  
b. preparing reports on the progress of legal case handling.

#### Article 20

The field of spiritual services as referred to in Article 14 paragraph (2) letter f has the following duties:  
a. providing spiritual guidance to victims;  
b. coordinating with related parties; and  
c. preparing reports and recommendations for follow-up services.

#### Article 21

The field of psychology as referred to in Article 14 paragraph (2) letter g has the following duties:  
a. providing psychological guidance to victims;  
b. preparing recommendations for follow-up services; and  
c. coordinating and making referrals to follow-up services.

#### Article 22

1. PPT shall have at least 5 (five) counselors.  
2. Counselors as referred to in paragraph (1) include:  
a. counselors in the field of law;  
b. counselors in the field of health;  
c. counselors in the field of psychology;  
d. counselors in the field of social services; and  
e. counselors in the field of spiritual services.

#### Article 23

The organizational structure of PPT established by the Regent involves relevant SKPD with community institutions adjusted to needs.

#### Article 24

Further provisions regarding the development, mechanisms and networked working relationships of PPT shall be further regulated by Regent Regulation.

# CHAPTER V

## FORMS AND MECHANISMS OF PROTECTION

## Part One

Prevention

#### Article 25

1. Prevention efforts against violence toward Children and Women shall be carried out in an integrated manner by the Regional Government coordinated by the agency having the principal tasks and functions of coordination in the field of women's empowerment and child protection.  
2. Prevention efforts as referred to in paragraph (1) shall be implemented by:  
a. establishing a working network in efforts to prevent violence and handle acts of violence through coordination, integration, synchronization of violence prevention based on partnership patterns;  
b. establishing a violence prevention system, mapping of locations or areas prone to acts of violence;  
c. organizing communication, information, and critical education about the rights of children and women for the community;  
d. conducting socialization about laws and regulations related to the protection of children and women; and  
e. prevention efforts as referred to in paragraph (1) shall be carried out in an integrated manner coordinated by the SKPD in charge of women's empowerment and child protection.

#### Article 26

Prevention efforts shall also be carried out by:  
a. the community;  
b. families; and  
c. parents.

## Part Two

Services

#### Article 27

1. The provision of services to victims shall be carried out in an integrated manner by PPT.  
2. PPT as referred to in paragraph (1) may receive and send case referrals from or to other service units in a networked manner.

#### Article 28

The provision of services to victims shall be implemented with:  
a. no charges;  
b. speed;  
c. safety and comfort;  
d. empathy;  
e. non-discrimination;  
f. integration;  
g. easy accessibility; and  
h. guaranteed confidentiality.

#### Article 29

Forms of services to victims include:  
a. complaint, consultation, and counseling services;  
b. accompaniment services;  
c. health services;  
d. social rehabilitation services;  
e. legal services;  
f. repatriation and social reintegration services; and  
g. psychological services.

#### Article 30

Complaint, consultation, and counseling services as referred to in Article 29 letter a include:  
a. initial identification or recording of victims; and  
b. consent to take action.

#### Article 31

Accompaniment services as referred to in Article 29 letter b include:  
a. accompanying victims during the examination and health recovery process;  
b. accompanying victims during the medicolegal process;  
c. accompanying victims during the examination process at the Police, Prosecutor's Office and court;  
d. monitoring the interests and rights of victims in the examination process at the Police, Prosecutor's Office and Court;  
e. protecting the privacy and confidentiality of victims from all parties who have no interest, including coverage by mass media;  
f. coordinating with other companions; and  
g. providing continuous handling up to the rehabilitation stage.

#### Article 32

Health services as referred to in Article 29 letter c include:  
a. first aid to victims;  
b. treatment and recovery of physical injuries aimed at restoring the physical condition of victims carried out by medical and paramedical personnel; and  
c. referral to health services.

#### Article 33

Social rehabilitation services as referred to in Article 29 letter d constitute services provided by companions in order to restore the traumatic condition of victims, including the provision of safe houses to protect victims from various threats and intimidation against victims and provide social support so that victims have self-confidence, strength, and independence in resolving their problems, by:  
a. providing spiritual guidance to victims; and  
b. psychological recovery of victims.

#### Article 34

Legal services as referred to in Article 29 letter e to assist victims in undergoing the judicial process by:  
a. providing legal consultation that includes information regarding the rights of victims and the judicial process;  
b. accompanying victims at the investigation, prosecution, and examination levels in court hearings and helping victims to fully describe the violence they experienced; and  
c. coordinating with fellow law enforcers, volunteer companions, and social workers so that the judicial process proceeds as it should.

#### Article 35

1. Repatriation and social reintegration services as referred to in Article 29 letter f aim to return victims to their families and social environment.  
2. Repatriation and social reintegration services as referred to in paragraph (1) shall be carried out by the Regional Government in coordination with:  
a. other Regional Governments; and  
b. related agencies and institutions both governmental and non-governmental.

#### Article 36

Psychological services as referred to in Article 29 letter g aim to:   
a. provide psychological accompaniment and services; and  
b. provide psychological recovery of victims from trauma.

## Part Three

Empowerment

### Paragraph 1

Empowerment of Women Victims of Violence

#### Article 37

Forms of empowerment of women victims of violence include:  
a. job training, including:  
1. skills training;  
2. field work practice; and  
3. apprenticeship.  
b. productive economic enterprises and joint business groups, including:  
1. entrepreneurial skills training;  
2. facilitation of joint business group formation; and  
3. business implementation assistance.  
c. capital assistance, including:  
1. assistance with work facilities and infrastructure; and  
2. facilitation of working capital assistance.

### Paragraph 2

Protection and Fulfillment of Children's Rights

#### Article 38

1. Protection for children in conflict with the law is implemented through:  
a. treatment of children in a humane manner in accordance with the dignity and rights of children;  
b. provision of special accompanying officers for children from an early stage;  
c. provision of special facilities and infrastructure;  
d. imposition of appropriate sanctions for the best interests of the child;  
e. continuous monitoring and recording of the development of children in conflict with the law.  
f. provision of guarantees to maintain relationships with parents or family; and  
g. protection from identity disclosure through mass media and to avoid labeling.  
2. Forms of fulfillment of the rights of child victims of violence include:  
a. rehabilitation efforts both within institutions and outside institutions;  
b. provision of safety guarantees for victim witnesses and expert witnesses, whether physical, mental, or social; and  
c. provision of accessibility to obtain information regarding case developments.  
3. PPT and the community are obligated to fulfill the rights of child victims of violence.

#### Article 39

1. In an effort to guarantee the fulfillment of children's rights, the Regional Government cooperates with the Children's Forum that has been established.  
2. The Children's Forum is an organization whose members are children who are administrators of children's organizations, studios or children's activity groups and the like, which are generally based on the development of talents, interests, abilities and use of free time.  
3. The Children's Forum has the following duties:  
a. conduct socialization about acts of violence to peers;  
b. actively participate in planning policies, programs and activities related to the protection of children from acts of violence;  
c. become a source of information on incidents of violence in their environment; and  
d. consult the needs and desires of child victims of violence with partners from related sectors.

## Part Four

Coordination of Services and Protection for Victims

#### Article 40

1. In an effort to provide and organize handling of services and protection for victims, PPT establishes FPKK.  
2. FPKK as referred to in paragraph (1) aims to:  
a. coordinate and synchronize the handling of PPT services;  
b. maintain and develop networks and referral systems; and  
c. collect, compile and present reports on violence.  
3. The management and membership of FPKK as referred to in paragraph (1) shall be determined by Decree of the Chair of PTT.  
4. The membership of FPKK as referred to in paragraph (3) is grouped into the following roles:  
a. health role;  
b. psychology role;  
c. legal role;  
d. social role; and  
e. economic role.  
5. Further provisions regarding the establishment, main duties, functions and membership of FPKK as referred to in paragraph (1) shall be further regulated by Regent Regulation.

# CHAPTER VI

## COOPERATION AND PARTNERSHIP

## Part One

Cooperation

#### Article 41

1. In order to achieve the objectives of protecting children and women from acts of violence as referred to in Article 3, the Regional Government cooperates with:  
a. the Government;  
b. the Provincial Government;  
c. other Regencies/Cities; and  
d. non-Governmental institutions.  
2. Cooperation as referred to in paragraph (1) includes:  
a. exchange of data and information;  
b. rehabilitation of victims of violence;  
c. repatriation and social reintegration; and  
d. provision of evidence and witnesses, and followed up in accordance with applicable law.  
3. Cooperation as referred to in paragraph (1) and paragraph (2) shall be set forth in the form of a Joint Agreement.

## Part Two

Partnership

#### Article 42

1. The Regional Government establishes partnerships with the business world in the protection of children and women from acts of violence.  
2. Partnership as referred to in paragraph (1) is carried out through:  
a. an information system on employment opportunities for women victims of violence;  
b. education and training for children and women victims of violence;  
c. educational assistance for children and women victims of violence who are at risk of dropping out of school; and  
d. fostering and increasing the economic independence of women victims of violence.  
3. Partnership as referred to in paragraph (1) and paragraph (2) shall be set forth in the form of a joint agreement.

# CHAPTER VII

## GUIDANCE AND SUPERVISION

#### Article 44

1. The Regional Government conducts guidance and supervision of the implementation of PPT Minimum Service Standards.  
2. Guidance and Supervision as referred to in paragraph (1) includes:  
a. coordination;  
b. guidance;  
c. education and training; and  
d. monitoring and evaluation.  
3. Coordination as referred to in paragraph (2) letter a covers aspects related to planning and implementation.  
4. Guidance as referred to in paragraph (2) letter b covers aspects related to planning, implementation, procedures, financing, quality, control and supervision.  
5. Education and training as referred to in paragraph (2) letter c shall be conducted by the SKPD in charge of women's empowerment and child protection.  
6. Monitoring and evaluation as referred to in paragraph (2) letter d shall be conducted periodically.

# CHAPTER VIII

## REPORTING

#### Article 43

1. PPT and FPKK prepare reports on the protection of Children and Women victims of violence to the Regent.  
2. Reporting as referred to in paragraph (1) shall be submitted in writing, covering:  
a. administration;  
b. finance;  
c. services; and  
d. performance.  
3. Submission of written reports as referred to in paragraph (2) shall be carried out at least once every 3 (three) months and shall be conducted in accordance with the provisions of laws and regulations.

# CHAPTER IX

## FUNDING

#### Article 45

1. Funding is provided by the Regional Government for activities to protect victims of acts of violence and is charged to the APBD.  
2. Management of funding allocation as referred to in paragraph (1) shall be adjusted to the needs of PPT by coordinating between SKPDs.

# CHAPTER X

## COMMUNITY PARTICIPATION

#### Article 46

1. The community participates in the implementation of protection for victims of violence.  
2. Community participation as referred to in paragraph (1) shall be in the form of:  
a. establishing family partners at the sub-district/village level by the community;  
b. establishing Child and Women protection units within community organizations;  
c. conducting independent socialization of the rights of Children and Women;  
d. providing first aid to victims; and  
e. reporting to the competent authorities if violence against victims occurs in their environment.  
3. Community participation as referred to in paragraph (1) shall be carried out by individuals, social community institutions, non-governmental organizations, educational institutions, religious institutions, the private sector, and mass media.

# CHAPTER XI

## CLOSING PROVISIONS

# ELUCIDATION OF REGIONAL REGULATION OF BANTUL REGENCY  

NUMBER 15 YEAR 2013 CONCERNING PROTECTION OF CHILDREN AND WOMEN VICTIMS OF VIOLENCE

##### I. GENERAL

One of the objectives of establishing a state is to provide protection to every citizen with a sense of security and comfort, this is explicitly stated in the Preamble of the 1945 Constitution of the Republic of Indonesia, Fourth Paragraph. It is stated that the national objective of the Indonesian nation is to protect all Indonesian people and the entire Indonesian homeland, to advance public welfare, to educate the life of the nation and to participate in implementing world order based on independence, lasting peace and social justice.

To achieve this, sustainable development efforts have been carried out which constitute a comprehensive, directed and integrated series of development, including development in promoting gender equality and women's empowerment. This is as affirmed in the Millennium Development Goals (MDGs). The implementation of gender equality and women's empowerment has become a priority that has always been improved from year to year, especially since 2000, when Presidential Instruction Number 9 of 2000 concerning Gender Mainstreaming (PUG) was issued at the urging of the State Minister for Women's Empowerment (Meneg PP) at that time.

This Presidential Instruction affirms that all Ministries and non-Ministerial government institutions in the National Government, Provinces, and Regencies/Cities must carry out gender mainstreaming in planning, implementation, monitoring, and evaluation of development policies and programs. This is a manifestation of efforts to fulfill the human rights of every Indonesian citizen, also intended so that gender equality and women's empowerment in Indonesia will improve.

Indonesia as a country that has ratified the International Convention, namely the Convention on Women; Convention on the Elimination of All Forms of Discrimination Against Women (CEDAW) in the form of Law Number 7 of 1984 concerning Ratification of the Convention on the Elimination of All Forms of Discrimination Against Women, has thereby bound itself to guarantee through laws and regulations, policies, programs and temporary special measures (affirmative action), to realize equality and justice between women and men, and the elimination of all forms of discrimination against women, including acts of violence against women and children.

It is a breath of fresh air for Indonesian women, when clearly explained in the Convention on Women which establishes principles and provisions to eliminate gaps, subordination and actions that violate women's rights and harm the position of women in law, family and society. These principles are clearly set forth in the substantive articles of the Convention on Women (articles 1-16).

Through Law Number 23 of 2002 concerning Child Protection, the Government recognizes the existence of children's rights. The inherent human rights of children include basic rights as human beings, namely the Right to Life, the Right to Growth and Development, the Right to Protection and the Right to Participation.

To fulfill the Right to life, children need food in sufficient quantities, healthy and nutritious, as well as optimal health access. Protection from violence that threatens their safety and health must be provided from an early age. Protection is needed to prevent physical, mental and sexual violence and exploitation. To fulfill the right to growth and development, children need space to play, exercise, education that is appropriate to their physical and mental development. In fulfilling children's rights, every government administrator, community and parents are obliged to understand and care about children's rights. The existence of legal instruments and law enforcement officials who defend the interests of children is needed for this protection effort. Laws and regulations that are principled in defending the best interests of children are needed to realize protection that is legal in nature. Furthermore, it is not uncommon to find cases of women and children who experience problems of physical, psychological, sexual and social violence. For example, women and children victims of persecution, neglect, eviction and mistreatment by parents or others. For women and children who experience these problems, the government must provide a Safe House or Integrated Service Center (PPT) for women and children victims of violence. The Safe House or PPT is a safe and friendly place for women and children. The PPT is equipped with companions from educators, doctors, psychologists, clerics/religious leaders and networked with lawyers for women and children as well as the RPK Unit at the Police.

The fate of women and children victims of violence must be given attention by the government. The large number of cases of violence against women and children are often caused by factors that develop in society, such as low levels of economy, education, environments in the industrial sector. Therefore, victims of violence like this need to receive protection in accordance with the principles of justice, truth, legal certainty, equality and upholding human rights.

For the empowerment of victims of violence, local governments can establish an Integrated Service Center and Violence Victim Protection Forum (FPKK) as a center for empowering women victims of violence which specifically has the main duties and functions for empowerment of children and women victims of violence.

From the above framework, the Government is responsible for taking actions both legally, politically, economically and socially to prevent, suppress, reduce, and eliminate all forms of violence against women and children. From these various efforts, a legal framework will be realized in the implementation of protection for children and women from violence.

##### II. ARTICLE BY ARTICLE

#### Article 1

Sufficiently clear.

#### Article 2

Letter a  
What is meant by "respect and fulfillment of victims' rights" is a series of actions to respect, appreciate and ensure the fulfillment of victims' rights.

Letter b  
What is meant by "justice and gender equality" is a process to be fair to men and women and equal conditions for men and women to obtain opportunities and their rights as human beings, so that they are able to play a role and participate in political, economic, socio-cultural activities, National defense and security and equality in enjoying the results of such development.

Letter c  
What is meant by "non-discrimination" is an attitude and treatment towards victims by not making distinctions on the basis of age, sex, race, ethnicity, religion and between groups.

Letter d  
What is meant by "the best empowerment for victims" is all recovery, rehabilitation, reunification actions concerning victims carried out by the government, community, legislative bodies and judicial bodies, must be the main consideration.

#### Article 3

Paragraph (1)  
Sufficiently clear.  

Paragraph (2)  
Point a  
What is meant by the "prevention" aspect is a protection strategy effort through:  
a. Primary prevention, all people, families, communities and the state in efforts to increase knowledge capacity, understanding and ensure that violence against women and children does not occur, including policy socialization, adequate services, supportive workplace policies, and life skills training for women and children. What is meant by life skills training includes conflict resolution without violence, stress management skills, resource management, effective decision making, effective interpersonal communication, guidance on the psychosocial development of women and children.  
b. Secondary prevention, aimed at high-risk community groups in efforts to increase skills, including training and victim services to ensure that violence against women and children does not occur in the next generation. Activities carried out here include conducting home visits for parents who have just had children to conduct self-assessment of whether they are at risk of committing violence against children in the future.  
c. Tertiary prevention, intended to increase parenting capacity that ensures violence against women and children is not repeated, what is done here is integrated services for women and children who are victims of violence, through counseling, stress management training.  

Point b  
What is meant by the "service and assistance" aspect is immediate activities and actions carried out by professionals and assistants in accordance with their respective professions in the form of counseling, therapy and advocacy for the strengthening and recovery of victims of violence.  

Point c  
What is meant by the "empowerment" aspect is the process of increasing the role of the community to participate in the implementation of protection for women and children victims of violence which aims to obtain access and participation in the process of protection of women and children broadly.

#### Article 4

Point a  
What is meant by "the right to have one's dignity respected as a human being" is upholding human rights.  

Point b  
What is meant by "health and psychological recovery from the suffering experienced by the victim" is assistance provided by health experts (doctors) for physical disorders suffered and from psychologists to victims suffering from trauma/other mental problems to restore the victim's mental condition.  

Point c  
Sufficiently clear.  

Point d  
What is meant by "obtaining information" is access and information about the location of complaint centers, PPT, and all matters related to the fulfillment of their rights and involvement in the decision-making process related to assistance and case developments containing values, meanings, and messages, whether data, facts or explanations that can be seen, heard, and read presented in various packages and formats in accordance with the development of information and communication technology electronically or non-electronically related to acts of violence.  

Point e  
What is meant by "confidentiality of identity" is an effort to guarantee certainty for victims that their identity, medical care and legal handling will not be disseminated.  

Point f  
What is meant by "optimal service" is service that includes medical, extensive medicolegal, psychosocial and legal.  

Point g  
What is meant by "the right to compensation" includes: economic empowerment, repatriation costs, health insurance, and education or skills.  

Point h  
What is meant by "continuous handling up to the rehabilitation stage" is handling that does not stop at physical and psychological healing, legal assistance to restore civil rights, but until the victim can resume life in society including restoration of good name and citizenship.  

Point i  
What is meant by "the right to complaint handling" is the availability of a special integrated service unit by officers.  

Point j  
Sufficiently clear.  

Point k  
What is meant by "the right to assistance" includes: psychologists, psychiatrists, health experts, religious leaders, advocates, and family members. Meanwhile, legal assistance is assistance provided by persons and/or legal aid institutions to victims at every level of examination and during the legal process.
