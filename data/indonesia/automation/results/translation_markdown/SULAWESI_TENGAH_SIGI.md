# CHAPTER I

## GENERAL PROVISIONS

#### Article 1

In this Regional Regulation, the following terms shall mean:  
1. Region is Sigi Regency.  
2. Regional Government is the Regent as an element of the Regional Government administration who leads the implementation of governmental affairs that are the authority of the autonomous Region.  
3. Regent is the Regent of Sigi.  
4. Regional House of Representatives, hereinafter abbreviated as DPRD, is the people's representative institution of the Sigi Regency Region as an element of the Regional government administration.  
5. Regional Work Unit, hereinafter abbreviated as SKPD, is the Regional Work Unit of Sigi Regency.  
6. Child is a person who has not yet reached 18 years of age, including a child in the womb.  
7. Woman is an adult human being of female gender and a person recognized by law as a woman.  
8. Prevention is an activity carried out to prevent the occurrence of acts of violence against women and children.  
9. Protection is all service actions to guarantee and protect the rights of victims of acts of violence organized by the Integrated Service Center.  
10. Violence is any act that results in or causes misery and suffering whether physical, sexual, psychological including neglect, threats of certain actions, coercion or arbitrary deprivation of freedom, whether occurring in public or in private life.  
11. Physical violence is any act that causes pain, injury, wounds or disability to a person's body, miscarriage or unconsciousness and/or causes death.  
12. Psychological violence is an act that causes fear, loss of self-confidence, loss of ability to act, distrust or severe psychological suffering to a person.  
13. Sexual violence is any act in the form of sexual harassment, forced sexual intercourse, whether unnatural or unwanted with another person for commercial purposes and/or certain purposes.  
14. Victim is a woman and child who experiences acts of violence. 15. Victim of acts of violence is an individual or group of people who experience physical, mental or emotional suffering, economic loss, or experience neglect, reduction or deprivation of their basic rights, as a result of violations of Human Rights (HAM), or criminal acts as well as acts of violence committed either by state apparatus or by the state or Regional Government apparatus or by individuals.  
16. Service is an activity and immediate action carried out by professional personnel in accordance with their respective professions in the form of counseling, therapy and advocacy for the empowerment and recovery of victims of violence.  
17. Assistance is an activity and action carried out by an assistant during the service process.  
18. Assistant is a person or representative of an institution who has the expertise to provide assistance.  
19. Repatriation is an effort to return victims of acts of violence to their family, substitute family, or community that can provide protection and fulfillment of their needs.  
20. Rehabilitation is the recovery of victims from psychosocial disorders and the restoration of normal social functioning, both in the family and in society.  
21. Social Reintegration is an effort to reunite victims with their family, community, institution, or other social environment that can provide protection.  
22. Institution is an agency/office/body/office within the scope of regional government and/or non-governmental organization (NGO) that provides assistance.  
23. Integrated Service Center, hereinafter abbreviated as PPT, is an institution providing services to victims of violence, based in a hospital, managed jointly in the form of medical services (including medico-legal), psychosocial and legal services.  
24. Integrated Service Center for Women and Children Empowerment, hereinafter abbreviated as P2TP2A, is an integrated service unit established by the Regional Government to provide services in the context of empowerment of women and children victims of acts of violence.  
25. Regional Action Plan is a foundation and guideline for related agencies, vertical agencies, and the community, in the context of implementing prevention and protection activities for women and children victims of acts of violence.  
26. Safe House is a temporary residence, provided to give protection to victims in accordance with predetermined standards.  
27. Community is individuals, families, groups, social organizations and/or community organizations.

# CHAPTER II

## PRINCIPLES AND OBJECTIVES

#### Article 2

The implementation of protection for women and children victims of violence is carried out based on the principles of:  
a. humanity, including respect for the rights of women and children, protection and security for victims, best, affordable and fast service, sensitivity and empathy, maintaining victim confidentiality;  
b. justice, including equality, legal certainty, presumption of innocence, equal rights before the law, best interests of victims, not blaming victims;  
c. transparency, accountability, and participatory;  
d. tolerance and non-discrimination in differences of race, between groups, gender, religion, ethnicity, age, economic background and social status.

#### Article 3

1. The objectives of implementing protection for women and children victims of violence are to:  
a. prevent the occurrence of all forms of violence against women and children;  
b. provide initial handling of victims;  
c. provide health services for victims both physically and psychologically;  
d. provide guarantees of protection, security, and legal certainty to women and children victims of violence, witnesses, and reporters;  
e. seek victim recovery and rehabilitation;  
f. empower victims and reintegration;  
g. conduct coordination and evaluation of handling violence against women and children.  
2. Violence as referred to in paragraph (1) may take the form of:  
a. physical violence;  
b. psychological violence;  
c. sexual violence;  
d. economic neglect; and  
e. restriction of public access.

# CHAPTER III

## RIGHTS OF WOMEN AND CHILDREN VICTIMS OF VIOLENCE

#### Article 4

Women and children victims of acts of violence receive the following rights:  
a. the right to have their dignity respected as human beings;  
b. the right to fast and affordable complaint handling and service.  
c. the right to determine their own decisions;  
d. the right to obtain information;  
e. the right to confidentiality;  
f. the right to compensation;  
g. the right of victims and their families to obtain facilities in the judicial process;  
h. the right to assistance;  
i. the right to health and psychological recovery from the suffering experienced by victims; and. 
j. the right to rehabilitation and social reintegration.

#### Article 5

Child victims of acts of violence in addition to receiving the rights as referred to in Article 4, also receive special rights, as follows:  
a. the right to obtain respect and services for survival, growth and development;  
b. the right to basic services;  
c. the right to be free from negative stigma; and  
d. the right to obtain freedom to determine choices in deciding their own affairs after parental divorce.

# CHAPTER IV

## OBLIGATIONS AND RESPONSIBILITIES

#### Article 6

Obligations and responsibilities in providing protection to women and children from acts of violence are the joint responsibility of:  
a. Regional Government;  
b. community.

#### Article 7

1. The obligations and responsibilities of Regional Government as referred to in Article 6 letter a, include:  
a. establishing policies, programs, and activities for the protection and recovery of women and children from acts of violence;  
b. implementing policies for the protection and recovery of women and children from acts of violence established by the government;  
c. conducting cooperation in the implementation of protection and recovery of women and children from acts of violence;  
d. providing support for facilities and infrastructure for the implementation of protection and recovery of women and children from acts of violence;  
e. allocating budget for the implementation of protection of women and children from acts of violence in accordance with the financial capacity of the Region; and  
f. fostering, supervising, evaluating the implementation of protection and recovery of women and children from acts of violence.  
2. In order to carry out the obligations and responsibilities as referred to in paragraph (1), the Regent establishes a Regional action plan as a basis for SKPD and UKPD.  
3. The Regional action plan as referred to in paragraph (2) is prepared by involving non-governmental organizations and parties that are concerned and carry out assistance work for women and children, with a term of duty of 3 (three) years.  
4. Implementation and technical instructions for the Regional action plan as referred to in paragraph (2) are regulated by Regent Regulation.

#### Article 8

The obligations and responsibilities of the community as referred to in Article 6 letter b, include prevention, handling and recovery actions for women and children victims of acts of violence.

# CHAPTER V

## COMMUNITY PARTICIPATION

#### Article 9

1. In implementing protection for women and children victims of violence, the community may:  
a. form family partners at the kelurahan/village level by the community;  
b. form women and children protection units within community organizations;  
c. conduct socialization of women's and children's rights independently;  
d. provide first aid to victims; and  
e. report to the competent authority if violence against victims occurs in their environment.  
2. Community participation as referred to in paragraph (1) is carried out by individuals, social community institutions, non-governmental organizations, educational institutions, religious institutions, the private sector, and mass media.

# CHAPTER VI

## PREVENTION OF ACTS OF VIOLENCE

#### Article 10

1. To prevent the occurrence of acts of violence against women and children, the Regional Government conducts empowerment and awareness to families, parents, and the community by providing information, counseling and/or guidance.  
2. In addition to empowerment and awareness as referred to in paragraph (1), the Regional Government undertakes efforts as follows:  
a. increasing the quantity and quality of education both formal, non-formal and informal;  
b. increasing accessibility to obtain education, training, funding, income enhancement, and social services;  
c. opening employment opportunities for women;  
d. building community participation and concern for the prevention of protection of women and children from acts of violence;  
e. building and providing a complete and easily accessible information system;  
f. building networks and cooperation with law enforcement apparatus, government apparatus, universities and various non-governmental organizations that work and/or care about women and children; and  
g. opening complaint posts for the protection of women and children from acts of violence based at the sub-district level down to the village.

#### Article 11

Prevention actions for acts of violence against women and children implemented by the community may take the form of:  
a. actively participating in programs and activities initiated by the Government, Regional Government, or other parties as long as they do not conflict with the principles of protection for women and children established in this Regional Regulation;  
b. activities initiated by the community as long as they do not conflict with the principles of protection for women and children established in this Regional Regulation.

#### Article 12

1. Prevention of the occurrence of acts of violence against women and children as referred to in Article 9, is implemented by SKPD and UPTD whose duties and functions are in the fields of:  
a. social affairs;  
b. health;  
c. education;  
d. manpower;  
e. women's empowerment and child protection;  
f. mental and spiritual; and  
g. peace and order.  
2. Prevention of acts of violence by SKPD and UPTD as referred to in paragraph (1), is implemented in an integrated and sustainable manner based on the technical instructions of the Regional Action Plan.

# CHAPTER VII

## SERVICES FOR VICTIMS OF ACTS OF VIOLENCE

#### Article 13

1. Forms of services provided to women and children victims of acts of violence, are as follows:  
a. complaint services;  
b. health services;  
c. legal aid services;  
d. repatriation services;  
e. psychological services; and  
f. social rehabilitation and reintegration services.  
2. Forms of services as referred to in paragraph (1), are implemented in accordance with Minimum Service Standards (SPM) established by the Government and Regional Government and implemented by SKPD and UPTD whose duties and functions are in the fields of:  
a. social affairs;  
b. health;  
c. women's empowerment and child protection; and  
d. mental and spiritual.  
3. In carrying out the duties and functions as referred to in paragraph (1), the Regional Government cooperates with Government agencies, other Regional Governments, and the community.

#### Article 14

1. In order to maximize the role of the Regional Government in implementing the service function for women and children victims of acts of violence, a safe house is established.  
2. Further provisions regarding safe house services as referred to in paragraph (1) are regulated by Regent Regulation.

# CHAPTER VIII

THE ROLE OF CUSTOMARY INSTITUTIONS IN THE PROTECTION OF WOMEN AND CHILDREN VICTIMS OF ACTS OF VIOLENCE

#### Article 15

1. Customary institutions that provide services for the settlement of cases of violence against women and children victims of acts of violence may carry out their functions in accordance with applicable customary provisions as long as they do not conflict with the principles and objectives established in this Regional Regulation.  
2. Certain cases that have consequences for criminal acts based on positive law related to the service function of customary institutions as referred to in paragraph (1) are transferred to the competent authority.  
3. Decisions and sanctions regarding cases of violence against women and children other than the provisions regulated in paragraph (2) that are produced through customary courts may be implemented in accordance with local customary provisions.

# CHAPTER IX

## INSTITUTIONAL ARRANGEMENTS

#### Article 16

1. In order to maximize protection services for women and children from acts of violence, the Regional Government through P2TP2A establishes an institutional system and working procedures that ensure integration, cooperation and sustainability in its programs and activities involving relevant parties in the Region.  
2. Further provisions regarding the organization and working procedures of P2TP2A as referred to in paragraph (1), are established by Regent Regulation.

##### I. GENERAL

Various acts of violence against women and children that occur within the household as well as outside the household will destroy the life of families, communities, government, nation, and state. Therefore the Regional Government of Sigi Regency is obligated to provide protection for women and children victims of violence which is an implementation of several laws and regulations, including Law Number 7 of 1984 concerning Ratification of the Convention on the Elimination of All Forms of Discrimination Against Women, Law Number 39 of 1999 concerning Human Rights, Law Number 23 of 2002 concerning Child Protection, Law Number 23 of 2004 concerning Elimination of Domestic Violence, and Law Number 21 of 2007 concerning Eradication of the Criminal Act of Trafficking in Persons.

Protection of Women and Children victims of violence is also an obligation as regulated in Law Number 23 of 2014 concerning Regional Government. On that basis, the Government of Sigi Regency has made various efforts to provide Protection to Women and Children Victims of Violence, however this is still far from expectations, due to the absence of continuous work synergy from the concerned parties, including the still very minimal understanding of the community caused by various things, among others the still strong patriarchal culture and entrenched poverty, handling by parties that have not been well coordinated. Therefore the Regional Regulation concerning Protection of Women and Children Victims of Violence is enacted to provide legal certainty and protection for victims.

The Regional Regulation concerning Protection of Women and Children Victims of Violence, gives responsibility to the Regional Government starting from efforts to prevent various forms of violence to handling for victims of violence. In its implementation, the Regional Government cooperates with the Government and Provincial Government, other Regional/city Governments, Community Organizations, Non-Governmental Organizations, Academic Parties, Journalist Parties and other stakeholders who are concerned and agree with the ideals of realizing a peaceful world without violence against women and children.

#### Article 2

Letter a What is meant by the principle of humanity is an existential state of humans as living beings with reason who are endowed with natural rights that are innate which must be recognized, appreciated, and respected by every person, including specific aspects attached to women according to their gender, which in the process of implementing protection of women and children victims of acts of violence must be respected and fulfilled by the state, among others including women's and children's human rights, protection and sense of security as victims of acts of violence, obtaining the best, affordable and fast service, sensitivity and empathy, and maintained confidentiality as victims of acts of violence.

Letter b What is meant by the principle of justice, is a mental state of every human being who naturally/innately wants to obtain a sense of justice as part of society and/or fulfilled sense of justice for every person in their position as citizens. In relation to protection of women and children victims of acts of violence whose implementation is regulated through this regional regulation, must fulfill the principle of justice at least including gender equality, legal certainty, presumption of innocence, equality of rights before the law, not blaming the victim, best interests for the victim. The meaning of best interests for the victim, is that in the process of handling cases experienced by victims, all parties involved in it, whether government, community, legislative, executive, judicial bodies must place the interests of victims as the most important in all situations.

#### Article 3

Paragraph (1) Point a Sufficiently Clear Point b What is meant by exploitation is an action with or without the victim's consent which includes prostitution, forced labor or service, slavery or practices similar to slavery, oppression, extortion, physical exploitation of sexual reproductive organs or unlawfully transferring/utilizing labor or transplanting organs and or body tissue or utilizing the labor or ability of a person by another party to obtain benefits both material and immaterial.

Point c
Sufficiently clear.

Point d What is meant by witness is a person who sees, hears and experiences events of violence and human rights violations Point e Mediation is conducted for cases of domestic violence that fall into the category of complaint offenses as regulated in Law Number 23 of 2004 concerning the Elimination of Domestic Violence.

Paragraph (2) Point a What is meant by "physical violence" is an act that results in pain, illness, or serious injury such as slapping, hitting, spitting, pulling hair/grabbing, kicking, burning with hot objects, hitting/injuring with weapons, and/or resulting in disability to a person's body, miscarriage, fainting and/or causing death.

Point b What is meant by "psychological violence" is an act that results in fear, loss of self-confidence, loss of ability to act, feelings of helplessness, and/or severe psychological suffering to a person. Psychological violence can be in the form of emotional abuse such as insults, comments that are hurtful or degrading to self-esteem, isolating the wife from the outside world, threatening or frightening as a means of imposing one's will.

Point c What is meant by "sexual violence" is any act that violates a person's human dignity based on gender discrimination that targets a person's body and sexuality, which results in or may result in physical, psychological, economic, sexual, political and/or social harm or suffering to the victim. This formulation is the essence of the findings of 15 forms of sexual violence, including: 1) Rape; An attack in the form of forced sexual intercourse using the penis toward the victim's vagina, anus or mouth. It can also use fingers or other objects. The attack is carried out with violence, threat of violence, detention, psychological pressure, abuse of power, or by taking advantage of a coercive environment. Indecent assault is another term for rape known in the Indonesian legal system. This term is used when rape is committed outside of forced penetration of the penis into the vagina and when sexual intercourse occurs with a person who is not yet able to give full consent, for example against a child or someone under 18 years of age.

Sexual violence also includes isolating (distancing) the wife from her spiritual needs, forcing sexual intercourse, imposing one's own sexual preferences or not paying attention to the wife's satisfaction. Sexual violence can also take the form of forced sexual intercourse committed against a person residing within the household, forced sexual intercourse against one person within the household with another person for commercial purposes and/or certain purposes.

Point d What is meant by "economic neglect" is neglecting the economic needs of the wife or children or family members or relatives such as not providing for the wife, even spending the wife's money, not providing or withholding the wages of domestic workers or drivers and includes acts of restricting and/or prohibiting family members from working properly.

Point e What is meant by "restriction of movement" is restricting the movement of the wife or children in developing their potential both for economic and social purposes such as prohibiting them from gathering and socializing in the community and restricting the movement of relatives such as preventing domestic workers or drivers from communicating or having contact with their families.

#### Article 4

Point d What is meant by the right to information is the right to obtain information, statements, ideas, and signs containing value, meaning, and messages, whether data, facts or explanations that can be seen, heard, and read presented in various packages and formats in accordance with the development of information and communication technology both electronically and non-electronically related to acts of violence.

Point e
Sufficiently clear.

Point f What is meant by the right to compensation includes economic empowerment, repatriation costs, health insurance, and education or skills.

Sufficiently Clear.

Point h What is meant by the right to assistance includes psychologists, psychiatrists, health experts, clergy, advocates, and family members. An assistant is an individual who works as a volunteer to provide protection and support to women and children victims of violence during the judicial process, these assistants can come from family members, friends or independent organizations that provide attention to witnesses and victims or advocates.

Point i What is meant by the victim's right to rehabilitation includes: access to medical services for physical and psychological recovery, legal assistance to restore civil rights

Point a
Sufficiently clear.

Point b The basic rights referred to include the right to education and access to parents during the handling process Point c What is meant by negative stigma is negative labeling (naming, symbolizing) by the public or society on the victim which can result in the victim's alienation in their own community, including other mental pressures.

Point d What is meant by freedom to determine choices after parental divorce is the legal consequence of the dissolution of marriage due to divorce. Based on the provisions in Article 41 of Law Number 1 of 1974 concerning Marriage ("Marriage Law") it is stated that the consequences of the dissolution of a marriage due to divorce are:
a. Both mother or father remain obligated to maintain and educate their children, solely based on the interests of the child, if there is a dispute regarding custody of the children, the Court gives its decision. b. The father is responsible for all maintenance and education costs required by the child, if the father in reality cannot fulfill such obligation the court may determine that the mother shares in such costs. c. The Court may require the former husband to provide living expenses and/or determine a certain obligation for the former wife.

The only rule that clearly and firmly provides guidance for judges in deciding the granting of custody of the child is contained in Article 105 of the Compilation of Islamic Law (KHI) which states: "In the event of divorce:
a. custody of a child who is not yet mumayyiz or not yet 12 years old is the mother's right. b. custody of a child who is already mumayyiz is given to the child to choose between the father or mother as the custodian. c. maintenance costs are borne by the father."
