REGENT OF TANAH BUMBU,

Considering : a. that Women and Children have the right to obtain protection from torture, threats, pressure, as well as the right to obtain equal treatment and opportunities to obtain justice and welfare in life in accordance with the mandate of the 1945 Constitution of the Republic of Indonesia;

b. that Women Empowerment and protecting the rights of Children is the obligation of the Regional Government and the Community in the Region;

c. that based on the provisions of Article 12 paragraph (2) letter b and letter H Division of Government Affairs in the Field of Women Empowerment and Child Protection Appendix to Law Number 23 of 2014 concerning Regional Government as has been amended several times most recently by Law Number 6 of 2023 concerning Stipulation of Government Regulation in Lieu of Law Number 2 of 2022 concerning Job Creation into Law, the Regional Government has the authority to implement Women Empowerment and

Child Protection in the Region;

d. that based on the considerations as referred to in letter a, letter b, and letter c, it is necessary to stipulate a Regional Regulation concerning Women Empowerment and Child Protection;

CHAPTER I

GENERAL PROVISIONS

Article 1

In this Regional Regulation, what is meant by:

1. Region is Tanah Bumbu Regency.
2. Regional Government is the head of the Region as an element of the Regional Government administration that leads the implementation of governmental affairs which are the authority of the autonomous Region.
3. Head of Region hereinafter referred to as Regent is the Regent of Tanah Bumbu Regency.
4. Regional House of Representatives hereinafter abbreviated as DPRD is the regional people's representative institution of Tanah Bumbu Regency.
5. Office is a Regional Agency that administers governmental affairs in the field of Women's Empowerment and Child Protection.
6. Regional Agency is an auxiliary element of the Regent and DPRD in the administration of governmental affairs which are the authority of the Region.
7. Women's Empowerment is an effort to obtain access and control over resources, economy, politics, social, culture, so that Women can organize themselves and increase self-confidence to be able to play a role and participate actively in solving problems, thereby being able to build capacity and self-concept.
8. Gender Mainstreaming is a strategy built to integrate gender into male and female. 9. Woman is an adult human of female sex and a person who is recognized by law as a Woman.
10. Child is a person who has not reached 18 (eighteen) years of age including a Child who is still in the womb. 11. Child in conflict with the law (ABH) is a Child in conflict with the law, a Child who is a victim of a criminal act and a Child who is a witness to a criminal act. 12. Violence is any act that results in suffering or hardship physically, psychologically, sexually, and/or neglect, including threats to commit acts, coercion or deprivation of liberty unlawfully.
13. Women's Protection is all efforts aimed at protecting Women and providing a sense of security in the fulfillment of their rights by providing consistent and systematic attention aimed at achieving gender equality.
14. Child Protection is all activities to guarantee and protect Children and their rights in order to live, grow, develop, and participate optimally in accordance with human dignity and worth, as well as receive protection from Violence and discrimination. 15. Special Protection is a form of protection received by Children in certain situations and conditions to obtain a guarantee of security.
16. Toddler is a Child aged 0 (zero) to 5 (five) years, in the early stages of human development.
17. School-Age Child is a Child aged 6 (six) to 18 (eighteen) years.
18. Neglected Child is a Child whose needs for mental and religious guidance as well as clothing, food, shelter, health, education, physical, and social services are not adequately met.
19. Child who becomes a victim of acts of Violence is a Child who experiences maltreatment such as being abused, humiliated, which endangers the Child physically, mentally and socially. 20. Child maltreatment is all forms of violence against children committed by those who should be responsible and/or those who have power over children, who should be trusted namely parents, close relatives, teachers, supervisors, law enforcement officers, caregivers and companions (World Health Organization).
21. Child in emergency situations is a Child who is in a situation and condition that endangers himself such as a Child victim of riots, a Child who becomes a refugee, a Child victim of natural disasters and a Child in armed conflict.
22. Child in conflict with the law is a Child in conflict with the law, a Child victim of a criminal act, and a Child who is a witness to a criminal act.
23. Child of isolated indigenous communities is a Child who lives in a situation of isolation where they cannot access basic needs.
24. Child who is economically exploited is a Child who is forced and/or deceived to be employed by parents or others with or without being paid.
25. Child who is sexually exploited is the use of a Child for sexual purposes with cash or other forms of compensation between the Child, buyer of sexual services, intermediary or agent and other parties who profit from the sexual trade of the Child.
26. Child who becomes a victim of Napza abuse is a Child who suffers from dependence on Napza caused by Napza abuse, either by their own will or due to encouragement or coercion by others. 27. Child with special needs is a Child who experiences physical and/or mental obstacles that interfere with their growth and development in accordance with the stages of Child growth and development.
28. Child victim of maltreatment is a Child who receives treatment that is not in accordance with the rights of the Child.
29. Village is a legal community unit that has territorial boundaries with authority to regulate and manage Government affairs, the interests of the local Community, based on Community initiative, rights of origin, and/or traditional rights that are recognized and respected in the Government system of the Unitary State of the Republic of Indonesia.
30. Child in need of Special Protection is a Child in emergency situations, Child in conflict with the law, Child from isolated indigenous communities, Child who is economically and/or sexually exploited, Child victim of narcotics, alcohol, psychotropic and other active substances (Napza) abuse, Child victim of kidnapping, sale and trafficking, Child victim of physical and/or psychological Violence, Child with special needs, and Child victim of maltreatment and neglect.
31. Child Trafficking is a criminal act or deed that meets one or more elements of recruitment, transportation, transfer of a Child using Violence or threat of violence, fraud, kidnapping, confinement, abuse of power, exploitation of a position of vulnerability or debt bondage for the purpose and/or resulting in the exploitation of the Child.
32. Child Rights are part of human rights that must be guaranteed, protected, and fulfilled by parents, family, Community, government/Regional Government, and the state.
33. Child Obligations are everything that must be carried out by a Child in accordance with the function and role of the Child.
34. Social Organization hereinafter referred to as Orsos is an institution/foundation/social association formed by the Community whether legal entity or non-legal entity that functions as a means of Community participation in implementing Social Welfare Enterprises (UKS).
35. Non-Governmental Organization hereinafter abbreviated as LSM is an organization/institution formed by members of the Indonesian citizen Community, voluntarily of their own accord and interested and engaged in the field of social welfare business determined by the organization/institution, as a manifestation of Community participation in efforts to improve the standard of living and welfare of the Community which emphasizes voluntary service. by
36. Napza are chemical substances that when introduced into the body either orally (drunk, smoked, inhaled and sucked) or injected, can affect a person's thoughts, mood, feelings and behavior. This can cause social disorder characterized by negative indications, long duration of use and excessive use.
37. Regional Technical Implementation Unit for Women and Child Protection hereinafter referred to as UPTD PPA is a regional technical implementation unit for Women and Child Protection at the Office of Women Empowerment and Child Protection that provides services for Women and Children who experience violence, discrimination, Special Protection, and other problems.
38. Community is individuals, families, groups, social organizations and/or community organizations.

Part Two Protection of Women's Rights

Article 13

(1) The Regional Government guarantees protection of Women's rights.

(2) Women's rights as referred to in paragraph (1)

include the fields of:

a. education;
b. health;
c. employment;
d. politics;
e. law; and
f. economic, cultural and social.

Article 14

Women's rights in the field of education as referred to in Article 13 paragraph (2) letter a include:

a. making policies to facilitate Women's access and revoking discriminatory policies that hinder the fulfillment of Women's rights to education; and
b. obtaining equal opportunities with men to obtain education and teaching in all types and levels of education pathways.

Article 15

Women's rights in the field of health as referred to in Article 13 paragraph (2) letter b include:

a. obtaining education on reproductive health;
b. obtaining health services;
c. obtaining family planning services;
d. obtaining services for pregnant mothers; and
e. childbirth and postpartum services.

Article 16

Women's rights in the field of employment as referred to in Article 13 paragraph (2) letter c include:

a. the existence of empowerment of Women entrepreneurs;
b. obtaining decent work; and
c. obtaining protection from the crime of trafficking in persons.

Article 17

Women's rights in the field of politics as referred to in Article 13 paragraph (2) letter d include:

a. facilitating the increase of Women's political participation in legislative institutions;
b. providing facilities and infrastructure for Women's organizations whose vision and mission is to fight for the fulfillment of rights and protection for Women;
c. conducting socialization to increase awareness of Women's rights in the political field; and
d. encouraging the formation of Women's forum organizations.

Article 18

Women's rights in the field of law as referred to in Article 13 paragraph (2) letter e include:

a. facilitating the increase of legal knowledge and awareness for Women; and
b. encouraging the formation of organizations that provide free legal services for Women.

Article 19

Women's rights in the field of economic, cultural and social as referred to in Article 13 paragraph (2) letter f include:

a. facilitating Women in improving family economy;
b. encouraging Women's activities as agents of change;
c. facilitating Women in increasing social activities; and
d. obtaining capital assistance.

Article 20

Further provisions regarding the procedures for implementing the fulfillment of Women's rights as referred to in Article 13 are regulated by Regent Regulation.

CHAPTER VI

CHILD PROTECTION

Part One General

Article 21

(1) The Regional Government carries out Child Protection in the Region.

(2) Child Protection as referred to in paragraph (1) is through the Protection of Children's Rights.

(3) Children's Rights as referred to in paragraph (2) are guided by the provisions of Regional Regulation of Tanah Bumbu Regency Number 11 of 2021 concerning the Implementation of Child-Friendly Regency (Regional Gazette of Tanah Bumbu Regency of 2021 Number 11, Supplement to the Regional Gazette of Tanah Bumbu Regency Number 126).

Part Two

Implementation of Child Protection

Article 22

(1) The Regional Government, NGOs/Social Organizations, the Community and parents/families are obligated to provide Child Protection consisting of:

a. Children in the womb;
b. Toddlers;
c. School-age Children;
d. Neglected and street Children; and
e. Children requiring Special Protection.
(2) The implementation of Child Protection as referred to in paragraph (1) is carried out through:
a. policies, programs, and mechanisms concerning:
1. prevention, supervision, complaints/reporting, and development of Child data information systems;
2. integrated handling for Children who are victims of violence, maltreatment, exploitation and neglect;
3. guarantees for the fulfillment of the rights of every Child who becomes a victim of crime, violence, maltreatment, exploitation and neglect including:
a) health recovery and maintenance services; b) continuity of education services;
c) psychosocial services; and
d) legal aid services.
4. provision of family support including:
a) counseling;
b) Child parenting education;
c) family mediation; and
d) economic support.
b. raising awareness and attitudes of the Community through socialization, education, and information regarding:
1. Children's Rights, Child Protection, prevention of child marriage and Child parenting; and
2. the impact of violence, maltreatment, exploitation, and neglect of Children.
c. increasing the capacity of Child Protection services which includes the development of institutional capacity and service provider personnel.
d. service provider personnel as referred to in letter c includes:
1. health;
2. education;
3. psychosocial;
4. parenting; and
5. legal aid.
e. increasing the ability of Children to recognize risks and dangers from situations or acts that may cause violence, exploitation, maltreatment, and neglect.
(3) The implementation of Child Protection as referred to in paragraph (2) letters a through e is carried out and/or coordinated by the SKPD responsible for Child Protection affairs.

Part Three Handling

Article 23

(1) The targets of Child handling include:

a. Children outside parental care; b. Children in disaster emergency situations;
c. Children in conflict with the law;
d. Children victims of physical, mental and/or sexual Violence;
e. Children subjected to maltreatment and neglect; f. Children victims of economic exploitation;

g. Children who are victims of the criminal act of human trafficking;

h. Children who are victims of gambling, abuse of narcotics, alcohol, psychotropic substances and other addictive substances (Napza); and i. Children who are in situations where the nature and conditions of the workplace can endanger the health, safety, and morals of the Child.

(2) Handling as referred to in paragraph (1) must be carried out immediately including:

a. identifying and receiving complaints/reports; b. rescue actions;
c. placement of the Child in a temporary shelter;
d. rehabilitation including:
1. health recovery services;
2. psychosocial recovery services; and
3. legal assistance/expert witness support.
e. social reintegration in the form of post-rehabilitation service support.

Article 24

(1) Identification and receipt of complaints/reports as referred to in Article 23 paragraph (2) letter a includes:

a. ensuring the readiness of complaint services for Child Protection issues;
b. following up on information or complaints/reports received regarding Child Protection issues; and
c. identifying the type of problem, needs, and handling plan.
(2) The SKPD responsible for Child Protection affairs must identify problems and receive complaints/reports.

Article 25

(1) Rescue actions as referred to in Article 23 paragraph (2) letter b are carried out if based on the results of identification it is known that the Child's safety is threatened.

(2) Actions as referred to in paragraph (1) are carried out by removing the Child from threatening situations and environments. must

(3) The SKPD responsible for Child Protection affairs must carry out rescue actions and coordinate with the Police Force/Civil Service Police Unit.

a. the Child's safety is threatened;

(3) In placement as referred to in paragraph (2), the Child receives services for the fulfillment of basic needs and psychosocial assistance.
(4) The SKPD responsible for social affairs must provide temporary shelter.

Part Four

Protection of Children in the Womb

Article 32

Protection of Children in the womb as referred to in Article 22 paragraph (1) letter a is carried out through:

a. provision of facilities and facilities for examination of Children in the womb;
b. provision of nutritious food and immunization for pregnant mothers;
c. provision of abortion prevention services;
d. provision of appropriate and prompt delivery process services;
e. provision of the best health services for mothers and Children during the delivery process and post-delivery; and
f. implementation of Protection of Children in the womb for pregnant mothers with problems.

Part Five

Protection of Toddler Children

Article 33

(1) Protection of Toddler Children as referred to in

Article 22 paragraph (1) letter b is carried out through:

a. provision of complete breastfeeding;
b. provision of nutritious food and complete basic immunization;
c. provision of Child growth and development program services and Child parenting patterns;
d. provision of Child daycare facilities;
e. provision of playgrounds; and
f. provision of special breastfeeding rooms at certain places.
(2) Protection of Toddler Children can be implemented through the PSTPA model and playgroups.
(3) PSTPA and/or playgroups as referred to in paragraph (2) must meet the following requirements: a. have been registered and received recommendations from the relevant SKPD within the Regional Government;
b. have adequate human resources and funding sources to manage PSTPA and/or playgroups; and
c. have facilities and infrastructure in accordance with service guidelines at PSTPA and/or playgroups.
(4) Protection of Toddler Children can be implemented if in special or urgent conditions.

Part Six

Protection of School-Age Children

Article 34

(1) Protection of School-Age Children as referred to in Article 22 paragraph (1) letter c includes:

a. receiving attention and guidance from teachers and/or their mentors without discrimination;
b. receiving attention and affection from family;
c. receiving religious guidance at school and in the Community environment;
d. receiving prevention services, health rehabilitation; care and
e. receiving compulsory education for a minimum of 9 (nine) years and being able to complete senior high school supported by a friendly and conducive environment; f. receiving education guarantees;
g. receiving guidance and counseling; and
h. providing adequate playgrounds and sports facilities.
(2) Every person and/or any party is obligated to protect School-Age Children from acts of violence, sexual harassment and food containing hazardous substances in the family, school, Office/agency/institution and Community environment.
(3) Every person and/or any party is obligated to protect School-Age Children from acts of stopping and/or expelling Children from school and/or causing Children to drop out of school.

Part Seven Protection of Neglected Children

Article 35

(1) Protection of Neglected Children as referred to in Article 22 paragraph (1) letter d, namely Children whose parents do not have the ability to care for Children, is implemented through institutional and non-institutional forms of service; (2) Institutional forms of service as referred to in paragraph (2) are implemented by Child Protection Houses or PSAA owned by both the Regional Government and the Community.

(3) Non-institutional forms of service as referred to in paragraph (2) are implemented in the family or Community environment that are not in institutional form.

(4) Child Protection Houses or PSAA owned by the Community as referred to in paragraph (2) must meet the following requirements:

a. have been registered and received recommendations from the relevant SKPD within the Regional Government and/or Regency/City government;
b. have adequate human resources and funding sources to manage Child Protection Houses or PSAA; and
c. have facilities and infrastructure as determined in the service guidelines for Child Protection Houses or PSAA.

Part Eight

Protection of Children Who Require Special Protection

Article 36

Children who require Special Protection as referred to in Article 22 paragraph (1) letter e consist of:

a. Children in emergency situations and conflict Areas;
b. Children in conflict with the law;
c. Children from remote indigenous communities;
d. Children who are economically and/or sexually exploited;
e. Children victims of narcotics abuse including aromatic glue sniffing that can cause hallucinogenic effects and the like;
f. Children victims of kidnapping, sale, and trafficking; g. Children victims of physical and/or psychological Violence;
h. Children with special needs; and
i. Children victims of maltreatment and neglect.

Paragraph 1

Children In Emergency Situations and Conflict Areas

Article 37

Special Protection for Children in emergency situations and conflict Areas as referred to in Article 36 letter a is in the form of fulfillment of basic needs consisting of:

a. basic social services;
b. education;
c. religious guidance;
d. health services;
e. psychological counseling and/or psychotherapy;
f. legal aid; and
g. recreational and educational activities.

Paragraph 2

Children In Conflict With the Law

Article 38

(1) Special Protection for Children in conflict with the law as referred to in Article 36 letter b includes Children in conflict with the law and Children victims of criminal acts, is the obligation and responsibility of the Regional Government, Police, Prosecutor's Office, Court, Parents, Family and Community.

(2) Handling of Children facing legal processes and implementation of Judge's decisions must continue to prioritize Children's Rights.

(3) Special Protection for Children in conflict with the law as referred to in paragraph (1) is implemented through:

a. treatment of Children in a humane manner in accordance with the dignity and Rights of Children;
b. provision of special Child accompanying officers from an early stage; c. provision of special facilities and infrastructure;
d. imposition of appropriate sanctions for the best interests of Children;
e. continuous monitoring and recording of the development of Children in conflict with the law; f. provision of guarantees to maintain relationships with parents or family; and
g. protection from identity disclosure through mass media and to avoid labeling.
(4) In the case of a Child in conflict with the law, law enforcement officers shall not conduct Child justicia examinations without the knowledge of parents or guardians, and is conducted with:
a. rehabilitation efforts through special institutions for the best interests of Children;
b. protection efforts from identity disclosure through mass media and to avoid labeling;
c. provision of safety guarantees for victim witnesses and expert witnesses, both physical, mental and social; and
d. provision of accessibility to obtain information regarding case developments.

Paragraph 3

Children From Remote Indigenous Communities

Article 39

(1) Special Protection for Children from remote indigenous communities as referred to in Article 36 letter c is carried out through the provision of facilities and infrastructure to be able to enjoy their own culture, recognize and practice their own religious teachings, and use their own language.

(2) Every person and/or and/or any parties are prohibited from obstructing Children as referred to in paragraph (1) from enjoying their own culture, recognizing and practicing their religious teachings, and using their own language without neglecting access to Community and cultural development.

Paragraph 4

Children Who Are Economically and/or Sexually Exploited

Article 40

(1) Special Protection for Children who are exploited economically and/or sexually as referred to in Article 36 letter d is carried out through:

a. prevention and supervision for Children who are utilized by certain parties as objects of exploitation;
b. disseminating and/or socializing legislation related to the Protection of Children who are exploited economically and/or sexually;
c. monitoring, reporting and imposing sanctions; and
d. involving various government agencies, companies, labor unions, NGOs, and the Community in the elimination of exploitation against Children.
(2) Every person and/or any party is prohibited from conducting economic and/or sexual exploitation against Children as referred to in paragraph (1).

Paragraph 5

Child Victims of Narcotics Abuse Including Scented Glue Sniffing and the Like that can Cause Hallucinogenic Effects and the Like

Article 41

(1) Special Protection for child victims of narcotics abuse including scented glue sniffing and the like that can cause hallucinogenic effects and the like as referred to in Article 36 letter e, and involved in the use, production and distribution thereof, is carried out through efforts of supervision, prevention, treatment, and rehabilitation by the Regional Government and the Community.

(2) Every person and/or any party is prohibited from deliberately placing, allowing, involving, ordering to involve Children in the abuse, production and distribution of Narcotics, Scented Glue and the like as referred to in paragraph (1).

Paragraph 6

Child Victims of Kidnapping, Sale and Trafficking

Article 42

(1) Special Protection for child victims of kidnapping, sale and trafficking as referred to in Article 36 letter f is carried out through efforts of supervision, protection, prevention, treatment, rehabilitation and reintegration by the Regional Government and the Community.

(2) To implement prevention efforts as referred to in paragraph (1) the Regional Government together with the Police, Prosecutor's Office, Courts, Community, NGOs and Social Organizations takes steps in the form of:

a. conducting socialization and/or campaigns on the prevention, handling and elimination of Child Trafficking practices;
b. conducting cooperation with other Regions as well as with other countries through bilateral or multilateral cooperation in accordance with legislation.
(3) Protection for child victims of Child Trafficking is implemented through Shelter Houses and/or other Child Protection Institutions, through referral from government agencies or the Community.
(4) Every child victim of kidnapping, sale and trafficking, has the right to obtain treatment and rehabilitation both physical, psychological and reintegration in the family environment as well as the Community.

Paragraph 7

Child Victims of Physical and/or Psychological Violence

Article 43

(1) Every child victim of physical and/or psychological Violence as referred to in Article 36 letter g obtains rehabilitation services both physical and psychosocial.

(2) Forms of protection for child victims of acts of Violence in the form of basic social services, education, religious guidance, health services, psychological counseling and/or psychotherapy, legal assistance, recreational activities, educational and empowerment of parents of child victims of acts of violence.

(3) Every person and/or any party is prohibited from committing acts of Violence against Children.

Paragraph 8

Children with Special Needs

Article 44

(1) Special Protection for Children with special needs as referred to in Article 36 letter h is carried out through efforts to:

a. treat Children humanely in accordance with the dignity and Rights of Children;
b. fulfill special needs; and
c. obtain the same treatment as other Children to achieve social integration as fully as possible and individual development.
(2) Every person and/or any party is prohibited from treating Children with special needs in a discriminatory manner, by ignoring their views including labeling and equalization in education for Children with special needs.

Paragraph 9

Child Victims of Mistreatment and Neglect

Article 45

(1) Special Protection for child victims of mistreatment and neglect as referred to in Article 36 letter i is carried out through supervision, prevention, treatment and rehabilitation.

(2) Every person and/or any party is prohibited from placing, allowing, involving, ordering to involve Children in situations of mistreatment and neglect as referred to in paragraph (1).

CHAPTER VII SAFE HOUSE

Article 46

(1) Women and child victims of Violence who require continuous handling have the right to stay in a safe house.

(2) The safe house as referred to in paragraph (1) provides a place or temporary safe shelter for victims of Violence until their rights are restored. (3) The operation and management of safe houses as referred to in paragraph (1) is carried out by the Regional Apparatus that performs functions in the field of Women's Empowerment and Child Protection.

(4) Further provisions regarding the operation of safe houses as referred to in paragraph (1) are regulated by Regent Regulation.

CHAPTER VIII

WOMEN'S EMPOWERMENT AND

CHILD PROTECTION BY VILLAGE GOVERNMENT

Article 47

(1) The Village Government implements Women's Empowerment and Child Protection in the Village.

(2) The implementation of Women's Empowerment and

Child Protection regulated in this Regional Regulation, applies mutatis mutandis in the implementation of Women's Empowerment and Child Protection by

the Regional Government in accordance with the provisions of legislation.

(3) Funding for the implementation of Women's Empowerment and Child Protection by the Regional Government as referred to in paragraph (1) is charged to the Village revenue and expenditure budget.

(4) Further provisions regarding the implementation of Women's Empowerment and Child Protection by the Regional Government are regulated by Regent Regulation.

CHAPTER IX

FAMILY QUALITY

Article 48

(1) The Regional Government is obliged to improve family quality in the form of:

a. improving family quality in realizing gender equality and Children's Rights.
b. strengthening and developing service provider institutions for improving family quality in realizing gender equality and Children's Rights whose working area is within the Region.
c. providing services for families in realizing gender equality and Children's Rights whose working area is within the Region.
(2) Provisions regarding the procedures for improving family quality as referred to in paragraph (1) are regulated in Regent Regulation.

CHAPTER X COOPERATION

Article 49

(1) In the effort to implement Women's Empowerment and Child Protection, the Regional Government may cooperate.

(2) Cooperation as referred to in paragraph (1) is carried out with:

a. the government;
b. the provincial government;

other Regional Governments;

d. vertical agencies;

e. advocates;

f. social workers;

g. companion volunteers;

h. spiritual advisors; and

i. community self-help organizations operating in the field of Women's Empowerment and Child Protection.

(3) The form of cooperation as referred to in paragraph (1)

is in the form of:

a. socialization;
b. provision of legal assistance;
c. health services;
d. victim assistance
e. counseling;
f. social rehabilitation; and
g. spiritual guidance.
(4) The procedures for implementing cooperation as referred to in paragraph (1) are in accordance with the provisions of legislation.

CHAPTER XI AWARDS

Article 50

(1) The Regent may grant awards for the participation of any person and/or entity that contributes to the Empowerment of Women and Protection of Children in the Region.

(2) The forms of awards as referred to in paragraph (1)

include:

a. plaque/certificate of appreciation;
b. grants; and/or
c. Regional incentives.
(3) Regional incentives as referred to in paragraph (2) letter c shall be in the form of:
a. Regional fiscal incentives; and/or
b. Regional non-fiscal incentives.
(4) The forms of Regional fiscal incentives as referred to in paragraph (3) letter a, shall be in the form of:
a. reduction and/or exemption of Regional taxes; and/or
b. reduction of Regional levies.
(5) The forms of Regional non-fiscal incentives as referred to in paragraph (3) letter b, shall be in the form of business licensing facilitation in the Region.
(6) The procedures for granting awards as referred to in paragraph (1) shall be guided by the provisions of laws and regulations.

CHAPTER XII

COMMUNITY PARTICIPATION

Article 51

(1) The Regional Government has the obligation to strengthen Community participation in the development of Women's Empowerment and Child Protection.

(2) Community participation as referred to in paragraph (1)

may be carried out in the form of:

a. socialization;
b. advocacy;
c. counseling;
d. coaching; e. supervision;
f. facilitation;
g. services;
h. studies;
i. training; j. capital provision; and
k. provision of facilities and infrastructure.
(3) Strengthening of Community participation as referred to in paragraph (1) may be carried out through:
a. strengthening cooperation;
b. strengthening institutional capacity for prevention and services from the Community, Islamic boarding schools and the business sector; and
c. financing support.

Article 52

(1) The business sector has responsibilities in the implementation of Women's Empowerment and Child Protection.

(2) The forms of business sector responsibility as referred to in paragraph (1) shall be carried out in the form of:

a. financial assistance to service institutions;
b. empowerment;
c. capacity building;
d. publication and information media;
e. provision of facilities and infrastructure;
f. establishment of special institutions for prevention and handling of violence complaints; and
g. non-binding social assistance.

CHAPTER XIII

FINANCING

Article 53

The financing of Women's Empowerment and Child Protection in this Regional Regulation shall be charged to the Regional revenue and expenditure budget and other lawful and non-binding sources in accordance with the provisions of laws and regulations, taking into account the Regional fiscal capacity.

Every person is prohibited from:

CHAPTER XIV PROHIBITIONS

Article 54

a. treating Women and Children in a discriminatory manner that results in Women and Children suffering losses, both material and moral, thus hindering their social function;

b. treating persons with disabilities in a discriminatory manner;

c. placing, allowing, involving, ordering the involvement of Women and Children with disabilities in situations of maltreatment and neglect;

d. placing, allowing, committing, ordering the commission of or participating in Violence against Women and Children;

e. committing Violence or threat of Violence to force Women and Children to engage in sexual intercourse with them or with others;

f. committing Violence or threat of violence, forcing, committing fraud, committing a series of lies, or persuading Women and Children to commit or allow obscene acts to be committed; g. placing, allowing, committing, ordering the commission of or participating in the kidnapping, sale and/or trafficking of Women and Children; h. obstructing Women and Children from enjoying their own culture, recognizing and practicing their religious teachings and/or using their own language without neglecting access to Community and cultural development; i. placing, allowing, committing, ordering the commission of or participating in economic and/or sexual exploitation of Women and Children; j. deliberately placing, allowing, involving Women and Children in the abuse, as well as production and distribution of narcotics and/or psychotropic substances; and/or

k. deliberately placing, allowing, involving, ordering the involvement of Women and Children in the abuse, as well as production and distribution of alcohol and other addictive substances.

CHAPTER XV

INVESTIGATION PROVISIONS

Article 55

(1) Civil Servant Investigators within the Regional Government are granted special authority to conduct investigations of criminal offenses as referred to in the applicable Code of Criminal Procedure. (2) The authority of investigators as referred to in paragraph (1) shall be:

a. receiving, seeking, collecting and examining information or reports concerning criminal offenses;
b. examining, seeking and collecting information or reports concerning criminal offenses;
c. requesting information and evidence from individuals or entities in connection with criminal offenses;
d. examining record books and other documents concerning criminal offenses; conducting searches to obtain evidence of books, records and other documents and confiscating such evidence;
f. requesting the assistance of experts in the course of carrying out criminal investigation tasks;
g. ordering to stop, prohibiting someone from leaving the room or place when an examination is in progress and examining the identity of the person and or documents carried as referred to in letter e;
h. photographing someone related to a criminal offense;
i. summoning people to be heard and examined as suspects or witnesses;
j. terminating investigations;
k. taking other actions necessary for the smooth conduct of criminal investigations according to law and which can be accounted for.
(3) Investigators as referred to in paragraph (1), in accordance with the applicable provisions, shall notify the commencement of the investigation and submit the investigation results to the public prosecutor through the Investigators of the Indonesian National Police in accordance with the provisions stipulated in the Criminal Procedure Code.

CHAPTER XVI CRIMINAL PROVISIONS

Article 56

(1) Any person who violates the provisions as referred to in Article 34 paragraphs (2) and (3), Article 39 paragraph (2), Article 40 paragraph (2), Article 41 paragraph (2), Article 43 paragraph (3), Article 44 paragraph (2) and Article 54, shall be punished by imprisonment of a maximum of 6 months or a fine of a maximum of Rp50,000,000.00 (fifty million rupiah).

(2) The criminal offense as referred to in paragraph (1)

constitutes a violation.

CHAPTER XVII

TRANSITIONAL PROVISIONS

Article 57

At the time this Regional Regulation comes into force, existing Regional policies regarding Women's Empowerment and Child Protection in the Region shall remain in effect, insofar as they do not conflict with this Regional Regulation.

Article 58

At the time the provisions of Article 624 of Law Number 1 of 2023 concerning the Criminal Code (State Gazette of the Republic of Indonesia of 2023 Number 1, Supplement to the State Gazette of the Republic of Indonesia Number 6842) come into force, then:

a. imprisonment as referred to in Article 56 paragraph (1) shall be replaced with a fine of a maximum of category I; and
b. the category I fine as referred to in letter a shall be Rp1,000,000.00 (one million rupiah).

CHAPTER XVIII

CLOSING PROVISIONS

Article 59

Regent Regulations as implementing regulations of this Regional Regulation shall be stipulated no later than 1 (one) year from the promulgation of this Regional Regulation.

Article 60

This Regional Regulation comes into force on the date of promulgation.

In order that everyone may know of it, orders the promulgation of this Regional Regulation by placement in the Regional Gazette of Tanah Bumbu Regency.

1. GENERAL

In order to realize Women who are capable physically, mentally, spiritually, socially, in knowledge, and skills so that they are ready to be empowered, as well as efforts to protect Women and Children from all acts of Violence in order to achieve the goal of welfare through a Regional regulation that can become a reference for all parties related to Women's Empowerment and Child Protection.

The drafting of the Regional regulation on Women's Empowerment and Child Protection has the intent and purpose as the implementation of the widest possible regional autonomy, thus the Women's Empowerment and Child Protection sector is one sector that is quite important to be managed and regulated in order to realize Women who are capable physically, mentally, spiritually, socially, in knowledge, and skills so that they are ready to be empowered, as well as efforts to protect Women and Children from all acts of Violence in order to achieve the goal of welfare through a draft Regional regulation that can become a reference for all parties related to Women's Empowerment and Child Protection.

The establishment of this Regional regulation is based on the principles of good legislative drafting and the principles of the material content of legislation.

The implementation of governmental affairs in the field of Women's Empowerment and Child Protection aims to increase efforts to Protect Women's rights, fulfill Children's Rights and Special Protection for Children and increase the role of Regional Government and the business sector, media and the Community in efforts for Women's Empowerment and Child Protection. Article 1

Sufficiently clear.

Article 2

Letter a

What is meant by "respect for human rights is that the protection and empowerment of women is carried out as a manifestation of respect for women as part of Human Rights.

Letter b

What is meant by "gender equality" is one of the basic rights as a human being, the right to live with dignity, free from fear and free to determine life choices is not only intended for men, women also have the same rights in essence.

Letter c

What is meant by "non-discrimination" is an attitude and treatment toward Children by not making distinctions based on age, gender, race, ethnicity, religion and between groups, and the condition of the Child.

Letter d

What is meant by "justice and legal certainty is that in carrying out the protection and empowerment of women it is based on justice and legal certainty of equality between men and women. Letter e

What is meant by "benefit" is providing the widest possible benefits for children and women for the interests of society, nation and state.

Letter f

What is meant by "participatory" is that every member of society is encouraged to play an active role in the decision-making process and implementation of child protection and women's empowerment, either directly or indirectly.

Letter g

What is meant by "the best interests of the Child" is that in all actions concerning Children carried out by the Regional Government, parents/family, and the Community, the best interests of the Child must be the primary consideration.

Article 3

Letter h

What is meant by "accountability" is a principle that requires that every activity and final result of state administration activities must be accountable to the community and the people as holders of the highest sovereignty of the state in accordance with the provisions of the applicable legislation.

Letter i

What is meant by "gender responsive" is consistent and systematic attention to the differences between women and men in society accompanied by efforts to eliminate structural and cultural barriers to achieve gender equality.
