CHAPTER I

GENERAL PROVISIONS

Article 1

In this Regent Regulation, what is meant by:

1.

Region is Pohuwato Regency.

2.

Regional Government is the Regent as an element of the Regional Government administration who leads the implementation of government affairs which are the authority of the autonomous region.

3.

Regent is the Regent of Pohuwato.

4.

Regional House of Representatives, hereinafter abbreviated as DPRD, is a regional people's representative institution positioned as an element of Regional Government administration.

5. Regional Apparatus is an auxiliary element of the Regent and DPRD in carrying out government affairs which are the authority of the Region.

6. Regional Long-Term Development Plan, hereinafter abbreviated as RPJPD, is a regional planning document for a period of 20 (twenty) years.

7.

8.

9.

Regional Medium-Term Development Plan, hereinafter abbreviated as RPJMD, is a regional planning document for a period of 5 (five) years.

Protection is all efforts aimed at providing a sense of security to victims carried out by family, advocates, social institutions, police, prosecutors, courts, or other parties either temporarily or based on court decisions.

Violence is any act unlawfully with or without means against physical and verbal that causes danger to life, body and/or results in the deprivation of a person's freedom.

10. Violence against women is any act based on gender differences that results or may result in misery or suffering of women physically, sexually or psychologically, including threats of certain actions, coercion or deprivation of freedom arbitrarily, whether occurring in the public domain or in private life.

11. Violence against children is any act against children that results in misery or suffering physically, mentally, sexually, psychologically, including neglect and ill-treatment that threatens bodily integrity and degrades the dignity of children.

12. Discrimination is any distinction, harassment or exclusion either directly or indirectly based on human differentiation on the basis of religion, ethnicity, race, ethnic group, social status, economic status, gender, language, political beliefs that result in reduction, deviation or elimination of recognition, implementation or use of human rights and basic freedoms in life both individually and collectively in the fields of politics, economy, law, social, culture and other aspects of life.

13. Human Trafficking is the act of recruitment, transportation, harboring, sending, transfer, or receipt of a person by threat of violence, use of violence, abduction, confinement, forgery, fraud and abuse of power or position of vulnerability, debt bondage or giving payment or benefits, so as to obtain consent from the person holding control over that other person, whether carried out within a country or between countries, for the purpose of exploitation or resulting in a person being exploited.

14. Child is someone who has not reached the age of 18 (eighteen) years including a child still in the womb.

15. Victim of violence is a woman or child who experiences suffering either physically, mentally or emotionally, economic loss, or experiences neglect, reduction or deprivation of their basic rights.

16. Prevention is a direct effort carried out by the Regional Government and/or community to prevent the occurrence of acts of violence against women and children.

17. Integrated Service Center, hereinafter abbreviated as PPT, is an Integrated Service Unit established by the Regional Government to provide services and rehabilitation to women and children who are victims of violence comprehensively including information services, assistance and legal aid, counseling services, medical services and safe houses through referrals.

18. Task Force for Prevention and Handling of Criminal Acts of Human Trafficking, hereinafter referred to as Task Force, is a coordinating institution tasked with coordinating prevention and handling efforts for criminal acts of human trafficking in Pohuwato Regency.

19. Safe house is a temporary residence used to provide protection to victims in accordance with determined operational standards. victims

20. Social reintegration is a process of preparing the community and victims that supports reunification into the family environment, school environment, work environment and community environment that can provide protection and fulfillment of victims' needs.

21. Victim recovery is services provided in the form of strengthening and developing the capabilities of a person who experiences acts of violence against women and children to be more empowered physically, psychologically, sexually, economically and socially.

22. Assistance is all actions in the form of counseling, psychological therapy, advocacy and spiritual guidance, for self-empowerment of women and children from acts of violence to resolve the problems faced.

23. Family is the smallest unit in society consisting of husband and wife, or husband and wife and their children, or father and his children, or mother and her children, or blood relatives in a direct line upward or downward to the third degree.

24. Parents are biological father and/or mother, or stepfather and/or stepmother, or adoptive father and/or mother.

25. Community is individuals, families, groups, and social organizations and/or community organizations.

Article 2

Protection of women and children from acts of violence is carried out based on the principles of

a. Humanity;

b. Justice and gender equality;

c. Non-discrimination;

d. Public order and legal certainty;

e. Transparency;

f. Protection.

Article 3

The purpose of protecting women and children from acts of violence, to:

a. Prevent acts of violence against women and children including human trafficking;
b. Eliminate all forms of acts of violence and exploitation against women and children;
c. Protect and provide a sense of security for women and children;
d. Provide services to women and children from acts of violence, reporters, and witnesses; and
e. Empower women and children victims of violence to be more empowered physically, psychologically, socially, and economically.

CHAPTER III

FORMS OF VIOLENCE

Article 4

Forms of violence include:

a. Physical violence;
b. Psychological violence;
c. Sexual violence;
d. Neglect;
e. Maltreatment;
f. Exploitation; and/or
g. Other violence.

CHAPTER IV

RIGHTS OF WOMEN AND CHILDREN VICTIMS OF VIOLENCE

Article 5

Women and children victims of violence receive the following rights:

a. Right to be respected for their dignity as human beings;
b. Right to recovery;
c. Right to determine their own decisions;
d. Right to obtain information;
e. Right to confidentiality;
f. Right to social rehabilitation;
g. Right to complaint handling that is quick, precise, comfortable and according to needs;
h. Right of victims and their families to obtain facilities in the judicial process;
i. Right to assistance; and
j. Right to a sense of security.

Article 6

Child victims of violence in addition to obtaining rights as referred to in Article 5, also receive special rights, as follows:

a. Right to survival, growth and development;
b. Right to basic population services;
c. Right to equal protection;

Right to be free from various stigma; and

Right to obtain freedom.

CHAPTER V

OBLIGATIONS AND RESPONSIBILITIES

Article 7

Obligations and responsibilities in providing protection to women and children from acts of violence are the shared responsibility of:

a. Regional Government;
b. Community.

Article 8

(1) The obligations and responsibilities of the Regional Government as referred to in Article 7 letter a, include:

a. establishing, implementing policies, programs, and conducting cooperation activities in the implementation of protection of women and children from acts of violence; b.

C. d. e. f. facilitating the establishment of institutional protection for women and children from acts of violence and providing support for facilities and infrastructure; allocating budget for the implementation of protection of women and children from acts of violence in accordance with regional financial capacity; fostering and supervising the implementation of protection of women and children from acts of violence; providing protection services for women and children victims of acts of violence; encouraging and increasing community participation; and

g. appointing parents and/or substitute family caregivers as a protection measure for children who are victims of acts of violence.

(2) In order to carry out the obligations and responsibilities as referred to in paragraph (1), the Regent establishes programs and action activities for the protection of women and children in one Regional Action Plan as a basis for Regional Apparatus in implementing the protection of women and children from acts of violence.

(3) The Regional Action Plan as referred to in paragraph (2), is part of the Regional Medium-Term Development Plan.

(4) Further provisions regarding the Regional Action Plan as referred to in paragraph (3), are regulated by Regent Regulation.

Article 9

(1) The obligations and responsibilities of the community as referred to in

(2)

Article 7 letter b, are implemented in the form of community participation. The form of community participation as referred to in paragraph (1), includes:

a. preventing the occurrence of acts of violence against women and children;
b. providing protection to women and children from acts of violence;
c. providing information and/or reporting acts of violence against women and children to law enforcement or authorized parties; and
d. participating in the handling of victims of acts of violence.
(3) The form of community participation as referred to in paragraph (2), is carried out responsibly in accordance with the provisions of laws and regulations.

Article 10

Every person who hears, sees, or knows of the occurrence of violence against women and children is obliged to make efforts in accordance with their capacity to:

a. Prevent and stop the ongoing acts of violence;
b. Provide protection to victims;
c. Provide emergency assistance; and/or
d. Assist in the process of submitting applications for protection determination Kk

CHAPTER VI

INSTITUTIONS

Article 11

(1) The Regional Government establishes an Integrated Service Center (PPT) in providing protection for women and children from acts of violence.

(2) The PPT as referred to in paragraph (1) is established at the Regency and District levels.

(3) The membership of PPT as referred to in paragraph (1) consists of elements of Regional Government, law enforcement, community organizations, non-governmental organizations, professional organizations, researchers and academics.

(4) Further provisions regarding the Organization and Work Procedures of PPT as referred to in paragraph (1) are regulated by Regent Regulation.

Article 12

In addition to establishing PPT as referred to in Article 11, in order to support the implementation of protection for women and children from acts of violence, the Regent establishes:

a. Task force on trafficking in persons;
b. Regional action committee for the elimination of the worst forms of child labor.

Article 13

(1) The task force as referred to in

Article 12 letter a, is composed of representatives from Regional Government, law enforcement, community organizations, non-governmental organizations, professional organizations, and researchers/academics.

(2) The task force as referred to in paragraph (1), is a coordinative institution tasked with coordinating the prevention and handling of trafficking in persons.

(3) Further provisions regarding the establishment, duties, and functions of the task force as referred to in paragraph (1) are regulated by Regent

Regulation.

Article 14

(1) The committee as referred to in Article 12 letter b, is composed of elements from Regional Government, law enforcement, community organizations, non-governmental organizations, professional organizations, researchers and academics.

(2) Further provisions regarding the duties and functions of the committee as referred to in paragraph (1) are regulated by Regent Regulation.

CHAPTER VII

IMPLEMENTATION OF PROTECTION

Part One

Prevention of Acts of Violence

Article 15

(1) The Regional Government conducts empowerment and awareness-raising to families, parents, and the community by providing information, guidance and/or counseling.

(2) In addition to empowerment and awareness-raising as referred to in paragraph

(1), is implemented by way of:

a. providing material on the prevention of acts of violence against women and children in both formal and informal education;
b. opening accessibility to obtain education, training, funding, income improvement and social services in accordance with applicable provisions;
c. opening employment opportunities for women;
d. building community participation and awareness towards the prevention and protection of women and children from acts of violence;
e. building and providing a complete and easily accessible information system;
f. building networks and cooperation with law enforcement apparatus, government apparatus, educational institutions, and various non-governmental organizations that work and/or care about women and children; and
g. opening an integrated service system for the protection of women and children from acts of violence in every village.

Article 16

(1) Prevention of the occurrence of acts of violence against women and children as referred to in Article 15, is carried out by Regional Apparatus whose duties and functions are in the fields of:

a. social affairs;
b. health;
c. education;
d. manpower;
e. population and civil registration:
f. law;
g. women's empowerment and child protection;
h. cooperatives and Micro, Small and Medium Enterprises;
i. mental and spiritual; and
j. peace and order.
(2) Prevention of acts of violence by Regional Apparatus as referred to in paragraph (1), is carried out in an integrated and continuous manner based on the Regional Long-Term Development Plan and Regional Medium-Term Development Plan.

Part Two
Legal Protection

Legal protection includes:

Article 17

a. Providing protection in safe houses (shelters);

b. Providing legal information to victims;

c. Providing assistance for victims as witnesses from the investigation process to the verdict;

d. Providing special legal protection for child victims of acts of violence can be done by appointing guardianship in accordance with applicable laws and regulations.

Part Three

Recovery

Article 18

Recovery includes:

a. Providing physical recovery in health service institutions;
b. Providing medicolegal services;
c. Assisting in the repatriation of victims;
d. Providing temporary protection in safe houses (shelters);
e. Providing psychosocial recovery and assistance;
f. Providing spiritual guidance services;
g. Preparing family, school, work and community environments, as well as economic empowerment.

Part Four

Coordination

Article 19

Coordination includes:

a. Conducting coordination and cooperation in handling cases of violence with integrated service institutions;
b. Conducting coordination and cooperation in handling cases of violence with integrated services between regions

Part Five

Community Participation

Article 20

Community Participation is carried out by way of:

a. Fostering community awareness of cases of violence against women and children;
b. Encouraging the community to actively participate in providing information and reporting acts of violence against women and children;
c. Fostering local wisdom in handling cases of violence;
d. Organizing the strengthening of community groups in handling acts of violence against women and children;
e. Disseminating information about laws and regulations related to acts of violence against women and children.

CHAPTER VIII

COOPERATION AND PARTNERSHIP

Part One

Cooperation

Article 21

(1) The Regional Government develops cooperation patterns in order to achieve the objectives of protecting women and children from acts of violence in accordance with the provisions of laws and regulations.

(2) Cooperation as referred to in paragraph (1) is carried out between the Regional Government and:

a. Central Government;
b. Provincial Government;
c. Other Regency/City Governments;
d. Universities;
e. Social and religious institutions;
f. Non-Governmental Organizations; and/or
g. Media.
(3) Cooperation as referred to in paragraph (2) includes:
a. exchange of data and information;
b. rehabilitation of victims of violence;
c. repatriation and social reintegration; and
d. provision of evidence and witnesses.
(4) Cooperation as referred to in paragraph (2) is implemented in accordance with the provisions of laws and regulations

Part Two

Partnership

Article 22

(1) The Regional Government may organize partnerships with the business sector in the protection of women and children from acts of violence; (2) Partnership as referred to in paragraph (1) is carried out through:

a. notification of job opportunity information for women victims of violence;
b. education and training for women victims of violence;
c. educational assistance for women and children victims of violence who are withdrawn from their education; and
d. fostering and increasing the economic independence of women victims of violence.
(3) Partnership as referred to in paragraph (1) and paragraph (2) is implemented in accordance with the provisions of laws and regulations.

CHAPTER IX

GUIDANCE AND SUPERVISION

Article 23

(1) The Regional Government is obliged to conduct guidance and supervision of the implementation of the protection of women and children from acts of violence.

(2) Guidance and supervision as referred to in paragraph (1) include: supervisionasreferredto

a. guidelines and standardfulfillment;

b. technical guidance and training;

c. coordination;

d. monitoring;

e. evaluation; and

f. reporting.

Article 24

The community may conduct supervision of the implementation of the protection of women and children from acts of violence organized by the Regional Government through the mechanism of conveying aspirations to the Regent or to the DPRD.

Article 25

Further provisions regarding the procedures for implementing guidance and supervision as referred to in Article 23 and Article 24 are regulated in the Regent's Regulation.

CHAPTER X

FINANCING

Article 26

Financing for the implementation of the protection of women and children from acts of violence may be sourced from:

a. State Revenue and Expenditure Budget
b. Regional Revenue and Expenditure Budget;
c. Non-binding community contributions;
d. Non-binding foreign assistance; and/or
e. Other legitimate and non-binding sources in accordance with the provisions of laws and regulations.

Article 27

(1) The Regional Government may provide financial assistance to community organizations, social organizations or non-governmental organizations that implement the protection of women and children from discrimination and acts of violence.

(2) Financial assistance as referred to in paragraph (1) is in accordance with the regional financial capacity, and is implemented in accordance with the provisions of laws and regulations in the field of regional finance.

CHAPTER XI

CLOSING PROVISIONS

Article 28

This Regional Regulation comes into force on the date of promulgation. In order that everyone may know of it, the promulgation of this Regional Regulation is ordered by placing it in the Regional Gazette of the Regency of

Pohuwato.
