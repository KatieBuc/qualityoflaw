CHAPTER I

GENERAL PROVISIONS

Article 1

In this Regional Regulation, what is meant by:

1. Region is Indramayu Regency. 2. Regional Government is the Regent together with Regional Apparatus as the element of regional government administration.
3. Regent is the Regent of Indramayu.
4. Regional People's Representative Council hereinafter abbreviated as DPRD is the Regional People's Representative Council of Indramayu Regency as the element of Regional Government administration.
5. Agency is the Regional Apparatus Working Unit authorized in the field of Prevention, Protection and Recovery of Women and Children as Victims of Acts of Violence in Indramayu Regency.
6. Woman is an adult human being of female gender and a person recognized by law as a woman.
7. Child is a person who has not reached 18 years of age, including a child in the womb and marriage cannot change the status of a child.
8. Prevention is an activity carried out to prevent the occurrence of acts of violence against women and children.
9. Recovery is rehabilitating victims until they are physically and/or psychologically healthy and accepted by the community again.

10.Protection is an activity and/or action in order to protect women and children from violence and exploitation.

11. Violence is any act that results in or causes misery and suffering whether physical, sexual, psychological including threats of certain actions, coercion or arbitrary deprivation of freedom, whether occurring in public or in private life.

12. Physical violence is any act that causes pain, injury, wounds or disability to a person's body, miscarriage or unconsciousness and/or causes death.

13. Psychological violence is an act that causes fear, loss of self-confidence, loss of ability to act, disbelief or severe psychological suffering to a person.

14. Sexual violence is any act in the form of sexual harassment, forced sexual intercourse, whether unnatural or unwanted with another person for commercial purposes and/or certain purposes.

15. Victim is a woman and child who experiences acts of violence, whether physical, psychological or sexual violence.

16. Service is an immediate activity and action carried out by professionals according to their respective professions in the form of counseling, therapy and advocacy for the empowerment and recovery of victims of violence.

17. Assistance is an activity and action carried out by an assistant during the service process.

18. Assistant is a person or representative of an institution who has the expertise to provide assistance.

19. Institution is an agency/body and/or non-governmental organization (NGO) that provides assistance.

20. Integrated Service Center hereinafter abbreviated as PPT, is an institution that provides services to victims of violence based in hospitals, managed jointly in the form of medical services (including medico-legal), psychosocial and legal services.

21. Safe house (Shelter) is temporary housing, provided to give protection to victims in accordance with predetermined standards.

22. Community is individuals, families, groups, social organizations and/or community organizations.

23. P2TP2A is one form of service facility for women and children in efforts to fulfill information and needs in the fields of education, health, economy, law, protection and handling of acts of violence and trafficking against women and children.

CHAPTER II

PRINCIPLES AND OBJECTIVES

Article 2

The implementation of Prevention, Protection and Recovery of Women and Children as Victims of Acts of Violence is carried out based on the principles of:

a. Respect for the rights of victims; b. Gender justice and welfare;
c. Non-discrimination; and
d. Best interests of victims.

Article 3

The objectives of implementing prevention, protection and recovery of women and children as victims of acts of violence is to provide prevention and protection covering aspects of:

a. Prevention;
b. Service and assistance;
c. Recovery; and
d. Empowerment.

CHAPTER III

RIGHTS OF VICTIMS

Article 4

Every victim has the right to obtain:

a. Protection;
b. Information;
c. Minimum service according to Minimum Service Standards (SPM);
d. Continuous handling up to the rehabilitation stage;
e. Confidential handling;
f. Psychological and legal assistance; and
g. Guarantee of rights relating to status as a family member and community member.

CHAPTER IV

OBLIGATIONS AND AUTHORITY

REGIONAL GOVERNMENT

Article 5

(1) The Regional Government is obligated to carry out efforts to prevent the occurrence of violence against women and children in the form of: a.collecting data and information about women and children victims of violence; b.conducting socialization of laws and regulations related to the implementation of prevention, protection and recovery of women and children as victims of violence and exploitation; c.conducting socialization about understanding and values of anti-violence against Women and Children through IEC (communication, information and education) both through community, schools and universities; d.conducting monitoring and evaluation of the implementation of prevention, protection and recovery of women and children as victims of violence and exploitation.

(2) The Regional Government in anticipating the occurrence of acts of violence against Women and Children, is authorized to provide and organize services for victims in the form of: a.providing and facilitating the formation of integrated service institutions for victims of acts of violence by involving institutions and community elements;

b. encouraging community awareness of the importance of protection for victims of acts of violence; c.conducting community empowerment for the prevention of acts of violence.

(3) The obligations and authority of the Regional Government are carried out by the Agency

CHAPTER V

PREVENTION AND PROTECTION

Part One

Institutional

Article 6

(1) In the framework of implementing prevention, protection and recovery for women and children victims of acts of violence in an integrated, harmonious, and sustainable manner, the Regent establishes P2TP2A at the Regency level.

(2) Further provisions regarding the formation of P2TP2A management, P2TP2A membership, and P2TP2A working procedures as referred to in paragraph (1) are regulated by the Regent.

(3) Regency P2TP2A may receive case referrals from Hospitals, Community Health Centers, NGOs, Safe Houses (Shelters) and the Community.

Part Two

Forms and Mechanisms of Prevention

Article 7

Forms of prevention of acts of violence against women and children carried out by P2TP2A can be implemented through: a.socialization activities of laws and regulations and/or regional regulations to the community related to the protection of the rights of women and children; b.training of P2TP2A members related to the implementation of their duties in preventing acts of violence against women and children.

Article 8

(1) Regency P2TP2A in carrying out its duties and functions may coordinate with the Religion and Social Welfare Section of the Secretariat of Indramayu Regency, sub-districts and competent parties in making efforts to prevent the occurrence of acts of violence against women and children.

(2) The mechanism for implementing prevention duties as referred to in paragraph (1) is further regulated by Regent Regulation.

Article 9

Forms of services for victims provided by P2TP2A include: a. medical services, in the form of treatment and recovery of physical injuries aimed at restoring the physical condition of victims carried out by medical and paramedical personnel; b. medico-legal services constitute a form of medical service for the purposes of evidence in the legal field;

c. Psychological services constitute services provided in order to restore the traumatic condition of victims, including the provision of safe houses to protect victims from various threats and intimidation against victims and to provide social support so that victims have self-confidence, strength and independence in resolving their problems;

d. Legal services to assist victims in undergoing legal proceedings;

e. Economic independence services in the form of skills training and providing economic access so that victims can be independent.

Article 10

(1) In carrying out the task of providing services to victims of violence against women and children, the Regency P2TP2A coordinates with the Sub-district Health Center.

(2) The mechanism for implementing tasks as referred to in paragraph (1) is regulated in the Standard Operating Procedure (SOP), further regulated by Regent Regulation.

Part Three

Accompaniment Mechanism

Article 11

(1) Accompaniment is carried out by individuals and institutions cooperating with P2TP2A.

(2) The accompaniment mechanism as referred to in paragraph (1) is set forth in the Standard Operating Procedure (SOP).

(3) Provisions regarding the Standard Operating Procedure (SOP) for the accompaniment mechanism as referred to in paragraph (2), are further regulated by Regent Regulation.

Part Four

Principles of Prevention, Protection and Recovery

Article 12

The implementation of prevention, protection and recovery for victims of violence is carried out with:

a. Swift, safe, empathetic;
b. Guarantee of confidentiality;
c. Easily accessible; and
d. No fees charged.

CHAPTER VI

RECOVERY

Part One

Minimum Service Standards

Article 13

(1) In the context of implementing prevention, protection and recovery for women and children victims of violence in an integrated, harmonious, and sustainable manner, the Regional Government establishes Minimum Service Standards (SPM).

(2) Further provisions regarding Minimum Service Standards as referred to in paragraph (1), are further regulated by Regent Regulation.

(3) The Regional Government is obligated to implement Minimum Service Standards in Hospitals, Health Centers, NGOs, Safe Houses (Shelters) designated by the Regent.

Part Two

Forms and Mechanisms of Recovery

Article 14

Forms of recovery for women and children victims carried out by P2TP2A can be implemented through:

a. rehabilitation and accompaniment activities are carried out for a maximum of one month; b. P2TP2A is obligated to provide competent resources such as psychiatrists and psychologists; c. P2TP2A is obligated to provide physical and psychological health tests for victims of violence;

CHAPTER VII

GUIDANCE, CONTROL AND

SUPERVISION

Article 15

(1) The Regent is authorized to conduct guidance, control and supervision of all activities related to the implementation of prevention, protection and recovery of women and children victims of violence and exploitation in the region.

(2) The authority as referred to in paragraph (1) is technically operationally implemented by the Agency.

Article 16

Guidance, control and supervision as referred to in Chapter VII are directed to:

a. increase the level of public awareness so as to be able to prevent, protect and/or restore the physical and/or psychological condition of women and children victims of violence;
b. fulfillment of the community's needs for information and protection services for women and children victims of violence;
c. provide facilities in order to support the improvement of efforts to implement prevention, protection, and recovery of women and children victims of violence;
d. improve the quality of health for women and children who experience violence.

CHAPTER VIII

COMMUNITY PARTICIPATION

Article 17

(1) The community may participate in efforts to prevent, protect and recover victims of violence against women and children.

(2) Community participation as referred to in paragraph (1) may be carried out by individuals or organizations.

(3) Community participation as referred to in paragraph (2) may be carried out in the form of: a. preventing the occurrence of violence against women and children;

b. submitting reports to the authorities if violence against women and children occurs; c. providing assistance to victims of violence against women and children.

CHAPTER IX

REPORTING

Article 18

(1) P2TP2A is obligated to report the implementation of prevention, protection and recovery of women and children victims of violence to the Regent.

(2) Reporting as referred to in paragraph (1) includes:

a. Administration;
b. Finance;
c. Services;
d. Performance.
(3) Submission of written reports as referred to in paragraph (2) is carried out at least once every 3 (three) months.

CHAPTER X

SOURCES OF FUNDING

Article 19

(1) Expenditure for programs and activities for the Implementation of Prevention, Protection and Recovery of Women and Children as Victims of Violence is sourced from the Regional Budget of Indramayu Regency.

(2) In addition to being sourced from the Regional Budget, expenditure for programs and activities as referred to in paragraph (1) may be sourced from the State Budget, Provincial Regional Budget and other legitimate and non-binding sources.

(3) To support the operational expenditure of P2TP2A, a budget sourced from the Regional Revenue and Expenditure Budget (APBD) may be provided.

CHAPTER XI

CRIMINAL SANCTIONS

Article 20

(1) Any person who obstructs, disrupts and/or hinders the implementation of programs for the implementation of prevention, protection and recovery of women and children victims of violence, shall be subject to imprisonment for a maximum of 3 (three) months or a fine of at most Rp. 50,000,000.00 (Fifty Million Rupiah).

(2) In addition to criminal acts as referred to in paragraph (1), whether in the form of criminal violations and/or actions that result in losses to the Regional Government, individuals or other parties, shall be subject to criminal law in accordance with applicable laws and regulations.

CHAPTER XII

INVESTIGATION

Article 21

(1) Certain Civil Servant Officials within the Regional Government are granted special authority as Investigators to conduct investigation of criminal acts, as referred to in the Criminal Procedure Code.

(2) The Investigators as referred to in paragraph (1) are certain civil servant officials within the Regional Government appointed by authorized officials in accordance with the provisions of laws and regulations.

(3) The authority of the Investigators as referred to in paragraph (1) is:

a. to receive, search for, collect, and examine information or reports regarding criminal acts so that such information or reports become more complete and clear;
b. to examine, search for, and collect information regarding individuals or Entities about the truth of acts committed in connection with criminal acts;
c. to request information and evidence from individuals or Entities in connection with criminal acts;
d. to examine books, records, and other documents regarding criminal acts;
e. to conduct searches to obtain evidence of bookkeeping, recording, and other documents, as well as to seize such evidence;
f. to request assistance from experts in the framework of carrying out criminal investigation duties;
g. to order a stop and/or prohibit someone from leaving a room or place when an examination is in progress and to examine the identity of persons, objects, and/or documents carried;
h. to photograph persons related to criminal acts;
i. to summon persons to be heard and examined as suspects or witnesses;
j. to terminate the investigation; and/or
k. to take other actions necessary for the smooth conduct of criminal investigations in accordance with the provisions of laws and regulations.
(4) The Investigators as referred to in paragraph (1) notify the commencement of the investigation and submit the investigation results to the Public Prosecutor through the Investigator official of the State Police of the Republic of Indonesia, in accordance with the provisions stipulated in the Criminal Procedure Code.

CHAPTER XIII

CLOSING PROVISIONS

Article 22

This Regional Regulation shall come into force on the date of promulgation. In order that everyone may know, orders the promulgation of this Regional Regulation by placing it in the Regional Gazette of Indramayu Regency

I. GENERAL

The State has an obligation to provide a sense of security to its citizens from threats and actions that can disturb and damage security, psychological, physical, sexual, and economic well-being. This is philosophically stated in the preamble of the 1945 Constitution, that the purpose of the establishment of the Unitary State of the Republic of Indonesia is to protect the entire nation and the whole homeland of Indonesia.

Because the Indonesian government has signed the Universal Declaration of Human Rights (UDHR 1948) and ratified CEDAW (Law Number 7 of 1984 on the elimination of all forms of Discrimination against Women), it is obliged to fulfill these provisions. The enforcement and fulfillment of Human Rights is the responsibility of the State.

Through Law Number 23 of 2002 on Child Protection, the government recognizes the existence of children's rights. The Human Rights inherent in children include, among others, basic rights as human beings, namely the Right to life, the Right to Growth and Development, the Right to Protection and the Right to Participation.

To fulfill the right to life, children need food in sufficient quantities, healthy and nutritious, as well as optimal health access. Protection from violence that threatens their safety and health must be provided from an early age. Protection is provided to prevent physical, mental and sexual violence and exploitation. To fulfill the right to growth and development, children need space to play, exercise, education appropriate to their physical and mental development. In fulfilling children's rights, every government administrator, community and parents are obliged to understand and care about children's rights. The existence of legal instruments and law enforcement officers who defend children's interests is necessary for this protection effort. Legislation based on the principle of defending the best interests of the child (The best Interest of Child) is necessary to realize legal protection.

Furthermore, it is not uncommon to find cases of women and children who experience problems of physical, psychological, sexual and social violence. For example, women and children victims of persecution, neglect, eviction and abuse by parents or other people. For women and children experiencing such problems, the government must provide a Safe House (Children protection home) or integrated service center for women and children (P2TP2A) for women and children victims of acts of violence. A Safe House (shelter) is a safe place and is Friendly for women and children.

The Integrated Service Center is equipped with assistants from educators, doctors, psychologists, clerics/religious leaders and networked with lawyers for women and children as well as the RPK Unit at the Police.

The fate of women and children victims of acts of violence must be attended to by the government. The large number of cases of acts of violence against women and children are often caused by factors that develop in society, for example low levels of economy, education, environment in the industrial sector. Therefore victims of acts of violence like this need to receive protection in accordance with the principles of truth, justice, legal certainty, equality and upholding Human Rights.

From the framework above, the regional government is responsible for taking actions both legally, politically, economically and socially to prevent, suppress, reduce, and eliminate all forms of violence against women. One such effort is the realization of a legal framework in providing protection for women and children victims of acts of violence.

II. ARTICLE BY ARTICLE

Article 1

Sufficiently Clear

Article 2

What is meant by "Respect for victims' rights" is a series of actions to respect, appreciate and ensure the fulfillment of victims' rights.

What is meant by "Gender justice and equality" is a process to be fair to men and women and equal conditions for men and women to obtain opportunities and their rights as human beings, in order to be able to play a role and participate in political, economic, social, cultural, national defense and security activities and equality in enjoying the results of such development.

What is meant by "Non-Discrimination" is an attitude and treatment toward victims without making distinctions based on age, gender, race, ethnicity, religion and between groups.

What is meant by "Best interests of victims" is that all actions concerning victims carried out by local government, community, legislative bodies and judicial bodies must be the primary consideration.

Article 3

Letter a

The "prevention" aspect is a protection strategy effort through:

a. Primary prevention, all people, families, communities and the state in efforts to increase knowledge capacity, understanding and ensuring that violence against women and children does not occur, including policy socialization, adequate services, supportive workplace policies, and life skills training for women and children.

What is meant by life skills training includes conflict resolution without violence, stress management skills, resource management, effective decision making, effective interpersonal communication, guidance on psychosocial development of women and children.

b. Secondary prevention, aimed at community groups with high risk in efforts to improve skills, including training and victim services to ensure that violence against women and children does not occur in the next generation. Activities carried out here include conducting home visits for parents who have just had children to conduct self-assessment of whether they are at risk of committing violence against children in the future.

c. Tertiary prevention, intended to improve parenting capacity that ensures violence against women and children does not recur, here what is done is integrated services for women and children who are victims of violence, through counseling, stress management training.

Letter b The "service and assistance" aspect is immediate activities and actions carried out by professional personnel and assistance according to their respective professions in the form of counseling, therapy and advocacy for the empowerment and recovery of victims of violence.

Letter c The "recovery" aspect is an effort to restore and rehabilitate the physical and mental condition of victims which then reunites them with the victim's family and community.

Letter d The "empowerment" aspect is a process of increasing the role of the community to participate in the provision of protection for women and children victims of violence which aims to obtain access and participation in the protection process for women and children broadly.

Article 4

Letter a

What is meant by "obtaining protection" is obtaining protection from individuals, groups and institutions both governmental and non-governmental.

Letter b

What is meant by "obtaining information" is access and information about the existence of complaint centers, PPT, and all matters related to the fulfillment of their rights and involvement in the decision-making process related to assistance and case developments. Letter c

What is meant by "minimum service" is service that includes medical, essential medicolegal, psychosocial and legal.  Letter d

What is meant by "continuous handling up to the rehabilitation stage" is handling that does not stop at physical and psychological healing, but until victims can resume life in society including restoration of their good name.

Letter e

What is meant by "confidential handling" is an effort to guarantee certainty for victims that their identity, medical treatment and legal handling will not be disseminated.

Letter f

What is meant by "obtaining psychological assistance" is assistance provided by psychologists to victims suffering from trauma/other mental problems to restore the victim's mental condition. While legal assistance is assistance provided by persons and/or legal aid institutions to persons at every level of examination and during the legal process.

Letter g

What is meant by "guarantee of rights related to victim status" is an effort to provide certainty and protection for victims as family members and community.
