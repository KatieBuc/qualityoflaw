CHAPTER I

GENERAL PROVISIONS

Article 1

In this Regional Regulation, what is meant by:

1. Central Government is the President of the Republic of Indonesia who holds governmental authority of the Unitary State of the Republic of Indonesia assisted by the Vice President and ministers as referred to in the 1945 Constitution of the State of the Republic of Indonesia.
2. Region is Pesisir Barat Regency.
3. Regional Government is the administration of Governmental Affairs by the Regional Government and Regional House of Representatives based on the principles of autonomy and assistance tasks with the principle of the widest possible autonomy within the system and principles of the Unitary State of the Republic of Indonesia as referred to in the 1945 Constitution of the State of the Republic of Indonesia.

4. Regional Government is the Regent as an element of regional government administration who leads the implementation of governmental affairs which are the authority of the autonomous region.

5. Regent is the Regent of Pesisir Barat.

6. Regional House of Representatives, hereinafter abbreviated as DPRD, is a people's representative institution positioned as an element of Regional Government administration.

7. Office of Women's Empowerment, Child Protection and Family Planning, hereinafter abbreviated as Dinas PPPAKB, is a Regional Apparatus Organization that implements and handles affairs of Protection of Women and Children.

8. Protection of Women and Children, hereinafter abbreviated as PPA, is a handling effort to protect and fulfill the rights of women and children from all forms of acts of violence, discrimination, special protection, and other problems.

9. Discrimination is any differentiation, harassment or exclusion either directly or indirectly based on human differences on the basis of religion, ethnicity, race, ethnic group, social status, economic status, gender, language, political beliefs which result in reduction, deviation or elimination of recognition, implementation or use of human rights and basic freedoms in life both individually and collectively in the political, economic, legal, social, cultural and other aspects of life.

10. Violence is any act unlawfully with or without using means against physical and psychological that causes danger to life, body or causes deprivation of a person's freedom.

11. Violence against women is any act based on gender differences that results in or may result in misery or suffering of women physically, sexually or psychologically, economic, social exploitation, including threats of certain acts, coercion or deprivation of freedom arbitrarily, whether occurring in the public sphere or in private life.

12. Violence against children is any act against children that results in misery or suffering physically, mentally, sexually, psychologically, economic, social exploitation, including neglect and mistreatment that threatens bodily integrity and degrades the dignity of children.

13. Violence against women and children in the household is any act against a woman and child that results in misery or suffering physically, sexually, psychologically, economically and/or household neglect including threats to commit acts of coercion or deprivation of freedom unlawfully within the household sphere.

14. Human Trafficking is the act of recruitment, transportation, harboring, sending, transfer, or receipt of a person by threat of violence, use of violence, abduction, confinement, falsification, fraud and abuse of power or vulnerable position, debt bondage or giving payments or benefits, so as to obtain consent from the person who holds control over that other person, whether conducted within the country or between countries, for the purpose of exploitation or resulting in the person being exploited.

15. Child is a person who has not reached the age of 18 (eighteen) years including a child who is still in the womb.

16. Child marriage is a marriage conducted through civil, religious or customary law with or without registration or official consent where one or both partners are children under the age of 18 years.

17. Woman is a person (human) who has genital organs, can menstruate, become pregnant, give birth to children, and breastfeed.

18. Victim of acts of violence is an individual or group of people who experience suffering either physically, mentally or emotionally, economic loss, or experience neglect, reduction or deprivation of their basic rights, as a result of human rights violations, or criminal acts as well as acts of violence committed either by state officials or by the state or Regional Government officials or by individuals, including the victim's heirs.

19. Protection is a series of fulfillment of the rights of women and children victims of violence and discrimination starting from prevention, handling and empowerment efforts.

20. Prevention is an effort made by the Regional Government to prevent acts of violence against women and children.
21. Regional Technical Implementation Unit for the Protection of Women and Children, hereinafter abbreviated as UPTD PPA, is a Regional technical implementation unit formed by the Regional Government in providing services for women and children who experience violence, discrimination, special protection, and other problems.

22. Regional Long-Term Development Plan, hereinafter referred to as RPJPD, is a regional development planning document for a period of 20 (twenty) years.

23. Regional Medium-Term Development Plan, hereinafter referred to as RPJMD, is a regional planning document for a period of 5 (five) years.
24. Task Force for the Prevention and Handling of Human Trafficking Crimes, hereinafter referred to as Task Force, is a coordinative institution tasked with coordinating prevention and handling efforts of human trafficking crimes in Pesisir Barat Regency.

25. Parents are biological father and/or mother, or father and/or mother, or adoptive father and/or mother.

27. Community is individuals, families, groups, and social organizations and/or community organizations.

28. Child-Friendly Regency is a regency that has a child rights-based development system through the integration of commitment and resources of the government, community, business world and media that are planned comprehensively and sustainably in policies, programs and activities to ensure the fulfillment of children's rights and special protection of children.

29. Private Sector is Non-Governmental Institutions, Business World and Mass Media.

CHAPTER II

OBJECTIVES, AND SCOPE

Part One

Objectives

Article 2

The objectives of protection of women and children from acts of violence are:
a. to prevent all forms of violence against women and children;
b. to provide protection and services to women and children victims of gender-based violence;

c. to provide a sense of security to women and children victims of violence;
d. to restore the physical, psychological and economic condition of women and children victims of violence;

e. the best interests of women and children victims of violence that occur in the domestic and/or public sphere; and

f. to provide certainty of assistance to the victim in the legal process against perpetrators of violence against women and children.

Part Two

Scope

Article 3

The scope of regulation in the Protection of Women and Children from acts of violence includes:

a. Forms of violence against women and children; b. Rights of victims;
c. Obligations and responsibilities;
d. Implementation of protection;
e. Cooperation and partnership;
f. Assistance;
g. Child-friendly regency;
h. Funding.

CHAPTER III

FORMS OF VIOLENCE AGAINST

WOMEN AND CHILDREN

Article 4

Forms of violence against women and children include: a. physical violence;

b. psychological violence;

c. sexual violence;

d. neglect;

e. exploitation; and/or

f. other violence.

Article 5

Physical violence as referred to in Article 4 letter a constitutes acts that result in pain accompanied by injury, wounds or disability to the body, miscarriage, unconsciousness and/or causing death of women and/or children.

Article 6

Psychological violence as referred to in Article 4 letter b constitutes acts that result in fear, loss of self-confidence, loss of ability to act, feelings of helplessness and/or psychological suffering in women and/or children.

Article 7

(1) Sexual violence as referred to in Article 4 letter c includes: a. non-physical sexual harassment;

b. physical sexual harassment;

c. forced contraception;

d. forced sterilization;

e. forced marriage;

f. sexual torture;

g. sexual exploitation;

h. sexual slavery; and

i. electronic-based sexual violence

(2) In addition to Sexual Violence Crimes as referred to in paragraph (1), Sexual Violence Crimes also include:

a. rape;
b. indecent acts;
c. intercourse with Children, indecent acts against Children, and/or sexual exploitation of Children;

LEGAL DIVISION SETDA PESISIR BARAT REGENCY

d. acts violating decency that are contrary to the victim's will and religious values recognized in Indonesia;

e. pornography involving Children or pornography that explicitly contains violence and sexual exploitation;

f. forced prostitution;

g. criminal acts of human trafficking aimed at sexual exploitation;

h. sexual violence within the household;

i. criminal acts of money laundering where the predicate crime is a Sexual Violence Crime; and

j. other criminal acts explicitly stated as Sexual Violence Crimes as regulated in the provisions of laws and regulations.

Article 8

Neglect as referred to in Article 4 letter d includes: a. acts of intentionally neglecting women within her household, whereas according to the law applicable to him or due to consent or agreement he is obligated to provide livelihood and care to that woman.

b. acts that result in the failure to fulfill the needs of children in a proper manner, whether physical, mental, spiritual or social, committed by parents, guardians, or other parties responsible for their care;

c. acts of intentionally neglecting to care for or attend to children as they should be, committed by parents, guardians, or any other parties responsible for their care.

Article 9

Exploitation as referred to in Article 4 letter e includes: a. acts of economic or sexual exploitation with the intention of benefiting oneself or others;

b. acts with or without the victim's consent that include but are not limited to prostitution, forced labor or services, slavery or similar practices, oppression, extortion, physical exploitation, sexual exploitation, reproductive organs, or unlawfully removing or transplanting organs and/or body tissues or exploiting the labor or abilities of a person by another party to obtain benefits whether material or immaterial;

Article 10

(1) Other violence as referred to in article 4 letter f constitutes threats of violence and coercion.

(2) Threats of violence as referred to in paragraph (1) include any unlawful act in the form of speech, writing, images, symbols, or body movements, whether with or without using means that cause fear or restrict a person's fundamental freedom.

(3) Coercion as referred to in paragraph (1) includes a situation where someone/victim is ordered to do something in such a way that the person does something contrary to their own will.

CHAPTER IV

RIGHTS OF VICTIMS

Article 11

Women and children victims of acts of violence receive the following rights:

a. the right to be respected in dignity and worth as human beings; b. the right to recovery;
c. the right to determine their own decisions;
d. the right to obtain information;
e. the right to confidentiality;
f. the right to social rehabilitation;
g. the right to complaint handling in an appropriate, comfortable, and needs-based manner;
h. the right of victims and their families to receive facilitation in the judicial process;
i. the right to assistance; and
j. to receive continuous handling until the rehabilitation stage.

Article 12

Child victims of acts of violence, in addition to receiving the rights as referred to in Article 11, also receive special rights as follows: a. the right to survival, growth, and development; b. the right to basic population services;

c. the right to equal protection;

d. the right to be free from various stigma; and

e. the right to obtain freedom.

I. GENERAL

Acts of violence against women and children are experiencing an increasingly rising trend. Triggering factors such as economic, social, cultural and social environment are becoming more complex along with the influence of technological developments. Concrete steps are needed to provide protection by all elements of Citizens as a unity of society, as well as the role of the Regional Government as a protector for its citizens with various programs to prevent acts of violence against women and children. One effort to prevent violence against women is carried out through women's empowerment and optimization of previously owned potential, so that it is expected to be able to improve and develop themselves to play a role and be protected from the potential for acts of violence. This condition will strengthen the form of appreciation and respect for women while maintaining their role and nature as women to play a role in educating and supporting the welfare and happiness of their families.

Efforts to prevent violence against children are a shared obligation of parents, families, communities and the private sector holistically and inseparably from one another. Community service is at the forefront when there is potential for violence against children. The mechanism for preventing violence and protecting child victims of violence involves multiple sectors coordinated by the Regional Government. The aforementioned efforts are carried out with the aim of providing social rehabilitation for child victims of violence, reuniting child victims of violence with their families and/or environment, and increasing the empowerment of child victims of violence.

In connection with this, to realize the provision of protection efforts for women and children victims of violence as well as for legal certainty in the implementation of women's empowerment and protection of child victims of violence in the Regional territory, it is necessary to regulate it in a Regional Regulation.
