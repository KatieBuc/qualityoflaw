CHAPTER I

GENERAL PROVISIONS

Article 1

In this Regional Regulation, what is meant by: 1. Region is Wonogiri Regency.

2. Regional Government is the Regent and Regional Apparatus as organizers of Regional Government.

3. Regent is the Regent of Wonogiri.

4. Violence is any act that results in suffering or hardship whether physical, mental, sexual, and economic.

5. Gender-Based Violence is any form of restriction, exclusion, differentiation and all forms of treatment carried out on the basis of sex and aimed at reducing, eliminating the recognition, enjoyment or use of human rights, the consequences of which are and not limited to physical, sexual, psychological and economic violence.

6. Violence Against Children is any form of restriction, differentiation, exclusion and all forms of treatment carried out against children, the consequences of which are and not limited to physical, sexual, psychological and economic violence.

7. Domestic Violence is any act toward a person especially women which results in physical, sexual, psychological suffering or hardship, and/or domestic neglect including threats to commit acts, coercion, or unlawful deprivation of liberty within the scope of the household.

8. Physical Violence is any act that results in pain, injury, wounds or disability to a person's body, miscarriage, unconsciousness and or causes death.

9. Psychological Violence is any act that results in fear, loss of self-confidence, loss of ability to act, feelings of helplessness and or severe psychological suffering in a person.

10. Sexual Violence is any act in the form of sexual harassment, forced sexual intercourse, forced sexual intercourse in an unnatural or unwanted manner, forcing sexual intercourse with others for commercial purposes and or certain purposes

11. Economic Violence is any act that neglects members in the form of not providing proper living care or maintenance.

12. Victim is a person who experiences violence and/or threats of violence within the scope of the household.

13. Victim of Gender-Based Violence is a person who due to their sex experiences physical, psychological, economic, social, sexual suffering, and other losses caused by state policy, acts of violence and or threats of violence within the scope of household and community.

14. Child Victim of Violence is a child who receives harsh treatment whether physically, psychologically, economically, socially, sexually, and other losses caused by state policy, acts of violence and/or threats of violence within the scope of household and community.

15. Protection is all efforts aimed at providing a sense of security to victims carried out by family, advocates, social institutions, police, prosecutors, courts, or other parties either temporarily or based on court order.

16. Temporary Protection is protection directly provided by the police and/or social institutions or other parties, before the issuance of a protection order from the court.

17. Protection Order is a determination issued by the Court to provide protection to victims.

18. Child is a person who has not reached 18 (eighteen) years of age, including a child who is still in the womb.

19. Child protection is all activities to guarantee and protect children and their rights so they can live, grow, develop, and participate, optimally in accordance with human dignity and worth, as well as receive protection from violence and discrimination.

20. Family is the smallest unit in society consisting of husband and wife, husband wife and their children, or father and his children, or mother and her children, or blood relatives in direct line upward or downward up to the third degree.

21. Parent is biological father and/or mother, or step father and/or mother, or adoptive father and/or mother.

22. Guardian is a person or body who in fact exercises custodial authority as a parent toward a child.

23. Child Rights are part of human rights that must be guaranteed, protected, and fulfilled by parents, family, community, government, and the state.

24. Community is individuals, families, groups, and social organizations and/or community organizations.

25. Assistance is all actions in the form of counseling, psychological therapy, advocacy, and spiritual guidance, for the purpose of self-empowerment of victims of gender-based violence and violence against children to resolve the problems faced.

26. Integrated Service Center hereinafter abbreviated as PPT is an institution providing services to child violence victims, which is managed jointly between the Regional Government and the community in the form of medical care (including medico-legal), psychosocial and legal services.

27. Social Reintegration is the process of preparing the community and victims that supports the reunification of victims into the family environment, family substitutes who can provide protection and fulfill the needs of victims.

28. Safe house (shelter) is a temporary residence used to provide protection to victims in accordance with established operational standards.

29. Gender is a concept that refers to the differentiation of roles and responsibilities of men and women that occurs as a result of and can change by the social and cultural conditions of society.

30. Paralegal is an individual, group, association, organization/institution without a legal background who provides assistance to fight for rights and justice in society.

31. Counseling is the process of providing assistance carried out by an expert to an individual experiencing a problem that leads to the resolution of the problem faced by the client.

CHAPTER II

PRINCIPLES AND OBJECTIVES

Article 2

The Implementation of Protection for Victims of Gender-Based Violence and Children is carried out based on respect and recognition of equal human rights and dignity, non-discrimination, gender equality and justice as well as protection of human rights and the best interests of victims.

Article 3

The Implementation of Protection for Victims of Gender-Based Violence and Children aims to provide protection and services to victims of gender-based violence and children as well as guarantee the fulfillment of children's rights so they can live, grow, develop and participate optimally in accordance with human dignity and worth, the best interests of children and receive protection from violence and discrimination, for the realization of quality Indonesian children with noble character and prosperity.

CHAPTER III

SCOPE

Article 4

(1) The scope of gender-based violence includes: a. husband, wife, and children;

b. persons who have family relations with persons as referred to in letter a due to blood relations, marriage, breastfeeding, care, and guardianship, who reside in the household, and/or

c. persons who work to assist the household and reside in that household.

(2) Persons who work to assist the household and reside in the household as referred to in paragraph (1) letter c are considered as family members for the period of time during which they are in the household concerned.

CHAPTER IV

PROHIBITION OF DOMESTIC VIOLENCE

Article 5

Every person is prohibited from committing domestic violence against persons within their household, by means of:

a. physical violence;
b. psychological violence;
c. economic violence
d. sexual violence; or
e. domestic neglect.

CHAPTER V

VICTIM RIGHTS

Article 6

(1) Victims of gender-based violence and child victims of violence have the right to receive:

a. protection from family, police, prosecution, courts, advocates, social institutions or other parties either temporarily or based on protection order stipulation from the court;
b. obtain protection for personal security, family, and property, and freedom from threats related to information that will be, is being, or has been given;
c. participate in the process of selecting and determining the form of protection and security support;
d. freedom from ensnaring questions;
e. obtain information regarding case developments and court decisions;
f. receive services that are fast, appropriate, comfortable, and according to needs.
g. health services in accordance with medical needs;
h. special handling related to victim confidentiality;
i. assistance by social workers and legal aid at every level of the examination process in accordance with statutory provisions and spiritual guidance services;
j. recovery and social reintegration;
k. receive legal, psychological, spiritual guidance, economic, and social assistance.
(2) The rights of victims as referred to in paragraph (1) are implemented in accordance with applicable statutory provisions.

CHAPTER VI

OBLIGATIONS AND RESPONSIBILITIES

Article 7

(1) The obligations of the Regional Government include:

a. preventing the occurrence of violence;
b. providing protection for victims of violence; c. providing recovery services and social reintegration; d. encouraging and increasing community participation; e. cooperating with service providers in efforts to prevent, protect and recover victims of violence; and
f. supervising the implementation of services to victims with service standards that involve the community; (2) To carry out the obligations as referred to in paragraph (1), the Regional Government has the authority to: a. formulate policies and programs on the elimination of gender-based violence and children;
b. establish and facilitate the implementation of integrated services;
c. provide facilities and infrastructure;
d. increase the capacity of service provider institutions;
e. coordinate and cooperate in the implementation of protection for victims of gender-based violence and children;
f. encourage community participation; and
g. conduct monitoring and evaluation.
(3) The Regional Government in carrying out the obligations as referred to in paragraph (1) must pay attention to the rights and obligations of parents, guardians, husbands, wives or other persons who are legally responsible to the victim.

CHAPTER VII

IMPLEMENTATION OF GENDER-BASED PROTECTION AND CHILD VICTIMS OF VIOLENCE

Part One

Institutional

Article 8

(1) The Regent through the Regional Apparatus establishes a community service work unit that functions as an ICS.

(2) The Regional Apparatus as referred to in paragraph (1) in implementing Protection for Women and Child Victims of Violence may coordinate with other Regional Apparatus.

Part Two

Integrated Service Center

Article 9

(1) The ICS as referred to in Article 8 paragraph (1) is established by the Regional Government in accordance with applicable statutory regulations.

(2) The establishment of the ICS as referred to in paragraph (1) its membership consists of elements of regional government, non-governmental institutions, health service institutions, law enforcement officials, professional personnel, companion volunteers, social workers, clergy, and safe house (shelter) managers, and social rehabilitation centers.

Article 10

The duties of the ICS are to seek prevention, recovery and social reintegration, provide legal protection, coordinate and seek to increase community participation, as well as monitoring and reporting.

Article 11

Prevention efforts as referred to in Article 10 include:

a. conducting communication, information, and education about gender-based violence and children;
b. socialization of statutory regulations related to gender-based violence and children;

Article 12

Recovery and social reintegration efforts as referred to in Article 10 include:

a. providing physical recovery at health service institutions; b. assisting in the repatriation of victims;
c. providing temporary protection in safe houses (shelters); d. providing psychosocial recovery and assistance; e. providing spiritual guidance services;
f. preparing families and communities, economic empowerment, and return to school and/or other educational institutions.

Article 13

Legal Protection as referred to in Article 10 includes:

a. providing protection in safe houses (shelters); b. providing assistance in the legal process at the police, prosecution and district court levels;
c. providing special legal protection for child victims of violence can be done by appointing guardianship in accordance with applicable regulations.

Article 14

Coordination and Cooperation as referred to in Article 10 include:

a. coordinating and cooperating in handling violence cases with integrated services;
b. coordinating and cooperating with integrated services between regencies/cities;
c. coordinating and cooperating with service provider institutions for victims of gender-based violence and children;
d. coordinating and consulting with the Regional Government.

Article 15

Increasing Community Participation as referred to in Article 10 includes:

a. fostering community awareness of cases of gender-based violence and children;
b. encouraging the community to actively participate in providing information and reporting the existence of gender-based violence and children;
c. fostering local wisdom in handling gender-based violence and children;
d. organizing strengthening of community groups in handling gender-based violence and children;
e. disseminating information about statutory regulations related to gender-based violence and children.

Article 16

Monitoring and Reporting as referred to in Article 10, includes monitoring, documentation and reporting of cases of gender-based violence and children.

Article 17

The implementation of integrated services is coordinated by the Regional Apparatus Work Unit that oversees Women's Empowerment and Child Protection.

Part Three

Cooperation

Article 18

(1) In implementing protection for victims of gender-based violence and children, the Regional Government may cooperate with the Government, Provincial Government, Regency/City Government and other institutions.

(2) Cooperation between the Regional Government and the Government and Provincial Government includes consultation, coordination and reporting.

(3) Cooperation between other Regency/City Governments includes coordination, advocacy, referral, repatriation, social reintegration and facilitation of Integrated Service System development, monitoring, evaluation and reporting.

CHAPTER VIII

HANDLING AND MECHANISM

Article 19

(1) The implementation of protection for victims is carried out in an integrated manner within the ICS.

(2) The ICS in handling medical, legal, psychological, and economic protection in its implementation may partner with regional apparatus and social community institutions engaged in the field of protection for victims of gender-based violence and children.

(3) The implementation of protection for victims as referred to in paragraph (1) must pay attention to religious norms and the rights and obligations of parents, guardians, husbands/other persons who are legally responsible to the victim.

(4) Provisions regarding the mechanism for handling protection as referred to in paragraph (1) are regulated by Regent Regulation.

CHAPTER IX

FORMS OF PROTECTION

Article 20

(1) Every victim of gender-based violence and children must receive protection in accordance with applicable provisions.

(2) Protection of victims of gender-based violence and children as referred to in paragraph (1) is provided to Wonogiri residents, both those occurring within the Wonogiri Regency area and outside the Wonogiri Regency area.

(3) Protection of victims of gender-based violence and children, in addition to being provided to Wonogiri residents as referred to in paragraph (2), is also provided to non-Wonogiri residents for incidents occurring within the Wonogiri Regency area.

Article 21

(1) Forms of protection for victims of gender-based violence and children include medical, legal, medicolegal (forensic medicine), economic, and psychological protection.

(2) The form of medical protection for victims of gender-based violence and children as referred to in paragraph (1) includes treatment and recovery of physical injuries aimed at restoring the physical condition of victims, provided by medical and paramedical personnel free of charge.

(3) The form of legal protection for victims of gender-based violence and children as referred to in paragraph (1) includes services and assistance to help victims through legal and judicial processes.

(4) The form of medicolegal protection for victims of gender-based violence and children as referred to in paragraph (1) includes medical services for evidentiary purposes in the legal field.

(5) The form of economic protection for victims of gender-based violence and children as referred to in paragraph (1) includes skills services and providing economic access so that victims can become independent.

(6) The form of psychological protection for victims of gender-based violence and children as referred to in paragraph (1) includes assistance to restore the traumatic condition of victims, including the provision of safe houses to protect victims from various threats and intimidation, and providing psychological support so that victims have self-confidence, strength and independence in resolving problems.

CHAPTER X

COMMUNITY PARTICIPATION

Article 22

(1) The community may participate in assisting prevention and handling efforts for victims of gender-based violence and children.

(2) Community participation as referred to in paragraph (1) is realized by:

a. providing information and/or reporting any violence of which they are aware;
b. providing protection for victims;
c. providing emergency assistance;
d. providing advocacy to victims and/or the community regarding the handling of gender-based violence and child cases;
e. assisting in the process of submitting applications for protection orders;
f. assisting in the repatriation and reintegration process. (3) Community participation may be carried out by individuals, groups or social community organizations. (4) Social community organizations that participate in the implementation of protection for victims of gender-based violence and children as referred to in paragraph (3) shall be given guidance by the Wonogiri Regency Government through regional apparatus in accordance with their main duties and functions. (5) Guidance as referred to in paragraph (4) may include counseling guidance, skills guidance, paralegal training and operational technical guidance.

CHAPTER XI

ASSISTANCE

Article 23

Assistance is provided by persons or institutions that have expertise in conducting counseling, therapy and advocacy for the empowerment and recovery of victims of violence and have cooperated with PPT.

Article 24

(1) Assistance includes all integrated efforts to restore and strengthen the condition of victims, carried out by the Police, Prosecutor's Office, Courts, Advocates, Social Workers, and Women's or Social Organizations that care for women and children.

(2) Assistance by the Police, Prosecutor's Office, Courts and Advocates as referred to in paragraph (1) shall be in accordance with the provisions of applicable laws and regulations.

CHAPTER XII

CONTROL, GUIDANCE AND SUPERVISION Article 25

(1) Control, guidance and supervision of the implementation of protection for victims of gender-based violence and children shall be carried out by the Regent.

(2) The daily implementation of control, guidance and supervision as referred to in paragraph (1) shall be carried out by the Regional Apparatus responsible for Women's Empowerment and Child Protection.

CHAPTER XIII

FINANCING

Article 26

All activities related to the implementation of protection for victims of gender-based violence and children shall be charged to the Regional Revenue and Expenditure Budget and/or other lawful sources in accordance with the provisions of laws and regulations.

CHAPTER XIV

CRIMINAL PROVISIONS

Article 27

Criminal acts in the field of child protection shall be subject to criminal penalties as stipulated in Law Number 23 of 2002 concerning Child Protection.

CHAPTER XV

TRANSITIONAL PROVISIONS

Article 28

Upon the entry into force of this Regional Regulation, all implementing regulations related to the implementation of protection for victims of gender-based violence and children shall remain in effect insofar as they are not contradictory and/or have not been replaced by new regulations.

CHAPTER XVI

CLOSING PROVISIONS

Article 29

This Regional Regulation shall come into force on the date of promulgation.

In order that everyone may know of it, orders the promulgation of this Regional Regulation by placement in the Regional Gazette of Wonogiri Regency

I GENERAL

Gender-based and child violence is a social phenomenon that has existed since ancient times and has become increasingly prevalent recently. In fact, gender-based and child violence has been increasing, both in number and in the forms and modus operandi which are becoming more diverse. Rape, sexual harassment, trafficking of women and children, domestic violence, sexual exploitation, violence against domestic workers, pornography, exploitation of migrant workers, and neglect, will apparently continue to be encountered in daily life, both in the domestic (household) sphere and in public.

The causal factors of gender-based and child violence are very complex and interrelated with one another. These factors include, among others, legal instruments that have not been able to provide protection to victims, the concept that women and children are family property (assets), media that insufficiently support reporting on violence against women and children, public services that are not yet optimal, customs that sometimes legalize violence, issues of poverty, erroneous interpretations of religious teachings, all of which are wrapped in patriarchal culture.

The handling of victims of gender-based violence and violence against children occurring in Wonogiri, in accordance with the provisions of Article 3 of Law Number 23 of 2002 concerning Child Protection and Article 13 of Law Number 23 of 2004 concerning the Elimination of Domestic Violence, requires the Regional Government to establish and develop a system and mechanism of cooperation for handling violence. Furthermore, based on the provisions of Article 26 paragraph (1) letter b of Law Number 32 of 2004 concerning Regional Government, it is stated that the Deputy Regional Head has the duty to assist the Regional Head in coordinating the activities of vertical agencies in the region, following up on reports and/or findings from supervisory apparatus oversight, implementing women's and youth empowerment, and seeking the development and preservation of socio-cultural and environmental life. Thus, it is necessary to establish a Regional Regulation concerning the Implementation of Protection for Victims of Gender-Based and Child Violence.

II ARTICLE BY ARTICLE

Article 1

Sufficiently clear

Article 2

What is meant by "non-discrimination" is protection for all victims of gender-based and child violence without distinguishing ethnicity, religion, race, class, sex, ethnic culture and language, legal status and physical or mental condition.

What is meant by "gender justice" is fair treatment given to women and men.

What is meant by "gender equality" is a condition and position that describes a harmonious, compatible and balanced relationship between men and women in obtaining opportunities/chances in access, participation, control and benefits in the implementation of development and enjoying the results of development in family life, as well as in society, nationhood and statehood.

What is meant by 'Best interests of the victim" is that in all actions concerning victims undertaken by the government, society, legislative bodies and judicial bodies, the best interests of the victim must be the primary consideration.

Article 3

What is meant by the right to live is the child's right to survival as stated in the principles of the Convention on the Rights of the Child, including the right to identity and the right to enjoy the highest attainable standard of health.

What is meant by "grow and develop" for children is as stated in the principles of the Convention on the Rights of the Child, including the right to education, the right to play, the right to create and recreate.
