CHAPTER I

GENERAL PROVISIONS

Article 1

In this Regional Regulation, what is meant by:
1. Region is the Regency of Regent Barito Kuala.
2. Regional Government is the Regent together with regional apparatus as elements of the Regional Government administration of Barito Kuala Regency.
3. Family Planning, Women's Empowerment and Child Protection Agency, abbreviated as BKBP3A is the Regional Working Unit of Barito Kuala Regency.
4. Integrated Service Center for Women's Empowerment and Children, hereinafter abbreviated as P2TP2A, is an integrated service unit established by the Regional Government to provide services to women and children victims of violence comprehensively through information services, assistance and legal aid, counseling services, medical services and safe houses through referral.
5. Task Force for the Prevention and Handling of Human Trafficking Crimes, hereinafter referred to as the Task Force is a coordinating institution tasked with coordinating prevention and handling efforts of human trafficking crimes in Barito Kuala Regency.
6. Woman is a person who has female genitalia who can menstruate and become pregnant or has obtained legal status as a woman.
7. Child is a person who has not reached the age of 18 (eighteen) years, including those in the womb.
8. Discrimination is any distinction, harassment or exclusion that is direct or indirect based on human differentiation on the basis of religion, ethnicity, race, ethnic group, class, social status, socio-economic status, gender, language, political beliefs that result in reduction, deviation or elimination of recognition, implementation or use of human rights and fundamental freedoms in life both individually and collectively in the political, economic, legal, social, cultural and other aspects of life.
9. Violence is any act that results in or may result in misery or suffering whether physical, sexual, economic, social, psychological to the victim;
10. Violence against women is any action based on gender differences that results in or may result in misery or suffering of women physically, sexually, economically, socially, psychologically including threats of certain actions. Coercion or deprivation of freedom, whether occurring in public or private life;
11. Violence against children is any action that results in misery or suffering to children physically, sexually, economically, socially, psychologically including threats of certain actions, coercion or deprivation of freedom.
12. Domestic violence is any act against a person, especially women, that results in misery or suffering physically, sexually, psychologically and or domestic neglect including threats to commit acts of coercion or deprivation of freedom unlawfully within the scope of the household;
13. Human trafficking is the act of recruitment, transportation, harboring, sending, transfer, or receipt of a person by threat of violence, use of violence, abduction, confinement, forgery, fraud, and abuse of power or vulnerable position, debt bondage or giving payment or benefits so as to obtain consent from the person holding control over that other person, whether carried out within the country or between countries, for the purpose of exploitation or resulting in the person being exploited;
14. Physical violence is any act that results in pain, injury, wounds or disability to a person's body, miscarriage, fainting and or causing death.
15. Sexual violence is any act in the form of sexual harassment, forced sexual intercourse, forced sexual intercourse in an unnatural or unwanted manner, forced sexual intercourse with others for commercial purposes and or certain purposes.
16. Economic violence is any act that deliberately neglects family members in the form of not providing a decent living, care or maintenance.
17. Psychological violence is any act that results in fear, loss of self-confidence, loss of ability to act, feelings of helplessness and or severe psychological suffering to a person.
18. Victim is a woman and child who experiences and/or suffers either directly or indirectly as a result of violence.
19. Protection of women is all activities aimed at providing a sense of security carried out by the Police, Prosecutor's Office, Courts, Social Institutions, or other parties who know or hear or have occurred violence against women.
20. Child protection is all activities to guarantee and protect children and their rights so they can live, grow, develop and participate optimally in accordance with human dignity and receive protection from violence and discrimination.
21. Companion is a person who has the expertise to accompany victims to conduct counseling, therapy and advocacy for strengthening and recovery of victims of violence.
22. Optimal service is a dynamic effort consisting of various parts that are regularly related, followed by performance offered by one party to another party by providing benefits, in order to achieve the best goal.
23. Information is a message (speech or expression) or collection of messages consisting of order sequences of symbols, or meanings that can be interpreted from messages or collections of messages and can be recorded or transmitted.
24. Parents are biological father and/or mother, or step father and/or mother, or adoptive father and or mother;
25. Family is the smallest unit in society consisting of husband and wife, or husband and wife and their children, or father and his children, and mother and her children and among people who are their dependents.
26. Society is individuals, families, groups, social organizations and/or community organizations.

CHAPTER II

PRINCIPLES AND OBJECTIVES

Article 2

Protection of women and children from acts of violence is carried out based on the principles of:
a. humanity;
b. justice and gender equality;
c. protection;
d. best interests of women and children; and
e. non-discrimination.

Article 3

The objectives of protecting women and children from acts of violence are to:
a. prevent acts of violence against women and children including human trafficking;
b. eliminate all forms of violence and exploitation against women and children;
c. protect, provide a sense of security for women and children;
d. provide services to women and children victims of acts of violence, reporters, and witnesses; and
e. facilitate and mediate household disputes to realize the integrity of a harmonious and prosperous household.

CHAPTER III

RIGHTS OF WOMEN AND CHILDREN

FROM ACTS OF VIOLENCE

Article 4

Women and children victims of acts of violence receive the following rights:
a. the right to be respected for their dignity as human beings;
b. the right to health and psychological recovery from the suffering experienced by victims;
c. the right to determine their own decisions;
d. the right to obtain information;
e. the right to confidentiality;
f. the right to compensation;
g. the right to social rehabilitation;
h. the right to complaint handling;
i. the right of victims and their families to obtain facilities in the judicial process; and
j. the right to assistance.

Article 5

Child victims of acts of violence, in addition to receiving the rights as referred to in Article 4, also receive the following special rights:
a. the right to respect and full use for survival, growth and development;
b. the right to basic services;
c. the right to equal protection;
d. the right to be free from various stigma; and
e. the right to obtain freedom.

CHAPTER IV

OBLIGATIONS AND RESPONSIBILITIES

Article 6

Obligations and responsibilities in the implementation of protection for women and children from acts of violence are the shared responsibility of:
a. Regional Government;
b. Community;
c. Family;
d. Parents;
e. Related institutions both directly and indirectly.

Article 7

1. The obligations and responsibilities of the regional government as referred to in Article 6 letter a, include:
a. implementing policies for the protection of women and children from acts of violence established by the government;
b. establishing policies, programs, and activities for the protection of women and children from acts of violence;
c. conducting cooperation in the implementation of protection for women and children from acts of violence;
d. providing support for facilities and infrastructure for the implementation of protection for women and children from acts of violence;
e. allocating budget for the implementation of protection for women and children from acts of violence in accordance with regional financial capacity; and
f. fostering and supervising the implementation of protection for women and children from acts of violence.
2. In order to carry out the obligations and responsibilities as referred to in paragraph (1), the regional government establishes action programs and activities for the protection of women and children in 1 (one) Regional Action Plan as a basis for SKPD in implementing protection for women and children from acts of violence.
3. The Regional Action Plan as referred to in paragraph (2), is part of the Regional Medium-Term Development Plan (RPJMD).
4. Further provisions regarding the Regional Action Plan as referred to in paragraph (2), shall be regulated by Regent Regulation.

Article 8

1. The obligations and responsibilities of the community as referred to in Article 6 letter b, shall be implemented in the form of community participation.
2. Forms of community participation as referred to in paragraph (1), include:
a. preventing the occurrence of acts of violence against women and children;
b. providing information and/or reporting acts of violence against women and children to law enforcement or authorized parties; and
c. participating in providing assistance and/or handling victims of acts of violence.
3. Forms of community participation as referred to in paragraph (2), shall be carried out responsibly in accordance with the provisions of laws and regulations.

Article 9

The obligations of family and/or parents as referred to in Article 6 letters c and d, who legally have full responsibility to prevent all forms of violence and protect women and children as family members.

CHAPTER V

PREVENTION OF ACTS OF VIOLENCE

Article 10

1. To prevent the occurrence of acts of violence against women and children, the regional government conducts empowerment and awareness-raising to families, parents, and the community by providing information, guidance and/or counseling.
2. In addition to empowerment and awareness-raising as referred to in paragraph (1), the Regional government conducts efforts as follows:
a. increasing the quantity and quality of education both formal and non-formal and informal;
b. opening accessibility to obtain education, training, funding, income enhancement and social services;
c. opening employment opportunities for women;
d. building community participation and concern for the prevention of protection of women and children from acts of violence;
e. building and providing a complete and easily accessible information system;
f. building networks and cooperation with law enforcement apparatus, government apparatus, universities and various non-governmental organizations that work and/or care for women and children; and
g. opening complaint posts for the protection of women and children from acts of violence.

Article 11

1. Prevention of the occurrence of acts of violence against women and children as referred to in Article 10, shall be implemented by SKPD whose duties and functions are in the fields of:
a. social affairs;
b. health;
c. education;
d. manpower;
e. women's empowerment and child protection;
f. mental and spiritual; and
g. public order and security.
2. Prevention of acts of violence by SKPD as referred to in paragraph (1), shall be implemented in an integrated and sustainable manner based on the Regional Action Plan.

CHAPTER VI

SERVICES FOR VICTIMS OF ACTS OF VIOLENCE

Article 12

1. Forms of services provided to women and children victims of acts of violence, are as follows:
a. complaint services;
b. health services and psychological services;
c. legal assistance;
d. repatriation;
e. rehabilitation, social reintegration; and
f. assistance services.
2. Forms of services as referred to in paragraph (1), shall be implemented in accordance with minimum service standards established by the government and implemented by SKPD whose duties and functions are in the fields of:
a. social affairs;
b. health;
c. women's empowerment and child protection;
d. mental and spiritual;
e. public order and security.
3. In carrying out the duties and functions as referred to in paragraph (1), the Regional government cooperates with government agencies, other regional governments, and the community.
4. Further provisions regarding the procedures for handling services for women and children from acts of violence as referred to in paragraph (1), paragraph (2), and paragraph (3), shall be regulated by Regent Regulation.

Article 13

The regional government is obliged to provide complaint services for the protection of women and children from acts of violence.

Article 14

The regional government and the community or social service institutions may establish recovery houses or safe houses.

Article 15

Further provisions regarding complaint services and social services as referred to in Article 13 and Article 14, shall be regulated by Regent Regulation.

CHAPTER VII

INSTITUTIONS

Article 16

1. In the context of providing protection services to women and children from acts of violence, the regional government establishes P2TP2A as an integrated service center for the protection of women and children from acts of violence including victims of acts of violence.
2. P2TP2A as referred to in paragraph (1), functions as an integrated service center in providing protection to women and children from acts of violence.
3. Further provisions regarding the Organization and Work Procedures of P2TP2A as referred to in paragraph (1), shall be stipulated by Regent Regulation.

Article 17

In addition to establishing P2TP2A as referred to in Article 16, in order to support the implementation of protection for women and children from acts of violence, the Regent establishes:
a. task force on the criminal act of trafficking in persons;
b. regional action committee for the elimination of the worst forms of child labor.

Article 18

1. The task force as referred to in Article 17 letter a, shall consist of representatives from the regional government, law enforcement, community organizations, non-governmental organizations, professional organizations, and researchers/academics.
2. The task force as referred to in paragraph (1), is a coordinative institution tasked with coordinating the prevention and handling of trafficking in persons.
3. Further provisions regarding the establishment, duties, and functions of the task force as referred to in paragraph (1), shall be stipulated by Regent Regulation.

Article 19

1. The committee as referred to in Article 17 letter b, shall consist of representatives from the regional government, law enforcement, community organizations, non-governmental organizations, professional organizations, and researchers/academics.
2. Further provisions regarding the duties and functions of the committee as referred to in paragraph (1), shall be stipulated by Regent Decree.

CHAPTER VIII

COOPERATION AND PARTNERSHIP

Part One

Cooperation

Article 20

1. In order to achieve the objectives of protection of women and children from acts of violence as referred to in Article 3, the Regional Government cooperates with:
a. the government;
b. other provinces;
c. regencies/cities; and
d. non-governmental institutions.

a. exchange of data and information;

c. provision of evidence and witnesses;

b. education and training for women victims of acts of violence;

d. fostering and increasing economic independence of women victims of acts of violence.
3. Partnership as referred to in paragraph (1) and paragraph (2), shall be set forth in the form of an agreement.

CHAPTER IX

GUIDANCE AND SUPERVISION

Article 22

1. The Regional Government is obliged to conduct guidance and supervision of the implementation of protection of women and children from acts of violence.
2. Guidance as referred to in paragraph (1), includes:
a. guidelines and fulfillment standards;
b. technical guidance and training;
c. provision of facilities;
d. monitoring; and
e. evaluation.
3. Guidance as referred to in paragraph (2), is in order to realize the objectives of protection of women and children from acts of violence in accordance with minimum service standards implemented by SKPD and the community.

Financing of the implementation of protection of women and children from acts of violence is sourced from:

1. The regional government may provide financial assistance to community organizations, social organizations or non-governmental organizations that implement protection of women and children from discrimination and acts of violence.
2. Financial assistance as referred to in paragraph (1), is in accordance with regional financial capacity, and is implemented in accordance with the provisions of laws and regulations in the field of regional finance.

CHAPTER XI

REPORTING

Article 27

1. P2TP2A is obliged to report in writing the implementation of protection of women and children victims of violence to the Regent.

a. administration;
b. finance;

3. Submission of the report as referred to in paragraph (2) is carried out at least once every 6 (six) months.

CHAPTER XII

CLOSING PROVISIONS

NUMBER 4 OF 2015 CONCERNING PROTECTION OF WOMEN AND CHILDREN FROM ACTS OF VIOLENCE

I. GENERAL

The State has an obligation to provide a sense of security to its citizens from threats and actions that can disturb and damage psychological (psychic), physical, sexual and economic security. This is philosophically stated in the preamble of the 1945 Constitution, that the purpose of the establishment of the Unitary State of the Republic of Indonesia is to protect the entire Nation and the Homeland of Indonesia.

Therefore, the Government of Indonesia has signed the Universal Declaration of Human Rights (UDHR 1948) and ratified CEDAW (Law Number 7 of 1984 concerning the Elimination of All Forms of Discrimination Against Women), and is therefore obliged to fulfill these provisions.

Human Rights state that, the enforcement and fulfillment of Human Rights is the responsibility of the State, especially the Government. Through Law Number 23 of 2002 concerning Child Protection, the Government recognizes the existence of children's rights. The inherent human rights of children include basic rights as human beings, namely the Right to Life, the Right to Growth and Development, the Right to Protection and the Right to Participation.

Protection is needed to prevent physical, mental and sexual violence and exploitation. To fulfill the right to growth and development, children need space to play, exercise, and education that is appropriate to their physical and psychological development. In fulfilling children's rights, every government administrator, community and parents are obliged to understand and care about children's rights. The existence of legal instruments and law enforcement officials who defend the interests of children is needed for this protection effort. Laws and regulations that are principled to defend the best interests of the child are needed to realize protection that is legal in nature.

The Government of Barito Kuala Regency has established an Integrated Service Center for Women and Children Empowerment (P2TP2A) as a center for empowerment of women victims of acts of violence which specifically has the main duties and functions for empowerment of women and children victims of acts of violence.

From the above framework, the Government of Barito Kuala Regency is responsible for taking legal actions to prevent, suppress, reduce, and eliminate all forms of violence against women and children, by making a Regional Regulation of Barito Kuala Regency concerning Protection of Women and Children from Acts of Violence.

II. ARTICLE BY ARTICLE

Article 1

Sufficiently clear.

Article 2

Point a
The principle of humanity becomes the foundation of the concept of protection of women and children from acts of violence, constituting respect for human rights and the dignity of every citizen and resident of Indonesia proportionally (the second principle of Pancasila).

Point b
The principle of justice and gender equality means that gender justice is a process to be fair to men and women. Gender equality is the equality of conditions for men and women to obtain opportunities and their rights as human beings so that they are able to play a role and participate in political, economic, social, cultural, governmental activities and equality in enjoying the results of development.

Point c
The principle of protection is a principle that functions to provide protection to women and children in order to provide peace in family and community life.

Point d
The principle of the best interests of women and children, that all actions concerning women and children carried out by families, communities, and Regional Government to fulfill the rights of women and children as regulated in laws and regulations.
Point e
The principle of non-discrimination, that in providing protection to women and children from acts of violence there is no differentiation based on age, sex, race, ethnicity, tribe, religion and between groups.

Article 3

Point a
What is meant by trafficking in persons is the act of recruitment, transportation, harboring, sending, transfer, or receipt of a person by threat of violence, use of violence, abduction, confinement, falsification of documents or identity, fraud, abuse of power or position of vulnerability, debt bondage or giving payment or benefits, so as to obtain consent from the person holding control or other person, whether carried out within the country or between countries for the purpose of exploitation or resulting in the person being exploited.

Point b
What is meant by exploitation is an act with or without the consent of the victim which includes prostitution, forced labor or service, slavery or practices similar to slavery, oppression, extortion, utilization of physical sexual reproductive organs or unlawfully, transferring/utilizing labor or transplanting organs and or body tissues or utilizing the labor or ability of a person by another party to obtain benefits both material and immaterial.

Point c
Sufficiently clear.

Point d
What is meant by witness is a person who sees, hears and experiences events of violence and human rights violations.

Point e
Mediation is carried out for cases of domestic violence that fall into the category of complaint offenses as regulated in Law No. 23 of 2004 concerning the Elimination of Domestic Violence.

Article 4

Point a
What is meant by the right to have one's dignity respected is upholding human rights.

Point b
Sufficiently clear.

Point c
Sufficiently clear.

Point d
What is meant by the right to information is the right to obtain information, statements, ideas, and signs that contain value, meaning and messages, whether data, facts or explanations that can be seen, heard, and read that are presented in various packages and formats in accordance with the development of information and communication technology both electronically and non-electronically related to acts of violence.

Point e
Sufficiently clear.

Point f
What is meant by the right to compensation includes economic empowerment, repatriation costs, health insurance, and education or skills.

Point g
What is meant by the victim's right to rehabilitation includes: access to medical services for physical and psychological recovery, legal assistance to restore civil rights, restoration of good name, citizenship.

Point h
What is meant by the right to complaint handling is the availability of a special integrated service unit by officers.

Point i
Sufficiently clear.

Point j
What is meant by the right to assistance includes among others psychologists, psychiatrists, health experts, clergy, advocates and family members.

What is meant by assistant is an individual who works as a volunteer to provide protection and support to women and children who are victims of acts of violence during the judicial process, these assistants can come from family members, friends or independent organizations that pay attention to witnesses and victims or advocates.

Article 5

Point a
Sufficiently clear.

Point b
The basic rights referred to include the right to education and access to parents during the handling process.

Point c
