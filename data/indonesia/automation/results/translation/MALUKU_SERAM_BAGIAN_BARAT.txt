BY THE GRACE OF ALMIGHTY GOD

REGENT OF WEST SERAM,

| Considering : | a. | that every citizen has the right to be free from | torture or treatment that degrades human dignity | and has the right to obtain a sense of security | and be free from all forms of violence; |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| | b. | that women and children as citizens, | including vulnerable groups who tend to experience | violence which constitutes an act that violates human | rights need to obtain protection; |
| | c. | that the number of violence against women and children in | West Seram Regency continues to increase and | expand while protection for women and | children has not been carried out optimally; |

d. that the legislation regulating the protection of women and children victims of violence has not yet regulated protection efforts in the regions so that institutional support and regulations are needed to guarantee its implementation;

e. that based on the considerations as referred to in letter a, letter b, letter c, and letter d, it is necessary to stipulate a regional regulation concerning the implementation of protection for women and children victims of violence.

CHAPTER I

GENERAL PROVISIONS

Article 1

In this Regional Regulation, what is meant by:

(1) Region is West Seram Regency.
(2) Regional Government is the Administration of Government Affairs by the regional government and the Regional House of Representatives.
(3) Regent is the Regent of West Seram.
(4) Regional House of Representatives, hereinafter abbreviated as DPRD, is the regional people's representative institution of West Seram Regency as an element of Regional Government administration.
(5) Administration is the form of implementation of Protection for Women and Child Victims of Violence.
(6) Protection is all efforts aimed at providing a sense of security to Victims carried out by family, advocates, social institutions, police, prosecution, courts, or other parties, either temporarily or based on court determination.
(7) Protection of Women is all activities aimed at providing a sense of security carried out by the police, prosecution, courts, social institutions, or other parties who know or hear that Violence against Women will occur or has occurred.
(8) Child Protection is all activities to guarantee and protect Children and their rights so that they can live, grow and develop and participate optimally in accordance with human dignity and receive Protection from Violence and discrimination.
(9) Violence is any act unlawfully with or without means against physical and psychological aspects that causes danger to life, body or results in the deprivation of a person's freedom.
(10) Violence against Women is any act based on gender distinction that results or may result in suffering or harm to Women physically, sexually or psychologically, economic exploitation, socially, including threats of certain actions, coercion or arbitrary deprivation of freedom, whether occurring in the public sphere or in private life. (11) Violence against Children is any act against a Child that results in suffering or harm physically, mentally, sexually, psychologically, economic exploitation, socially, including neglect and maltreatment that threatens the integrity of the body and degrades the dignity of the Child.
(12) Physical Violence is any act that results in pain, injury, wounds or disability to a person's body, miscarriage, unconsciousness and/or causes death.
(13) Sexual Violence is any act that violates a person's human dignity based on gender discrimination, which targets a person's body and sexuality that results or may result in physical, psychological, economic, sexual, political, and/or social harm or suffering to the victim.
(14) Economic violence is any act that neglects family members in the form of not providing proper livelihood, care or maintenance.
(15) Psychological Violence is any act that results in fear, loss of self-confidence, loss of ability to act, feelings of helplessness and/or severe psychological suffering to a person.
(16) Victim is Women and Children, as well as vulnerable groups, such as persons with disabilities, who experience acts of Violence.
(17) Victim of Violence is an individual or group of people who experience physical, mental or emotional suffering, economic loss, or experience neglect, reduction or deprivation of their basic rights, as a result of human rights violations, or criminal acts as well as acts of Violence committed either by state apparatus or by the state or Regional Government apparatus or by individuals.
(18) Service is immediate activities and actions carried out by assistants/paralegals, Professional personnel in accordance with their profession in the form of psychologists and social workers, including advocates for the empowerment and recovery of Victims of Violence.
(19) Assistance is activities and actions carried out by assistants during the service process.
(20) Assistant is a person or representative of an institution who has the expertise to provide assistance.
(21) Repatriation is an effort to return Victims of Violence to family, substitute family, or community or other social institutions that can provide social support and prevention efforts.
(22) Rehabilitation is the recovery of Victims from psychosocial disorders and restoration of normal social functioning, both in the family and in society.
(23) Recovery in the broad sense, hereinafter abbreviated as PDML, is a process of supporting women and child victims of violence to become empowered to make decisions and pursue a just, dignified, and prosperous life, through a victim-centered approach, rights-based, multidimensional, community-based, and sustainable, continuous, and participatory in nature, carried out by the regional government involving the role of family and community.
(24) Institution for the administration of protection of women and children (hereinafter abbreviated as LPPA) is an integrated service unit established by the regional government to provide services to women and child victims of violence comprehensively, with quality, including complaint and information services, assistance and legal aid, PDML (recovery in the broad sense) services, consisting of counseling, psychosocial, fulfillment of economic, social and cultural rights, medical services, and safe houses through a referral mechanism;
(25) Counseling is an effort to help and or assist victims in interpreting the facts related to choices, plans, or adjustments that need to be made in the psychological development of victims carried out by psychologists or psychiatrists.
(26) Psychosocial is a victim recovery mechanism that integrates social support in the community and fulfillment of victims' rights by the government including opening access and information for victims.
(27) Social Reintegration is an effort to reunite Victims with family, community, institutions, or other social environments that can provide Protection.
(28) Social Reintegration is an effort to reunite victims with family, community, institutions or other social environments that can provide protection and fulfillment of needs for victims.
(29) Integrated Service Center for Women and Children Empowerment, hereinafter abbreviated as LPPA, is an Integrated Service Unit established by the Regional government to provide services to Women and Child victims of violence comprehensively and with quality including Information services related to victims' rights, Service for Handling complaints of victims of violence against women and children, Health Services for women and child victims of violence, Psychosocial Services for women and child victims of violence, legal assistance services and repatriation and Social Reintegration services as well as safe house services through a referral mechanism.
(30) Regional Action Plan is the foundation and guideline for relevant agencies, vertical agencies, and the community, in carrying out activities for the Administration of Prevention and Protection of Women and Child Victims of Violence.
(31) Safe House is temporary housing provided to provide Protection to Victims in accordance with predetermined standards.
(32) Child is a person who has not yet reached the age of 18 (eighteen) years, including a Child who is still in the womb.
(33) Woman is an adult human of female sex and a person who is recognized by law as a Woman.
(34) Society is individuals, families, groups, social organizations and/or community organizations.
(35) Family is a person who has a blood relationship in a straight line upward or downward and in a collateral line up to the third degree, or who has a marital relationship, or a person who is a dependent of a Woman and/or Child.
(36) Parents are biological father and/or mother, or stepfather and/or stepmother, or adoptive father and/or adoptive mother.
(37) The Administration of Protection of Women and Children is based on the principles of:
a. respect for human rights;
b. non-discrimination;
c. justice and gender equality;
d. the best interests of the Child;
e. respect for the opinions of Women and Children; and f. justice and protection for women and child Victims of Violence.
g. The right of children to life, survival, and development. (38) The Administration of Protection of Women and Children aims to: a. provide Services and Protection to Women and child victims of violence based on gender-based violence and the best interests of children that occur in the home and in society (public);
b. prevent all forms of Violence against Women and Children; and
c. empower Women and Child Victims of Violence.
(39) The scope of Protection of Women and Children includes: a. institutional matters;
b. forms and mechanisms of handling;
c. prevention of acts of violence against women and children; d. assistance mechanisms;
e. services for Victims of Violence;
f. recovery of victims of violence; and
g. empowerment of Victims and their families.

CHAPTER II

RIGHTS OF WOMEN AND CHILDREN

Article 2

Every Woman and Child has the right:

a. to be respected for their dignity and worth as human beings; b. to receive Protection from family, community, Regional Government and/or other parties either temporarily or based on a court order for Protection;
c. to restoration of physical, psychological and sexual health according to the suffering experienced by Victims of Violence;
d. to special handling related to the confidentiality of Victims; e. to assistance by social workers and legal aid at every level of the examination process in accordance with the provisions of laws and regulations;
f. to spiritual guidance services; and
g. to determine their own decisions.

CHAPTER III

OBLIGATIONS AND RESPONSIBILITIES

Article 3

The obligation and responsibility to provide Protection to Women and Children is the responsibility of:

a. Regional government;
b. community;
c. family; and or parents

Article 4

(1) The obligations and responsibilities of the Regional Government as referred to in Article 3 letter a, include:

a. implementing policies for the Protection of Women and Children from acts of Violence established by the government;
b. establishing policies, programs and activities for the Protection of Women and Children from acts of Violence;
c. conducting cooperation in the Implementation of Protection of Women and Children from acts of Violence;
d. providing support for facilities and infrastructure for the implementation of Protection of Women and Children from acts of Violence;
e. allocating budget for the Implementation of Protection of Women and Children from acts of Violence according to the Regional financial capacity; and
f. fostering and supervising the Implementation of Protection of Women and Children from acts of Violence.
(2) In order to carry out the obligations and responsibilities of the Regional Government as referred to in paragraph (1), the regent establishes programs and action activities for the Protection of Women and Children in a Regional action plan.
(3) The Regional action plan as referred to in paragraph (2) is part of the Regional medium-term development plan. (4) Further provisions regarding the Regional action plan as referred to in paragraph (2) are regulated by Regent Regulation.

Article 5

The obligations and responsibilities of the community as referred to in Article 3 letter b, include:

a. preventing the occurrence of acts of Violence against Women and Children; b. providing Protection to Victims;
c. providing emergency assistance;
d. providing information and/or reporting acts of Violence against Women and Children to the authorized parties; and e. participating in the handling of Victims of Violence.

Article 6

The obligations and responsibilities of families and/or parents as referred to in Article 3 letter c include:

a. preventing all forms of Violence and protecting Women and Children as family members.
b. Providing support for women and children victims of violence who are part of their family members

CHAPTER IV

IMPLEMENTATION OF PROTECTION

Part One

Institutions

Article 7

(1) The Implementation of Protection of Women and Children Victims of Violence is carried out in an integrated manner through P2TP2A.

(2) The establishment of P2TP2A. as referred to in paragraph (1) is stipulated by Regent Decree.

(3) Further provisions regarding the position, duties, functions and working procedures of P2TP2A. as referred to in paragraph (1) are regulated by Regent Regulation.

Part Two

Forms and Prevention Mechanisms

Article 8

Forms of prevention of acts of Violence against Women and Children carried out by P2TP2A. can be implemented through: a. socialization activities of laws and regulations to the community related to the Protection of the rights of Women and Children; and b. training of P2TP2A. members and OPD, vertical institutions and other related service institutions regarding the implementation of their duties in preventing acts of Violence against Women and Children. c. Implementation of coordination mechanisms between institutions that have main duties and functions related to the prevention and handling of violence against women and children

d. Implementation of referral mechanisms in the handling and recovery of victims of violence against women and children

Article 9

(1) LPPA in carrying out its duties and functions may coordinate with P2TP2A. Sub-district, Village and competent parties in carrying out prevention and handling efforts for acts of Violence against Women and Children.

(2) Further provisions regarding the mechanism for implementing prevention duties as referred to in paragraph (1), in accordance with standard operating procedures.

(3) Further provisions regarding the mechanism for implementing duties as in paragraph (1) are regulated by Regent regulation.

Article 10

Service standards for Victims organized by P2TP2A. in cooperation with related institutions, include:

a. medical services;
b. medicolegal services;
c. psychosocial services;
d. legal services; and
e. support services for economic independence.

Article 11

(1) P2TP2A. coordinates with P2TP2A. Sub-district and Village which is conducted every 3 (three) months in carrying out service duties for Victims of Violence against Women and Children.

(2) The implementation of service duties as referred to in paragraph (1) is carried out in accordance with standard operating procedures.

Part Three

Prevention of Acts of Violence

Article 12

(1) The Regional Government conducts empowerment and awareness raising to families, parents, and the community by providing information, guidance and/or counseling to prevent the occurrence of acts of Violence against Women and Children

(2) In addition to empowerment and awareness raising as referred to in paragraph (1), the Regional Government makes the following efforts:

a. increasing the quantity and quality of education both formal and non-formal and informal;
b. opening accessibility to obtain education, training, funding, income enhancement and social services; c. opening job opportunities for Women;
d. building community participation and concern to carry out prevention and against Women and Children from acts of Violence;
e. building and providing a complete information system that is easily accessible by women and victims of violence including poor women
f. building networks and cooperation with law enforcement apparatus, government apparatus, universities and various non-governmental organizations that work and/or are concerned and focused on Women and Children victims of violence; and
g. opening community-based complaint posts at the village/Negeri level for the Protection of Women and Children from acts of Violence.

Article 13

(1) Prevention of acts of Violence against Women and Children as referred to in Article 12, is carried out by regional apparatus work whose duties and functions are in the fields of:

a. social;
b. health;
c. education;
d. manpower;
e. Women's empowerment and Child Protection;
f. mental and spiritual; and
g. peace and order.
(2) Prevention of acts of Violence by regional apparatus work units as referred to in paragraph (1) is carried out based on the Regional Action Plan.

Part Four

Assistance Mechanisms

Article 14

(1) Assistance is carried out by individuals or persons and/or institutions in cooperation with P2TP2A and building case referral mechanisms.

(2) The assistance mechanism as referred to in paragraph (1), is carried out in accordance with standard operating procedures (SOP).

(3) Further provisions regarding the assistance mechanism as referred to in paragraph (1) are regulated by Regent Regulation.

Part Five

Service Standards for Victims of Acts of Violence

Article 15

(1) The Regent establishes minimum service standards for services provided to Women and Child Victims of acts of Violence.

(2) Forms of services provided to Women and Child Victims of acts of Violence, are as follows:

a. complaint services;
b. health services;
c. legal assistance services;
d. repatriation services;
e. social reintegration;
f. identification services; and
g. Rehabilitation, Psychosocial and psychological services. (3) The Regional Government cooperates with government agencies, the Provincial Government, other regency/Regency governments and the community in carrying out duties and functions as referred to in paragraph (2). (4) Further provisions regarding the procedures for service and handling of Women and Child Victims of acts of Violence as referred to in paragraph (1), paragraph (2) and paragraph (3) shall be regulated by Regent Regulation.

Article 16

(1) The Regional Government is obliged to provide complaint services, in order to provide Protection to Women and Children from acts of Violence.

(2) The Regional Government is obliged to provide recovery houses or safe houses.

(3) Further provisions regarding complaint services and social services as referred to in paragraph (1) and paragraph (2) shall be regulated by Regent Regulation.

Part Six

Empowerment of Victims of Acts of Violence

Article 17

(1) The Regional Government is obliged to conduct empowerment of Women Victims of acts of Violence.

(2) Empowerment of Victims of acts of Violence as referred to in paragraph (1), shall be implemented by P2TP2A by involving other service institutions

(3) In conducting empowerment of Women Victims of acts of Violence as referred to in paragraph (1), the Regional Government prepares empowerment programs in the economic field, which include:

a. Providing necessary needs for entrepreneurship training for Women Victims of acts of Violence, in order to improve business knowledge, attitudes and skills;
b. facilitating the implementation of various job training and skills training;
c. providing assistance in developing productive economic enterprises;
d. exploring cooperation with small, medium and large enterprises, as well as financial institutions to develop the businesses of Women Victims of acts of Violence;
e. endeavoring to provide capital for Women Victims of acts of Violence; and
f. expanding access to information and promoting the products of Women Victims of acts of Violence.
(4) In conducting empowerment of Women Victims of acts of Violence as referred to in paragraph (1), the Regional Government may establish cooperation with job training institutions organized by the private sector.

Article 18

(1) Empowerment of Women Victims of acts of Violence as referred to in Article 17, shall be conducted by providing training to improve abilities, skills and independence.

(2) Training as referred to in paragraph (1), shall be conducted through: a. on-the-job training;

b. pre-placement training; and

c. job readiness training.

CHAPTER V

COOPERATION AND PARTNERSHIP

Part One

Cooperation

Article 19

(1) The Regional Government may conduct cooperation in order to achieve the objectives of the Implementation of Protection for Women and Children. (2) Cooperation as referred to in paragraph (1) shall be conducted with: a. the government;

b. the provincial government;

c. other regency/Regency governments; and

d. non-governmental institutions.

(3) Cooperation as referred to in paragraph (1), includes: a. exchange of data and information;

b. rehabilitation of Victims of acts of Violence;

c. repatriation and social reintegration; and

d. provision of evidence and witnesses and followed up in accordance with statutory regulations.

(4) Cooperation as referred to in paragraph (2) and paragraph (3), shall be conducted in the form of joint agreements.

Part Two

Partnership

Article 20

(1) The Regional Government establishes partnerships with the business sector in the Implementation of Protection for Women and Children.

(2) Partnership as referred to in paragraph (1), shall be conducted through: a. Notification of job opportunity information for Women Victims of Violence;

b. education and training for Women Victims of Violence; c. educational assistance for Women and Child Victims of Violence who are withdrawn from education; and

d. fostering and increasing the economic independence of Women Victims of Violence.

(3) Partnership as referred to in paragraph (1) and paragraph (2) shall be implemented in the form of agreements.

CHAPTER VI

GUIDANCE AND SUPERVISION

Part One

Guidance

Article 21

(1) Regional Apparatus are obliged to conduct guidance on the implementation of protection for women and child victims of violence.

(2) Guidance as referred to in paragraph (1), includes: a. guidelines and fulfillment standards;

b. technical guidance and training;

c. provision of facilities;

d. monitoring; and

e. evaluation.

Part Two

Supervision

Article 22

(1) Regional Apparatus are obliged to conduct supervision of the Implementation of Protection for Women and Child Victims of Violence.

(2) Supervision as referred to in paragraph (1), shall be conducted with the principles of:

a. professionalism;
b. transparency; and
c. accountability.
(3) In addition to supervision conducted by the Regional Government as referred to in paragraph (1), the community may conduct supervision of the Implementation of Protection for Women and Child Victims of Violence.
(4) Supervision conducted by the community as referred to in paragraph (3) shall be by conveying aspirations to the Regent or DPRD.

CHAPTER VII

COMMUNITY PARTICIPATION

Article 23

(1) The community participates in the Implementation of Protection for Women and Child Victims of Violence.

(2) Community participation as referred to in paragraph (1) shall be in the form of: a. establishing family partners at the sub-district and village level by the community;

b. establishing women and child protection units within community organizations;

c. provision of safe houses or shelters;

d. provision and management of orphanages

e. establishing rehabilitation centers for women and child victims of violence; f. conducting socialization of women's and children's rights independently; g. providing first aid to victims; and

h. reporting to the competent authorities if violence against victims occurs in their environment.

(3) Community participation as referred to in paragraph (1) shall be conducted by individuals, social community institutions, non-governmental organizations, educational institutions, religious institutions, the private sector, and mass media.

CHAPTER VIII

REPORTING

Article 24

(1) LPPA is obliged to report the Implementation of Protection for Women and Child Victims of Violence to the Regent.

(2) Reporting as referred to in paragraph (1) shall be submitted in writing, covering:

a. administration;
b. finance;
c. services; and
d. performance.
(3) Submission of written reports as referred to in paragraph (2), shall be implemented at least once every 3 (three) months.

CHAPTER IX

SOURCE OF FUNDS

Article 25

Funds for the Implementation of Protection for Women and Child Victims of Violence, are sourced from:

a. regional revenue and expenditure budget; and
b. other legal sources in accordance with statutory provisions.

Article 26

(1) The Regional Government may provide financial assistance to community organizations, social organizations, or non-governmental organizations that implement Protection for Women and Child Victims of Violence.

(2) Financial assistance as referred to in paragraph (1), shall be in accordance with the Region's financial capacity and implemented in accordance with statutory provisions in the field of regional finance.

CHAPTER X

CLOSING PROVISIONS

Article 27

LPPA that already exists at the time this Regional Regulation comes into effect, shall continue to carry out its duties until the establishment of P2TP2A. based on the provisions in this Regional Regulation.

Article 28

Implementing regulations of this Regional Regulation must be established no later than 1 (one) year from the promulgation of this Regional Regulation.

Article 29

This Regional Regulation comes into effect on the date of promulgation.

In order that everyone may know of it, orders the promulgation of this Regional Regulation by placement in the Regional Gazette of West Seram Regency.

I. GENERAL

The State has an obligation to provide Protection to every citizen in accordance with the preamble of the 1945 Constitution of the Republic of Indonesia. That Women and Children are among vulnerable groups who tend to experience Violence and therefore need to receive Protection. Violence against Women and Children constitutes a violation of human rights and therefore their dignity and honor need to be protected and their right to life guaranteed without discrimination.

Violence is any unlawful act with or without the use of means against the physical and psychological that creates danger to life, body or causes the deprivation of a person's freedom.

Furthermore, Violence against Women is any action based on gender differences that results in or may result in suffering or hardship for Women physically, sexually or psychologically, including threats of certain actions, coercion or arbitrary deprivation of freedom, whether occurring in the public sphere or in private life. Violence against Children is any act toward a Child that results in suffering or hardship physically, mentally, sexually, psychologically, including neglect and maltreatment that threatens bodily integrity and demeans the dignity of the Child. The existence of Women and Child Victims of Violence has not yet received adequate services and therefore the implementation is needed

of comprehensive Protection for Women and Children.

In order to prevent and address Violence against Women and Children in West Seram Regency so as to avoid Violence, threats of Violence, torture or treatment that demeans human dignity and honor, it is necessary to provide Protection for Women and Child Victims of Violence in the form of a Regional Regulation.

This Regional Regulation regulates Protection efforts for Women and Children, particularly in terms of institutions, forms and mechanisms of prevention, prevention of acts of Violence, assistance mechanisms, services for Victims of acts of Violence and empowerment of Victims of acts of Violence.
