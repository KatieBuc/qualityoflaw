REGIONAL REGULATION OF LAMANDAU REGENCY

NUMBER 16 OF 2015

CONCERNING

INTEGRATED SERVICE FOR WOMEN AND CHILDREN EMPOWERMENT

BY THE GRACE OF ALMIGHTY GOD

REGENT OF LAMANDAU,

Considering : a. that acts of violence against women and children which may result in physical or psychological victims constitute a violation of human rights, discrimination against women and children and crimes against human dignity, thereby hindering the creation of gender justice and equality;

b. that protection for women and children victims of violence is an effort to provide a sense of security and freedom from all acts of violence guaranteed by laws and regulations;

c. that the regional government is obligated to regulate and manage the handling and provision of services for women and children victims of violence which include law enforcement, legal protection, health services, and spiritual aspects;

d. that to improve protection for women and children victims of violence, it is necessary to establish a mechanism that can provide integrated services;

e. that based on the considerations as referred to in letters a, b, c and d, it is necessary to stipulate a Regional Regulation of Lamandau Regency concerning Integrated Service for Women and Children Empowerment.

CHAPTER I

GENERAL PROVISIONS

Article 1

In this Regional Regulation, what is meant by:

1. Region is Lamandau Regency.
2. Regional Government is the administration of Government affairs by the Regional Government and the Regional House of Representatives according to the principles of Autonomy and Co-Administration with the principle of the broadest autonomy in the system and principles of the Unitary State of the Republic of Indonesia as referred to in the 1945 Constitution.
3. Regional Government is the Regent together with Regional Apparatus as Elements of Regional Government Administration.
4. Regent is the Regent of Lamandau.
5. Regional House of Representatives is the Regional House of Representatives of Lamandau Regency.
6. Regional Secretary is the Regional Secretary of Lamandau Regency. 7. Child is a person who has not reached the age of 18 (eighteen) years, and is not married, including a child still in the womb. 8. Woman is every person who is physically born as a woman.
9. Victim is a woman and or child who experiences misery and or suffering either directly or indirectly as a result of an act of violence.
10. Prevention is all efforts carried out by government and non-government institutions aimed at providing a sense of security to women and children.
11. Protection is all efforts aimed at providing a sense of security to victims carried out by the government, family, advocates, social institutions or other community institutions, police, prosecutors, courts or other parties either temporarily or based on court decisions or determinations, in connection with acts of violence against children and women.
12. Child Protection is all activities carried out to guarantee and protect children and their rights so they can live, grow, develop, and participate optimally in accordance with human rights and dignity, and receive protection from violence and discrimination.
13. Women's Protection is all activities carried out to guarantee and protect women and their rights so they can live, grow, develop, and participate optimally in accordance with human rights and dignity, and receive protection from violence and discrimination.
14. Misery is any act that results in or can result in suffering whether psychological, physical, sexual, economic, or social to the victim.
15. Physical violence is any act that causes pain, injury, wounds or disability to a person's body, miscarriage, fainting and or causes death.
16. Psychological violence is an act that causes fear, loss of self-confidence, loss of ability to act, feelings of helplessness and or severe psychological suffering in women and or children.
17. Sexual violence is any act in the form of sexual harassment, coercion of sexual relations with others for commercial purposes and or certain purposes.
18. Service is an action carried out as soon as possible to the victim, when seeing, hearing and knowing of, during or after the occurrence of violence against the victim.
19. Companion is a person or institution that has the ability to provide assistance to victims to conduct counseling, therapy, legal services and advocacy, for strengthening victim recovery.
20. Safe House is a temporary residence used to provide protection for women and child victims in accordance with the determined standard operating procedures or abbreviated as SOP.
21. Community is individuals, families, groups, social organizations and or other community organizations.
22. Integrated Service Center for the Empowerment of Women and Children hereinafter abbreviated as P2TP2A is one form of service vehicle for women and children in an effort to fulfill information and needs in the fields of education, health, economics, politics, law, protection and prevention of acts of violence and trafficking against women and children.

CHAPTER II

PRINCIPLES AND OBJECTIVES

Article 2

Integrated services for the empowerment of women and children are carried out based on Pancasila, the 1945 Constitution of the Republic of Indonesia, as well as the principles and basic rights as regulated in the Convention on women and children, which include:

a. Respect for human rights.
b. Justice and gender equality.
c. Non-discrimination;
d. The best interests of women and or children;
e. The right to life, survival, and development; f. Appreciation for the opinions of women and children;
g. Justice and gender equality; and
h. Protection of victims.

Article 3

The objectives of organizing integrated services for the empowerment of women and children are:

a. To guarantee the fulfillment of the rights of women and children in accordance with applicable laws and regulations.
b. To prevent and protect women and children against violence. c. To encourage the community to carry out its obligations in the prevention and protection of women and children in accordance with applicable laws and regulations.
d. To restore victims as a result of acts of violence experienced. e. To take action against perpetrators of domestic violence.
f. To provide disaggregated data by sex and information on issues of empowerment and protection of women and children for the community that needs it.
g. To encourage the provision of facilities, infrastructure and various types of services in various areas of life for women and children such as data and information centers, counseling, psychological and medical therapy, assistance, education and training, referral centers, skills training and so on as an effort to improve the quality of life of women, welfare and protection of children managed by the community independently.

CHAPTER III

RIGHTS AND OBLIGATIONS

Article 4

The rights of children are as follows:

a. Every child has the right to live, grow, develop and participate properly in accordance with human dignity, and to receive protection from violence and discrimination; b. Every child has the right to a name as an identity and citizenship status;
c. Every child has the right to worship according to their religion, to think and express themselves in accordance with their level of intelligence and age, under parental guidance;
d. Every child has the right to know their parents, to be raised and cared for by their own parents;
e. In the event that for any reason the parents cannot guarantee the child's growth and development, or the child is in a state of neglect, then the child has the right to be cared for and adopted as a foster child or adopted child by other persons in accordance with the provisions of the applicable laws and regulations;
f. Every child has the right to obtain health services and social security in accordance with their physical, mental, spiritual and social needs;
g. Every child has the right to obtain education and instruction for the development of their personality and level of intelligence in accordance with their interests and talents;
h. Specifically for children with disabilities, they have the right to obtain special education, while children with exceptional abilities have the right to receive special education;
i. Every child has the right to express and have their opinion heard, to receive, seek, and provide information in accordance with their level of intelligence and age for their self-development in accordance with moral values and propriety;
j. Every child has the right to rest and utilize leisure time, to associate with peers, to play, to engage in recreation, and to be creative in accordance with their interests, talents, and level of intelligence for self-development;
k. Every child with disabilities has the right to obtain rehabilitation, social assistance, and maintenance of social welfare standards; and l. Every child while under the care of parents, guardians, or any other party responsible for their care, has the right to receive protection from the following treatment:
Discrimination;
Exploitation, whether economic or sexual;
Neglect;
Cruelty, violence, and abuse;
Injustice; and
Other mistreatment.
m. Every child has the right to be cared for by their own parents, unless there are valid reasons and/or legal rules indicating that such separation is in the best interests of the child and constitutes a last resort.
n. Every child has the right to obtain protection from: - Abuse in political activities;
Involvement in armed conflicts;
Involvement in social unrest;
Involvement in events containing elements of violence; and - Involvement in warfare.
o. Every child has the right to obtain protection from being targets of persecution, torture, or imposition of inhuman punishment. p. Every child has the right to obtain freedom in accordance with the law. q. Arrest, detention, or criminal imprisonment of a child shall only be carried out if in accordance with applicable law and can only be carried out as a last resort.
r. Every child deprived of liberty has the right to:
Receive humane treatment and placement separated from adults;
Obtain legal assistance or other assistance effectively at every stage of applicable legal remedies; and
Defend themselves and obtain justice before an objective and impartial juvenile court in closed hearings for the law. s. Every child who is a victim or perpetrator of sexual violence or who is in conflict with the law has the right to confidentiality.
t. Every child who is a victim or perpetrator of a criminal act has the right to obtain legal assistance and other assistance.

Article 5

Victims of domestic violence are entitled to receive: a. Protection from family, police, prosecution, courts, legal aid, social institutions, or other parties either temporarily or based on a protection order from the court; b. Health services in accordance with medical needs;

c. Special handling related to victim confidentiality; d. Accompaniment by social workers and legal assistance at every level of the examination process in accordance with the provisions of laws and regulations; and

e. Spiritual guidance services.

Article 6

Every child has the obligation to:

a. Respect parents, guardians, and teachers;
b. Love family, community, and care for friends;
c. Love the homeland, nation and state;
d. Perform worship in accordance with the teachings of their religion; and
e. Practice noble ethics and morals.

Article 7

Every person is prohibited from committing domestic violence against persons within their household, by means of:

a. Physical violence;
b. Psychological violence;
c. Sexual violence; and
d. Household neglect.

Article 8

(1) Physical violence is an act that results in pain, illness, or serious injury.

(2) Psychological violence is an act that results in fear, loss of self-confidence, loss of ability to act, a sense of helplessness, and/or severe psychological suffering to a person. (3) Sexual violence includes:

a. Coercion of sexual relations committed against a person residing within the scope of that household.
b. Coercion of sexual relations against a person within the scope of their household with another person for commercial and/or specific purposes.
(4) Household neglect is an act of failing to provide livelihood, failing to provide care, or failing to provide maintenance to persons within the scope of their household.
(5) Neglect also applies to every person who causes economic dependence by restricting and/or prohibiting decent work inside or outside the home so that the victim is under the control of that person.

Article 9

(1) The state, government, society, family, and parents have the obligation and responsibility for the implementation of child protection. (2) The government is responsible for efforts to prevent domestic violence.

Article 10

(1) The state, government, society, family, and parents have the obligation and responsibility for the implementation of child protection. (2) The government is responsible for efforts to prevent domestic violence.

(3) Every person who hears, sees or knows of the occurrence of domestic violence is obligated to make efforts in accordance with their capabilities to:

a. Prevent the continuation of criminal acts;
b. Provide protection to victims;
c. Provide emergency assistance; and
d. Assist in the process of filing applications for protection orders.

CHAPTER IV

IMPLEMENTATION OF PROTECTION FOR CHILDREN AND VICTIMS OF DOMESTIC VIOLENCE

Article 11

(1) Every child receives protection to worship according to their religion. (2) Before the child can determine their choice, the religion adhered to by the child follows the religion of their parents.

(3) The state, government, society, family, parents, guardians, and social institutions guarantee the protection of children in adhering to their religion. (4) Protection of children in adhering to their religion includes fostering, guidance, and practice of religious teachings for children.

Article 12

(1) The Government is obliged to provide facilities and organize comprehensive health efforts for children, so that every child obtains optimal health status from the womb.

(2) Comprehensive health efforts include promotive, preventive, curative and rehabilitative for both basic health services and referral. (3) Parents and families are responsible for maintaining children's health and caring for children from the womb.

(4) The State, government, family and parents are obliged to protect children from acts of:

a. Removal of children's organs and/or children's body tissue without regard to children's health;
b. Sale and purchase of children's organs and/or body tissue; and
c. Health research that uses children as research subjects without parental consent and does not prioritize the best interests of the child.

Article 13

(1) Regional governments are obliged to organize basic education of at least 9 (nine) years for all children.

(2) The State, government, family and parents are obliged to provide the widest possible opportunity for children to obtain education. (3) Education is directed at:

a. Development of attitudes and personality abilities of children, talents, mental and physical abilities to reach their optimal potential;
b. Development of respect for human rights and fundamental freedoms;
c. Development of respect for parents, cultural identity, language and their own values, national values where the child resides, from where the child originates, and civilizations that are different from their own civilization;
d. Preparation of children for a responsible life; and e. Development of respect and love for the environment. (4) Children with physical and/or mental disabilities are given equal opportunities and accessibility to obtain regular education and special education.
(5) Children in and around schools must be protected from acts of violence committed by teachers, school administrators or their peers in the school concerned or other educational institutions.

Article 14

(1) The Government is obliged to organize maintenance and care for neglected children, both in institutions and outside social institutions.

(2) The Government in organizing maintenance and care is obliged to strive for and assist children, so that children can:

a. Participate;
b. Freely express opinions and think according to their conscience and religion;
c. Freely receive oral or written information in accordance with the age stage and development of the child;
d. Freely associate and assemble;
e. Freely rest, play, recreate, create and engage in cultural arts; and
f. Obtain play facilities that meet health and safety requirements.

Article 15

The Government and other state institutions are obliged and responsible to provide special protection to children in emergency situations, children in conflict with the law, children from minority and isolated groups, children exploited economically and/or sexually, children who are trafficked, children who are victims of narcotics, alcohol, psychotropic, and other addictive substance (napza) abuse, children victims of kidnapping, sale and trafficking, children victims of physical and/or mental violence, children with disabilities, and children victims of maltreatment and neglect.

Article 16

(1) Within 1x24 (one times twenty-four) hours from knowing or receiving a report of domestic violence, the police must immediately provide temporary protection to the victim.

(2) Temporary protection is provided for a maximum of 7 (seven) days from when the victim is received or handled.

(3) Within 1x24 (one times twenty-four) hours from the provision of protection, the police must request a protection order from the court.

Article 17

In providing temporary protection, the police may cooperate with health workers, social workers, volunteer assistants, and/or spiritual counselors to accompany victims.

Article 18

The police are obliged to provide information to victims about the victim's right to receive services and assistance.

Article 19

The police must immediately conduct an investigation after knowing or receiving a report about the occurrence of domestic violence.

Article 20

The police immediately convey to the victim about:

a. Identity of officers for introduction to the victim;
b. Domestic violence is a crime against human dignity; and
c. The police's obligation to protect victims.

Article 21

In providing health services to victims, health workers must:

a. Examine the victim's health in accordance with professional standards; b. Make a written report of the examination results of the victim and visum et repertum at the request of the police investigator or a medical certificate that has the same legal force as evidence.

Article 22

In providing services, social workers must:

a. Conduct counseling to strengthen and provide a sense of security for victims;
b. Provide information regarding victims' rights to obtain protection from the police and protection orders from the court;
c. Escort victims to safe houses or alternative residences; and
d. Conduct integrated coordination in providing services to victims with the police, social services, social institutions needed by victims.

Article 23

In providing services, volunteer assistants may:

a. Inform victims of their right to obtain one or several assistants;
b. Accompany victims at the investigation, prosecution or court examination level by guiding victims to objectively and completely describe the domestic violence they experienced;
c. Listen empathetically to all victim statements so that victims feel safe being accompanied by assistants; and
d. Actively provide psychological and physical reinforcement to victims.

Article 24

In providing services, spiritual counselors must provide explanations regarding rights, obligations and provide strengthening of faith and piety to victims.

Article 25

In providing protection and services, advocates are obliged to: a. Provide legal consultation that includes information regarding victims' rights and the judicial process;

b. Accompany victims at the investigation, prosecution, and examination levels in court hearings and assist victims to completely describe the domestic violence they experienced; and

c. Coordinate with fellow law enforcers, volunteer assistants, and social workers so that the judicial process proceeds as it should.

Article 26

(1) Victims have the right to directly report domestic violence to the police either at the place where the victim is located or at the crime scene.

(2) Victims may authorize family or other persons to report domestic violence to the police either at the place where the victim is located or at the crime scene.

(3) In the event the victim is a child, the report may be made by parents, guardians, caregivers, or the child concerned which is carried out in accordance with the provisions of applicable laws and regulations.

Article 27

The Chief Judge within 7 (seven) days from receipt of the application must issue a decree containing a protection order for victims and other family members, unless there are justifiable reasons.

Article 28

Application to obtain a protection order may be submitted by:

a. Victim or victim's family;
b. Friend of the victim;
c. Police;
d. Assisting volunteers; and
e. Spiritual counselor.

Article 29

(1) Application for protection order shall be submitted orally or in writing.

(2) In the event the application is submitted orally, the clerk of the local district court must record the application.

(3) In the event the application for protection order is submitted by family, friend of the victim, police, assisting volunteer, or spiritual counselor, the victim must give consent. In certain circumstances, the application may be submitted without the victim's consent.

Article 30

Upon application of the victim or their representative, the court may consider to:

a. Establish a special condition;
b. Amend or revoke a special condition of the protection order.

Article 31

(1) Protection order may be granted for a maximum period of 1 (one) year.

(2) Protection order may be extended by court ruling. (3) Application for extension of protection order shall be submitted 7 (seven) days before the expiration of its validity period.

Article 32

(1) The court may declare one or more additional protection orders.

(2) In granting additional protection orders, the court must consider statements from the victim, health workers, social workers, assisting volunteers, and/or spiritual counselors.

Article 33

(1) Based on consideration of potential danger, the court may declare one or more additional conditions in the protection order. (2) In granting additional conditions in the protection order, the court must consider statements from the victim, health workers, social workers, assisting volunteers, and/or spiritual counselors.

Article 34

(1) Police may arrest and subsequently detain without warrant a perpetrator believed to have violated a protection order, even if the violation was not committed at the place where the police officer is stationed.

(2) Arrest and detention as referred to in paragraph (1), must be accompanied by an arrest and detention warrant after 1x24 (one times twenty-four) hours.

(3) Suspension of detention shall not apply to detention as referred to in paragraphs (1) and (2).

Article 35

(1) To provide protection to the victim, police may arrest the perpetrator with sufficient preliminary evidence for having violated a protection order.

(2) Arrest as referred to in paragraph (1), may be followed by detention accompanied by a detention warrant within 1x24 (one times twenty-four) hours.

Article 36

(1) Victim, police or assisting volunteer may submit a written report regarding suspected violation of a protection order.

(2) In the event the court receives a written report as referred to in paragraph (1), the perpetrator is ordered to appear within 3x24 (three times twenty-four) hours for examination.

(3) Examination as referred to in paragraph (2) shall be conducted by the court at the place where the perpetrator lived with the victim at the time the violation is suspected to have occurred.

Article 37

(1) If the court knows that the perpetrator has violated a protection order and is suspected to commit further violations, then the court may require the perpetrator to make a written statement containing a commitment to comply with the protection order.

(2) If the perpetrator still disregards the written statement as referred to in paragraph (1), the court may detain the perpetrator for a maximum of 30 days.

(3) Detention as referred to in paragraph (2), shall be accompanied by a detention warrant.

CHAPTER V

VICTIM RECOVERY

Article 38

For the purpose of recovery, victims may obtain services from: a. Health workers;

b. Social workers;

c. Assisting volunteers; and

d. Spiritual counselors.

Article 39

(1) Health workers must examine the victim in accordance with their professional standards. (2) In the event the victim requires treatment, health workers must restore and rehabilitate the victim's health.

Article 40

Social workers, assisting volunteers, and/or spiritual counselors must provide services to victims in the form of counseling to strengthen and/or provide a sense of security for victims.

CHAPTER VI

INSTITUTIONAL FRAMEWORK

Article 41

(1) In order to provide protection services to women and children victims of violence, the Regional Government forms P2TP2A.

(2) Membership of P2TP2A as referred to in (1), consists of: a. regional government elements;

b. community elements; and

c. academic elements.

(3) Membership of P2TP2A as referred to in paragraph (2), shall be stipulated by Regent's Decree.

Article 42

(1) Integrated Service Center for Women and Children Empowerment (P2TP2A) facilitates the provision of various services to the community both physical and non-physical which include information, referral, consultation/counseling, skills training and other activities.

(2) Integrated Service Center for Women and Children Empowerment (P2TP2A) can also serve as a place to conduct training for cadres who have great commitment and concern for women and children's issues in all fields (health, education, economy, politics, law and protection of women and children from violence and human trafficking) to then be able to work together and participate in providing services to the community within a framework of improving quality of life and protection for women and children.

Article 43

Programs that can be carried out by the Integrated Service Center for Women and Children Empowerment (P2TP2A) include: a. Women empowerment.

b. Protection of women and children from violence and human trafficking.

c. Communication, information and education.

d. Increasing community member participation.

e. Capacity building for managers.

Article 44

Success indicators of the Integrated Service Center for Women and Children Empowerment (P2TP2A) are as follows:

a. Establishment and functioning of the Integrated Service Center for Women and Children Empowerment (P2TP2A) that provides women empowerment services, protection of women and children in the fields of law, politics, education, health, economy, acts of violence and human trafficking.
b. Increasing number of women and children utilizing the Integrated Service Center for Women and Children Empowerment (P2TP2A). c. Increasing number of women and children receiving training. d. Functioning of dialogue and communication mechanisms between government, community and business sector.
e. Existence of cooperation between the Integrated Service Center for Women and Children Empowerment (P2TP2A) with government and business sector.

CHAPTER VII

REPORTING

Article 45

(1) Integrated Service Center for Women and Children Empowerment (P2TP2A) must report the implementation of activities to the Regent.

(2) Submission of written report as referred to in paragraph (1), shall be carried out at least once every 3 (three) months.

CHAPTER VIII

FINANCING

Article 46

Financing for the implementation of Integrated Service for Women and Children Empowerment is sourced from:

a. Regional revenue and expenditure budget of Lamandau Regency. b. Other legal and non-binding sources.

CHAPTER IX

CLOSING PROVISIONS

Article 47

This Regional Regulation shall come into force on the date of promulgation.

In order that everyone may know of it, orders the promulgation of this Regional Regulation by placement in the Regional Gazette of Lamandau Regency.
