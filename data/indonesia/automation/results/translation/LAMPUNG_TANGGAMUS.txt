REGENT OF TANGGAMUS,

Government is obliged to be responsible in the implementation of protection of women and children against acts of violence;

Implementation of Child-Friendly Regency, with regard to Protection services, prevention of violence against women and children, regulations are needed to govern the various roles of regional apparatus institutions and other institutions;

c. that based on the considerations as referred to in letters a and b, it is necessary to establish a Regional Regulation on

Implementation of Protection of Women and Children;

CHAPTER I

GENERAL PROVISIONS

Article 1

In this Regional Regulation, what is meant by:

1. Region is a legal community unit that has territorial boundaries authorized to regulate and manage government affairs and the interests of the local community on its own initiative based on community aspirations within the system of the Unitary State of the Republic of Indonesia.
2. Regional Government is the administration of government affairs by regional government and DPRD according to the principles of autonomy and assistance tasks with the principle of the widest possible autonomy in the system and principles of the Unitary State of the Republic of Indonesia as referred to in the 1945 Constitution of the Republic of Indonesia;
3. Regional Government is the Regent and Regional Apparatus as elements of regional government administration of Tanggamus Regency 4. Regent is the Regent of Tanggamus
5. Regional House of Representatives hereinafter abbreviated as DPRD is the Regional House of Representatives of Tanggamus Regency
6. Regional Apparatus abbreviated as PD is an auxiliary element of the Regent and Regional House of Representatives of Tanggamus Regency in the administration of Government Affairs which are the authority of Tanggamus Regency.
7. Regional Planning, Development, Research and Innovation Agency hereinafter abbreviated as Bapperida is the Regional Planning, Development, Research and Innovation Agency in Tanggamus Regency.
8. Women's Empowerment, Child Protection, Population Control and Family Planning Service hereinafter abbreviated as DPPPA DALDUK and KB is the Women's Empowerment, Child Protection, Population Control and Family Planning Service of Tanggamus Regency.
9. District is the working area of the sub-district head as a regional apparatus of Tanggamus regency
10. Village or called Pekon, hereinafter referred to as village/pekon, is a legal community unit that has territorial boundaries authorized to regulate and manage the interests of the local community, based on origins and customs that are recognized and respected in the government system of the Unitary State of the Republic of Indonesia.
11. Child is a person who has not yet reached 18 (eighteen) years of age, including a child still in the womb.
12. Child's rights are part of human rights that must be guaranteed, protected, and fulfilled by parents, family, community, government, and the State.
13. Family is the smallest unit in society consisting of husband and wife, husband-wife and their children, or father and their children, or mother and their children, or blood relatives in direct line upward or downward to the third degree.
14. Parents are biological father and/or mother, or stepfather and/or stepmother, or adoptive father and/or mother.
15. Guardian is a parent or body that in fact exercises parental authority as a parent towards the child. 16. Woman is a human being who can menstruate, become pregnant, give birth and breastfeed.
17. Child Marriage is a marriage conducted through civil, religious or customary law with or without registration or official consent where one or both partners are children under the age of 18 years.
18. Children in Need of Special Protection hereinafter abbreviated as AMPK are Children in need of special protection. 19. Worst Forms of Work for Children hereinafter referred to as BPTA are the worst forms of work for children.
20. Neglected child is a child whose needs are not properly fulfilled, whether physical, mental, spiritual or social.
21. Street child is a child whose life is irregular by spending most of his/her time outside the home to seek a living on the streets or in public places.
22. Child with disability/disability is a child who experiences physical and/or mental impairment so as to disrupt his/her growth and development naturally.
23. Gifted child is a child who has extraordinary intelligence or has special potential and/or talent not limited to intellectual abilities, but also in other fields.
24. Child in conflict with the law is a child who is in conflict with the law, a child who becomes a victim of a criminal act, and a child who becomes a witness to a criminal act.
25. Adopted Child is a Child whose rights are transferred from the environment of authority of the Family of Parents, lawful Guardian, or other person responsible for the care, education and raising of the Child into the environment of the Family of adoptive Parents based on a court decision or determination.
26. Foster Child is a Child who is cared for by a person or institution to be provided with guidance, maintenance, care, education, and health because the Parents or one of the Parents is unable to ensure the proper growth and development of the Child.
27. Child economically exploited is an act with or without the consent of the child victim which includes but is not limited to prostitution, forced work or service, slavery or practices similar to slavery, oppression, extortion, physical, sexual exploitation, reproductive organs, or unlawfully transferring or transplanting organs and/or body tissues or exploiting the labor or ability of the child by another party to obtain material benefit.
28. Child sexually exploited is any form of exploitation of sexual body organs or other body organs of a child to obtain benefit, including but not limited to all activities of prostitution and sexual abuse.
29. Child labor are children both male and female who are involved in economic activities that interfere with or hinder the process of growth and development and are dangerous to the physical and mental health of children.
30. Custody is the authority of Parents to care for, educate, maintain, foster, protect and develop the Child in accordance with the religion professed and in accordance with the ability, talent, and interest.
31. Child's Rights are part of human rights that must be guaranteed, protected, and fulfilled by Parents, Family, community, state, government and regional government.
32. Community is individuals, families, groups, and social organizations and/or community organizations.
33. Human trafficking is the act of recruiting, transporting, sheltering, sending, transferring, or receiving a person by threat of violence, use of violence, abduction, confinement, forgery, fraud, abuse of power or vulnerable position, debt bondage or giving payments or benefits, so as to obtain consent from the person holding control over that person, whether conducted within the country or between countries, for the purpose of exploitation or resulting in the person being exploited.
34. Protection is any effort aimed at providing a sense of security to victims carried out by the family, advocates, social institutions, police, prosecution, courts, or other parties either temporarily or based on court determination.
35. Protection of women is any effort made to ensure the fulfillment of women's rights as a form of respect for human rights.
36. Implementation of Child Protection is a series of activities carried out by the Regional Government, community, and parents, which are aimed at preventing, reducing risk, and handling victims of acts of violence, exploitation, maltreatment and neglect of children.
37. Child Protection is all activities to guarantee and protect children and their rights so that they can live, be healthy, intelligent, grow and develop and participate optimally in accordance with human dignity and receive protection from neglect, violence and discrimination.
38. Violence Against Women is any act based on gender differences that results in or may result in misery or suffering of women physically, sexually or psychologically, economic, social exploitation including threats of certain actions, coercion or deprivation of freedom arbitrarily, whether occurring in the public sphere or in private life. 39. Violence Against Children is any act against a child that results in misery or suffering physically, mentally, sexually, and psychologically.
40. Domestic Violence Against Women and Children is any act against a woman and child that results in misery or suffering physically, sexually, psychologically, economically and/or domestic neglect including threats to commit acts of coercion or deprivation of freedom unlawfully within the scope of the household.
41. Exploitation of Children is any act involving children in activities that can harm the welfare and growth and development or endanger the safety of children with the aim of enabling others to obtain economic, sexual, social, or political benefits, including if therein there is a restriction or elimination of opportunities for children to obtain their rights.
42. Maltreatment of Children is any action towards children, including placing children in situations that can cause adverse impacts on the welfare, safety, dignity and development of children.
43. Assistance is social work that has professional competence in the field.
44. Child Neglect is any act of neglecting the fulfillment of basic needs, care, and maintenance so as to disrupt or hinder the growth and development of children, including leaving children in dangerous situations.
45. Prevention is the effort of the Regional Government and community in creating conditions that can prevent the occurrence of violence, maltreatment, exploitation and neglect of children.
46. Risk Reduction is early action towards children and their families who are in vulnerable or at-risk situations of experiencing various forms of violence, maltreatment, exploitation, and neglect.
47. Handling is an action that includes identification, rescue, rehabilitation and reintegration of children who become victims of violence, maltreatment, exploitation, and or neglect.
48. Care Environment is care by parents and care outside parental care. Care outside parental care consists of care by foster parents or adoptive parents as well as care in institutions such as orphanages or child social care institutions or other similar names.
49. PATBM is a movement from a network or group of citizens at the community level who work in a coordinated manner to achieve the goals of child protection.
50. Regional Long-Term Development Plan hereinafter abbreviated as RPJPD is a regional development planning document for a period of 20 (twenty) years.
51. Regional Medium-Term Development Plan hereinafter referred to as RPJMD is a regional development planning document for a period of 5 (five) years.
52. Safe house is a temporary residence used to provide protection to victims in accordance with determined operational standards.
53. Child Participation Forum / Regional child forum is an organization that accommodates the aspirations of children and/or groups of children in Tanggamus Regency.
54. Repatriation is an effort to return Women and Children Victims of Violence from abroad to the debarkation point/entry point or receiving area to the area of origin.
55. Social reintegration is an effort to reunite witnesses and/or victims with the family, substitute family, or community that can provide protection and fulfill the needs of witnesses and/or victims.
56. Women's Protection Service Provider Institution is a government-owned institution or legally incorporated community institution that makes efforts to Protect Women from violence, whether in the implementation of policies, activities, or administration in the provision of services
57. Child Social Welfare Institution is a social organization or social association that carries out the Implementation of Social Welfare for children established by the community, whether incorporated or not incorporated.
58. Alternative care is child care carried out by Child Social Welfare Institutions.
59. Child participation is the involvement of children in the decision-making process and enjoying changes relating to their lives either directly or indirectly which is carried out with the consent and will of the child based on awareness and understanding.
60. Child Data Information System is the collection, management, and utilization of child data which is one of the requirements in the Implementation of Child Protection.
61. Task Force for the Prevention and Handling of Human Trafficking Crimes hereinafter referred to as the Task Force is a coordinating institution tasked with coordinating the prevention and handling of human trafficking crimes in Tanggamus Regency.
62. Child-Friendly Regency/City (KLA) is a Regency/City that has a Child Rights-based Development system through the Integration of Commitment and Resources of the Government, Community and Business World that are planned comprehensively and sustainably in policies, programs and Activities to ensure the fulfillment of Child Rights, up to the sub-district and pekon levels.
63. Parahita Ekapraya Award (APE) is an award given by the central government to Ministries or Institutions, Provincial and Regency/City Regional Governments for their commitment in efforts to realize gender equality, women's empowerment and child protection.
64. Gender mainstreaming working group hereinafter referred to as Pokja PUG is a forum for consultation for implementers and movers of gender mainstreaming from various agencies/institutions in the region.
65. Technical Implementation Unit of Service, hereinafter referred to as UPTD is an organization that carries out technical operational activities and/or certain technical support activities at the Women's Empowerment, Child Protection, Population Control and Family Planning Service of Tanggamus Regency.
66. Technical Implementation Unit of Service for Integrated Service Center for Women and Child Protection hereinafter referred to as UPTD PPA is the Technical Implementation Unit of Service for Integrated Service Center for Women and Child Protection.
67. Private Sector is Non-Governmental Institutions, Business World and Mass Media. 68. Child-Friendly Community Health Center is a community health center that provides services to children completely and in an integrated manner while taking into account the needs of children.
69. Child-Friendly School (SRA) is a formal, non-formal, and informal education unit that seeks to guarantee, fulfill, and respect rights, provide protection, and support child participation.
70. Child-friendly Islamic boarding school is an effort to create Islamic boarding schools and their surrounding environment so that children can feel comfortable, clean, at home, devout in worship, happy to learn, play and interact.
71. Child-friendly place of worship is a place of worship that provides an environment that is comfortable, safe, and in accordance with the needs of children.
72. Women-Friendly and Child-Caring Village (DRPPA) is a village that integrates gender perspective and child rights in the governance of village government administration, village development, as well as guidance and empowerment of village communities, which is carried out in a planned, comprehensive, sustainable manner, in accordance with Indonesia's development vision.
73. Integrated Service and Referral System hereinafter abbreviated as SLRT is a service system that helps identify the needs of the poor and vulnerable based on profiles in the List of Beneficiaries and connects them with social protection and poverty alleviation programs carried out by the central, provincial and regency/city governments according to their needs.
74. Violence Against Women and Children Team or abbreviated as (Tim KtP/A) concerning Sexual Violence Crimes (TPKS) is the health sector that plays an important role in efforts to fulfill the right to health services for victims of Violence Against Women and Children (KtP/A), especially in recognizing or identifying cases of KtP/A, providing comprehensive services to victims of KtP/A, including making medical, social, and legal referrals.

CHAPTER II

PURPOSE, OBJECTIVES AND SCOPE

Article 2

The purpose and objectives of protecting women and children from acts of violence are:

a. To prevent all forms of violence against women and children; b. To provide protection and services to women and children victims of gender-based violence;
c. To provide a sense of security to women and children victims of violence; d. To restore the physical, psychological and economic condition of women and children victims of violence;
e. The best interests of women and children victims of violence occurring in the domestic and/or public sphere; and
f. To prosecute perpetrators of violence against women and children.

CHAPTER III

SCOPE

Article 3

The Scope in this Regional Regulation is as follows: a. Forms of Violence against Women and Children b. Rights of Victims of Acts of Violence Against Women and Children c. Obligations and Responsibilities

d. Protection of Women and Children

e. Cooperation, Partnership and Empowerment

f. Guidance and Supervision

g. Child Friendly Regency (KLA) and Parahita Ekapraya Award (APE)

CHAPTER IV

FORMS OF VIOLENCE AGAINST WOMEN AND CHILDREN Article 4

Forms of violence against women and children, among others: a. Physical violence;

b. Psychological violence;

c. Sexual violence;

d. Neglect;

e. Exploitation;

f. Other forms of violence, namely:

1) Victims of pornography;
2) Victims of disasters and conflicts;
3) Victims of drugs;
4) Victims infected with HIV/AIDS;
5) Victims of terrorism;
6) Victims of Stigmatization;
7) Worst Forms of Child Labor (BPTA);
8) Street children and deviant behavior;
9) Persons with disabilities and/or;
10) Children Requiring Special Protection and others.

Article 5

Physical violence as referred to in Article 4 letter a constitutes an act that results in pain accompanied by injury, wounds or defects on the body, miscarriage, loss of consciousness and/or causes death of women and/or children.

Article 6

Psychological violence as referred to in Article 4 letter b constitutes an act that results in fear, loss of self-confidence, loss of ability to act, sense of helplessness and/or psychological suffering of women and/or children.

Article 7

Sexual violence as referred to in Article 4 letter c includes: a. Acts in the form of sexual harassment;

b. Forced sexual intercourse;

c. Forced unnatural sexual intercourse; and/or d. Forced sexual intercourse with others for commercial purposes and/or certain purposes.

Article 8

Neglect as referred to in Article 4 letter d includes: a. Acts of deliberately neglecting women within the scope of their household, whereas according to the law applicable to them or due to agreement or contract they are obligated to provide living and care to such women.

b. Acts that result in the failure to fulfill the needs of children in a proper manner, whether physical, spiritual or social, committed by parents, guardians, or other parties responsible for their care.

c. Acts of deliberately neglecting to care for or take care of children as they should, committed by parents, guardians, or any other party responsible for their care.

Article 9

Exploitation as referred to in Article 4 letter e includes: a. Acts of economic or sexual exploitation with the intent to benefit oneself or others;

b. Acts with or without the consent of the victim which include but are not limited to prostitution, forced labor or services, slavery or similar practices, oppression, extortion, physical, sexual, reproductive organ exploitation, or unlawfully transferring or transplanting organs and/or body tissues or exploiting the labor or abilities of a person by another party to obtain material or immaterial benefits.

Article 10

(1) Other forms of violence as referred to in Article 4 letter f constitute threats of violence and coercion.

(2) Threats of violence as referred to in paragraph (1) include any unlawful act in the form of speech, writing, images, symbols, or body movements, with or without using means that create fear or restrict a person's fundamental freedom.

(3) Coercion as referred to in paragraph (1) includes a situation where a person/victim is ordered to do something in such a way that the person does something contrary to their own will.

CHAPTER V

RIGHTS OF VICTIMS OF ACTS OF VIOLENCE AGAINST WOMEN AND CHILDREN Article 11 Women and children victims of acts of violence receive the following rights:

a. Right to be respected for their dignity as human beings; b. Right to recovery;
c. Right to determine their own decisions;
d. Right to obtain information;
e. Right to confidentiality;
f. Right to social rehabilitation and social reintegration;
g. Right to appropriate, comfortable complaint handling according to their needs;
h. Right of victims and their families to obtain facilitation in the judicial process;
i. Right to assistance; and
j. To receive continuous treatment up to the rehabilitation stage.

Article 12

Child victims of acts of violence in addition to receiving rights as referred to in Article 11, also receive special rights, as follows: a. Right to survival, growth, and development; b. Right to basic population services;

c. Right to equal protection;

d. Right to be free from various stigmas; and

e. Right to obtain freedom.

CHAPTER VI

OBLIGATIONS AND RESPONSIBILITIES

Article 13

Obligations and responsibilities in providing protection and Empowerment of women and children, among others:

a. Regional Government;
b. Private Sector and Community Institutions;
c. Community;
d. Family and/or parents;
e. Business World; and
f. Mass Media.

Part One

Regional Government

Article 14

(1) The obligations and responsibilities of the Regional Government as referred to in Article 13 letter a, include:

a. Establishing policies for the protection and empowerment of women and children;
b. Implementing policies, protection, and empowerment of women and children from acts of violence in accordance with the provisions of laws and regulations;
c. Conducting cooperation in the administration of protection and empowerment of women and children;
d. Providing support for facilities and infrastructure for the implementation of protection and empowerment of women and children;
e. Allocating budget for the administration of protection and empowerment of women and children;
f. Guiding and supervising the administration of protection and empowerment of women and children; and
g. Realizing a Child Friendly Regency.
(2) In order to carry out the obligations and responsibilities as referred to in paragraph (1), the Regent together with the DPRD establish programs and activities for action on protection and empowerment of women and children in the Regional Medium-Term Development Plan (RPJMD).
(3) The RPJMD as referred to in paragraph (2) also contains the Regional Action Plan (RAD) on the protection of women and children.
(4) Further provisions regarding the Regional Action Plan as referred to in paragraph (3), shall be regulated by Regent Regulation.

Part Two

Private Sector and Community Institutions

Article 15

(1) The obligations and responsibilities of the private sector and community as referred to in Article 13 letters b and c, are administered in the form of participation of the private sector and community.

(2) Forms of participation of the private sector and community as referred to in paragraph (1), include:

a. Preventing acts of violence against women and children; b. Providing information and/or reporting acts of violence against women and children to law enforcement or authorized parties; and
c. Participating in the handling of victims of violence. (3) Forms of participation of the private sector and community as referred to in paragraph (2), are carried out responsibly in accordance with the provisions of laws and regulations.

Part Three

Family and/or Parents

Article 16

The obligation of family and/or parents as referred to in Article 13 letter d, who legally have full responsibility to prevent all forms of violence and protect women and children as family members.

Part Four

Business Sector

Article 17

The Obligations and Responsibilities of Business Sector, as referred to in Article 13 letter e among others:

(1) Company policy with Child and women perspective in order to provide protection;
(2) Products intended for Children must be safe for Children and/or comply with Indonesian National Standards;
(3) Contributing to the fulfillment of Children's Rights through corporate social responsibility.

Part Five

Mass Media

Article 18

The obligation of Mass Media as referred to in Article 13 letter f, is carried out through the dissemination of information and educational materials that are beneficial from social, cultural, educational, religious, and Children's health aspects by taking into account the best interests of women and Children.

CHAPTER VII

PROTECTION OF WOMEN AND CHILDREN

Part One

Prevention of violence against women and children

Article 19

(1) To prevent acts of violence against women and children, the Regional Government conducts empowerment and awareness to families, parents, children and the community by providing information, guidance and/or counseling, trainings and realizing Child-Friendly Regency.

(2) In addition to empowerment and awareness as referred to in paragraph (1), the Regional Government makes efforts as follows:

a. Providing material on prevention of acts of violence against women and children in formal, non-formal, and informal education;
b. Provision of accessibility to obtain education, training, funding, income enhancement and social services;
c. Creating a friendly and safe work environment atmosphere for women;
d. Building community participation and concern for the prevention of protection of women and children from acts of violence; e. Building and providing an easily accessible information system; f. Building networks and cooperation with law enforcement apparatus, government apparatus, universities and various non-governmental organizations that are engaged and/or care about women and children.

Article 20

(1) Prevention of acts of violence against women and children as referred to in Article 19 is implemented by Regional Apparatus whose duties and functions are in the field of:

a. Regional planning and Innovation;
b. Social;
c. Health;
d. Education;
e. Manpower;
f. Ministry of Religion;
g. Religious Court;
h. Population and civil registration;
i. Ministry of Law and Human Rights;
j. Women's empowerment and child protection;
k. Family planning;
l. Public Works and Public Housing;
m. Tourism;
n. Cooperatives and micro enterprises;
o. Environment;
p. Village community empowerment;
q. Regional disaster management;
r. Police;
s. Civil service police.
(2) Prevention of violence by Regional Apparatus as referred to in paragraph (1), is implemented in an integrated and sustainable manner based on RPJPD and RPJMD.

Part two

Forms and Service Mechanisms for Violence Against Women and Children Article 21

(1) In the framework of providing protection services to women and children from acts of violence, the Regional Government establishes the Regional Technical Implementation Unit for Women and Children Protection (UPTD PPA), Integrated Referral Service System (SLRT), Violence Against Women/Children Team (Tim KTP/A) at community health centers, Hospitals, schools, Islamic Boarding Schools, Women-Friendly and Child-Caring Villages (DRPPA).

(2) The Regional Government through UPTD PPA as referred to in paragraph (1), functions to implement services:

a. Community complaints;
b. Victim outreach;
c. Case management;
d. Temporary shelter;
e. Mediation;
f. Victim assistance; and
g. Victim repatriation.
(3) Further provisions regarding the organization and working procedures of UPTD PPA as referred to in paragraph (1) are stipulated by Regent Regulation. (4) To assist in the protection of women and children, the Regional Government establishes:
a. Task Force for Prevention and Handling of Human Trafficking Crimes;
b. Task Force for Prevention of Child Marriage;
c. Task Force for Child-Friendly Regency;
d. Regional Action Committee for the elimination of the worst forms of child labor or Worst Forms of Child Labor (BPTA); e. Regional Children's Forum (FAD);
f. Community-Based Integrated Child Protection (PATBM) PENA BISMA;
g. Domestic Violence Forum (KDRT);
h. Women and Children Protection Task Force (Satgas PPA); i. Women-Friendly and Child-Caring Villages (DRPPA);
j. KTP/A TEAM at Hospitals, Community Health Centers, schools, Islamic Boarding Schools.
(5) Further provisions regarding the establishment, duties, and functions of the task force as referred to in paragraph (4) are stipulated by Regent Regulation.

Article 22

(1) Forms of Regional Government services to victims implemented by UPTD PPA and others as referred to in Article 21 include: a. Medical services;

b. Medicolegal services (visum);

c. Psychosocial services;

d. Legal services;

e. Economic independence services;

f. Psychological services; and

g. Psychiatric services.

(2) Further provisions regarding service mechanisms according to standard operating procedures are regulated in Regent Decree.

Article 23

(1) Implementation of Assistance as referred to in Article 21 includes all integrated efforts to recover and strengthen the condition of victims, carried out by health workers, education, police, prosecutor's office, court, advocates, social workers, assistance volunteers and spiritual counselors.

(2) Assistance as referred to in paragraph (1) is in accordance with the provisions of laws and regulations.

Part Three

Service Principles

Article 24

The provision of services to victims is carried out with principles, among others free of charge, fast, safe, empathetic, non-discriminatory, easily accessible and confidential.

CHAPTER VIII

COOPERATION, PARTNERSHIP AND EMPOWERMENT

Part One

Cooperation

Article 25

(1) In order to achieve the objectives of women and children protection, cooperation can be carried out with:

a. Central Government;
b. Provincial Government;
c. Other Regency/City Government;
d. Non-Governmental Institutions;
e. Community;
f. Universities;
g. Business Sector;
h. Mass Media;
(2) Cooperation as referred to in paragraph (1) includes: a. Exchange of data and information;
b. Recovery of victims of violence;
c. Repatriation and social reintegration;
d. Provision of evidence and witnesses; and
e. Followed up in accordance with applicable law.
(3) Cooperation as referred to in paragraph (1) and paragraph (2) is outlined in the form of Cooperation Agreement in accordance with the provisions of laws and regulations.

Part Two

Partnership

Article 26

(1) The Regional Government establishes partnerships with the business sector and mass media in the protection of women and children from acts of violence in accordance with the provisions of laws and regulations.

(2) Partnership with the business sector as referred to in paragraph (1) is carried out through:

a. Notification of employment opportunity information for women and children victims of violence;
b. Education and training for women and children victims of violence.
(3) Partnership with mass media as referred to in paragraph (1) is carried out through the dissemination of information and educational materials that are beneficial from social, cultural, educational, religious, and health aspects of women and children by taking into account the best interests of women and children victims of violence.

Part Three

Empowerment

Article 27

The strengthening and development of Women's Empowerment Service Provider Institutions shall be implemented through:

a. capacity building of Women's Empowerment Service Provider Institutions;
b. capacity building of human resources managing Women's Empowerment Service Provider Institutions;
c. assistance in the implementation of Women's Empowerment services; d. strengthening networks among Women's Empowerment Service Provider Institutions at the regency level
e. facilitation of efforts to fulfill standards of Women's Empowerment Service Provider Institutions; and
f. development of communication, information, and education regarding Women's Empowerment services

CHAPTER IX

GUIDANCE AND SUPERVISION

Article 28

(1) The regional government shall carry out Guidance and Supervision through:

a. Coordination
b. Guidance
c. Education and Training
d. Monitoring and Evaluation
(2) The implementation of coordination as referred to in paragraph (1) letter a shall be conducted among stakeholders on a cross-sectoral basis with Regional Apparatus, and Community institutions in accordance with the provisions of laws and regulations.
(3) The implementation of Guidance as referred to in paragraph (1) letter b towards women and children who become victims through guidance on religious values and social values, Counseling, Social Rehabilitation, and Social Assistance by the competent Regional Apparatus together with other related Agencies.
(4) The implementation of education and training as referred to in paragraph (1) letter c shall be provided to women and children who become victims as an effort to improve the physical and mental condition of women and children in fulfilling their rights and needs by the competent Regional Apparatus, Vocational Training Centers and other related Agencies.
(5) The implementation of guidance and supervision towards women and children who become victims as referred to in paragraph (2), paragraph (3) and paragraph (4) shall be carried out through monitoring and evaluation of the targets to be achieved.

CHAPTER X

CHILD-FRIENDLY REGENCY (KLA)

AND PARAHITA EKAPRAYA AWARD (APE)

Article 29

(1) To realize a Child-Friendly Regency (KLA) and Parahita Ekapraya Award (APE), the Regional Government, Private Sector and Community Institutions shall pay attention to the rights of children and women related to the right to life, growth and development, protection and participation.

(2) The Regional Government, Private Sector and Community Institutions in realizing a Child-Friendly Regency are incorporated in the Child-Friendly Regency Task Force and PUG Working Group.

(3) The Child-Friendly Regency Task Force as referred to in paragraph (3) is responsible for the fulfillment of children's rights related to: a. Civil rights and freedoms;

b. Family environment and alternative care;

c. Basic health and welfare;

d. Education, use of leisure time and cultural arts activities; e. Special protection of children;

f. As well as Institutional matters;

(5) The PUG Working Group as referred to in paragraph (2), is responsible for the fulfillment of women's rights with a gender perspective.

(6) Provisions related to Child-Friendly Regency (KLA) and PUG are regulated in Laws and Regulations.

CHAPTER XI

FUNDING

Article 30

Financing for the implementation of women's and children's protection shall be sourced from:

a. Regional Revenue and Expenditure Budget (APBD); and
b. Other legitimate and non-binding sources.

Article 31

(1) Funding for protection of women and children victims of violence includes:

a. Medical services, which include doctor's examinations, treatment costs, hospital costs, medicine costs and medical support costs; b. Medicolegal services, which include examinations for Visum et Repertum and et Psikiatrikum;
c. Psychosocial services, which consist of counseling and psychological therapy as well as safe houses (shelters);
d. Legal services;
e. Economic empowerment, in the form of services for skills training and providing economic access.
(2) Funding management shall be carried out through technical institutions appointed by the Regional Government in a transparent manner and legally accountable.

CHAPTER XII

SANCTIONS

Article 32

In the event of violence against Women and Children, sanctions shall be imposed in accordance with the provisions of Laws and Regulations.

CHAPTER XIII

CLOSING PROVISIONS

Article 33

The implementing regulations of this Regional Regulation shall be stipulated no later than 2 (two) months from the date this Regional Regulation is promulgated.

Article 34

This Regional Regulation shall come into force on the date of promulgation.

In order that everyone may know of it, the promulgation of this Regional Regulation is ordered by placing it in the Regional Gazette of Tanggamus Regency.

CONCERNING

IMPLEMENTATION OF WOMEN'S AND CHILDREN'S PROTECTION I. GENERAL.

Acts of violence against women and children are experiencing an increasing trend. Triggering factors such as economic, social, cultural and social environment are becoming increasingly complex along with the influence of technological developments. Concrete steps are needed to provide protection by all elements of Citizens as a unity of society, as well as the role of the Regional Government as a protector of its citizens with various programs to prevent acts of violence against women and children. One of the efforts to prevent violence against women is carried out through women's empowerment and optimization of previously possessed potential, so that it is expected to be able to improve and develop themselves to play a role and be protected from potential acts of violence. This condition will strengthen the form of appreciation and respect for women and continue to maintain their role and nature as a woman to play a role in educating and supporting the welfare and happiness of their families.

Efforts to prevent violence against children are a shared obligation of parents, families, communities and the private sector in a holistic and inseparable manner. Community services are the frontline when there is potential for violence against children. The mechanism for preventing violence and protecting child victims of violence involves multiple sectors coordinated by the Regional Government. The intended efforts are carried out with the direction of providing social rehabilitation for child victims of violence, reuniting child victims of violence with their families and/or environment, and increasing the empowerment of child victims of violence.

In connection with this matter, to realize the provision of protection efforts for women and children victims of violence as well as for legal certainty in the implementation of women's empowerment and protection of child victims of violence in the regional area, it is necessary to regulate it in a Regional Regulation.
