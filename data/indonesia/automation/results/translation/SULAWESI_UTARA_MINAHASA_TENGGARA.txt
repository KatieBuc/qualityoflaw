REGIONAL REGULATION OF SOUTHEAST MINAHASA REGENCY NUMBER 8 OF 2016

CONCERNING PROTECTION OF WOMEN AND CHILDREN VICTIMS OF VIOLENCE

BY THE GRACE OF ALMIGHTY GOD THE REGENT OF SOUTHEAST MINAHASA,

Considering : a. that the State guarantees every person including women and children the right to be free from torture or treatment that degrades human dignity and the right to obtain security and freedom from all forms of violence and discrimination as guaranteed by the 1945 Constitution of the Republic of Indonesia;

b. that all forms of acts of violence against women and children constitute violations of human rights so their dignity and honor need to be protected and their right to life guaranteed in accordance with their nature and disposition without discrimination;

c. that violence against women and children in the Region continues to increase, so protection efforts are needed for women and children victims of violence through institutional support and Regional Regulations that guarantee protection for women and children victims of violence;

d. that based on the considerations as referred to in letters a, b and c, it is necessary to establish a Regional Regulation concerning Protection of Women and Children Victims of Violence.

In View of : 1. Article 18 Paragraph (6) of the 1945 Constitution of the Republic of Indonesia;

CHAPTER I GENERAL PROVISIONS

In this Regional Regulation, the following terms shall mean:

1. Region is Southeast Minahasa Regency.
2. Regional Government is the administration of governmental affairs by the Regional Government and the Regional House of Representatives according to the Principles of Autonomy and assistance duties with the principle of the broadest possible Autonomy in the system and principles of the Unitary State of the Republic of Indonesia as referred to in the 1945 Constitution of the Republic of Indonesia.
3. Regional Government is the Regional Head as an element of regional government administration who leads the implementation of governmental affairs that are the authority of the autonomous region.
4. Regent is the Regent of Southeast Minahasa.
5. Provincial Government is the Provincial Government of North Sulawesi.
6. Regional House of Representatives, hereinafter abbreviated as DPRD, is the Regional People's Representative Institution of Southeast Minahasa Regency as an element of Regional Government administration.
7. Regional Work Unit, hereinafter abbreviated as SKPD, is a Regional Government Work Unit related to the implementation of child protection.
8. Regional Revenue and Expenditure Budget, hereinafter abbreviated as APBD, is the financial plan of the Region that is discussed and jointly approved by the Regional Government and DPRD and established by Regional Regulation.
9. Victim is a woman and child who experiences suffering and/or distress either directly or indirectly as a result of violence.
10. Child is a person who has not yet reached the age of 18 (eighteen) years, including a child still in the womb.
11. Child Protection is all activities to guarantee and protect children and their rights so that they can live, grow and develop and participate optimally in accordance with human dignity and receive protection from violence, exploitation, discrimination, and neglect.
12. Special protection is a form of protection received by Children in certain situations and conditions to obtain a guarantee of security against threats that endanger themselves and their lives in their development.
13. Parent is the biological father and/or mother, or stepfather and/or stepmother, or adoptive father and/or adoptive mother.
14. Guardian is a person or body who in fact exercises parental authority as a parent over a child.
15. Family is the smallest unit in society consisting of husband, wife and children, or father and children, or mother and children, or blood relatives in a direct line upward or downward to the third degree.
16. Community is family, groups and social organizations and/or community organizations.
17. Social Organization, hereinafter referred to as Orsos, is an institution/foundation/social association formed by the community, whether legal entity or not, which functions as a means of community participation in implementing Social Welfare Efforts (UKS).
18. Child Protection Social House, hereinafter abbreviated as RSPA, is a child protection service unit for child victims of violence and child trafficking as a continuation of temporary shelter that functions to provide protection, recovery, rehabilitation, advocacy and referral.
19. Social Services for Children is physical, mental and social services aimed at helping children to grow and develop normally.
20. Social Services for Neglected Children is social services for children whose parents do not have the ability to meet the needs of children to grow and develop normally.
21. Child-friendly school is an educational institution that recognizes and respects children's rights to obtain education, health, opportunities to play and have fun, protection from violence and abuse, freedom to express views, and participate in making decisions according to their capacity.
22. Social Work Profession is a profession based on a framework of knowledge, values and technical skills and can be used as a vehicle in implementing social welfare efforts.
23. Integrated Service Center, hereinafter abbreviated as PPT, is a unified unit that organizes integrated services for witnesses and/or victims of criminal acts of human trafficking as well as women and children victims of violence.
24. Forum for the Protection of Victims of Violence, hereinafter abbreviated as FPKK, is a coordination forum for the prevention, handling and empowerment of victims of violence against women and children whose implementation is networked and formed at the Regency, District and Village levels.

Article 2

The principles of protection for Victims are:

a. respect and fulfillment of the rights of victims;
b. justice and gender equality;
c. non-discrimination;
d. best interests of victims; and
e. empowerment.

Article 3

The objectives of Victim Protection are:

a. to prevent violence against women and children;
b. to provide services to Victims; and
c. to empower women victims of violence.

Article 4

The scope of protection for victims includes prevention, service, empowerment and handling of victims of violence in the Region.

CHAPTER II VIOLENCE

Article 5

Forms of violence against women and children include:

a. physical violence;
b. psychological violence;
c. sexual violence;
d. domestic neglect;
e. exploitation; and
f. other violence.

Article 6

Physical violence as referred to in Article 5 letter a is that which results in pain, illness or serious injury.

Article 7

Psychological violence as referred to in Article 5 letter b is an act that results in fear, loss of self-confidence, loss of ability to act, feelings of helplessness and/or severe psychological suffering to a person.

Article 8

Sexual violence as referred to in Article 5 letter c includes:

a. forced sexual intercourse committed against a person residing within the scope of the household and/or outside the scope of the household; and
b. forced sexual intercourse against a person within the household scope with another person for commercial purposes and/or specific purposes.

Article 9

Domestic neglect as referred to in Article 5 letter d is:

a. the act of neglecting a person within the household scope whereas according to the applicable law for the person concerned or due to an agreement or contract he/she is obliged to provide life, care or maintenance to that person.
b. the act of neglecting a person within the household scope, whereas according to the law applicable to him/her or due to an agreement or contract he/she is obliged to provide life, care, or maintenance to that person; and
c. an act that results in economic dependence by restricting and/or prohibiting decent work inside or outside the home so that the victim is under the control of that person.

Article 10

Exploitation as referred to in Article 5 letter e is caused by:

a. acts that exploit economically or sexually with the intent to benefit oneself or others;
b. acts with or without the victim's consent which include but are not limited to prostitution, forced labor or services, slavery or similar practices, oppression, extortion, exploitation of physical, sexual, reproductive organs, or unlawfully removing or transplanting organs and/or body tissues or exploiting the labor or ability of a person by another party to obtain benefits both material and immaterial; and
c. all forms of exploitation of sexual organs or other body organs of the victim to obtain benefits, including but not limited to all activities of prostitution or molestation.

Article 11

Other violence as referred to in Article 5 letter f is caused by:

a. threat of violence includes: any unlawful act in the form of words, writing, images, symbols, or body movements, whether or not using means that give rise to fear or restrict a person's fundamental freedom; and
b. coercion, includes: a situation where a person/victim is ordered to do something in such a way that the person does something that is contrary to their own will.

CHAPTER III RIGHTS OF VICTIMS

Article 12

Every Victim receives the following rights: a. the right to have their dignity respected as a human being; b. the right to health and psychological recovery from the suffering experienced by the victim; c. the right to determine their own decisions; d. the right to obtain information; e. the right to confidentiality of identity; f. the right to compensation; g. the right to social rehabilitation; h. the right to complaint handling; i. the right to obtain facilitation in the judicial process; and j. the right to assistance.

Article 13

Child victims of violence in addition to receiving the rights as referred to in Article 13, also receive special rights, as follows:

a. the right to respect for survival, growth and development;
b. the right to basic services;
c. the right to equal protection;
d. the right to be free from various stigma; and
e. the right to obtain freedom.

CHAPTER IV OBLIGATIONS AND RESPONSIBILITIES

Part One Regional Government

Article 14

(1) The Regional Government is obligated and responsible to carry out efforts to protect victims of violence, in the form of:

a. establishing PPT;
b. establishing FPKK;
c. determining policies;
d. formulating program and activity planning;
e. providing facilities and infrastructure support; and
f. allocating budget.
(2) In implementing the obligations and responsibilities of the Regional Government as referred to in paragraph (1), the Regional Government formulates a Regional Action Plan for the protection of victims of violence.

Article 15

The Regional Government provides services for victims of violence in the form of:

a. facilitating the establishment of PPT;
b. facilitating facilities and infrastructure for PPT in accordance with regional financial capacity; and
c. facilitating FPKK as a network forum for handling victims of violence.

Article 16

The Regional Government is obligated and responsible to:

a. supervise the provision of services to victims of violence, with minimum service standards; and
b. provide funds for the protection of victims through the Regional Budget and/or other legitimate regional financial sources.

Article 17

In carrying out the obligations and responsibilities of the Regional Government as referred to in Article 14, Article 15 and Article 16, the Regent grants authority to the agency that has the main duties and functions of coordination in the field of women's empowerment and child protection.

Part Two

Society

Article 18

In addition to the obligations and responsibilities of the Regional Government as referred to in Article 14, Article 15 and Article 16, the community has the following obligations and responsibilities:

a. prevent violence against women and children;
b. report if violence occurs;
c. protect victims; and
d. provide emergency assistance;

Part Three

Family

Article 19

The family is obligated and responsible to carry out prevention efforts and protection of victims in the form of:

a. providing understanding about the prevention of domestic violence;
b. supervising every family member;
c. parents are prohibited from employing underage children in fulfilling the family's economy; and
d. parents must provide opportunities for school-age children to obtain adequate education.

CHAPTERV PROTECTION OF VICTIMS

Article 20

(1) Prevention efforts against violence against women and children are carried out in an integrated manner by the Regional Government coordinated by the agency that has the main duties and functions of coordination in the field of women's empowerment and child protection.

(2) Prevention efforts as referred to in paragraph (1) are implemented by:

a. forming a work network in violence prevention efforts;
b. conducting coordination, integration, synchronization of violence prevention based on partnership patterns;
c. forming a violence prevention system; and
d. conducting socialization on laws and regulations related to the protection of women and child victims of violence.

Article 21

In addition to the prevention efforts carried out by the Regional Government as referred to in Article 20, prevention efforts must also be carried out by:

a. family and/or closest relatives;
b. community;
c. educational institutions; and
d. other community institutions.

CHAPTER VI INTEGRATED SERVICE CENTER

Article 22

(1) PPT is established by the Regional Government in the form of a Technical Implementation Unit (UPT).

(2) PPT as referred to in paragraph (1) is a functional work unit that has the duties, main tasks, and functions of providing protection to women and children from acts of violence.

(3) PPT as referred to in paragraph (1) is tasked with providing temporary services and protection in the form of a safe house for victims of violence.

(4) In the event that PPT does not have a safe house (shelter) as referred to in paragraph (3), then victims of violence are referred to a PPT that has a safe house.

(5) PPT as referred to in paragraph (1) is established by Regional Regulation.

Article 23

(1) The organizational structure of PPT established by the Regional Government as referred to in Article 22 paragraph (1) consists of:

a. General Chairperson:
b. Secretary:
c. Treasurer; and
d. Executive Chairperson who oversees divisions.
(2) The divisions as referred to in paragraph (1) letter d, at a minimum include:
a. complaint service division:
b. health service division:
c. social rehabilitation service division;
d. repatriation and social reintegration division; and
e. legal aid service division.
(3) In carrying out its duties, PPT is responsible to the Regent through the Head of the agency that has the main duties and functions of coordination in the field of women's empowerment and child protection.

Article 24

The complaint service division as referred to in Article 23 paragraph (2) letter a has the duties:

a. conducting interviews and observation of the victim's condition;
b. making recommendations for follow-up services;
c. conducting coordination and referrals to follow-up services and related parties; and
d. conducting administration of the complaint process.

Article 25

The health service division as referred to in Article 23 paragraph (2) letter b has the duties:

a. conducting examinations, treatment, and follow-up care for victims;

b.conducting coordination of health rehabilitation implementation and medico-legal;

c. conducting supporting examinations and laboratory on evidence;

d. conducting consultations with specialist doctors or making referrals; and

e. making case reports.

Article 26

The social rehabilitation service division as referred to in Article 23 paragraph (2) letter c has the duties:

a. providing assistance during the case handling process; and
b. conducting counseling.

Article 27

The repatriation and social reintegration division as referred to in Article 23 paragraph (2) letter d has the duties:

a. conducting coordination with relevant agencies for the repatriation of victims;
b. making progress reports on the process of repatriation assistance and social rehabilitation; and
c. conducting monitoring for at least three months after the victim is returned to their family.

Article 28

The legal aid service division as referred to in Article 23 paragraph (2) letter e has the duties:

a. accompanying and defending every legal service process; and
b. making progress reports on legal handling.

Article 29

(1) PPT formed by the Regional Government must have at least three counselors.

(2) Counselors as referred to in paragraph (1) include:

a. counselors in the field of law;
b. counselors in the field of health; and
c. counselors in the field of psychology.

Article 30

Further provisions regarding the development, mechanism and networked working relationships of PPT are regulated by Regent Regulation.

Part One

Services

Article 31

(1) The provision of services to victims is carried out in an integrated manner by PPT.

(2) PPT as referred to in paragraph (1) may receive and send case referrals from or to other service units in a networked manner.

Article 32

The provision of services to victims is implemented with:

a. promptness;
b. safety and comfort;
c. empathy;
d. non-discrimination;
e. accessibility;
f. no fees charged; and
g. guaranteed confidentiality.

Article 33

Forms of services to victims include:

a. complaint, consultation, and counseling services;
b. assistance services;
c. health services;
d. social rehabilitation services;
e. legal services; and
f. repatriation and social reintegration services.

Article 34

Complaint, consultation, and counseling services as referred to in Article 33 letter a include:

a. initial identification or recording of victims; and
b. informed consent.

Article 35

Assistance services as referred to in Article 33 letter b include:

a. accompanying victims during the examination and health recovery process;
b. accompanying victims during the medico-legal process;
c. accompanying victims during the examination process at the Police, Prosecutor's Office and court;
d. monitoring the interests and rights of victims in the examination process at the Police, Prosecutor's Office and Court;
e. protecting the privacy and confidentiality of victims from all unauthorized parties, including reporting by mass media;
f. coordinating with other companions; and
g. providing continuous handling up to the rehabilitation stage.

Article 36

Health services as referred to in Article 33 letter c include:

a. first aid to victims;
b. treatment and recovery of physical injuries aimed at restoring the physical condition of victims carried out by medical and paramedical personnel; and
c. referral to health services.

Article 37

Social rehabilitation services as referred to in Article 33 letter d are services provided by companions in order to restore the traumatic condition of victims, including the provision of safe houses to protect victims from various threats and intimidation against victims and provide social support so that victims have confidence, strength, and independence in resolving their problems, by:

a. providing spiritual guidance to victims; and
b. psychological recovery of victims.

Article 38

Legal services as referred to in Article 33 letter e to assist victims in undergoing the judicial process by:

a. providing legal consultation that includes information regarding the rights of victims and the judicial process;
b. accompanying victims at the level of investigation, prosecution, and examination in court hearings and assisting victims to fully describe the violence they experienced;
c. coordinating with fellow law enforcers, volunteer companions, and social workers so that the judicial process runs as it should.

Article 39

(1) Repatriation and social reintegration services as referred to in Article 33 letter f aim to return victims to their families and social environment.

(2) Repatriation and social reintegration services as referred to in paragraph (1) are carried out by the Regional Government in coordination with:

a. Regency/City Governments within one Provincial area; and
b. relevant agencies and institutions both government and non-government.

Part Two Empowerment

Paragraph 1 Empowerment of Women Victims of Violence

Article 40

Forms of empowerment of women victims of violence include:

a. job training;
b. productive economic business and joint business groups; and
c. capital assistance.

Article 41

Job training as referred to in Article 40 letter a includes:

a. skills training;
b. field work practice; and
c. apprenticeship.

Article 42

Productive economic business and joint business groups as referred to in Article 40 letter b include:

a. entrepreneurship skills training;
b. facilitation of joint business group formation; and
c. business implementation assistance.

Article 43

Capital assistance as referred to in Article 40 letter c includes:

a. work facilities and infrastructure assistance; and
b. facilitation of working capital assistance.

Paragraph 2 Fulfillment of the Rights of Child Victims of Violence

Article 44

(1) Regional Government, PPT, and the community are obliged to fulfill the rights of child victims of violence.

(2) Forms of fulfillment of the rights of child victims of violence as referred to in paragraph (1) include the fulfillment of children's basic rights in accordance with their needs.

Part Three

Coordination of Victim Protection

Article 45

(1) In an effort to provide and organize service handling for victims, the Regional Government establishes FPKK.

(2) FPKK as referred to in paragraph (1) aims to:

a. coordinate and synchronize PPT service handling;
b. maintain and develop networks and referral systems; and
c. collect, compile and present violence reports.
(3) The management and membership of FPKK as referred to in paragraph (1) is determined by Regent Decree.
(4) The membership of FPKK as referred to in paragraph (3) is grouped in the following roles:
a. health role;
b. psychology role;
c. legal role;
d. social role; and
e. economic role.
(5) Further provisions regarding the establishment, duties, principles, and functions as well as membership of FPKK as referred to in paragraph (1) are regulated by Regent Regulation.

CHAPTER VII SUPERVISION AND MONITORING

Article 46

(1) The Regional Government conducts supervision and monitoring of the implementation of minimum service standards for every PPT in the Region.

(2) Supervision and Monitoring as referred to in paragraph (1) include:

a. coordination;
b. guidance;
c. education and training; and
d. Monitoring and evaluation.
(3) Coordination as referred to in paragraph (2) letter a covers aspects related to planning and implementation.
(4) Guidance as referred to in paragraph (2) letter b covers aspects related to planning, implementation, governance, funding, quality, control and monitoring.
(5) Education and training as referred to in paragraph (2) letter c is carried out in accordance with laws and regulations.
(6) Monitoring and evaluation as referred to in paragraph (2) letter d is conducted periodically every 6 months.

CHAPTER VIII FUNDING

Article 47

Funding for victim protection activities carried out by the Regional Government is charged to the APBD and/or other legal and non-binding sources in accordance with the provisions of laws and regulations.

CHAPTER IX COMMUNITY PARTICIPATION

Article 48

(1) The community participates in the implementation of protection for Victims.

(2) Community participation as referred to in paragraph (1) consists of:

a. establishing family partners at the sub-district/village level by the community;
b. establishing women and child protection units within community organizations;
c. conducting independent socialization of women's and children's rights;
d. providing first aid to victims; and
e. reporting to the competent authorities if violence against victims occurs in their environment.
(3) Community participation as referred to in paragraph (1) is carried out by individuals, social community institutions, non-governmental organizations, educational institutions, religious institutions, the private sector, and mass media.

CHAPTER X SANCTIONS

Article 49

(1) Any person who commits any form of violence as referred to in Article 5 shall be punished in accordance with the provisions of Laws and Regulations.

(2) Regional Work Units that do not implement the provisions of Article 14, Article 15 and Article 16 shall be subject to administrative sanctions.

(3) Any person who directly witnesses violence as referred to in Article 5 and does not report it to the competent authorities shall be punished in accordance with the provisions of applicable Laws and Regulations.

CHAPTER XI CLOSING PROVISIONS

Article 50

This Regional Regulation comes into force on the date of promulgation.

In order that everyone may know of it, the promulgation of this Regional Regulation is ordered by placing it in the Regional Gazette of Southeast Minahasa Regency.

I. GENERAL

Acts of violence against women and children constitute violations of human rights, therefore it is necessary to protect their dignity and honor and guarantee their right to life in accordance with their nature and disposition without discrimination. In order to prevent and overcome violence against women and children in Southeast Minahasa Regency so that they are protected from violence, threats of violence, torture or treatment that degrades human dignity and honor, it is necessary to provide protection for women and child victims of violence in the form of regional regulations.

Until now, laws and regulations governing the protection of women and child victims of violence have not regulated protection efforts in the Region, therefore institutional and regulatory support is needed to ensure its implementation.

This Regional Regulation regulates protection efforts for victims, particularly in terms of prevention, services and empowerment of women and child victims of violence in Southeast Minahasa Regency.

II. ARTICLE BY ARTICLE

Article 1

Sufficiently clear.

Article 2

Letter a

What is meant by "respect and fulfillment of victims' rights" is the guarantee of fulfillment of victims' basic rights.

Letter b

What is meant by "justice" is a condition where every person, both men and women, are treated equally and obtain equal opportunities to gain opportunities (access), as well as welfare;

What is meant by "gender equality" is equality of rights, opportunities, benefits and decision-making between women and men, including in entering employment opportunities both in the formal and informal sectors.

Letter c

What is meant by "non-discrimination" is an attitude and treatment towards victims without making distinctions based on age, gender, race, ethnicity, religion and between groups.

Letter d

What is meant by "the best interests of the victim" is that all best actions concerning victims carried out by the Government, community, legislative bodies and judicial bodies, the best interests of the victim must be the primary consideration.

Letter e

What is meant by "empowerment" is strengthening victims of violence to be able to endeavor and work independently after they are recovered and provided with health and social rehabilitation services.

Article 21

Letter a

Prevention efforts in the family and/or closest relatives can be conducted by strengthening resilience in the household such as: practicing religious values, managing household time, and communication among family members.

Letter b

Prevention efforts in the community include: cultivating environmental awareness of acts of violence occurring in their environment.

Letter c

Educational institutions can participate in efforts to provide educational punishment, seeking to abolish provisions that do not favor victims of violence.

Letter d

What is meant by "community institutions" are institutions formed by members of the community of Citizens of the Republic of Indonesia voluntarily on the basis of shared vision, mission, profession, function and activities to participate in development in order to achieve national goals within the framework of the Unitary State of the Republic of Indonesia based on Pancasila, consisting of religious organizations, non-governmental organizations, professional organizations, private organizations, social organizations, political organizations, mass media, and other forms of organizations.

Article 31

Paragraph (1) Sufficiently clear.

Paragraph (2)

What is meant by "other service units" is a unified unit that performs integrated service functions for women and children victims of violence. This service unit can be located at the Integrated Service Center (PPT) and Integrated Crisis Center (PKT) based at Hospitals, Community Health Centers, Integrated Service Center for Women and Child Empowerment (P2TP2A), Women and Children Service Unit (UPPA), Trauma Center Shelter House (RPTC), Children's Social Shelter House (RPSA), BP4, and other security institutions, prosecutors' offices, courts, Women Crisis Center (WCC), and other similar institutions. This service can be in the form of one roof (one stop crisis center) or in the form of a network, depending on the needs in each region.

Article 35

Letter a

Sufficiently clear.

Letter b

What is meant by "medico-legal" is medical services to provide optimal professional assistance in utilizing medical science for the purposes of law enforcement and justice. Medico-legal services include among others: visum et repertum and visum et psikiatrikum.

What is meant by "visum et repertum" is a written statement made by a doctor in the field of forensic medicine at the request of the authorized investigator regarding the results of a medical examination of the victim based on their expertise and under oath, for the purposes of judicial proceedings.

What is meant by "visum et psikiatrikum" is a statement given by a Psychiatrist regarding the mental health condition of the victim needed to clarify a case and for the purposes of judicial proceedings.
