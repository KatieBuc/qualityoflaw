BY THE GRACE OF ALMIGHTY GOD

REGENT OF DEMAK,

Considering:

a. that all forms of violence, especially gender-based violence and violence against children are violations of human rights and crimes against human dignity as well as forms of discrimination;
b. that victims of gender-based violence and children must receive protection, both from the regional government and/or the community in order to be protected and free from violence and/or threats of violence within the scope of the household and community;
c. that based on Law Number 23 of 2002 concerning Child Protection and Law Number 23 of 2004 concerning the Elimination of Domestic Violence, the Regional Government together with the community is obliged to make efforts to prevent, protect, and restore victims of gender-based violence and children;
d. that based on the considerations as referred to in letter a, letter b and letter c, it is necessary to establish a Regional Regulation concerning the Implementation of Protection for Victims of Gender-Based Violence and Children;

CHAPTER I

GENERAL PROVISIONS

Article 1

In this Regional Regulation, what is meant by:
1. Region is Demak Regency.
2. Regional Government is the Regent and Regional Apparatus as elements of regional government administration.
3. Regent is the Regent of Demak.
4. Regional Apparatus Work Unit hereinafter abbreviated as SKPD is the Regional Apparatus Work Unit in Demak Regency.
5. Gender is a concept that refers to the roles and responsibilities of men and women that occur as a result of and can be changed by the social and cultural circumstances of society.
6. Child is a person who has not yet reached the age of 18 (eighteen) years, including a child still in the womb.
7. Protection is all efforts aimed at providing a sense of security and fulfilling the rights of victims carried out by family, integrated services, advocates, social institutions, police, prosecutors, courts, or other parties either temporarily or based on court determination.
8. Child protection is all activities to guarantee and protect children and their rights so that they can live, grow, develop, and participate optimally in accordance with human dignity and worth, and receive protection from violence and discrimination.
9. Implementation of protection is a series of activities aimed at preventing the occurrence of violence, providing protection and recovery services and social reintegration, conducting coordination and cooperation, and increasing community participation coordinated by the SKPD in charge of Women's Empowerment and Child Protection.
10. Violence is any act that results in or may result in misery or suffering whether physical, sexual, economic, social, psychological to the victim.
11. Gender-Based Violence is any form of restriction, exclusion, differentiation and all forms of treatment carried out on the basis of gender and aimed at reducing, eliminating the recognition, enjoyment or use of human rights, the consequences of which include but are not limited to physical, sexual, psychological and economic violence.
12. Violence Against Children is any form of restriction, differentiation, exclusion and all forms of treatment carried out against children the consequences of which include but are not limited to physical, sexual, psychological and economic violence.
13. Victim of Gender-Based Violence is a person who because of their gender experiences physical, psychological, economic, social, sexual suffering, and other losses resulting from state policies, acts of violence and or threats of violence within the scope of the household and community.
14. Child Victim of Violence is a child who receives harsh treatment whether physical, psychological, economic, social, sexual, and other losses resulting from state policies, acts of violence and or threats of violence within the scope of the household and community, including children who become perpetrators of violence.
15. Victim Recovery is all efforts to strengthen victims of gender-based violence and violence against children to be more empowered, whether physically, psychologically, socially, economically or sexually.
16. Psychosocial Assistance is all actions in the form of counseling, psychological therapy, advocacy, and spiritual guidance, for the self-empowerment of victims of gender-based violence and children to resolve the problems they face
17. Integrated Services is a series of activities to provide protection for victims of gender-based violence and children carried out jointly by relevant agencies or institutions as a unified implementation, prevention efforts, health services, psychosocial rehabilitation, repatriation, social reintegration, and legal assistance for victims of gender-based violence and children.
18. Social Reintegration is the process of preparing the community and victims that supports the reunification of victims into the family environment, family substitutes who can provide protection and fulfillment of the needs of victims.
19. Safe House (shelter) is a temporary residence used to provide protection to victims in accordance with the determined operational standards.
20. Community is individuals, families, groups, social organizations, and/or community organizations.

Article 2

Protection of victims of gender-based violence and children is based on Pancasila and grounded in the 1945 Constitution, as well as basic principles that include:
a. Non-discrimination;
b. The best interests of the child;
c. The right to life, survival and development;
d. Respect for the views of the child;
e. Confidentiality;
f. Empowerment;
g. Not blaming the victim;
h. Decision in the hands of the victim; and
i. Integration.

Article 3

The objectives of protection of victims of gender-based violence and children include:
a. providing a sense of security for victims;
b. obtaining legal handling; and
c. seeking recovery and social reintegration.

CHAPTER II

RIGHTS OF VICTIMS

Article 4

1. Every victim of gender-based violence and child victim of violence has the right to:
a. obtain protection for the security of themselves, their family and property, and be free from threats related to testimony that will be, is being, or has been given;
b. participate in the process of selecting and determining the form of protection and security support;
c. obtain information regarding case developments and court decisions;
d. obtain integrated services that are fast, appropriate, comfortable, and according to needs;
e. recovery and social reintegration;
f. obtain legal, psychological, spiritual, economic, social assistance and interpreters;
g. special handling related to victim confidentiality;
h. obtain guarantees of their rights related to their status as wife/mother, husband/father, child and household member as well as community member; and
i. obtain information about laws and regulations that protect victims.
2. The Rights of Victims as referred to in paragraph (1) are implemented in accordance with the provisions of laws and regulations.

CHAPTER III

OBLIGATIONS AND AUTHORITIES OF THE REGIONAL GOVERNMENT

Article 5

1. In implementing protection for victims of gender-based violence and children, the Regional Government is obliged to:
a. carry out all efforts to prevent the occurrence of violence;
b. provide protection for victims of violence;
c. provide recovery services and social reintegration for victims;
d. encourage and increase community participation;
e. cooperate with service providers in efforts to prevent, protect and recover victims of violence; and
f. supervise the implementation of services to victims involving the community.
2. To carry out the obligations as referred to in paragraph (1), the Regional Government has the authority to:
a. formulate policies and programs on the elimination of gender-based violence and violence against children;
b. facilitate the implementation of integrated services;
c. provide facilities and infrastructure;
d. increase the capacity of service provider institutions;
e. conduct coordination and cooperation in the implementation of protection for victims of gender-based violence and children; and
f. conduct monitoring and evaluation.
3. The regional government in carrying out obligations as referred to in paragraph (1) and paragraph (2) must pay attention to the rights and obligations of parents, guardians, husbands, wives or other persons who are legally responsible for the victim.

CHAPTER IV

IMPLEMENTATION OF PROTECTION

Part One

Institutions

Article 6

Integrated services assist the Regional Government in implementing protection for victims of gender-based violence and child victims of violence.

Part Two

Integrated Services

Article 7

1. Integrated services as referred to in Article 6 shall be established by the Regional Government in accordance with laws and regulations.
2. The establishment of integrated services as referred to in paragraph (1) shall consist of members from regional government elements, non-governmental institutions, health service institutions, law enforcement officials, professional personnel, companion volunteers, social workers, religious figures, safe houses (shelters), and social rehabilitation centers.
3. Standard Operating Procedures (SOP) for Integrated Services as referred to in paragraph (1) shall be regulated by Regent Regulation.

Article 8

The duties of integrated services include:
a. pursuing prevention;
b. recovery and social reintegration;
c. providing legal protection;
d. conducting mediation, coordination and cooperation;
e. pursuing increased community participation; and
f. monitoring and evaluation.

Part Three

Service Principles

Article 9

In carrying out duties as referred to in Article 8, integrated services shall apply the following principles:
a. easy;
b. appropriate; and
c. comfortable;

Part Four

Prevention Efforts

Article 10

Prevention efforts as referred to in Article 8 letter a include:
a. organizing communication, information, and education about gender-based violence and violence against children; and
b. socialization of laws and regulations related to gender-based violence and violence against children.

Part Five

Recovery and Social Reintegration Efforts

Article 11

Recovery and Social Reintegration Efforts as referred to in Article 8 letter b, include:
a. providing physical recovery at health service institutions;
b. providing medicolegal services;
c. assisting victim repatriation;
d. providing temporary protection in safe houses (shelters);
e. providing psychosocial recovery and assistance;
f. providing religious guidance services; and
g. conducting family and community preparation, economic empowerment and return to school and/or other educational institutions.

Part Six

Legal Protection

Article 12

Legal protection as referred to in Article 8 letter c includes:
a. providing assistance in legal processes;
b. providing special legal protection for child victims of violence which may be carried out through the appointment of guardianship in accordance with applicable regulations.

Part Seven

Mediation, Coordination and Cooperation

Article 13

Mediation, coordination and cooperation as referred to in Article 8 letter d include:
a. conducting mediation in problem resolution with related parties;
b. conducting coordination and cooperation in handling violence cases with Provincial integrated services;
c. conducting coordination and cooperation with integrated services between regencies;
d. conducting coordination and cooperation with service provider institutions for victims of gender-based violence and violence against children; and
e. conducting coordination with the Regional Government.

Part Eight

Community Participation

Article 14

Community participation as referred to in Article 8 letter e shall be carried out through:
a. providing protection for victims;
b. providing emergency assistance;
c. providing advocacy to victims and or the community regarding the handling of gender-based violence and violence against children cases;
d. assisting in the process of submitting applications for protection orders;
e. conveying information to authorized officials related to cases of gender-based violence and violence against children;
f. fostering local wisdom in handling gender-based violence and violence against children;
g. organizing strengthening of community groups in handling gender-based violence and violence against children; and
h. disseminating information about laws and regulations related to gender-based violence and violence against children;

Part Nine

Monitoring and evaluation

Article 15

Monitoring and evaluation as referred to in Article 8 letter f includes monitoring, documentation and evaluation of cases of gender-based violence and violence against children.

Article 16

The implementation of integrated services shall be coordinated by the SKPD responsible for Women's Empowerment and Child Protection.

CHAPTER V

COOPERATION

Article 17

1. In organizing protection for victims of gender-based violence and violence against children, the regional government shall cooperate with the Central Government, Provincial Government and Regency/City Government and other institutions.
2. Cooperation between the Regional Government and the Central Government and Provincial Government as referred to in paragraph (1) includes consultation, coordination and reporting.
3. Cooperation between the Regional Government and Regency/City as referred to in paragraph (1) includes coordination, advocacy, referral, repatriation, social reintegration, facilitation of Integrated Service System development, monitoring, evaluation and reporting.

CHAPTER VI

CONTROL, GUIDANCE AND SUPERVISION

Article 18

1. The Regent shall conduct control, guidance and supervision of the implementation of protection for victims of gender-based violence and violence against children.
2. The implementation of control, guidance and supervision as referred to in paragraph (1) shall be carried out daily by the SKPD responsible for Women's Empowerment and Child Protection.

CHAPTER VII

FINANCING

Article 19

All activities related to the implementation of protection for victims of gender-based violence and violence against children shall be charged to the Regional Revenue and Expenditure Budget as well as other lawful and non-binding receipts.

CHAPTER VIII

TRANSITIONAL PROVISIONS

Article 20

At the time this Regional Regulation comes into force, all implementing regulations related to the protection of victims of gender-based violence and violence against children shall remain in force insofar as they are not contrary to this Regional Regulation.

CHAPTER IX

CLOSING PROVISIONS

Article 21

This Regional Regulation shall come into force on the date of promulgation.

In order that everyone may know of it, the promulgation of this Regional Regulation is ordered by its placement in the Regional Gazette of Demak Regency.

I. GENERAL

In the National Action Plan for the Elimination of Violence Against Women of 1999, it is stated that Women as mothers of the nation and children as successors of the nation are creatures of God Almighty, and therefore must be protected and their honor, dignity, and self-worth safeguarded in a fair and proportional manner, both legally, economically, politically, socially and culturally, without distinguishing ethnicity, religion, race and class in community, national and state life.

To realize the achievement of enforcement and fulfillment of human rights, the government is obliged to provide protection and security for women and children as set forth in a policy both at the national and regional levels.

The Government is responsible for taking actions both legally, politically, economically and socially to prevent, suppress, reduce and eliminate all forms of violence against women and children, whether in the form of policies applicable both at the national and regional levels.

Acts of violence against women and children can occur in the public sphere as well as in the domestic sphere (within the household) and can occur at any time, in peaceful situations or conflict. Acts of violence against women and children are actions that violate, hinder, negate and ignore the human rights of women and children's rights.

II. ARTICLE BY ARTICLE

Article 1

Sufficiently clear.

Article 2

letter a
What is meant by "non-discrimination" is protection for all victims of gender-based violence and children without distinguishing ethnicity, religion, race, class, gender, ethnicity, culture, and language, legal status and physical or mental condition.

letter b
What is meant by "best interests of the child" is all actions concerning child victims of violence carried out by the government, legislative bodies and judicial bodies, then the best interests of the child must be the primary consideration.

letter c What is meant by "life" is the most fundamental human right for children which is protected by the state, government, society, family and parents.

What is meant by "survival" is as stated in the principles of the Convention on the Rights of the Child, including the right to identity.

What is meant by "child development" is as stated in the principles of the Convention on the Rights of the Child, including the right to education, the right to play, the right to create and recreate.

letter d
What is meant by "respect for the views of the child" is respect for the rights of children to participate and express their opinions in decision-making, especially if it concerns matters that affect their lives.

letter e
What is meant by "confidentiality" is every action taken to ensure victims are in a safe condition from threats or other actions that threaten the life and psychology of victims.

letter f
What is meant by "Empowerment" is every action to motivate victims to be able to control their own lives to shape the future according to their wishes.

letter g
What is meant by "not blaming the victim" is an attitude and treatment of not blaming the victim for the occurrence of violence experienced.

letter h
What is meant by "Decision in the hands of the victim" is the right of victims of gender-based violence to determine the best choice in resolving their problems.

letter i
What is meant by "integration" is that in the implementation of protection for victims of gender-based violence and children, it is carried out by building coordination between service providers, including medical services, legal assistance, psychologists, religious counselors, social workers, police.
