CENTRAL SULAWESI PROVINCE

REGIONAL REGULATION OF BUOL REGENCY

NUMBER 07 OF 2018

CONCERNING

WOMEN'S EMPOWERMENT AND CHILD PROTECTION

BY THE GRACE OF ALMIGHTY GOD

REGENT OF BUOL,

Considering : a. that every citizen has the same right to live a dignified life in accordance with the principles of humanity, equality, and justice;

b. that to ensure women's empowerment and child protection in accordance with human dignity and to receive protection from violence, discrimination and other violations, women's empowerment and child protection must be carried out in an integrated, sustainable manner through the acceleration of the fulfillment and protection of the rights of women and children in their personal lives, family, community, nation and state;

c. that in accordance with the provisions of Article 12 paragraph (2) letter b of Law Number 23 of 2014 concerning Regional Government as has been amended several times most recently by Law Number 9 of 2015 concerning the Second Amendment to Law Number 23 of 2014 concerning Regional Government, women's empowerment and child protection is one of the mandatory government affairs under the authority of Regional Government;

d. that based on the considerations as referred to in letter a, letter b, and letter c, it is necessary to establish a Regional Regulation concerning Women's Empowerment and Child Protection;

In View of : 1. Article 18 paragraph (6) of the 1945 Constitution of the Republic of Indonesia;

2. Law Number 51 of 1999 concerning the Establishment of Buol Regency, Morowali Regency, and Banggai Kepulauan Regency (State Gazette of the Republic of Indonesia of 1999 Number 179, Supplement to the State Gazette of the Republic of Indonesia Number 3900) as has been amended by Law Number 11 of 2000 concerning the Amendment to Law Number 51 of 1999 concerning the Establishment of Buol Regency, Morowali Regency, from Banggai Kepulauan Regency (State Gazette of the Republic of Indonesia of 2000 Number 78, Supplement to the State Gazette of the Republic of Indonesia Number 3966);

3. Law Number 23 of 2002 concerning Child Protection (State Gazette of the Republic of Indonesia of 2002 Number 109, Supplement to the State Gazette of the Republic of Indonesia Number 4235) as has been amended by Law Number 35 of 2014 concerning the amendment to Law Number 23 of 2002 concerning Child Protection (State Gazette of the Republic of Indonesia of 2014 Number 297, Supplement to the State Gazette of the Republic of Indonesia Number 5606);

4. Law Number 23 of 2014 concerning Regional Government (State Gazette of the Republic of Indonesia of 2014 Number 244, Supplement to the State Gazette of the Republic of Indonesia Number 5587) as has been amended several times, most recently by Law Number 9 of 2015 concerning the Second Amendment to Law Number 23 of 2014 concerning Regional Government (State Gazette of the Republic of Indonesia of 2015 Number 58, Supplement to the State Gazette of the Republic of Indonesia Number 5679);

5. Regulation of the State Minister for Women's Empowerment Number 3 of 2008 concerning Guidelines for the Implementation of Child Protection;

6. Regulation of the State Minister for Women's Empowerment and Child Protection Number 5 of 2010 concerning Guidelines for the Establishment and Development of Integrated Service Centers (State News of the Republic of Indonesia of 2010 Number 446);

CHAPTER I GENERAL PROVISIONS

Article 1

In this Regional Regulation, what is meant by:

1. Region is Buol Regency.
2. Regional Government is the head of the Region as an element of the Regional Government organizer who leads the implementation of Government Affairs which are the authority of the autonomous Region.
3. Regent is the Regent of Buol Regency.
4. Regional Apparatus hereinafter abbreviated as PD is the Regional Apparatus within the Government of Buol Regency.
5. Women's empowerment is an effort to empower women to obtain access and control over economic, political, social and cultural resources so that women can manage themselves and increase self-confidence to be able to play a role and actively participate in solving problems, so as to be able to build capacity and self-concept.
6. Protection of women is all efforts to guarantee and protect the rights of women so that they can live healthily and participate optimally in accordance with human dignity and receive protection from neglect, violence, and discrimination, both in the form of preventive, repressive and post-remedial protection (assistance and treatment).
7. Child protection is all activities to guarantee and protect children and their rights so that they can live, grow, develop and participate optimally in accordance with human dignity, and receive protection from violence and discrimination.
8. Child is a person who has not yet reached 18 (eighteen) years of age, including a child who is still in the womb and not yet married.
9. Prevention is a direct effort made by the Regional Government, community and parents and/or family to prevent violations of the rights of women and children.
10. Violence is any act that results in misery or suffering physically, sexually, psychologically and/or domestic neglect including threats to commit acts, coercion, or unlawful deprivation of a person's freedom.
11. Children's forum is a forum for child participation formed with the aim of facilitating the involvement of children in the decision-making process and enjoying changes concerning their lives both directly and indirectly, which are carried out with the agreement and will of all children based on awareness and understanding, in accordance with age and level of maturity of thought.
12. Child-Friendly Regency hereinafter abbreviated as KLA is a Regency that has a child rights-based development system through the integration of commitments and resources of the government, community and business world that are comprehensively and sustainably planned in policies, programs and activities to ensure the fulfillment of children's rights.
13. Abandoned child is a child whose needs are not properly fulfilled, whether physical, mental, spiritual or social.
14. Social welfare is a condition in which the material, spiritual, and social needs of citizens are fulfilled so that they can live decently and be able to develop themselves, so that they can carry out their social functions.
15. Gender mainstreaming hereinafter abbreviated as PUG is a strategy built to integrate gender into an integral dimension of planning, formulation, implementation, monitoring and evaluation of national development program policies.
16. Community is individuals, families, groups and social organizations and/or community organizations.
17. Women victims of violence are women who experience acts that result in misery or suffering physically, sexually or psychologically, including threats of certain acts, coercion or arbitrary deprivation of freedom, whether occurring in the public sphere or in private life.
18. Child victims of violence are children who experience acts that result in misery or suffering physically, mentally, sexually, psychologically or suffering that threatens life, body and degrades human dignity.
19. Human trafficking is the act of recruitment, transportation, harboring, transfer, relocation, or receipt of a person by means of threat of violence, use of violence, abduction, confinement, fraud, deception, abuse of power or vulnerable position, debt bondage or giving payment or benefits, so as to obtain consent from the person who has control over that other person, whether carried out within the State or between States.
20. Complaint handling is a series of actions carried out by integrated service providers to follow up on reports of acts of violence against women and children, submitted by victims, families or the community.
21. Social rehabilitation is a service aimed at restoring and developing the capacity of a person experiencing social dysfunction to be able to carry out social functions properly.
22. Law enforcement is the action of apparatus authorized by the state to implement laws and regulations.
23. Focal Point PUG is the OPD apparatus who has the ability to conduct gender mainstreaming in their respective work units.
24. Legal assistance is legal services provided by legal assistants and advocates to carry out witness assistance and/or victim assistance processes for violence against women and children that are gender sensitive.
25. Repatriation is an effort to return women and child victims of violence from abroad to the point of debarkation/entry point, or from the receiving Region to the Region of origin.
26. Social reintegration is an effort to reunite victims with family, substitute family, or community that can provide protection and fulfillment of needs for victims.
27. Safe house is a temporary shelter that aims to provide protection and a sense of security to victims of violence.
28. Transit home is a forum for guidance and welfare services for street children that carries out skills assistance/guidance activities to ensure that children do not carry out activities on the streets so they can grow and develop normally.
29. Spiritual guidance is an effort to restore mental and systematic awareness of differences between women and men in society accompanied by efforts to eliminate structural and cultural barriers in achieving gender equality.
30. Gender responsive is consistent and systematic attention to the differences between women and men in society accompanied by efforts to eliminate structural and cultural barriers in achieving gender equality.
31. Gender sensitivity or gender awareness is the ability to recognize gaps in power relations between women and men in the family and in the community, the impact of gender-based division of labor on women and men, and the experiences, problems, needs, interests and aspirations of women and men are different.
32. Public service provider is every state organizing institution, corporation, independent institution established based on Law for public service activities, and other legal entities established solely for public service activities.
33. Integrated Service Center for Women's Empowerment and Child Protection hereinafter abbreviated as P2TP2A is an integrated service center in efforts to empower women in various fields of development, as well as protect women and children from various types of discrimination and acts of violence, including human trafficking, established by the government or community-based, which includes: referral centers, business consultation centers, reproductive health consultation centers, integrated crisis centers (PKT), trauma recovery centers (trauma centers), women's crisis centers (women crisis centers), training centers, science and technology information centers (PIPTEK), safe houses (shelters), transit homes, children's friendly telephone (TESSA) 129 or other forms.
34. Community Organization is an organization established and formed by the community voluntarily based on common aspirations, will, needs, interests, activities and goals to participate in development for the achievement of the objectives of the Unitary State of the Republic of Indonesia based on Pancasila.
35. Parents are biological father and/or mother, or step father and/or step mother, or adoptive father and/or adoptive mother.
36. Family is the smallest unit in society consisting of husband and wife or husband, wife and their children, or father and children, or mother and children, or blood relatives in a straight line upward or downward up to the third degree.
37. Customs are traditional local wisdom values that apply and are still practiced from generation to generation in the community.
38. Child-Friendly Regency Task Force is a coordinative institution at the regency level that coordinates policy, program and activity efforts to realize a child-friendly regency.
39. Regional Action Plan for Child-Friendly Regency Development is a document containing policies, programs and activities to realize a Child-Friendly Regency.

CHAPTER II

RIGHTS OF WOMEN AND CHILDREN

Article 2

Every woman and child has basic rights as a human being that must be protected, advanced, upheld and fulfilled in accordance with the provisions of laws and regulations.

Article 3

The rights of women as referred to in Article 2 include:

a. live peacefully, safely, peacefully, happily, prosperously physically and spiritually;
b. enjoy a good and healthy living environment;
c. live in a family within a legal marriage bond based on the provisions of laws and regulations;
d. obtain work according to ability, requirements and fair and just wages;
e. specifically for women with disabilities have the right to receive facilities and special treatment in public services;
f. play an active role in politics and government according to their potential;
g. obtain information and legal services;
h. obtain services to increase their income;
i. obtain other rights according to their humanitarian benefits based on the provisions of laws and regulations;

Article 4

The rights of children as referred to in Article 2 include:

a. obtain education and teaching according to age level, physical and mental condition, intelligence and interests and talents;
b. live, grow, develop, and participate properly in accordance with human dignity and receive protection from acts of violence, exploitation, discrimination and neglect, cruelty, violence and abuse, injustice and mistreatment;
c. have needs fulfilled by family;
d. receive religious guidance;
e. obtain their identity;
f. obtain other rights according to the provisions of laws and regulations;
g. specifically for children with disabilities have the right to receive facilities and special treatment in their services;

Article 5

In addition to the rights of women and children as referred to in Article 3 and Article 4, women and children victims of violence have the right to:

a. have their dignity and honor respected as human beings;
b. the right to complaints and services that are fast and affordable;
c. obtain information and legal certainty;
d. confidentiality of the case being undergone;
e. compensation for the case being undergone;
f. obtain facilities in the judicial process;
g. obtain assistance;
h. obtain physical and psychosocial health recovery from the suffering experienced by victims;
i. determine their own decisions;
j. obtain rehabilitation and social reintegration;
k. obtain respect and services for survival, growth and development;
l. obtain basic services;
m. be free from negative stigma; and
n. obtain freedom to determine choices deciding for themselves after their parents' divorce;

Article 6

The implementation of women's empowerment and child protection is based on the principles of:

a. best interests for women and children;
b. gender justice and gender equality;
c. local wisdom;
d. law enforcement and legal certainty;
e. non-discrimination;
f. the right to life, survival, and development;
g. respect for the opinion of children; and

CHAPTER III

IMPROVEMENT OF WOMEN'S QUALITY OF LIFE

Part One

General

Article 7

(1) In order to improve the quality of life for women, the regional government is responsible for:

a. respecting and guaranteeing women's rights without discrimination in accordance with human dignity;
b. implementing gender mainstreaming;
c. establishing a gender mainstreaming working group;
d. enhancing women's empowerment in roles, quality and capacity in the fields of:
1. politics;
2. law;
3. social; and
4. economy.
e. strengthening and developing institutions providing women's empowerment services in the Region;
(2) The Regional Government's responsibility in efforts to improve the quality of life for women as referred to in paragraph (1) is implemented by the Regional Apparatus that administers government affairs in the field of women's empowerment and child protection.

Part Two

Implementation of Gender Mainstreaming

Article 8

(1) The Regent is responsible for the implementation of gender mainstreaming.

(2) The Regent's responsibility as referred to in paragraph (1) may be delegated to the Vice Regent.

Article 9

In implementing gender mainstreaming as referred to in Article 7 paragraph (1) letter b, the Regent designates the Regional Apparatus that administers government affairs in the field of women's empowerment and children as the coordinator for the implementation of gender mainstreaming.

Part Three

Establishment of Gender Mainstreaming Working Group

Article 10

(1) In an effort to accelerate the institutionalization of gender mainstreaming, a gender mainstreaming working group is established in all Regional Apparatus.

(2) Members of the gender mainstreaming working group are all heads/leaders of Regional Apparatus.

(3) The Regent designates the Head of the Regional Apparatus that administers Government Affairs in the field of Regional Development Planning as the Chair of the gender mainstreaming working group and the Head of the Regional Apparatus that administers government affairs in the field of women's empowerment and child protection as the Head of the Secretariat of the gender mainstreaming working group.

(4) The establishment of the gender mainstreaming working group as referred to in paragraph (1) is stipulated by a Regent's Decree.

Article 11

The gender mainstreaming working group as referred to in Article 10 has the duties of:

a. promoting and facilitating gender mainstreaming to each Regional Apparatus;
b. carrying out socialization and advocacy of gender mainstreaming to Sub-district Heads and Village Heads;
c. preparing annual programs;
d. encouraging the realization of gender-responsive planning and budgeting;
e. preparing the work plan of the gender mainstreaming working group every year;
f. being responsible to the Regent through the Vice Regent;
g. formulating policy recommendations to the Regent;
h. preparing the Regional Gender profile;
i. conducting monitoring of gender mainstreaming in each agency;
j. establishing a technical team to conduct analysis of the regional budget;
k. preparing the regional action plan for gender mainstreaming; and
l. encouraging the implementation of the selection and designation of gender mainstreaming focal points in each Regional Apparatus.

Article 12

(1) The Technical Team as referred to in Article 11 letter j consists of apparatus who understand gender-responsive budget analysis.

(2) The regional action plan for regional gender mainstreaming as referred to in Article 11 letter k contains:

a. gender mainstreaming in laws and regulations;
b. gender mainstreaming in the development cycle;
c. strengthening of gender mainstreaming institutions; and
d. strengthening community participation.

Article 13

(1) The gender mainstreaming focal point in each Regional Apparatus as referred to in Article 11 letter 1 consists of officials and/or staff in charge of planning and/or program tasks.

(2) The gender mainstreaming focal point as referred to in paragraph (1), has the duties of:

a. promoting gender mainstreaming in the work unit;
b. facilitating the preparation of work plans and budgeting of Regional Apparatus that are gender-responsive;
c. carrying out training, socialization, advocacy of gender mainstreaming to all officials and staff within the Regional Apparatus;
d. reporting the implementation of gender mainstreaming to the leadership of the Regional Apparatus; e. encouraging the implementation of gender analysis on policies, programs, and activities in the work unit; and
f. facilitating the preparation of gender data in each Regional Apparatus.
(3) The gender mainstreaming focal point as referred to in paragraph (1), is selected and designated by the Regional Apparatus.

Part Four

Enhancement of Women's Role, Quality, and Capacity

Paragraph 1

Field of Politics

Article 14

Efforts to enhance the role, quality and capability of women in the political field as referred to in Article 7 paragraph (1) letter d number 1 include:

a. conducting socialization to increase women's political participation in general elections;
b. implementing programs that encourage the level of women's involvement in political processes and public positions;
c. facilitating women's self-development in organizing, associating and assembling;
d. involving women in decision-making concerning women's interests;

Paragraph 2

Legal Field

Article 15

Efforts to enhance the role, quality and capability of women in the legal field as referred to in Article 7 paragraph (1) letter d number 2 include:

a. conducting socialization to increase women's legal knowledge and awareness;
b. mapping and reviewing gender-biased policies;
c. involving women's organizations in the process of formulating and discussing regional legal products related to women's participation.

Paragraph 3

Social Field

Article 16

Efforts to enhance the role, quality and capability of women in the social field as referred to in Article 7 paragraph (1) letter d number 3 include:

a. enhancement of knowledge and skills to encourage fulfillment of education in accordance with possessed potential;
b. enhancement and fulfillment of health quality for women;
c. enhancement of awareness and knowledge about independent, healthy and prosperous family planning including access to consultation services and marriage registration;
d. enhancement of women's awareness of the impacts of early marriage;
e. facilitating and promoting the preservation of customs and the development of science, technology, arts and culture for the advancement of women;
f. enhancing women's awareness and knowledge of independent, healthy and prosperous family planning;
g. conducting programs to reduce maternal mortality rates;
h. encouraging the establishment of women's organizations.

Paragraph 4

Economic Field

Article 17

Efforts to enhance the role, quality and capability of women in the economic field as referred to in Article 7 paragraph (1) letter d number 4 include:

a. implementing programs, activities, training to enhance women's skills and expertise in order to realize improvement in women's welfare;
b. facilitating strengthening of business capital for women with weak economic capacity;
c. marketing products from businesses of women with weak economic capacity;
d. training for developing women's capabilities through economic empowerment of pre-prosperous families;
e. comprehensive entrepreneurship training for women.

CHAPTER IV

PROTECTION OF WOMEN

Part One

General

Article 18

(1) The Regional Government in efforts to protect women is responsible for:

a. establishing and implementing policies, programs and activities for the protection of women;
b. making efforts to prevent acts of violence against women including criminal acts of human trafficking;
c. increasing the effectiveness of services for women victims of acts of violence;
d. increasing efforts to protect female workers;
e. strengthening and developing institutions providing protection services for women;
f. conducting cooperation with the Government, other Regional Governments, private parties, business entities and/or community non-governmental organizations in efforts to prevent acts of violence against women; and
g. involving the active role of families, customary leaders, religious leaders, communities and community social institutions in the field of protection of women.
(2) The responsibilities of the Regional Government in efforts to protect women as referred to in paragraph (1) shall be implemented by the Regional Apparatus that administers Governmental Affairs in the field of women's and children's empowerment and in its implementation may coordinate with relevant agencies.

Part Two

Prevention of Acts of Violence Against Women

Article 19

In order to prevent acts of violence against women, the Regional Government is obligated to:

a. conduct socialization regarding the prevention of violence against women by optimizing the role of customary, cultural, religious institutions and community organizations;
b. provide guidelines on handling acts of violence against women;
c. involve community participation in recovery and reintegration;
d. develop an integrated database and information system on acts of violence against women;
e. strengthen the function of community organizations operating in the field of women's and children's empowerment; and
f. provide public facilities that are comfortable and safe for women.

Part Three

Effectiveness of Services for Women Victims of Acts of Violence

Paragraph 1

General

Article 20

(1) Protection of women victims of acts of violence is implemented through integrated services.

(2) The provision of integrated services for women victims of acts of violence as referred to in paragraph (1) uses the principles of:

a. gender responsive;
b. non-discrimination;
c. equal and respectful relations;
d. maintaining confidentiality;
e. providing a sense of security and comfort;
f. respecting individual differences;
g. non-judgmental;
h. respecting the choices and decisions of victims;
i. sensitive to the background and condition of victims and the use of appropriate language understood by victims;
j. fast and simple;
k. empathy; and
l. fulfillment of women's rights.

Article 21

Forms of integrated services for women victims of acts of violence include:

a. handling complaints;
b. health rehabilitation;
c. social rehabilitation;
d. law enforcement and legal assistance; and
e. repatriation and social reintegration.

Paragraph 2

Handling Complaints

Article 22

Integrated services for handling complaints as referred to in Article 21 letter a are implemented by:

a. hospitals;
b. community health centers;
c. women's service units;
d. trauma center shelters;
e. women crisis centers;
f. advisory board for marriage guidance and preservation;
g. police;
h. prosecutor's office;
i. courts;
j. ministry of religious affairs; and/or
k. legal aid institutions.
(1) Complaints of acts of violence against women may be made by reporters consisting of victims, families, communities and/or community organizations in the field of protection of women and children.
(2) Reporters as referred to in paragraph (1) may file complaints:
a. directly;
b. by telephone; and/or
c. by letter.
(3) In addition to handling complaints submitted by reporters, officers at service institutions as referred to in Article 22 are also authorized to handle victims of acts of violence against women obtained through other information.
(4) Further provisions regarding the procedures for complaint handling services as referred to in paragraph (1) and paragraph (2) are regulated in a Regent Regulation.

Paragraph 3

Health Rehabilitation

Article 24

(1) Health rehabilitation as referred to in Article 21 letter b constitutes efforts that include aspects of:

a. promotive;
b. preventive;
c. curative; and
d. rehabilitative.
(2) Health rehabilitation as referred to in paragraph (1) is carried out comprehensively, accessible 24 (twenty-four) hours at the Regional General Hospital, with quality and conducted in accordance with health service standards.
(3) Women victims of acts of violence have the right to free health services, including:
a. medical services;
b. medicolegal services; and
c. visum.
(4) Further provisions regarding the procedures for implementing health services as referred to in paragraph (3) are regulated in a Regent Regulation.

Paragraph 4

Social Rehabilitation

Article 25

(1) Social rehabilitation as referred to in Article 21 letter c is aimed at restoring and developing the capacity of women victims of violence who experience social dysfunction to be able to carry out their social functions in society properly.

(2) Social rehabilitation as referred to in paragraph (1) includes services:

a. counseling;
b. provision of safe houses; and
c. spiritual guidance.
(3) The provision of services as referred to in paragraph (2) is organized by:
a. Regional Apparatus that organizes government affairs in the social field;
b. Regional Apparatus that organizes government affairs in the field of women and child empowerment, and may cooperate with integrated service centers, women crisis centers or existing social community organizations; or
c. religious agencies and/or organizations.

Article 26

(1) Counseling services as referred to in Article 25 paragraph (2) letter a aim to:

a. help women victims of violence recognize their problems and find effective ways to overcome them themselves;
b. empower women victims of violence to determine their future;
c. provide moral support to women victims of violence in facing the process they are going through; and
d. make women victims of violence feel accepted in the community environment.
(2) Counseling services are carried out by gender-responsive social rehabilitation officers, consisting of:
a. counselors;
b. psychologists; and
c. psychiatrists.
(3) Counseling services are conducted in a place that ensures a sense of security, comfort and confidentiality of information from women victims of violence.
(4) Stages of counseling services include:
a. initial counseling;
b. follow-up counseling;
c. case discussion; and
d. site visits.

Article 27

(1) The Regional Government may provide safe houses for women victims of violence.

(2) The service of providing safe houses as referred to in Article 25 paragraph (2) letter b aims to protect women victims of violence from intimidation by perpetrators or other parties.

(3) The location of safe houses as referred to in paragraph (1) must be kept confidential.

(4) Criteria for women victims of violence who require safe houses include:

a. their life is threatened;
b. receiving rejection from family or community;
c. requiring intensive services but their residence is relatively far; and/or
d. neglected if not placed in a safe house.
(5) The provision of safe house services is carried out by social rehabilitation officers who have gender sensitivity or gender awareness, consisting of:
a. social workers/volunteers;
b. security officers; and/or
c. community leaders.
(6) Further provisions regarding the provision of safe house services are regulated in a Regent Regulation.

Article 28

(1) Spiritual guidance as referred to in Article 25 paragraph (2) letter c aims at psychological recovery.

(2) Spiritual guidance as referred to in paragraph (1) is implemented based on the principles of:

a. not imposing any religion or belief; and
b. leaving the final decision in the hands of the victim.
(3) Spiritual guidance is carried out by spiritual guidance officers.
(4) Spiritual guidance officers as referred to in paragraph (3) consist of consultants from the Marriage Counseling, Guidance and Preservation Board, Officers of the Office of Religious Affairs, religious leaders, who have gender sensitivity or gender awareness.

Paragraph 5

Law Enforcement and Legal Assistance

Article 29

(1) Law enforcement and legal assistance services as referred to in Article 21 letter d constitute actions by law enforcement officials to implement statutory regulations in order to handle cases of violence against women, especially to provide sanctions against perpetrators and provide protection for witnesses and/or victims.

(2) Law enforcement and legal assistance services as referred to in paragraph (1) are carried out by prioritizing the best interests of the victim.

Article 30

(1) Law enforcement and legal assistance services are aimed at providing assistance and protection to women victims of violence and witnesses with a gender-responsive approach so that victims obtain justice and legal certainty.

(2) Types of legal assistance services include:

a. legal assistance in the reporting process and preparation of investigation reports;
b. legal assistance in the prosecution process at the prosecutor's office;
c. legal assistance in the examination process in court;
d. legal assistance in the court hearing decision process until there is a final legal decision; and
e. legal assistance in the mediation process.
(3) The Regional Government provides legal assistance to women victims of violence based on statutory provisions.
(4) In providing legal assistance as referred to in paragraph (3), the Regional Government may cooperate with legal aid institutions, women crisis centers, advocates and/or other gender-sensitive legal assistants.

Paragraph 6

Repatriation and Social Reintegration

Article 31

(1) Repatriation is aimed at returning women victims of violence to their region of origin.

(2) Repatriation of women victims of violence is accompanied by social reintegration efforts.

Article 32

(1) Social reintegration is carried out in order to reunite women victims of violence with family, substitute family, or community that can provide protection and fulfillment of needs for victims.

(2) Social reintegration is aimed so that:

a. women victims of violence can continue their social life; and
b. women victims of violence can have a decent life.
(3) Social reintegration as referred to in paragraph (1) is carried out after victims receive social rehabilitation services.

Article 33

The Regional Government implements repatriation and social reintegration of women victims of violence in accordance with statutory regulations.

Part Four

Employment Protection

Article 34

(1) Employers who employ women must maintain decency, safety and security while in the workplace.

(2) Employers must place female workers in places that are safe for women's reproductive health.

(3) Employers must fulfill the rights of female workers to receive wages in accordance with statutory provisions.

(4) Employers must provide special breastfeeding facilities at the company.

Article 35

(1) Female workers/laborers under 18 (eighteen) years of age are prohibited from being employed between 23.00 WIB and 07.00 WIB.

(2) Employers are prohibited from employing pregnant female workers/laborers which according to medical statements is dangerous for the health and safety of their pregnancy.

(3) Employers who employ female workers/laborers between 23.00 WIB and 07.00 WIB, must:

a. provide food and nutritious drinks; and
b. maintain decency and security while in the workplace.
(4) Employers must provide shuttle transportation for female workers/laborers who depart and return from work between 23.00 WIB and 05.00 WIB.

CHAPTER V

IMPROVEMENT OF FAMILY QUALITY

Article 36

(1) In order to improve family quality, the Regional Government is responsible for:

a. making efforts to improve family quality to realize gender equality and children's rights;
b. providing services for families in realizing gender equality and children's rights; and
c. strengthening and developing institutions providing family quality improvement services.
(2) The responsibility of the Regional Government in efforts to improve family quality as referred to in paragraph (1) is carried out by Regional Apparatus in accordance with their duties and functions.

Article 37

The Regional Government shall make efforts to improve family quality as referred to in Article 36 paragraph (1) letter a including:

a. coaching;
b. counseling;
c. supervision;
d. facilitation of family development implementation;
e. improving the health quality of pregnant and lactating mothers;

Article 38

Coaching as referred to in Article 37 letter a, includes:

a. improving child quality by providing access to information, education, counseling and services on child care, nurturing and development through child protection programs and parenting pattern development;
b. improving youth quality by providing access to information, education, counseling and services on family life through planned generation activities, youth information and counseling centers and youth family development;
c. improving the quality of life of the elderly through elderly coaching programs, elderly family development, elderly coaching and counseling; and
d. improving family economy.

Article 39

Counseling as referred to in Article 37 letter b, includes:

a. providing protection and assistance for self-development through improving family capabilities and skills;

d. improving access and opportunities to receive information and economic resources through efforts to increase prosperous family income and improve women's economic productivity; and
e. making efforts to empower female heads of household.

Article 40

Supervision as referred to in Article 37 letter c, includes:

a. conducting evaluation and monitoring of child protection programs and parenting pattern development;
b. conducting evaluation and monitoring of youth quality improvement;
c. conducting evaluation and monitoring of elderly quality of life improvement;
d. conducting evaluation and monitoring of family economic improvement; and
e. conducting evaluation and monitoring of family capability and skill improvement.

Article 41

Provision of services for families as referred to in Article 36 paragraph (1) letter b includes:

a. communication, information and education;
b. contraceptive devices and drugs;
c. recording and reporting of family planning services;
d. adequate health service facilities and infrastructure; and
e. breastfeeding corner rooms in every public facility.

Article 42

Communication, information and education as referred to in Article 41 letter a constitutes communication activities to increase knowledge and improve the attitudes and behavior of families and the community in population and family planning programs.

Article 43

(1) In addition to the Regional Government, private parties are required to provide breastfeeding corner rooms.

(2) Breastfeeding corner rooms as referred to in paragraph (1) constitute proper and adequate rooms.

(3) Private parties that do not provide breastfeeding corner rooms as referred to in paragraph (2) shall be subject to administrative sanctions in the form of:

a. written warning;
b. restriction of business activities;
c. suspension of business activities;
d. administrative fine; and/or

CHAPTER VII

FULFILLMENT OF CHILDREN'S RIGHTS

Part One

General

Article 47

(1) The Regional Government in efforts to fulfill children's rights is responsible for:

a. establishing and implementing policies and programs/activities for the fulfillment of children's rights;
b. respecting and guaranteeing the human rights of every child without distinguishing ethnicity, religion, race, class, gender, ethnicity, culture and language, child's legal status, child's birth order, and physical and/or mental condition;
c. strengthening and developing institutions providing family quality improvement services in realizing children's rights;
d. guaranteeing the protection, care, and welfare of children with due regard to the rights and obligations of parents, guardians, or other persons legally responsible for the child;
e. providing facilities and infrastructure in the implementation of fulfillment of children's rights;
f. guaranteeing the right of children to express opinions in accordance with the child's age and level of intelligence;
g. conducting supervision and coaching in the context of implementing the fulfillment of children's rights;
h. conducting cooperation with private parties and/or NGOs in the implementation of child protection;
(2) The Government's responsibility as referred to in the implementation of Institutional and Government Protection in the field of Child Empowerment.

Part Two

Birth Registration and Education

Article 48

(1) The Regional Government is obligated to organize child birth registration with the issuance of birth certificates.

(2) Birth certificates as referred to in paragraph (1) are organized free of charge.

Article 49

The Regional Government guarantees the implementation of a 9 (nine) year compulsory education program for children aged 7 (seven) to 15 (fifteen) years.

Article 50

(1) Every education unit organizer to children to obtain education services without discrimination.

(2) Every education unit organizer is prohibited from expelling children from educational institutions unless there is a guarantee for the continuity of the child's education.

(3) In the case where a child victim of violence experiences dropping out of school, the education unit organizer is required to coordinate with related agencies.

Article 51

(1) Children with disabilities and children with special needs are given equal opportunities to obtain formal, nonformal and informal education services, including inclusive education programs.

(2) The regional government provides educators for inclusive education in every education unit.

Article 52

Children in conflict with the law, children who experience pregnancy outside of marriage and/or unwanted pregnancy as well as child victims of HIV/AIDS transmission are protected in their rights to obtain education.

Part Three

Health and Social

Article 53

(1) The Regional Government must provide comprehensive health services for children, so that every child obtains optimal health status from the womb.

(2) Comprehensive health services as referred to in paragraph (1) include:

a. promotive, preventive, curative and rehabilitative efforts for basic health services;
b. exemption from financial burdens for malnourished children, children with disabilities, children with special needs, children infected with HIV/AIDS, child workers, child victims of narcotics, alcohol, psychotropic and other addictive substance abuse, children who are victims of violence, and child victims of human trafficking.
(3) The Regional Government, community, family and parents must ensure that children who are born are protected from diseases that threaten survival and/or cause disability.

Article 54

(1) Promotive efforts for basic health services as referred to in Article 53 paragraph (2) letter a are aimed at improving health status more optimally.

(2) Promotive efforts as referred to in paragraph (1) are carried out by providing socialization or education to the community regarding healthy living patterns which include:

a. the importance of exclusive breastfeeding for infants;
b. the importance of immunization for toddlers;
c. clean living patterns;
d. the importance of nutritious food;
e. the dangers of drugs and smoking; and
(3) In addition to promotive efforts as referred to in paragraph (2), the Regional Government may carry out other activities in the framework of health provision.

Article 55

(1) Preventive efforts for basic health services as referred to in Article 53 paragraph (2) letter a are aimed at preventing the occurrence of diseases or health problems.

(2) Efforts as referred to in paragraph (1) are carried out by:

a. requiring mothers to provide exclusive breastfeeding to infants up to 6 (six) months of age;
b. providing free immunization and vitamins to infants and toddlers;
c. providing nutritious food to toddlers from underprivileged families;
d. optimizing the role of posyandu for health services for mothers and toddlers;
e. conducting periodic health examinations; and
f. providing sanitation facilities and clean water in every school.

Article 56

(1) Curative efforts for basic health services as referred to in Article 53 paragraph (2) letter a are aimed at preventing diseases from becoming more severe through treatment.

(2) Treatment as referred to in paragraph (1) is provided free of charge for children from underprivileged families.

Article 57

(1) Rehabilitative efforts for basic health services as referred to in Article 53 paragraph (2) letter a are aimed at maintaining and restoring conditions after illness.

(2) Rehabilitative efforts as referred to in paragraph (1) are carried out through periodic health examinations.

Article 58

(1) The Regional Government must provide social welfare for:

a. children in conflict with the law;
b. child victims of violence who are economically and sexually exploited, neglected children, child victims of abduction and child victims of human trafficking;
c. children who do not have parents and are cared for by parties or families who are unable;
d. child victims of natural disasters or social disasters; and
e. children with disabilities;
(2) Social welfare as referred to in paragraph (1) may be in the form of providing services:
a. health;
b. education;
c. legal assistance;
d. accompaniment;
e. social, mental and spiritual guidance;
f. social rehabilitation; and/or
g. reintegration.

Part Four

Employment

Article 59

The Regional Government conducts periodic supervision of every person/entity that employs children in efforts to protect child workers.

Article 60

Every person/entity that employs children must consider the requirements:

a. minimum age of 15 (fifteen) years;
b. obtain written consent from the parents/guardian of the child worker;
c. have a written employment agreement between the employer and the parents/guardian of the child worker and obtain validation from the authorized agency;
d. not employ children at night;
e. maximum working time of 3 (three) hours per day;
f. not be employed in places or environments that can interfere with the child's growth and development process, whether physically, mentally, morally and intellectually or the child's health;
g. provide opportunities to obtain education in accordance with their talents and interests;
h. employ for light types of work; and
i. provide one day off per week.

Article 61

In the case where children are employed together with adult workers, the workplace of children must be separated from the workplace of adult workers.

Article 62

(1) Every person/entity is prohibited from employing and involving children in the worst forms of work.

(2) The worst forms of work referred to in paragraph (1) include:

a. all work in the form of slavery or similar practices;
b. all work that exploits, provides, or offers children for prostitution, pornography production, pornographic performances, or gambling;
c. all work that exploits, provides, or involves children in the production and trade of alcoholic beverages, narcotics, psychotropics, and other addictive substances; and/or
d. all work that endangers the health, safety, or morals of children.

Part Five

Facilitation of the Juvenile Justice System

Article 63

(1) Facilitation of the juvenile justice system includes criminal and civil justice.

(2) Facilitation of the criminal justice system as referred to in paragraph (1) includes:

a. provision of companions or child volunteers during examination by investigators;
b. provision of legal assistance and psychologist services;
c. involving members/investigators of local police in efforts to prevent criminal acts committed by Children, children as victims or children as witnesses;
d. realizing restorative justice processes for children in conflict with the law; and
e. rehabilitation and social reintegration services;
(3) Fulfillment of children's rights in the civil justice system as referred to in paragraph (1) includes provision and delivery of consultation services and legal assistance during the judicial process.
(4) In facilitating the juvenile justice system as referred to in paragraph (1) and paragraph (2), the authorized Regional Government is the Regional Government that administers government affairs in the field of women and child protection and social affairs.

Part Six

Children's Forum

Article 64

(1) In organizing the fulfillment of children's rights to express opinions and participate, the Regional Government facilitates the establishment of a children's forum.

(2) The children's forum as referred to in paragraph (1) represents children in the region, both the representation of children's geographical domicile, components of children's socio-cultural groups and children's educational background.

(3) In every formulation of policies related to children, the Regional Government must consider and accommodate children's opinions submitted through the children's participation forum.

(4) Further provisions regarding the establishment of the children's forum as referred to in paragraph (1) to paragraph (3) are stipulated by Regent Decree.

Part Seven

Child-Friendly Regency

Article 65

(1) In the framework of fulfilling children's rights in an integrated, systematic and sustainable manner, the Regional Government establishes a Regional Action Plan for Child-Friendly Regency Development.

(2) The Regional Action Plan for Child-Friendly Regency Development contains:

a. the concept of Child-Friendly Regency concerning objectives, strategies and roles of related parties;
b. children's rights; and
c. approach to developing a Child-Friendly Regency.

Article 66

(1) The Regional Action Plan for Child-Friendly Regency Development is directed towards the fulfillment of children's rights divided into 5 (five) target groups, comprising:

a. civil rights and freedoms;
b. family environment and alternative care;
c. basic health and welfare;
d. education, utilization of leisure time, and cultural activities; and
e. special protection.
(2) Further provisions regarding the Regional Action Plan for Child-Friendly Regency Development as referred to in paragraph (1) shall be regulated in a Regent Regulation.
(1) In order to ensure the effectiveness of the implementation of the Regional Action Plan for Child-Friendly Regency Development in the Region, a Child-Friendly Regency Task Force is established.
(2) The establishment and membership composition of the Child-Friendly Regency Task Force shall be stipulated by Regent Decree.
(3) The Child-Friendly Regency Task Force shall be domiciled at the Regional Apparatus Organization that administers government affairs in the field of women empowerment and child protection.
(4) The Child-Friendly Regency Task Force as referred to in paragraph (1) shall have the following main duties:
a. coordinate the implementation of policies and development of Child-Friendly Regency;
b. conduct socialization, advocacy and information communication and education on Child-Friendly Regency policies;
c. collect baseline data for Child-Friendly Regency development;
d. conduct needs analysis sourced from baseline data;
e. disseminate baseline data;
f. determine program focus and priorities in realizing a Child-Friendly Regency, adjusted to regional potential;
g. formulate a 5 (five) year regional action plan for Child-Friendly Regency and working mechanisms; and
h. conduct monitoring, evaluation and reporting at least once (1) a year.

Article 68

In carrying out its duties, the Child-Friendly Regency Task Force may coordinate and cooperate with P2TP2A institutions and other institutions that provide child protection services.

ON WOMEN'S EMPOWERMENT AND CHILD PROTECTION

I. GENERAL

The 1945 Constitution as the constitutional foundation has explicitly regulated the importance of protection of Human Rights, including women's and children's rights. In addition to the guarantee from the 1945 Constitution of the Republic of Indonesia, the guarantee that every person must have equal dignity and degree has also been recognized internationally. The Universal Declaration of Human Rights or UDHR of 1948 which underpins Human Rights in general has guaranteed that all people are born with freedom, having equal dignity and rights without exception.

The 1945 Constitution of the Republic of Indonesia mandates the Regional Government to regulate and administer regional government affairs aimed at realizing the welfare of the community through improving services, protection and empowerment of the community. As a community component that plays an important role in realizing a quality generation and as successors to the nation's struggle, women and children must receive guarantees for the fulfillment of their rights.

To ensure the implementation of women's empowerment and child protection in accordance with human dignity and in the context of carrying out mandatory affairs, the regional government needs to provide legal certainty in the implementation of women's empowerment and child protection through the formulation of a policy. The formulation of such policy is a form of support and concrete effort from various parties, starting from the Regional Government, community and other parties in ensuring the fulfillment of women's and children's rights.

The Implementation of Women's Empowerment and Child Protection is carried out based on the principles of:

a. non-discrimination;
b. the best interests of women and children;
c. the right to life, survival, and development;
d. respect for children's opinions; e. Legal certainty;
f. local wisdom; and justice and gender equality.

The formulation of this Regional Regulation on Women's Empowerment and Child Protection aims to:

a. provide guidance in planning and implementing Women's Empowerment and Child Protection policies;
b. increase Women's Empowerment efforts and fulfillment of children's rights in order to realize improved quality of life for women and children in the Region;
c. increase protection efforts for women and children from actions and policies that violate the rights of women and children;
d. increase the value of local wisdom and the role of custom in Women's Empowerment and Child Protection; and
e. increase the role of Regional Government and other community institutions in efforts for Women's Empowerment and Child Protection.

This Regional Regulation concerning Women and Child Protection regulates:

a. Improving the quality of life of women which includes government responsibility, implementation of PUG, Efforts to increase the role, quality and capacity of women in the political, legal, social and economic fields;
b. Protection of women which includes regional government responsibility in the protection of women, prevention of violence against women, protection for women victims of violence, and complaint handling;
c. Child protection which includes the responsibility of regional government, parents/family and community in the protection of children as well as efforts made by the regional government in the implementation of child protection. In addition, the need to establish children's forums and Child-Friendly Regency as a form of appreciation in realizing protection and fulfillment of children's rights.
