PROTECTION OF WOMEN

BY THE GRACE OF ALMIGHTY GOD

THE REGENT OF BEKASI,

a. that women are assets who play a very important role in the process of continuation and creation of quality generations so they need to receive protection guarantees from acts of violence and discrimination and need to be empowered in order to actualize their potential optimally;

b. that in order to realize the fulfillment of women's constitutional rights to be free from torture, treatment that degrades human dignity, the right to obtain security and freedom from all violence and to improve the quality of life of women, they need to receive protection and empowerment from the regional government of Bekasi Regency; C. that the implementation of protection of women in Bekasi Regency has not been carried out optimally and is accompanied by violations of women's rights, therefore comprehensive regulations are needed to provide direction, foundation, and legal certainty;

d. that based on the considerations as referred to in letter a, letter b, and letter c, it is necessary to establish a Regional Regulation on the Protection of Women;

CHAPTER I

GENERAL PROVISIONS

Article 1

In this Regional Regulation what is meant by:

1.

Regency is the Regency of Bekasi.

2. Regent is the Regent of Bekasi.

3. Regency Government is the Regent as an element of regional government administration who leads the implementation of government affairs that are the authority of the autonomous region.

4. Regional Secretary is the Regional Secretary of Bekasi Regency

5. Regional Apparatus is the auxiliary element of the Regent and Regional House of Representatives in government affairs that are the authority of the region.

6. Woman is a person who is female from infancy, adolescence, adulthood to the elderly, who has been recognized by law.

7. Protection is all efforts aimed at providing a sense of security to women carried out by family parties, advocates, social institutions, police, prosecutors, courts or other parties either temporarily or based on court orders.

Protection of Women is all activities aimed at providing a sense of security carried out by the Regional Government, community, and/or other parties who know or hear that violence against women will occur or has occurred. 8.

9. Discrimination is any restriction, harassment or exclusion that is directly or indirectly based on differentiation of humans on the basis of religion, ethnicity, race, ethnic group, group, class, social status, economic status, gender, language, political beliefs, which results in the reduction, deviation or elimination of the recognition, implementation or use of human rights and fundamental freedoms in life both individually and collectively in the fields of politics, economics, law, education, social, culture and other aspects of life.

10. Family is the smallest unit in society consisting of husband, wife, or husband, wife and their children, or father and children, or mother and children, or blood relatives in direct line upward or downward up to the third degree.

11. Household is husband, wife, children, people who have family relations due to blood relations, marriage, breastfeeding, care, and representation, and/or domestic workers and reside in that household.

12. Victim is a woman who experiences misery and/or suffering either directly or indirectly as a result of violence and life vulnerability.

13. Violence is any act that results in or may result in misery or suffering whether physical, psychological, sexual, economic and social as well as discrimination against victims that creates danger to life, body or results in the deprivation of a person's freedom.

14. Violence Against Women is any act that results in or may result in misery or suffering whether physical, psychological, sexual, socio-economic and discrimination, including threats of certain actions, coercion or deprivation of freedom, whether occurring in public or private life.

15. Domestic Violence is any act against a person, especially women, that results in misery or suffering physically, sexually, psychologically and/or threats of domestic neglect including threats to commit

LO acts of coercion or deprivation of freedom unlawfully within the household scope.

16. Physical Violence is any act that results in pain, injury, wounds or disability to a person's body, miscarriage, fainting and or causes death.

17. Psychological Violence is an act that results in fear, loss of self-confidence, loss of ability to act, feelings of helplessness and/or other psychological suffering to a person

18. Sexual Violence is any act in the form of sexual harassment, coercion of sexual relations, coercion of unnatural or unwanted sexual relations, coercion of sexual relations with others for commercial purposes and or certain other purposes.

19. Women's Rights is a set of rights inherent in a woman or group of people not to experience discrimination, misery or suffering either directly or indirectly committed by state apparatus or by the state or by regional government apparatus or individuals which are natural rights given by God Almighty.

20. Service is an action taken as soon as possible to victims when seeing, hearing and or knowing that violence against victims will occur, is occurring or has occurred.

21. Integrated Service is a series of activities to provide protection for witnesses and victims of violence carried out jointly by relevant agencies or institutions as a unified implementation of health rehabilitation, social rehabilitation, repatriation, social reintegration, and legal assistance.

22. Disability is an individual who has physical or mental limitations.

23. Complaint is a series of actions taken by the organizer of the Integrated Service Center to follow up on reports of acts of violence against victims of violence.

24. Health Rehabilitation is the recovery of victims of violence from the health disorders they suffer.

25. Social Rehabilitation is a service aimed at restoring the psychological condition and developing the abilities of a person who experiences social dysfunction so that they can carry out their social functions properly.

26. Legal Assistance is legal services provided by assistants, law enforcement officers which include providing legal consultation, exercising power of attorney, representing, assisting, defending and taking other legal actions for victims of violence.

27. Repatriation is an effort to return victims of violence from the receiving area/where the violence occurred to the area of origin or to the family.

28. Social Reintegration is an effort to reunite victims of violence with family, community, institutions or other social environments that can provide protection.

29. Regional Technical Implementation Unit for the Protection of Women and Children, hereinafter abbreviated as UPTD PPA, is an institution established by the Regional Government to provide services to women and children who experience criminal and civil problems.

30. Safe House is a shelter for victims during the assistance process, for the safety and comfort of victims from threats and danger from perpetrators.

31. Standard Operating Procedure, hereinafter abbreviated as SOP, is standard steps that must be taken in protecting victims starting from complaints/identification, health rehabilitation, social rehabilitation, legal services to repatriation and social reintegration of witnesses and/or victims.

CHAPTER II

RIGHTS OF WOMEN

Part One

Basic Rights of Women

Article 2

(1) Women have the right:

a. to life;
b. to form a family and bear descendants;
c. to self-development;
d. to obtain justice;
e. to personal freedom;
f. to security;
g. to welfare; and
h. to participate in government.
(2) Every woman has the right:
a. to live peacefully, safely, in peace, happily, prosperous physically and spiritually;
b. to be free from all threats of violence and/or exploitation;
c. to live in a family within lawful marriage bonds;
d. to sustain life and improve their standard of living;
e. to have work that they like and also the right to fair employment conditions;
f. to obtain education and instruction in all types, levels and pathways of education in accordance with specified requirements;
g. to a healthy and clean environment;
h. to receive fair wages according to their work that is comparable and commensurate with their human dignity;
i. to elect and/or be elected in general elections, regional head elections, village head elections and/or other political office elections based on equal rights through direct, general, free, confidential, honest and fair voting according to applicable laws and regulations;
j. to receive special protection in carrying out their work or profession against matters that may threaten their safety and/or health related to women's reproductive function; and
k. special rights inherent to women due to their reproductive function are guaranteed and protected by law.
1. to receive legal protection and assistance for problems they face in accordance with the provisions of laws and regulations
m. other rights in accordance with the provisions of laws and regulations.
(3) Every woman with disabilities, elderly, pregnant has the right to receive facilities and special treatment.
(4) Every elderly woman, with physical and/or mental disabilities, has the right to receive care, education, training and special assistance from the Regional Government to ensure a decent life in accordance with their human dignity.
(5) The Regional Government shall endeavor to provide an old-age protection guarantee system for elderly women.

Part Two

Rights of Women with Disabilities

Article 3

Every woman with disabilities has the right:

a. to reproductive health;
b. to accept or refuse the use of contraceptives;
c. to receive greater protection from multiple discrimination;
d. to receive greater protection from acts of violence, including sexual violence and exploitation;
e. to obtain education in all units, pathways, types and levels of education;
f. to obtain decent work and livelihood in accordance with the type and degree of disability, education and abilities;
g. to obtain accessibility for their independence;
h. to obtain rehabilitation, social assistance and maintenance of social welfare standards; and
i. to obtain equal rights to develop their talents, abilities and social life.

Part Three

Rights of Women Victims of Violence

Article 4

Every woman victim of violence has the right to:

a. receive protection;
b. to determine their own decisions;
c. receive information;
d. receive complaint handling;
e. receive assistance;
f. receive continuous handling up to the rehabilitation stage; and
g. receive confidential handling from individuals, groups or institutions whether government or non-government.

Article 5

Women victims of acts of violence have the right to receive protection, information, integrated services, continuous handling up to the rehabilitation stage and confidential handling from individuals, groups or institutions whether government or non-government.

Part Four

Special Rights for the Protection of Women

Article 6

(1) Women have the right to receive special protection from matters that threaten their safety and/or health.

(2) Special protection is given to women because their reproductive function is guaranteed and protected by law.

(3) Special protection as referred to in paragraph (2) consists of health services related to: a. b.

C. d. menstruation; pregnancy; childbirth; and breastfeeding

(4) Health services as referred to in paragraph (3) may be provided by parties employing female workers in accordance with health service standards.

Article 7

(1) Women have equal opportunities without discrimination to obtain employment.

(2) Equal opportunities as referred to in paragraph (1) are realized through the fulfillment of women's rights to receive equal treatment from parties who employ them.

(3) Parties employing female workers must provide protection in accordance with applicable laws and regulations.

(4) Protection that must be provided by parties employing female workers as referred to in paragraph (3) shall be further regulated by Regent Regulation.

CHAPTER III

FORMS OF VIOLENCE

Article 8

Forms of violence include:

a. Physical Violence;
b. Psychological Violence;
c. Sexual Violence; and
d. Domestic Violence;

Article 9

Physical Violence as referred to in Article 8 letter a is caused by acts that result in pain, bruising, injury, wounds or disability to a person's body, miscarriage, unconsciousness and/or cause death.

Article 10

Psychological Violence as referred to in Article 8 letter b is caused by acts that result in fear, loss of self-confidence, loss of ability to act, feelings of helplessness and/or severe psychological suffering in a person.

Article 11

(1) Sexual Violence as referred to in Article 8 letter c is caused by:

a. acts in the form of sexual harassment;
b. sexual assault;
c. coercion of sexual relations in an unnatural or unwanted manner; and
d. coercion of sexual relations with others and/or exploitation of women for commercial purposes and other specific purposes.
(2) Coercion of sexual relations as referred to in Article 11 paragraph (1) letter c is excluded for persons with mental disorders or sexual disorders in marital relations.

Article 12

Domestic Violence as referred to in Article 8 letter d is caused by:

a. acts that neglect women within the household sphere by someone who is obligated to provide livelihood, care or maintenance to such women;
b. acts that result in economic dependence by restricting and/or prohibiting decent work inside or outside the home so that the victim is under someone's control; and
c. acts committed by family members that result in pain, bruising, injury, wounds or disability to a person's body, miscarriage, unconsciousness and/or cause death.

CHAPTER IV

PREVENTION, PROTECTION SERVICES, AND EMPOWERMENT Part One

Prevention of Violence Against Women

Article 13

(1) The Regional Government shall undertake integrated efforts to prevent violence against women in the Region.

(2) The integrated efforts to prevent violence against women as referred to in paragraph (1) shall be coordinated by the Regional Apparatus responsible for the protection and empowerment of women. (3) The prevention efforts as referred to in paragraphs (1) and (2) shall be implemented by means of:

a. conducting coordination, integration, synchronization of violence prevention based on partnership patterns that may involve community organizations, universities, professionals as well as traditional leaders and/or community figures;
b. conducting socialization regarding laws and regulations relating to the protection of the rights of women victims of violence;
c. establishing an integrated violence prevention system;
d. providing ease of access to obtain civil rights, education, health, employment, training and funding, welfare improvement and social services;
e. providing education regarding women's rights to the community;
f. cultivating community participation and concern for the protection of women from acts of violence;
g. building and providing a complete and easily accessible information system; and
h. establishing a cooperation network in efforts to prevent acts of violence with law enforcement apparatus, government apparatus, universities and various non-governmental organizations that work and/or care for women.

Article 14

(1) The prevention of acts of violence against women as referred to in Article 13 shall be implemented by the Regional Apparatus responsible for the affairs of:

a. health;
b. education;
c. employment;
d. socio-political;
e. protection and empowerment of women; and f. peace and order; and
(2) The prevention of acts of violence by Regional Apparatus as referred to in paragraph (1) shall be implemented in an integrated and continuous manner based on the Regional Action Plan with the Regional Apparatus responsible for the Protection and Empowerment of Women.

(1) Part Two

Protection Services

Article 15

The provision of services to victims shall be conducted in an integrated manner by UPTD PPA.

(2) UPTD PPA as referred to in paragraph (1) may receive and send case referrals from or to other service units in a networked manner.

Article 16

Forms of services for the protection of women and women victims of violence include: a. complaint, consultation and counseling services;

b. accompaniment services;

C. health services;

d. social rehabilitation services; e. legal services and/or legal aid; and f. repatriation and social reintegration services.

Article 17

Complaint, consultation, and counseling services as referred to in Article 16 letter a include:

a. identification or initial recording of victims;
b. follow-up recommendations; and
c. follow-up approval.

Article 18

(1)

Accompaniment services as referred to in Article 16 letter b include:

a. accompanying victims during the examination process and health recovery;
b. accompanying victims during the medical examination process for legal purposes;
c. accompanying victims during the examination process at the police, prosecutor's office and court;
d. monitoring the interests and rights of victims in the examination process at the police, prosecutor's office and court;
e. maintaining the privacy and confidentiality of victims from all interested parties, including news coverage by mass media;
f. coordinating with other companions; and
g. providing rehabilitation.

(2)

Accompaniment Services as referred to in paragraph (1) which are continuous handling up to the stage referred to constitute service activities and actions carried out by companions originating from individuals or representatives from institutions that have expertise in accompanying victims to conduct counseling, therapy, and advocacy for the strengthening and recovery of victims of violence.

Article 19

Health services as referred to in Article 16 letter c shall be implemented by the Regional Apparatus responsible for health affairs in the form of:

a. medical assistance to victims;
b. care and recovery of physical injuries aimed at restoring the physical condition of victims carried out by medical and paramedical personnel;
c. conducting visum for investigation purposes;
d. provision of services in accordance with SOPs to women victims of violence; and
e. health service referrals in accordance with the provisions of laws and regulations.

Article 20

(1) Social rehabilitation services as referred to in Article 16 letter d shall be implemented by UPTD PPA in the form of:

a. provision of spiritual guidance to victims; and
b. psychological recovery of victims.
(2) Social rehabilitation services as referred to in paragraph (1) constitute services provided in order to restore the traumatic condition of victims, including the provision of safe houses to protect witnesses and victims from various threats and intimidation.

Article 21

Legal services as referred to in Article 16 letter e are to assist victims in undergoing the judicial process by means of:

a. providing legal consultation that includes information regarding victim rights and the judicial process;
b. accompanying victims at the investigation, prosecution and examination levels in court hearings and assisting victims to fully describe the violence they experienced; and
c. coordinating with law enforcement officers, volunteer companions and social workers so that the judicial process runs as it should.

Article 22

(1) Repatriation and social reintegration services as referred to in Article 16 letter f aim to return victims to their families and social environment;

(2) Repatriation and social reintegration services as referred to in paragraph (1) shall be conducted by the Regional Government in coordination with:

a. regency/city governments within one provincial area; and
b. relevant agencies and institutions both government and non-government.

Part Three

Women's Empowerment

Article 23

(1) The empowerment of women victims of violence shall be conducted in an integrated manner in accordance with the provisions of laws and regulations.

(2) The empowerment of women victims of violence as referred to in paragraph (1) is directed to obtain opportunities and rights as human beings in order to be able to play a role and participate in activities: a. health;

b. education;

C. economy; d. law; e. employment; f. politics and government.

Article 24

women's empowerment in the field of

(1) The implementation of health empowerment as referred to in Article 23 paragraph (2) letter a shall be implemented by the Regional Apparatus responsible for Health affairs.

(2) Forms of empowerment in the health field as referred to in paragraph (1) are in the form of health services in order to undergo a healthy, safe reproductive and sexual life, and free from coercion and/or violence.

(3) Health services as referred to in paragraph (2) include:

a. Health services for the period before pregnancy, pregnancy, childbirth and after delivery;
b. Pregnancy regulation, contraceptive services and sexual health;
c. Reproductive health services; and
d. Services and provision of adequate lactation rooms at every Regional government agency, public facilities and industry.
(4) Health services as referred to in paragraph (2) shall be implemented through promotive, preventive, curative and rehabilitative approaches.

Article 25

(1) Empowerment of women in the field of education as referred to in Article 23 paragraph (2) letter b is implemented by Regional Apparatus with authority in Education in the form of formal and non-formal education.

(2) Non-formal education as referred to in paragraph (1) aims to improve women's capabilities.

(3) Non-formal education as referred to in paragraph (2) includes women's empowerment education, skills education and job training.

(4) The implementation of formal and non-formal education as referred to in paragraph (1) is carried out in accordance with statutory provisions.

Article 26

Implementation of Women's Empowerment in the economic field as referred to in Article 23 paragraph (2) letter c is implemented by Regional Apparatus with authority in micro, small and medium enterprises and other related agencies in the form of: a. provision of skills and job training;

b. facilitation of the formation and strengthening of productive economic business groups;

C. facilitation of the development of marketing networks.

(1) Implementation

Article 27

Empowerment of Women in the legal field as referred to in Article 23 paragraph (2) letter d is implemented by Regional Apparatus and other related institutions in the form of:

a. increasing awareness and knowledge in the legal field through communication, information and education services; and
b. facilitation of access and legal consultation services.
(2) Empowerment of women in the legal field as referred to in paragraph (1) is implemented in accordance with the provisions of statutory regulations.

Article 28

Implementation of Women's Empowerment in the field of

Women in the field of employment as referred to in Article 23 paragraph (2) letter e is implemented by Regional Apparatus with authority in manpower and other related agencies in the form of: a. b. women have equal opportunities without discrimination to obtain employment; and employers who employ female workers are required to provide protection that includes welfare, safety and health.

Article 29

(1) Implementation of Women's Empowerment in the field of politics and government as referred to in Article 23 paragraph (2) letter f is implemented by the National Unity and Politics Agency and other related agencies in the form of:

a. involvement of women in decision-making at various levels;
b. providing opportunities for women to hold public office;
c. participation in general elections; and
d. self-development through organizations to associate, assemble and express opinions.
(2) Empowerment of women in the field of politics and government as referred to in paragraph (1) is implemented in accordance with statutory regulations.
(3) The Regional Government guarantees women to have the right to vote and/or be elected in general elections, regional head elections, village head elections and/or other political office elections based on equal rights through direct, general, free, secret, honest and fair voting in accordance with the provisions of statutory regulations.
(4) The Regional Government provides opportunities for women to be appointed as Regional Government officials and occupy strategic positions in regional government.
(5) The Regional Government and/or Political Parties are responsible for providing political education for women In the effort

CHAPTER V

OBLIGATIONS AND RESPONSIBILITIES

Article 30

protection of women victims

The Regional Government is obligated to: violence,

a. ensure the fulfillment of women's rights and the provision of guarantees of security from acts of violence, and efforts to exploit women in the region;

b. establish policies, programs, activities for the protection of women and guarantees for victim handling to eliminate all forms of discrimination and acts of violence against women in the region;

c. provide guidance and supervision in the effort to protect women carried out by Regional Apparatus handling the Protection and Empowerment of Women;

d. endeavor to establish a women's protection institution;

e. establish policies, programs and activities for the protection of women that have been determined; and

f. conduct socialization of statutory regulations related to the protection of women victims of violence and discrimination;

Article 31

The Regional Government is responsible for:

a. implementing activities to ensure the fulfillment of women's rights;
b. providing facilities and infrastructure for women's protection;
c. applying minimum service standards in women's protection services and standard operating procedures for repatriation and social reintegration that apply;
d. providing implementing officers and functional officers at UPTD PPA;
e. involving the community in the implementation of women's protection.
f. providing adequate budget for women's protection programs according to regional capacity; and
g. conducting guidance and protection of women in the Region. on supervision of the implementation

CHAPTER VII

MONITORING, EVALUATION AND REPORTING

Part One

Monitoring

Article 34

(1) To ensure synergy, continuity and effectiveness in the implementation of policies, programs and activities for the empowerment and protection of women, the Regional Government conducts monitoring. (2) Monitoring as referred to in paragraph (1) is intended to determine the progress and obstacles in the implementation of policies, programs and activities for the empowerment and protection of women in

the Region.

(3) Monitoring as referred to in paragraph (2) is conducted periodically starting from planning to policies, programs and activities for the empowerment and protection of women for the current year.

(4) The results of monitoring as referred to in paragraph (3) are reported to the Regent periodically.

Part Two

Evaluation

Article 35

(1) Evaluation of the implementation of policies, programs and activities for the protection of women is conducted at the end of every fiscal year.

(2) The results of the evaluation of the implementation of policies, programs and activities for the protection of women are used as input for the formulation of policies, programs and activities for the following year.

Part Three

Reporting

(2) Participation as referred to in paragraph (1) is carried out by individuals, groups as well as social community organizations, non-governmental organizations, educational institutions, religious institutions, business community and mass media.

(3) Community participation as referred to in paragraph (1) may take the form of:

(4)

Further provisions regarding community participation as referred to in paragraph (2) are regulated by Regent Regulation.

CHAPTER IX

FINANCING

Article 39

Financing for the implementation of women's protection in the Region is sourced from the regional revenue and expenditure budget and other legitimate and non-binding sources of funds.

CHAPTER X

ADMINISTRATIVE SANCTIONS

Article 40

(1) Whosoever person or institution or entity that intentionally does not provide protection to women resulting in the occurrence of acts of violence against women, allows acts of violence to occur, and/or does not report to the relevant agency may be subject to administrative sanctions.

(2) Business Entities or non-governmental organizations are obliged to provide Protection for Women and acts of Violence in their workplace environment.

(3) Business Entities or non-governmental organizations are obliged to report acts of violence in their workplace environment to the relevant agency.

(4) Violations as referred to in paragraph (1), paragraph (2) and paragraph (3)

may be subject to administrative sanctions in the form of:

a. verbal warning;
b. written warning;
c. temporary cessation of activities;
d. termination of cooperation;
e. revocation of registered certificate;
f. revocation of operational permit;
g. sealing;
h. withdrawal of facilities; and/or i. administrative fine of at least Rp.10,000,000 (Ten Million Rupiah) and at most Rp. 50,000,000 (Fifty Million Rupiah)
(5) Provisions regarding procedures for the application of administrative sanctions as referred to in paragraph (4) are regulated in the Regent Regulation.
