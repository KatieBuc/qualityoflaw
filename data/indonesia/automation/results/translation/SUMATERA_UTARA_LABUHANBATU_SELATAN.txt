PROTECTION OF WOMEN AND CHILDREN FROM ACTS OF VIOLENCE BY THE GRACE OF GOD ALMIGHTY

REGENT OF SOUTH LABUHANBATU,

Considering : a. that violence against women and children in South Labuhanbatu Regency continues to increase causing community members to feel unsafe in carrying out their lives, so integrated protection efforts are needed;

b. that in order to optimize the implementation of protection of women and children from acts of violence in South Labuhanbatu Regency, it is necessary to provide direction, foundation, and legal certainty to all parties involved in the implementation of protection of women and children;

c. that based on the considerations as referred to in letter a and letter b, it is necessary to establish a Regional Regulation on Protection of Women and Children from

Acts of Violence.

CHAPTER I

GENERAL PROVISIONS

Article 1

In this Regional Regulation, the following terms shall mean:

1. Region is South Labuhanbatu Regency.
2. Regional Government is the Regent as an element of the Regional Government administrator who leads the implementation of governmental affairs which are the authority of the autonomous region.
3. Regent is the Regent of South Labuhanbatu.
4. Discrimination is any distinction, harassment or exclusion that is direct or indirect based on human differentiation on the grounds of religion, ethnicity, race, ethnic group, social status group, economic status, gender, language, political beliefs that result in the reduction, deviation or elimination of recognition, implementation or use of human rights and fundamental freedoms in life both individually and collectively in the political, economic, legal, social, cultural and other aspects of life.
5. Violence is any act that results in suffering or pain whether physical, sexual, psychological, and/or domestic neglect including threats to commit acts, coercion, or unlawful deprivation of a person's liberty. 6. Child is a person who has not reached 18 years of age, including a Child who is still in the womb.
7. Woman is a person who is female. 8. Violence against Women is any action that results in or may result in suffering or pain of Women physically, sexually, economically, socially, psychologically, including threats of certain actions, coercion or deprivation of freedom, whether occurring in public or private life.
9. Violence against Children is any action that results in or may result in suffering of Children physically, psychologically, sexually, neglect, exploitation, and other violence.
10. Victim is a Woman and Child who experiences violence and/or threat of violence.
11. Protection of Women is all activities aimed at providing a sense of security carried out by the Regional Government, police, prosecutor's office, court, social institutions, or other parties who know or hear that Violence against Women will or has occurred.
12. Child Protection is all activities to guarantee and protect Children and their rights so that they can live, grow, develop and participate optimally in accordance with human dignity and receive protection from acts of Violence and discrimination.
13. Service is an action taken as soon as possible to Victims when seeing, hearing and knowing that Violence against Victims will, is or has occurred.
14. Companion is a person or representative from an institution who has the expertise to accompany Victims to conduct counseling, therapy and advocacy, integrated for the empowerment and recovery of Violence Victims.
15. Gender is the difference in nature, role, function and status between Women and men that is not based on biological differences, but rather based on socio-cultural relations influenced by broad societal structures.
16. Gender Mainstreaming is a strategy built to integrate Gender into an integral dimension of planning, formulation, implementation, monitoring, and evaluation of policies, programs, and development activities in the region.
17. Office for Women's Empowerment and Child Protection, hereinafter abbreviated as Dinas PPPA, is an office that administers matters of Women's Empowerment and Child Protection.
18. Integrated Service Center for Women's Empowerment and Child Protection, hereinafter abbreviated as P2TP2A, is an integrated Service center in the effort to empower Women and Children established by the Regional Government with the active participation of the Community which includes information centers, consultation centers, complaint services, primary health care, counseling, legal assistance, repatriation and social reintegration, shelter/temporary safe house (shelter), and referral centers.
19. Safe House is a shelter/temporary protection house for Victims, during the accompaniment process, for the safety and comfort of Victims from threats and dangers of perpetrators, and is only known to interested parties such as certain personnel at UPT P2TP2A for reasons of safety and security of Victims.
20. Standard Operating Procedure, hereinafter abbreviated as SOP, is the standard steps that must be taken in protecting Victims starting from complaints/identification, rehabilitation, health, Social Rehabilitation, advocacy to repatriation and social reintegration of witnesses and/or Victims.
21. Family is the smallest unit in the Community consisting of husband and wife, or husband and wife and their Child, or father and his Child, or mother and her Child or blood Family in a straight line upwards or downwards up to the third degree and/or if any up to the fourth degree.
22. Household is husband, wife, and Child, people who have Family relations due to blood relations, marriage, breastfeeding, care, representation, and/or Household workers and reside in that Household.
23. Community is individuals, Families, groups, and social organizations and/or community organizations.
24. Regional Revenue and Expenditure Budget, hereinafter abbreviated as APBD, is the Regional Revenue and Expenditure Budget of South Labuhanbatu Regency.
25. Social Rehabilitation is the process of refunctionalization and development to enable a person to carry out their social functions properly in Community life.

Article 2

The Protection of Women and Children from acts of Violence is implemented based on the principles of:

a. respect for Human Rights;
b. the right to life, survival, and development;
c. non-discrimination;
d. the best interests of the Child;
e. justice and Gender equality; and
f. victim protection.

Article 3

This Regional Regulation is intended to provide a legal basis for the Regional Government in providing protection to Women and Children from acts of Violence.

Article 4

This Regional Regulation aims to:

a. respect and protect Human Rights;
b. guarantee the rights of every Woman and Child to survival, growth and development;
c. non-discrimination;
d. protect Women and Children from threats, fear, torture and or treatment that degrades dignity, self-esteem and human dignity;
e. prevent forms of violence against Women and Children; f. eliminate all forms of violence and exploitation against Women and Children;
g. protect and provide a sense of security for Women and Children; h. provide services to Women and Children who are victims of violence, reporters, and witnesses;
i. protect and prevent all forms of discrimination against Women and Children in the region;
j. Women's Empowerment;
k. the best interests of the Child; and
l. realize a region that is suitable for Women and Children.

Article 5

The scope of this Regional Regulation includes:

a. forms of Violence;
b. rights of Victims;
c. obligations and responsibilities;
d. implementation of protection of Women and Children from acts of Violence; e. coordination and cooperation;
f. participation of the Community and business sector;
g. guidance and supervision;
h. reporting; and
i. sources of funding.

CHAPTER II

FORMS OF VIOLENCE

Article 6

Forms of Violence against Women and Children may include: a. physical violence;

b. psychological violence;

c. sexual violence;

d. neglect; and

e. exploitation.

Article 7

Physical violence as referred to in Article 6 letter a is caused by acts that result in pain, injury, wounds or disabilities to a person's body, miscarriage, unconsciousness and/or causing death.

Article 8

Psychological violence as referred to in Article 6 letter b is caused by acts that result in fear, loss of self-confidence, loss of ability to act, feelings of helplessness and/or severe psychological suffering to a person.

Article 9

Sexual violence as referred to in Article 6 letter c is caused by: a. acts in the form of sexual harassment;

b. forced sexual relations;

c. forced sexual relations in an unnatural or unwanted manner; and/or d. forced sexual relations with others for commercial and or certain purposes.

Article 10

Neglect as referred to in Article 6 letter d is caused by: a. acts that result in the failure to fulfill the needs of Children properly, whether physical, mental, spiritual or social, committed by parents, guardians, or other parties responsible for care; b. acts of deliberately neglecting to maintain, care for, or look after Children as they should be, committed by parents, guardians or any other party responsible for their care; c. acts of neglecting a person within the scope of their Household, whereas according to the law applicable to them or due to an agreement or contract they are obliged to provide livelihood, care, or maintenance to that person; and/or

d. acts that result in economic dependency by limiting and/or prohibiting decent work inside or outside the home so that the Victim is under the control of that person.

Article 11

Exploitation as referred to in Article 6 letter e is caused by: a. acts that exploit economically or sexually with the intention of benefiting oneself or others;

b. acts with or without the consent of the Victim which include but are not limited to prostitution, forced labor or Services, slavery or similar practices, oppression, extortion, exploitation of physical, sexual, reproductive organs, or unlawfully removing or transplanting organs and/or body tissues or exploiting the labor or ability of a person by another party to obtain material or immaterial benefits; and/or

c. all forms of exploitation of sexual organs or other body organs of the Victim for profit, including but not limited to all activities of prostitution or indecency.

CHAPTER III

RIGHTS OF VICTIMS

Article 12

Every Victim has the right to obtain:

a. protection;
b. information;
c. health services in accordance with medical needs;
d. continuous handling up to the rehabilitation stage;
e. special handling related to the confidentiality of Victims; f. assistance by social workers and legal aid at every level of the examination process in accordance with the provisions of Laws and Regulations; g. guarantee of rights related to the status as a Family member or member of the Community; and
h. spiritual guidance services.

CHAPTER IV

OBLIGATIONS AND RESPONSIBILITIES

Part One

General

Article 13

Obligations and responsibilities in the implementation of protection of Women and Children from acts of Violence are the joint responsibility of: a. Regional Government;

b. community;

c. family; and

d. parents or guardians.

Part Two

Obligations and Responsibilities of Regional Government

Article 14

The Regional Government is obligated and responsible for the implementation of protection of Women and Children from acts of Violence.

Article 15

(1) The Regional Government shall:

a. implement policies for the protection of Women and Children from acts of Violence established by the government;
b. establish policies, programs, and activities for the protection of Women and Children from acts of Violence; and
c. cooperate with law enforcement apparatus, government apparatus, as well as educational, health, hospital institutions, and various NGOs that care for Women and Children in the implementation of protection of Women and Children from acts of Violence.
(2) The Regional Government is responsible for providing support for facilities and infrastructure for the implementation of protection of Women and Children from acts of Violence.
(3) In order to carry out the obligations as referred to in paragraph (1) letter b, the Regional Government establishes action programs and activities for the protection of Women and Children in 1 (one) Regional Action Plan as a basis for regional apparatus in implementing protection for Women and Children Victims of Violence.
(4) The Regional Action Plan as referred to in paragraph (3), is part of the Regional Medium Term Development Plan (RPJMD).
(5) The Regional Action Plan as referred to in paragraph (3), is further regulated by Regent Regulation.

Article 16

(1) The obligations and responsibilities of the Community as referred to in Article 13 letter b, are carried out in the form of Community participation.

(2) Forms of Community participation as referred to in paragraph (1), include: a. forming Family partners at the Sub-district/Village level;

b. forming units for the protection of Women and Children within community organizations;

c. conducting independent socialization of the rights of Women and Children; d. providing first aid to Victims; and

e. reporting to the competent authorities if Violence against Victims occurs in their environment.

(3) Community participation as referred to in paragraph (1) is carried out by individuals, social community institutions, community self-help institutions, educational institutions, religious institutions, private sector, and mass media.

(4) Forms of Community participation as referred to in paragraph (2), are carried out responsibly in accordance with the provisions of laws and regulations.

Article 17

The obligations of the Family and/or parents as referred to in Article 13 letter c and letter d have full responsibility to prevent all forms of Violence and protect Women and Children as Family members.

CHAPTER V

IMPLEMENTATION OF PROTECTION OF WOMEN AND CHILDREN FROM ACTS OF VIOLENCE

Part One

General

Article 18

In order to provide Services and protection to Women and Children from acts of Violence, the Regional Government establishes P2TP2A.

Part Two

Establishment of P2TP2A

Article 19

(1) P2TP2A as referred to in Article 18 consists of elements of the Community, universities, professional organizations, Community organizations concerned with the Community.

(2) The establishment of P2TP2A as referred to in paragraph (1) is determined by decision of the Regent.

(3) The implementation of Services to Victims is carried out in an integrated manner by Village/Sub-district P2TP2A, District P2TP2A, and Regency P2TP2A.

(4) P2TP2A as referred to in paragraph (1) has the task of:

a. receiving complaints/reports of Violence against Women and/or Children, consultation, and counseling;
b. receiving and referring to other Service units in stages;
c. providing legal assistance;
d. health;
e. Social Rehabilitation;
f. legal Services;
g. repatriation and social reintegration; and
h. providing protection to Victims, reporters and witnesses.
(5) P2TP2A may provide temporary services and protection in the form of a Safe House.
(6) In the event that P2TP2A does not yet have a Safe House as referred to in paragraph (5), then Victims of Violence are referred to P2TP2A that has a Safe House.
(7) P2TP2A consists of several Divisions consisting of:
a. Complaints Service division;
b. health Services division;
c. legal aid consultation; and
d. rehabilitation, Repatriation and Social Reintegration.

Article 20

(1) In carrying out the task of Service to Women and Children Victims of Violence as referred to in Article 19 paragraph (4), P2TP2A shall coordinate with the District P2TP2A.

(2) The mechanism for carrying out tasks as referred to in paragraph (1) shall be conducted in accordance with SOPs.

(3) Further provisions regarding the duties of P2TP2A as referred to in paragraphs (1) and (2) shall be regulated by Regent Regulation.

Part Three

Mechanism for Prevention and Forms of P2TP2A Service

Article 21

The Regional Government shall prevent acts of Violence against Women and Children through:

a. socialization activities on laws and regulations to the Community related to the protection of the rights of Women and Children;
b. conducting empowerment and awareness raising to Families, parents and the Community by providing information, guidance and/or counseling. c. increasing the quantity and quality of both formal and non-formal and informal education;
d. opening accessibility to obtain education, training, funding, income enhancement and social Services;
e. opening employment opportunities for Women;
f. building participation and awareness of the Community to implement prevention and protection of Women and Children from acts of Violence;
g. building and providing a complete and easily accessible information system;
h. building networks and cooperation with law enforcement apparatus, government apparatus, universities and various community non-governmental organizations that work and/or care for Women and Children;
i. opening complaint posts for the protection of Women and Children from acts of Violence; and
j. training for P2TP2A members related to the implementation of their duties in preventing Violence against Women and Children.

Article 22

(1) The prevention of Violence against Women and Children as referred to in Article 21 shall be carried out by Regional apparatus whose duties and functions are in the fields of:

a. social affairs;
b. health;
c. education;
d. manpower;
e. Women's Empowerment and Child Protection;
f. mental and spiritual affairs; and
g. peace and public order.
(2) Violence prevention by Regional apparatus as referred to in paragraph (1) shall be carried out in an integrated and sustainable manner based on the Regional action plan.
(3) Further provisions regarding the mechanism for implementing prevention as referred to in paragraph (1) shall be regulated by Regent Regulation.

Article 23

Forms of P2TP2A Service include:

a. Medical services, in the form of treatment and recovery of physical injuries aimed at restoring the physical condition of Victims carried out by medical and paramedical personnel;
b. Psychosocial services are services provided in order to restore the traumatic condition of Victims, including the provision of Safe Houses to protect Victims from various threats and intimidation for Victims and provide social support so that Victims have confidence, strength and independence in resolving their problems;
c. Medico-legal services are a form of medical service for the purposes of evidence in the field of law;
d. Legal services to assist Victims in undergoing the legal process; and e. Economic independence services in the form of skills training and providing economic access so that Victims can be independent.

Part Four

Mechanism for Assistance by Individuals and/or Institutions in Cooperation with P2TP2A

Article 24

(1) Assistance to Women and Children Victims of acts of Violence shall be carried out by individuals and/or institutions in cooperation with P2TP2A.

(2) Assistance as referred to in paragraph (1) shall be conducted according to SOPs.

(3) Further provisions regarding assistance as referred to in paragraph (2) shall be regulated by Regent Regulation.

Part Five

Service and Assistance

Article 25

The provision of Service and assistance to Victims shall be conducted with the principles of:

a. fast, safe, and empathetic;
b. the existence of confidentiality guarantees;
c. easily accessible; and
d. no fees charged.

Article 26

(1) Forms of Service provided to Women and Children Victims of Violence are as follows:

a. Complaint services;
b. Health services;
c. legal aid;
d. repatriation;
e. rehabilitation, social reintegration, and medico-legal;
f. Identification services; and
g. Psychological services.
(2) Forms of Service as referred to in paragraph (1) shall be implemented in accordance with minimum Service standards established by the government and carried out by Regional apparatus whose duties and functions are in the fields of:
a. social affairs;
b. health;
c. Women's Empowerment and Child Protection; and
d. mental and spiritual affairs.
(3) In carrying out the duties and functions as referred to in paragraph (2), the regional government shall cooperate with government agencies, provincial government, other district/city governments, the Community, Families and parents.
(4) Further provisions regarding the procedures for Service and handling of Women and Children Victims of Violence as referred to in paragraphs (1), (2), and (3) shall be regulated by Regent Regulation.

Part Six

Data and Information Management

Article 27

(1) The Regional Government through Regional apparatus overseeing Women and Children shall compile data and information regarding the protection of Women and Children from acts of Violence.

(2) Data and information as referred to in paragraph (1) include: a. Women and Children who are Victims of acts of Violence; b. fulfillment of the rights of Women and Children;

c. Women and Children at risk or vulnerable; and

d. Women and Children who require special protection.

(3) In managing data and information on Women and Children as referred to in paragraph (2), it is mandatory to:

a. develop a data and information system;
b. create disaggregated data;
c. conduct studies on the development of issues of Women and Children regarding acts of Violence; and
d. conduct periodic publication of data and information that is publicly accessible.
(4) As material for the completeness of data and information as referred to in paragraph (3), the Regional apparatus overseeing the protection of Women and Children shall cooperate and request data from related agencies.

Article 28

(1) Data and information as referred to in Article 27 shall become material for the formulation of policies, programs, budgets, monitoring and evaluation of the implementation of protection of Women and Children from acts of Violence.

(2) The formulation of policies, programs, budgets, monitoring and evaluation as referred to in paragraph (1) shall be carried out with the mechanism of: a. making mapping, long-term, medium-term and short-term plans; b. making agreements on the periodization of monthly and annual working meetings; c. making agreements on the collection, mapping and presentation of data between Regional apparatus organizations;

d. incorporating agreements into programs and budgets; and e. conducting scheduling of monitoring, evaluation and reporting.

Article 29

(1) Regional apparatus overseeing Women and Children shall formulate service policy regarding the protection of Women and Children from acts of Violence.

(2) The formulation of service policy as referred to in paragraph (1) shall be carried out with the mechanism of:

a. formulating case reporting policies related to Women and Children who are Victims of acts of Violence;
b. developing reporting and referral mechanisms between regional apparatus organizations overseeing the protection of Women and Children from acts of Violence with related agencies and with P2TP2A; and
c. preparing budgets for the implementation of policies for the protection of Women and Children from acts of Violence.

Part Seven

Fulfillment of Facilities and Infrastructure

Article 30

(1) Regional Government, Community and private sector provide facilities and infrastructure for the protection of Women and Children, including providing breastfeeding facilities for Children, child-friendly play areas, recreational and creative areas, Safe Houses, as well as establishing Integrated Service Centers for the Protection of Women and Children.

(2) Facilities and infrastructure as referred to in paragraph (1) must meet the following criteria:

a. ensure safety, comfort and health;
b. motivate Children's creativity; and
c. contain educational elements.
(3) The provision of facilities and infrastructure as referred to in paragraph (2) is for the fulfillment of minimum Service standards.
(4) The provision of facilities and infrastructure by the Regional Government is carried out in stages adjusted to the financial capacity of the Region.

Part Eight

Social Welfare

Article 31

(1) The fulfillment of social welfare of Women and Children aims to prevent, reduce vulnerability risks and handle Victims of acts of Violence.

(2) The fulfillment of social welfare of Women and Children as referred to in paragraph (1) is coordinated by regional apparatus that handles social issues.

Article 32

(1) The fulfillment of social welfare of Women and Children for prevention as referred to in Article 31 paragraph (1) includes:

a. enhancement of the Regional Government's role in the protection of Children including Children with special needs, Children with disabilities and other vulnerable Children;
b. raising Community awareness including Children regarding the protection and participation of Women and Children;
c. capacity building of the Community, Family, including Children and social welfare institutions, regarding Child protection;
d. capacity building of protection services for Women and Children;
e. guarantee of education continuity in accordance with the 12 (twelve) year Compulsory Basic Education program; and
f. guarantee of obtaining basic health Services.
(2) The fulfillment of social welfare of Women and Children for reducing vulnerability risks, includes:
a. identification and/or early detection of vulnerable Women and Children; b. mediation and counseling for vulnerable Women and Children;
c. services and facilitation of support for vulnerable Women and Children; and d. parenting support for Children who need alternative care.
(3) The fulfillment of social welfare of Women and Children for handling Victims, includes:
a. fulfillment of the rights of Women and Children who are Victims; b. referral services;
c. rehabilitation;and
d. reintegration.
(4) The handling of Child Victims as referred to in paragraph (3) is entitled to protection from local mass media coverage both electronic and print to ensure the Child's future.

Part Nine

Gender Mainstreaming

Article 33

To implement the protection of Women and Children, the Regional Government organizes Gender Mainstreaming which includes:

a. providing guidance for regional apparatus in formulating Gender integration strategies carried out through planning, preparation, implementation, budgeting, monitoring and evaluation of development program and activity policies.
b. developing Gender-based information systems;
c. facilitating institutional strengthening and development of Gender Mainstreaming mechanisms in government institutions, women's study centers, research and development institutions, as well as non-governmental institutions; d. implementing development, education, health, economy, law, Human Rights and politics, environment and social aspects that pay attention to Gender aspects and are child-friendly;
e. organizing protection of Women especially protection for Victims of Violence, female workers, Women Victims of human trafficking, elderly Women, and Women with disabilities, and disaster-affected areas; and
f. facilitating the strengthening and development of working networks of institutions or community organizations and the business sector for the implementation of Gender mainstreaming.

Article 34

(1) Every regional apparatus must conduct Gender-responsive budgeting planning.

(2) Gender-responsive budgeting planning as referred to in paragraph (1) is coordinated by the regional apparatus in the field of Women and Children.

Article 35

(1) The Regional Government may establish a Gender Mainstreaming Working Group.

(2) The Working Group as referred to in paragraph (1) consists of regional apparatus and related vertical agencies as well as non-governmental institutions, universities, business sectors and religious-based institutions as well as Communities working for the protection of Women and Children.

(3) The establishment of the Gender Mainstreaming Working Group as referred to in paragraph (2) is stipulated by Regent's Decree.

(4) Each member of the Working Group as referred to in paragraph (2) may appoint a Gender Mainstreaming Focal Point.

Article 36

(1) The duties of the Gender Mainstreaming Working Group as referred to in Article 35 paragraph (1), include:

a. facilitating the implementation of Gender Mainstreaming to regional apparatus, Sub-districts, Urban Villages and Villages;
b. preparing work programs in the framework of implementing Gender Mainstreaming;
c. developing working networks in accordance with duties and functions as well as authorities to encourage the implementation of Gender Mainstreaming; d. carrying out socialization, advocacy, coordination, and training on Gender Mainstreaming;
e. raising and discussing Gender issues that may cause gaps through Forum Group Discussion (FGD); f. collecting, formulating reports from SKPD, Sub-district Heads, Urban Village Heads, and Village Heads regarding the implementation of Gender Mainstreaming in each work unit; and
g. preparing and submitting reports on the implementation of Gender Mainstreaming to the Regent.
(2) Further provisions regarding the duties of the Gender Mainstreaming Working Group as referred to in paragraph (1) are regulated by Regent's Regulation.

Part Ten

Social Behavior Change That Favors Children

Article 37

(1) Social behavior change is carried out with the aim of changing ways of thinking, acting and behaving in accordance with parenting and education patterns that protect Children.

(2) Behavior change is intended to change policies, programs and budget allocations along with their action plans to change social behavior of individuals, groups, government and non-government institutions to implement Child protection.

(3) The Regional apparatus authorized to implement behavior change as regulated in paragraphs (1) and (2) are Regional apparatus that handle social affairs, religion, health, education, Community empowerment, population, employment, information and communication as well as Child protection under the coordination of Regional Apparatus Organization that handles the protection of Women and Children.

Article 38

Forms of behavioral change that can be carried out to implement Child Protection as referred to in Article 37 paragraph (2) include: a. developing Children according to their abilities, talents, and interests;

b. preventing marriage at child age;

c. caring for, maintaining, educating and protecting Children;

d. preventing brawls between students;

e. providing opportunities for mothers to provide exclusive breast milk for Children; f. instilling noble morals and respect for parents; g. instilling cultural identity, local language, love for the environment; and h. providing special protection to Children in emergency situations, Children in conflict with the law, Children from minority and isolated groups, Children exploited economically and/or sexually, Children who are trafficked, Children who are Victims of narcotics, alcohol, psychotropic, and other addictive substances (narcotics) abuse, Child Victims of kidnapping, sale and trafficking, Child Victims of Violence both physical and/or mental, Children with disabilities, and Child Victims of maltreatment and neglect, Child Victims of HIV/AIDS transmission and Child Victims of social conflict.

Part Eleven

Child-Friendly Regency Development Policy

Article 39

(1) Child-Friendly Regency Development aims to build Regional Government initiatives that lead to efforts to transform the concept of Child rights into policies, programs and activities to ensure the fulfillment of Child rights.

(2) Child-Friendly Regency Development Policy as referred to in paragraph (1) is implemented based on principles that include: a. good governance;

b. non-discrimination;

c. culture and local wisdom;

d. best interests of the Child; and

e. respect for the views of the Child;

f. inclusive; and

g. multicultural.

(3) Child-Friendly Regency Development Policy is directed at strengthening institutions for the fulfillment of Child rights and special protection, including: a. civil rights and freedoms;

b. right to Family environment and alternative care;

c. right to basic health and Child welfare;

d. right to education, use of leisure time and cultural activities; and e. right to special protection.

(4) Further provisions regarding Child-Friendly Regency Development as referred to in paragraph (1) are regulated by Regent Regulation.

CHAPTER VI

COORDINATION AND COOPERATION

Part One

Coordination

Article 40

(1) The Regent coordinates protection of Women and Children against acts of Violence with:

a. government;
b. regency/city regional government;
c. vertical agencies;
d. educational institutions; and
e. community institutions.
(2) Coordination as referred to in paragraph (1) includes:
a. religious affairs;
b. health affairs;
c. education affairs;
d. social affairs;
e. legal affairs;
f. manpower affairs; and
g. other affairs.
(3) Coordination as referred to in paragraph (2) is carried out by Regional Apparatus according to their authority.

Part Two

Cooperation

Article 41

(1) In certain cases the Regional Government may cooperate with: a. other regional governments;

b. police;

c. advocates;

d. health workers;

e. social workers;

f. Companion volunteers;

g. spiritual counselors;

h. other law enforcers, to assist Victims in the court trial process.

i. national commission on anti-Violence against Women;

j. Indonesian Child Protection Commission or Child Protection Institution; and/or

k. certain parties desired for the benefit of Victims.

(2) Cooperation as referred to in paragraph (1) may be in the form of: a. socialization;

b. provision of legal aid;

c. health Services;

d. Victim assistance;

e. counseling;

f. Social Rehabilitation;

g. spiritual guidance; and/or

h. repatriation and social reintegration.

CHAPTER VII

COMMUNITY AND BUSINESS PARTICIPATION

Article 42

(1) Everyone may participate in providing protection to Women and Children from acts of Violence.

(2) Participation as referred to in paragraph (1) may be carried out by: a. reporting acts of Violence against Women and/or Children; b. providing first aid to Victims;

c. independently socializing the rights of Women and Children; and/or d. forming independent Community social institutions.

Article 43

(1) Community social institutions as referred to in Article 42 paragraph (2) letter d, implementing protection of Women and Children against acts of Violence must coordinate with P2TP2A.

(2) The Regional Government encourages the business world to participate in the protection of Women and Children against acts of Violence.

(3) Participation as referred to in paragraph (1) may be in the form of: a. socialization to employees in the company environment regarding the dangers of acts of Violence against Women and Children; and

b. assistance as a form of corporate social responsibility.

CHAPTER VIII

DEVELOPMENT AND SUPERVISION

Article 44

(1) The Regional Government conducts development and supervision of the implementation of protection for Women and Child Victims of Violence.

(2) Development and supervision as referred to in paragraph (1) is carried out periodically by Regional Apparatus that have affairs in the empowerment of Women and Children against acts of Violence.

(3) Development as referred to in paragraph (1) includes:

a. guidelines and fulfillment standards;
b. technical guidance and training;
c. provision of facilities;
d. monitoring; and
e. evaluation.
(4) Supervision as referred to in paragraph (1) is carried out with the principles of: a. professional;
b. transparent; and
c. accountable.

Article 45

Development and supervision as referred to in Article 44, is carried out in order to realize the objectives of implementing protection for Women and Children from acts of Violence in accordance with minimum Service standards established by laws and regulations.

CHAPTER IX

REPORTING

Article 46

(1) Regional Apparatus that oversees the affairs of empowerment of Women and Children from acts of Violence reports the implementation of protection for Women and Child Victims of Violence to the Regent.

(2) Reporting as referred to in paragraph (1) is submitted in writing, including:

a. administration;
b. finance;
c. Services; and
d. performance.
(3) Submission of written reports as referred to in paragraph (2), is carried out at least once every 3 (three) months.

CHAPTER X

SOURCES OF FUNDS

Article 47

Funds for the implementation of protection of Women and Children from acts of Violence, are sourced from:

a. Regional Revenue and Expenditure Budget; and
b. other legitimate sources in accordance with the provisions of laws and regulations.

Article 48

(1) The Regional Government may provide financial assistance to Community organizations, social organizations, or Community non-governmental organizations and universities that implement protection for Women and Child Victims of Violence.

(2) Financial assistance as referred to in paragraph (1), is in accordance with the financial capacity of the Region and is implemented in accordance with the provisions of laws and regulations.

CHAPTER XI

CLOSING PROVISIONS

Article 49

This Regional Regulation shall come into force on the date of promulgation.

I. GENERAL

Violence against Women and Children constitutes a violation of Human Rights and therefore it is necessary to protect their dignity and honour and to guarantee their right to life without discrimination. Based on empirical evidence it is revealed that Women and Children are groups that are often victims of violence. Many factors cause violence against Women and Children to occur, among them is the patriarchal cultural factor that still occurs in society which views Women as lower than men. In addition, wrong perceptions about violence against Women and Children are also still frequently found, which regard violence as normal and as the right of the perpetrator. Violence against Women and Children can be seen from the type, perpetrator, place of occurrence, time, age and consequences of the act of violence. The violence against Women and Children mentioned above is general in nature and has no relevance to the type of education, occupation and income, social position, religion and belief, nationality, ethnicity and race attached to men and Women. This means that in all types of social strata, violence against Women and Children can and continues to occur as long as the inequality in the relationship between men and Women is still believed in and manifested in social life

| In order to prevent and overcome Violence against Women and Children in South Labuhanbatu Regency so that they are protected from Violence, | threats of Violence, torture or treatment that degrades human dignity and | honour, there is a need for a legal umbrella in the form of regulations | in the Region. |
|---|---|---|---|
| Thus far, laws and regulations governing the | protection of Women and Children from acts of Violence have not yet regulated protection efforts | in the Region so that institutional support and | regulations that can guarantee its implementation are needed. |
| The formulation of this Regional Regulation on Protection of Women and Children from | Acts of Violence is intended to provide a legal basis for the | Regional Government in providing protection for Women and Children in | the area of South Labuhanbatu Regency. |

Article 2

Point a What is meant by "the principle of respect for human rights" is that the implementation of protection for Women and Children victims of violence is to guarantee the fulfillment of the rights of victims of violence as a manifestation of respect for human rights.

Point b What is meant by "the right to life, survival, and development" is Rights as stipulated in the provisions of the 1945 Constitution.

Point e What is meant by "the principle of Gender equality and justice" is a process of equality and conditions for men and Women to obtain equal opportunities and rights as human beings, so as to be able to play a role and participate in political, economic, social and cultural activities, national defense and security and equality in enjoying the results of such development.

Point f What is meant by "the principle of protection of victims' Rights" is the protection of personal self, Family, honour, dignity, and property under their control, a sense of security and protection from threats of fear, freedom from torture or treatment that degrades human dignity.
