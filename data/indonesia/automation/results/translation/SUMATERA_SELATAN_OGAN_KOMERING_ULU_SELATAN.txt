the Regency Government;

Women and Children;

CHAPTER I

GENERAL PROVISIONS

Part One

Definitions, Meanings and Terms

Article 1

In this Regional Regulation, what is meant by:

1. Regency is the South Ogan Komering Ulu Regency.
2. Regency Government is the Government of South Ogan Komering Ulu Regency.
3. Regent is the Regent of South Ogan Komering Ulu Regency.
4. Regional Apparatus hereinafter abbreviated as PD is the Regional Apparatus within the Government of South Ogan Komering Ulu Regency.
5. Office of Population Control Family Planning Women's Empowerment and Child Protection hereinafter abbreviated as DPPKBPP-PA is the Regional Apparatus that administers the affairs of Women's Empowerment and Child Protection in South Ogan Komering Ulu.
6. Protection of Women is all efforts to guarantee and protect the rights of Women so that they can live healthily and participate optimally in accordance with human dignity and obtain protection from neglect, violence and discrimination, whether in the form of preventive, repressive or post-remedial protection (assistance and treatment).
7. Child Protection is all activities to guarantee and protect Children and their rights so that they can live, grow, develop, and participate optimally in accordance with human dignity, and receive protection from violence and discrimination.
8. Child is a person who has not reached the age of 18 (eighteen) years, including a Child who is still in the womb.
9. Child with Disability is a Child who has physical, mental, intellectual, or sensory limitations for a long period of time which, in interacting with the environment and community attitudes, may encounter obstacles that make it difficult to participate fully and effectively based on equal rights.
10. Child with Excellence is a Child who has extraordinary intelligence or has special potential and/or talent not limited to intellectual ability, but also in other fields.
11. Prevention is a direct effort made by the Regency Government, community and parents and/or family to prevent violations of Women's rights and Children's Rights.
12. Children's Forum is a forum for Children's participation formed with the aim of facilitating Children's involvement in the decision-making process and enjoying changes concerning their lives either directly or indirectly, which is carried out with the consent and will of all Children based on awareness and understanding, in accordance with age and level of maturity of thinking.
13. Child-Friendly Regency hereinafter abbreviated as KLA is a Regency that has a development system based on Children's rights through the integration of government commitments and resources, the community and the business world that are comprehensively and sustainably planned in policies, programs and activities to ensure the fulfillment of Children's Rights.
14. Neglected Child is a Child whose needs are not adequately met, whether physical, mental, spiritual, or social.
15. Woman victim of violence is a Woman who experiences actions that result in suffering or distress physically, sexually or psychologically, including threats of certain actions, coercion or arbitrary deprivation of liberty, whether occurring in the public sphere or in private life.
16. Child victim of violence is a Child who experiences actions that result in suffering or distress physically, mentally, sexually, psychologically or neglect that threatens life, body and degrades human dignity.
17. Human trafficking is the act of recruitment, transportation, harboring, sending, transfer, or receipt of a person by threat of violence, use of violence, abduction, confinement, forgery, fraud, abuse of power or vulnerable position, debt bondage or giving payment or benefits, so as to obtain consent from the person holding control over that other person, whether carried out within the State or between States, for the purpose of exploitation or resulting in the person being exploited.
18. Complaint handling is a series of actions carried out by integrated service providers to follow up on reports of acts of violence against Women and Children, submitted by victims, families or the community.
19. Social rehabilitation is a service aimed at restoring and developing the ability of a person experiencing social dysfunction so that they can perform their social functions normally.
20. Law enforcement is the action of officers authorized by the state to implement laws and regulations.
21. Legal aid is legal services provided by legal assistants and advocates to conduct the process of assisting witnesses and/or victims of violence against Women and Children that is gender-sensitive.
22. Repatriation is an effort to return Women and Child victims of violence from abroad to the point of debarkation/entry point, or from the receiving area to the area of origin.
23. Social reintegration is an effort to reunite victims with their family, substitute family, or community that can provide protection and meet the needs of the victim.
24. Safe house is a temporary shelter that aims to provide protection and a sense of security to victims of violence in accordance with established standards.
25. Shelter home is a forum for coaching and welfare services for street Children that carries out social assistance/guidance activities, maintenance, care, education, and health, skills guidance in order to ensure that Children do not engage in activities on the streets so that they can grow and develop normally.
26. Spiritual guidance is an effort to restore the psyche through strengthening the religion adhered to by the victim. 27. Gender responsive is consistent and systematic attention to the differences between Women and men in society accompanied by efforts to eliminate structural and cultural barriers in achieving gender equality.
28. Gender Sensitivity or Gender Awareness is the ability to identify gaps in power relations between Women and men within the family and within the community, the impact of the division of labor based on gender on Women and men, and that the experiences, problems, needs, interests and aspirations of Women and men are different.
29. Public service provider is every state administrative institution, corporation, independent institution established under the Law for public service activities, and other legal entities established solely for public service activities.
30. Community Organization is an organization founded and formed by the community voluntarily based on shared aspirations, will, needs, interests, activities and objectives to participate in development for the achievement of the goals of the Unitary State of the Republic of Indonesia based on Pancasila.
31. Parents are biological father and/or mother, or stepfather and/or stepmother, or adoptive father and/or adoptive mother. 32. Family is the smallest unit in society consisting of husband and wife or husband, wife and their Child, or father and his Child, or mother and her Child, or blood relatives in direct ascending or descending line up to the third degree. 33. Integrated Service Center for Women's Empowerment and Child, hereinafter abbreviated as P2TP2A is an integrated service center in the effort to empower Women in various fields of development, as well as the protection of Women and Children from various types of discrimination and acts of violence, including human trafficking, which is established by the government or community-based.
34. Human Immunodeficiency Virus hereinafter abbreviated as HIV is a virus that attacks the immune system and if not treated can reduce the human immune system until the condition of Acquired Immuno Deficiency Syndrome (AIDS) occurs.

Part Two

Principles, Objectives and Scope

Article 2

(1) The implementation of Protection of Women is based on the principles of:

a. the best interests of Women;
b. gender justice and gender equality;
c. local wisdom; and
d. legal certainty.
(2) The implementation of Protection of Children is based on the principles of: a. non-discrimination;
b. the best interests of the Child;
c. the right to life, survival, and development;
d. respect for the views of the Child; and e. legal certainty.

Article 3

The regulation of Protection of Women and Children aims to: a. serve as a guideline for the Regency Government in planning, policies and strategies for the Protection of Women and Children;

b. protect Women and Children from actions, decisions and policies that violate the rights of Women and Children;

c. prevent the occurrence of violations of the Rights of Women and Children;

d. enhance the empowerment of Women and Children;

e. enhance the role and values of local wisdom in the Protection of Women and Children; and

f. enhance the role of Regional Apparatus and non-

Government entities in the Regency in efforts for the Protection of Women and Children.

Article 4

The scope of regulation of the Protection of Women and Children includes:

a. prevention;
b. implementation of Protection of the Rights of Women and Children;
c. handling;
d. empowerment; and
e. guidance and supervision.

CHAPTER II

RIGHTS OF WOMEN AND RIGHTS OF CHILDREN

Part One

Rights of Women

Article 5

Every Woman has the right to:

a. obtain protection from acts of discrimination; b. obtain protection from economic exploitation and sexual exploitation;
c. obtain protection from physical, psychological, sexual violence, and neglect;
d. obtain decent work in accordance with her capabilities;
e. obtain work leave while receiving wages/salary in accordance with laws and regulations; f. obtain education and instruction in all types, levels and pathways of education;
g. obtain special protection in the performance of work or profession against matters that may threaten her safety and/or health in connection with the reproductive function of Women;
h. have the right to choose, be chosen, be appointed in employment, positions, and professions in accordance with requirements and laws and regulations; and
i. ownership and management of joint property in marriage in accordance with the provisions of Laws and Regulations.

Part Two

Rights of the Child

Article 6

(1) Every Child has the right:

a. every child has the right to protection by parents, family, community, and the state;
b. the rights of the child are human rights and for their benefit the rights of the child are recognized and protected by law even from the womb;
c. every child from the womb has the right to live, survive, and improve their standard of living;
d. every child from birth has the right to a name and citizenship status;
e. every child with physical and/or mental disabilities has the right to receive care, education, training, and special assistance at state expense, to ensure their life in accordance with human dignity, increase self-confidence, and the ability to participate in community, national, and state life;
f. every child has the right to worship according to their religion, think, and express themselves in accordance with their intellectual level and age under the guidance of parents and/or guardians;
g. every child has the right to know who their parents are, to be raised, and cared for by their own parents;
h. in the event that the child's parents are unable to raise and care for the child properly, then the child may be cared for or adopted as a child by another person in accordance with the provisions of legislation;
i. every child has the right to be raised, cared for, nurtured, educated, directed, and guided in their life by their parents or guardians until adulthood in accordance with the provisions of legislation;
j. every child has the right to obtain adoptive parents or a guardian based on a court decision if both parents have died or due to a valid reason are unable to carry out their obligations as parents;
k. adoptive parents or guardians as referred to in letter j must carry out the obligations of actual parents;
l. every child has the right to receive legal protection from all forms of physical or mental violence, neglect, maltreatment, and sexual abuse while in the care of parents or guardians, or other parties responsible for the care of the child;
m. in the event that parents, guardians, or caregivers of the child commit any form of physical or mental abuse, neglect, maltreatment, and sexual abuse including rape, and/or murder of the child who should be protected, they must be subject to aggravated punishment;
n. every child has the right not to be separated from their parents against the child's own wishes, unless there are legal reasons and regulations that show that the separation is in the best interests of the child;
o. in the circumstances as referred to in letter n, the child's right to continue to meet directly and maintain personal contact regularly with their parents is still guaranteed by law;
p. every child has the right to obtain education and teaching in the context of personal development in accordance with their interests, talents, and level of intelligence;
q. every child has the right to seek, receive, and provide information in accordance with their intellectual level and age for their self-development as long as it is in accordance with moral values and propriety;
r. every child has the right to rest, associate with children of the same age, play, recreate, and create in accordance with their interests, talents, and level of intelligence for their self-development;
s. every child has the right to obtain health services and social security appropriately, in accordance with their physical and spiritual mental needs;
t. every child has the right not to be involved in war events, armed conflicts, social unrest, and other events containing elements of violence;
u. every child has the right to obtain protection from economic exploitation activities and any work that endangers them, so that it can disrupt their education, physical health, morals, social life, and spiritual mentality;
v. every child has the right to obtain protection from exploitation and sexual abuse, kidnapping, child trafficking, as well as from various forms of narcotics, psychotropics, and other addictive substances abuse;
w. every child has the right not to be subjected to persecution, torture, or imposition of inhuman punishment;
x. every child has the right not to be deprived of their freedom unlawfully;
y. arrest, detention, or imprisonment of a child may only be carried out in accordance with applicable law and can only be implemented as a last resort;
z. every child deprived of their freedom has the right to receive humane treatment and with attention to the needs of personal development according to their age and must be separated from adults, except in their best interests; aa. every child deprived of their freedom has the right to obtain legal assistance or other assistance effectively at every stage of applicable legal proceedings; bb. every child deprived of their freedom has the right to defend themselves and obtain justice before an objective and impartial Juvenile Court in a hearing closed to the public.
(2) Every child who is a victim or perpetrator of sexual violence or who is in conflict with the law has the right to confidentiality.
(3) Every Child with Disabilities in addition to receiving the rights as referred to in paragraph (1) is also entitled to obtain:
a. special education/special education; b. rehabilitation;
c. social assistance; and
d. maintenance of social welfare standards.
(4) Every Child who has excellence has the right to receive special education.

CHAPTER III

OBLIGATIONS AND RESPONSIBILITIES

Part One

Kabupaten Government

Article 7

(1) The Kabupaten Government in efforts to protect Women is obligated and responsible to: a. guarantee Women's Rights without discrimination in accordance with the provisions of Legislation; b. establish and implement policy programs and activities for the protection of Women; c. cooperate with the community and/or Community Organizations in the implementation of Women's Protection in accordance with the provisions of Legislation;

d. carry out rehabilitation and empowerment of Women who are victims of violence; e. provide special protection to Women victims of violence and human trafficking; and

f. take preventive action in order to prevent violations of Women's rights.

(2) The responsibility of the Kabupaten Government in efforts to protect Women's Rights as referred to in paragraph (1) is implemented by DPPKBP-PPPA.

Article 8

(1) The Regency Government in its efforts to protect Children is obliged and responsible to: a. respect the fulfillment of Children's rights without distinguishing ethnicity, religion, race, class, gender, ethnic group, culture and language, legal status, birth order, and physical and/or mental condition; b. provide support for facilities, infrastructure, and availability of human resources in the administration of Child Protection;

c. guarantee the protection, maintenance, and welfare of Children with due regard to the rights and obligations of Parents, Guardians, or other persons legally responsible for Children;

d. guarantee Children to express their right to express opinions in accordance with the age and intelligence level of Children;

e. establish and implement policies and programs/activities for the protection of Children's rights; f. cooperate with the community and/or Community Organizations in administering Child Protection in accordance with the provisions of Laws and Regulations;

g. conduct rehabilitation for Children who are victims of acts of violence;

h. provide special protection to Children: 1. in emergency situations;

2. in conflict with the law;

3. from minority and isolated groups;

4. who are exploited economically and/or sexually;

5. who are victims of narcotics, alcohol, psychotropic, and other addictive substance abuse; 6. who are victims of pornography;

7. with HIV/AIDS;

8. victims of abduction, sale, and/or trafficking;

9. victims of physical and/or psychological violence;

10. victims of sexual crimes;

11. victims of terrorist networks;

12. persons with disabilities;

13. victims of mistreatment and neglect; 14. with deviant social behavior; and

15. who are victims of stigmatization from labeling related to their parents' condition.

i. organize inclusive education for Children with special needs;

j. take preventive measures to prevent violations against Children; and

k. implement and support National policies in the administration of Child Protection in the Regency conducted through efforts to realize Child-Friendly Regency, Child-Friendly District and Child-Friendly Village.

(2) The responsibility of the Regency Government in efforts to protect Children as referred to in paragraph (1) is implemented by DPPKBP-PPPA.

Article 9

(1) The Regency Government in order to carry out its responsibilities, establishes programs and activities for the protection of Women and Children with a Regional Action Plan as a basis for Regional Apparatus in implementing the protection of Women and Children.

(2) Further provisions regarding the Regional Action Plan as referred to in paragraph (1) are regulated by Regent Regulation.

Part Two

Community

Article 10

(1) The community is obliged and responsible in administering the protection of Women and Children including:

a. participation in prevention and providing support for the development of programs to prevent violations of the rights of Women and Children;
b. providing information and/or reporting violations of the rights of Women and Children to the competent authorities; and
c. monitoring the law enforcement process for cases of violations of the rights of Women and Children. (2) Community responsibility as referred to in paragraph (1) is in accordance with the provisions of laws and regulations and the values of local wisdom of the local community.

Part Three

Parents and/or Family

Paragraph 1

Responsibility for the Protection of Women's Rights Article 11 Family responsibilities in efforts to protect women include:

a. protecting women from conflict and domestic violence that degrades the dignity of women;
b. providing equal opportunities for women to develop themselves in the fields of:
1. education;
2. social;
3. economy; and
4. politics and culture.
c. preventing violations of women's rights in the household.

Paragraph 2

Responsibility for the Protection of Children's Rights Article 12

(1) Parents in efforts to protect Children are responsible for:

a. raising, maintaining, educating, and protecting Children;
b. developing Children in accordance with their abilities, talents, and interests;
c. providing formal and non-formal education; d. guiding Children to behave in accordance with religion, ethics and morals as well as customs; and e. preventing marriage at Child age. (2) In the event that parents do not exist, or their whereabouts are unknown, or for some reason, are unable to carry out their obligations and responsibilities, then the obligations and responsibilities as referred to in paragraph (1) may be transferred to the family.

CHAPTER IV

PREVENTION

Part One

Regency Government

Article 13

(1) Efforts made by the Regency Government to prevent violations of Women's rights include:

a. conducting socialization on the prevention of violence against Women by optimizing the role of customary, cultural, religious institutions and community organizations;
b. providing guidelines on handling acts of violence against Women;
c. involving community participation in recovery and reintegration;
d. developing an integrated database and information system on acts of violence against Women;
e. developing family empowerment programs; f. strengthening the function of community organizations working in the field of Women's empowerment; and g. providing comfortable and safe public facilities for Women.
(2) Efforts made by the Regency Government to prevent violations of Children's rights include:
a. preventing acts of violence against Children by optimizing the role of customary, cultural, religious institutions and community organizations; b. developing child-friendly schools;
c. encouraging increased child participation through the establishment of Child Forums;
d. providing guidelines on handling acts of violence against Children;
e. involving community participation in recovery services and reintegration of child victims of violence; f. developing an integrated database and information system on acts of violence against Children;
g. strengthening the function of community organizations working in the field of Child protection; and
h. encouraging the establishment of Child-Friendly Regency.

Part Two

Community

Article 14

(1) Efforts made by the community to prevent violations of Women's rights include:

a. monitoring various conditions occurring in their environment that may cause violations of Women's rights;
b. strengthening the role of community organizations, religious organizations and family environment; and
c. preventing unregistered marriage/siri marriage.
(2) Efforts made to prevent violations of Children's rights include: a. monitoring various conditions occurring in their environment that cause violations of Children's rights;
b. strengthening the role of community organizations, religious organizations and family environment; and
c. preventing marriage at Child age.

Part Three

Parents and/or Family

Article 15

(1) Efforts made by the family to prevent violations of Women's rights include:

a. protecting women from various violations of rights in the household; and
b. encouraging the formation of harmonious, educative and democratic families;
(2) Efforts made by parents to prevent violations of Children's rights include:
a. providing good examples to children in accordance with religious teachings and customs;
b. supervising children in activities inside or outside the home;
c. providing opportunities for children to engage in dialogue to express opinions democratically; and d. protecting children from various acts of violation of Children's rights in the household.

CHAPTER V

ADMINISTRATION OF PROTECTION

OF WOMEN AND CHILDREN

Part One

Administration of Women's Protection

Paragraph 1

General

Article 16

The Regency Government organizes the Protection of Women by involving:

a. family;
b. community leaders;
c. religious leaders;
d. teachers and/or academics;
e. society; and
f. community organizations in the field of Women's protection.

Paragraph 2

Marriage

Article 17

During marriage or after divorce, a Woman has the same rights as the man who is her husband/ex-husband over all matters related to the consequences of marriage and/or divorce.

Paragraph 3

Health

Article 18

(1) The Regency Government provides adequate health and maternity facilities for pregnant and nursing mothers.

(2) Public service providers are required to provide special facilities for nursing mothers.

Paragraph 4

Employment

Article 19

(1) Employers who employ Women are required to maintain decency, safety and security in the workplace.

(2) Employers are required to place Women workers in places that are safe for Women's reproductive health.

(3) Employers are required to fulfill the rights of Women workers to receive wages in accordance with the provisions of laws and regulations.

(4) Employers provide special facilities for nursing mothers in their companies.

Article 20

(1) Women workers/laborers who are at most 18 (eighteen) years old are prohibited from being employed between 23.00 WIB and 07.00 WIB.

(2) Employers are prohibited from employing pregnant Women workers/laborers who, according to a doctor's statement, are dangerous to the health and safety of their pregnancy or themselves during working hours from 23.00 WIB to 07.00 WIB.

(3) Employers who employ Women workers/laborers between 23.00 WIB and 07.00 WIB, are required to:

a. provide nutritious food and drinks; and b. maintain decency and security in the workplace.
(4) Employers are required to provide shuttle transportation for Women workers/laborers who depart and return from work between 23.00 WIB and 05.00 WIB.

Paragraph 5

Education

Article 21

Women are guaranteed the right to obtain education at every level of formal and non-formal education.

Paragraph 6

Social

Article 22

(1) The Regency Government organizes the empowerment of Women who experience socio-economic vulnerability through productive economic business activities.

(2) Community Organizations may carry out empowerment for Women who experience socio-economic vulnerability as referred to in paragraph (1) in accordance with the provisions of Laws and Regulations.

Part Two

Organization of Child Rights Protection

Paragraph 1

Health

Article 23

(1) The Regency Government's efforts to provide Child protection in the health sector include: a. free health services for Children from poor families;

b. organizing optimal health for Children to obtain a good degree of health from the womb;

c. ensuring that babies receive exclusive breastfeeding from their mothers properly and naturally;

d. ensuring Children receive adequate nutrition; e. ensuring that newborn Children are screened; and

f. ensuring the provision of monitoring the growth and development of toddlers and pre-school age children.

(2) The Regency Government, family, and parents protect children from attempts to transplant their body organs to other parties.

Paragraph 2

Education

Article 24

Children in formal and non-formal educational institutions must be protected from acts of violence.

Article 25

Every Child aged 7 (seven) to 18 (eighteen) years who has not completed education, exceeding 3 (three) years beyond the age limit of formal education may pursue education through non-formal education units:

a. Equivalency program package A equivalent to Elementary School and Madrasah Ibtidaiyah;
b. Equivalency program package B equivalent to Junior High School and Madrasah Tsanawiyah; and
c. Equivalency program package C equivalent to Senior High School, Madrasah Aliyah and Vocational High School.

Article 26

(1) Children with special needs and/or who have potential intelligence and/or special talents are given equal opportunities and facilities to obtain regular education as well as inclusive education.

(2) Inclusive education as referred to in paragraph (1) is organized by the Regency Government and the community.

Article 27

Every organizer of formal education is prohibited from expelling a Child without a guarantee for the continuity of the Child's education.

Article 28

(1) The Regency Government provides scholarships and/or educational assistance for:

a. children from poor families;
b. neglected children; and
c. children residing in remote areas. (2) The implementation of scholarship programs and/or educational assistance as referred to in paragraph (1) is supported by the participation of the community and community organizations.
(3) Further provisions regarding the provision of scholarships and or educational assistance as referred to in paragraph (1) are regulated by Regent Regulation.

Article 29

Children in conflict with the law, Children who are victims of violence and sexual abuse and Children who are victims of HIV/AIDS transmission are protected in their right to obtain education.

Article 30

The Regency Government and the community organize Early Childhood Education (PAUD).

Paragraph 3

Employment

Article 31

Employers are prohibited from employing Children.

Article 32

(1) The provisions as referred to in Article 31 may be exempted for Children aged between 13 (thirteen) years to 15 (fifteen) years, to perform light work as long as it does not interfere with physical, mental, and social development and health.

(2) Employers who employ Children in light work as referred to in paragraph (1) must meet the requirements including:

a. written permission from parents or guardians;
b. work agreement between the employer and the parents or guardians;
c. maximum working time of 3 (three) hours;
d. carried out during the day and does not interfere with school time;
e. occupational safety and health;
f. clear employment relationship; and
g. receive wages in accordance with the provisions of laws and regulations.
(3) The provisions as referred to in paragraph (2) letters a, b, f, and g are exempted for Children who work in their family business.

Article 33

In the event that a company employs Children as referred to in Article 32, then the workplace for Children must be separated from the workplace for adult workers.

Article 34

(1) Children may perform work in the workplace that is part of an educational or training curriculum approved by the authorized official.

(2) Children as referred to in paragraph (1) are at least 14 (fourteen) years old.

Article 35

(1) Every person is prohibited from employing and involving Children in the worst forms of work.

(2) The worst forms of work as referred to in paragraph (1) include:

a. all work in the form of slavery or similar practices;
b. all work that exploits, provides, or offers Children for: 1. prostitution;
2. production of pornography;
3. pornographic performances;
4. gambling;
5. production and trade of alcoholic beverages; 6. narcotics;
7. psychotropics; and
8. other addictive substances; and/or
c. all work that endangers the health, safety, or morals of Children.

Paragraph 4

Social

Article 36

(1) The Regency Government is obliged to provide care for neglected Children.

(2) The care as referred to in paragraph (1) may be carried out by Community Organizations in the social field.

(3) In the event that a Child is neglected because for some reason the parents neglect their obligations, the Family or Regency Government or Community Organization as referred to in paragraph (2) may submit an application to the court to designate the Child as a neglected Child.

(4) The court decision as referred to in paragraph (3) simultaneously determines the place of shelter and care for the neglected Child concerned.

Article 37

(1) The Regency Government organizes skills training for street Children and neglected Children. (2) The skills training as referred to in paragraph (1) is organized by Regional Apparatus whose duties and functions are in the social and manpower fields and may cooperate with Community Organizations.

Article 38

(1) The Regency Government provides shelters to foster street Children and social homes for neglected Children.

(2) The shelter as referred to in paragraph (1) may be organized in cooperation with Community Organizations.

(3) Further provisions regarding the shelter as referred to in paragraph (1) shall be regulated by Regent Regulation.

Article 39

The implementation of protection for Women and Children is carried out by Regional Apparatus whose duties and functions relate to the protection of Women and Children.

CHAPTER VI

HANDLING

Part One

Handling of Women and Children Victims of Violence Article 40

The implementation of handling Women and Children victims of violence includes:

a. handling of complaints from victims of violence; b. health services;
c. social rehabilitation;
d. law enforcement and legal aid; and
e. repatriation and social reintegration.

Article 41

(1) The implementation of handling Women and Children victims of violence as referred to in Article 40 is carried out through integrated services.

(2) Integrated services for Women and Children victims of violence as referred to in paragraph (1) use the principles of:

a. gender responsive;
b. non-discrimination;
c. equal and respectful relations;
d. maintaining confidentiality;
e. providing a sense of safety and comfort;
f. respecting individual differences;
g. non-judgmental;
h. respecting the choices and decisions of victims; i. being sensitive to the background and conditions of victims and the use of appropriate language understood by victims;
j. fast and simple;
k. empathy;
l. fulfillment of the rights of Women and Children; and m. free of charge.

Article 42

Complaint handling services for victims of violence against Women and Children are carried out by: a. hospitals;

b. community health centers;

c. P2TP2A;

d. Women and Children Service Units (UPPA); e. Trauma Center Protection Houses (RPTC); f. Child Social Protection Houses (RPSA);

g. Women Crisis Centre (WCC)

h. Marriage Advisory, Guidance and Preservation Board (BP4);

i. Police;

j. Prosecutor's Office;

k. Court;

l. Legal Aid Institute (LBH);

m. Child Protection Institute (LPA);

n. Social Welfare Protection Institute; and o. Shelters.

Article 43

(1) Complaints of acts of violence against Women and Children as referred to in Article 42 may be made by reporters consisting of:

a. Victims;
b. Family;
c. Community; and/or
d. community organizations in the field of protection of Women and Children.
(2) Reporters as referred to in paragraph (1) may make complaints:
a. directly;
b. by telephone; and
c. by letter.
(3) In addition to handling complaints submitted by reporters, officers at service institutions as referred to in Article 42 are authorized to handle Women and Children victims of violence obtained through other information.
(4) Further provisions regarding the procedures for complaint handling services as referred to in paragraph (1) and paragraph (2) shall be regulated by Regional Regulation.

Part Two

Health Services for Women and Children Victims of Violence

Article 44

(1) Health services for Women and Children victims of violence include the aspects of:

a. Promotive;
b. Preventive;
c. Curative;
d. rehabilitative.
(2) Health services for Women and Children victims of violence are carried out:
a. Comprehensively;
b. accessible 24 (twenty-four) hours;
c. with quality; and
d. in accordance with health service standards. (3) Special health services for Children victims of violence must prioritize the best interests of the Child.
(4) Women and Children victims of violence are entitled to health services, including:
a. medical services;
b. medicolegal services; and
c. psychosocial counseling.
(5) Further provisions regarding the procedures for implementing health services as referred to in paragraph (4) shall be regulated by Regional Regulation.

Part Three

Social Rehabilitation Services for Women and Children Victims of Violence

Article 45

(1) Social rehabilitation services for Women and Children victims of violence are aimed at restoring and developing the capacity of victims experiencing social dysfunction to be able to carry out their social functions in society properly.

(2) Social rehabilitation services as referred to in paragraph (1) include:

a. counseling services;
b. provision of safe houses;
c. spiritual guidance.
(3) The provision of services as referred to in paragraph (2) is organized by:
a. Regional Apparatus having duties and functions in the social field;
b. DPPKBPP-PA;
c. P2TP2A;
d. Women Crisis Centre (WCC);
e. community organizations in the social field; and/ or
f. religious institutions.

Article 46

(1) Counseling services as referred to in Article 45 paragraph (2) letter a aim to:

a. help victims recognize their problems and find effective ways to overcome them themselves;
b. empower victims to determine their future;
c. provide moral support for victims in facing the processes they undergo; and
d. make victims feel accepted in the community environment.
(2) Counseling services are carried out by gender-responsive social rehabilitation officers, consisting of: a. counselors;
b. psychologists; and
c. psychiatrists.
(3) Counseling services are carried out in a place that guarantees a sense of safety, comfort and confidentiality of information from victims.
(4) The stages of counseling services include:
a. initial counseling;
b. follow-up counseling;
c. case conference; and d. home visit.

Article 47

(1) The Regency Government may provide safe houses for Women and Children victims of violence. (2) Safe houses as referred to in paragraph (1)

aim to protect Women and Children victims of violence from intimidation by perpetrators or other parties.

(3) The location of safe houses as referred to in paragraph (1) must be kept confidential.

(4) The criteria for Women and Children victims of violence requiring safe houses include:

a. life threatened;
b. receiving rejection from family or community;
c. requiring intensive services but their residence is relatively far away; and/or
d. neglected if not placed in a safe house.
(5) The provision of safe house services is carried out by social rehabilitation officers who have gender sensitivity or gender awareness, consisting of:
a. social workers/volunteers;
b. security officers; and
c. community leaders.
(6) Further provisions regarding the provision of safe house services shall be regulated by Regional Regulation.

Article 48

(1) Every Woman and Child victim of violence is provided spiritual guidance aimed at psychological recovery.

(2) Spiritual guidance as referred to in paragraph (1) is carried out based on the principles:

a. not imposing any religion or belief; and
b. leaving the final decision in the hands of the victim (self determination).
(3) Spiritual guidance as referred to in paragraph (2) is carried out by spiritual guidance officers.
(4) Spiritual guidance officers as referred to in paragraph (3) consist of:
a. consultants of the Advisory, Development and Preservation of Marriage Agency (BP4);
b. Officers of the Religious Affairs Office (KUA);
c. Ustads or Islamic scholars;
d. Priests;
e. Ministers; and
f. other religious leaders who have gender sensitivity or gender awareness.

Part Four

Law Enforcement Services and Legal Assistance

Article 49

(1) Law enforcement services and legal assistance are actions of law enforcement officials provided to Women and Children victims of violence and/or witnesses.

(2) Law enforcement services and legal assistance as referred to in paragraph (1) are carried out by prioritizing the best interests of the victim.

Article 50

(1) Law enforcement services and legal assistance as referred to in Article 49 are aimed at providing accompaniment and protection to Women and Children victims of violence and witnesses with a gender-responsive approach so that victims obtain justice and legal certainty of rights.

(2) Types of legal assistance services as referred to in paragraph (1) include:

a. legal assistance in the reporting process and preparation of the official report;
b. legal assistance in the prosecution process at the prosecutor's office;
c. legal assistance in the examination process at the court;
d. legal assistance in the court decision process until there is a final legal decision; and
e. legal assistance in the mediation process.
(3) The Regency Government provides legal assistance to Children and Women victims of violence free of charge.
(4) In providing legal assistance as referred to in paragraph (3), the Regency Government may cooperate with:
a. Legal Aid Institutions (LBH);
b. Women Crisis Center (WCC);
c. Advocates; and/or
d. other legal assistants who are gender sensitive.

Part Five

Repatriation Services and Social Reintegration Article 51

(1) Repatriation of victims of violence is aimed at returning Women and Children victims of violence to their area of origin.

(2) Repatriation of Women and Children victims of violence as referred to in paragraph (1) is accompanied by social reintegration efforts.

(3) The Regency Government carries out repatriation and social reintegration of Women and Children victims of violence in accordance with the provisions of Laws and Regulations.

CHAPTER VII

WOMEN EMPOWERMENT

Article 52

(1) The Regency Government guarantees the implementation of empowerment for Women.

(2) The implementation of Women empowerment as referred to in paragraph (1) is supported by community participation and Community Organizations.

Article 53

(1) The Regency Government provides skills training for capacity building of Women. (2) In providing training as referred to in paragraph (1), the Regency Government may cooperate with training institutions organized by community organizations. (3) The Regency Government expands access and opportunities for Women in obtaining skills and employment.

Article 54

(1) The Regency Government facilitates:

a. provision of business capital assistance for Women with weak economies;
b. marketing of products from Women's businesses; c. organization of community capacity development training through economic empowerment of pre-prosperous families;
d. expansion of access, information and provision of knowledge to Women regarding product marketing efforts; and
e. provision of comprehensive entrepreneurship training to Women.
(2) The Regency Government in facilitating activities as referred to in paragraph (1) cooperates with the community and/or community organizations.

CHAPTER VIll

CHILD PARTICIPATION

Article 55

(1) Every Child has the right to participate in expressing opinions, ideas, and thoughts related to life based on awareness and understanding, in accordance with age and level of maturity of thinking.

(2) Child participation as referred to in paragraph (1) may be carried out through Child Forums starting from the village level, sub-district within the regency.

(3) Child Forums as referred to in paragraph (2) represent Children, both geographic domicile representation, socio-cultural groups and educational background of Children.

(4) The establishment of Child Forums as referred to in paragraph (2) is facilitated by the Regency Government. (5) The establishment of Child Forums as referred to in paragraph (4) is determined by Regent Decree.

Article 56

(1) In every formulation of policies related to Children, the Regency Government pays attention to and accommodates Children's Opinions conveyed through Child Forums.

(2) The Regency Government includes representatives of Child Forums in the Regency Development Planning Consultation.

CHAPTER IX

CHILD-FRIENDLY REGENCY

Article 57

(1) The Regency Government conducts guidance to the Sub-District Government in the development of Child-Friendly Regency.

(2) Guidance as referred to in paragraph (1) includes:

a. coordination;
b. facilitation;
c. guidance;
d. supervision;
e. consultation; and
f. education and training.
(3) Provisions regarding Child-Friendly Regency are further regulated by Regional Regulation.

CHAPTER X

GUIDANCE AND SUPERVISION

Article 58

(1) The Regent conducts guidance and supervision of the implementation of Protection of Women and Children. (2) Guidance as referred to in paragraph (1) may be carried out in the form of:

a. training for government officials and law enforcers on prevention and handling of violence;
b. training for religious leaders, community leaders, customary leaders, educators, journalists and media managers in order to prevent violence against Women and Children;
c. training for health workers who provide health services to Women and Children victims of violence at community health centers, integrated service units, and general hospitals;
d. training for social rehabilitation officers and spiritual guidance officers in order to protect Women and Children victims of violence; and
e. enhancement of professionalism of officers, managers of violence case data against Women and Children at community health centers, integrated service units and hospitals.
(3) The Regent in conducting guidance and supervision as referred to in paragraph (1) may delegate to the Head of DPPKBPP-PA.

Article 59

(1) The Head of DPPKBPP-PA is obligated to submit a report on the implementation of the Women and Children protection program to the Regent.

(2) The report as referred to in paragraph (1) is submitted periodically 1 (one) time in 3 (three) months.

Article 60

(1) The Regent conducts supervision and evaluation of the implementation of Protection of Women and Children periodically 1 (one) time in 3 (three) months.

(2) The evaluation results as referred to in paragraph (1) constitute evaluation material for Women and Children protection program policies in the Regency.

CHAPTER XI

FINANCING

Article 61

Financing required for the implementation of Child-Friendly Regency activities is charged to:

a. Regency Regional Revenue and Expenditure Budget; b. State Revenue and Expenditure Budget; and c. other legal and non-binding sources.

CHAPTER XII

CLOSING PROVISIONS

Article 62

This Regent Regulation shall come into force on the date of promulgation.

In order that everyone may know of it, orders the promulgation of this Regional Regulation by placing it in the Regional Gazette of Ogan Komering Ulu Selatan Regency.
