# Law No. 2019-008

Law No. 2019-008 on Combating Gender-Based Violence

STATEMENT OF REASONS

By ratifying international human rights instruments, the Malagasy State has committed itself to combating Gender-Based Violence.

Through the Constitution, Madagascar has adopted sacrosanct principles such as respect for and protection of fundamental freedoms and rights, the establishment of a rule of law and the elimination of all forms of injustice, corruption, inequality and discrimination.

However, despite the efforts undertaken, Gender-Based Violence remains a Malagasy reality. Indeed, the issue of gender remains a problem in our society in which social roles concerning the feminine and masculine are institutionalized, and entrenched in mentalities and culture. This situation generates inequalities and even violence mainly to the detriment of women.

Furthermore, it must be noted that Madagascar has no specific text enabling it to effectively combat Gender-Based Violence. It is in this context that it appeared important to develop a specific law on the matter.

This law strengthens existing repressive provisions and introduces innovations in the prevention and care of victims. A participatory approach was adopted throughout the process of drafting this law, which was also examined within the Penal System Reform commission.

This law establishes a national mechanism to combat Gender-Based Violence. It further punishes new categories of offences such as marital rape, harmful traditional practices, sexist insult and economic violence, and establishes a victim protection system.

It comprises twenty-two articles subdivided into four Titles:

- Title I on "General Provisions" comprises two Chapters on Scope of Application (Chapter I) and on definitions (Chapter II);
- Title II concerning repression comprises ten articles (Articles 3 to 12);
- Title III on prevention, protection and care is broken down into eight articles (Articles 13 to 20);
- Title IV on final provisions comprises two articles (Articles 21 and 22).

Such is the purpose of this law.

The National Assembly and the Senate have adopted in their respective plenary sessions, the law whose content follows:

## TITLE I

GENERAL PROVISIONS

### Chapter One: Scope of Application

#### Article 1: Without prejudice to the provisions of the Criminal Code and the

Code of Criminal Procedure, the purpose of this law is to strengthen the legal regime for the prevention, prosecution, repression of acts of Gender-Based Violence, care and reparation as well as the protection of victims.

### Chapter II: Definitions

#### Article 2: For the purposes of this law, the terms:

1° "Gender" means the roles, behaviors, activities and attributions that a given society considers at a given time as appropriate for men and women.

2° "Gender-Based Violence means any act of violence directed against a person by reason of their sex, and causing or likely to cause harm or physical, sexual or psychological suffering whether in public life or in private life.

Gender-Based Violence is understood to encompass, without being limited to, the forms of violence listed below:

- physical, sexual, psychological and economic violence exercised within the family, including beatings, sexual abuse inflicted on children, harmful traditional practices to both sexes, violence within the couple, and violence related to exploitation;
- physical, sexual, psychological and economic violence exercised within society, including sexual abuse, sexual harassment, procuring and forced prostitution;
- physical, sexual, psychological and economic violence perpetrated or tolerated by the State, wherever it occurs, 3° Union is understood as the bond between a man and a woman who are married or who behave as such.

4° Victim: Any person who has suffered an act of violence or abuse or mistreatment.

5° Harmful traditional practices: means any act derived from customs and traditions that violates human rights.

6° Unnatural sexual act: Any sexual act committed on the person of another, contrary to good morals and the natural order of things.

## TITLE II: ON REPRESSION

#### Article 3: Forced sterilization is the act of committing on a man or

a woman, without their free consent or without a justified medical decision, any act of a nature to deprive him or her of their biological reproductive capacities. It is punished by one to five years' imprisonment and a fine of one hundred thousand Ariary to five hundred thousand Ariary (100,000 Ariary to 500,000 Ariary).

#### Article 4: Any act of intimidation; threat of reprisals or

reprisals against victims of Gender-Based Violence as well as members of their family, witnesses and denouncers aimed at obstructing care or criminal prosecution, constitutes an offence punishable by a sentence of six months to two years' imprisonment and a fine of one hundred thousand Ariary to five hundred thousand Ariary (100,000 Ariary to 500,000 Ariary).

#### Article 5: Any individual who commits an act derived from customs and traditions that

violates the physical integrity of a child or a woman is punished by six months to five years' imprisonment and a fine of one hundred thousand Ariary to one million Ariary (100,000 Ariary to 1,000,000 Ariary).

#### Article 6: Any act of sexual penetration, of whatever nature,

committed on the spouse or on the person engaged in a union, by violence, coercion, threat is an offence punished by two to five years' imprisonment and a fine of one hundred thousand Ariary to one million Ariary

(100,000 Ariary to 1,000,000 Ariary).

#### Article 7: Unnatural sexual practice on the person of another, by

violence, coercion or threat is punished by two to five years' imprisonment and a fine of two million Ariary to four million Ariary (2,000,000 Ariary to 4,000,000 Ariary).

#### Article 8: Any individual who gives an order, uses words, gestures,

writings, messages, and this repeatedly, utters threats, composes constraints, or uses any other means for the purpose of obtaining, from a person, favors of any nature whatsoever, including sexual, for their benefit or a third party against the will of the harassed person, shall be punished by one to three years' imprisonment and a fine of one hundred thousand Ariary to one million Ariary

(100,000 Ariary to 1,000,000 Ariary).

When the act is committed by two or more persons with or without collusion against a single person, the penalty shall be two to five years' imprisonment and a fine of two hundred thousand Ariary to two million Ariary (200,000 Ariary to 2,000,000 Ariary).

#### Article 9: Any person who perpetrates acts and/or utters

words resulting in a degradation of the psychological, mental or physical health of the victim is punished by one to three years' imprisonment and/or a fine of two hundred thousand Ariary to two million Ariary (200,000 Ariary to 2,000,0000 Ariary).

#### Article 10: Any act consisting of depriving a person of their fundamental

freedoms and/or isolating them from the outside world outside of any legal provisions or judicial decision, shall be punished by six months to five years imprisonment and a fine of one hundred thousand Ariary to one million Ariary (100,000 Ariary to 1,000,000 Ariary).

#### Article 11: Any statement made or any act with sexist connotations against

a person that violates their dignity due to its degrading or humiliating nature or creates an intimidating, hostile or offensive situation against them, constitutes sexist insult.

Sexist insult is punished by a fine of one hundred thousand Ariary to five hundred thousand Ariary (100,000 Ariary to 500,000 Ariary).

#### Article 12: Anyone who has deprived or restricted their spouse or the person

engaged with them in a union, of their rights related to access to financial resources is punished by a fine of one hundred thousand Ariary to five hundred thousand Ariary

(100,000 Ariary to 500,000 Ariary).

## TITLE III: PREVENTION,

PROTECTION AND SUPPORT

#### Article 13: The State formulates and implements the policy

to combat Gender-Based Violence. It mobilizes the necessary resources in this matter.

A national mechanism to combat Gender-Based Violence ensures the coordination, management and monitoring of actions. The implementation of this mechanism is established by regulation.

#### Article 14: The State ensures health care, psychosocial support and

legal-judicial assistance to victims to assert their rights.

#### Article 15: Any person, including family members,

neighbors, friends, local authorities, religious dignitaries, social workers, medical personnel, having knowledge of a case of gender-based violence, must report it to the competent administrative or judicial authorities under penalty of the sanctions provided for in article 62 para. 1 of the Penal Code.

#### Article 16: The report may be made verbally or in writing.

The author of the report may remain anonymous if they wish.

The authority seized must record the declaration of report and follow up on it. It has the obligation to transmit it to the Judicial Police and/or to the competent Court as soon as possible.

#### Article 17: The President of the competent Court, at the request of the victim or

their legal representative, after communication to the Public Prosecutor, may issue a protection order. They may in particular:

- without prejudice to the right of Misintaka, authorize the victim to temporarily leave the common or conjugal home and prohibit the alleged perpetrator from approaching the victim;
- authorize the concealment of the victim's home and the election of domicile.

The order determines the duration and scope of the measure.

It is subject to appeal in accordance with the provisions of article 235 of the Code of Civil Procedure.

#### Article 18: The summary judge may at any time and after opinion of the Public

Prosecutor increase, restrict the scope of the prescribed measures or terminate them, at the request of one of the parties in case of occurrence of new facts.

#### Article 19: The trial relating to a case of Gender-Based Violence may

be held in camera in accordance with the provisions of the Code of Criminal Procedure.

The decision related thereto is pronounced in public hearing.

#### Article 20: The authorities having received the report or any other person

providing support to the victim are bound by the obligation of confidentiality. Failure to comply with this obligation is punishable by the penalties provided for in article 378 of the Penal Code.

## TITLE IV: FINAL PROVISIONS

#### Article 21: Regulatory texts shall be adopted as necessary for

the application of certain provisions of this law.

#### Article 22: This law shall be published in the Official Journal of the Republic.

It shall be executed as law of the State.
