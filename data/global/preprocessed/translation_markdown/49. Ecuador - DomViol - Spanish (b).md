WOMEN

STATEMENT OF REASONS

Violence against women affects all women in the country and in the world. Violence manifests itself through the existence of power relations between men and women, in which the supremacy of the masculine devalues the feminine and establishes forms of control expressed in different types of violence. In many societies it is a practice that is naturalized in social relations, which does not distinguish age, ethnic or racial belonging, socioeconomic condition, physical condition, comprehensive health status, migratory status, or sex-gender identity. Historically, women have fought against gender violence exercised upon them solely because they are women. In Latin America and the Caribbean, violence against women was considered a private matter, in which the State should not interfere and the magnitude of the problem barely transcended, therefore it was not considered an issue to be addressed at the level of regulations and public policy; Ecuador was no exception.

Through the action of women's struggle, in the 1980s in Ecuador, violence began to be made visible as a public health problem and was given treatment at the political level. Visibility became more pronounced when Ecuador signed the Convention for the Elimination of All Forms of Discrimination against Women in July 1980 and ratified it in November 1981. Later, Ecuador adhered to the Inter-American Convention to Prevent, Punish and Eradicate Violence Against Women of Belém do Para (January 1995) and subscribed to the Beijing Platform for Action (1995).

Against this background, in 1994 the Women's Commissariats were created and in 1995 the "Law against Violence against Women and the Family" known as Law 103 was issued, through which the State assumed a role through the Justice system. This regulation recognized intrafamily violence as a problem that transcended private life to the public sphere, as well as the existence of three types of violence: physical, psychological and sexual. It also established various protective measures and civil sanctions, aimed at preventing acts of violence experienced by women.

In 1994, the Law on Free Maternity Care and Child Care was promulgated, which gave rise to the formation of User Committees as a mechanism for citizen participation, to promote citizen co-responsibility in the care and promotion of women's health. A few years later, the struggle of the Ecuadorian women's movement achieved another important milestone, which was the consolidation of a Public Institution to guarantee Equality between Men and Women" and through it to make visible the structural gender inequality gaps, in which historically we women have lived, and thus be able to consolidate public policies that allow us to overcome them. This is how in 1997 the National Women's Council - CONAMU was created.

In that same year, the Law on Labor Protection for Women was approved, which established the obligation to designate women in at least 20% to be integrated into the Superior Courts of Justice, Courts, Notaries and Registries.

The participation and mobilization of Ecuadorian women that was generated from the V United Nations Conference on Women, in Beijing 1995, and the approval of the Platform for Action, strengthened the political role of women in promoting their political rights and their participation in decision-making. Thus, in the process of creating the 1998 Constitution, it was possible to incorporate fundamental provisions for the promotion and strengthening of gender equality and women's human rights. Among the most important milestones achieved were: a) The right to personal integrity and to a life free of violence, to equality before the law and non-discrimination; b) Equitable participation of women and men in the political sphere; c) The right of women to make free and responsible decisions about their sexual and reproductive life; d) Formal recognition of domestic work as productive labor; e) Equality and co-responsibility in the family and support for female heads of household, f) Non-discriminatory education that promotes gender equity; and, g) The State's obligation to approve public policies to promote women's equality.

A decade later, prior to the 2008 Constituent Assembly process, under the criteria of parity, sequence and alternation of women and men in the formation of electoral lists, significant participation of women was achieved in the construction of Ecuador's new Constitution and important conquests were achieved, among others: a) Maintaining all women's rights conquered in the 1998 Constitution; b) Secular State that implies the right to freedom of conscience and to make decisions; c) The right to real or material equality; d) Right to decide (make free and responsible decisions about their body, health, sexual life and reproduction); e) Parity of women and men; f) Special protection for victims of sexual crimes and gender violence, no revictimization; g) Eradication of sexism and machismo, and discriminatory practices; h) Prohibition of access to public office for those who owe alimony or are responsible for sexual crimes or gender violence; i) Reconciliation of productive work with reproductive work. Finally, today we have a Constitution that guarantees women's fundamental rights.

In the area of Work and Economy, important milestones were achieved, such as salary regulation, and it was established that the State would promote the incorporation of women into paid work, under conditions of equal rights and opportunities, guaranteeing identical remuneration for equal work between men and women. Within the Ecuadorian Labor Code, a section is established regarding women's work after pregnancy, with some guidelines for their protection and non-discrimination.

The voice of women's organizations has revealed chilling figures such as those presented in the National Survey on Family Relations and Gender Violence against Women (2011) which demonstrates that, in Ecuador, 6 out of every 10 women had been victims of violence, that is, more than 3.2 million women have suffered some type of violence because they are women. On the other hand, 1 out of every 4 women has experienced sexual violence; however, psychological violence is the most recurrent form of gender violence. In 76% of cases of gender violence against women, the aggressor has been their partner or ex-partner. From the statistics shown, the need to have a Unified Registry that compiles qualitative and quantitative data permanently updated on the situation of violence against women and that reveals the dimension and magnitude of the problem is inferred.

A new advance occurred when the Comprehensive Organic Criminal Code in 2014 classified these three types of violence and included femicide as a crime. Physical injuries, mutilations and other consequences resulting from violence entail high social, family, economic and personal costs, causing the victim and the family nucleus low self-esteem; falling into poverty; psychological problems, which generate not only expenses for medical care; days of abandonment of work; disabilities, decreased intellectual and physical performance; appearance of physical or mental illnesses, with the most serious consequence: death. These individual and family costs evidently also affect the State, which must permanently invest in redressing the effects of this public health problem, and prevention of violence against women and its eradication constitutes the best measure.

According to the constitutional provision, the State must consider the necessary measures to prevent, eliminate and punish all forms of violence, especially that exercised against women; however, the measures adopted have been insufficient in institutional action to prevent, protect and care for women victims of violence. It is necessary to have a new Law that articulates a National System for the Prevention and Eradication of Gender Violence against Women, which will coordinate, plan, organize and execute comprehensive and complementary actions to link all public powers and make effective women's right to a life free of violence.

This Law provides in particular to focus State action on awareness-raising and prevention of violence and with citizen participation, under the principle of co-responsibility. These two actors must guarantee, through policies, plans and programs, the transformation of sociocultural patterns and the eradication of practices that naturalize violence against women. This Law also establishes three components for the eradication of violence: care, protection and reparation of women victims of violence to guarantee their safety and integrity and to resume their life project.

This Law is organic in character because its provisions will prevail over other norms; it regulates the exercise of constitutional rights and guarantees since it develops principles regarding women's human rights, and incorporates provisions from international treaties. For this purpose, the legislation of Spain, Mexico, Peru, Uruguay and Colombia have been taken as references. Based on these normative contributions and responding to the proposals of different women's organizations, protective measures have been expanded within this legal body.

The National Assembly of Ecuador unanimously, on July 11, 2017, resolved to categorically condemn all types of violence exercised against girls, adolescents and women of Ecuador; to urge the institutions of the Judicial Function to strengthen care services for women victims of violence through permanent training and capacity building of their staff; and the increase of specialized care units for gender violence. Likewise, to persuade the Executive Function to strengthen its plans, programs and actions in favor of the eradication of all forms of violence against girls, adolescents and women, with special emphasis on the education sector; as well as to request that society in general become a fundamental actor in the process of transforming sociocultural patterns that maintain discrimination and violence against women. The National Assembly assumes the commitment to enact laws that contemplate efficient and effective mechanisms for alerting violence against women. For all these reasons, we propose this Draft Comprehensive Organic Law to Prevent and Eradicate Violence against Women, as a response to a demand from citizens and especially from women.

Violence is everyone's concern.

## TITULO I

GENERAL PROVISIONS

### CAPITULO I

OF THE OBJECT, PURPOSE AND SCOPE OF APPLICATION OF THE LAW

#### Art. 1.

Object. The object of this Law is to prevent and eradicate all types of violence against women: girls, adolescents, young women, adults and older adults, in all their diversity, in public and private spheres; especially when they are in multiple situations of vulnerability or risk, through comprehensive policies and actions of prevention, care, protection and reparation of victims; as well as through the re-education of the aggressor and work on masculinities.

Priority and specialized attention shall be given to girls and adolescents, within the framework of the provisions of the Constitution of the Republic and international instruments ratified by the Ecuadorian State..

#### Art. 2.

Purpose. This Law aims to prevent and eradicate violence exercised against women, through the transformation of sociocultural patterns and stereotypes that naturalize, reproduce, perpetuate and sustain inequality between men and women, as well as to care for, protect and provide reparation to victims of violence..

#### Art. 3.

Scope. This law shall be applicable and observed by all natural and legal persons who are or act in Ecuadorian territory.

Ecuadorian women in situations of human mobility who are abroad shall be subjects of protection and assistance of the diplomatic missions or consular offices of Ecuador, whatever their migratory status..

#### Art. 4.

Definitions. For purposes of application of this Law, the following terms are defined:

- 1. Gender-based violence against women.- Any action or conduct based on their gender that causes or does not cause death, harm and/or physical, sexual, psychological, economic or patrimonial, gyneco-obstetric suffering to women, both in the public and private spheres.
- 2. Harm.- Is the damage caused to a person as a consequence of a specific event. In this case, harm implies the injury, impairment, diminishment, grievance, of a right of the victim.
- 3. Gender stereotypes.- Is any preconception of attributes and characteristics possessed or roles that are or should be performed by men and women, respectively.
- 4. Victims.- Women and/or other members of the family nucleus who suffer violence or harm executed by a member of the family are considered.
- 5. Aggressor.- One who commits an action or omission that implies any form of violence against women.
- 6. Public sphere.- Space in which the political, productive tasks of society and remunerated services are carried out, linked to the management of the public.
- 7. Private sphere.- Space in which reproductive tasks are carried out; of the care economy, remunerated or not, linked to family and domestic matters.
- 8. Power relations.- Actions, omissions and social, political, economic, cultural or symbolic practices that determine the imposition of the will of a person or group over that of another, from a relationship of domination or subordination, which implies the asymmetric distribution of power and access to and control of material and immaterial resources between men and women.
- 9. Discrimination against women.- Denotes any distinction, exclusion or restriction based on their condition as such, which has the purpose or result of diminishing or nullifying the recognition of women, attacking human rights and fundamental freedoms in the political, economic, social, cultural spheres, or in any other.
- 10. Revictimization.- Are new aggressions, intentional or not, that the victim suffers during the various phases of care and protection, as well as during judicial or extrajudicial proceedings, such as: unjustified delay in proceedings, lack of protection, denial and/or unjustified lack of effective attention, among other delayed, inadequate or non-existent responses, by competent state institutions.
- 11. Single Registry of violence against women.- Is a georeferenced registry of violence against women that will record data on sex, age, ethnic self-identification, sex-gender condition, level of education, migratory status, marital status of the victim and the aggressor, the type of violence, the existence of previous complaints, sentence and other additional data that respond to international human rights standards.
- 12. Guiding Expenditure Classifier.- Is a technological tool developed by the governing body of public finances, which seeks to link the activities and budgets of institutional programs with components of equality policies (gender, disabilities, interculturality, human mobility and generational). This tool verifies to what extent these components are being incorporated into the budget of public institutions and facilitates the monitoring of budget execution by each entity.
- 13. Masculinities.- Is the sociocultural construction about roles and values associated with the behavior of men. It advocates that they be exercised without machismo or supremacy or violence towards women..

#### Art. 5.

State obligations. The State, through all levels of government, has the unavoidable obligations to promote, protect, guarantee and respect the human rights of women: girls, adolescents, adults and older adults, through the adoption of all political, legislative, judicial, administrative, control and any other measures that are necessary, timely and adequate to ensure compliance with this Law and avoid revictimization and impunity.

These state obligations shall be included in the National Development Plan and in the Development Plans: regional, provincial, metropolitan districts, cantonal and parochial; and, they shall be guaranteed through a specific action plan included in the General State Budget..

#### Art. 6.

Co-responsibility. The State is responsible for guaranteeing the right of women: girls, adolescents, adult women and older women, to a life free of violence. Society, the family and the community are responsible for participating in the actions, plans and programs for the eradication of violence against women, undertaken by the State at all its levels and for intervening in the formulation, evaluation, and social control of public policies that are created for this purpose..

#### Art. 7.

Approaches. In the application of this Law, the following approaches shall be considered:

- a) Gender approach.- Allows understanding the social and cultural construction of roles between men and women, which historically have been a source of inequity, violence and violation of rights and which must be modified in favor of roles and social practices that guarantee full equality of opportunities between diverse persons and a life free of violence.
- b) Human rights approach.- Determines as objective and result, the recognition, unrestricted respect and full realization of the human rights of all persons, including the right to integrity and to a life free of violence.
- c) Interculturality approach.- Recognizes the existence of the different communities, peoples and nationalities that make up the State, respecting all those expressions in the diverse cultural contexts. Under this approach, discriminatory practices that favor violence are not accepted.
- d) Intergenerational approach.- Recognizes the existence of specific needs and rights at each stage of life, childhood, adolescence, maturity and adulthood; and, establishes the priority of identifying and addressing vulnerabilities in said stages of life.
- e) Comprehensive approach.- Considers that violence against women: girls, adolescents, young women, adults and older adults is structural and multicausal and is present in all spheres of life, therefore, interventions must be carried out in all spaces in which women develop.
- f) Intersectionality approach.- Identifies and values the social, economic, political, cultural, religious, ethnic, geographic, physical and other conditions that are simultaneously part of the individual and community identity of women and adapts to these realities the actions, services and public policies intended for the prevention and eradication of violence against women and the care, protection and restitution of rights of the victim..

#### Art. 8.

Guiding principles. For purposes of the application of this Law, in addition to the principles contemplated in the Constitution of the Republic, in the international human rights instruments ratified by Ecuador, and other applicable regulations, the following shall govern:

- a) Equality and non-discrimination.- Equality is guaranteed and all forms of discrimination are prohibited.

No woman may be discriminated against, nor may her rights be diminished, in accordance with the Constitution of the Republic, international instruments and other applicable regulations.

b) Diversity.- The diversity of women is recognized, regardless of their age and condition, in accordance with what is established in the Constitution of the Republic, Organic Law on Identity Management and Civil Data and applicable criminal regulations.

c) Empowerment.- Empowerment is recognized as the set of actions and tools provided to women to guarantee the full exercise of their rights.

It also refers to the process by which women regain control over their lives, which implies among other aspects, the increase of self-confidence, the expansion of opportunities, greater access to resources, control over them and decision-making.

d) Mainstreaming.- The various approaches established in this Law are respected, at all levels and throughout the cycle of public and private management and of society in general, and guarantees comprehensive treatment of the issue of violence.

e) Pro-person.- The most favorable interpretation shall be applied for the effective validity and protection of their rights for the protection and guarantee of rights of women victims or in potential situation of violence.

f) Progressive realization.- Applies to the positive obligations that the State has to progressively satisfy and protect the rights considered in this Law.

g) Autonomy.- The freedom that a woman has to make her own decisions in the different spheres of her life is recognized..

#### Art. 9.

Rights of women. Women: girls, adolescents, young women, adults and older adults, in all their diversity, have the right to the recognition, enjoyment, exercise and protection of all human rights and freedoms contemplated in the Constitution of the Republic, the international instruments ratified by the State and in current regulations, which includes, among others, the following:

- 1. To a life free from violence in the public and private spheres, that favors their development and well-being;
- 2. To respect for their dignity, integrity, privacy, autonomy and not to be subjected to any form of discrimination or torture;
- 3. To receive, in a context of interculturality, an education based on principles of equality and equity;
- 4. To receive clear, accessible, complete, truthful, timely information, in Spanish or in their own language, appropriate to their age and socio-cultural context, in relation to their rights, including their sexual and reproductive health; to know the protection mechanisms; the location of provision of care, emergency, support and comprehensive recovery services; and other procedures contemplated in this Law and other concordant regulations;
- 5. To have interpretation, language adaptation and augmentative communication, as well as additional support adjusted to their needs, that allow guaranteeing their rights, when they have a disability condition;
- 6. To be guaranteed the confidentiality and privacy of their personal data, that of their descendants or that of any other person who is under their custody or care;
- 7. To receive comprehensive protection and care through adequate and effective services, in an immediate and free manner for the victim and their dependents with sufficient, accessible and quality coverage;
- 8. To receive guidance, counseling, legal representation or consular assistance, in a free, immediate, specialized and comprehensive manner on the various matters and processes that their situation requires;
- 9. To give their informed consent for medical-legal examinations that are performed in cases of sexual violence and, whenever possible, to choose the sex of the professional for the performance thereof;
- 10. To be heard in all cases personally by the competent administrative or judicial authority, and to have their opinion considered at the time of making a decision that affects them. Special attention shall be given to the age of the victims, to the context of violence and intimidation in which they may find themselves.
- 11. To receive sensitive treatment, avoiding revictimization, taking into account their age, their disability situation or other conditions or circumstances that require special attention;
- 12. Not to be confronted, neither themselves nor their family units with the aggressors. The imposition of alternative methods of conflict resolution in care, protection or criminal processes is prohibited;
- 13. To the truth, to justice, to comprehensive reparation and to guarantees of non-repetition in the face of acts constituting violence, before the competent administrative and judicial bodies;
- 14. To have their labor rights recognized, to guarantee salary equality between men and women, without any discrimination and to prevent them from having to abandon their workplace due to causes of violence.
- 15. To the immediate assistance of the public force at the moment the victims request it;
- 16. To have equal opportunities in access to public functions and to participate in public affairs, including decision-making;
- 17. To communication and advertising without sexism, violence and discrimination;
- 18. To safe and protected housing. Women victims of gender-based violence constitute a group with the right to preferential protection in access to housing;
- 19. To have their permanence or general working conditions respected, as well as their specific labor rights, such as those related to maternity and lactation;
- 20. To receive protection against situations of threat, intimidation or humiliation;
- 21. Not to be exploited and to receive adequate protection in case of denial of the labor benefits to which they are entitled by law;
- 22. Not to be dismissed or subjected to labor sanctions for absence from work or disability, due to their status as a victim of violence; and,
- 23. The others established in the current legal system..

#### Art. 10.

Types of violence. For purposes of application of this Law and without prejudice to what is established in international human rights instruments and in the Comprehensive Organic Criminal Code and the Law, the following types of violence are considered:

- a) Physical violence.- Any act or omission that produces or could produce harm or physical suffering, pain or death, as well as any other form of mistreatment or aggression, corporal punishment, that affects physical integrity, causing or not causing injuries, whether internal, external or both, this as a result of the use of force or of any object used with the intention of causing harm and its consequences, without consideration of the time required for recovery.
- b) Psychological violence.- Any action, omission or pattern of conduct aimed at causing emotional harm, diminishing self-esteem, affecting honor, provoking discredit, belittling personal dignity, disturbing, degrading cultural identity, expressions of youth identity or controlling the conduct, behavior, beliefs or decisions of a woman, through humiliation, intimidation, confinement, isolation, forced treatments or any other act that affects her psychological and emotional stability.

Psychological violence includes emotional manipulation, control through surveillance mechanisms, harassment or bullying, all abusive conduct and especially behaviors, words, acts, gestures, writings or electronic messages aimed at pursuing, intimidating, blackmailing and monitoring the woman, regardless of her age or condition and that may affect her emotional stability, dignity, prestige, physical or mental integrity; or, that may have negative repercussions with respect to her employment, in the continuation of school or university studies, in promotion, recognition in the workplace or outside of it. It also includes threats, verbal announcements or acts, that result in physical, psychological, sexual, labor or patrimonial harm, with the purpose of intimidating the subject of protection of this Law.

c) Sexual violence.- Any action that implies the violation or restriction of the right to sexual integrity and to decide voluntarily about her sexual and reproductive life, through threats, coercion, use of force and intimidation, including rape within marriage or other relational and kinship relations, whether or not there is cohabitation, the intentional transmission of sexually transmitted infections (STIs), as well as forced prostitution, trafficking for purposes of sexual exploitation, abuse or sexual harassment, forced sterilization and other analogous practices. The involvement of girls and adolescents in sexual activities with an adult or with any other person who is in a position of advantage over them is also sexual violence, whether due to their age, due to reasons of their greater physical or mental development, due to the relationship of kinship, affection or trust that binds them to the girl or adolescent, due to their position of authority or power; early pregnancy in girls and adolescents, early age marriage, female genital mutilation and the use of the image of girls and adolescents in pornography.

d) Economic and patrimonial violence.- It is any action or omission aimed at causing impairment of the economic and patrimonial resources of women, including those of the conjugal partnership and the property partnership of de facto unions, through:

- 1. The disturbance of the possession, tenure or ownership of their movable or immovable property;
- 2. The loss, removal, destruction, retention or misappropriation of objects, work instruments, personal documents, goods, securities and patrimonial rights;
- 3. The limitation of economic resources intended to satisfy their needs or the deprivation of the indispensable means to live a dignified life; as well as the evasion of compliance with their maintenance obligations;
- 4. The limitation or control of their income; and,
- 5. Receiving a lower salary for equal work, within the same workplace.
  - e) Symbolic violence.- It is all conduct that, through the production or reproduction of messages, values, symbols, icons, signs and impositions of gender, social, economic, political, cultural and religious beliefs, transmits, reproduces and consolidates relations of domination, exclusion, inequality and discrimination, naturalizing the subordination of women.
  - f) Political violence.- It is that violence committed by a person or group of persons, directly or indirectly, against women who are candidates, militants, elected, designated or who hold public office, human rights defenders, feminists, political or social leaders, or against their family. This violence is aimed at shortening, suspending, impeding or restricting their action or the exercise of their position, or to induce or force them to carry out an action against their will or incur an omission, in the fulfillment of their functions, including lack of access to public goods or other resources for the adequate fulfillment of their functions.
  - g) Gynecological-obstetric violence.- It is considered any action or omission that limits the right of pregnant or non-pregnant women to receive gynecological-obstetric health services. It is expressed through mistreatment, the imposition of non-consented cultural and scientific practices or the violation of professional secrecy, the abuse of medicalization, and that not established in protocols, guidelines or norms; actions that consider the natural processes of pregnancy, childbirth and postpartum as pathologies, forced sterilization, the loss of autonomy and capacity to freely decide about their bodies and their sexuality, negatively impacting the quality of life and sexual and reproductive health of women in all their diversity and throughout their life, when this is carried out with invasive practices or physical or psychological mistreatment..

#### Art. 11.

Concurrence of violence. The different types of violence against women provided for in this Law may occur against the same person, simultaneously, in the same context and in one or several areas..

#### Art. 12.

Areas where violence against women occurs. These are the different spaces and contexts in which the types of gender-based violence against women occur: girls, adolescents, young women, adults and older adults. They include, among others, the following:

- 1. Intrafamily or domestic.- Includes the context in which violence is exercised within the family nucleus. Violence is perpetrated by the spouse, the partner in a de facto union, the cohabitant, ascendants, descendants, sisters, brothers, relatives by consanguinity and affinity, and persons with whom the victim maintains or has maintained family, intimate, affective, conjugal, cohabitation, courtship or cohabitation ties;
- 2. Educational.- Includes the teaching and learning context in which violence is perpetrated by teachers, administrative staff, classmates or other members of the educational community at all levels;
- 3. Workplace.- Includes the work context where the right to work is exercised and where productive activities are carried out, in which violence is perpetrated by persons who have a work relationship or coexistence with the victim, regardless of the hierarchical relationship.

It includes conditioning hiring or permanence in employment through favors of a sexual nature; the refusal to hire the victim or to respect her permanence or general working conditions; public discrediting of the work performed and lack of access to equal remuneration for equal task or function, as well as the impediment to women to have the period of pregnancy and lactation credited to them;

4. Sports.- Includes the public or private context in which violence is exercised in formative, high-performance, professional, adapted/Paralympic, amateur, school or social sports practice;

5. State and institutional.- Includes the context in which violence is perpetrated in the exercise of state authority, expressly or tacitly, and which translates into actions or omissions, originating from the State. It includes any action or omission by institutions, legal persons, public servants or personnel of private institutions; and, any type of collective or organization, which, failing to comply with their responsibilities in the exercise of their functions, delay, obstruct or prevent women from having access to public policies and their derived services; and, from exercising the rights provided for in this Law;

6. Centers of Deprivation of Liberty.- Includes the context where violence is exercised in centers of deprivation of liberty, by personnel working in the centers;

7. Media and cyber.- Includes the context in which violence is exercised through public, private or community media, whether by traditional means or by any information technology, including social networks, virtual platforms or any other;

8. In public or community space.- Includes the context in which violence is exercised individually or collectively in public places or spaces, private spaces with public access; neighborhood or community coexistence spaces; public transportation and other common-use spaces, both rural and urban, through any physical, verbal or sexual connotation action not consented to, that affects the safety and integrity of women, girls and adolescents;

9. Health centers and institutions.- Includes the context where violence is exercised in public and private health centers, against users of the National Health System, perpetrated by administrative staff, assistants and health professionals; and,

10. Emergencies and humanitarian situations.- Includes the context where violence is exercised in emergency situations and disasters that promote inequalities between men and women, that put at risk the physical, psychological and sexual integrity of women: girls, adolescents, young women, adults and older adults..

## TITULO II

COMPREHENSIVE NATIONAL SYSTEM TO PREVENT AND ERADICATE VIOLENCE AGAINST

WOMEN

### CAPITULO I

GENERAL PROVISIONS OF THE SYSTEM

#### Art. 13.- DEFINITION OF THE COMPREHENSIVE NATIONAL SYSTEM TO PREVENT AND ERADICATE

VIOLENCE AGAINST WOMEN.- The Comprehensive National System to Prevent and Eradicate Violence against Women is the organized and articulated set of institutions, norms, policies, plans, programs, mechanisms and activities aimed at preventing and eradicating violence against women, through prevention, care, protection and comprehensive reparation of the rights of victims.

The System shall be organized in an articulated manner at the national level, within the framework of deconcentration and decentralization processes for adequate provision of services in the territory. Citizen participation shall be guaranteed, as well as mechanisms for transparency and accountability to citizens..

#### Art. 14.

Purpose of the System. The purpose of the System is to prevent and eradicate violence against women through the design, formulation, execution, supervision, monitoring and evaluation of norms, policies, programs, mechanisms and actions, at all instances and at all levels of government, in an articulated and coordinated manner..

#### Art. 15.

Principles of the System. The Comprehensive National System for the Prevention and Eradication of Violence against women: girls, adolescents, young women, adults and older adults, is supported, among others, by the following principles:

- 1. Non-criminalization.- The authorities, in accordance with the provisions of the legal system, shall not treat the surviving victim as a suspect or responsible for the commission of the acts she reports.
- 2. Non-revictimization.- No woman shall be subjected to new aggressions, intentional or not, during the various phases of care, protection and reparation, such as: unjustified delay in processes, denial or unjustified lack of effective care, among other late, inadequate responses by public and private institutions. Women shall not be revictimized by any person involved in prevention, care, protection or reparation processes.
- 3. Confidentiality.- No one may publicly use information, personal background or the judicial past of the victim to hold her responsible for the violation of her rights. Stigmatization, prejudice and subjective considerations are prohibited. Confidentiality must be maintained regarding matters submitted to their knowledge. Women, in consideration of their own interest, may make their case public. This principle shall not prevent public servants from reporting acts of violence of which they become aware, nor shall it prevent the generation of disaggregated statistics and information.
- 4. Gratuity.- All actions, mechanisms, procedures and any other derived proceedings, carried out by comprehensive public entities of the System and recognized by this Law, shall be free of charge.
- 5. Timeliness and speed.- All actions, procedures and measures contemplated in this Law must be immediate, agile and timely, which will imply the elimination of unnecessary administrative procedures that make impossible the timely care of victims.
- 6. Territoriality of the System.- All instances comprising the System shall have the duty to coordinate and articulate actions at the deconcentrated and decentralized level. For the fulfillment of the purposes and objectives of this Law, actions aimed at preventing and eradicating the different forms of violence, as well as restoring violated rights, must be established at the territorial level..

#### Art. 16.- GENERATION OF INFORMATION.-

1. Single Registry of Violence against Women.- Under the responsibility of the governing entity of the System in coordination with the governing entity of Citizen Security and Public Order and the Council of the Judiciary, it will have the variables that allow characterizing this problem and standardize, process and update the information generated by the agencies that make up the National Comprehensive System for the Prevention and Eradication of Violence against Women.

2. National Observatory on Violence against Women.- Its purpose will be the elaboration of reports, studies and proposals for the effective implementation of this Law, through the production, systematization and analysis of quantitative and qualitative data and information on violence against women, arising from both the Single Registry of Violence against Women and other public or private information sources. The National Observatory on Violence against Women will be under the responsibility of the governing entity of the National Comprehensive System for the Prevention and Eradication of Violence against Women..

#### Art. 17.

Sustainability of the System. The Institutions of the System, through the budget allocated by the governing entity of Public Finance, will prioritize the implementation of this Law, which will be incorporated in the Guiding Classifier of Expenditure on Gender Equality Policies..

#### Art. 18.

Policy Guidelines. The National Comprehensive System to Prevent and Eradicate Violence against Women, in order to guarantee the principles and rights recognized in this Law, in the Constitution of the Republic and in the international instruments on the matter, will apply the following guidelines, without prejudice to those established in this regulation:

- 1. For the prevention and eradication of violence against women, the diversity of women living in Ecuador and their specific needs must be considered. The Executive Function will ensure that this diversity is present in the design of policies and plans intended to make their rights effective, under this Law;
- 2. Address the need for structural change of the causes and social, economic, cultural and political conditions that violate the right to a life free of violence, in all areas contemplated in this Law;
- 3. Incorporate in its design, the problems reflected in official statistics on the different manifestations of violence and discrimination against women;
- 4. For care and protection aimed at preserving, repairing and restoring the rights of women victims of violence, response must be provided through special and expeditious services and mechanisms, adapted to local contexts, with the proper budget for their fulfillment;
- 5. To guide individual or collective reparation of the rights of women victims of violence, the reconstruction of the life project and the assurance of guarantees of non-repetition must be guaranteed; and,
- 6. Generate dialogue processes respectful of the principles of interculturality and legal pluralism, with communities, peoples and nationalities for the prevention and eradication of violence against women and the protection of victims..

#### Art. 19.

Public Policy Instruments. The public policy instruments that are part of the National Comprehensive System for the Prevention and Eradication of Violence against Women, are the following:

- 1. National Development Plan;
- 2. National Agendas for Equality;
- 3. National Plan for the Prevention and Eradication of Violence against Women, Girls and Adolescents, formulated in a participatory manner by the governing entity of the System; and,
- 4. Strategies for the Prevention and Eradication of Violence against Women, which will be formulated in a participatory manner and will be part of the Development and Territorial Planning Plans of all Decentralized Autonomous Governments.

These public policy instruments must contemplate the coordinated action of all responsible entities, at the national and local level to optimize resources and efforts being made..

### CAPITULO II

ON THE GOVERNANCE OF THE SYSTEM, FUNCTIONS, MEMBERS AND THEIR RESPONSIBILITIES

#### Art. 20.- GOVERNANCE OF THE SYSTEM.- The governance of the System is under the responsibility of the governing entity of Justice

and Human Rights and Worship.

The governing entity of the System has the authority to convene any other public, private or civil society entity to comply with what is established in this Law..

#### Art. 21.

Powers of the governing body. The powers of the governing body of the System shall be the following:

- 1. To coordinate the implementation of the National Comprehensive System to Prevent and Eradicate Violence against Women, with the entities that comprise it;
- 2. To prepare an annual report on the progress in the execution of public policy regarding the prevention and eradication of violence against women, which shall serve for accountability to citizens about the results achieved to guarantee women a life free of violence;
- 3. To formulate and issue public policy on matters of prevention and eradication of violence against women;
- 4. To coordinate with the governing entities of public finance and national planning, the monitoring and tracking of the programming and budget of each of the institutions with competencies in this System, in relation to the prevention and eradication of violence against women;
- 5. To monitor and oversee inter-institutional coordination through the mechanisms set forth in this Law;
- 6. To formulate the National Plan for the prevention and eradication of violence against women, in coordination with the members of the System;
- 7. To design and implement awareness and consciousness-raising campaigns directed at citizens for the prevention and eradication of violence against women;
- 8. To promote the active participation of the public and private sector, international cooperation and organized civil society in programs for prevention, attention, protection and reparation of violence against women;
- 9. To promote and coordinate with the instances of inter-institutional cooperation for prevention and eradication of violence against women, the definition and application of local public policy;
- 10. To promote the creation of citizen oversight committees for compliance with this Law as a mechanism of transparency, citizen participation and social control, which also allow diagnosing needs for reforms of laws, plans, programs or projects executed in favor of victims of violence;
- 11. To monitor the application of administrative protection measures established in the Law;
- 12. To coordinate the execution of protection policies for women: girls, adolescents, adults and older adults to prevent and eradicate violence and promote equality and non-discrimination;
- 13. To integrate the systems for prevention and eradication of violence against women with the System for Promotion and Protection of Rights;
- 14. To design in coordination with the respective entities of the System, specialized programs for training, guidance, education, comprehensive care free of charge, directed at aggressors and potential aggressors, through strategies that transform the stereotypes, patterns and sexist behaviors that generate violence against women;
- 15. To request from the member institutions of the System, the design and application of affirmative action mechanisms in services, to guarantee the effectiveness of the implementation of the administrative protection measures granted; and,
- 16. To establish inter-institutional coordination mechanisms, both at the national and local level for the implementation of public policies for eradication of violence against women..

#### Art. 22.

Members of the System. The following national and local entities comprise the National Comprehensive System to Prevent and Eradicate Violence against Women:

- 1. Governing body of Justice and Human Rights;
- 2. Governing body of Education;
- 3. Governing body of Higher Education;
- 4. Governing body of Health;
- 5. Governing body of Citizen Security and Public Order;
- 6. Governing body of Labor;
- 7. Governing body of Economic and Social Inclusion;
- 8. National Councils for Equality;
- 9. Council for Regulation and Development of Information and Communication;
- 10. National Institute of Statistics and Census;
- 11. Integrated Security Service ECU 911;
- 12. Council of the Judiciary;
- 13. State Attorney General's Office;
- 14. Public Defender's Office;
- 15. Ombudsman's Office; and,
- 16. A representative elected by the assembly of each associative body of the Decentralized Autonomous Governments.

The different public entities and levels of government have the obligation to coordinate and articulate among themselves and with linked actors, actions of prevention, attention, protection and reparation. In all activities, the principle of decentralization and deconcentration in the provision of services and in the execution of measures shall be observed..

### CAPITULO III

PREVENTION AXIS

#### Art. 40.

Prevention axis. It shall articulate the policies, plans, programs, projects, mechanisms, measures and actions necessary for the prevention of violence against women, girls, adolescents, youth, adults and older adults.

Prevention through awareness and consciousness-raising mechanisms is aimed at progressively eliminating sociocultural patterns and stereotypes that are justified or naturalized in order to eradicate violence against women.

In compliance with the principle of co-responsibility, civil society and the family in all its types, may propose, promote and develop activities to prevent and eradicate violence against women: girls, adolescents, youth, adults and older adults in their diversity, as well as be an active part of the plans and programs generated from the State for the same purpose..

#### Art. 41.

Measures for prevention. The State, through the entities that make up the System, within the scope of their competencies, shall apply the following policies, plans, programs, projects, guidelines and actions, without prejudice to the functions established for each institution:

- 1. Implement in the mentioned areas, the recommendations regarding international mechanisms on Human Rights of women, both at the Universal and Regional level;
- 2. Design models, protocols and other inter-institutional coordination regulations for the prevention of violence against women, which contemplate and define the articulation of specialized actions;
- 3. Implement a National Program for mainstreaming the Gender Approach in the curriculum of all levels of the formal and non-formal, intercultural and bilingual education system.

In addition to the incorporation of educational programs and subjects that address the transformation of sociocultural patterns as a mechanism for preventing violence against women;

4. Implement a National Training and Evaluation Program, permanent and mandatory, on gender approach, human rights and prevention of violence against women, girls, adolescents, youth, adults, older adults, directed at public servants;

5. Generate a coordination mechanism with civil society organizations, to implement and strengthen policies for the prevention of violence against women, at the territorial level;

6. Develop and implement a policy on detection, risk assessment, early warning of violence and referral to competent authorities, in the public and private sector, specifying the responsibility of the institutions that are part of the subsystem;

7. Generate lines of scientific research on gender violence for qualitative and quantitative studies, and analysis in connection with academia;

8. Design and implement a communication strategy that promotes the rights of women, girls, adolescents, youth, adults and older adults and that transforms sociocultural patterns to prevent violence against women;

9. Regulate and prohibit the dissemination of communicational and advertising content in audiovisual, radio, written and digital media that incites, produces and reproduces violence against women; they must also develop content related to awareness, prevention, protection, sanction and reeducation for the eradication of violence against women and members of the family group;

10. Implement plans, programs and projects for training in rights, empowerment and socioeconomic autonomy of women;

11. Implement reeducation programs for aggressors, on human rights, with emphasis on gender, masculinities and violence;

12. Create a comprehensive and specific strategy for the prevention of violence in communities, peoples and nationalities; and,

13. Formulate, apply and update in the different official and ancestral languages;

In compliance with the principle of co-responsibility, private institutions and social organizations shall apply what corresponds according to their scope and competence, in accordance with the provisions and guiding principles contemplated in this regulation. Special emphasis will be placed on early prevention with girls and adolescents..

#### Art. 42.

General obligations of media and advertising. It is the responsibility of media outlets, both public, private and community, to ensure that the dissemination of information in all its forms and related to violence against women, is treated with the corresponding informative objectivity in favor of the defense of human rights and dignity of women victims of violence and their children; they must also develop content related to awareness, prevention, protection, sanction and reeducation for the eradication of violence against women, girls, adolescents, youth, adults and older adults and members of the family group.

Advertising or information placed on public roads, shopping centers, spaces with greater concentration, must not disseminate sexist, chauvinist messages or that violate the rights of persons. The governing body of Transport and Public Works must remove the advertising placed. Decentralized Autonomous Municipal Governments must create an ordinance that sanctions the placement of sexist billboards, with double meaning, in public or private spaces..

### CAPITULO IV

CARE AXIS

#### Art. 43.

Care axis. The authorities within the scope of their respective competencies, must provide medical, psychological, socioeconomic care and legal advice to women victims of violence, in a specialized, intersectional, interdisciplinary, comprehensive, immediate and free manner. Women, girls, adolescents, youth, adults and older adults who are victims of violence, shall receive special, priority and timely care from any authority and in all public or private services necessary to guarantee dignified treatment, with quality and warmth, in all instances, levels and modalities of services.

Assistance to victims of violence shall be the responsibility of professionals specialized in the matter..

#### Art. 44.

Measures for comprehensive care. The following guidelines and actions must be implemented:

- 1. Design models, protocols and other inter-institutional coordination regulations for comprehensive and specialized care for victims of violence, which contemplate and define the articulation of services, considering the specificity of care for women;
- 2. Strengthen the network of specialized and free services for legal, psychological, medical and socioeconomic care for victims of violence;
- 3. Expand coverage, improve the quality of services and strengthen spaces for comprehensive care in violence, such as specialized care centers and shelters for women victims of violence, with emphasis on rural areas.
- 4. Networks of support among victims of violence will be created, with special emphasis on the training of community promoters;
- 5. Create specialized comprehensive care spaces for specific areas and types of violence, set forth in this Law;
- 6. Create and strengthen exclusive, safe and confidential physical spaces for care of victims of violence in all institutions responsible for their care;
- 7. Guarantee the specialization and permanent training of multidisciplinary teams with a gender, human rights and violence against women, girls, adolescents, youth, adults and older adults approach to strengthen the comprehensive care of victims;
- 8. Promote the implementation of socioeconomic incentive policies in favor of women victims of violence and their families, through affirmative action policies that will be implemented by public and private institutions, according to their competencies..

### CAPITULO V

PROTECTION AXIS

#### Art. 45.

Protection axis. Protection as part of the National Comprehensive System for Prevention and Eradication of violence against women, girls, adolescents, young women, adults and older adult women, shall seek to guarantee the integrity and security of women victims of violence and indirect victims, as well as their dignity, autonomy, integrity and well-being, considering vulnerability and risk factors and support for measures dictated through the Justice Administration System or generation of necessary administrative measures in favor of victims of violence whose cases are not judicialized.

Protection measures imposed by the competent authority are of immediate application, to safeguard life, physical, psychological, sexual integrity; and the patrimonial, economic and labor rights of women in situations of violence and their dependents..

#### Art. 46.

Proportionality. The comprehensive protection measures regulated by this Law shall be granted to victims of violence in an appropriate manner and proportional to the severity of the act that threatens or violates their rights, to the damages suffered by the victim and to their particular circumstances..

#### Art. 47.

Immediate protection measures. Protection measures shall be of immediate and provisional character; they shall have the purpose of avoiding or ceasing the threat or violation of life and integrity, in relation to acts of violence against women, girls, adolescents, young women, adults and older adult women, determined in this legal body.

The governing entity of the System must provide for all public and private sector entities to determine administrative or internal prevention and protection measures in case of any act of violence established in this Law..

#### Art. 48.- Urgent actions.- Urgent actions are those that will be executed by the National Police

when there exists or is presumed an imminent violation or risk to the life and integrity of the victim, according to the established protocols as follows:

- a) Respond immediately to an alert generated by: panic button, call to the ECU 911 Integrated Service, video surveillance, patrol, police surveillance and other alert mechanisms;
- b) Activation of security and protection protocols for women victims of gender violence;
- c) Accompaniment of the victim to reintegrate her to her habitual residence, when so requested or so that she may take her belongings, as the case may be;
- d) Accompany the victim to the competent authority to request the issuance of the aid bulletin and the order of restriction of approach to the victim by the alleged aggressor, in any public or private space; and,
- e) Request specialized attention from the entities that make up the National Comprehensive System for Prevention and Eradication of violence against women, in favor of the victim and the persons who depend on her..

#### Art. 49.

Competent bodies to grant immediate administrative protection measures. The competent authorities to grant immediate administrative protection measures are:

- a) Cantonal Rights Protection Boards; and,
- b) Political Lieutenancies.

In places where there are no Cantonal Rights Protection Boards, the National Police Commissariats shall be the competent entities to grant immediate administrative protection measures.

These bodies may not deny the granting of immediate administrative protection measures, for reasons of territorial scope..

#### Art. 50.

Functions of the Cantonal Rights Protection Boards. Without prejudice to those already established in other normative bodies, the following attributions correspond to the Cantonal Rights Protection Boards:

- a) Hear ex officio or at the request of a party, cases of threat or violation of the rights of women: girls, adolescents, young women, adults and older adult women, within the framework of their jurisdiction; and, order the administrative protection measures that are necessary to protect the threatened right or restore the violated right;
- b) Bring the necessary actions before the competent judicial bodies in cases of non-compliance with their decisions;
- c) Request from public officials of the central and sectional administration, the information and documents they require for the fulfillment of their functions;
- d) Keep the registry of persons on whom protection measures have been applied and provide the information to the Single Registry of Violence against Women;
- e) Report to the competent authorities, the commission of acts of violence of which they have knowledge; and,
- f) Monitor that in institutional regulations and practices, care entities do not violate the rights of women, girls, adolescents, young women, adults and older adult women..

#### Art. 51.

Immediate Administrative Protection Measures. Immediate administrative protection measures shall be ordered immediately when there is a violation of the integrity of a woman victim of violence. They shall be granted by the Political Lieutenants at the parish level; and, at the cantonal level, by the Cantonal Boards for the Protection of Rights.

In addition to the administrative measures established in other regulations in force, the granting of one or more of the following immediate protection measures shall be contemplated:

- a) Issue the writ of assistance and the order of restriction of approach to the victim by the alleged aggressor, in any public or private space;
- b) Order the restitution of the victim to the habitual domicile, when she has been removed from it by the violent act and so requests it, with sufficient guarantees to protect her life and integrity;
- c) At the request of the victim, the insertion of her and her dependents in a protection program shall be ordered in order to safeguard their safety and integrity, in coordination with the governing body of public policies on Justice, the network of shelters, specialized care centers and interinstitutional coordination spaces, at the territorial level;
- d) Prohibit the aggressor from hiding, transferring, changing the residence or place of domicile of his or her daughters or sons or persons dependent on him or her, without prejudice to other actions that may be initiated;
- e) Prohibit the aggressor, by himself or through third parties, actions of intimidation, threats or coercion against the woman who is in a situation of violence or any member of her family;
- f) Order the aggressor to leave the domicile when his presence constitutes a threat to the physical, psychological or sexual integrity or the life of the woman or any of the family members;
- g) Order the carrying out of the inventory of movable and immovable property of common ownership or legitimate possession of the woman victim of violence;
- h) Provide for the installation of alert devices, risk devices or electronic alert devices, in the dwelling of the woman victim of violence;
- i) Provide for the activation of the protection and care services provided for in the National Comprehensive System to prevent and eradicate violence against Women;
- j) Provide for the insertion of the woman victim of violence and her dependents in social and economic inclusion, health, education, labor and care programs aimed at priority care groups under the responsibility of the governing body of public policies on Social Inclusion and other local instances that provide this service;
- k) Provide for follow-up to verify the rectification of the conduct of violence against women by the respective technical units, of the governing bodies of public policies on Social Inclusion, Health, and other local instances that provide this service, through a reasoned report;
- l) Prohibit the aggressor from concealing or withholding property or documents owned by the victim of violence; and in case of having concealed or withheld them, order the aggressor to immediately return personal use objects, identity documents and any other document or object of ownership or custody of the woman victim of violence or persons who depend on her;
- m) Provide, when necessary, for the flexibility or reduction of the work schedule of women victims of violence, without their labor or salary rights being affected;
- n) Order the temporary suspension of activities carried out by the alleged aggressor in sports, artistic, care or formal and informal education institutions; and,
- o) All those that guarantee the integrity of women in situations of violence..

#### Art. 52.- Strengthening and criterion of specialization in the Cantonal Boards for the Protection of

Rights. The Cantonal Boards for the Protection of Rights shall have specialized personnel in the protection of rights and their respective alternates for the granting, application and follow-up of immediate administrative protection measures..

SECTION I

PROCEDURE AND GRANTING OF IMMEDIATE PROTECTION MEASURES

#### Art. 53.- Procedure.- The procedure for ordering immediate administrative protection measures

shall be established in the regulation that for this purpose shall be issued by the Governing Body of the National Comprehensive System for the Prevention and Eradication of Violence against Women. This shall be agile in all its phases and shall not require professional counsel. The authority within its competencies shall have the obligation to adopt the corresponding measures to guarantee the life and integrity of women, girls and adolescents victims of violence..

#### Art. 54.

Petition. Any person or group of persons who has knowledge of the commission of acts or acts of violence may request the granting of urgent actions and immediate administrative protection measures, in favor of the victim, verbally or in writing, before the National Police, for the urgent ones; and the Cantonal Boards for the Protection of Rights and Political Lieutenancies, for the administrative ones..

#### Art. 55.- Granting of immediate administrative protection measures that have the purpose of

stopping the violation of women's rights. Immediate administrative protection measures shall be granted immediately by the Cantonal Boards for the Protection of Rights and the Political Lieutenancies when the right to personal integrity and dignified life of the woman is being violated or has been violated.

Within a maximum period of twenty-four hours, the body that granted the immediate administrative protection measure shall bring to the attention of the judicial bodies the fact and the measure granted so that it may ratify, modify or revoke it..

#### Art. 56.- Granting of immediate administrative protection measures that have the purpose of

preventing the violation of women's rights. Once the Cantonal Board for the Protection of Rights learns of the request for granting of immediate administrative protection measures, it shall verify by the mere description of the facts, the risk of violation of the right to personal integrity and dignified life of women and shall grant or deny them immediately. The Cantonal Board for the Protection of Rights that grants the immediate administrative protection measures shall specify and individualize the measure or measures, as well as the circumstances of time, manner and place in which they must be fulfilled based on the level of risk situation and condition of vulnerability of the victims of gender violence.

Within a maximum period of three days the body that granted the immediate protection measure shall bring to the attention of the judicial bodies the fact and the measure granted so that it may ratify, modify or revoke the measure..

#### Art. 57.

Registry of granted measures. The cases and measures granted must be registered in the Single Registry of Violence against Women..

#### Art. 58.

Principle of appealability. Immediate administrative protection measures shall be granted to women victims of gender violence, without prejudice to a process being active, whether in indigenous or ordinary justice..

SECTION II

EARLY WARNING SYSTEM

#### Art. 59.

Early Warning System. The Early Warning System is a mechanism that allows femicide due to gender violence to be prevented, through the analysis of the information contained in the Single Registry of Violence against Women, through the identification of the risk of a possible victim and the activation of the protection and care services determined in this Law..

#### Art. 60.

Identification of a possible victim. The governing entity for citizen security and order shall develop and execute the identification of women whose life and integrity are at risk due to gender-based violence, based on the analysis of data from the Single Registry of Violence against Women. This analysis involves the specification or estimation of a quantitative or qualitative model for the identification of risk of a possible victim, which must be categorized according to risk levels..

#### Art. 61.

Articulation. The governing entity for citizen security and public order shall articulate the Early

Warning System in coordination with:

- 1. The governing entity for Justice and Human Rights;
- 2. The governing entity for Education;
- 3. The governing entity for Health;
- 4. The governing entity for Labor;
- 5. The governing entity for Economic and Social Inclusion;
- 6. The Integrated Security Service ECU 911; and,
- 7. Other institutions that the governing entity for citizen security and public order may consider..

### CAPITULO VI

PILLAR OF REPARATION THROUGH AFFIRMATIVE ACTION MEASURES

#### Art. 62.

Mechanisms for comprehensive reparation. In the event that the commission of acts or facts constituting violence against women: girls, adolescents, young women, adults, older adults, is declared by ruling, the competent judicial authority shall order comprehensive reparation for the material and immaterial damage caused.

Reparation may include, among other forms, the restitution of the right, economic or patrimonial compensation, rehabilitation, satisfaction, guarantees that the act will not be repeated, the obligation to refer to the competent authority to investigate and sanction, recognition measures, public apologies, the provision of public services, health care, among others.

Reparation for material damage shall also include compensation for the loss or reduction of the income of victims of violence, expenses incurred as a result of the acts and pecuniary consequences that have a causal link with the facts of the case.

Reparation for immaterial or moral damage may include both the suffering or afflictions caused to direct and indirect victims and their relatives, the impairment of values very significant to the persons, as well as alterations of a non-pecuniary nature in the conditions of existence of the victim..

#### Art. 63.

Standards for reparation measures. For the process of requesting and determining reparation measures, prosecutors, male and female judges must take into account the following standards:

- 1. Know the expectations of women victims of violence, without attributing to them the burden of identification and proof;
- 2. Inform women victims of violence about the scope of reparation measures and the means of execution;
- 3. Reparation measures shall be based on the principle of proportionality and comprehensiveness;
- 4. Contain details of the institutions that execute the reparation measure, the time within which the reparation measure must be executed and the periodicity of compliance with said measures..

#### Art. 64.

Affirmative action measures to guarantee reparation. Without prejudice to the provisions of the Comprehensive Organic Criminal Code, the competent authority may take into account all programs and projects implemented by public institutions..

#### Art. 65.

General responsibility of the institutions that make up the System. State institutions, within the framework of their competencies and upon prior sentence or resolution of the competent authority, shall immediately comply with and execute the mechanisms for reparation of the rights of victims of violence, ordered by virtue of this Law..

### CAPITULO VII

SOCIAL PARTICIPATION AND OVERSIGHT FOR THE ENFORCEMENT OF THIS LAW

#### Art. 66.

Promotion of participation and organizational strengthening. To ensure compliance with this Law, the participation of women, social and community organizations, and other social actors at all levels of government and functions responsible for the formulation of public policies shall be promoted, within the framework of this Law.

To this end, without prejudice to other measures adopted for this purpose, the following shall be complied with:

- a) The agencies responsible for the promotion of social participation at each level of government shall promote and strengthen the participation of women's, social, community, and civil society organizations, as well as the creation of national and local committees of service users in order to observe and monitor compliance with the provisions of this Law.
- b) Networks, collectives, movements, and women's organizations shall develop actions to follow up, oversee, exercise social control, or observe the actions of the bodies that make up the system provided for in this Law..

GENERAL PROVISIONS

FIRST.- The institutions that form part of the National Comprehensive System for the Prevention and Eradication of Violence against Women must register the budget allocated within the Guiding Classifier of Expenditure on Gender Equality Policies, in the area of prevention and eradication of gender-based violence..

SECOND.- The State, through the Ministry of Economy and Finance, shall guarantee the timely provision and disbursement of the necessary resources to fulfill the obligations and responsibilities arising from this Law..

THIRD.- The entities that form part of the National Comprehensive System for the Prevention and Eradication of Violence against Women shall strive to have specialized personnel in violence against women and strengthen the technical teams for the care of women victims of violence, which must be comprised of professionals specialized in medicine, psychology, social work, and related areas..

FOURTH.- State entities shall develop annual awareness campaigns for their public servants on issues related to the prevention of violence against women.. FIFTH.- The National Councils for Equality, within the framework of their competencies, shall implement processes to monitor the provisions contained in this Law.. SIXTH.- Female and male public servants who fail to comply with the obligations set forth in this Law or contravene the provisions thereof, its regulations, protocols, as well as related laws and regulations, shall be sanctioned in accordance with the provisions established in labor legislation, without prejudice to civil or criminal actions that sanction the same act.

In the case of the authorities established in article 131 of the Constitution of the Republic, who fail to fulfill their responsibilities and functions contemplated for the application of this Law, they shall be subject to political prosecution by the National Assembly..

SEVENTH.- The National Comprehensive System for the Prevention and Eradication of Violence against Women, through its governing body, shall present an annual accountability report on the plans, programs, and projects that each of the institutions that comprise it have executed within the framework of application of this Law..

EIGHTH.- The Decentralized Autonomous Cantonal Governments, through the Cantonal Boards for the Protection of Rights, have the obligation to assume the competence of granting immediate administrative protection measures.

The General Police Superintendencies and Police Commissariats shall be responsible for assuming the competence of granting immediate administrative protection measures in cantons where the Cantonal Boards for the Protection of Rights have not assumed it..

TRANSITORY PROVISIONS:

FIRST.- Within a maximum period of ninety days from the publication of this law, the general implementing regulations shall be issued..

SECOND.- The National Council for Gender Equality, the Ministry of Justice, Human Rights and Worship, the Ministry of Economy and Finance, and the Ministry of Labor, within 12 months from the enactment of this Law, shall design and implement a transition plan on the implementation of the National System for the Prevention and Eradication of Violence against Women..

THIRD.- The institutions that form part of the National Comprehensive System for the Prevention and Eradication of Violence against Women shall issue the secondary regulations and protocols necessary for the application and full force of this Law, within the scope of their competencies, within a maximum period of 90 days from the publication of the General Regulation of this Law in the Official Registry..

FOURTH.- The institutions that form part of the National Comprehensive System for the Prevention and Eradication of Violence against Women shall have a maximum period of twelve months from the publication of this law to implement plans, programs, projects, public services, allocate human resources and goods, adapt registries, databases or any other form of information or technologies, in accordance with what is established in this legal body.. FIFTH.- The Ministry of Labor must require the public and private sectors, within six months from the publication of this law, to adapt their internal regulations to the approaches, principles, areas, and other provisions contained in this legal body, for which it shall conduct follow-up and provide advice to those who require it..

SIXTH.- The National Assembly, through the Permanent Specialized Commissions on Justice and State Structure, Workers' Rights and Social Security, Right to Health, Autonomous Governments, Decentralization, Competencies and Territorial Organization, and Education, Culture, Science and Technology, shall request semi-annual information from the institutions that make up the National Comprehensive System for the Prevention and Eradication of Violence against Women, regarding compliance with this Law..

SEVENTH.- The Working Group for the construction of the Single Registry of Violence against Women shall be formed, with all public institutions that make up the system, so that within 6 months the Single Registry of Violence against Women is presented, the basis for the correct application of this law; this shall be headed and directed by the governing Ministry in Citizen Security and Public Order..

EIGHTH.- The National Assembly, within a maximum period of 120 days from the publication of this law, shall analyze the pertinence of typifying in the Comprehensive Organic Criminal Code the types of violence that are added in this law..

NINTH.- Within one hundred eighty days from the publication of this Law, the Cantonal Councils for the Protection of Rights shall establish ordinances as part of local public policies to prevent and eradicate violence against women..

TENTH.- The decentralized autonomous governments, at all levels, within a period not exceeding one hundred eighty days, counted from the entry into force of this Law, shall update the Development Plans prepared, in which the necessary, timely, and adequate measures and policies must be included to ensure compliance with this Law and prevent revictimization and impunity..

If the infraction affects a person from one of the priority care groups, in a situation of double vulnerability, or with catastrophic or highly complex illnesses, the sanction shall be the maximum penalty, increased by one third..

SEVENTH.- Reform article 159 of the Comprehensive Organic Criminal Code with the following text: "Art. 159: Contraventions of Violence against women or members of the family nucleus. A person who wounds, injures, or strikes a woman or members of the family nucleus, causing damage or illness that limits or conditions their daily activities for a period not exceeding three days, shall be punished with a custodial sentence of ten to thirty days.

A person who physically assaults a woman or members of the family nucleus by means of kicks, slaps, pushes, or any other manner that signifies use of physical force without causing injury, shall be punished with a custodial sentence of five to ten days or community service of sixty to one hundred twenty hours and comprehensive reparation measures.

A person who commits acts of removal, destruction, retention of objects, work instruments, personal documents, or property of the de facto or conjugal partnership, in cases where it does not constitute an autonomous crime typified in this Code, shall be punished with community service of forty to eighty hours and the return of the goods or payment of their monetary value, and comprehensive reparation measure.

A person who, by any means, utters improprieties, expressions of discredit or dishonor against a woman or members of the family nucleus, in cases where it does not constitute an autonomous crime typified in this Code, shall be punished with fifty to one hundred hours of community service, and psychological treatment shall be ordered for the aggressor and the victims, as well as comprehensive reparation measures..
