Basis and limits of criminal law coercion

Protection of man and other fundamental social values constitutes the basis and limits for determining criminal offences, prescribing criminal sanctions and their application, to the extent necessary for the suppression of such offences. Legality in determining criminal offences and prescribing criminal sanctions

A punishment or other criminal sanction may be imposed only for an act which, before it was committed, was determined by law as a criminal offence and for which a punishment was prescribed by law. Punishment and warning measures may be imposed only on a perpetrator who is culpable for the committed criminal offence. Criminal sanctions and their general purpose

(1) Criminal sanctions are: punishments, warning measures, security measures and educational measures. (2) The general purpose of prescribing and imposing criminal sanctions is the suppression of acts which violate or endanger values protected by criminal legislation. 1. General provisions on criminal offences

A criminal offence is an act which is determined by law as a criminal offence, which is unlawful and which is committed with culpability. Manner of commission of a criminal offence

(1) A criminal offence may be committed by action or omission. (2) A criminal offence is committed by omission when the perpetrator has failed to perform an action which he was obliged to perform. (3) A criminal offence may also be committed by omission even if it is not determined by law as an omission, if the perpetrator by omission of an obligatory action has fulfilled the elements of a criminal offence. Time of commission of a criminal offence

(1) A criminal offence is committed at the time when the perpetrator acted or was obliged to act, regardless of when the consequence of the act occurred. (2) For an accomplice it is considered that the criminal offence was committed at the time when he acted or was obliged to act. Place of commission of a criminal offence

(1) A criminal offence is committed at the place where the perpetrator acted and was obliged to act or where the consequence of the act occurred in whole or in part, and in the case of an attempt also where the consequence of the attempted act according to his intent should have or could have occurred. (2) For an accomplice it is considered that the act was committed also at the place where he undertook the act of complicity. (deleted)

(2) For a criminal offence committed out of negligence the perpetrator may be culpable only when determined by law. (1) A perpetrator who at the time of commission of an unlawful act determined by law as a criminal offence was unable to comprehend the significance of his act or was unable to control his actions due to mental illness, temporary mental disturbance, retarded mental development or other serious mental disturbance (insanity) is not accountable. (2) A perpetrator of a criminal offence whose capacity to comprehend the significance of his act or capacity to control his actions was substantially diminished due to any condition from paragraph 1 of this Article (substantially diminished accountability) may be punished more leniently. (3) The culpability of a perpetrator of a criminal offence who by use of alcohol, drugs or in another manner brought himself into a state in which he was unable to comprehend the significance of his act or to control his actions is determined according to the time immediately before bringing himself into such a state. (4) A perpetrator who under the circumstances from paragraph 3 of this Article committed a criminal offence in a state of substantially diminished accountability may not have his punishment mitigated. A criminal offence is committed with intent when the perpetrator was aware of his act and wanted its commission or when the perpetrator was aware that he could commit the act and consented to it. A criminal offence is committed out of negligence when the perpetrator was aware that by his action he could commit the act, but recklessly believed that it would not occur or that he would be able to prevent it or when he was not aware that by his action he could commit the act although according to the circumstances under which it was committed and according to his personal characteristics he was obliged and could have been aware of that possibility. Liability for a graver consequence

When a graver consequence resulted from a criminal offence for which the law prescribes a more severe punishment for that act, such punishment may be imposed if the perpetrator acted with negligence in relation to that consequence, and also with intent if it does not constitute the elements of another criminal offence. (1) An act committed in unavoidable factual error is not a criminal offence. (2) Unavoidable factual error exists if the perpetrator was not obliged and could not have avoided the error regarding any factual circumstance which constitutes an element of the criminal offence or regarding any factual circumstance which, if it had actually existed, would make the act permissible. (3) If the perpetrator was in factual error due to negligence, there shall be a criminal offence committed out of negligence when prescribed by law. (1) An act committed in unavoidable legal error is not a criminal offence. (2) Unavoidable legal error exists if the perpetrator was not obliged and could not have known that the act he committed was prohibited. (3) A perpetrator who did not know that the act was prohibited, but was obliged and could have known, may have his punishment mitigated. 2. Attempt of a criminal offence and voluntary abandonment

(1) Whoever with intent commences the commission of a criminal offence, but does not complete it, shall be punished for the attempt of a criminal offence for which the law prescribes a punishment of imprisonment of five years or a more severe punishment, and for the attempt of another criminal offence only when the law expressly prescribes punishment also for the attempt. (2) Commencement of the commission of a criminal offence also includes the use of a specific means or application of a specific manner of commission, if they are determined by law as elements of the criminal offence. (3) The perpetrator shall be punished for the attempt with the punishment prescribed for the criminal offence, and may also be punished more leniently. A perpetrator who attempts to commit a criminal offence by an unsuitable means or against an unsuitable object may be exempted from punishment. (1) A perpetrator who attempted the commission of a criminal offence, but voluntarily abandoned its commission, may be exempted from punishment. (2) In the case of voluntary abandonment, the perpetrator shall be punished for those actions which constitute another independent criminal offence, which is not encompassed by the criminal offence from whose commission the perpetrator abandoned. 3. Complicity in a criminal offence

(1) The perpetrator of a criminal offence is a person who undertakes the act of commission of a criminal offence or who commits a criminal offence through another person, provided that other person cannot be considered the perpetrator of the criminal offence. (2) If several persons, by participation in the act of commission with intent or out of negligence, jointly commit a criminal offence, or by realising a joint decision by another action with intent substantially contribute to the commission of the criminal offence, each of them shall be punished with the punishment prescribed for that act. (1) Whoever with intent instigates another to commit a criminal offence shall be punished as if he committed it himself. (2) Whoever with intent instigates another to commit a criminal offence for which the law prescribes a punishment of imprisonment of five years or a more severe punishment, and the act is not even attempted, shall be punished as for the attempt of a criminal offence. (1) Whoever with intent aids another in the commission of a criminal offence shall be punished as if he committed it himself, and may also be punished more leniently. (2) Aiding in the commission of a criminal offence is considered in particular: giving advice or instructions on how to commit a criminal offence, placing at the perpetrator's disposal means for the commission of a criminal offence, creating conditions or removing obstacles for the commission of a criminal offence, as well as promised concealment in advance of the criminal offence, the perpetrator, the means by which the criminal offence was committed, traces of the criminal offence or objects obtained by the criminal offence. Limits of liability and punishability of accomplices

(1) A co-perpetrator is liable within the limits of his intent or negligence, and an instigator and aider within the limits of his intent. (2) A co-perpetrator, instigator or aider who voluntarily prevented the commission of the criminal offence may be exempted from punishment. (3) Personal relations, characteristics and circumstances due to which the law excludes culpability or allows exemption from punishment, on which the existence of a lesser or graver form of the act depends or which influence the sentencing, may be taken into account only for that perpetrator, co-perpetrator, instigator or aider in whom such relations, characteristics and circumstances exist. Punishment of instigators and aiders for attempt and lesser criminal offence

(1) If the criminal offence remained at the attempt stage the instigator and aider shall be punished for the attempt. (2) If the perpetrator committed a lesser criminal offence than the one to which the instigation or aiding relates, and which would be encompassed by it, the instigator and aider shall be punished for the criminal offence that was committed. (3) The provision of paragraph 2 of this Article shall not apply if the instigator would be punished more severely by application of Article 24 paragraph 2 of this Code. 4. Special provisions on liability for criminal offences committed through media

(1) For criminal offences committed through media the editor is liable, that is, the person who replaced him at the time of publication of the information, if:

- 1) by the end of the main hearing before the first instance court the author remained unknown; - 2) the information was published without the consent of the author; - 3) at the time of publication of the information there were factual or legal obstacles to prosecution of the author, which still persist. - (2) The editor is not liable, that is, the person replacing him if for justified reasons he did not know of any of the circumstances specified in paragraph 1 items 1 to 3 of this Article. Liability of publisher, printer and producer

(1) When the conditions from Article 28 of this Code exist, the following are liable:

- 1) the publisher - for a criminal offence committed through a non-periodic printed publication, and if there is no publisher or there are factual or legal obstacles to his prosecution - the printer who knew of this; - 2) the producer - for a criminal offence committed through a compact disc, gramophone record, magnetic tape and other auditive means, film for public and private showing, slides, video means or similar means intended for a wider circle of persons. - (2) If the publisher, printer or producer is a legal entity or state body, the liable person is the person responsible for publishing, printing or production. 1. Purpose of punishment, types of punishments and conditions for their imposition

Within the general purpose of criminal sanctions (Article 4 paragraph 2), the purpose of punishment is:

- 1) preventing the perpetrator from committing criminal offences and influencing him not to commit criminal offences in the future; - 2) influencing others not to commit criminal offences; - 3) expressing social condemnation for the criminal offence and the obligation to respect the law; - 4) strengthening morality and influencing the development of social responsibility. The following punishments may be imposed on the perpetrator of a criminal offence:

- 1) long-term imprisonment; - 2) imprisonment; - 3) fine; - 4) community service work. - (1) Long-term imprisonment, imprisonment and community service work may be imposed only as principal punishments. - (2) A fine may be imposed both as a principal and as an accessory punishment. - (3) If several punishments are prescribed for one criminal offence, only one may be imposed as the principal punishment. - (1) Long-term imprisonment may not be shorter than thirty nor longer than forty years. - (2) Long-term imprisonment may be prescribed for the most serious criminal offences, provided it may not be prescribed as the only punishment for a specific criminal offence. - (3) Long-term imprisonment may not be imposed on:
- 1) a person who at the time of commission of the criminal offence had not reached twenty-one years of age; - 2) a person whose accountability at the time of commission of the criminal offence was substantially diminished (Article 14 paragraph 2); - 3) for the attempt of a criminal offence. - (1) Imprisonment may not be shorter than thirty days nor longer than twenty years. - (2) Imprisonment from paragraph 1 of this Article is imposed in full years and months, and up to six months also in days. Fine in daily amounts

(1) In cases when it is possible to determine the income and expenses of the perpetrator of a criminal offence the court may impose a fine in daily amounts. (2) The number of daily amounts may not be less than ten, nor greater than three hundred sixty days. (3) The amount of the daily amount of the fine is determined by dividing the difference between the income and necessary expenses of the perpetrator of the criminal offence in the past calendar year by the number of days in the year, provided that one day of the fine may not be less than five euros nor greater than one thousand euros. (1) Community service work may be imposed for criminal offences for which a fine or imprisonment of up to five years is prescribed. (3) This punishment is imposed with the consent of the perpetrator of the criminal offence and may not be longer than sixty hours during one month. (4) Community service work is any socially useful work which does not offend human dignity and is not performed for the purpose of acquiring profit. (6) If the perpetrator does not perform community service work, this punishment shall be replaced with imprisonment, in such a way that every commenced sixty hours of community service work shall be replaced with imprisonment of one month duration. General rules on sentencing

(1) The court shall impose on the perpetrator of a criminal offence a sentence within the limits prescribed by law for that act, having in mind the purpose of punishment and taking into account all circumstances which influence the sentence to be lesser or greater (mitigating and aggravating circumstances), and in particular: the degree of culpability, the motives from which the act was committed, the intensity of endangerment or violation of the protected good, the circumstances under which the act was committed, the previous life of the perpetrator, his personal circumstances, his behaviour after the committed criminal offence, and in particular his relationship toward the victim of the criminal offence as well as other circumstances relating to the personality of the perpetrator. (2) When sentencing a fine the court shall particularly take into account also the property status of the perpetrator. (3) A circumstance which is an element of the criminal offence may not be taken into account also as an aggravating, or mitigating circumstance, except if it exceeds the measure necessary for the existence of the criminal offence or a specific form of the criminal offence or if there are two or more such circumstances, and only one is sufficient for the existence of a graver, or lesser form of the criminal offence. (1) If a criminal offence is committed out of hatred toward another person because of national or ethnic affiliation, affiliation with a race or religion or because of the absence of such affiliation, disability, sex, sexual orientation or gender identity, the court shall assess that circumstance as aggravating, unless it is prescribed as an element of the basic or graver form of the criminal offence. (2) If a criminal offence is committed toward a person who belongs to a particularly vulnerable category of persons (children, persons with disabilities, pregnant women, elderly persons, refugees), the court shall take that circumstance as aggravating. Purpose of Suspended Sentence and Judicial Admonition

(1) Warning measures are: suspended sentence and judicial admonition. (2) Within the general purpose of criminal sanctions (Article 4 paragraph 2), the purpose of a suspended sentence and judicial admonition is that a penalty not be imposed on the perpetrator of a criminal offense for minor criminal offenses when this is not necessary for the purposes of criminal law protection and when it can be expected that a warning with the threat of punishment (suspended sentence) or a warning alone (judicial admonition) will sufficiently influence the perpetrator not to commit further criminal offenses. (1) By a suspended sentence, the court determines a penalty for the perpetrator of a criminal offense and simultaneously determines that it will not be executed if the convicted person, during a period determined by the court, which may not be shorter than one year nor longer than five years (probation period), does not commit a new criminal offense. (2) The court may, in the suspended sentence, determine that the penalty will be executed also if the convicted person fails within a specified period to return property benefits obtained by the commission of the criminal offense, fails to compensate for damage caused by the criminal offense, or fails to fulfill other obligations provided for in criminal law provisions. The court shall determine the time limit for fulfilling these obligations within the determined probation period. (3) Security measures imposed together with a suspended sentence shall be executed. Conditions for Imposing a Suspended Sentence

(1) A suspended sentence may be imposed when a prison sentence of up to two years has been determined for the perpetrator. (2) For criminal offenses for which a prison sentence of ten years or a more severe penalty may be imposed, a suspended sentence may not be imposed. (3) A suspended sentence may not be imposed if more than five years have not elapsed since the finality of a conviction by which a penalty was imposed on the perpetrator for an intentional criminal offense. A suspended sentence may not be imposed on a perpetrator who has already received two suspended sentences. (4) In deciding whether to impose a suspended sentence, the court shall, taking into account the purpose of the suspended sentence, particularly consider the personality of the perpetrator, his previous life, his conduct after the commission of the criminal offense, the degree of guilt, and other circumstances under which the act was committed. (5) If both a prison sentence and a monetary fine have been determined for the perpetrator, a suspended sentence may be imposed only for the prison sentence. Revocation of Suspended Sentence Due to a New Criminal Offense

(1) The court shall revoke the suspended sentence if the convicted person, during the probation period, commits one or more criminal offenses for which a prison sentence of two years or longer has been imposed. (2) If the convicted person, during the probation period, commits one or more criminal offenses for which a prison sentence of less than two years or a monetary fine has been imposed, the court shall, after assessing all circumstances relating to the committed criminal offenses and the perpetrator, and particularly the relatedness of the committed criminal offenses, their significance, and the motives from which they were committed, decide whether to revoke the suspended sentence. In doing so, the court is bound by the prohibition on imposing a suspended sentence if, for the criminal offenses determined in the suspended sentence and for the new criminal offenses, it should impose a sentence exceeding two years of imprisonment (Article 54 paragraph 1). (3) If it revokes the suspended sentence, the court shall, by applying the provisions of Article 48 of this Code, impose a single prison sentence for both the previously committed and the new criminal offense, taking the sentence from the revoked suspended sentence as determined. (4) If it does not revoke the suspended sentence, the court may impose a suspended sentence or a penalty for the newly committed criminal offense. For a convicted person who is sentenced to imprisonment for the new criminal offense, the time spent serving this sentence shall not be counted in the probation period determined by the suspended sentence for the previous offense. (5) If the court finds that a suspended sentence should also be imposed for the new criminal offense, by applying the provisions of Article 48 of this Code, it shall determine a single sentence for both the previously committed and the new criminal offense and shall determine a new probation period which may not be shorter than one year nor longer than five years, counting from the day the new judgment becomes final. If the convicted person commits another criminal offense during the new probation period, the court shall revoke the suspended sentence and impose a prison sentence by applying the provision of paragraph 3 of this Article. Revocation of Suspended Sentence Due to a Previously Committed Criminal Offense

(1) The court shall revoke the suspended sentence if, after its imposition, it determines that the convicted person committed a criminal offense before being placed on probation and if it assesses that there would have been no grounds for imposing a suspended sentence had that offense been known. In such a case, it shall apply the provision of Article 55 paragraph 3 of this Code. (2) If the court does not revoke the suspended sentence, it shall apply the provision of Article 55 paragraph 4 of this Code. Revocation of Suspended Sentence for Failure to Fulfill Specified Obligations

If a convicted person has been ordered by the suspended sentence to fulfill an obligation under Article 53 paragraph 2 of this Code and fails to fulfill that obligation within the time limit specified in the judgment, the court may, within the probation period, extend the time limit for fulfilling the obligation or may revoke the suspended sentence and impose the penalty that was determined in the suspended sentence. If it determines that the convicted person, for justified reasons, cannot fulfill the imposed obligation, the court shall release him from fulfilling that obligation or replace it with another appropriate obligation provided for by law. Time Limits for Revoking a Suspended Sentence

(1) A suspended sentence may be revoked during the probation period. If the convicted person commits during that period a criminal offense that entails revocation of the suspended sentence, and this is established by judgment only after the expiration of the probation period, the suspended sentence may be revoked no later than one year from the day the probation period expired. (2) If the convicted person fails to fulfill an obligation under Article 53 paragraph 2 of this Code within the specified time limit, the court may, no later than one year from the day the probation period expired, order the execution of the penalty determined in the suspended sentence. Occurrence of Legal Consequences of Conviction

(1) Convictions for certain criminal offenses or to certain punishments may have as a legal consequence the termination or loss of certain rights or prohibition of acquisition of certain rights. (2) Legal consequences of conviction cannot occur when a fine has been imposed on the perpetrator for a criminal offense, a suspended sentence unless revoked, a judicial admonition, or when the perpetrator has been released from punishment. (3) Legal consequences of conviction may be provided only by law and occur by force of the law by which they are provided. Types of Legal Consequences of Conviction

(1) Legal consequences of conviction relating to termination or loss of certain rights are:

- 1) termination of performance of public functions; - 2) termination of employment or termination of performance of a certain calling or occupation; - 3) loss of certain permits or approvals granted by decision of a state authority or local self-government authority. - (2) Legal consequences of conviction consisting of prohibition of acquisition of certain rights are:
- 1) prohibition of acquisition of certain public functions; - 2) prohibition of acquisition of a certain title, calling or occupation or promotion in service; - 3) prohibition of acquisition of the rank of military officer; - 4) prohibition of obtaining certain permits or approvals granted by decision of state authorities or local self-government authorities. Commencement and Duration of Legal Consequences of Conviction

(1) Legal consequences of conviction occur on the date the judgment becomes final. (2) In the event that after the judgment becomes final, on the basis of which legal consequences of conviction occurred, that judgment is amended by extraordinary legal remedy, the occurrence or further duration of legal consequences of conviction is harmonized with the new decision. (3) Legal consequences of conviction consisting of prohibition of acquisition of certain rights may be prescribed for a duration of up to ten years at most. (4) Time spent serving a sentence is not counted in the duration of the legal consequence of conviction. (5) Rehabilitation terminates the legal consequences of conviction provided in Article 116 paragraph 2 of this Code. REHABILITATION, TERMINATION OF LEGAL CONSEQUENCES OF CONVICTION AND PROVISION OF DATA FROM CRIMINAL RECORDS

(1) By rehabilitation the conviction is erased and all its legal consequences cease, and the convicted person is considered not to have been convicted. (2) Rehabilitation occurs either on the basis of law itself (statutory rehabilitation) or at the request of the convicted person on the basis of a court decision (judicial rehabilitation). (3) Rehabilitation does not affect the rights of third parties based on the conviction. (1) Statutory rehabilitation is granted only to persons who before the conviction to which the rehabilitation relates had not been convicted or who by law were considered not to have been convicted. (2) Statutory rehabilitation occurs if:

- 1) a person who was found guilty but released from punishment or to whom a judicial admonition was given, within one year from the date the judgment becomes final, does not commit a new criminal offense; - 2) a person to whom a suspended sentence was given, during the probation period and within one year after the expiration of the probation period, does not commit a new criminal offense; - 3) a person who was sentenced to a fine, community service sentence or imprisonment up to six months, within three years from the date that sentence was served, became statute-barred or was remitted, does not commit a new criminal offense; - 4) a person who was sentenced to imprisonment over six months up to one year, within five years from the date that sentence was served, became statute-barred or was remitted, does not commit a new criminal offense. - (3) Statutory rehabilitation does not occur if an accessory punishment has not yet been served or if security measures are still in effect. - (1) Judicial rehabilitation may be granted to a person who was sentenced to imprisonment over one up to two years if within five years from the date that sentence was served, became statute-barred or was remitted, they do not commit a new criminal offense. - (2) A person who was sentenced to imprisonment over two up to three years may be granted judicial rehabilitation if within eight years from the date that sentence was served, became statute-barred or was remitted, they do not commit a new criminal offense. - (3) In the cases referred to in paragraphs 1 and 2 of this Article, the court shall grant rehabilitation if it finds that the convicted person by their conduct has merited rehabilitation and if, according to their means, they have compensated for the damage caused by the criminal offense, whereby the court is obligated to take into account all other circumstances relevant to granting rehabilitation, and especially the nature and significance of the offense. - (4) Judicial rehabilitation cannot be granted if an accessory punishment has not yet been served or if security measures are still in effect. Judicial Rehabilitation of a Person Who Has Been Convicted Multiple Times

A person who has been convicted multiple times may be granted rehabilitation by the court only if the conditions from Arts. 119 and 120 of this Code are met with respect to each criminal offense for which they were convicted. In assessing whether to grant rehabilitation in such a case, the court shall take into account the circumstances from Article 120 paragraph 3 of this Code. (2) In deciding on termination of a legal consequence of conviction, the court shall take into account the behavior of the convicted person after conviction, whether they compensated for the damage caused by the criminal offense and returned the pecuniary gain acquired by commission of the criminal offense, as well as other circumstances indicating the justification for termination of the legal consequence of conviction. (1) Unless otherwise provided in this Code, criminal prosecution cannot be undertaken when the following periods have elapsed:

- 1) twenty-five years from commission of a criminal offense for which long-term imprisonment may be imposed by law; - 2) twenty years from commission of a criminal offense for which imprisonment over fifteen years may be imposed by law; - 3) fifteen years from commission of a criminal offense for which imprisonment over ten years may be imposed by law; - 4) ten years from commission of a criminal offense for which imprisonment over five years may be imposed by law; - 5) five years from commission of a criminal offense for which imprisonment over three years may be imposed by law; - 6) three years from commission of a criminal offense for which imprisonment over one year may be imposed by law; - 7) two years from commission of a criminal offense for which imprisonment up to one year or a fine may be imposed by law. - (2) If multiple punishments are prescribed for a criminal offense, the statute of limitations is determined according to the most severe prescribed punishment. Course and Interruption of Statute of Limitations for Criminal Prosecution

(1) The statute of limitations for criminal prosecution begins from the day the criminal offense was committed. If a consequence of the criminal offense occurs later, the statute of limitations for criminal prosecution begins from the day the consequence occurred. (2) The statute of limitations does not run during the time when prosecution by law cannot be commenced or continued. (3) The statute of limitations for criminal prosecution for an offense committed against a minor does not run until that person reaches eighteen years of age. (4) The statute of limitations is interrupted by every procedural act undertaken to discover the criminal offense or to discover and prosecute the perpetrator for the committed criminal offense. (5) The statute of limitations is also interrupted when the perpetrator during the running of the limitation period commits an equally serious or more serious criminal offense. (6) With each interruption, the statute of limitations begins to run anew. (7) The statute of limitations for criminal prosecution occurs in any case when double the time required by law for the statute of limitations for criminal prosecution has elapsed. (1) Unless otherwise provided in this Code, an imposed sentence cannot be executed when the following periods have elapsed:

- 1) twenty years from sentencing to imprisonment over fifteen years; - 2) fifteen years from sentencing to imprisonment over ten years; - 3) ten years from sentencing to imprisonment over five years; - 4) five years from sentencing to imprisonment over three years; - 5) three years from sentencing to imprisonment over one year or community service sentence; - 6) two years from sentencing to imprisonment up to one year or to a fine. - (2) Execution of a sentence of long-term imprisonment is not subject to statute of limitations. Statute of Limitations for Execution of Accessory Punishment and Security Measure

(1) The statute of limitations for execution of a fine as an accessory punishment occurs when two years have elapsed from the date the judgment imposing that punishment becomes final. (2) The statute of limitations for execution of the security measure of mandatory psychiatric treatment in freedom, mandatory treatment of drug addicts, mandatory treatment of alcoholics and confiscation of objects occurs when five years have elapsed from the date the decision imposing those measures becomes final. (3) The statute of limitations for execution of the security measures of prohibition of performance of calling, activity and duty, prohibition of driving a motor vehicle and expulsion of a foreigner from the country occurs when the time for which those measures were imposed has elapsed. (4) Execution of the security measure of mandatory psychiatric treatment and custody in a health institution is not subject to statute of limitations, but if more than five years have elapsed from the finality of the judgment imposing the security measure and execution has not commenced, the court shall examine whether execution of this measure is still necessary. Course and Interruption of Statute of Limitations for Execution of Punishment and Security Measure

(1) The statute of limitations for execution of punishment begins from the day the judgment imposing the punishment became final, and if a suspended sentence was revoked - from the day the decision on revocation became final. (2) If by an act of amnesty or pardon or by court decision on extraordinary legal remedy an imposed sentence was reduced, the time required for occurrence of the statute of limitations is determined according to the new sentence, but the course of the statute of limitations is counted from the earlier final judgment. (3) The statute of limitations does not run during the time when execution of punishment by law cannot be undertaken. (4) The statute of limitations is interrupted by every act of the competent authority undertaken to execute the punishment. (5) With each interruption, the statute of limitations begins to run anew. (6) The statute of limitations for execution of punishment occurs in any case when double the time required by law for the statute of limitations for execution of punishment has elapsed. (7) In case of occurrence of the statute of limitations referred to in paragraph 6 of this Article, commenced execution of punishment is suspended. (8) The provisions of paragraphs 2 to 5 of this Article are applied mutatis mutandis to the statute of limitations for execution of security measures. Non-Application of Statute of Limitations for Criminal Prosecution and Execution of Punishment

Criminal prosecution and execution of punishment are not subject to statute of limitations for criminal offenses provided in Arts. 264 to 276b, 401, 401a, 422 to 424 and 426 to 431 of this Code, nor for criminal offenses for which according to ratified international treaties statute of limitations cannot occur. (1) Persons covered by an act of amnesty are granted release from criminal prosecution or full or partial release from execution of punishment, the imposed punishment is replaced with a milder punishment, rehabilitation is granted or individual or all legal consequences of conviction are eliminated. (2) Amnesty may eliminate the following security measures: prohibition of performance of calling, activity and duty, prohibition of driving a motor vehicle and expulsion of foreigners from the country. (1) By pardon a person specifically designated by name is granted release from criminal prosecution or full or partial release from execution of punishment, the imposed punishment is replaced with a milder punishment or suspended sentence, rehabilitation is granted, a shorter duration of a certain legal consequence of conviction is determined or individual or all legal consequences of conviction are eliminated. (2) Pardon may eliminate or determine a shorter duration of the security measures of prohibition of performance of calling, activity and duty, prohibition of driving a motor vehicle and expulsion of a foreigner from the country. Effect of Amnesty and Pardon on Rights of Third Parties

Granting of amnesty or pardon does not affect the rights of third parties based on the conviction. APPLICATION OF CRIMINAL LEGISLATION OF MONTENEGRO

Temporal Application of Criminal Legislation

(1) The law that was in force at the time of commission of the criminal offense is applied to the perpetrator of a criminal offense. (2) If after commission of the criminal offense the law was amended one or more times, the law that is most lenient for the perpetrator shall be applied. (3) The law with a specified temporal duration is applied to the perpetrator of a criminal offense that is defined by that law, regardless of when they are tried, unless that law provides otherwise. (4) Security measures and educational measures prescribed by a new law may be applied to the perpetrator if they are not more unfavorable for them than those that could have been applied under the law that was in force at the time of commission of the offense. Application of Criminal Legislation on the Territory of Montenegro

(1) Criminal legislation of Montenegro applies to everyone who commits a criminal offense on its territory. (2) Criminal legislation of Montenegro also applies to everyone who commits a criminal offense on a domestic vessel, regardless of where the vessel is located at the time of commission of the offense. (3) Criminal legislation of Montenegro also applies to everyone who commits a criminal offense in a domestic civil aircraft or in a domestic military aircraft, regardless of where the aircraft was located at the time of commission of the criminal offense. Application of Criminal Legislation of Montenegro to Perpetrators of Certain Criminal Offenses Committed Abroad Criminal legislation of Montenegro applies to everyone who commits abroad a criminal offense from Arts. 357 to 369, Arts. 371 to 374 and Arts. 447 to 449b of this Code or from Arts. 258 and 268 of this Code if the counterfeiting relates to money that at the time of commission of the criminal offense was legal tender in Montenegro. Application of Criminal Legislation of Montenegro to a Citizen of Montenegro Who Commits a Criminal Offense Abroad Criminal legislation of Montenegro applies to a citizen of Montenegro even when they commit abroad any other criminal offense, except criminal offenses listed in Article 135 of this Code, if they are found on the territory of Montenegro or are extradited to it. Under the conditions from paragraph 1 of this Article, criminal legislation of Montenegro also applies to a perpetrator who became a citizen of Montenegro after commission of the criminal offense

Application of Criminal Legislation of Montenegro to a Foreigner Who Commits a Criminal Offense Abroad

(1) Criminal legislation of Montenegro also applies to a person who is not a citizen of Montenegro who outside the territory of Montenegro commits against it or its citizen a criminal offense when the criminal offenses listed in Article 135 of this Code are not in question, or commits a criminal offense from Arts. 276a, 276b, 422, 422a, 423 and 424 of this Code in the commission of which a citizen of Montenegro was involved in any way, if they are found on the territory of Montenegro or Serbia or are extradited to SCG. (2) Criminal legislation of Montenegro also applies to a person who is not a citizen of Montenegro who commits abroad against a foreign state or against a foreigner a criminal offense for which under the law of the country in which it was committed imprisonment of four years or a more severe punishment may be imposed, if they are found on the territory of Montenegro and are not extradited to a foreign state. Unless otherwise provided by this Code, the court in such a case may not impose a more severe punishment than that prescribed by the law of the country in which the criminal offense was committed. Special Conditions for Criminal Prosecution

(1) If in the case referred to in Article 134 of this Code criminal proceedings were commenced or completed in a foreign state, criminal prosecution in Montenegro shall be undertaken only with approval of the Supreme State Prosecutor of Montenegro. (2) In the case referred to in Article 134 of this Code, criminal prosecution of a foreigner may, subject to reciprocity, be transferred to a foreign state. (3) In the cases referred to in Arts. 136 and 137 of this Code, criminal prosecution shall not be undertaken if:

- 1) the perpetrator fully served the sentence to which they were sentenced abroad; - 2) the perpetrator was acquitted abroad by a final judgment or the sentence became statute-barred or was remitted; - 3) an appropriate security measure was executed abroad against the non-accountable perpetrator; - 4) under foreign law the criminal offense is prosecuted at the request of the injured party and such request was not filed. - (4) In the cases referred to in Arts. 136 and 137 of this Code, criminal prosecution shall be undertaken only when the criminal offense is punishable also under the law of the country in which the offense was committed, except for criminal offenses from Arts. 268, 276a, 276b, 422, 422a, 423 and 424 of this Code. When in the cases referred to in Arts. 136 and 137 paragraph 1 of this Code, under the law of the country in which the offense was committed that criminal offense is not punishable, criminal prosecution may be undertaken only with approval of the Supreme State Prosecutor. - (5) In the case referred to in Article 137 paragraph 2 of this Code, if the offense in question at the time it was committed was considered a criminal offense according to general legal principles recognized in international law, prosecution may be undertaken in Montenegro with approval of the Supreme State Prosecutor, regardless of the law of the country in which the criminal offense was committed. Crediting of Detention and Sentence Served Abroad

Detention, any other deprivation of liberty in connection with the criminal offense, deprivation of liberty during extradition proceedings, as well as a sentence that the perpetrator served pursuant to a judgment of a foreign court shall be credited toward the sentence imposed by a domestic court for the same criminal offense, and if the sentences are not of the same type, crediting shall be performed at the discretion of the court. Application of the General Part of This Code

The provisions of the general part of this Code apply to all criminal offenses defined by this Code or other law. Meaning of Terms in This Code

(1) The territory of Montenegro means the land territory, territorial sea and water surfaces within its borders, as well as the airspace above them. (2) Criminal legislation of Montenegro means this Code as well as all criminal law provisions contained in other laws of Montenegro. (3) An official is considered to be:

- 1) a person who performs official duties in a state authority; - 2) an elected, appointed or designated person in a state authority, local self-government authority or a person who permanently or temporarily performs official duties or official functions in those authorities; - 3) a person in an institution, company or other entity entrusted with exercise of public powers, who decides on rights, obligations or interests of natural or legal persons or on public interest; - 4) another person who performs official duties on the basis of law, regulations adopted on the basis of law, contract or arbitration agreement, as well as a person to whom exercise of certain official duties or tasks is factually entrusted; - 5) a military person except when provisions of Chapter Thirty-Six of this Code are in question; 5a) a person who in a foreign state performs a legislative, executive, judicial or other public function for a foreign state, a person who in a foreign state performs official duties on the basis of law, regulations adopted on the basis of law, contract or arbitration agreement, a person who performs an official duty in an international public organization and a person who performs a judicial, prosecutorial or some other function in an international court. (4) A responsible person is considered to be the owner of a company or other entity or a person in a company, institution or other entity to whom, considering their function, invested assets or on the basis of authorization, a certain range of tasks in management of property, production or other activity or in performance of supervision over them is entrusted or to whom performance of certain tasks is factually entrusted. A responsible person is also considered to be an official when criminal offenses are in question in which the perpetrator is designated as a responsible person and are not provided in this Code in the chapter on criminal offenses against official duty, that is, as criminal offenses of an official. (5) A military person is considered to be: a professional military person (contract soldier, non-commissioned officer, contract non-commissioned officer, officer and contract officer), a person in reserve composition (reserve soldier, reserve non-commissioned officer and reserve officer), a civilian person performing a certain military duty and a person who during a state of war or state of emergency is subject to military obligation. (6) When an official, responsible person or military person is designated as perpetrator of certain criminal offenses, the persons listed in paragraphs 3, 4 and 5 of this Article may be perpetrators of those offenses, unless from the elements of a particular offense or from a particular regulation it follows that the perpetrator may be only one of those persons. (7) A child is considered to be a person who has not reached fourteen years of age. (8) A juvenile is considered to be a person who has reached fourteen years of age but has not reached eighteen years of age. (9) A minor is considered to be a person who has not reached eighteen years of age. (10) A perpetrator is considered to be a principal, co-principal, instigator and accomplice. (11) A victim is a person to whom physical or mental pain or suffering, material damage or violation of human rights and freedoms was caused by an unlawful act prescribed by law as a criminal offense. (12) Pecuniary gain acquired by a criminal offense is considered to be pecuniary gain acquired by a criminal offense, directly or indirectly, consisting of any increase or prevention of decrease of property that occurred by commission of the criminal offense, property for which pecuniary gain acquired by a criminal offense was exchanged or with which it was united or into which it was converted, as well as any other gain obtained from pecuniary gain acquired by a criminal offense or property for which pecuniary gain acquired by a criminal offense was exchanged or into which it was converted, regardless of whether it is located on the territory of Montenegro or outside it, as well as all documents proving any property right. (13) A bribe, within the meaning of this Code, is a gift or other unlawful material or non-material benefit, regardless of value. (14) Force is also considered to be use of hypnosis or stupefying agents with the aim of bringing someone against their will into an unconscious state or incapacitating them for resistance. (15) Elections are considered to be elections for the Assembly of Montenegro, the President of Montenegro, local self-government authorities and other elections that are called and held on the basis of the Constitution and law. (15a) Political subjects are considered to be subjects in accordance with the regulation governing financing of political subjects and election campaigns. (15b) An election campaign is considered to be a campaign in accordance with the regulation governing financing of political subjects and election campaigns. (16) A referendum is considered to be a vote of citizens in which decisions are made on issues for which this is determined by the Constitution or law. (17) Narcotic drugs are considered to be substances and preparations that are declared to be narcotic drugs by regulations based on law. (18) A movable thing is also considered to be any produced or collected energy for providing light, heat or motion, a telephone pulse, as well as computer data and computer program. (19) A computer system is considered to be any device or group of interconnected or interdependent devices, of which one or more, depending on the program, performs automatic data processing. (20) Computer data is considered to be any presentation of facts, data or concepts in a form suitable for processing in a computer system, including programs by means of which a computer system performs its functions. (21) A computer program is considered to be a set of ordered computer data on the basis of which a computer system performs its functions. (22) A computer virus is a computer program that endangers or changes the functions of a computer system and changes, endangers or unlawfully uses computer data. (23) Computer traffic data is considered to be all computer data generated by computer systems that form a chain of communication between two computer systems that are communicating including themselves. (24) A protected natural asset is also considered to be an asset that under regulations on protection of natural assets enjoys preliminary protection. (25) A cultural asset is also considered to be an asset that under regulations on protection of cultural assets enjoys preliminary protection, part of a cultural asset and protected surroundings of an immovable cultural asset. (26) Money is metal and paper money or money made from some other material that on the basis of law is in circulation in Montenegro or in a foreign state. (27) Signs of value are also considered to be foreign signs of value. (28) A motor vehicle is considered to be any means of transport with motor propulsion in land, water and air traffic. (29) A document is considered to be any object that is suitable or designated to serve as proof of some fact that has significance for legal relations, as well as computer data. (30) A file, letter, shipment and document may also be in electronic form. (31) Members of a family or family community are also considered to be former spouses, blood relatives and relatives from full adoption in direct line without limitation, and in collateral line up to and including the fourth degree, relatives from incomplete adoption, affinal relatives up to and including the second degree, persons living in the same family household and persons who have a common child or a child is on the way to being born although they never lived in the same family household. (32) The expression "shall not be punished" means that in that case there is no criminal offense. (33) When an act of a criminal offense is defined by a continuous verb, the offense is considered to have been committed if the act was performed once or more times. Whoever by violation of law or in another unlawful manner prevents or obstructs candidacy in elections shall be punished by a fine or imprisonment up to one year. (1) Whoever by force or threat compels another or otherwise unlawfully influences them to exercise or not exercise the right to vote in elections or in a referendum or to vote for or against a certain candidate, electoral list or proposal shall be punished by a fine or imprisonment up to three years. (1) Whoever by force, threat or in another unlawful manner prevents or obstructs the holding of voting at a polling place shall be punished by imprisonment up to three years. (1) A crew member or passenger of a private ship or private aircraft who commits violence or piracy on the high seas or in a place not under the jurisdiction of any state against persons on another ship or aircraft or detains, seizes, damages or destroys another ship or aircraft or goods located on them or causes damage exceeding the amount of forty thousand euros, shall be punished by imprisonment from three to fifteen years.

Failure to Provide Assistance to a Person Injured in a Traffic Accident

(1) A driver of a motor vehicle or other means of transport who leaves without assistance a person who was injured by that means of transport or whose injury was caused by that means, shall be punished by a fine or imprisonment up to two years.

(2) If serious bodily injury to the injured person has resulted from the failure to provide assistance, the perpetrator shall be punished by a fine or imprisonment up to three years.

(3) If death of the injured person has resulted from the failure to provide assistance, the perpetrator shall be punished by imprisonment from three months to four years.

CRIMINAL OFFENCES AGAINST HUMANITY AND OTHER GOODS PROTECTED BY INTERNATIONAL LAW

Whoever, with the intention to wholly or partially destroy a national, ethnic, racial or religious group, orders the commission of killings or serious bodily injuries or serious impairment of the physical or mental health of members of the group or that the group be placed in such living conditions that lead to the complete or partial extermination of the group or that measures be applied to prevent births among members of the group or that forcible transfer of children to another group be carried out or whoever commits any of the stated acts with the same intention, shall be punished by imprisonment of at least eight years or a penalty of long-term imprisonment. Whoever, in violation of the rules of international law, as part of a widespread or systematic attack directed against the civilian population, orders: the commission of killings; placing the population or a part thereof in such living conditions that lead to their complete or partial extermination; enslavement; forcible displacement; torture; rape; coercion into prostitution; coercion into pregnancy or sterilisation in order to change the ethnic composition of the population; persecution or expulsion on political, religious, racial, national, ethnic, cultural, gender or any other grounds; imprisonment or abduction of persons without providing information about this in order to deprive them of legal protection; oppression of a racial group or establishment of the dominance of one such group over another; or other similar inhumane acts by which serious suffering is intentionally caused or health is seriously endangered or whoever commits any of the stated acts, shall be punished by imprisonment of at least five years or a penalty of long-term imprisonment. War crime against the civilian population

(1) Whoever, in violation of the rules of international law during war, armed conflict or occupation, orders an attack on the civilian population, settlement, individual civilians, persons incapacitated for combat or on members or facilities of humanitarian organisations or peace missions; an indiscriminate attack by which the civilian population or civilian objects under special protection of international law are hit; an attack on military targets knowing that it will cause casualties among the civilian population or infliction of damage to civilian objects that is in obvious disproportion to the expected military effect; that bodily injuries, torture, inhumane treatment, biological, medical or other scientific experiments, removal of tissue or organs for transplantation be carried out on the civilian population or that other actions be carried out that impair health or cause great suffering or orders displacement or resettlement or forcible denationalisation or conversion to another religion; coercion into prostitution or rape; application of measures of intimidation and terror, taking of hostages, collective punishment, unlawful deprivation of liberty and imprisonment; deprivation of the right to a proper and impartial trial; declaration of the rights and actions of citizens of the enemy state as prohibited, suspended or inadmissible in court proceedings; coercion into service in the armed forces of the enemy power or in its intelligence service or administration; coercion into service in the armed forces of persons younger than eighteen years; coercion into forced labour; starvation of the population; unlawful seizure, appropriation or destruction of the property of the population on a large scale that is not justified by military necessity; taking of unlawful and disproportionately large contributions and requisitions; reduction of the value of domestic money or unlawful issuance of money or whoever commits any of the stated acts, shall be punished by imprisonment of at least five years. (2) The penalty from paragraph 1 of this article shall also be imposed on whoever, in violation of the rules of international law during war, armed conflict or occupation, orders: an attack on objects specially protected by international law and objects and installations with dangerous force such as dams, embankments and nuclear power plants; that civilian objects under special protection of international law, undefended places and demilitarised zones be targeted; long-term and large-scale damage to the environment that can harm the health or survival of the population or whoever commits any of the stated acts. (3) Whoever, during war, armed conflict or occupation, orders that killings be carried out against the civilian population or whoever commits such an act shall be punished by imprisonment of at least ten years or a penalty of long-term imprisonment. (4) Whoever, in violation of the rules of international law during war, armed conflict or occupation, as an occupier, orders or carries out the transfer of parts of their civilian population to the occupied territory shall be punished by imprisonment of at least five years. (5) Whoever threatens to commit one or more acts from paragraphs 1 and 2 of this article shall be punished by imprisonment from six months to five years. War crime against the wounded and sick

(1) Whoever, in violation of the rules of international law during war or armed conflict, orders that bodily injuries, torture, inhumane treatment, biological, medical or other scientific experiments, removal of tissue or organs for transplantation or other actions that impair health or cause great suffering be carried out against the wounded, sick, shipwrecked or medical or religious personnel or orders the unlawful destruction or appropriation on a large scale of material, means of medical transport and supplies of medical institutions or units that is not justified by military necessity or whoever commits any of the stated acts, shall be punished by imprisonment of at least five years. (2) Whoever, during war, armed conflict or occupation, orders that killings be carried out against the wounded or sick or whoever commits such an act shall be punished by imprisonment of at least ten years or a penalty of long-term imprisonment. War crime against prisoners of war

(1) Whoever, in violation of the rules of international law, orders that bodily injuries, torture, inhumane treatment, biological, medical or other scientific experiments, removal of tissue or organs for transplantation or other actions that impair health or cause great suffering be carried out against prisoners of war or orders coercion into performing service in the armed forces of the enemy or deprivation of the right to a proper and impartial trial; or whoever commits any of the stated acts, shall be punished by imprisonment of at least five years. (2) Whoever, during war, armed conflict or occupation, orders that killings be carried out against prisoners of war or whoever commits such an act shall be punished by imprisonment of at least ten years or a penalty of long-term imprisonment. Organising and inciting the commission of genocide and war crimes

(1) Whoever agrees with another to commit any of the criminal offences from Articles 426 to 430 of this Code shall be punished by imprisonment from three months to three years. (2) Whoever organises a group for the commission of criminal offences from paragraph 1 of this article shall be punished by imprisonment from five to fifteen years. (3) Whoever becomes a member of a group from paragraph 1 of this article shall be punished by imprisonment from one to eight years. (4) The perpetrator of the act from paragraphs 1 and 3 of this article who discloses the agreement or group before committing a criminal offence as part of it or for it, or the perpetrator of the act from paragraph 2 of this article who prevents the commission of the act from paragraph 1 of this article may be punished more leniently. (5) Whoever calls for or incites the commission of criminal offences from Articles 426 to 430 of this Code shall be punished by imprisonment from two to ten years. Use of prohibited means of combat

(1) Whoever, during war or armed conflict, orders the use of combat means or methods of combat that are prohibited by the rules of international law or uses them themselves shall be punished by imprisonment from two to ten years. (2) If several persons were killed as a result of the act from paragraph 1 of this article, the perpetrator shall be punished by imprisonment of at least five years or a penalty of long-term imprisonment. Unlawful killing and wounding of the enemy

(1) Whoever, in violation of the rules of international law during war or armed conflict, kills or wounds an enemy who has laid down arms or surrendered unconditionally or has no means of defence shall be punished by imprisonment from one to fifteen years. (3) If the killing from paragraph 1 of this article was committed in a cruel manner or from greed or if several persons were killed, the perpetrator shall be punished by imprisonment of at least ten years or a penalty of long-term imprisonment. (4) The penalty from paragraph 3 of this article shall also be imposed on whoever, in violation of the rules of international law during war or armed conflict, orders that in combat there must be no survivors among the enemy or conducts combat against the enemy on that basis. (1) Whoever orders the unlawful seizure of property from the killed or wounded on the battlefield or whoever carries out such seizure shall be punished by imprisonment from one to five years. (1) A citizen of Montenegro who during war or armed conflict assists the enemy in carrying out requisition, seizure of food or other goods, or in implementing any coercive measures against the population shall be punished by imprisonment from two to ten years. (2) The punishment from paragraph 1 of this article shall also be imposed on a citizen of Montenegro who during war politically or economically collaborates with the enemy. (3) If the act from paragraphs 1 and 2 of this article resulted in the death of one or more persons or caused danger to human life or was accompanied by severe violence or great destruction or led to endangering the security of the economic or military strength of the country, the perpetrator shall be punished by imprisonment of at least ten years. (1) A military person who during combat or immediately before combat fails to perform their duty and as a result harmful consequences occur for the military unit or for the combat situation shall be punished by imprisonment from two to ten years. (2) The punishment from paragraph 1 of this article shall also be imposed on a military person who during combat or immediately before combat arbitrarily or fraudulently abandons their duty. (3) If serious consequences have occurred as a result of the acts from paragraphs 1 and 2 of this article, the perpetrator shall be punished by imprisonment of at least ten years. Abandoning Position Contrary to Orders

(1) A military commander who contrary to orders abandons a position with the unit entrusted to him, before having exhausted all possibilities of defence, shall be punished by imprisonment from two to twelve years. (2) If serious consequences have occurred as a result of the act from paragraph 1 of this article, the perpetrator shall be punished by imprisonment of at least ten years. Abandoning Damaged Ship and Aircraft Prematurely

(1) The commander of a warship who during war or armed conflict abandons a damaged ship before having performed his duty in accordance with regulations on ship service shall be punished by imprisonment from two to ten years. (2) A crew member of a warship who during war or armed conflict abandons a damaged ship before the ship's commander has issued an order to abandon, or a crew member of a military aircraft who during war or armed conflict abandons a damaged military aircraft before having performed his duty in accordance with regulations on flying and use of aircraft, shall be punished by imprisonment from one to eight years. (3) If serious consequences have occurred as a result of the acts from paragraphs 1 and 2 of this article, the perpetrator shall be punished for the act from paragraph 1 of this article by imprisonment of at least ten years, and for the act from paragraph 2 of this article by imprisonment from two to ten years. (1) Whoever immediately before combat or during combat by causing discontent among military persons, spreading disturbing news, fleeing, throwing away weapons or ammunition, causing or spreading fear, or in any other way weakens combat morale in the unit or causes damage to the combat situation shall be punished by imprisonment from two to twelve years. (2) A military commander who does not take necessary measures against a subordinate or junior who during combat or immediately before combat spreads fear among soldiers or in any other way weakens the combat morale of the unit or causes damage to the combat situation shall be punished by imprisonment from one to eight years. (3) If serious consequences have occurred as a result of the acts from paragraphs 1 and 2 of this article, the perpetrator shall be punished by imprisonment of at least ten years. Unauthorized Access to Military Facility

(1) Whoever unauthorizedly enters a military facility or makes sketches or drawings of military facilities or combat equipment or photographs them or records them in any other way, or publishes recordings although knowing this is prohibited, shall be punished by imprisonment up to three years. (2) If the act from paragraph 1 of this article was committed during a state of war, armed conflict or state of emergency, the perpetrator shall be punished by imprisonment from one to eight years. (1) A military person who during a state of war, armed conflict or state of emergency does not inform a superior, senior officer or military command of an event that obviously requires urgent military measures shall be punished by imprisonment up to three years. (2) If serious consequences have occurred as a result of the act from paragraph 1 of this article, the perpetrator shall be punished by imprisonment from two to ten years. Criminal Offence Committed by Order of a Superior

A subordinate shall not be punished if he commits a criminal offence by order of a superior which relates to official duty, unless the order related to the commission of a criminal offence for which a punishment exceeding five years of imprisonment may be imposed, and the subordinate knew that the execution of the order constitutes a criminal offence. If, before the date of entry into force of this code, an educational measure of enhanced supervision in another family was imposed on a minor, the court which tried the minor in the first instance shall replace that measure with another for which legal conditions exist, but which may not, either by type or duration, be more severe than the imposed measure. On the date of commencement of application of this code, all criminal law provisions contained in other laws that are in conflict with this code shall cease to be valid. This code shall enter into force on the eighth day from the date of publication in the "Official Gazette of the Republic of Montenegro", and shall be applied three months after the date of entry into force.
