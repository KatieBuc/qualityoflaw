## CHAPTER I - GENERAL PROVISIONS

Activities aimed at the prevention, early detection, care and eradication of domestic violence are declared to be of general interest. The provisions of this law are of public order.

Domestic violence constitutes any action or omission, direct or indirect, which by any means impairs, unlawfully limiting the free exercise or enjoyment of a person's human rights, caused by another with whom one has or has had a dating relationship or with whom one has or has had an affective relationship based on cohabitation and originated by kinship, by marriage or by de facto union.

The following are manifestations of domestic violence, whether or not they constitute a crime:

- A) Physical violence. Action, omission or pattern of conduct that harms a person's bodily integrity.
- B) Psychological or emotional violence. Any action or omission aimed at disturbing, degrading or controlling a person's conduct, behavior, beliefs or decisions, through humiliation, intimidation, isolation or any other means that affects psychological or emotional stability.
- C) Sexual violence. Any action that imposes or induces sexual behaviors on a person through the use of: force, intimidation, coercion, manipulation, threat or any other means that nullifies or limits sexual freedom.
- D) Patrimonial violence. Any action or omission that with manifest illegitimacy implies damage, loss, transformation, subtraction, destruction, distraction, concealment or retention of goods, work instruments, documents or economic resources, intended to coerce the self-determination of another person.

CHAPTER II - JURISDICTION AND COMPETENCE

Courts with competence in family matters shall also have jurisdiction over non-criminal matters of domestic violence and over personal or patrimonial matters arising therefrom.

Courts and Prosecutors' Offices with competence in family matters shall also be competent to attend to urgent situations of domestic violence. To this end, the Supreme Court of Justice and the Ministry of Education and Culture, upon proposal of the Court Prosecutor's Office, shall determine, as appropriate, the shift regime to attend, during working and non-working hours and days, all matters requiring their intervention in accordance with this law.

Justices of the Peace, in the interior of the Republic, regardless of their category, shall have urgent competence to understand matters of domestic violence, being able to provisionally order the pertinent measures established in this law for the protection of alleged victims, and must refer the matters to the corresponding Court of First Instance, necessarily within forty-eight hours of having learned of the facts, whose resolution shall be binding.

All judicial proceedings in matters of domestic violence shall be mandatorily notified to the corresponding Prosecutor, from the beginning. The Prosecutor must intervene in all matters relating to the persons and interests of victims of domestic violence.

## CHAPTER III - STANDING OF THE COMPLAINANT AND SUMMONING OF THIRD PARTIES TO TRIAL

Any person who becomes aware of an act of domestic violence may give notice to the competent Judge in the matter, who must adopt the measures deemed pertinent in accordance with the provisions of this law.

Provided that the notice presents plausibility, no liability of any kind shall fall upon whoever has given it.

The Judge, ex officio or at the request of the Public Ministry, may summon third parties to the trial.

CHAPTER IV - PROTECTIVE MEASURES

In all matters of domestic violence, in addition to the measures provided for in article 316 of the General Process Code, the Judge, ex officio, at the request of a party or the Public Ministry, must order all measures aimed at protecting the life, physical or emotional integrity of the victim, personal freedom and security, as well as the economic assistance and patrimonial integrity of the family unit. The Supreme Court of Justice may provide for the use of expert action protocols that it shall regulate, for the purposes of detecting and qualifying situations of domestic violence. The courts may order their urgent use, prior to the adoption of the measures referred to in the following article. (*)

For these purposes, the following measures, or similar ones, may be adopted to fulfill the precautionary purpose: 1) Order the removal of the aggressor from the common residence and the immediate delivery of their personal effects in the presence of the Bailiff. Likewise, a judicial inventory shall be made of the movable property that is removed and of that which remains at the place, and testimony may be issued at the request of the parties. 2) Order the return to the domicile or residence of the victim who has left it for reasons of personal security, in the presence of the Bailiff. 3) Prohibit, restrict or limit the presence of the aggressor at the domicile or residence, places of work, study or other places frequented by the victim. 4) Prohibit the aggressor from communicating, relating, interviewing or developing any similar conduct in relation to the victim, other affected persons, witnesses or complainants of the act. 5) Seize the weapons that the aggressor has in their possession, which shall remain in custody of the Court, in the manner it deems pertinent. Prohibit the aggressor from using or possessing firearms, notifying the competent authority for such purposes. 6) Set a provisional maintenance obligation in favor of the victim. 7) Order the mandatory attendance of the aggressor to rehabilitation programs. 8) Likewise, if appropriate, provisionally resolve everything related to maintenance and, where applicable, custody, possession and visits. In the event that the Judge decides not to adopt any measure, their resolution must express the grounds for such determination. (*)

In all cases, the Judge shall order the Bailiff or whoever deemed appropriate, to supervise compliance and shall convene a hearing, within a period not exceeding ten days from the adoption of the measure, for the purposes of its evaluation. In case of non-appearance, the Judge shall order the conduct of the aggressor. If the measures ordered are not complied with, the Judge shall order the arrest of the aggressor for a maximum period of forty-eight hours, without prejudice to the provisions of articles 21.3, 374.1, 374.2 and 374.4 of the General Process Code. Once the precautionary measure has been adopted and the referred hearing has been held, the file must be remitted to the Court that had been hearing the proceedings related to the family involved. The procedure for the adoption of precautionary measures shall be as provided by articles 313, 314 and 315 of the General Process Code. Whenever it is proven that a right intrinsic to the human being is violated or threatened, the Judge must immediately decree the corresponding precautionary measures, in a reasoned manner. Likewise, it shall proceed when the prior hearing of the aggressor may frustrate the successful outcome of the measure.

In evidentiary matters, the provisions of the General Process Code shall apply, bearing in mind the objective and purpose of this law and the provisions contained in the following articles.

Once the precautionary measures established in article 10 of this law have been adopted, the Court shall ex officio order a situational diagnosis among the subjects involved. It shall be prepared in an interdisciplinary manner and shall have the purpose of determining the physical or psychological damages suffered by the victim, evaluating the situation of danger or risk and the social environment. This diagnosis must be available to the Court at the time of the hearing set in article 11 of this law. If, due to the characteristics of the situation, it is considered necessary to adopt medical, psychological or other measures or treatments with respect to any of the subjects involved, the Court may entrust their implementation to one of the public or private institutions competent in the matter. For the purposes of complying with the provisions of the preceding article, the Ministry of Education and Culture, through the National Institute of Family and Women, shall promote the training of experts in domestic violence, with interdisciplinary work capacity, who shall be incorporated into the orbit of the Forensic Technical Institute. The corresponding regulations shall entrust the National Institute of Family and Women to establish the requirements that applicants must meet to accredit their expert competence in the area of domestic violence regulated by this law.

The Supreme Court of Justice shall incorporate this category of professionals into the Single Registry of Experts. It shall also incorporate into this Registry those who accredit before the Ministry of Education and Culture -which shall have for this purpose the collaboration of the University of the Republic or authorized Universities- notorious competence in the matter at the time of entry into force of this law.

In all cases, the guiding principle shall be to prevent secondary victimization, prohibiting confrontation or joint appearance of the victim and the aggressor in the case of children and adolescents under 18 years of age. In the case of the adult victim who requires such confrontation and it is certified that they are in condition to carry it out, it may be conducted. The Court shall provide the form and technical means to receive the statement, applying the principles of immediacy, concentration and contradiction. It may, where appropriate, previously request that the interdisciplinary team report whether the victim is in condition to be questioned at that time.

Situations of domestic violence must be evaluated from the perspective of comprehensive protection of human dignity. Likewise, it shall be especially considered that the acts constituting domestic violence to be proven constitute, in general, situations linked to the privacy of the home, knowledge of which resides in the core of persons affected by the acts of violence.

## CHAPTER V - MANDATORY LEGAL ASSISTANCE

The Supreme Court of Justice must guarantee mandatory legal assistance to the victim, for which it shall be empowered to enter into agreements with public or private entities specialized in the matter. (*)

## CHAPTER VI - COORDINATION OF PROCEEDINGS

When a Court with jurisdiction in criminal matters or a Court with jurisdiction in matters of minors intervenes in a situation of domestic violence, whatever resolution it adopts, it must remit, within forty-eight hours of having become aware of the facts, complete testimony of the proceedings and the resolution adopted to the Judge with jurisdiction in matters of domestic violence. Likewise, when indictment with imprisonment has been ordered, it must communicate the release or granting of temporary leaves or any form of conclusion of the process to the competent Court in matters of domestic violence, prior to its effectuation. It must also bring it to the knowledge of the victim at their actual domicile and their attorney at the constituted domicile, in the latter case if it is within the knowledge of the Court, in the manner it deems most effective to obtain the protection purpose pursued by this law.

In the same manner, Courts with urgent jurisdiction in matters of domestic violence shall communicate the facts with criminal appearance that have come to their knowledge, within twenty-four hours, to the Criminal Court on Duty.

The same obligation is provided for representatives of the Public Ministry among themselves.

CHAPTER VII - PREVENTION OF DOMESTIC VIOLENCE AND PROMOTION OF COMPREHENSIVE CARE FOR THE VICTIM

The State must adopt all necessary measures to prevent, sanction and eradicate domestic violence and promote comprehensive support for the victim.

The rehabilitation and social reintegration of the aggressor must form part of a policy that seeks to protect all related persons. Assistance and treatment must be instruments of this policy.
