CRIMINAL CODE

OF THE REPUBLIC OF LITHUANIA

GENERAL PART

## CHAPTER I

GENERAL PROVISIONS

### Article 1. Purpose of the Criminal Code of the Republic of Lithuania

1. The Criminal Code of the Republic of Lithuania is a unified criminal law, the purpose of which is to protect human and citizen rights and freedoms, the interests of society and the state from criminal acts by means of criminal law.

2. This Code:

- 1) defines which acts are crimes and criminal misdemeanours and prohibits them;
- 2) establishes punishments, measures of criminal and educational influence for acts provided for in this Code, and compulsory medical measures;
- 3) establishes the grounds and conditions of criminal liability, as well as the grounds and conditions under which persons who have committed criminal acts may be released from criminal liability or punishment.
  - 3. The provisions of this Code are harmonised with the provisions of the European Union legal acts referred to in the Annex to this Code.

### Article 2. Fundamental provisions of criminal liability

1. A person is held liable under this Code only if the act committed by him was prohibited by the criminal law in force at the time the criminal act was committed.

2. Ignorance of the law does not release from criminal liability.

3. A person is held liable under the criminal law only if he is guilty of committing a criminal act and only if at the time of commission of the act it was possible to demand from him conduct complying with the law.

4. Only a person whose act constitutes the corpus delicti of a crime or criminal misdemeanour provided for by the criminal law is held liable under the criminal law.

5. Punishments, measures of criminal or educational influence and compulsory medical measures are imposed only in accordance with the law.

6. No one may be punished for the same criminal act twice.

## CHAPTER II

APPLICATION OF THE CRIMINAL LAW

### Article 3. Temporal application of the criminal law

1. The criminality of an act and the punishability of a person shall be determined by the criminal law in force at the time of commission of that act. The time of commission of a criminal act is the time of action (inaction) or the time of occurrence of the consequences provided for by the criminal law, if the person intended the consequences to occur at a different time.

2. A criminal law that eliminates the criminality of an act, mitigates the punishment or otherwise alleviates the legal situation of the person who committed a criminal act has retroactive effect, i.e. applies to persons who committed a criminal act before such law entered into force, as well as to persons serving a sentence and persons having a criminal record.

3. A criminal law that establishes the criminality of an act, makes the punishment more severe or otherwise aggravates the legal situation of the person who committed a criminal act does not have retroactive effect. An exception is constituted by the provisions of this Code establishing liability for genocide (Article 99), conduct with persons prohibited by international law (Article 100), killing of persons protected by international humanitarian law (Article 101), deportation or transfer of civilians (Article 102), injury, torture or other inhuman treatment of persons protected by international humanitarian law or violation of protection of their property (Article 103), forced use of civilians or prisoners of war in the enemy's armed forces (Article 105), destruction of protected objects or pillage of national values (Article 106), aggression (Article 110), prohibited war attack (Article 111), use of prohibited means of warfare (Article 112), negligent performance of commander's duties (Article 1131).

4. The measures of criminal or educational influence and compulsory medical measures to be imposed are those provided for by the criminal law in force at the time of adoption of the court's decision. Extended confiscation of property is imposed only if it was provided for by the criminal law in force at the time of commission of the prohibited act under this Code.

### Article 4. Application of the criminal law to persons who committed criminal

acts in the territory of the State of Lithuania or in vessels or aircraft bearing

the flag or identification marks of the State of Lithuania

1. Persons who committed criminal acts in the territory of the State of Lithuania or in vessels or aircraft bearing the flag or identification marks of the State of Lithuania are held liable under this Code.

2. The place of commission of a criminal act is the place where the person acted or had to and could act, or the place where the consequences provided for by the criminal law occurred. The place of commission of a criminal act by accomplices is the place where this act was committed, and if one of the accomplices acted in another place – the place of his action.

3. Where the same criminal act was committed both in the territory of the State of Lithuania and abroad, it shall be deemed that this act was committed in the territory of the State of Lithuania if it was started, completed or interrupted in this territory.

4. The issue of criminal liability of persons who, in accordance with the norms of international law, enjoy immunity from criminal jurisdiction and who committed a criminal act in the territory of the State of Lithuania shall be resolved in accordance with the international agreements of the Republic of Lithuania and this Code.

### Article 5. Criminal liability of citizens of the Republic of Lithuania and other persons permanently

residing in Lithuania for crimes committed abroad

Citizens of the Republic of Lithuania and other persons permanently residing in Lithuania are held liable under this Code for crimes committed abroad.

### Article 6. Criminal liability of foreigners for crimes committed abroad

against the State of Lithuania

Foreigners who do not have a permanent place of residence in the Republic of Lithuania are held liable under the criminal law if they committed abroad crimes against the State of Lithuania provided for in Articles 114–128 of this Code.

### 7 straipsnis. Criminal liability for crimes provided for in international

treaties

Persons are liable under this Code, regardless of their citizenship and place of residence, as well as the place of commission of the crime and whether the act is punishable under the laws of the place of commission of the crime, when they commit crimes for which liability is provided for on the basis of international treaties:

- 1) crimes against humanity and war crimes (Articles 99–113¹);
- 2) trafficking in human beings (Article 147);
- 3) purchase or sale of a child (Article 157);
- 4) manufacture, possession or distribution of counterfeit money or securities (Article 213);
- 5) legalization of criminally obtained property (Article 216);
- 6) taking bribes (Article 225);
- 7) trading in influence (Article 226);
- 8) giving bribes (Article 227);
- 9) piracy (Article 251¹);
- 10) terrorist and terrorism-related crimes (Article 252¹ paragraphs 1 and 2);
- 11) unlawful conduct with nuclear or radioactive materials or other sources of ionizing radiation (Articles 256, 256¹ and 257);
- 12) crimes related to handling narcotic or psychotropic, poisonous or potent substances (Articles 259–269);
- 13) crimes against the environment (Articles 270, 270¹, 270², 270³, 271, 272, 274).

### 8 straipsnis. Criminal liability for crimes committed abroad

1. A person who has committed abroad crimes provided for in Articles 5 and 6 of this Code is liable under criminal law only if the act committed is recognized as a crime and is punishable under the criminal law of both the state of the place of commission of the crime and the Criminal Code of the Republic of Lithuania. If a person who committed a crime abroad is tried in the Republic of Lithuania, but different penalties are provided for this crime in both states, the penalty for the offender is imposed according to the laws of the Republic of Lithuania, but it may not exceed the maximum amount established in the criminal laws of the state of the place of commission of the crime.

2. A person who has committed crimes provided for in Articles 5, 6 and 7 of the Criminal Code of the Republic of Lithuania is not liable under this Code if he:

- 1) has served the sentence imposed by a foreign state court;
- 2) has been released from serving all or part of the sentence imposed by a foreign state court;
- 3) has been acquitted by a foreign state court judgment, released from criminal liability or punishment, or no punishment was imposed due to the statute of limitations or other legal grounds provided for in that state.
  - 3. A citizen of the Republic of Lithuania or another person permanently residing in Lithuania who has committed abroad one or more crimes provided for in Articles 129, 135, 138, 148, 149, 150, 151, 151¹, 152¹, 153 of this Code, paragraph 1 of Article 162, paragraph 3 of Article 307, paragraph 3 of Article 308, paragraphs 2 and 3 of Article 309, are punished regardless of whether the act is punishable under the criminal law of the state of the place of commission of the crime.

Lietuvos Respublikos pilietis, padaręs nusikalstamą veiką Lietuvos Respublikoje ar kitos valstybės teritorijoje, gali būti išduotas užsienio valstybei arba perduotas Tarptautiniam baudžiamajam teismui tik remiantis Lietuvos Respublikos tarptautine sutartimi arba Jungtinių Tautų Organizacijos Saugumo Tarybos rezoliucija.

Užsienietis, padaręs nusikalstamą veiką Lietuvos Respublikoje ar kitos valstybės teritorijoje, išduodamas atitinkamai valstybei arba perduodamas Tarptautiniam baudžiamajam teismui tik remiantis Lietuvos Respublikos tarptautine sutartimi arba Jungtinių Tautų Organizacijos Saugumo Tarybos rezoliucija.

3. Lietuvos Respublikos pilietis ar užsienietis gali būti neišduodamas, jeigu:

- 8) there are other grounds provided for in international treaties of the Republic of Lithuania.
  - 4. Persons who have been granted asylum or temporary protection under the laws of the Republic of Lithuania shall not be punished under the Criminal Law of the Republic of Lithuania and shall not be extradited to foreign states for criminal offences for which they were persecuted abroad, except in the cases provided for in Article 7 of this Code.

## CHAPTER III

CRIME AND CRIMINAL MISDEMEANOR

### Article 10. Types of Criminal Acts

Criminal acts are divided into crimes and criminal misdemeanors.

### Article 11. Crime

1. A crime is a dangerous act (action or omission) prohibited by this Code, for which a custodial sentence is prescribed.

2. Crimes are intentional and negligent. Intentional crimes are divided into minor, less serious, serious and very serious.

3. A minor crime is an intentional crime for which the maximum penalty prescribed by the criminal law does not exceed three years of imprisonment.

4. A less serious crime is an intentional crime for which the maximum penalty prescribed by the criminal law exceeds three years of imprisonment but does not exceed six years of imprisonment.

5. A serious crime is an intentional crime for which the maximum penalty prescribed by the criminal law exceeds six years of imprisonment but does not exceed ten years of imprisonment.

6. A very serious crime is an intentional crime for which the maximum penalty prescribed by the criminal law exceeds ten years of imprisonment.

### Article 12. Criminal Misdemeanor

A criminal misdemeanor is a dangerous act (action or omission) prohibited by this Code, for which a penalty not involving deprivation of liberty is prescribed, except arrest.

### Article 13. Age from which a person is liable under criminal law

1. A person who had reached the age of sixteen at the time of committing a crime or criminal misdemeanor is liable under this Code, and in the cases provided for in paragraph 2 of this Article – fourteen years.

2. A person who had reached the age of fourteen at the time of committing a crime or criminal misdemeanor is liable for murder (Article 129), grievous bodily harm (Article 135), rape (Article 149), sexual assault (Article 150), theft (Article 178), robbery (Article 180), extortion (Article 181), destruction or damage to property (Article 187(2)), seizure of firearms, ammunition, explosives or explosive substances (Article 254), theft, extortion or other unlawful taking of narcotic or psychotropic substances (Article 263), damage to vehicles or roads and devices located on them (Article 280(2)).

3. A person who had not reached the age of fourteen at the time of committing a dangerous act provided for in this Code may be subject to educational measures or other measures in accordance with the procedure established by the laws of the Republic of Lithuania.

### Article 14. Forms of Guilt

A person is found guilty of committing a crime or criminal misdemeanor if he committed the act intentionally or negligently.

### Article 15. Intentional Crime and Criminal Misdemeanor

1. A crime or criminal misdemeanor is intentional if it was committed with direct or indirect intent.

2. A crime or criminal misdemeanor is committed with direct intent if:

- 1) in committing it, the person was aware of the dangerous nature of the criminal act and desired to act in such manner;
- 2) in committing it, the person was aware of the dangerous nature of the criminal act, foresaw that the consequences provided for in this Code may occur as a result of his action or omission, and desired them.
  - 3. A crime or criminal misdemeanor is committed with indirect intent if, in committing it, the person was aware of the dangerous nature of the criminal act, foresaw that the consequences provided for in this Code may occur as a result of his action or omission, and although he did not desire them, he consciously allowed them to occur.

### Article 16. Negligent Crime and Criminal Misdemeanor

1. A crime or criminal misdemeanor is negligent if it was committed due to criminal overconfidence or criminal carelessness.

2. A crime or criminal misdemeanor is committed due to criminal overconfidence if the person who committed it foresaw that the consequences provided for in this Code may occur as a result of his action or omission, but recklessly hoped to avoid them.

3. A crime or criminal misdemeanor is committed due to criminal carelessness if the person who committed it did not foresee that the consequences provided for in this Code may occur as a result of his action or omission, although under the circumstances of the act and given his personal characteristics he could and should have foreseen it.

4. A person is punished for committing a crime or criminal misdemeanor by negligence only in cases separately provided for in the Special Part of this Code.

### Article 17. Insanity

1. A person is insane if, in committing an act prohibited by this Code, he, due to a mental disorder, was unable to understand its dangerousness or to control his actions.

2. A person found insane by the court is not liable under this Code for the dangerous act committed. The court may apply to him compulsory medical measures provided for in Article 98 of this Code.

### Article 18. Diminished Responsibility

1. A person is found by the court to have diminished responsibility if, in committing an act prohibited by this Code, that person, due to a mental disorder which is not sufficient grounds to find him insane, was unable to fully understand the dangerous nature of the criminal act or to control his actions.

2. A person who has committed a criminal misdemeanor, a negligent crime or a minor or less serious intentional crime and is found by the court to have diminished responsibility is liable under criminal law, but the penalty may be mitigated on the basis of Article 59 of this Code, or he may be released from criminal liability and subject to the criminal impact measures provided for in Article 67 of this Code or the compulsory medical measures provided for in Article 98 of this Code.

3. A person who has committed a serious or very serious crime and is found by the court to have diminished responsibility is liable under criminal law, but the penalty may be mitigated on the basis of Article 59 of this Code.

### Article 19. Liability of a person for a criminal act committed under the influence of

alcohol, narcotic, psychotropic or other psychoactive substances

1. A person who committed a criminal act under the influence of alcohol, narcotic, psychotropic or other psychoactive substances is not released from criminal liability.

2. A person who committed a criminal misdemeanor, a negligent crime or a minor or less serious intentional crime while being intoxicated or drugged against his will and therefore not fully capable of understanding the dangerous nature of the criminal act or controlling his actions, is released from criminal liability.

3. A person who, under the conditions specified in paragraph 2 of this Article, committed a serious or very serious crime is liable under criminal law, but the penalty may be mitigated on the basis of Article 59 of this Code.

### 20 straipsnis. Criminal liability of a legal entity

1. A legal entity is liable only for criminal acts for which the liability of a legal entity is provided in the Special Part of this Code.

2. A legal entity is liable for criminal acts committed by a natural person only in the case where the criminal act was committed for the benefit or in the interests of the legal entity by a natural person acting individually or on behalf of the legal entity, if he, holding a management position in the legal entity, had the right to:

- 1) represent the legal entity, or
- 2) make decisions on behalf of the legal entity, or
- 3) control the activities of the legal entity.
  - 3. A legal entity may be liable for criminal acts also in the case where they were committed for the benefit of the legal entity by an employee or authorized representative of the legal entity on the instruction or with the permission of the person referred to in paragraph 2 of this Article or due to inadequate supervision or control.
  - 4. A legal entity may be liable for criminal acts committed under the conditions specified in paragraphs 2 or 3 of this Article by another legal entity controlled by it or representing it, if they were committed for the benefit of the aforementioned legal entity on the instruction or with the permission of a person holding a management position therein or a person authorized by it or due to inadequate supervision or control.
  - 5. The criminal liability of a legal entity does not eliminate the criminal liability of a natural person who committed, organized, instigated or assisted in committing a criminal act. The criminal liability of a legal entity for a criminal act committed, organized, instigated or assisted in committing by a natural person for its benefit or in its interests does not eliminate the criminal liability of the natural person, nor does the fact that the natural person is released from criminal liability for this act or is not prosecuted for other reasons.
  - 6. Under this Code, the state, municipality, state and municipal institution and establishment, and international public organization are not liable. State and municipal enterprises, as well as public institutions whose owner or shareholder is the state or municipality, and joint-stock companies and closed joint-stock companies, all or part of whose shares belong by right of ownership to the state or municipality, are not considered state and municipal institutions and establishments and are liable under this Code.

## IV SKYRIUS

STAGES AND FORMS OF CRIMINAL ACTS

### 21 straipsnis. Preparation to commit a crime

1. Preparation to commit a crime is the search for or adaptation of means and instruments, the drafting of a plan of action, the gathering of accomplices or other intentional creation of conditions facilitating the commission of a crime. A person is liable only for preparation to commit a serious or very serious crime.

2. A person for preparation to commit a crime is liable under paragraph 1 of this Article and the article of this Code which provides for the corresponding completed crime. The punishment of such person may be mitigated pursuant to Article 62 of this Code.

### 22 straipsnis. Attempt to commit a criminal act

1. An attempt to commit a criminal act is an intentional act or omission by which the commission of a crime or criminal offence is directly commenced, if the act was not completed due to circumstances beyond the control of the offender.

2. An attempt to commit a criminal act also exists when the offender does not realize that he cannot complete the act because he is attempting an improper object or is using improper means.

3. A person for an attempt to commit a criminal act is liable under paragraph 1 or 2 of this Article and the article of this Code which provides for the corresponding completed crime. The punishment of such person may be mitigated pursuant to Article 62 of this Code.

### 23 straipsnis. Voluntary abandonment of completing a criminal act

1. Voluntary abandonment of completing a crime or criminal offence exists when a person of his own will discontinues a criminal act that has been commenced while realizing that he can complete it.

2. A person who has voluntarily abandoned completing a crime or criminal offence is liable under this Code only in the case where the act committed contains the elements of another crime or criminal offence.

3. When a criminal act is committed by several persons, an organizer or instigator who has voluntarily abandoned completing it is not liable under this Code if he took all measures within his power to prevent the accomplices from committing the criminal act which he organized or instigated, and that act was not committed or its consequences did not occur. Moreover, under this Code an aider is not liable if he voluntarily refused to participate in the criminal act, notified the other accomplices or law enforcement institutions thereof, and that act was not committed or it was committed without his assistance.

4. A person who attempted to voluntarily abandon completing a crime or criminal offence but failed to avoid the criminal act or its consequences is liable under the criminal law, however his punishment may be mitigated pursuant to Article 59 of this Code.

### 24 straipsnis. Complicity and types of accomplices

1. Complicity is the intentional joint participation of two or more persons who have agreed among themselves, are culpable and have reached the age established in Article 13 of this Code, in committing

2. Accomplices to a criminal act are the perpetrator, organizer, instigator and aider.

3. A perpetrator is a person who committed a criminal act himself or by employing non-culpable persons or persons who have not reached the age established in Article 13 of this Code, or other persons who are not guilty of that act. If a criminal act was committed by several persons together, each of them is considered a perpetrator (co-perpetrator).

4. An organizer is a person who formed an organized group or criminal association, led them or coordinated the activities of their members, or prepared a criminal act or led it.

5. An instigator is a person who induced another person to commit a criminal act.

6. An aider is a person who assisted in committing a criminal act by giving advice, instructions, providing means or removing obstacles, protecting or covering other accomplices, having promised in advance to hide the offender, the instruments or means of committing the criminal act, the traces of this act or items obtained by criminal means, as well as a person who promised in advance to dispose of items obtained or produced from the criminal act.

### 25 straipsnis. Forms of complicity

1. Forms of complicity are a group of accomplices, an organized group, a criminal association.

2. A group of accomplices exists when at any stage of a criminal act two or more persons agree to commit, continue or complete a criminal act, if at least two of them are perpetrators.

3. An organized group exists when at any stage of a criminal act two or more persons agree to commit several crimes or one moderately serious, serious or very serious crime and each member of the group, in committing the crime, performs a certain task or has a different role.

4. A criminal association exists when three or more persons unite for joint criminal activity – to commit one or several moderately serious, serious or very serious crimes – who are connected by permanent mutual ties and a distribution of roles or tasks. An anti-constitutional group or organization and a terrorist group are equivalent to a criminal association.

### 26 straipsnis. Criminal liability of accomplices

1. Accomplices are liable only for those criminal acts committed by the perpetrator that were covered by their intent.

2. If the perpetrator's criminal act was interrupted at the preparation or attempt stage, the organiser, instigator and aider are liable for preparation or attempt as accomplices to commit a criminal act.

3. If there are circumstances excluding, mitigating or aggravating the criminal liability of one accomplice, they are not taken into account when deciding on the criminal liability of other accomplices.

4. The organiser, instigator or aider is liable under the article of this code providing for liability for the act committed by the perpetrator, and Article 24(4, 5 or 6) of this code.

5. Members of a criminal association, regardless of their role in committing a criminal act covered by their intent, are liable under Article 249 of this code as perpetrators.

### 27 straipsnis. Recidivism of crimes

1. Recidivism of crimes exists when a person who has already been convicted of an intentional crime committed when of full age, and if the conviction has not been extinguished or cancelled in the manner prescribed by law, again commits one or more intentional crimes. Such person is a recidivist.

2. Recidivism of crimes is dangerous, and a person who has committed crimes may be recognised by the court as a dangerous recidivist if this person:

- 1) having an unexpired conviction for a very serious crime, commits a new very serious crime;
- 2) being a recidivist, commits a new very serious crime;
- 3) being a recidivist, if at least one of the crimes constituting recidivism is very serious, commits a new serious crime;
- 4) having three convictions for serious crimes, commits a new serious crime.
  - 3. The court, when passing a conviction for the last crime, taking into account the personality of the offender, the degree of fulfilment of criminal intentions, the nature of participation in committing the crimes and other circumstances of the case, may recognise the person as a dangerous recidivist.
  - 4. The court, when deciding on recognising a person as a dangerous recidivist, does not take into account convictions for crimes that the person committed when younger than eighteen years of age, negligent crimes, crimes for which the conviction has been extinguished or cancelled, as well as crimes committed abroad in the cases provided for in Article 97(9) of this code.
  - 5. The recognition of a person as a dangerous recidivist loses force when the person's conviction is extinguished or cancelled.

## V SKYRIUS

CIRCUMSTANCES EXCLUDING CRIMINAL LIABILITY

### 28 straipsnis. Necessary defence

1. A person has the right to necessary defence. This right may be exercised regardless of whether the person could have avoided the assault or sought help from other persons or authorities.

2. A person is not liable under this code if, without exceeding the limits of necessary defence, the person committed an act formally corresponding to the elements of a crime or misdemeanour provided for in the criminal law when defending himself or herself or another person, property, inviolability of the dwelling, other rights, public or state interests from a dangerous assault that has commenced or is directly imminent.

3. The limits of necessary defence are exceeded when a person is killed or health is seriously impaired with direct intent, if the defence clearly did not correspond to the nature and danger of the assault. An act committed due to great confusion or fear caused by a dangerous assault, or when defending against intrusion into a dwelling, is not considered to exceed the limits of necessary defence.

4. A person who has exceeded the limits of necessary defence is liable under criminal law, but the punishment may be mitigated pursuant to Article 62 of this code.

### 29 straipsnis. Detention of a person who has committed a criminal act

1. A person is not liable under this code for actions when, by chasing, stopping, preventing escape or by other actions towards a person who has committed a criminal act and is actively trying to avoid detention, causes property damage, slight health impairment or serious health impairment through negligence, and when detaining at the scene of the crime a person who has intentionally killed or attempted to kill,

– serious health impairment, if the person who committed the criminal act could not otherwise be detained.

2. The rules of necessary defence established in Article 28 of this code apply to the actions of a person repelling the resistance of a person who has committed a criminal act.

### 30 straipsnis. Performance of professional duties

1. A person is not liable under this code for damage caused in the performance of professional duties if the person has not exceeded the powers established by laws or other legal acts.

2. A person is liable under this code for damage caused in the performance of professional duties if the person exceeded the powers established by laws or other legal acts, but the punishment may be mitigated pursuant to Article 59 of this code.

### 31 straipsnis. Extreme necessity

1. A person is not liable under criminal law for an act committed in order to avert a danger threatening to himself or herself, other persons or their rights, public or state interests, if this danger could not be averted by other means and the damage caused is less than that sought to be avoided.

2. A person who has created a dangerous situation by his or her actions may rely on the provisions of extreme necessity only if the dangerous situation was created through negligence.

3. A person may not justify failure to perform a duty by the provisions of extreme necessity if, due to profession, duties or other circumstances, the person must act in conditions of greater danger.

### 32 straipsnis. Performance of a law enforcement authority's task

1. A person is not liable under this code if the person lawfully performed actions imitating a criminal act.

2. A person is not liable under criminal law if the person participated in the activities of a criminal association or organised group and in the criminal acts committed by it while performing another lawful task of a law enforcement authority and did not exceed the limits of this task.

3. If, when performing actions imitating a criminal act or performing another task of a law enforcement authority, a person exceeded the limits of this task, the person is liable under criminal law, but the punishment may be mitigated pursuant to Article 59 of this code.

4. Law enforcement authorities are the police, other pre-trial investigation and prosecution institutions, as well as criminal intelligence entities.

Nr. XI-2241, 2012-10-02, Žin., 2012, Nr. 122-6100 (2012-10-20)

### 33 straipsnis. Execution of an order

1. A person is not liable under criminal law for an act committed in execution of a lawful order, decree or instruction.

2. A person is liable under criminal law if the person executed a knowingly criminal order, decree or instruction.

3. A person who has refused to execute a criminal order, decree or instruction is not liable under criminal law. Such person may be liable under this code only if the act committed contains the elements of another criminal act.

### 34 straipsnis. Justified professional or economic risk

1. A person is not liable under this code for actions which, although they result in consequences provided for by criminal law, were performed under justified professional or economic risk for a purpose beneficial to society.

2. Risk is considered justified if the act performed corresponds to modern science and technology, and the indicated purpose could not be achieved by actions not involving risk, and the person who took the risk took the necessary safety measures to protect interests safeguarded by law from harm.

### Article 35. Scientific Experiment

1. A person who caused damage while conducting a lawful scientific experiment shall not be liable under this Code if the experiment was based on scientifically approved methodologies, the problem being addressed is of exceptional importance to science, and the experimenter took the necessary measures to avoid harm to interests protected by law.

2. Scientific experimentation is prohibited without the free consent of an experiment participant who has been informed about possible consequences.

3. Scientific experimentation is prohibited, except in cases provided for by law, with a pregnant woman, her fetus, a minor, a person with mental disorders, and a person deprived of liberty.

## CHAPTER VI

RELEASE FROM CRIMINAL LIABILITY

### Article 36. Release from Criminal Liability When a Person or Criminal

Act Has Lost Dangerousness

A person who has committed a criminal act shall be released from criminal liability if the court finds that before the examination of the case in court, this person or the act committed by him or her has become non-dangerous due to a change in circumstances.

### Article 37. Release from Criminal Liability Due to the Crime's

Insignificance

A person who has committed a crime may be released by the court from criminal liability if, due to the extent of damage caused, the object of the crime, or particularities of other elements of the crime, the act is considered insignificant.

### Article 38. Release from Criminal Liability When the Offender and

the Victim Reconcile

1. A person who has committed a criminal misdemeanor, a negligent crime, or a minor or less serious intentional crime may be released by the court from criminal liability if:

- 1) he or she confessed to committing the criminal act and
- 2) voluntarily compensated for or remedied the damage caused to a natural or legal person or agreed on the compensation for or remedy of such damage, and
- 3) reconciled with the victim or a representative of a legal person or state institution, and;
- 4) there is reason to believe that he or she will not commit new criminal acts.
  - 2. A dangerous recidivist, as well as a person who has previously been released from criminal liability as having reconciled with the victim, if less than four years have passed from the date of reconciliation until the commission of a new act, may not be released from criminal liability on the grounds provided for in paragraph 1 of this Article.
  - 3. If a person released from criminal liability pursuant to paragraph 1 of this Article commits a criminal misdemeanor or negligent crime within one year or, without justifiable reasons, fails to comply with the terms and procedure of the court-approved agreement on compensation for damage, the court may revoke the decision on release from criminal liability and decide on the criminal liability of such person for all criminal acts committed.
  - 4. If a person released from criminal liability pursuant to paragraph 1 of this Article commits a new intentional crime within one year, the previous decision to release from criminal liability shall cease to be valid and a decision shall be made on the criminal liability of such person for all criminal acts committed.

Nr. XI-677, 2010-02-11, Žin., 2010, Nr. 20-927 (2010-02-18)

### Article 39. Release from Criminal Liability When There Are Mitigating

Circumstances

A person who has committed a criminal misdemeanor, a negligent crime, or a minor intentional crime may, by a reasoned decision of the court, be released from criminal liability if:

- 1) he or she committed a criminal act for the first time and
- 2) there are at least two circumstances mitigating liability provided for in paragraph 1 of Article 59 of this Code, and
- 3) there are no circumstances aggravating liability.

Article 39(1). Release from Criminal Liability When a Person Actively Assisted in Disclosing Criminal Acts Committed by Members of an Organized Group or Criminal Association

1. A person suspected of having participated in an organized group or criminal association in committing criminal acts or of having belonged to a criminal association may be released from criminal liability if he or she confessed to having participated in committing such a criminal act or having belonged to a criminal association and actively assisted in disclosing criminal acts committed by members of the organized group or criminal association.

2. Paragraph 1 of this Article shall not apply to a person who participated in intentional killing or who has already been released from criminal liability on such grounds, as well as to the organizer or leader of an organized group or criminal association.

### Article 392. Release of a Whistleblower from Criminal Liability

1. A person who has committed a criminal misdemeanor, a negligent crime, or a minor or less serious crime may be released by the court from criminal liability if:

- 1) he or she has been recognized as a whistleblower under the Law of the Republic of Lithuania on the Protection of Whistleblowers and
- 2) confessed to committing a criminal act, and
- 3) actively assisted in disclosing a criminal act committed by another person, and
- 4) the criminal act which he or she helped to disclose is, by its nature, more dangerous than the criminal act committed by him or her.
  - 2. A dangerous recidivist, a person who has a criminal record, who was the organizer of the criminal act which he or she helped to disclose, may not be released from criminal liability on the grounds specified in paragraph 1 of this Article.
  - 3. If a person released from criminal liability pursuant to paragraph 1 of this Article commits a criminal misdemeanor or negligent crime within one year, the court may revoke the decision on release from criminal liability and decide on the criminal liability of such person for all criminal acts committed.
  - 4. If a person released from criminal liability pursuant to paragraph 1 of this Article commits a new intentional crime within one year, the previous decision to release from criminal liability shall cease to be valid and a decision shall be made on the criminal liability of such person for all criminal acts committed.

Nr. XIII-1844, 2018-12-20, paskelbta TAR 2018-12-28, i. k. 2018-21873

### Article 40. Release from Criminal Liability on Bail

1. A person who has committed a misdemeanor, a negligent crime or a minor or less serious intentional crime may be released from criminal liability by the court if there is a request from a person worthy of the court's trust to transfer the offender to their responsibility on bail. Bail may be assigned with or without a deposit.

2. A person may be released from criminal liability by the court on bail if:

- 1) they have committed a criminal act for the first time and
- 2) they have fully acknowledged their guilt and regret having committed the criminal act, and
- 3) they have at least partially compensated or remedied the damage caused or have undertaken to compensate it, if such damage was caused, and
- 4) there is reason to believe that they will fully compensate or remedy the damage caused, will comply with the law and will not commit new criminal acts.
  - 3. The guarantor may be the offender's parents, close relatives or other persons worthy of the court's trust. When making a decision, the court takes into account the guarantor's personal characteristics or the nature of their activities and the possibility of exerting a positive influence on the offender.
  - 4. The bail period is set from one to three years.
  - 5. When requesting to transfer a person on bail with a deposit, the guarantor undertakes to pay a deposit of the amount set by the court. Taking into account the guarantor's personal characteristics and financial situation, the court sets the amount of the deposit or decides on the release from criminal liability on bail without a deposit. The deposit is returned upon expiry of the bail period if the person for whom bail was provided did not commit a new criminal act during the bail period set by the court.
  - 6. The guarantor has the right to refuse bail. In this case, the court, taking into account the reasons for refusing bail, decides on the return of the deposit, as well as on the person's criminal liability for the criminal act committed, the appointment of another guarantor or the release of the person from criminal liability.
  - 7. If a person released from criminal liability on bail committed a new misdemeanor or a negligent crime during the bail period, the court may annul the decision to release from criminal liability and decide on the criminal liability of this person for all acts committed.
  - 8. If a person released from criminal liability on bail committed a new intentional crime during the bail period, the previous decision to release them from criminal liability ceases to be valid and the criminal liability of this person for all criminal acts committed is decided.

## CHAPTER VII

PUNISHMENT

### Article 41. Punishment and its Purpose

1. Punishment is a measure of state coercion imposed by court judgment on a person who has committed a crime or misdemeanor.

2. The purpose of punishment:

- 1) to deter persons from committing criminal acts;
- 2) to punish the person who has committed a criminal act;
- 3) to deprive or restrict the convicted person's ability to commit new criminal acts;
- 4) to influence persons who have served their punishment to comply with the law and not to commit crimes again;
- 5) to ensure the implementation of the principle of justice.

### Article 42. Types of Punishments

1. The following punishments may be imposed on a person who has committed a crime:

- 1) community service;
- 2) fine;
- 3) restriction of liberty;
- 4) arrest;
- 5) fixed-term imprisonment;
- 6) life imprisonment.
  - 2. The following punishments may be imposed on a person who has committed a misdemeanor:
- 1) community service;
- 2) fine;
- 3) restriction of liberty;
- 4) arrest.
  - 3. Only one punishment may be imposed on a person who has committed one crime or misdemeanor.
  - 4. In the cases provided for in Articles 63 and 64 of this Code, two punishments may be imposed.
  - 5. If more than two punishments of different types are imposed for several crimes committed, the court, when imposing the final aggregate punishment, leaves two punishments from the imposed punishments: one of them – the most severe, and the other selected at its discretion.
  - 6. Together with the punishment, based on Articles 67, 68, 681, 682, 71, 72, 721, 722, 723 and 724 of this Code, a person who has committed a criminal act may be imposed one or more of the following penal measures – prohibition from using a special right, deprivation of public rights, deprivation of the right to perform certain work or engage in certain activities, payment to the fund for victims of crimes, confiscation of property, obligation to live separately from the victim and (or) not to approach the victim closer than a specified distance, participation in programmes for changing violent behaviour, extended confiscation of property, obligation to report a change of residence or departure from it. The payment to the fund for victims of crimes is not imposed together with a fine.
  - 7. The types of punishments for legal persons and the specifics of imposing punishments on minors are established by Articles 43 and 90 of this Code.

### Article 43. Types of Punishments for Legal Persons

1. The following punishments may be imposed on a legal person for a criminal act committed:

- 1) fine;
- 2) restriction of the legal person's activities;
- 3) liquidation of the legal person.
  - 2. The court, having imposed a punishment on a legal person, may decide to publish this judgment through the mass media. The judgment by which the court imposes a punishment on a legal person for crimes provided for in Chapter XXXIII of this Code must be published through the mass media.
  - 3. Only one punishment may be imposed on a legal person for one criminal act.
  - 4. Punishments imposed on legal persons are not specified in the sanctions of the articles of the Special Part of this Code. When imposing a punishment on a legal person, the court is guided by the list of punishments established in part 1 of this Article.
  - 5. Together with the punishment, based on Articles 67, 71, 72 and 723 of this Code, a legal person may be imposed one or more of the following penal measures – payment to the fund for victims of crimes, confiscation of property, extended confiscation of property. The payment to the fund for victims of crimes may be imposed only together with the punishment of restriction of the legal person's activities.

### 46 straipsnis. Community service

1. The court assigns community service in cases provided in the Special Part of this Code. Community service is assigned only if the accused consents.

2. Community service is assigned from one month to one year. The term of punishment is calculated in months. A person sentenced to community service is obligated to work unpaid from 10 to 40 hours per month for the benefit of society during the time assigned by the court.

3. The time for performing community service and the number of work hours is not specified in the article's sanction. This is determined by the court when assigning the punishment, but this time may not be longer than 480 hours when the punishment is assigned for a crime, and 240 hours when the punishment is assigned for a criminal misdemeanor.

4. Work for the convicted person is selected by the institution executing the punishment together with municipal executive institutions.

5. If for objective reasons a person cannot perform the community service assigned to them under this article, the court may release them from this punishment, instead assigning one or more penal impact measures provided in Chapter IX of this Code, or one or more educational impact measures provided in Chapter XI when a minor is released from the community service punishment.

6. If a person due to justifiable reasons has not completed the assigned community service hours within the time assigned by the court, the court may extend the community service time until this person completes the assigned hours.

7. If a person evades performing community service, the institution executing the punishment warns the convicted person about possible legal consequences. If a person continues to evade performing community service even after the warning, the court, upon submission by the institution executing the punishment, may replace the community service with a fine or restriction of liberty. Community service is replaced with a fine or restriction of liberty according to the rules established in Articles 47, 48 and 65 of this Code.

### 47 straipsnis. Fine

1. A fine is a monetary punishment assigned by the court in cases provided in the Special Part of this Code.

2. A fine is calculated in amounts of the minimum living standard (MLS).

3. The fine is established:

- 1) for a criminal misdemeanor – from 15 to 500 MLS amounts;
- 2) for a minor crime – from 50 to 2,000 MLS amounts;
- 3) for a less serious crime – from 100 to 4,000 MLS amounts;
- 4) for a serious crime – from 150 to 6,000 MLS amounts;
- 5) for a negligent crime – from 20 to 750 MLS amounts.
  - 4. For a legal person, a fine is established from 200 to 100,000 MLS amounts.
  - 5. The amount of the fine for a committed criminal act is not specified in the article's sanction. It is determined by the court when assigning the punishment.
  - 6. For criminal acts provided in Chapter XXXIII of this Code, the fine assigned cannot be less than the established amount of the object of the criminal act, the property damage caused by the perpetrator, or the property benefit received or sought by the perpetrator for themselves or another person. When there are several criteria for calculating the amount of the fine, the amount of the fine assigned is calculated according to the one whose value, expressed in money, is the greatest. When a fine is assigned following the rules established in this part, the final fine assigned by the court for criminal acts provided in Chapter XXXIII of this Code may exceed the maximum fine amounts specified in parts 3 and 4 of this article, but cannot be less than the minimum fine amounts provided in parts 3 and 4 of this article.
  - 7. If a person does not have the funds to pay the fine assigned by the court, the court, following the rules established in Article 65 of this Code, may replace this punishment with community service with the consent of the convicted person.
  - 8. If a person evades paying the fine voluntarily and there are no possibilities to collect it, the court may replace the fine with restriction of liberty. When replacing the fine with restriction of liberty, the court follows the rules established in Articles 48 and 65 of this Code.

### 48 straipsnis. Restriction of liberty

1. The court assigns the punishment of restriction of liberty in cases provided in the Special Part of this Code.

2. The punishment of restriction of liberty may be assigned from three months to two years. The term of punishment is calculated in years and months.

3. When assigning the punishment of restriction of liberty, the court usually assigns intensive supervision to the accused – control of the convicted person's whereabouts according to a set time by electronic monitoring means. The court determines the place and time of the convicted person's presence.

4. Together with intensive supervision or instead of it, the court may oblige a person who has been assigned the punishment of restriction of liberty to:

- 1) participate in a behavior correction program;
- 2) work unpaid for up to 200 hours within the time determined by the court, but not longer than the restriction of liberty period, in healthcare, social service institutions or non-governmental organizations that care for elderly persons, persons with disabilities or other people in need of assistance;
- 3) compensate for property damage caused by the criminal act or a part thereof, or eliminate such damage by their own work;
- 4) start working or register with the Employment Service under the Ministry of Social Security and Labour of the Republic of Lithuania, study;
- 5) undergo treatment for alcoholism, drug addiction, substance abuse or venereal disease when the convicted person consents.
  - 5. Together with intensive supervision, obligations or instead of them, the court may prohibit a person who has been assigned the punishment of restriction of liberty from:
- 1) visiting certain places;
- 2) communicating with certain persons or groups of persons;
- 3) possessing, using, acquiring, keeping themselves or transferring for safekeeping to other persons certain objects;
- 4) using psychoactive substances.
  - 6. The court may assign to a person who has been assigned the punishment of restriction of liberty, at the request of other participants in criminal proceedings, as well as at its own discretion, one or more penal impact measures provided in Chapter IX of this Code, or one or more educational impact measures provided in Chapter XI when the punishment of restriction of liberty is assigned to a minor, and (or) other prohibitions or obligations not provided in the criminal law, if, in the court's opinion, this would have a positive impact on their behavior.
  - 7. During the execution of the punishment of restriction of liberty, the court, upon submission by the institution executing the punishment, may replace intensive supervision, assigned penal impact measures, educational impact measures, obligations and (or) prohibitions with other penal impact measures provided in Chapter IX of this Code or educational impact measures provided in Chapter XI when the punishment of restriction of liberty is assigned to a minor, obligations, prohibitions, assign additional penal impact measures provided in Chapter IX of this Code or educational impact measures provided in Chapter XI when the punishment of restriction of liberty is assigned to a minor, and (or) obligations and (or) prohibitions specified in parts 4, 5 and 6 of this article, as well as intensive supervision, if this would have a positive impact on the convicted person's behavior.
  - 8. If the convicted person evades serving the punishment of restriction of liberty, the court, upon submission by the institution executing the punishment, may additionally assign penal impact measures provided in Chapter IX of this Code or educational impact measures provided in Chapter XI when the punishment of restriction of liberty is assigned to a minor, and (or) obligations and (or) prohibitions specified in parts 4, 5 and 6 of this article, as well as intensive supervision, or replace the punishment of restriction of liberty with arrest. The punishment of restriction of liberty is replaced with arrest according to the rules established in Articles 49 and 65 of this Code.

### 49 straipsnis. Arrest

1. Arrest is imposed by the court in cases provided for in the special part of this Code.

2. Arrest is short-term deprivation of liberty. The term of arrest is calculated in days.

3. For a crime, arrest is established from fifteen to ninety days, for a criminal misdemeanor – from ten to forty-five days of arrest.

4. The term of arrest for a criminal act is not specified in the sanction of the article. It is determined by the court when imposing the sentence.

5. Became invalid 2019-07-06.

6. Arrest is not imposed on pregnant women and may not be imposed on persons raising a child up to three years of age, taking into account the interests of the child.

### 50 straipsnis. Fixed-term deprivation of liberty

1. Fixed-term deprivation of liberty is imposed by the court in cases provided for in the special part of this Code. The term of the sentence is calculated in years, months and days.

2. A sentence of fixed-term deprivation of liberty may be imposed from three months to twenty years. If a sentence is imposed according to Article 64 of this Code, when a new crime is committed without having served the sentence, a sentence of deprivation of liberty of up to twenty-five years may be imposed.

3. Became invalid 2020-07-01.

### 51 straipsnis. Life imprisonment

1. The sentence of life imprisonment is imposed by the court in cases provided for in the special part of this Code.

2. After the convicted person has served twenty years of life imprisonment, the question of replacing the life imprisonment sentence imposed on him with a fixed-term imprisonment sentence is examined and resolved by the court based on the submission of the institution executing the sentence. If the court decides to replace the life imprisonment sentence with a fixed-term imprisonment sentence, the term of fixed-term imprisonment cannot be shorter than five years and longer than ten years from the date of entry into force of the court's decision. When deciding the question of replacing the life imprisonment sentence with a fixed-term imprisonment sentence, the court takes into account the risk of criminal behavior of the convicted person, the behavior of the convicted person during the serving of the sentence, the purposes of the sentence and the effect of the sentence served on the convicted person, whether the convicted person compensated or removed a significant part of the damage caused and undertakes to fully compensate or remove it. The procedure for replacing the life imprisonment sentence with a fixed-term imprisonment sentence is established by the Code of Criminal Procedure of the Republic of Lithuania.

4. The twenty-year term of life imprisonment referred to in paragraph 2 of this Article is calculated from the application of detention (arrest) or from the beginning of serving the imposed life imprisonment sentence. If the convicted person commits a new intentional crime during the serving of the life imprisonment sentence, the course of calculation of the twenty-year term referred to in paragraph 2 of this Article is interrupted and the term begins to be calculated anew from the date of commission of the intentional crime.

### 52 straipsnis. Restriction of activities of a legal entity

1. The court, when imposing a sentence of restriction of activities of a legal entity, prohibits the legal entity from engaging in certain activities or obliges to close a certain division of the legal entity.

2. The activities of a legal entity may be restricted from one to five years. The term of this sentence is calculated in years and months.

### 53 straipsnis. Liquidation of a legal entity

The court, when imposing a sentence of liquidation of a legal entity, obliges the legal entity to terminate all economic, commercial, financial or professional activities and close all divisions of the legal entity within the period established by the court.

## VIII SKYRIUS

IMPOSITION OF SENTENCE

### 54 straipsnis. General grounds for imposing sentence

1. The court imposes a sentence according to the sanction of the article of the special part of this Code providing for liability for the committed criminal act, in compliance with the provisions of the general part of this Code.

2. When imposing a sentence, the court takes into account:

- 1) the degree of danger of the committed criminal act;
- 2) the form and type of guilt;
- 3) the motives and purposes of the committed criminal act;
- 4) the stage of the criminal act;
- 5) the personality of the perpetrator;
- 6) the form and type of the person's participation as an accomplice in committing the criminal act;
- 7) mitigating and aggravating circumstances;
- 8) the harm caused by the criminal act.

3. If the imposition of the sentence provided for in the sanction of the article would clearly contradict the principle of justice, the court, guided by the purpose of punishment, may impose a milder sentence with reasoning.

### 55 straipsnis. Imposition of sentence on a person being tried for the first time for a negligent or minor or less serious intentional crime

For a person being tried for the first time for a negligent or minor or less serious intentional crime, the court usually imposes sentences not related to arrest or fixed-term deprivation of liberty. When imposing a sentence of arrest or fixed-term deprivation of liberty, the court must justify its decision.

### 56 straipsnis. Imposition of sentence on a dangerous recidivist for committing an intentional crime

A dangerous recidivist for an intentional crime is imposed a sentence stricter than the average of the imprisonment sentence established in the sanction of the article for the committed crime. A different sentence may be imposed on a dangerous recidivist only on the grounds provided for in Article 62 of this Code.

### 57 straipsnis. Imposition of sentence for preparation and attempt to commit a criminal act

1. A sentence for preparation or attempt to commit a crime or criminal misdemeanor is imposed in the general procedure, taking into account the danger of the actions committed by the perpetrator, the extent to which the criminal intent was implemented, and the reasons why the criminal act was not completed.

2. For preparation or attempt to commit a criminal act, based on Article 62 of this Code, a milder sentence than that provided for a completed criminal act may be imposed.

### 58 straipsnis. Imposition of sentence on accomplices of a criminal act

1. A sentence for accomplices of a crime or criminal misdemeanor is imposed in the general procedure, taking into account the type, form, role and nature of the person's participation as an accomplice in committing the criminal act.

2. Members of an organized group for committing a crime are usually imposed a stricter sentence than members of a group of accomplices.

### 59 straipsnis. Mitigating Circumstances

1. The mitigating circumstances are as follows:

- 1) the offender provided assistance to the victim or by other active actions avoided or attempted to avoid more serious consequences;
- 2) the offender confessed to having committed an act provided for in the criminal law and sincerely regrets it or helped to clarify this act or the persons involved in it;
- 3) the offender voluntarily compensated or remedied the damage caused;
- 4) the criminal act was committed due to very difficult financial or desperate situation of the offender;
- 5) the act was committed due to mental or physical coercion, if such coercion does not eliminate criminal liability;
- 6) the commission of the act was influenced by provocative or risky behavior of the victim;
- 7) the act was committed at the request of the victim whose condition is hopeless;
- 8) the act was committed in violation of the conditions of legality of detention of a person who committed a criminal act, necessity, professional duty or execution of a law enforcement task, industrial or economic risk, scientific experiment;
- 9) the act was committed exceeding the limits of necessary defense, where the criminal law provides for liability for exceeding the limits of necessary defense;
- 10) the act was committed due to great agitation caused by unlawful actions of the victim;
- 11) the act was committed by a person with limited sanity;
- 12) the act was committed by a person intoxicated or stupefied against his will;
- 13) unsuccessful voluntary abandonment of committing a criminal act.
  - 2. The court may recognize as mitigating circumstances other circumstances not specified in paragraph 1 of this Article.
  - 3. When imposing a sentence, the court does not take into account such a mitigating circumstance which is provided for in the law as an element of the crime.

### 60 straipsnis. Aggravating Circumstances

1. The aggravating circumstances are as follows:

- 1) the act was committed by a group of accomplices. The court, taking into account the nature and degree of each accomplice's participation in committing the criminal act, may not recognize this circumstance as aggravating;
- 2) the act was committed by an organized group;
- 3) the act was committed for hooligan or selfish motives;
- 4) the act was committed by torturing the victim or otherwise acting extremely cruelly or mocking him;
- 5) the act was committed against a minor child;
- 6) the act was committed against a person who, due to illness, disability, old age or other reasons, was in a helpless state, without his request, or the act was committed against a minor by taking advantage of his dependence or abusing trust, authority or influence;
- 7) the act was committed against a pregnant woman when it is obvious that she is pregnant;
- 8) the act was committed by taking advantage of a public or another person's misfortune;
- 9) the act was committed by a person intoxicated by alcohol, narcotic, psychotropic or other psychoactive substances, if these circumstances influenced the commission of the criminal act;
- 10) the act was committed in a generally dangerous manner or using explosives, explosive substances or firearms;
- 11) the committed act resulted in serious consequences or posed a real threat to the victim's life;
- 12) the act was committed with the aim of expressing hatred towards a group of persons or a person belonging to it on the grounds of age, sex, sexual orientation, disability, race, skin color, nationality, language, origin, ethnic origin, social status, religion, beliefs or views;
- 13) the act was committed by a recidivist;
- 14) the acts provided for in paragraphs 3 and 4 of Article 149, paragraphs 3 and 4 of Article 150, paragraph 2 of Article 151, Articles 1511, 1521, 153, 162, paragraph 3 of Article 307, paragraph 3 of Article 308, paragraphs 2 and 3 of Article 309 were committed by a close relative, family member or cohabiting person of the victim minor.

### 61 straipsnis. Imposition of Sentence when there are Mitigating and (or) Aggravating

Circumstances

1. The court, when imposing a sentence, takes into account whether only mitigating or only aggravating circumstances have been established, or whether there are both mitigating and aggravating circumstances, and evaluates the significance of each circumstance.

2. The court, having assessed the mitigating and (or) aggravating circumstances, their number, nature and mutual relationship, as well as other circumstances specified in paragraph 2 of Article 54, selects with reasoning a lighter or stricter type of sentence, as well as the size of the sentence to be imposed, calculating from its average.

3. When determining the average of the sentence provided for in the law, the minimum and maximum sentence size provided for in the sanction of the article is added and the result is divided in half. If the minimum sentence size for the criminal act committed is not specified in the sanction of the article, when determining the average sentence, the minimum size of that type of sentence is followed.

4. If the offender voluntarily confessed to having committed a crime, sincerely regrets it, actively helped to clarify the crime and there are no aggravating circumstances, the court imposes on him a sentence of imprisonment not exceeding the average of the sentence provided for in the sanction of the article for the crime committed or a non-custodial sentence.

5. A sentence of imprisonment not exceeding the average of the sentence provided for in the sanction of the article for the crime committed may be imposed by the court on a person who participated in intentional killing if he confessed to all his committed criminal acts and actively helped to disclose an intentional killing committed by members of an organized group or criminal association.

6. Paragraph 5 of this Article does not apply to the organizer or leader of intentional killing, organized group or criminal association.

1. If multiple criminal acts are committed, the court imposes a sentence for each criminal act separately, then imposes a final aggregate sentence. When imposing a final aggregate sentence, the court may absorb the sentences or add them fully or partially.

3. When sentences are fully added, all imposed more lenient sentences are added to the most severe sentence imposed for one of the committed criminal acts.

4. - 1) there is an ideal concurrence of criminal acts;
- 2) the committed criminal acts differ greatly in terms of dangerousness and are attributed to different types or categories of criminal acts according to Articles 10 or 11 of this Code;
- 3) for one criminal act, twenty years of imprisonment or life imprisonment was imposed.
  - 6. If, when imposing a final sentence, part of the imposed sentences can be absorbed, and others – only fully or partially added, the court aggregates sentences by way of absorption and addition of sentences.

### Article 75. Suspension of execution of punishment

1. For a person convicted of arrest for the first time, the court may suspend the execution of the imposed punishment from three months to one year and assign him intensive supervision – control of the location of the convicted person at a specified time by means of electronic monitoring. The location and time of the convicted person shall be determined by the court. Together with intensive supervision, the court may assign to the convicted person one or more mutually compatible measures of criminal impact provided for in Chapter IX of this Code and (or) the obligations established in paragraphs 5 and 6 of this Article.

2. For a person sentenced to imprisonment for not more than six years for crimes committed through negligence or for not more than four years for one or more minor or less serious intentional crimes, the court may suspend the execution of the imposed punishment from one to three years.

3. For a person sentenced to imprisonment for more than six years for crimes committed through negligence or for more than four years for one or more minor or less serious intentional crimes, or for not more than five years for a serious crime, except for serious crimes provided for in Chapters XV, XVI, XVIII, XX, XXI, XXIII, XXXIII, XXXV, XXXVII, XLIV of this Code, the court may partially suspend the execution of the imposed punishment. When partially suspending the execution of the punishment, the court shall determine the part of the custodial sentence to be served immediately after the entry into force of the court judgment, which may not be shorter than six months and longer than two years, as well as the term of suspension of execution of the remaining unserved punishment from one to three years.

4. The execution of punishment under paragraphs 1, 2, 3 of this Article may be suspended if the court decides that there is sufficient ground to believe that the objectives of the punishment will be achieved without actual serving of the punishment or after the person has served only part of it.

5. When suspending the execution of punishment under paragraphs 2 and 3 of this Article, the court shall assign to the convicted person one or more mutually compatible measures of criminal impact provided for in Chapter IX of this Code, the intensive supervision established in paragraph 1 of this Article and (or) the following obligations:

- 1) to apologize to the victim;
- 2) to provide assistance to the victim while he is being treated;
- 3) to undergo treatment for addiction diseases when the convicted person consents;
- 4) to raise and care for his minor children, care for their health, support them;
- 5) to start working or studying, continue work or studies;
- 6) to participate in a behaviour correction programme;
- 7) not to leave home at certain times, unless related to work or studies;
- 8) not to leave the city (district) of residence without permission from the institution supervising the convicted person;
- 9) not to visit certain places or not to communicate with certain persons or groups of persons;
- 10) not to use psychoactive substances;
- 11) not to possess, use, acquire certain objects or not to engage in certain activities.
  - 6. At the request of the person or other participants in criminal proceedings, as well as at its own discretion, the court may assign other obligations not provided for in the criminal law which, in the court's opinion, should have a positive influence on the behaviour of the convicted person.
  - 7. When assigning the measures of criminal impact provided for in Chapter IX of this Code and (or) the obligations provided for in paragraphs 5 and 6 of this Article, the court shall determine the time within which the convicted person must fulfil them.
  - 8. When imposing a penalty of arrest or imprisonment and not suspending the execution of punishment in the manner established in this Article, the court must justify its decision.
  - 9. The procedure and conditions for the execution of suspension of execution of punishment shall be established by the Law of the Republic of Lithuania on Probation.

### Article 76. Release from punishment due to illness

1. A person who has committed a criminal act may be released from punishment if, before the adoption of the court judgment, he becomes ill with a serious incurable disease due to which serving the punishment would be too difficult. In this case, the court, when adopting the conviction judgment, shall impose a punishment on such person and release him from serving the punishment. When deciding this issue, the court shall take into account the gravity of the criminal act committed, the personality of the convicted person and the nature of the illness.

2. A person who becomes ill with a serious incurable disease after the adoption of the judgment may be released from further serving of the punishment. When deciding this issue, the court shall take into account the gravity of the criminal act committed, the personality of the convicted person, his behaviour during the serving of the punishment, the nature of the illness and the time of the punishment served.

3. A person whose mental state is disturbed after committing a criminal act or the imposition of punishment and who, as a result, cannot understand the essence of his actions or control them, shall be released from further serving of the punishment. When releasing such person from punishment, the court shall decide on the application of compulsory medical measures. If such person recovers, he may be sent to continue serving the punishment. In such case, the time during which compulsory medical measures were applied shall be counted towards the term of imprisonment on a day-for-day basis.

## CHAPTER XI

SPECIFICS OF CRIMINAL LIABILITY OF MINORS

### Article 80. Purpose of the specifics of criminal liability of minors

The purpose of the specifics of criminal liability of minors provided for in this Chapter and in paragraphs 2, 3 of Article 13, paragraph 4 of Article 27 and paragraph 4 of Article 97 of this Code:

- 1) to ensure that liability corresponds to the age and social maturity of these persons;
- 2) to limit the penalty of imprisonment and increase the possibilities of applying measures of educational impact to these persons;
- 3) to help the minor change his way of life and behaviour by combining punishment for the committed criminal act with the development of his personality, upbringing, elimination of the causes of unlawful behaviour;
- 4) to prevent the minor from committing new criminal acts.

### Article 81. Application of the provisions of the Chapter

1. The provisions of this Chapter shall apply to persons who had not reached eighteen years of age at the time of committing the criminal act.

2. The provisions of Articles 90−94 of this Code, as well as the measures of educational impact provided for in points 1, 2, 3 and 5 of paragraph 1 of Article 82, may be applied to a person who had reached eighteen years of age but had not reached twenty-one years of age at the time of committing the criminal act, if the court, having regard to the nature of the criminal act committed, the motives and other circumstances of the case, and, if necessary, to the explanations or conclusion of a specialist, decides that such person is equivalent to a minor in terms of social maturity and the application of the specifics of criminal liability to him would correspond to the purpose provided for in Article 80 of this Code.

## XII SKYRIUS

STATUTE OF LIMITATIONS FOR CRIMINAL LIABILITY

### 95 straipsnis. Statute of limitations for conviction

1. A person who has committed a criminal act may not receive a conviction if:

- 1) the following period has passed:
  - a) three years since the criminal offence was committed;
  - b) eight years since the negligent or minor intentional crime was committed;
  - c) twelve years since the medium gravity intentional crime was committed;
  - d) fifteen years since the serious crime was committed;
  - e) twenty-five years since the very serious crime was committed;
  - f) thirty years since the crime related to intentional deprivation of another person's life was committed;
- 2) during the time specified in point 1 of part 1 of this Article, the person did not hide from pre-trial investigation or court and did not commit a new intentional criminal act.
  - 2. The limitation period shall be calculated from the commission of the criminal act until the date of adoption of the judgment.
  - 3. If minors have suffered from criminal acts provided for in Chapters XVIII, XX, XXI, XXIII and XLIV of this Code, the limitation period may not expire before that person reaches the age of twenty-five.
  - 4. If a person who has committed a criminal act has hidden from pre-trial investigation or court, the running of the limitation period shall be suspended. The running of the limitation period shall resume from the day on which the person is detained or on which he or she personally arrives at the pre-trial investigation officer, prosecutor or court. However, a conviction may not be issued if twenty-five years have passed since the person committed the criminal act, or thirty years have passed since the person committed a crime related to intentional deprivation of another person's life, and the running of the limitation period has not been interrupted due to the commission of a new intentional criminal act.
  - 5. If a person who has committed a criminal act has immunity from criminal jurisdiction under the laws of the Republic of Lithuania or norms of international law and permission to prosecute him or her has not been obtained from the competent institution, the running of the limitation period shall be suspended.

The running of the limitation period shall resume from the day on which permission is obtained from the competent institution to prosecute the person who has committed a criminal act or he or she otherwise loses the immunity specified in this part.

6. During the trial proceedings, the running of the limitation period shall be suspended for the period during which:

- 1) the court declares a break in the trial or postpones the trial due to the non-attendance of the accused or his or her defence counsel;
- 2) the court declares a break in the trial until a court-ordered expert examination, specialist examination is carried out or a request for legal assistance to a foreign state is executed;
- 3) the court declares a break in the trial and instructs the prosecutor or pre-trial investigation judge to perform procedural actions provided for in the Code of Criminal Procedure of the Republic of Lithuania;
- 4) the court declares a break in the trial for a newly invited defence counsel of the accused to familiarise himself or herself with the case materials.
  - 7. In cases provided for in part 5 of this Article, a conviction may not be issued if a period five years longer than that provided for in part 1 of this Article has passed since the beginning of the limitation period.
  - 8. If a person commits a new intentional criminal act before the expiry of the periods specified in this Article, the running of the limitation period shall be interrupted. In this case, the running of the limitation period for the first criminal act shall begin to be calculated from the day on which the new intentional crime or criminal offence was committed.
  - 9. There is no statute of limitations for the following crimes provided for in this Code:
- 1) genocide (Article 99);
- 2) internationally prohibited treatment of people (Article 100);
- 3) enforced disappearance (Article 1001);
- 4) torture (Article 1003);
- 5) killing of persons protected by international humanitarian law (Article 101);
- 6) deportation or transfer of civilians (Article 102);
- 7) injury, torture or other inhuman treatment of persons protected by international humanitarian law or violation of the protection of their property (Article 103);
- 8) forced use of civilians or prisoners of war in enemy armed forces (Article 105);
- 9) destruction of protected objects or looting of national values (Article 106);
- 10) aggression (Article 110);
- 11) prohibited war attack (Article 111);
- 12) use of prohibited means of warfare (Article 112);
- 13) negligent performance of commander's duties (Article 1131).

### 96 straipsnis. Statute of Limitations for the Execution of a Conviction Judgment

1. A conviction judgment shall not be executed if:

- 1) it has not been executed:
  - a) within two years when a sentence has been imposed for a criminal misdemeanor, or
  - b) within three years when a non-custodial sentence has been imposed, or when a custodial sentence imposed does not exceed two years, or
  - c) within five years when a custodial sentence imposed does not exceed five years, or
  - d) within ten years when a custodial sentence imposed does not exceed ten years, or
  - e) within fifteen years when a custodial sentence imposed exceeds ten years or life imprisonment has been imposed, and
- 2) during the period specified in point 1 of paragraph 1 of this Article, the convicted person does not evade serving the imposed sentence and does not commit a new criminal act.
  - 2. The statute of limitations period for the execution of a conviction judgment shall be calculated from the day the judgment enters into force until the commencement of execution of the judgment.
  - 3. If the convicted person evades serving the sentence after the judgment enters into force, the running of the statute of limitations shall be suspended.

In this case, it shall resume from the day on which the convicted person voluntarily arrived to serve the sentence or was apprehended. However, the judgment may not be executed if fifteen years have elapsed from the day it entered into force, and when imprisonment for more than ten years or life imprisonment has been imposed – twenty years, and the running of the statute of limitations has not been interrupted due to the commission of a new criminal act.

4. If the convicted person commits a new criminal act before the expiry of the statute of limitations period for the execution of the conviction judgment, the running of the statute of limitations shall be interrupted. In this case, the statute of limitations period for the execution of the conviction judgment shall begin to run from the commission of the new crime or criminal misdemeanor.

5. When imposing a sentence for a new criminal act, the court shall be guided by the rules laid down in Article 64 of this Code.

## XIII SKYRIUS

CRIMINAL RECORD

### 97 straipsnis. Criminal Record

1. Persons convicted for the commission of a crime against whom a conviction judgment adopted by a court of the Republic of Lithuania or another Member State of the European Union has entered into force shall be deemed to have a criminal record. Persons convicted for the commission of a crime in a non-Member State of the European Union shall also be deemed to have a criminal record if, on the basis of international treaties of the Republic of Lithuania, information has been received that a conviction judgment adopted by a court of that State has entered into force against them. The court shall take the criminal record into account when imposing a sentence for the commission of a new criminal act, deciding on the release of the offender from punishment or criminal liability, and also when declaring a person a dangerous recidivist.

2. Taking into account a criminal record, only those rights and freedoms of citizens may be restricted which are provided for restriction by the laws of the Republic of Lithuania.

3. The following shall be deemed to have a criminal record:

- 1) persons whose execution of sentence has been postponed – during the period for which the execution of the sentence was postponed;
- 2) persons convicted of negligent crimes – during the period of serving the sentence;
- 3) persons convicted of intentional crimes and who have actually served the imposed sentence – during the period of serving the sentence and after serving the sentence or release from serving the sentence:
  - a) for three years if they were convicted of a minor or less serious crime;
  - b) for five years if they were convicted of a serious crime;
  - c) for eight years if they were convicted of a very serious crime;
  - d) for ten years if they are dangerous recidivists or if, in the case specified in paragraph 2 of Article 51 of this Code, their life imprisonment sentence was replaced by a fixed-term custodial sentence.
    - 4. For minors convicted of the crimes provided for in point 3 of paragraph 3 of this Article, the criminal record periods after serving the sentence or release from serving the sentence shall be reduced by half.
    - 5. The periods specified in point 3 of paragraph 3 and paragraph 4 of this Article shall be calculated from the serving of the imposed sentence or release from serving the sentence.
    - 6. When the periods specified in this Article expire, the criminal record shall be extinguished and the persons shall be deemed to have no criminal record.
    - 7. When at least half of the criminal record period has elapsed, the court may, at the request of the convicted person, shorten the criminal record period or remove the criminal record.
    - 8. If a person with a criminal record commits a new crime or criminal misdemeanor, the running of the extinguishment of the criminal record shall be interrupted. In this case, the period for the extinguishment of the criminal record for the previous criminal act shall begin to run from the serving of the sentence for the new crime or criminal misdemeanor. A person shall be deemed to have a criminal record for each criminal act until the criminal record for the most serious of them is extinguished.
    - 9. The court, when imposing a sentence for the commission of a new criminal act, deciding on the release of the offender from punishment or criminal liability, and also when declaring a person a dangerous recidivist, shall not take into account the conviction judgment referred to in paragraph 1 of this Article adopted by a court of another State that has entered into force, if:
- 1) taking into account the conviction judgment adopted by the court that has entered into force would violate fundamental human rights and (or) freedoms;
- 2) the act committed is not considered a crime under this Code;
- 3) the person at the time of committing the criminal act had not reached the age from which, under the criminal laws of the Republic of Lithuania, criminal liability is possible for the act committed by him;
- 4) the information received about the conviction judgment adopted by a court of another State is insufficient and the State that forwarded this information does not provide it within the established period;
- 5) there are other grounds established in international treaties of the Republic of Lithuania.

## XIV SKYRIUS

COMPULSORY MEDICAL MEASURES

### Article 98. Compulsory Medical Measures

1. The court may apply the following compulsory medical measures to persons recognised by the court as not criminally responsible or having diminished criminal responsibility, as well as to persons whose mental state deteriorated after committing a criminal act or after sentencing and who, as a result, cannot comprehend the nature of their actions or control them:

- 1) outpatient observation in primary mental health care conditions;
- 2) inpatient observation under general observation conditions in specialised mental health care institutions;
- 3) inpatient observation under enhanced observation conditions in specialised mental health care institutions;
- 4) inpatient observation under strict observation conditions in specialised mental health care institutions.
  - 2. The court applies outpatient observation to a person who, due to the dangerousness of the act committed and their mental disorder, does not need to be observed and treated in an inpatient facility, or who can continue outpatient treatment when their mental state improves after inpatient treatment.
  - 3. The court applies inpatient observation under general observation conditions to a person who needs to be observed and treated in a specialised inpatient facility due to their mental disorder.
  - 4. The court applies inpatient observation under enhanced observation conditions to a person who needs to be observed and treated in a specialised inpatient facility due to the dangerousness of the act committed and their mental disorder.
  - 5. The court applies inpatient observation under strict observation conditions to a person who attempted against human life or health, is particularly dangerous to others due to mental disorder, and must be observed and treated in a specialised inpatient facility.
  - 6. The court does not establish a time period for the application of compulsory medical measures. They are applied until the person recovers or their mental state improves and their dangerousness ceases. The court must, not less than once every six months, decide on the extension of the application of compulsory medical measures, change of type, or termination of their application, based on the opinion of the health care institution.
  - 7. If it is not necessary to apply compulsory medical measures to a person, and also if the court terminates the application of these measures, the person may be placed by the court under the care or custody of relatives or other persons, and medical observation may be established for them at the same time.

## CHAPTER XV

CRIMES AGAINST HUMANITY AND WAR CRIMES

### Article 99. Genocide

A person who, with the aim of physically destroying all or part of persons belonging to any national, ethnic, racial, religious, social or political group, organised, directed or participated in killing them, torturing them, injuring them, disrupting their mental development, deporting them, or otherwise creating such living conditions that resulted in their death or partial death, restricted the birth rate of persons belonging to those groups, or forcibly transferred their children to other groups, shall be punished by imprisonment from five to twenty years or by imprisonment for life.

### Article 100. Treatment of Persons Prohibited by International Law

A person who intentionally, in implementing or supporting a policy of a state or organisation to attack civilians on a large scale or systematically, killed them or seriously damaged their health; created such living conditions that resulted in the death of persons; enslaved persons; deported or forcibly transferred populations; unlawfully imprisoned or otherwise restricted the physical freedom of persons in violation of international law; tortured; raped or sexually assaulted persons, involved them in sexual slavery or forced them into prostitution; unlawfully deprived a forcibly impregnated woman of liberty with the aim of changing the ethnic composition of the population or committing another violation of international law; forcibly sterilised persons or committed other similar acts of sexual violence; persecuted any group or community of persons on political, racial, national, ethnic, cultural, religious, gender or other grounds prohibited by international law; detained, arrested, abducted persons or otherwise deprived them of their liberty where such deprivation of liberty is not recognised, or failed to report on the fate or whereabouts of persons; practised apartheid, shall be punished by imprisonment from five to twenty years or by imprisonment for life.

Article 100¹. Enforced Disappearance

A person who, acting as a representative of the state or as a person or group of persons acting with the permission, support or consent of the state, detained, arrested, abducted a person or otherwise deprived them of their liberty, where there is refusal to acknowledge such detention, arrest, abduction or deprivation of liberty, or concealed the fate or whereabouts of the disappeared person,

Article 100². Separation of Children

A person who unlawfully separated children, knowing that these children, their parents or guardians suffered from the criminal acts provided for in Article 100¹ of this Code,

Article 100³. Torture

1. A person who, in implementing state policy or acting with the permission, instigation, support or consent of the state, tortured a person in any manner or otherwise treated them with extreme cruelty, thereby causing the victim severe physical or mental pain or great suffering,

2. Torture within the meaning of this Article does not include pain or suffering arising from, inherent in or directly associated with the application of lawful state coercive measures and (or) sanctions.

### Article 101. Killing of Persons Protected by International Humanitarian Law

A person who, during war, armed conflict, aggression, occupation or annexation, ordered, incited or organised the killing of, or killed, persons protected by international humanitarian law, shall be punished by imprisonment for life.

### Article 102. Deportation or Transfer of Civilians

A person who, during war, armed conflict or during aggression, occupation or annexation, ordered, incited or organised the deportation of, or deported, civilian population from occupied or annexed territory to the territory of the occupying or annexing state or a third state; ordered, incited or organised the transfer or forced expulsion of, or transferred or forcibly expelled, civilian population of the occupied or annexed state within the territory of that state in violation of international law; ordered, incited or organised the transfer of, or transferred, civilian population of the occupying state to the territory of the occupied country,

### Article 103. Injury to Persons Protected by International Humanitarian Law,

Their Torture or Other Inhuman Treatment or Violation of Protection of Their Property

1. A person who, during war or armed conflict or during aggression, occupation or annexation, ordered, incited or organised inhuman treatment of, or treated inhumanely, persons protected by international humanitarian law: seriously injured, caused illness to them or tortured them; performed biological or medical experiments on them, unlawfully removed their organ or tissue for transplantation, unlawfully took their blood or otherwise treated them inhumanely; took them hostage; punished them with criminal penalties without a decision of an independent and impartial court or without guarantees of defence in court; applied collective punishments; raped or sexually assaulted persons, involved them in sexual slavery or forced them into prostitution; forcibly sterilised or impregnated them; used intimidation and terror measures; unlawfully restricted or deprived them of their liberty; separated children from parents or guardians; caused them a threat of death from starvation; unlawfully seized, confiscated the property of civilians or carried out large-scale misappropriation thereof not justified by military necessity; insultingly humiliated their dignity; forced them to convert to another faith; desecrated the remains of the deceased,

2. A person who committed the act provided for in paragraph 1 of this Article by using deception shall be punished by imprisonment from seven to fifteen years.

### 114 straipsnis. Coup d'état

1. Any person who organized or participated in a conspiracy to carry out a coup d'état or participated in a coup, shall be punished by imprisonment for a term of four to twenty years.

2. Any person who, while committing the act provided for in paragraph 1 of this article, used armed force or if as a result of his act serious consequences occurred, life imprisonment.

3. A person who participated in the conspiracy provided for in paragraphs 1 and 2 of this article shall be released from criminal liability if he voluntarily provided a state institution with important information about the planned coup d'état.

### 115 straipsnis. Attempt on the life of the President of the Republic of Lithuania

Any person who attempted on the life of the President of the Republic of Lithuania, life imprisonment.

### 116 straipsnis. Attempt on the life of a representative of another state or international public organization

Any person who attempted on the life of a representative of another state or international public organization officially present in the Republic of Lithuania, life imprisonment.

### 117 straipsnis. Treason

A citizen of the Republic of Lithuania who, during war or after the declaration of a state of war, went over to the side of the enemy or assisted the enemy in acting against the State of Lithuania,

### 118 straipsnis. Assisting another state in acting against the Republic of Lithuania

1. Any person who assisted another state or its organization in acting against the Republic of Lithuania – its constitutional order, sovereignty, territorial integrity, defense or economic power, shall be punished by imprisonment for a term of two to seven years.

2. A person who committed in this article representatives and their carried out activities, directed against the constitutional order, sovereignty, territorial integrity, defense or economic power of the Republic of Lithuania shall be released from criminal liability.

3. Paragraph 2 of this article shall not apply to a person who has already been released from criminal liability on the grounds established in this article or in Article 119 of this Code, also if as a result of the commission of the act provided for in paragraph 1 of this article a person died or other serious consequences occurred.

4. A legal person shall also be liable for the act provided for in this article.

### 119 straipsnis. Espionage

1. A person who, with the purpose of transmitting to a foreign state or its organisation, seized, purchased or otherwise collected information which is a state secret of the Republic of Lithuania, or transmitted this information to a foreign state, its organisation or their representative,

2. A person who, while executing a task of another state or its organisation, seized, purchased or otherwise collected or transmitted information which is a state secret of the Republic of Lithuania, or other information of interest to foreign state intelligence, shall be punished by deprivation of liberty from six to fifteen years.

3. A person who committed [the act provided for] in part 1 of this article [and disclosed] the representatives to whom the collected information which is a state secret of the Republic of Lithuania was to be or was transmitted, shall be released from criminal liability.

4. A person who committed [the act provided for] in part 2 of this article and disclosed the activities they executed related to collecting or transmitting information which is a state secret of the Republic of Lithuania or other information of interest to foreign state intelligence, shall be released from criminal liability.

5. Parts 3 and 4 of this article shall not apply to a person who has already been released from criminal liability on the grounds established in this article or in Article 118 of this Code, and also if due to commission of the act provided for in part 1 or 2 of this article a person died or other serious consequences occurred.

6. A legal person shall also be held liable for the acts provided for in this article.

## XVII SKYRIUS

CRIMES AGAINST HUMAN LIFE

### 129 straipsnis. Murder

1. A person who killed another person shall be punished by deprivation of liberty from seven to fifteen years.

2. A person who killed:

- 1) a minor;
- 2) a person in a helpless condition;
- 3) his close relative or family member;
- 4) a pregnant woman;
- 5) two or more persons;
- 6) by torture or in another particularly cruel manner;
- 7) in a manner dangerous to the lives of other persons;
- 8) for hooligan motives;
- 9) for mercenary motives;
- 10) due to the victim's service or performance of civic duties;
- 11) seeking to conceal another crime;
- 12) seeking to obtain the victim's organ, tissue or cells;
- 13) seeking to express hatred towards a group of persons or a person belonging to it due to shall be punished by deprivation of liberty from eight to twenty years or by deprivation of liberty for life.

### 130 straipsnis. Murder in a state of sudden strong agitation

A person who killed a person in a state of sudden strong agitation due to unlawful or particularly insulting conduct of the victim towards him or a person close to him,

### 131 straipsnis. Murder of a newborn

A mother who killed her newborn due to a condition caused by childbirth shall be punished by arrest or by deprivation of liberty for up to five years.

### 132 straipsnis. Negligent deprivation of life

1. A person who through negligence deprived another person of life,

2. A person who through negligence deprived two or more persons of life,

3. A person who committed the act provided for in part 1 or 2 of this article while violating special safety rules of conduct established by legal acts,

4. A legal person shall also be held liable for the act provided for in part 3 of this article.

### 133 straipsnis. Incitement to suicide or driving to suicide

A person who incited a person to commit suicide or drove a person to suicide by cruel or deceitful conduct shall be punished by restriction of liberty or arrest, or deprivation of liberty for up to four years.

### 134 straipsnis. Assistance in suicide

A person who, at the request of a hopelessly ill person, assisted him in committing suicide shall be punished by community service or arrest, or deprivation of liberty for up to four years.
