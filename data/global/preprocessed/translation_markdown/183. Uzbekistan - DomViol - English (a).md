General part

Section one. General provisions

## Chapter I. Tasks and principles of the Criminal code

#### Article 1. Penal legislation of the Republic of Uzbekistan

The criminal legislation of the Republic of Uzbekistan is based on the Constitution and the universally recognized norms of international law and consists of this Code.

#### Article 2. Code tasks

The Criminal Code has the task of protecting from criminal encroachments the individual, their rights and freedoms, the interests of society and the state, property, the environment, peace, the security of mankind, and also the prevention of crimes, education of citizens in the spirit of observance of the Constitution and the laws of the republic.

For the implementation of these tasks the Code determines the bases and the principles of responsibility, what socially dangerous acts are crimes, establishes punishments and other measures of legal impact which can be applied to persons who committed socially dangerous acts.

#### Article 3. Principles of the Code

The Criminal Code is based on the principles of legality, equality of citizens before the law, democracy, humanity, justice, culpable responsibility, inevitability of responsibility.

#### Article 4. Principle of legality

Crime, punishability of an act and other legal consequences of its commission are determined only by the Criminal Code.

No one can be found guilty of committing a crime and subjected to punishment except by a court verdict and according to the law. A person found guilty of committing a crime has the rights and performs the duties established by the law. https://cis-legislation.com/document.fwx?rgn=1013 1/15

#### Article 5. Principle of equality of citizens before the law

Persons who committed crimes have the identical rights and obligations and are equal before the law without distinction as to sex, race, nationality, language, religion, social origin, beliefs, personal and social status.

#### Article 6. Principle of democratism

Public associations, self-government institutions of citizens or collectives can be involved in the cases provided by the law in the correction of persons who committed crimes.

#### Article 7. Principle of humanity

Punishment and other measures of legal impact do not aim at causing physical suffering or humiliation of human dignity.

To a person who committed a crime, a penalty shall be imposed or other measure of legal impact which is necessary and sufficient for their correction and the prevention of new crimes is applied. Severe measures of punishment can be appointed only on condition that the goals of punishment cannot be achieved by means of application of milder measures provided by the relevant article of the Special part of this Code.

#### Article 8. Concept of justice

Punishment or other measure of legal impact applied to a person guilty of committing a crime shall be just, that is correspond to the gravity of the crime, degree of guilt and public danger of their personality. No one can bear responsibility for the same crime twice.

#### Article 9. Principle of guilty responsibility

A person is subject to responsibility only for those socially dangerous acts in the commission of which their guilt, in the procedure established by the law, will be proved.

#### Article 10. Principle of inevitability of responsibility

Each person in whose act the elements of a crime are established shall be subject to responsibility.

- c) it is committed in the territory of Uzbekistan, and the criminal result occurred beyond its limits;
- d) forms in total or along with other acts a crime part of which is committed in the territory of Uzbekistan.

In case of commission of a crime on an air, ocean or river vessel which is outside Uzbekistan and not in the territory of a foreign state, responsibility comes under this Code if the said vessel is flying the flag or is registered in the port of Uzbekistan.

The question of responsibility of foreign citizens who according to current laws, international treaties or agreements are not subject to the jurisdiction of the courts of Uzbekistan, in case of commission of a crime by them in the territory of the Republic of Uzbekistan is resolved on the basis of rules of international law.

#### Article 12. Operation of the Code concerning persons who committed crimes outside

Uzbekistan

Citizens of the Republic of Uzbekistan, and also persons without citizenship who are permanently residing in Uzbekistan for the crimes committed in the territory of another state are subject to responsibility under this Code if they were not punished according to the court verdict of the state in the territory of which the crime was committed.

A citizen of Uzbekistan cannot be extradited for a crime committed in the territory of a foreign state unless otherwise provided by international treaties or agreements.

Foreign citizens, and also persons without citizenship who are not permanently residing in Uzbekistan for crimes committed outside its limits are subject to responsibility under this Code only in the cases provided by international treaties or agreements.

#### Article 13. Operation of the law in time

Crime and punishability of an act is determined by the law existing at the time of its commission. The time of commission of a crime is recognized as the time of commission of the socially dangerous act, unless an Article of this Code determines the moment of the completion of the crime as the moment of commission of action or failure to act. The time of commission of a crime is recognized as the time of occurrence of criminal consequences if an Article of this Code determines the completion of the crime as the moment of occurrence of such consequences.

The law eliminating the criminality of an act, reducing the penalty or otherwise improving the situation of a person, has retroactive force, that is extends to persons who committed the corresponding act before entry into force of such law including to persons serving or who served a sentence, but having a criminal record. The law establishing the criminality of an act, strengthening punishment or otherwise worsening the situation of a person, has no retroactive force.

Section second. Responsibility bases

## Chapter III. Crime

#### Article 14. Concept of crime

A crime is recognized as a culpable socially dangerous act (action or failure to act) prohibited by this Code under the threat of punishment.

An act which causes or creates a real threat of harm to the objects protected by this Code is recognized as socially dangerous.

#### Article 15. Classification of crimes

Crimes, depending on their nature and degree of public danger, are subdivided into: crimes not constituting a major public danger; less serious crimes; serious crimes; especially serious crimes.

Crimes not constituting a major public danger include intentional crimes for which the law prescribes custodial sanction not exceeding three years, and also crimes committed through negligence for which the law prescribes custodial sanction not exceeding five years.

Less serious crimes include intentional crimes for which the law prescribes custodial sanctions for a term of more than three years, but not exceeding five years, and also crimes committed through negligence for which the law prescribes custodial sanction for a term of more than five years. Serious crimes include intentional crimes for which the law prescribes custodial sanction for a term of more than five, but not exceeding ten years.

Especially serious crimes include intentional crimes for which the law prescribes custodial sanction for a term exceeding ten years or life imprisonment.

#### Article 16. Responsibility for crime and its basis

Responsibility for crime is the legal consequence of commission of a socially dangerous act which is expressed in condemnation, application of punishment or other measures of legal impact by a court to a person guilty of crime commission.

The basis of responsibility is commission of an act containing all elements of the corpus delicti provided by this Code.

## Chapter IV. Persons which are subject to responsibility

#### Article 17. Responsibility of physical persons

Physical, sane persons who had attained sixteen years of age before commission of the crime are subject to responsibility.

Persons who had attained fourteen years of age before commission of the crime are subject to responsibility for crimes provided by Articles 97, 98, 104 - 106, 118, 119, parts four - eight of Article 126-1, Articles 137, 164 - 166, 169, parts two and three of Article 173, Articles 220, 222, 247, 252, 263, 267, 271, parts two and three of Article 277 of this Code.

Persons who had attained eighteen years of age before commission of the crime are subject to responsibility for crimes provided by Articles 122, 123, 125-1, parts one and two of Article 126-1, Articles 127, 127-1, 128-1, 144, 146, 193-195, 205-210, 225, 226, 230-232, 234, 235, 279-302 of this Code.

Responsibility of persons who committed crime under the age of eighteen years applies in accordance with general provisions and taking into account the special features provided by Section Six of the General Part of this Code.

#### Article 18. Sanity

A person who during commission of a crime understood the socially dangerous nature of their actions (inaction) and controlled them is responsible.

A person who during commission of a socially dangerous act was in a state of insanity, that is could not realize the nature of their actions (inaction) or control them owing to chronic or temporary mental disorder, feeble-mindedness or other mental disorder, is not subject to responsibility.

To a person who committed a socially dangerous act in a state of insanity, compulsory measures of a medical nature may be imposed by a court.

#### Article 18-1. Responsibility of person suffering from mental disorder which does not

exclude sanity

A sane person who during commission of a crime owing to mental disorder could not fully realize the nature of their actions (inaction) or control them, is subject to responsibility. To a person suffering from mental disorder which does not exclude sanity, a court may impose compulsory measures of a medical nature along with punishment.

#### Article 19. Responsibility for crime committed in state of intoxication

A person who committed a crime in a state of alcohol intoxication or under the influence of drugs, their analogs, psychotropic or other substances affecting intellectual and volitional activities is not exempted from liability. Such state cannot be a basis for recognition of a person as insane.

## Chapter V. Guilt

#### Article 20. Forms of guilt

A person can be found guilty of a crime if the socially dangerous act provided by this Code was committed intentionally or through negligence.

#### Article 21. Intentional crime

A crime whose completion is determined by this Code as the moment of commission of a socially dangerous act is recognized as intentional if the person who committed it understood the socially dangerous nature of the act and wished its commission.

A crime whose completion is determined by this Code as the moment of occurrence of socially dangerous consequences may be committed with direct or indirect intent.

A crime is recognized as committed with direct intent if the person who committed it understood the socially dangerous nature of the act, foresaw its socially dangerous consequences and wished their occurrence.

A crime is recognized as committed with indirect intent if the person who committed it understood the socially dangerous nature of the act, foresaw its socially dangerous consequences and consciously allowed their occurrence.

#### Article 22. Negligent crime

A negligent crime is recognized as a socially dangerous act committed through recklessness or carelessness.

A crime is recognized as committed through recklessness if the person who committed it foresaw the possibility of occurrence of socially dangerous consequences of their conduct provided by law and, consciously failing to observe precautionary measures, groundlessly assumed that these consequences would not occur. A crime is recognized as committed through carelessness if the person who committed it did not foresee the possibility of occurrence of socially dangerous consequences of their conduct provided by law though they should have and could have foreseen them.

#### Article 23. Crime with dual guilt

If as a result of intentional crime commission a person causes other socially dangerous consequences through negligence with which the law connects increased responsibility, such crime is recognized as committed intentionally.

#### Article 24. Innocent causation of harm

An act is recognized as committed innocently if the person who committed it did not understand, should not have and could not have understood the socially dangerous nature of the act or did not foresee its socially dangerous consequences and based on the circumstances of the matter should not have and could not have foreseen them.

## Chapter VI. Uncompleted crime

#### Article 25. Preparation for crime and attempted crime

Preparation for a crime is recognized as an act of a person creating conditions for commission or concealment of an intentional crime interrupted before its commission due to circumstances beyond their control.

Attempted crime is recognized as the commencement of commission of an intentional crime, uncompleted due to circumstances beyond the person's control.

Responsibility for preparation and attempt applies under the same Article of the Special Part of this Code as for completed crime.

#### Article 26. Voluntary abandonment of crime commission

Voluntary abandonment of crime is recognized as the cessation by a person of preparatory actions or cessation of crime commission if the person understood the opportunity to complete the crime, and also prevention of occurrence of criminal result if the person understood the possibility of its occurrence.

Voluntary abandonment of crime commission excludes responsibility.

A person who voluntarily abandoned completion of a crime is subject to responsibility under this Code if the act actually committed by them contains all elements of another corpus delicti.

## Chapter VII. Complicity in crime

#### Article 27. Concept of complicity in crime

Complicity is recognized as joint participation of two or more persons in commission of an intentional crime.

#### Article 28. Types of accomplices

The following are recognized as accomplices in crime: perpetrators, organizers, instigators and accessories.

A perpetrator is a person who fully or partially directly commits a crime, or commits a crime using other persons who, under this Code, are not subjects of criminal liability, or by other means.

An organizer is a person who directs the preparation or commission of a crime.

An instigator is a person who incites another to commit a crime.

An accessory is a person who facilitates the commission of a crime by advice, instructions, provision of means or removal of obstacles, as well as a person who promises in advance to conceal the offender, the instruments, traces or means of committing the crime, or objects obtained by criminal means, and likewise a person who promises in advance to acquire or sell such objects.

#### Article 29. Forms of complicity

The following are recognized as forms of complicity in crime: simple complicity; complex complicity; organized group; criminal organization.

Simple complicity is participation in the commission of a crime by two or more persons without prior agreement.

Complex complicity is participation in the commission of a crime by two or more persons with prior agreement.

An organized group is the preliminary association of two or more persons into a group for joint criminal activity.

A criminal organization is the preliminary association of two or more organized groups for engaging in criminal activity.

#### Article 30. Limits of criminal liability for complicity in crime

Organizers, instigators and accessories bear criminal liability under the same Article of the Special Part of this Code as perpetrators.

Persons who created an organized group or criminal organization, or who direct them, bear criminal liability for all crimes committed by the criminal formation if they were covered by their intent. Failure to report to authorities a crime that is reliably known to be in preparation, being committed or having been committed, where not promised in advance, entails criminal liability only in the cases provided by Articles 155-1 and 241 of this Code.

Close relatives of a suspect, accused person or defendant are not subject to criminal liability for failure to report or concealment not promised in advance.

## Chapter VIII. Plurality of crimes

#### Article 32. Repetition of crimes

Repetition of crimes is the commission of two or more crimes at different times, provided for by the same part or Article, or, in cases specially specified in this Code, by different Articles of the Special Part, for none of which the person was convicted. Both completed crimes and punishable preparation for or attempts at crimes, as well as crimes committed in complicity, are recognized as repeated.

A crime is not recognized as repeated if the person was released from criminal liability or punishment for earlier committed acts.

A crime consisting of a series of identical criminal acts united by common intent and directed toward a single purpose, and constituting in total one continuing crime, is not recognized as repeated.

A crime consisting of prolonged failure to perform a duty, characterized by the continuous fulfillment of the elements of a single ongoing crime, is not recognized as repeated.

#### Article 33. Aggregate of crimes

Aggregate of crimes is the commission of two or more criminal acts provided for by different Articles or different parts of the same Article of the Special Part of this Code, for none of which the person was convicted and for which the person is subject to criminal liability. https://cis-legislation.com/document.fwx?rgn=1013 8/15

If an act committed by a person contains the elements of crimes provided for by different parts of the same Article of the Special Part of this Code, criminal liability is imposed under that part of the Article which establishes the more severe punishment.

#### Article 34. Recidivism of crimes

Recidivism is the commission by a person of a new intentional crime after conviction for a previously committed intentional crime.

Dangerous recidivism is the commission by a person of a new intentional crime identical to that for which the person was previously convicted, or, in cases specially specified in this Code, under other Articles of the Special Part.

Especially dangerous recidivism is the commission by a person of a new intentional crime for which the person is sentenced to imprisonment for a term of at least five years, where:

- a) the person commits an especially serious crime, if the person was previously convicted for an especially serious crime or twice for serious crimes, and for each of them a custodial sentence of at least five years was imposed;
- b) the person commits a serious crime, if the person was previously convicted twice for serious crimes or in any sequence for a serious and an especially serious crime, and for each of them a custodial sentence of at least five years was imposed.

A person may be recognized as an especially dangerous recidivist according to a court verdict. When considering the question of recognizing a person as an especially dangerous recidivist, criminal records based on sentences of courts of other states may also be considered.

When considering the question of recognizing a person as an especially dangerous recidivist, criminal records for crimes committed by the person under the age of eighteen, as well as criminal records expunged or removed in accordance with the procedure established by law, are not considered.

Articles of the Special Part of this Code providing for criminal liability for commission of a crime by an especially dangerous recidivist are applied in cases where the person was recognized in accordance with the procedure established by law as an especially dangerous recidivist before committing that crime.

#### Article 36. Insignificance of act

An act or omission, although falling under the elements of an act provided for by this Code as a crime, but lacking public danger due to its insignificance, is not a crime.

#### Article 37. Justifiable defense

An action committed under circumstances of justifiable defense is not a crime, that is, when protecting the person or rights of the defender or another person, the interests of society or the state against unlawful encroachment by causing harm to the encroacher, provided that the limits of justifiable defense were not exceeded.

Exceeding the limits of justifiable defense is recognized as a manifest disproportion between the defense and the nature and danger of the encroachment.

The right to justifiable defense belongs to a person irrespective of the availability of an opportunity to seek help from other persons or authorities or to avoid the encroachment in another way. Intentional provocation of an encroachment for the purpose of causing harm is not justifiable defense.

#### Article 38. Emergency

An act that caused harm to rights and interests protected by law, committed under circumstances of emergency, that is, to eliminate a danger threatening the person or rights of that person or other citizens, or the interests of society or the state, if the danger under the circumstances could not be eliminated by other means and if the harm caused is less significant than the harm prevented, is not a crime.

An act committed under circumstances of emergency is lawful if the person did not exceed its limits. Exceeding the limits of emergency is causing harm to rights and interests protected by law when the danger could be eliminated by other means or when the harm caused is more significant than the harm prevented.

In assessing the lawfulness of an act committed under circumstances of emergency, the nature and degree of the danger prevented, the reality and imminence of its occurrence, the actual ability of the person to prevent it, the person's state of mind in the circumstances, and other facts of the case are considered. #### Article 40. Execution of the order or other obligation

Harm caused in the course of lawful execution of an order or other directive, as well as job responsibilities, is not a crime.

Harm caused to rights and interests protected by law is not a crime in the case of justified professional or economic risk for achievement of a socially useful purpose.

In the case of justified professional or economic risk, liability for harm caused does not arise even if the desired socially useful result was not achieved and the harm was more significant than the socially useful purpose pursued.

The risk is not recognized as justified if it obviously entailed a threat of death to people, environmental disaster or other grave consequences.

## Chapter X. Concept, purposes and types of punishments

#### Article 42. Concept and purposes of punishment

Punishment is a measure of coercion applied on behalf of the state by court verdict to a person found guilty of a crime, and consisting in the deprivation or restriction of certain rights and freedoms of the convict as provided by law.

Punishment is applied for the purpose of reformation, preventing the continuation of criminal activity, and also preventing the commission of new crimes both by the convict and by other persons.

Punishments in the form of restriction on service or the direction in disciplinary part are applied only to military personnel.

Deprivation of certain right may be applied not only as a principal punishment, but also as an additional punishment.

#### Article 44. Penalty

A fine is a monetary payment to the state treasury imposed on a person found guilty in amounts established by this Code.

A fine is established ranging from five up to six hundred basic settlement sizes.

In case of evasion by a person convicted to pay a fine as punishment within the terms established for enforcement, or impossibility of recovery of the fine during the term established for enforcement due to absence of property of the debtor on which recovery can be levied, and also in case of failure to pay the fine after the period of delay or violation of conditions of payment of the fine by installments, a court replaces the outstanding amount of the fine with punishment in the form of obligatory social work, corrective labor, restriction on service, restriction of liberty or imprisonment. In this case obligatory social work is appointed at the rate of two and a half hours for the fine amount corresponding to one basic settlement size, but for a period of not more than four hundred eighty hours, corrective labor, restriction on service, restriction of liberty or imprisonment - at the rate of one month for the fine amount corresponding to sixteen basic settlement sizes, but for a period of not more than three years.

#### Article 45. Deprivation of certain right

Deprivation of certain right of a person consists in prohibition imposed on the guilty person for the term appointed by a court to hold certain positions at companies, in organizations or institutions or to be engaged in certain activities. The type of such position or activity is determined by a court in the judgment of conviction.

Deprivation of certain right is established for crimes which are directly connected with the position held by the guilty person or their activities for a period of one year up to five years as a primary punishment and for a period of one year up to three years as an additional punishment.

If deprivation of certain right is not appointed to a guilty person as a primary punishment, it may be appointed by a court as an additional punishment to any kind provided by the relevant article of the Special Part of this Code.

In case of imposition of deprivation of certain right as an additional punishment to imprisonment, the restriction extends to all the time of serving by the convict of the primary punishment and, moreover, for the term established by the sentence. In case of imposition of this punishment as additional to other primary punishments and in case of conditional conviction, its term is calculated from the moment of the entry of the sentence into legal force.

Punishment in the form of deprivation of the right to engage in business activity concerning persons who are engaged in business activity, except as specified, which caused the death of a person or other grave consequences is not imposed.

#### Article 45-1. Obligatory social jobs

Obligatory social work consists in mandatory involvement of the convict in performance of unpaid socially useful work. Where the convict has work or studies, obligatory social work is performed during time free from work or study.

Places (objects) at which convicts may perform obligatory social work and the type of obligatory social work are determined by the bodies supervising execution of this punishment.

Obligatory social work is established for a period of one hundred twenty to four hundred eighty hours and not more than four hours per day within six months, and in case of circumstances not depending on the convict - up to one year.

Obligatory social work is not applied to persons who have reached retirement age, persons who have not reached sixteen years, to pregnant women, women having children under three years, to persons with disability of the first and second group, to military personnel, foreign citizens and persons not permanently residing in the Republic of Uzbekistan.

In case of evasion by a convict from serving the sentence, a court replaces the unexpired term of obligatory social work with punishment in the form of restriction of liberty or imprisonment at the rate of one day of restriction of liberty or imprisonment for four hours of obligatory social work. Time of evasion from the served sentence is not credited.

#### Article 46. Corrective works

Corrective labor consists in mandatory involvement of a person in work with deduction of ten to thirty percent of the salary to the state treasury and is performed according to the court verdict at the place of employment of the convict or in other places determined by the bodies supervising execution of this punishment.

Corrective labor is established for a period of six months up to three years.

Corrective labor is not applied to persons who have reached retirement age, persons not able-bodied, pregnant women and women having children under three years, to military personnel. In case of evasion totaling more than one tenth of the appointed term of serving corrective labor, a court replaces the unserved sentence of corrective labor with punishment in the form of restriction of liberty or imprisonment for the same term. Time of evasion from the served sentence is not credited.

#### Article 47. Restriction on service

Restriction on service consists in deprivation of a serviceman serving military service under contract of certain rights and privileges with deduction to the state treasury in the amount of ten to thirty percent of monetary pay during the term determined by a court.

Restriction on service is applied for a period of two months up to three years in cases stipulated in Articles of the Special Part of this Code. For a crime not constituting great public danger or a negligent crime which did not cause grave consequences, a court may, considering the circumstances of the case and the identity of the convict, instead of imprisonment for a term of not more than three years, or corrective labor apply restriction on service for the same term.

During the term of punishment in the form of restriction on service the convict cannot be promoted, a military or special rank is not assigned and this time is not credited to length of service for assignment of the next military or special rank, and also for awarding of pension.
