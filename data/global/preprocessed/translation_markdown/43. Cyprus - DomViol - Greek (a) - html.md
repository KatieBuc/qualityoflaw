# 1. This Law shall be cited as the Violence in the Family (Prevention and Protection of Victims) Law of 2000.

## 1.

This Law shall be cited as the Violence in the Family (Prevention and Protection of Victims) Law of 2000.

## 2. In this Law, unless the context otherwise requires—

"minor person" means a person who has not completed the eighteenth year of age; "competent person" means for the purposes of article 10 of this Law any police officer, family counsellor or welfare officer; "violence" means violence as defined in article 3 of this Law; "typing" includes printing by any means; "statement" includes any narration of events made either in words or in any other manner; "Court" means a competent court exercising criminal jurisdiction;

"Committee" means the Advisory Committee established under article 7 of this Law; "therapeutic treatment for self-control" means the therapeutic treatment referred to in article 25 of this Law; "deposition" includes interview; "family member" means— (a) a man and a woman who—

(i) have contracted a lawful marriage whether or not the marriage subsists, or

(ii) cohabit or cohabited as husband and wife; (b) parents of the persons referred to in paragraph (a); (c) children of the persons referred to in paragraph (a) whether they are natural or adopted children of one or both of the said persons as well as the grandchildren of the persons referred to in paragraph (a); (d) any person who resides with any of the above persons; "video recording" means the recording by any device in moving images of objects, events, organisms and persons whether they speak or move or not which can be reproduced and presented using any technical means; "family residence" means the place where the victim of violence has his usual residence, irrespective of which of the two spouses or other occupants it belongs to or irrespective of the percentages of ownership;

"Family Counsellor" means the Family Counsellor appointed under article 6 of this Law; "Minister" means the Minister of Labour and Social Insurance

"Psychiatrist" means a registered doctor under the Registration of Doctors Law, recognised as holding the specialty of psychiatry under the Doctors (Special Qualifications) Regulations

"Psychologist" means a registered professional psychologist under the Registration of Professional Psychologists Law. 3.—(1) Violence, for the purposes of this Law, means any act, omission or behaviour by which physical, sexual or psychological harm is caused to any family member by another family member and includes violence exercised for the purpose of achieving sexual contact without the victim's consent, as well as the restriction of his freedom.

(2) Notwithstanding the interpretation of the term "violence" on the basis of subsection (1), the above meaning includes the offences referred to in articles 4(2) and 5 of this Law as well as the offence referred to in article 147 of the Criminal Code.

(3) An act or behaviour which constitutes violence, on the basis of subsections (1) and (2) of this article, or an offence, on the basis of articles 174, 175 and 177 of the Criminal Code when committed in the presence of a minor family member, is deemed to be violence exercised against the said minor insofar as it is capable of causing psychological harm to him. The said act or behaviour constitutes an offence punishable on the basis of subsection (4) of this article.

(4) Any person who exercises violence on the basis of subsection (1) commits an offence under this Law, which is punishable, except in the case of common assault which is punishable by two years' imprisonment and in the case where a more severe penalty is provided in another law or in this Law, with imprisonment of up to five years or with a fine of up to three thousand pounds or with both penalties.

4.—(1) Where the offences referred to in the first column of subsection (2) below are committed by one family member against another family member, they are deemed, for the purposes of this Law, to be of increased seriousness and the Court, in cases where the charge is based on the articles of the Criminal Code referred to in the second column of subsection (2), may impose the increased penalties provided in the third column of the same subsection instead of the penalties provided in the said articles of the Criminal Code.

(2) The offences to which subsection (1) refers are the following:

## 5.

Notwithstanding the provisions of any law, the offenses of rape, according to articles 144 and 145 of the Criminal Code, and attempted rape, according to article 146 of the same Code, may be considered to have been committed by a spouse against a spouse, if, based on the facts of the case, these offenses would be constituted, in the event that the perpetrator and the victim were not married, and are punished according to the provisions of the Criminal Code.

6.—(1) The Minister appoints suitable persons to perform the duties of Family Counselor for the purpose of better implementation of the provisions of this Law.

(2) The Family Counselor exercises the following powers: (α) Receives complaints regarding possible exercise of violence and conducts necessary investigations; (β) advises, guides and mediates for the mitigation of family problems that may have led or may lead to the exercise of violence; (γ) makes arrangements for the immediate medical examination of the complainant and where he deems it necessary accompanies him; (δ) makes a report to the Police for the investigation of any commission of a criminal offense; (ε) conducts upon instructions of the Court investigations regarding the financial situation of the family generally and the perpetrator specifically in the case that an exclusion order may be issued; (στ) conducts investigations and makes arrangements regarding the accommodation of the accused or his family in case of issuance of an exclusion order; (ζ) proceeds immediately with all necessary arrangements for medical or other examination of a child in respect of whom there is reasonable suspicion of abuse by a family member; (η) exercises any other power that the Minister assigns to him: (θ) in cases where a minor-

(i) is hosted in a shelter either being himself a victim of violence or being a witness in a case of domestic violence or

(ii) following a complaint by his parent, resides in a residence other than his family residence, proceeds immediately with all necessary arrangements, so that all minor members of the family in relation to which a complaint has been submitted to the Police for commission of an offense of violence causing psychological harm or in relation to which there is reasonable suspicion for the commission of an offense of violence causing psychological harm, receive examination by a psychologist or by a psychiatrist.

(3) The Family Counselor, in the performance of his duties may request the assistance of any state official, the police and any other suitable person.

(4) The Family Counselor in the exercise of the powers referred to in paragraph (α) of subsection (2) of this article acts, after written approval of the Director of Social Welfare Services, in accordance with the provisions of article 5(1) of the Criminal Procedure Law and applies to the same extent and in the same manner the provisions of subsections (2), (3) and (4) of the same article.

(5) In case of information or complaint for commission of an offense of violence against a minor, the Family Counselor may, if he deems it appropriate depending on the seriousness of the complaint or information, request the views, advice and opinion of the multidisciplinary team which is appointed pursuant to article 8 of this Law for the better handling of the case and report the fact to the Director of Social Welfare Services.

(6) The Family Counselor exercises the powers granted to him by paragraphs (ζ) and (θ) of subsection (2) of this article, without the consent of the person or persons having parental care for the said minor being necessary and subsequently reports the case to the Police, provided that the Attorney General of the Republic is informed immediately and in writing of this fact.

(7)(α) The Director of Social Welfare Services may exercise the powers granted to him by article 4 and other relevant articles of the Children Law or any other law that would replace it in cases where the grounds exist for exercise of the powers granted by paragraph (ζ) of subsection (2) of this article to the Family Counselor. (β) The Director of Social Welfare Services may, if the Family Counselor is unable or refuses to exercise the powers referred to in subsection (4) above or if he deems it appropriate weighing the seriousness of the case to act himself pursuant to the said subsection instead of the Family Counselor, exercise them himself or assign them to another experienced official of his Department.

7.—(1) An Advisory Committee is established for the purpose of prevention and combating of domestic violence. Specifically the Committee— (α) Monitors the problem of domestic violence in Cyprus; (β) proceeds with the information and enlightenment of the public and professionals by various means, including special conferences, training programs and seminars; (γ) promotes scientific research regarding domestic violence; (δ) promotes services for addressing all aspects of the problem of domestic violence; (ε) monitors the effectiveness of relevant services operating, as well as the implementation and observance of relevant legislation.

(2) The Committee is composed of persons who are appointed in their personal capacity by the Council of Ministers and have knowledge and experience of the subject. The appointments are made from the public and private sector. The members must not exceed eleven and their term of office is five years.

The persons appointed from the public sector are selected from the Ministries and from the services referred to in subsection (4) of this article and the persons appointed from the private sector are selected from members of associations or organizations involved in the prevention and combating of domestic violence in equal proportion.

(3) One member of the Committee is appointed by the Council of Ministers as chairman and has the responsibility to convene meetings and preside over the meetings and another member is designated as substitute of the chairman in case of his absence.

(4) The Ministries and services referred to in subsection (2) of this article are—

- The Ministry of Health;

- The Ministry of Justice and Public Order;

- The Ministry of Education and Culture (Educational Psychologist);

- The Social Welfare Services of the Ministry of Labour and Social Insurance;

- The Law Office;

- The Police.

(5) The Committee may employ scientific or other necessary personnel and have an Office.

(6) The Committee regulates by internal regulations matters of procedure concerning it.

8.—(1) The Council of Ministers, by its notification published in the Official Gazette of the Republic, may establish a multidisciplinary or multidisciplinary teams with members persons who possess the necessary qualifications and the necessary experience for the provision of advice, views, opinions, as well as any other assistance, regarding the better treatment of minors or other persons who are victims of violence.

(2) The members of the multidisciplinary team are appointed from a list of persons which the Minister prepares and submits to the Council of Ministers:

Provided that the members of the team that will deal with matters concerning minor victims of violence must have the following specialties:

- Child Psychiatrist;
- Paediatrician;
- Clinical Psychologist;
- Social Worker of the Ministry of Labour and Social Insurance responsible for child matters:

Provided further that the Minister shall have the right to include in the team any person who has such other qualifications, which the Minister considers necessary.

(3) The Council of Ministers may include in the notification by which the multidisciplinary team is appointed or in a subsequent notification regulations for the better functioning of the team.

## 9.

In case of complaint, the testimony of the victim is taken by a police officer of the same sex, unless otherwise requested by the victim or the family counsellor when the victim is a minor.

10.—(1) Subject to the provisions of subsection (2) of this article, in the trial of violence offences under the provisions of this or other law, videotaped testimony given to a competent person by any person who is a victim of violence or witness to the commission of an offence in violation of the provisions of this Law may, with the permission of the court, be adduced as evidence.

(2) For the application of the provisions of subsection (1) of this article, the following conditions and limitations apply: (α) The videotaping must relate to the case under trial· (β) Permission is not granted for the adduction of videotaped testimony—

(i) if the person whose testimony was videotaped cannot appear in court for cross-examination (if this is requested under this article)·

(ii) if the rules for taking videotaped testimony referred to in article 11 of this Law were not followed·

(iii) if the court, taking into account all the circumstances of the case, finds that the interests of the administration of justice require that the videotaped testimony not be accepted- (γ) a transcribed and typed sound track of the videotape on which the testimony was recorded is presented together with the videotaped testimony.

(3) Any statement contained in videotaped testimony which is accepted as evidence under this article is considered as direct oral testimony of the person making the statement and consequently— (α) It is accepted as evidence of any fact which would be accepted in the case of direct oral testimony. (β) No such statement may be used as corroborative evidence of other testimony by the same witness.

(4) The application of the provisions of subsection (1) of this article does not affect the power of the Court to exclude any admissible evidence if it finds that this serves the interests of justice.

## 11. The rules for taking videotaped testimony are as follows:

(α) The name, address, occupation and capacity of the person taking the testimony as well as of the person operating the video camera are stated or recorded before the commencement of the testimony· (β) the place, date and time of commencement of taking the testimony as well as the time the testimony concluded are stated or recorded· (γ) the name, address, occupation and other details concerning the person giving the testimony are stated or recorded· (δ) a statement by the person taking the testimony to the person giving the testimony that it will be videotaped and that it may be presented in Court as evidence and a statement by the person giving the testimony that they agree to the taking of the videotaped testimony are recorded. Furthermore, this statement is recorded and signed by the person giving the testimony with relevant certification by the person taking the testimony. This provision does not apply in the case of a minor who is not accompanied by the person having parental care or by a welfare officer or family counsellor. In the case where they are accompanied by any of the above persons, that person gives the consent.

(ε) [Deleted]

12.—(1) The Court, when examining an application for the presentation of videotaped testimony, may, if in its opinion the interests of justice so require, order that certain parts of the videotape not be presented as evidence. The Court in exercising this discretion takes into account the possible harm that may be done to the accused or to any accused if the accused are more than one, and to what extent this outweighs the benefit of presenting the videotaped testimony or part thereof. Also the Court takes into account any pressures exerted on the witness with the purpose of forcing them not to appear and testify in Court.

(2) The Court may order that parts of the videotaped testimony or of the typed transcription of the sound track of the videotape be deleted if it should find that they do not constitute admissible evidence or if it finds this expedient in the exercise of its powers under subsection (4) of article 10 of this Law.

## 13.

In cases where videotaped testimony is taken under article 10 of this Law, the person whose testimony was videotaped is called as a witness by the party that requested the presentation of the videotaped testimony, and this witness is made available to the other party for purposes of cross-examination subject to the provisions of article 19 of this Law. On points covered by the videotaped testimony, examination-in-chief is not conducted except with the permission of the Court:

Provided that in the case where the witness is called to testify for purposes of cross-examination, the provisions of article 55 of the Criminal Procedure Law shall apply.

## 14.

Without prejudice to the provisions of article 10 of the Evidence Law, a complaint made by a victim of a violence offence to any police officer, family counsellor, welfare officer, psychologist, doctor, including psychiatrist, who examines the victim, educator, members of the Advisory Committee, member of the Association for the Prevention and Treatment of Domestic Violence or members of the victim's close family environment within a reasonable time from its commission, constitutes evidence.

15.—(1) The Court may, following an application by the police, issue a warrant for the arrest of any person who is complained against for any act of violence under this Law.

(2) A person arrested under subsection (1) is brought before the Court within twenty-four hours of their arrest, to be charged with the violence offence or for a remand warrant to be issued under article 24 of the Criminal Procedure Law.

(3) Inquiries are conducted and the case is tried without delay. Until the case is tried, the Court may either order the detention of the accused or allow their release, after they give satisfactory security that they will appear before the Court on the date of the hearing of the case and that they will comply with the conditions that the Court deems necessary to impose for the protection of family members, including the condition not to visit or harass in any way a member of their family.

(4) The Attorney General of the Republic may consent to the trial of a case of violence under this Law by a judge exercising criminal jurisdiction.

## 16.

The Court may find the accused guilty on the sole testimony of the victim provided that it was not possible under the circumstances to secure corroborative testimony.

17.—(1) If during the examination of a minor by a psychiatrist or psychologist for the purposes of assessment or psychotherapy reference is made by the minor that they suffered abuse from any person, the testimony of the psychiatrist or psychologist may be admitted in court as an exception to the hearsay rule.

(2) The court shall not convict any person based solely on the testimony referred to in subsection (1) above, unless such testimony is corroborated in material particulars by other independent testimony which may include expert testimony.

18.—(1) In the trial of cases for the commission of offences of violence, the Court— (a) Orders that the whole or part of the case be heard in camera and (b) may order that the testimony of any victim of violence and any other person for whom there is reasonable suspicion that they are at any risk or threat because they will testify as a witness, or that their testimony may be adversely affected, be taken in the absence of the accused after giving all those instructions and making all necessary arrangements so that the accused receives notice of the testimony of the said witness and cross-examines them.

(2) Without prejudice to the generality of subsection (1), the measures mentioned below may be used for the purposes of protecting witnesses: (a) The placement of a special partition; or (b) the use of closed-circuit television; or (c) use of any other means or system, in such a way that the witness is not visible to the accused and vice versa. In order to safeguard the rights of the accused, appropriate technological arrangements or other facilities should be made in the above cases so that the accused can follow the proceedings audibly and give instructions to their lawyer:

- 19. The Court may intervene in the cross-examination of minors or other victims of violence and give appropriate instructions to avoid their intimidation, from an aggressive or intense manner of asking questions or from questions containing threats of any form.
- 20. Notwithstanding the provisions of section 14 of the Evidence Law, a spouse of a person accused of an offence of violence within the meaning of this Law is a competent witness if they are the victim of violence and is a competent and compellable witness if the victim of violence is another member of the family.

21.—(1) The Court may, during or after the trial of a case of violence with a minor as victim, order for any period of time it deems necessary the removal of the said victim and their placement in a safe place or the assignment of their care to the Director of Social Welfare Services of the Ministry of Labour and Social Insurance.

(2) The Court may issue an interim order for the removal of a minor victim by applying the provisions of section 22 of this Law.

22.—(1) The Court may, following an application by a member of the family or the police or the prosecutor or the Attorney General of the Republic or the Family Counsellor or another person acting on behalf of any of them, issue an interim exclusion order of the suspect or removal order of a minor victim, until a criminal case is registered and tried against the accused for a criminal offence of violence.

(2) The Court issues the order at any time following an application accompanied by an affidavit of the victim or, in the case of a minor victim, of any person who is in a position to have direct knowledge of the facts or by any other evidence, which creates a prima facie risk of exercise of violence or repetition of violence, including testimonies of the victim or other persons in any form, certificates, confirmations and other evidence under this or another law.

(3)(a) The interim order is valid for a period of up to eight days from the day of its service on the suspect and is returnable to the Court within that period at a time and day to be fixed by the Registrar. (b) On the day and time fixed by the Registrar, the Court hears the suspect or any affected or interested person who will appear and decides whether to terminate the validity of the order or whether to extend it for up to eight additional days. (c) The Court may further extend the validity of the order for up to eight days in each case, without however the total validity of the order exceeding twenty-four days before the registration of criminal proceedings against the suspect. (d) The Court may, after the registration of criminal proceedings against the suspect, issue or extend an exclusion order or removal order of a minor victim with validity until the trial of the case.

23.—(1) The Court may issue against a person accused of committing any offence of violence, under this Law, an order, which shall be valid for the period and with the terms it may set and which prohibits them from entering or remaining in the family home. This order is called an "exclusion order".

(2) For the issuance of the exclusion order it is required— (a) To be proved to the satisfaction of the Court that the accused has a history of repeated acts of violence against members of their family or that they had two convictions in the last two years for similar offences; or (b) the violence exercised to have caused such actual physical, sexual or psychological harm, as to endanger the life, physical integrity or sexual or psychological health of the victims or (c) the accused refuses to undergo therapeutic self-control treatment imposed as a condition for the purposes of applying section 33 of the Criminal Code or otherwise.

(3) The Court in the exclusion order it issues specifies a date before the expiry of the exclusion period during which it examines the possibility of extending or modifying this order.

During the above examination, the Court hears the views of the accused, of the complainant male or female and of any other person affected by the issuance of the order, except where these are minors and it is not deemed appropriate to testify against the accused, as well as the views of the competent services.

(4) The accused may request a review or cancellation of the order before the expiry of the period specified therein.

(5) Exclusion orders are imposed both instead of any other penalty subject to the provisions of subsection (6) of this section or together with other penalties which the Court has the power to impose under any other law.

(6) The Court does not issue an exclusion order in cases where it simultaneously imposes a sentence of imprisonment for any period exceeding six months. In cases where a sentence of imprisonment of less than six months is imposed, an exclusion order may be issued simultaneously with the sentence of imprisonment but the validity will commence after the release of the accused.

(7) A person against whom an exclusion order has been issued and while the said order is in force violates any of the terms contained therein commits an offence punishable by imprisonment of up to two years. The provisions of section 15 of this Law for expedited trial of violence cases apply also in cases of commission of offences in violation of the provisions of this subsection.

24.—(1) When issuing an exclusion order, the Court takes into account the ownership of the family home and issues an appropriate order regarding the residence of the suspect or accused or their family as follows: (a) If the accused does not have a share of ownership in the family home exceeding one half, the Court does not examine the issue of the accused's residence, but refers the issue for examination to the Family Counsellor; (b) if the accused has an undivided share of ownership in the family home exceeding one half, the Court examines the issue of the accused's residence and consequently gives the instructions it deems necessary regarding the residence of themselves or their family or members thereof.

(2) The Court, when giving instructions in the case as paragraph (b) of subsection (1) of this section, examines among other things the financial means of the accused and their family, in relation to the residence of themselves or their family or any member thereof, and provides the accused with the right to apply to the Court within a reasonable time and request the change of address of the family home for which the order applies, if they find suitable accommodation for their family to move.

(3) For the purposes of this section, the term "suitable accommodation" means the accommodation which the accused finds for their family pursuant to subsection (2) of this section, which must be such as to ensure as far as possible the smooth continuation of the living and functioning of the accused's family therein.

25.—(1) The Court, if it deems it appropriate, may, instead of imposing any other penalty on the accused, accept their request to place them under probation, pursuant to the Probation and Other Methods of Treatment of Offenders Law, with the special condition that they will undergo therapeutic self-control treatment by specialists or with other conditions that the Court will deem necessary, to avoid the repetition of acts of violence.

(2) The Court, if it deems it appropriate, may impose on the accused a sentence of imprisonment with suspension irrespective of the provisions of section 5 of the Law on the Conditional Suspension of the Execution of a Sentence of Imprisonment in Certain Cases, and place the accused under probation during the suspension and under the special condition or any other conditions referred to in subsection (1) above.

26.—(1) A Fund is established with the name "Fund for Victims of Violence" for the achievement and promotion of the purposes of this Law. The Fund shall be a legal person and in its name shall have perpetual succession and a seal of a specified form and shall have the power to acquire, hold and dispose of property, to contract, to bring and defend actions or other legal proceedings and generally to do all that is necessary for the purposes of its establishment, utilization and development.

(2) All contributions, donations, legacies and grants shall be deposited in the Fund.

(3) All contributions to the Fund, of whatever form, shall be deemed to be made for charitable purposes.

(4) Unless the contribution is made for a specific purpose, the disposal of amounts deposited in the Fund shall be made at the discretion of the Advisory Committee based on the priorities and programs that it will draw up from time to time.

(5) From this Fund, amounts shall be allocated for the purposes of addressing immediate needs, finding accommodation pursuant to section 24 of this Law, supporting victims of violence and other purposes that the Committee would deem appropriate and the Minister would approve.

27.—(1) The Fund is managed by the Advisory Committee which shall convene specifically as Manager of the Fund.

(2) The Court and the Attorney General of the Republic shall have and exercise in relation to the Fund the same powers that they have according to the provisions of the Associations and Foundations Law or other relevant law, as if the Fund were a charitable foundation and had been registered in accordance with the provisions of the said Law.

28.—(1) Subject to subsection (2) of this section, the Advisory Committee acting as Manager of the Fund may invest and utilize monies of the Fund in any manner it would deem necessary for the strengthening and better development thereof.

(2) In the event that the Advisory Committee intends to use monies of the Fund for purposes of investment or development thereof and the amount to be used exceeds half of the total deposits in the Fund, then the Committee must, before proceeding with the said action, submit its decision to the Minister for approval.

## 29.

The Advisory Committee acting as Manager of the Fund with the approval of the Minister issues regulations for the better operation of the Fund, including the manner of disposal of the resources and assets of the Fund.

## 31.

The establishment and operation of a shelter for providing safe accommodation to victims of violence is permitted and in the case where a protection shelter operates by virtue of a certificate of suitability issued by the Minister by virtue of this article, the victims of violence residing in it receive legal protection from any harassment.

## 32.

An accused or any person on his behalf or on his own harasses or intimidates a victim of violence or a witness in a case of violence or their relative in any place, in a manner that affects or may affect the investigation or trial of a case of violence or that causes mental distress to a victim of violence or a witness in a case of violence knowing that they are a victim of violence or a witness in a case of violence, commits an offence punishable by imprisonment up to three years or by a fine up to one thousand five hundred pounds or by both such penalties and in the case where the harassment or intimidation is done against a victim residing in a shelter the offence is punishable by imprisonment up to five years or by a fine up to three thousand pounds or by both such penalties.

## 33.

The provisions of article 15 of this Law for expedited trial of a case of violence apply also in cases of offences committed in violation of the provisions of article 32 of this Law.

34.—(1) When an offence is reported under this Law, the name and address of the victim of violence or of the complaining person as well as of the person against whom the complaint is made as well as other details which may lead to the identification of their identity, may not be disclosed or published by any mass media or otherwise.

(2) Violation of the provisions of this paragraph constitutes an offence punishable by imprisonment for a period up to two years or by a fine up to one thousand pounds or by both such penalties.

35.—(1) Notwithstanding the provisions of any other law, it is prohibited to deliver, receive or publish: (a) any video-recorded testimony of a victim or witness taken by virtue of article 10 of this Law to or from any person; (b) any other than that referred to in the above paragraph (a) testimony of a victim or witness to an offence of violence, which was taken in any manner other than that referred to in the above paragraph (a), to or from any person not related to the investigation, prosecution or trial of the case.

(2) Notwithstanding the provisions of this article or any other law, during the trial of offences of violence, by virtue of this Law, a person who gives video-recorded testimony by virtue of article 10 or an accused who is called to plead and does not confess guilt, are entitled by their written application to the prosecuting authority to request to be provided with a copy of the transcribed and typed audio tape of the videotape on which the testimony was recorded, which will be produced as evidence before the Court:

Provided that the above persons may, after their written application to the prosecuting authority, be permitted to view the said videotape on which the testimony was recorded.

(3) Whoever violates the provisions of this article commits an offence punishable by imprisonment up to five years or by a fine up to three thousand pounds or by both such penalties.

35A. Whoever fails to report a case of violence against a minor person or a person with serious mental or psychological disabilities, which comes to his knowledge, commits an offence and upon conviction is liable to imprisonment up to two years or to a fine up to one thousand pounds or to both such penalties.

36.—(1) The Committee prepares budgets of revenue and expenditure for the full and effective exercise and fulfilment of its responsibilities and the purposes of its establishment which are approved by the competent Ministry and keeps full accounting books.

(2) The revenue of the Committee comes from state grants, the amount of which is decided by the Council of Ministers.

(3) The Committee keeps full accounting books and its accounts are submitted to and audited by the Auditor General of the Republic in the same manner as accounts in the Public Service are audited:

Provided that the Auditor General of the Republic may proceed to audit the accounting books of the Committee whenever he may deem it necessary.

## 37.

The Committee prepares and submits to the Minister and to the House of Representatives an annual report on its activities as a Committee.

38.—(1) The Council of Ministers may issue Regulations for the better implementation of the provisions of this Law.

(2) Without prejudice to the generality of subsection (1), the Council of Ministers may regulate by Regulations the registration, operation and anything else related to the protection shelter, including the imposition of fees and charges.

## 39.

The Supreme Court may issue Procedural Regulations for the better implementation of the provisions of articles 15, 21, 22, 23, 24 and 25 of this Law.
