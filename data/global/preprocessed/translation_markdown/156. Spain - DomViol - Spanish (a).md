JUAN CARLOS I

KING OF SPAIN

To all who see and understand this.

Know: That the Cortes Generales have approved and I hereby sanction the following organic law.

STATEMENT OF REASONS

I

Gender-based violence is not a problem that affects the private sphere. On the contrary, it manifests itself as the most brutal symbol of the inequality existing in our society. It is violence directed at women for the very fact of being women, for being considered by their aggressors as lacking the minimum rights of freedom, respect and decision-making capacity.

Our Constitution incorporates in its article 15 the right of all to life and physical and moral integrity, without being subjected in any case to torture or to inhuman or degrading punishment or treatment. Furthermore, our Constitution continues, these rights bind all public authorities and only by law may their exercise be regulated.

The United Nations at the Fourth World Conference of 1995 already recognised that violence against women is an obstacle to achieving the objectives of equality, development and peace and violates and undermines the enjoyment of human rights and fundamental freedoms. It also defines it broadly as a manifestation of historically unequal power relations between women and men. There already exists even a technical definition of battered woman syndrome consisting of "the aggressions suffered by women as a consequence of the sociocultural conditions that act on the male and female gender, placing her in a position of subordination to men and manifested in the three basic spheres of personal relationships: abuse within couple relationships, sexual assault in social life and harassment in the workplace."

In Spanish reality, aggressions against women have a special incidence, with greater awareness today than in previous periods about this, thanks, to a large extent, to the effort made by women's organisations in their fight against all forms of gender-based violence. It is no longer an "invisible crime," but produces a collective rejection and evident social alarm.

II

Public authorities cannot be indifferent to gender-based violence, which constitutes one of the most flagrant attacks on fundamental rights such as freedom, equality, life, security and non-discrimination proclaimed in our Constitution. Those same public authorities have, in accordance with the provisions of article 9.2 of the Constitution, the obligation to adopt positive action measures to make these rights real and effective, removing the obstacles that prevent or hinder their fullness.

In recent years there have been legislative advances in Spanish law on combating gender-based violence, such as Organic Law 11/2003, of 29 September, on Specific Measures on Public Safety, Domestic

Violence and Social Integration of Foreigners; Organic Law 15/2003, of 25 November, amending Organic Law 10/1995, of 23 November, on the Criminal

Code, or Law 27/2003, of 31 July, regulating the Protection Order for Victims of Domestic Violence; in addition to laws approved by various Autonomous

The Law aims to address the recommendations of international bodies in the sense of providing a comprehensive response to violence exercised against women.

In this regard, reference may be made to the Convention on the Elimination of All Forms of Discrimination against Women of 1979; the United Nations Declaration on the Elimination of Violence against Women, proclaimed in December 1993 by the General

Union in this regard.

The scope of the Law covers both preventive, educational, social, care and post-victim assistance aspects, as well as civil regulations that affect the family or cohabitation sphere where aggressions mainly occur, as well as the principle of subsidiarity in Public Administrations. Likewise, the punitive response that all manifestations of violence regulated by this Law must receive is addressed decisively.

Gender-based violence is approached by the Law in an integral and multidisciplinary manner, starting with the process of socialisation and education.

The achievement of equality and respect for human dignity and the freedom of individuals must be a priority objective at all levels of socialisation.

The Law establishes awareness-raising and intervention measures in the educational sphere. It reinforces, with specific reference to the advertising field, an image that respects the equality and dignity of women. It supports victims through the recognition of rights such as the right to information, free legal assistance and other social protection and economic support rights. It therefore provides a comprehensive legal response covering both procedural rules, creating new instances, as well as substantive criminal and civil rules, including the appropriate training of health, police and legal operators responsible for obtaining evidence and applying the law.

Awareness-raising and intervention measures are also established in the health sphere to optimise early detection and physical and psychological care for victims, in coordination with other support measures.

Situations of violence against women also affect minors who are within their family environment, direct or indirect victims of this violence. The

Law also contemplates their protection not only for the protection of the rights of minors, but also to effectively guarantee the protection measures adopted with respect to women.

III

The Law is structured in a preliminary title, five titles, twenty additional provisions, two transitional provisions, one repealing provision and seven final provisions.

The preliminary title contains the general provisions of the Law referring to its purpose and guiding principles.

Title I determines the awareness-raising, prevention and detection and intervention measures in different spheres. In the educational sphere, the obligations of the system for the transmission of values of respect for the dignity of women and equality between men and women are specified. The fundamental objective of education is to provide comprehensive training that allows them to shape their own identity, as well as

In the field of advertising, it must respect the dignity of women and their right to a non-stereotyped and non-discriminatory image, whether displayed in public or private media. In its title IV the Law introduces rules of a criminal nature, through which it is intended to include, within the aggravated types of injuries, a specific one that increases the criminal sanction when the injury occurs against someone who is or has been the wife of the perpetrator, or woman who is or has been linked to him by an analogous relationship of affection, even without cohabitation. Minor coercion and minor threats of any kind committed against the women mentioned above will also be punished as crimes.

For the citizenry, for women's groups and specifically for those who suffer this type of aggression, the Law wants to give a firm and forceful response and show firmness by embodying them in specific criminal types.

In title V, the so-called Judicial Protection is established to guarantee adequate and effective treatment of the legal, family and social situation of victims of gender-based violence in intrafamily relations.

From the judicial point of view we are faced with a complex phenomenon in which it is necessary to intervene from different legal perspectives, which must cover from procedural and substantive rules to provisions relating to assistance to victims, intervention that is only possible through specific legislation.

A Law for the prevention and eradication of violence against women must be a

Law that includes procedural measures that allow agile and summary procedures, such as that established in Law 27/2003, of 31 July, but, in addition, that combines, in the civil and criminal spheres, protection measures for women and their sons and daughters, and precautionary measures to be executed urgently.

The current regulations, civil, criminal, advertising, social and administrative present many deficiencies, mainly due to the fact that until now this issue has not been given a global and multidisciplinary response. From the criminal point of view the response can never be a new grievance for women.

As for the legal measures assumed to guarantee adequate and effective treatment of the legal, family and social situation of victims of violence against women in intrafamily relations, the following have been adopted: in accordance with the Spanish legal tradition, a specialisation formula has been chosen within the criminal order, of the

Examining Magistrates, creating the Courts on Violence against Women and excluding the possibility of creating a new jurisdictional order or the assumption of criminal competences by Civil Judges. These Courts will hear the investigation, and, where appropriate, the ruling of criminal cases on violence against women, as well as those related civil cases, so that both at first instance are subject to procedural treatment before the same court. This ensures the guaranteeing mediation of due criminal process in the intervention of the fundamental rights of the alleged aggressor, without thereby reducing in the slightest the legal possibilities that this Law provides for the greater, more immediate and effective protection of the victim, as well as the resources to prevent repetitions of aggression or escalation of violence.

PRELIMINARY TITLE

#### Article 1. Object of the Law.

1. The purpose of this Law is to act against violence that, as a manifestation of discrimination, the situation of inequality and the power relations of men over women, is exercised over the latter by those who are or have been their spouses or by those who are or have been linked to them by similar relationships of affection, even without cohabitation.

2. This law establishes comprehensive protection measures whose purpose is to prevent, punish and eradicate this violence and provide assistance to women, their minor children and minors subject to their guardianship, or custody, victims of this violence.

3. The gender-based violence referred to in this Law includes every act of physical and psychological violence, including assaults on sexual freedom, threats, coercion or arbitrary deprivation of liberty.

4. The gender-based violence referred to in this Law also includes violence exercised against their relatives or close persons who are minors by the persons indicated in the first paragraph with the objective of causing harm or damage to women.

#### Article 2. Guiding principles.

Through this Law, a comprehensive set of measures is articulated aimed at achieving the following purposes:

- a) Strengthen citizen awareness and prevention measures, providing public authorities with effective instruments in the educational, social services, health, advertising and media fields.
- b) Establish rights of women victims of gender-based violence, enforceable before Public Administrations, and thus ensure rapid, transparent and effective access to the services established for that purpose.

c) Reinforce, until the minimum requirements demanded by the objectives of the law are achieved, the social services of information, care, emergency, support and comprehensive recovery, as well as establish a system for the most effective coordination of existing services at municipal and regional level.

d) Guarantee rights in the labor and civil service sphere that reconcile the requirements of the employment relationship and public employment with the circumstances of those workers or civil servants who suffer gender-based violence.

e) Guarantee economic rights for women victims of gender-based violence, in order to facilitate their social integration.

f) Establish a comprehensive system of institutional protection in which the General State Administration, through the Special Delegation of the Government against Violence against Women, in collaboration with the State Observatory on Violence against Women, promotes the creation of public policies aimed at offering protection to the victims of the violence contemplated in this Law.

g) Strengthen the current criminal and procedural framework to ensure comprehensive protection, from the jurisdictional bodies, to victims of gender-based violence.

h) Coordinate the resources and instruments of all kinds of the different public authorities to ensure the prevention of acts of gender-based violence and, where appropriate, the appropriate punishment of those guilty of them.

i) Promote the collaboration and participation of entities, associations and organizations that act against gender-based violence from civil society.

j) Encourage the specialization of professional groups involved in the process of information, care and protection of victims.

k) Guarantee the principle of mainstreaming of measures, so that in their application the specific needs and demands of all women victims of gender-based violence are taken into account.

## TITLE I

Awareness, prevention and detection measures

#### Article 3. Awareness plans.

1. From the responsibility of the State Government and immediately upon the entry into force of this law, with the corresponding budgetary allocation, a State Plan for Awareness and Prevention of Gender-Based Violence shall be launched on a permanent basis that shall include at least the following elements:

- a) That it introduce into the social arena the new scales of values based on respect for fundamental rights and freedoms and for equality between men and women, as well as on the exercise of tolerance and freedom within the democratic principles of coexistence, all from the perspective of gender relations.
- b) Aimed at both men and women, from a community and intercultural approach, including the field of information and digital technologies.
- c) That it contemplate a comprehensive programme of complementary training and retraining of professionals who intervene in these situations.
- d) Controlled by a Commission with broad participation, which shall be created within a maximum period of one month, in which the presence of victims and their environment, institutions, professionals and persons of recognized social prestige related to the treatment of these issues must be ensured.

The Government Delegation against Gender-Based Violence, having heard the Commission referred to in the previous paragraph, shall prepare the annual evaluation Report on the State Plan for Awareness and Prevention of Gender-Based Violence and shall submit it to the Cortes Generales.

2. Public authorities, within the framework of their competencies, shall also promote specific information and awareness campaigns in order to prevent gender-based violence.

### CHAPTER I

In the field of education

#### Article 4. Principles and values of the education system.

1. The Spanish education system shall include among its aims training in respect for fundamental rights and freedoms and equality between men and women, as well as in the exercise of tolerance and freedom within the democratic principles of coexistence.

Likewise, the Spanish education system shall include, within its quality principles, the elimination of obstacles that hinder full equality between men and women and training for conflict prevention and for their peaceful resolution.

2. Early Childhood Education shall contribute to developing in children learning in the peaceful resolution of conflicts.

3. Primary Education shall contribute to developing in pupils their capacity to acquire skills in the peaceful resolution of conflicts and to understand and respect equality between the sexes.

4. Compulsory Secondary Education shall contribute to developing in pupils the capacity to relate to others in a peaceful manner and to know, value and respect equal opportunities for men and women.

5. Baccalaureate and Vocational Training shall contribute to developing in pupils the capacity to consolidate their personal, social and moral maturity, which allows them to act in a responsible and autonomous manner and to critically analyze and assess gender inequalities and to promote real and effective equality between men and women.

6. Education for adults shall include among its objectives developing activities in the peaceful resolution of conflicts and promoting respect for the dignity of persons and for equality between men and women.

7. Universities shall include and promote in all academic fields training, teaching and research in gender equality and non-discrimination in a transversal manner.

#### Article 5. Immediate schooling in cases of gender-based violence.

The competent Administrations must provide for the immediate schooling of children who are affected by a change of residence resulting from acts of gender-based violence.

#### Article 6. Promotion of equality.

### CHAPTER II

Labor rights and Social Security benefits

#### Article 21. Labor and Social Security rights.

1. The female worker who is a victim of gender-based violence shall have the right, under the terms provided in the Workers' Statute, to the reduction or reorganization of her working time, to geographical mobility, to change of workplace, to the adaptation of her job position and to the support she may require due to her disability for her reintegration, to the suspension of the employment relationship with reservation of her job position and to the termination of the employment contract.

4. Absences or lack of punctuality at work motivated by the physical or psychological situation derived from gender-based violence shall be considered justified and shall be remunerated, when so determined by the social care services or health services, as appropriate, without prejudice to such absences being communicated by the worker to the company as soon as possible.

5. Self-employed female workers who are victims of gender-based violence who cease their activity to make effective their protection or their right to comprehensive social assistance, shall be considered in a situation of temporary cessation of activity under the terms provided in the revised text of the General Social Security Law, approved by Royal Legislative Decree 8/2015, of October 30, and their obligation to contribute shall be suspended for a period of six months, which shall be considered as effective contribution for the purposes of Social Security benefits. Likewise, their situation shall be considered as equivalent to being registered.

For the purposes of the provisions in the preceding paragraph, a contribution base equivalent to the average of the bases contributed during the six months prior to the suspension of the obligation to contribute shall be taken.

This Law has the character of an Organic Law, with the exception of the following provisions: title I, title II, title III, Articles 42, 43, 44, 45, 46, 47, 70, 71, 72, as well as the first, second, sixth, seventh, eighth, ninth, eleventh, thirteenth, fifteenth, sixteenth, seventeenth, eighteenth, nineteenth and twentieth additional provisions, the second transitional provision and the fourth, fifth and sixth final provisions. Fourth final provision. Regulatory empowerment.

1. The Government is empowered to issue, within six months from the publication of this Law in the «Official State Gazette», the provisions that may be necessary for its application.

Through the Ministry of Justice, the necessary measures for the implementation of the Courts of Violence against Women, as well as for the adaptation of the structure of the Public Prosecutor's Office to the provisions of this Law, shall be adopted within said period.

2. Within six months from the entry into force of this Organic Law, the General Council of the Judiciary shall issue the necessary regulations for the organisation of court sessions, adaptation of duty services to the existence of the new Courts of Violence against Women, and coordination of the Judicial Police with said Courts.

Fifth final provision. Regulatory amendments.

The Government, within six months from the approval of this Law, shall proceed to amend Article 116.4 of Royal Decree 190/1996, of 9 February, approving the Penitentiary Regulations, establishing the obligation for the Penitentiary Administration to carry out the specific treatment programmes for inmates referred to in this Law. Within the same period, Royal Decree 738/1997, of 23 May, approving the Regulation on aid to victims of violent crimes and crimes against sexual freedom, and Royal Decree 996/2003, of 25 July, approving the Regulation on free legal assistance, shall be amended.

CONSOLIDATED LEGISLATION

Within the period mentioned in the preceding subsection, the State and the Autonomous

Communities, within the scope of their respective competences, shall adapt their regulations to the provisions contained in this Law.

Sixth final provision. Amendment of Law 1/1996, of 10 January, on Free Legal Assistance.

Subsection 5 of Article 3 of Law 1/1996, of 10 January, on Free Assistance, is amended, worded as follows:

«5. Nor shall it be necessary for victims of gender-based violence to previously prove lack of resources when they request specialised free legal defence, which shall be provided to them immediately, without prejudice to the fact that if the right thereto is not subsequently recognised, they must pay the lawyer the fees accrued for his or her intervention.»

Seventh final provision. Entry into force.

This Organic Law shall enter into force thirty days after its publication in the «Official State Gazette», except for the provisions of titles IV and V, which shall do so after six months.

Therefore, I order all Spaniards, private individuals and authorities, to observe and enforce this organic law.
