LAW NO. 2011-26 OF 09 JANUARY 2012 on the prevention and punishment of violence against women PreFaCe

Violence against women constitutes a crucial development problem that affects women in their very dignity.

The number of women subjected to violence in Africa in general, and in Benin in particular, is not negligible. Indeed, the study commissioned by the Ministry of

Family, Social Affairs, National Solidarity, Persons with Disabilities and Elderly Persons on violence against women at the national level revealed that: out of approximately 52% of the population consisting of women, 69% declared having suffered violence at least once in their lifetime. More than half of the women surveyed (51.5%) have suffered at least once in their lifetime physical or psychological suffering. This suffering endured includes shouting at women (72.8%); threats of divorce (32.8%); refusing to eat what the woman has prepared (30.9%); complaints about the woman's behavior to the mother-in-law (26.9%); insults to the woman in front of strangers (22.6%); sexual violence (28.5%); rape of girls aged 2-14 years (1.4%); abduction (8.5%) and excision (15.0%). In view of this prevalence and the reluctance of perpetrators of violence against women, the Government promulgated Law No. 2011-26 of 09 January 2012, on the prevention and punishment of violence against women.

This law constitutes both a mechanism for combating and developing women, a notable advance in the legal protection of Beninese women and a better valorization of the principle of gender equality.

Women, being part of vulnerable groups, this legal text makes it possible to punish violations of the rights recognized to them and to fight against discrimination, except positive discrimination.

The advent of this law constitutes the success of all the different actors who contributed to it, I would like to mention: civil society, the national representation, the Ministry of Family and the Government without forgetting the development partners who work for the cause of women in Benin.

Law No. 2011-26 of 09 January 2012 on the prevention and punishment of violence against women will certainly restore hope to women.

Its proper application and ownership by women themselves depend on it.

Fatouma AMADOU DJIBRIL

### CHAPTER I

PURPOSE AND DEFINITION OF CONCEPTS

#### Article 1: The purpose of this law is to combat all forms of

violence against women and girls in the Republic of Benin. Through its criminal, civil and social components, it aims to provide a multidisciplinary response to violence against women and girls.

#### Article 2: Violence against women is defined, for the purposes of this

law, as all acts of violence directed against the female sex and causing or likely to cause women physical, sexual or psychological harm or suffering, including the threat of such acts, coercion or arbitrary deprivation of liberty, whether in public or private life.

The violations concern:

- Physical or moral, sexual and psychological violence committed within the family such as beatings, marital rape, sexual assault and abuse, female genital mutilation as provided for by Law 2003-03 of 03 March 2003 on the punishment of the practice of female genital mutilation in the Republic of Benin, forced or arranged marriages, "honor" crimes and other traditional practices harmful to women.
- Physical or moral, sexual and psychological violence committed within the community including rape, sexual assault and abuse, sexual harassment as provided for by Law 2006- LAW NO. 2011-26 OF 09 JANUARY 2012 on the prevention and punishment of violence against women 19 of 05 September 2006 on the punishment of sexual harassment and protection of victims in the Republic of Benin and intimidation at work, in educational institutions and other places, procuring, trafficking, forced prostitution.

Under this law, also considered as violence against women is the act, by a medical or paramedical agent, of failing to provide a woman during childbirth with all the required diligence, or of refraining from performing their professional duty.

#### Article 3: For the purposes of this law, the following terms mean:

- abortion: the act of using means or substances intended to cause the premature expulsion of the fetus or, more generally, the artificial termination of pregnancy in women;

- harassment: the act of giving orders, using words, gestures, writings, messages, and this, repeatedly, making threats, imposing constraints, exerting pressure or using any other means for the purpose of obtaining from a person in a situation of vulnerability or subordination favors of any kind whatsoever including sexual favors for one's own benefit or for the benefit of a third party against the will of the harassed woman;

- incest: sexual intercourse perpetrated on a person with whom one has kinship ties up to and including the third degree;

- incitement of minors to debauchery: the act of acting on minors with a view to satisfying the passions of others or in any case as an intermediary agent of corruption and debauchery;

- forced marriage: any marriage or cohabitation contracted or decided without the free and informed consent of the two parties concerned;

- female genital mutilation: all interventions including partial or total removal of the external genital organs of women or injury to the female genital organs carried out for cultural or religious reasons or for any other non-therapeutic reason;

- pedophilia: erotic attraction of an adult towards children. More specifically, the adult who engages in touching or maintains sexual relations with a minor;

- traditional practices harmful to women: acts derived from customs and traditions that harm women. These include in particular:

- dietary restrictions in case of pregnancy or childbirth,
- force-feeding which consists of excessively feeding minor girls with a view to making them physically fit for marriage,
- degrading widowhood rites,
- infringements on women's freedom of movement,
- pressure on women through children.
  - forced prostitution: the act of causing one or more persons to perform one or more acts of a sexual nature, by force, by threat of force or coercion or by taking advantage of the incapacity of said persons to give their consent freely in order to obtain a pecuniary or other advantage;
  - procuring: the activity of one who facilitates the debauchery of others by serving as an intermediary, thereby deriving benefit from the fruits of this activity;
  - forced sterilization: the act of committing or causing to be committed on a woman without her free consent or without a justified medical decision, an act likely to deprive her of her biological and organic reproductive capacity;
  - rape: any act of vaginal, anal or oral penetration by another person's sex organ or vaginal or anal penetration by any object without the intelligent and voluntary consent of the penetrated person.

However, consent is not valid in the case of female minors under sixteen (16) years of age.

The penetrated person is not required to fight against their aggressor.

The fact of being married to the penetrated person is not an excuse for the crime of rape;

- violence in the family environment: abuse of power or intentional negligence with a view to dominating, subjugating, controlling or assaulting women physically, verbally, psychologically, patrimonially, LOI N°2011-26 DU 09 JANVIER 2012 portant prévention et répression des violences faites aux femmes economically and sexually, inside or outside the family home, whether the aggressor is or has been linked to their victim by kinship relations, by blood or by marriage, by marriage relations, by cohabitation or whether they maintain or have maintained a de facto relationship with them;

- violence in the professional environment: refusal, in violation of the law, to hire the victim by contract, to maintain their employment or to respect the general working conditions; depreciation of work performed, threats, intimidation, humiliations, exploitation and any type of distraction based on sex;

- domestic violence: any physical or sexual violence committed by a person against the person of another when the two parties are married, cohabiting, or consanguineous, or living in the same house, or when the two parties have had an intimate relationship in the past, but are no longer together;

- economic violence: the act of using one's means to slow down or prevent the economic or financial development of any person, or the act of preventing any person from enjoying their socio-economic rights;

- patrimonial violence: any act or negligence affecting the survival of the victim and consisting of transforming, removing, destroying, retaining or misappropriating objects, personal documents, goods and valuables, patrimonial rights or economic resources intended to cover their needs and which may extend to damage caused to property common to or owned by the victim;

- physical violence: any type of act resulting in non-accidental harm, through the use of physical force or any type of weapon or object that may or may not cause internal, external or both injuries;

- psychological or moral violence: the act of subjecting any person to repeated actions or words having the object or effect of degrading living conditions likely to undermine their rights and dignity, to impair their physical or mental health or to compromise their plans or future.

It is also an act or negligence causing prejudice to psychological stability, abandonment, repeated inattention, excessive jealousy, insults and humiliations, devaluation,

- sexual violence: any act having the effect of degrading or causing harm to the body and/or sexuality of the victim and which, consequently, undermines their freedom, dignity and physical integrity.

It is the expression of an abuse of power dictated by the supremacy of men over women who are thus denigrated and treated as objects; all other similar forms that harm or undermine the dignity, integrity or freedom of women;

- zoophilia: the act by any person through cunning, violence, threats or through any form of coercion or artifice, of forcing a person to have sexual relations with an animal.

of sex, race and religion up to the age of sixteen (16) years.

#### Article 5: The fight for equality between men and women constitutes

a national priority.

To this end, principles and teachings of mutual respect between the sexes, learning to live together, rejection and condemnation of violence, development of critical thinking and analysis against violence and all inequalities between women and men, will be taken into account in education programs.

- education in respect for fundamental rights and freedoms and equality between men and women in the exercise of tolerance and freedom in accordance with democratic principles of common life;
- education for conflict prevention and their peaceful resolution, in all contexts of personal, family and social life;
- early detection of violence in the family context, especially against women.

### CHAPTER II

ON ADVERTISING AND MEANS

OF COMMUNICATION

#### Article 10: Any advertising that uses

degrading, devaluing, dehumanizing and vexatious representations of women and men and the relations between them is considered unlawful.

#### Article 11: The High Authority for Audiovisual and Communication (HAAC)

must ensure the equitable representation of women and men by the media, particularly by avoiding as much as possible degrading and vexatious representations, by fighting against sexist stereotypes, by avoiding an unbalanced presence of persons of both sexes in broadcast programs. The specifications of broadcasters will mandatorily include these principles.

It must exercise its sanctioning power over the media concerned.

#### Article 12: Upon publication of this law, the High Authority for

- verification of pornographic content in various media programs;

### CHAPTER III

ON HEALTH AND SOCIAL DOMAIN

#### Article 13: The State has the obligation to promote through

socio-health structures, early detection of cases of violence against women.

To this end, it must develop awareness, initial and continuing training programs for socio-health personnel with the aim of improving clinical and psychological care and helping in the rehabilitation of women victims.

In all cases of rape, the justice system must request competent health structures for the purpose of mandatory HIV/AIDS screening test and any other sexually transmitted infection for the victim and its perpetrator in order to assess the possibility of contamination that would create more harm to the victim.

The costs are borne by the State.

#### Article 14: The initial and continuing training of all

health professionals as well as professionals in the medico-social sector includes specific education dedicated to violence perpetrated against women and their consequences in terms of public health.

This education which will take place throughout the years of training including in a multidisciplinary manner and which will be evaluated, aims to promote prevention, early detection, assistance and rehabilitation of women victims of violence.

### CHAPTER IV

ON JUDICIAL AND PARAJUDICIAL DOMAIN

#### Article 15: The State has the obligation to promote judicial and

parajudicial institutions for handling cases of violence against women.

To this end, it must develop awareness, initial and continuing training programs for judicial and parajudicial personnel, with the aim of improving effective care and rehabilitation of women victims.

Given that most rapes and domestic violence occur in private places, hidden from the eyes of potential witnesses, Beninese justice must broaden the categories of evidence for the prosecution and take into account oral testimonies of victims.

The testimony of a victim alone can support an intimate conviction.

#### Article 16: The initial and continuing training of all judicial

and parajudicial professionals includes mandatory specific education dedicated to domestic and sexual violence and their consequences in terms of public health.

This multidisciplinary education aims to promote prevention, assistance and rehabilitation of women victims of violence and shall be subject to annual evaluation by the relevant ministries.

Each public prosecutor's office must designate at least one of its trained members who shall be responsible for representing the public prosecution service in all domestic and sexual violence cases.

Each court of first instance must designate at least one investigating judge who shall be responsible for investigating all domestic and sexual violence cases.

The State must strengthen the capacities of judicial police officers so that in each police station or gendarmerie brigade, domestic and/or sexual violence cases are handled.

### CHAPITRE I

RIGHT TO INFORMATION, SOCIAL ASSISTANCE

AND FREE LEGAL AID

#### Article 17: The State must make effective women's enjoyment of their

rights to physical and moral integrity, to freedom, to security as well as to equality and non-discrimination on grounds of sex.

#### Article 18: In each department, social promotion centers

must take care of women victims of violence for the purpose of providing them with emergency, reception and assistance social services. These services are organized in such a way as to respond to urgent needs and to provide lasting multidisciplinary support.

The multidisciplinary services provided in this framework specifically include:

- information for victims;
- psychological and psychiatric support;
- social support;
- health support;
- service of judicial police officers;
- follow-up of legal and administrative procedures;
- preventive training on women/men equality;
- support for training and professional integration;
- ease of access to reception centers.

#### Article 19: Minors who are in the custody and under the supervision

of the assaulted person also have the right to comprehensive social assistance through these social services.

#### Article 20: Women victims of violence, including young girls

threatened with forced or arranged marriage, young girls in care, sexually abused are considered as priority in access to reception centers.

In case of domestic or marital violence where both parties occupy the same dwelling, the assaulted party shall have the right to continue to occupy the dwelling temporarily.

### CHAPITRE II

RIGHTS RELATING TO EMPLOYMENT

#### Article 21: The female employee victim of violence within or outside the company

shall have the right, upon her request and after favorable opinion from the occupational health physician, to temporary reduction or reorganization of her working time, to geographical transfer, to assignment to another establishment, to suspension of her employment contract and to resignation without notice. Upon expiration of the suspension of her employment contract, the employee shall resume her previous employment.

#### Article 22: Absences or non-compliance with working hours justified

by the physical or psychological situation of the employee related to violence against women may only give rise to sanction upon decision of the social services, support services or health services. The employer must be informed within seventy-two (72) hours.

The employee benefits from a guarantee of remuneration during these absences.

#### Article 23: The head of the company must take all necessary measures

with a view to preventing, putting an end to and/or sanctioning any statement, act or

verbal or non-verbal behavior with sexual or sexist connotation or any other behavior based on sex or taking into account actual or presumed sexuality, having the object or effect of undermining the rights and dignity of the woman or young girl, or creating an intimidating, hostile, degrading, humiliating or offensive environment, in particular through information to employees, the establishment of investigation procedures and interim measures.

#### Article 24: The occupational health physician is authorized to propose

individual measures such as transfers or job transformations justified by considerations relating in particular to age, physical resistance, violence suffered by women within or outside the company or to the physical and mental health condition of workers.

#### Article 25: The woman, public service employee, victim of violence,

who would be obliged to abandon her work position in the locality where she was serving, in order to ensure the effectiveness of her protection or her right to integrated social assistance, shall enjoy a preferential right to occupy another work position appropriate to her corps and grade, presenting similar characteristics, which would be vacant and to be filled. The competent public administration shall be required to inform the woman victim of violence of vacant positions to be filled in the same locality or in the localities that the person concerned would expressly request.

### CHAPITRE I

CIVIL PROVISIONS

#### Article 29: When in application of this law, a woman seizes a

court for violations of her physical or psychological integrity, or that of children, the residence of the child is automatically determined by the competent judge in favor of the woman victim.

The decision may be modified by the judge or the court according to the judgment.

### CHAPITRE II

CRIMINAL PROVISIONS

#### Article 30: For any criminal offense that punishes physical

or sexual violence, the fact that the victim and the perpetrator have a

domestic relationship, as defined in article 3 of this law, shall be considered as an aggravating circumstance.

The maximum penalty for misdemeanors is increased by five (05) years of imprisonment and that for felonies is increased by at least ten (10) years.

#### Article 31: Any person who is guilty or an accomplice of a marriage

forced or arranged or forced cohabitation, as defined in Article 3 of this law, is punished by imprisonment of one (01) year to three (03) years and a fine of five hundred thousand (500,000) francs to two million (2,000,000) francs.

All persons who are accomplices in the planning and/or execution of such marriage or cohabitation are equally guilty.

#### Article 32: Psychological violence as defined in Article 3 of this

law is punished by a fine of up to one million (1,000,000) francs.

#### Article 33: Economic violence as defined in Article 3 of this

law is punished by a fine ranging from five hundred thousand (500,000) francs to two million (2,000,000) francs, without prejudice to civil compensation equal to the compensatory reparation provided for by the code of persons and the family.

#### Article 34: Forced prostitution as defined in Article 3 of this

law is punished by imprisonment of one (01) year to five (05) years and a fine of one million (1,000,000) francs to ten million (10,000,000) francs.

If the offense is followed or preceded by another offense or if the victim is a minor under sixteen (16) years of age, the sentence is increased to at least ten (10) years.

#### Article 35: Forced sterilization as defined in Article 3 of this

law is punished by imprisonment of one (01) year to five (05) years and a

#### Article 36: Zoophilia as defined in Article 3 of this law is

punished by imprisonment of at least ten (10) years and a fine which cannot be less than twenty-five million (25,000,000) francs.

#### Article 37: All traditional practices harmful to women

constitute common law offenses and are punished as such. All other acts of violence not specifically provided for by this law are in accordance with the legislation in force.
