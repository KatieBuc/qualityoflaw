# DEMOCRATIC REPUBLIC OF TIMOR-LESTE

DEMOCRATIC REPUBLIC OF TIMOR-LESTE

DEMOCRATIC REPUBLIC OF TIMOR-LESTE

NATIONAL PARLIAMENT ACT

Law Against Domestic Violence

Preamble

Domestic violence is a problem of all eras, perhaps one of the most complex social problems of our time.

In the last three decades, various guidelines arising from international legal texts have raised awareness of the need to prevent and investigate crimes of domestic violence, in addition to establishing reparative measures for its victims, particularly with regard to equality and discrimination in the International Covenant on Civil and Political Rights and in the Convention on the Elimination of All Forms of Discrimination against Women, already ratified by the Timorese State.

The principles of the international human rights instruments already ratified by Timor-Leste, including those of the Convention on the Rights of the Child, are reflected in the law now being approved.

Likewise, the measures enshrined in this law aim, in accordance with the Constitution of the Democratic Republic of Timor-Leste, to guarantee respect for human rights and the integrity of the family as a fundamental social and cultural unit of Timor-Leste, recognizing that it is the family that is first obligated to a special duty to protect and defend especially vulnerable groups such as women, children, the elderly and disabled, against all forms of violence, exploitation, discrimination, abandonment, oppression, sexual abuse and other ill-treatment.

However, it is not only within the family that protection for the most vulnerable must exist, it being the obligation of all citizens to prevent acts of domestic violence and facilitate assistance to its victims.

Finally, the State cannot fail to ensure protection for its citizens, with a coordination role with all public and private entities and community leaders in implementing the policy of preventing domestic violence and supporting victims.

The National Parliament decrees, pursuant to Article 92 and paragraph 1 of Article 95 of the Constitution of the Republic, to have the force of law, the following:

## CHAPTER I

GENERAL PROVISIONS

#### Article 1

Object

This law establishes the legal regime applicable to the prevention of domestic violence and the protection and assistance to its victims.

#### Article 2

Concept of domestic violence

1. For the purposes of this instrument, domestic violence is considered to be any act or sequence of acts committed in a family context, with or without cohabitation, by a family member against any of its members, when there is an ascendancy, particularly physical or economic, in the family relationship, or by one person in relation to another with whom they had an intimate relationship, from which results or may result damage or physical, sexual or psychological suffering, economic abuse, including threats such as intimidating acts, bodily offenses, aggression, coercion, harassment, or deprivation of liberty.

2. Forms of domestic violence include, among others:

- a) Physical violence, understood as any conduct that offends bodily integrity or health;
- b) Sexual violence, understood as any conduct that compels one to witness, maintain or participate in unwanted sexual relations, even within marriage, through intimidation, threat, coercion or use of force, or that limits or nullifies the exercise of sexual and reproductive rights;
- c) Psychological violence, understood as any conduct that causes emotional damage and diminished self-esteem, aimed at degrading or controlling the actions, behaviors, beliefs and decisions of another, through threat, constraint, humiliation, manipulation, isolation, constant surveillance, systematic persecution, insult, blackmail, ridicule, exploitation, limitation of the right to movement or any other means that causes harm to psychological health and self-determination;
- d) Economic violence, understood as any conduct that constitutes retention, subtraction, partial or total destruction of personal objects, work instruments, impediment to work inside or outside the home, personal documents, goods, valuables and economic rights or resources, including those intended to satisfy personal and household needs.

#### Article 3

Family

For the purposes of this law, the following relations are considered members of a family:

- a) Spouses or ex-spouses;
- b) Persons who live or have lived in conditions analogous to those of spouses, even without cohabitation;
- c) Ascendants and descendants of both or only one of the spouses or of those in the situation provided for in the previous paragraph, provided they are part of the same context of dependence and family economy;
- d) Any other person who is part of the same context of dependence or family economy, including those who carry out domestic-labor activity therein on a continuous and subordinate basis.

## CHAPTER II

FUNDAMENTAL PRINCIPLES

#### Article 4

Principle of equality

Every individual, regardless of ancestry, nationality, social condition, sex, ethnicity, language, age, religion, disability, political or ideological convictions, culture and educational level, enjoys the fundamental rights inherent to the dignity of the human person, being assured equal opportunities to live without violence and the right to preserve their physical and mental integrity.

#### Article 5

Principle of Consent

1. Without prejudice to other applicable provisions within the scope of criminal and criminal procedural legislation, any intervention to support the victim must be carried out after the victim gives their free and informed consent and is limited by full respect for their will.

2. Specific support intervention, under the terms of this law, for young victims of domestic violence, aged 16 years or older, depends only on their consent.

3. Specific support intervention, under the terms of this law, for children or young victims of domestic violence, under the age of 16 years, depends on the consent of the legal representative, or in their absence or if they are the perpetrator of the crime, of the entity designated by law and the consent of the child or young person aged 12 years or older.

4. The consent of the child or young person aged between 12 and 16 years is sufficient to legitimize the specific support intervention under the terms of this law, if circumstances prevent the receipt, in due time, of a declaration on the consent of the legal representative, or in their absence or if they are the perpetrator of the crime, of the entity designated by law.

5. The child or young victim of domestic violence, under the age of 12 years, has the right to express their opinion, according to their age and degree of maturity, on the specific support under the terms of this law.

6. The victim may, at any time, freely revoke their consent, by themselves or through their legal representative.

#### Article 6

Protection of the victim who lacks capacity to give consent

1. Outside the scope of criminal proceedings, any support intervention for a victim who lacks capacity to give consent may only be carried out for their direct benefit.

2. Whenever, under the terms of the law, an adult lacks, by virtue of mental disturbance, illness or similar reason, the capacity to consent to an intervention, it may not be carried out without the authorization of their representative, or in their absence or if they are the perpetrator of the crime, of an authority or a person or instance designated under the terms of the law.

3. The victim in question must, as far as possible, participate in the authorization process.

#### Artigo 7.º

Princípio da informação

The State, through the criminal police bodies, the Public Prosecutor's Office, the Public Defender's Office, and medical and social services, ensures that the victim is provided with adequate information for the protection of their rights.

#### Artigo 8.º

Obrigações profissionais e regras de conduta

Any technical support intervention for the victim must be carried out in observance of professional norms and obligations, codes of conduct, standardized operating procedures, universal principles of human rights, as well as the rules of conduct applicable to the specific case.

#### Artigo 9.º

Sensibilização da opinião pública

The Government develops public awareness campaigns through the media in order to promote a culture of non-violence and combat gender-based stereotypes, based on respect for the rights and duties of all and with a view to changing behaviors likely to generate violence against particularly vulnerable groups.

#### Artigo 10.º

Informação

1. O Governo deve elaborar material informativo e formativo sobre a prevenção e identificação e os factores da violência doméstica, dando particular atenção ao material destinado aos profissionais, bem como à transcrição, publicação e difusão de textos internacionais sobre esta problemática.

2. O Governo deve ainda elaborar e distribuir, a título gratuito, em todo o território nacional, um guia destinado às vítimas de violência doméstica, em que constem informações práticas sobre os seus direitos assim como dos instrumentos existentes à sua disposição.

3. O Governo prepara acções de formação e material de informação especificamente para os Chefes de Suco e de Aldeia, tendo em conta a posição privilegiada dos líderes das comunidades para a disseminação da informação.

#### Artigo 11.º

Ensino

1. O Governo, como forma de combate à violência, integra nos planos curriculares escolares temas relacionados com os direitos humanos, em particular questões relacionadas com o género, incluindo os valores do afecto, da sexualidade e o princípio da resolução negociada dos conflitos.

2. Compete ao membro do Governo responsável pela educação a elaboração dos planos curriculares de cada ciclo de ensino.

#### Artigo 12.º

Estudo e investigação

The State, by itself or in cooperation with other institutions, supports and encourages study and research in the field of the factors of physical, psychological, sexual and economic domestic violence.

## CAPÍTULO III

COOPERAÇÃO INSTITUCIONAL

#### Artigo 13.º

Intervenção do Estado

1. Compete ao Governo a promoção e o desenvolvimento do Plano de Acção Nacional para a prevenção e apoio no domínio da violência doméstica, em colaboração com toda a sociedade e em especial com a família e os órgãos de poder local, nos termos desta lei.

2. Compete ao Governo a coordenação e articulação das po-líticas, medidas e acções sectoriais, a nível nacional e comunitário.

3. Os programas previstos neste capítulo são executados fa-seadamente, conforme definido no Plano de Acção Nacio-nal para a violência doméstica.

4. Os serviços de apoio médico, de apoio jurídico e policial são devidos desde o inicio de vigência da presente lei.

#### Artigo 14.º

Entidades coordenadoras

1. O Governo assegura a existência de uma entidade pública que colabora na definição, coordenação e acompanha-mento do Plano de Acção Nacional referido no artigo anterior.

2. A entidade pública referida no número anterior e o membro do Governo responsável pela promoção da igualdade devem colaborar com, entre outros, os membros do Governo responsáveis pelas áreas da segurança, da saúde, educação, justiça e solidariedade social.

3. O Governo elabora anualmente um relatório sobre as activi-dades desenvolvidas e o programa para o ano seguinte, que são remetidos ao Parlamento Nacional.

## CAPÍTULO IV

APOIO E ASSISTÊNCIA ÀS VÍTIMAS

### SECÇÃO I

APOIO ÀS VÍTIMAS

#### Artigo 15.º

Atendimento às vítimas

1. O Governo, através do membro responsável pela solidariedade social, estabelece, gere e supervisiona a rede nacional de centros de apoio às vitimas de violência doméstica, que são responsáveis pela assistência directa, refúgio e orientação das vítimas.

2. Os centros de apoio são compostos por centros de acolhi-mento e casas de abrigo, que trabalham coordenadamente.

3. Nos distritos onde não haja casas de abrigo, os centros de acolhimento operam em coordenação com a casa de abrigo mais próxima.

4. O Governo, através do membro responsável pela solidarie-dade social, elabora um conjunto de directrizes operacionais para supervisionar a criação e gestão dos centros de apoio mencionados nos números anteriores.

#### Artigo 16.º

Objectivos das casas de abrigo

1. São objectivos das casas de abrigo:

- a) Acolher temporariamente vítimas de violência domés-tica, acompanhadas ou não de filhos menores, sempre que, por razões de segurança, não possam permanecer na sua residência habitual;
- b) Garantir assistência psicológica e/ou cuidados médicos, assistência social e apoio jurídico adequados à situação da vítima;
- c) Nos casos em que tal se justificar, promover, durante a permanência na casa de abrigo, as competências pessoais, profissionais e sociais dos utentes, capazes de evitar eventuais situações de exclusão social e de contribuir para a sua efectiva reinserção social.
  - 2. O Governo, através do membro responsável pela solidarie-dade social, define, através de legislação complementar, os procedimentos comuns a todas as casas de abrigo, nomeadamente no que concerne a direitos das vítimas, acesso à informação, admissões, duração máxima de estada e tratamento ambulatório.

#### Artigo 17.º

Direitos e deveres

1. Os utentes e os filhos menores acolhidos em casas de abrigo gozam, em especial, dos seguintes direitos:

- a) Alojamento e alimentação em condições de dignidade;
- b) Usufruto de um espaço de privacidade e de um certo grau de autonomia na condução da sua vida pessoal adequados à idade e situação;
- c) Beneficiar de um espaço seguro e saudável dentro da casa;
- d) Acesso à escola mais próxima da casa de abrigo.
  - 2. Constitui dever especial dos utentes e dos filhos menores acolhidos em casas de abrigo cumprir com as respectivas regras de funcionamento.

#### Artigo 18.º

Gratuitidade

The services provided through the national network of support centers for victims of domestic violence are free of charge.

#### Artigo 19.º

Participação

The reception centers must notify the National Police of Timor-Leste (PNTL) or the Public Prosecutor's Office of situations of victims of domestic violence of which they become aware, for the purposes of instituting criminal proceedings, in respect of the confidentiality and privileged nature of the information shared between the victim and their counselor, analogous to the relationship established between a doctor and patient.

### SECÇÃO II

ASSISTÊNCIA ÀS VÍTIMAS

#### Artigo 20.º

Serviço de assistência de emergência

1. É criado um serviço de emergência para assistência às víti-mas de violência doméstica, com o fim de as informar sobre as providências adequadas à sua situação.

2. O serviço de emergência disponibiliza uma linha de aten-dimento telefónico, de carácter anónimo, por um período temporal e nas condições a fixar por despacho ministerial.

3. Em casos de urgência, o serviço comunicará aos serviços policiais competentes a necessidade de intervenção ime-diata e, se for caso disso, procederá ao encaminhamento das vítimas para as casas de abrigo.

#### Article 21.º

Direct assistance to victims

1. Specialized assistance services are created for the submission of complaints regarding the commission of crimes relating to domestic violence, assistance and guidance of the respective victims with hospital services, organizations in the reference network of support services for victims of domestic violence and the PNTL.

2. The implementation of the services provided for in the previous number is carried out in a phased manner, through a joint order of the member of the Government responsible for domestic violence and for the areas of security, health and social solidarity.

3. The Government, through the body responsible for social security, guarantees the information and specialized training of officials working in the services referred to in number 1.

#### Article 22.º

Assistance in hospital services

Whenever a patient reveals that they have been a victim or the clinical diagnosis allows for the conclusion that a crime relating to domestic violence has occurred, the specialized hospital assistance service is called to intervene, namely:

- a) To provide assistance and medical follow-up to victims of domestic violence taking into account the needs of victims, particularly children;
- b) To proceed with the safeguarding of evidence relating to possible crimes, namely through carrying out examinations or forensic medical examinations or adopting other precautionary measures appropriate to the specific case;
- c) To inform the victim of the rights that assist them and the measures that can be adopted, as well as the obligation of hospital authorities to report the facts to the police authorities;
- d) To immediately communicate the facts to the police authorities or the Public Prosecutor's Office;
- e) To prepare a report on the situation and measures taken and forward it to the competent authorities;
- f) To refer the victim to a shelter, if the situation warrants it, and the victim requests it.

#### Article 23.º

Duties of social assistance services

The assistance and support services are responsible for, namely:

- a) Providing services appropriate to the needs of victims of domestic violence, in accordance with their Professional Code of Ethics and standardized operational procedures;
- b) Providing special services to children who are victims of domestic violence;
- c) Reporting cases of domestic violence to law enforcement agents, in compliance with the Professional Code of Ethics;
- d) Conducting counseling sessions with victims of domestic violence;
- e) Facilitating, in case of need, the removal of the victim to a location that is appropriate to their needs, particularly in the case of child victims;
- f) Preparing reports and other documentation relating to assisted cases for use by the Police, Public Prosecutor's Office and Courts;
- g) At the request of the victim, providing support and accompanying them in court;
- h) Participating in the promotion of the creation of safety networks for victims of domestic violence at the community level.

#### Article 24.º

Police Assistance

1. The specialized police assistance services intervene in cases of crimes relating to domestic violence through communication from hospital services and victim support services.

2. The specialized police assistance services in the district services of the PNTL are responsible for:

- a) Providing the victim with all necessary collaboration, namely by informing them of their rights;
- b) Referring the victim, when requested, to a shelter or support center;
- c) Ensuring, whenever this proves necessary, that the victim receives immediate medical and psycho-social assistance provided by specialized personnel;
- d) Ensuring, in case of danger to psychological stability, that an assessment is made by a mental health professional, so that the victim can continue to have the necessary support from the competent bodies;
- e) Preparing a summary report of the observation made, the measures adopted and the evidence collected, to be attached to the criminal complaint to be sent to the Public Prosecutor's Office, within five days after becoming aware of the facts;
- f) Communicating to the Public Defender's Office through a summary report, if the victim does not have financial means to appoint a lawyer without prejudice to their livelihood, within a maximum period of five days after becoming aware of the facts.

#### Article 25.º

Legal Assistance

1. In all procedural acts the victim of domestic violence must be accompanied by a lawyer or public defender, if they do not have financial means to appoint a lawyer.

2. It is the responsibility of the lawyer or public defender, namely:

- a) To provide legal counseling to victims of domestic violence;
- b) To report the occurrence of cases of violence to the Police and the Public Prosecutor's Office, whenever this does not imply a breach of professional secrecy;
- c) To advise victims, witnesses and family members about the progress of judicial proceedings related to cases of domestic violence;
- d) To monitor the treatment given to cases by law enforcement agents and judicial operators, that is, the Police, the Public Prosecutor's Office and the Courts;
- e) To contact relevant entities, bodies and community groups for the purposes of dealing with cases of domestic violence;
- f) To advise victims with regard to access to other services that may be necessary;
- g) To facilitate access by the parties to information relating to specific cases under the terms indicated in this diploma and other legal provisions in force.

#### Article 26.º

Measures for the reintegration of victims

1. It is the responsibility of the Government to promote and support the creation and operation of associations, or other organizations, if it considers the existing conditions insufficient.

2. Support associations aim to protect victims of domestic violence, giving priority to programs aimed at their support and accompaniment, personal and professional development in accordance with their social needs.

#### Article 27.º

Support measures for aggressors

The Government, through the member responsible for the promotion of equality, must promote the development of public or private initiative projects aimed at aggressors in order to raise their awareness and lead them to adopt non-violent behaviors.

#### Article 28.º

Assistance by the Public Prosecutor's Office

It is the responsibility of the Public Prosecutor's Office, in addition to its obligations within the scope of criminal proceedings, within the scope of the fight against domestic violence:

- a) To provide direct assistance to victims who seek the respective services and inform them of their rights and ways to exercise them, namely through the services of the Public Defender's Office, if they do not have the means to appoint a lawyer without prejudice to their own livelihood and that of their family;
- b) To refer victims to hospital care or to shelters, if this has not yet been done.

## CHAPTER V

ON MAINTENANCE

#### Article 29.º

Right to maintenance

The victim who is a spouse or ex-spouse, lives or has lived in conditions analogous to those of spouses, even without cohabitation, is a descendant or ascendant of the aggressor, has the right to receive maintenance, provided they prove their need.

#### Article 30.º

Amount of the maintenance obligation

The amount of the maintenance obligation is fixed taking into account the possibilities of the provider and the needs of the recipient, namely the possibility of the latter partially providing for their own subsistence.

#### Article 31.º

Modality of the obligation

1. The content of the maintenance obligation may be fixed by written agreement between the provider and the recipient, or their legal representative in case of minority or incapacity, or fixed by the competent court.

2. If, after the amount has been fixed by agreement or judicial decision, the circumstances determining its fixing are modified, the amount of the maintenance provision may be increased or reduced, equally by agreement or judicial decision.

#### Article 32.º

Provisional maintenance provision

1. The Court may, at any moment, ex officio or at the request of the victim or the Public Prosecutor, grant a provisional maintenance provision.

2 . There shall be no place, in any case, for the restitution of a provisional maintenance provision already received.

3. In case of economic insufficiency of the defendant, the maintenance owed is borne by the Services of the Ministry of Social Solidarity.

#### Article 33.º

Social Reintegration Assistance

1. The Ministry responsible for social services supports the victims in the process of social reintegration as well as in the provision of maintenance when such proves necessary.

2. The extent and nature of the support to be provided is defined by decree of the member of Government responsible for social solidarity.

#### Article 34.º

Procedure

1. The request for maintenance provision may be joined to the corresponding criminal proceedings.

2. The rules provided in articles 831.º and following of the Code of Civil Procedure apply in other respects.

## CHAPTER VI

CRIMINAL ASPECTS

#### Article 35.º

Domestic violence crimes

For purposes of application of this law, domestic violence crimes are considered:

- a) The legal types enshrined in articles 153.º, 154.º, 155.º and 156.º of the Penal Code;
- b) The types of wrongful act provided in articles 138.º,139.º 141.º, 145.º,146.º, 167.º, 171.º, 172.º, 175.º, 177.º, 178.º and 179.º, whenever in addition to the fulfillment of the typical factual elements contained in the incriminating norm one of the circumstances described in article 2.º of this law occurs.

#### Article 36.º

Public nature of domestic violence crimes

The domestic violence crimes referred to in article 35.º are of a public nature.

#### Article 37.º

Coercive measures

In addition to the coercive measures provided in the Code of Criminal Procedure, in case of domestic violence crime, the defendant may be subject, by determination of the trial judge, to the measure of coercive removal from the family residence whenever there are indications of violence that reasonably lead to the prediction that the acts of aggression may be repeated in a manner to create danger to the life or to the physical, psychological or sexual integrity of the victim, as well as to the prohibition of contact with the latter.

#### Article 38.º

Choice and determination of penalty

1. The court may substitute the prison sentence with a fine penalty provided that the prerequisites set forth in article 67.º of the Penal Code are met, the safety of the victim is ensured, the aggressor agrees to submit to treatment or monitoring by victim support services and it is beneficial for the maintenance of the family unit.

2. The defendant may also be sentenced to an accessory penalty of prohibition of contact with the victim, for a maximum period of 3 years, whenever it is considered that the application of the principal penalty is insufficient to prevent the repetition of identical acts.

#### Article 39.º

Protection of witnesses

Whenever it proves necessary, the competent court applies procedural measures for the protection of witnesses in proceedings relating to domestic violence, to victims and to persons with knowledge of the facts constituting the subject matter of the proceedings or other information relevant to the decision, under the applicable law.

#### Article 40.º

Professional secrecy

1. Technical and non-technical personnel, in service at reception centers, shelters and specialized assistance services, are bound to professional secrecy in relation to facts of which they have become aware solely by reason of the professional relationship established with the victims in their care.

2. When the consent of a victim in their care is requested and such consent is obtained of free will, the duty of professional secrecy of the referred personnel ceases, if called to testify or to provide other information to judicial authorities.

## CHAPTER VII

FINAL PROVISIONS

#### Article 41.º

Regulation

It is incumbent upon the Government to approve the norms necessary for the implementation and development of this law within a period of 180 days.

#### Article 42.º

Entry into force

This law enters into force on the day following its publication.
