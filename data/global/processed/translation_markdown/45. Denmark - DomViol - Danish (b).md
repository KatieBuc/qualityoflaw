The Folketing has adopted and We by Our consent have confirmed the following act:

## Chapter 1

Restraining orders and area prohibition orders

### Section 1. By a restraining order, a person may be prohibited from approaching another person through personal, oral or written

contact, including by electronic communication, or otherwise contacting or following the other person.

### Section 2. A restraining order may be issued if

1) there is a reasonable suspicion that a person

a) has violated the peace of another by harassing or annoying the other person by contact etc. as set out in

### section 1, or

b) has committed against the other person a criminal act that may be equated with such a violation of peace, and

2) there are specific reasons to assume that the person concerned will continue to violate the other person as set out in no. 1.

Subsection 2. A restraining order may also be issued if

1) there is a reasonable suspicion that a person has committed a violation of the Criminal Code's provisions on homicide, robbery, deprivation of liberty, violence, arson, rape or other sexual offence or attempt at one of the said offences, and

2) the victim or his or her next of kin, considering the gravity of the offence, cannot reasonably be expected to tolerate contact etc. with the person concerned as set out in section 1.

### Section 3. By an area prohibition order, a person may be prohibited from staying or moving in a specifically delimited

area in the vicinity of another person's residence or workplace, place of education or place of stay or other area where the person frequently moves.

### Section 4. An area prohibition order may be issued if the conditions for a restraining order pursuant to section 2 are met, when

1) the suspicion concerns frequently repeated violation pursuant to section 2, subsection 1, no. 1, intentional violation of a restraining order pursuant to section 1 or a criminal offence covered by section 2, subsection 2, or section 8, no. 1, and

2) a restraining order pursuant to section 1 cannot be considered sufficient to protect the other person.

### Section 5.

A restraining order is issued for a specified period of up to 5 years. An area prohibition order is issued for a specified period of up to 1 year.

### Section 6.

A restraining order or area prohibition order may be extended to cover a member of the victim's household if it is deemed necessary for the purpose of the restraining order or area prohibition order.

## Chapter 2

Expulsion orders

### Section 7.

By an expulsion order, a person over 18 years of age may be prohibited from staying in his or her home.

### Section 8. A person may be expelled when

1) there is a reasonable suspicion that the person has committed against a member of his or her household a violation of sections 210, 213 or 266 of the Criminal Code or a violation covered by chapters 24-26 of the Criminal Code

## and which by law may result in imprisonment for 1 year and 6 months, or that the person has

acted in a manner that otherwise implies a threat of violence against a member of the household, and

2) according to the information obtained, there are specific reasons to assume that the person by remaining in the home will commit a criminal offence referred to in no. 1.

### Section 9. In connection with the processing of a case concerning expulsion, the police may detain a person who

may reasonably be assumed to have acted in a manner that implies a threat of violence against a member of the person's household.

Subsection 2. For the detention, the rules on arrest in sections 755-759 and

### section 760

, subsection 1, of the Administration of Justice Act otherwise apply. The detention may not be extended beyond 24 hours.

### Section 10. An expulsion order is issued for a specified period of up to 4 weeks.

### Section 11.

At the latest simultaneously with a decision on expulsion, the police must notify the municipality of the case. Subsection 2. When notifying the municipality, information and documents in the case must also be sent, including electronically stored data, that are necessary for the social authorities' processing of the case.

## Chapter 3

Common provisions

### Section 12. Restraining orders, area prohibition orders and expulsion orders must not be disproportionate to the disruption

thereby caused to the person's circumstances, the consideration for the person or persons whom the measure is to protect, and the nature of the conduct displayed by the person.

### Section 13.

Restraining orders, area prohibition orders and expulsion orders do not cover contact, stay or movement that for special reasons must be considered justified.

### Section 14.

Restraining orders, area prohibition orders and expulsion orders may be decided when a person whom the measure is to protect requests it, or when general considerations require it.

### Section 15. In cases concerning restraining orders, area prohibition orders and expulsion orders, the police advise the parties on the rules in this

act when there is reason to do so.

Subsection 2. Decisions on restraining orders, area prohibition orders and expulsion orders are made by the chief of police. Subsection 3. The Director of Public Prosecutions handles complaints about restraining orders, area prohibition orders and expulsion orders. The Director of Public Prosecutions' decision cannot be appealed.

### Section 16.

Decisions on restraining orders, area prohibition orders and expulsion orders must be served. When serving area prohibition orders and expulsion orders, the person concerned must be advised of the right to demand that the decision be brought before the court.

## Chapter 4

Judicial review

### Section 17.

The person who is issued an area prohibition order or expelled may, within 14 days after the decision has been served on the person concerned, demand that the police bring the decision before the district court.

Subsection 2. The person who demands that a decision on an area prohibition order or expulsion order be brought before the court (the complainant) must provide the police with an address and a possible telephone number to which summons to a possible court hearing can be sent. Service of summons to a court hearing is not necessary.

Subsection 3. The case must be brought before the district court as soon as possible and at the latest within 1 week after receipt of the request at the place where information in the case can most appropriately be assumed to be obtained. In cases concerning expulsion, however, the case must be brought within 24 hours.

Subsection 4. A request to bring the case before the court does not have suspensory effect.

### Section 18.

A lawyer is appointed for the person who demands that a decision on expulsion be brought before the court. A lawyer may be appointed for the person who demands that a decision on an area prohibition order be brought before the court if the court, considering the nature of the case, the nature of the intervention, the complainant's person and the circumstances in general, finds it desirable. The appointed lawyer has the same powers as a defence counsel in a criminal case. Regarding remuneration and reimbursement of expenses for an appointed lawyer, the same rules apply as for appointed defence counsel, cf. section 741 of the Administration of Justice Act.

Subsection 2. A lawyer may be appointed for a victim who must give testimony in court. The rules in sections 741 c and 741 d of the Administration of Justice Act apply correspondingly. Regarding remuneration and reimbursement of expenses for the appointed lawyer, the same rules apply as in cases where free legal aid has been granted, cf. chapter 31 of the Administration of Justice Act.

### § 19. The case shall be dealt with in writing or orally as determined by the court.

Stk. 2. If the case is dealt with orally, a court hearing shall be held as soon as possible. If the request for bringing the case before the court is made upon service of a first-instance decision on expulsion, a court hearing shall be held within 5 days.

Stk. 3. The complainant has the right to be present at any court hearing and to make a statement, unless the court finds that this for special reasons is futile or harmful to the person concerned. The case may be dealt with even if the person concerned fails to appear. If a lawyer has been appointed pursuant to section 18(1), however, the case may only be dealt with if the appointed lawyer has appeared.

Stk. 4. The court makes its decision on the basis of the documents of the case and any statement in court by the complainant. In special circumstances, the court may allow further presentation of evidence.

Stk. 5. The court's decision is made by order. If the decision is that the intervention is approved, the order shall state the specific circumstances of the case on which it is based that the conditions are met.

Stk. 6. If the complainant is ordered to pay legal costs in a criminal case concerning matters that have given rise to the exclusion order or the expulsion, the judgment in the criminal case shall also order the person concerned to pay the costs of the case concerning the exclusion order or expulsion. However, the court may exceptionally decide that these costs shall be paid in whole or in part by the State Treasury if special circumstances warrant it.

### § 20. Otherwise, the rules of the Administration of Justice Act on criminal procedure apply.

Stk. 2. With regard to claims for compensation, section 1018 h of the Administration of Justice Act applies. With regard to detention pursuant to section 9, however, section 1018 a of the Administration of Justice Act applies when expulsion is not carried out or approved.

## Kapitel 5

Penal provisions

### § 21. Any person who wilfully violates a restraining order pursuant to section 1, an exclusion order pursuant to section 3 or an expulsion

pursuant to section 7 shall be liable to a fine or imprisonment for up to 2 years.

Stk. 2. When determining the penalty pursuant to subsection 1, it shall be considered as an aggravating circumstance that the conduct has constituted part of a systematic and persistent persecution or harassment.

Stk. 3. Violation of subsection 1 shall only be prosecuted upon request of the aggrieved person, unless public interests require it.

Entry into force provisions, etc.

### § 25.

This Act enters into force on 1 March 2012.
