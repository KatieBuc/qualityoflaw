PRELIMINARY PROVISIONS

###### Art. 1.- An infraction that laws punish with police penalties

is a contravention. An infraction that laws punish with correctional penalties is an offense. An infraction that laws punish with an afflictive or infamous penalty is a crime.

###### Art. 2.- Any attempt to commit a crime may be considered as

the crime itself, when it manifests itself with a beginning of execution, or when the guilty party, despite having done everything in his power to consummate it, does not achieve his purpose for reasons independent of his will; these circumstances being subject to the judges' appreciation.

###### Art. 3.- Attempts to commit an offense are not deemed offenses, except

in cases where a special provision of law so determines.

###### Art. 4.- Contraventions, offenses and crimes that are

committed may only be punished by virtue of a provision of law promulgated prior to their commission.

###### Art. 5.- The provisions of this code are not appli-

cable to military contraventions, offenses or crimes.

## LIBRO PRIMERO:

PENALTIES IN CRIMINAL AND

###### Art. 6.- Penalties in criminal matters are afflictive and infa-

mous; or infamous only.

###### Art. 7.- (Modified by Law 46-99 of May 20,

1999). Afflictive and infamous penalties are: 1st. Major imprisonment; 2nd. Detention and 3rd. Minor imprisonment.

###### Art. 8.- Civic degradation is an infamous penalty.

###### Art. 9.

Penalties in correctional matters are: 1st., banishment; 2nd., confinement; 3rd., temporary imprisonment; 4th., temporary interdiction of certain civic, civil or family rights; 5th., fine.

###### Art. 10.- Penalties pronounced by law for crimes, of-

fenses and contraventions shall always be imposed, without prejudice to restitutions and damages that may result in favor of the aggrieved parties.

###### Art. 11.- Penalties common to criminal and co-

rrectional matters are: subjection of the convicted person to the supervision of the high police, fine** and special confiscation of the corpus delicti, when it is property of the convicted person, that of things produced by the offense, and lastly, that of those which served for its commission or which were intended for that purpose.

OF PENALTIES IN CORRECTIONAL MATTERS

###### Art. 37.- (Modified implicitly by article 8 of the

Constitution of the Republic). Every person convicted of banishment shall be taken, by order of the Government, outside the territory of the Republic. The duration of banishment may not exceed three years nor be less than one.

###### Art. 38.- (Modified implicitly by article 8 of the

Constitution of the Republic). If before the expiration of the penalty the banished person enters Dominican territory, he shall be convicted, once the identity of his person is verified, to imprisonment for a time at least equal to that which remained for the expiration of the banishment, without the conviction imposed in this case being able to be pronounced for a longer time.

###### Art. 39.- (Modified by Law 4381 of February 7, 1956

G.O. 7945 and modified implicitly by article 8 of the Constitution of the Republic). Every person convicted of confinement shall be taken to the provincial or district capital city or to the municipality of the territory of the Republic indicated by the conviction sentence. The duration of this penalty shall be six months at least, and two years at most. In the event that the confined person leaves the place of his confinement, he shall be convicted of correctional imprisonment, for a time equal to that which remained for the expiration of the confinement.

###### Art. 40.- Every person convicted of correctional imprisonment shall be detained

in a correctional house. He shall be assigned, according to his choice, to one of the workshops established in the house. The duration of this penalty shall be six days at least, and two years at most; except in cases of recidivism or others in which the law provides 22 Penal Code of the Dominican Republic otherwise. The calculation of time for the duration of penalties is twenty-four hours for each day of arrest, and thirty days for each month.

###### Art. 41.- A part of the product of the work of those detained

for correctional offense shall be allocated to the common expenses of the house, another to provide them with some advantages or relief during their detention, if they deserve it, reserving the third part to form a fund for them, which shall be delivered to them upon their release from prison. In compliance with these provisions, what the regulations that the Executive Power issues on the matter prescribe shall be observed.

###### Art. 42.- The courts that hear cases in correctional matters

may, in certain cases, deprive the convicted person of part or all of the exercise of his following civic, civil and family rights: 1o., of voting and election; 2o., of eligibility; 3o., of being a juror or appointed to exercise other public functions, or for positions in the administration; 4o., of carrying weapons; 5o., of voting or suffrage in family deliberations; 6o., of being a guardian or curator of persons other than his own children, and with the assent of the family; 7o., of being experts or serving as witnesses in public acts; 8o., of giving testimony in court, unless received as simple information.

###### Art. 43.- The courts shall not pronounce the interdiction referred to

in the preceding article, except when the law expressly authorizes or orders it.

###### Art. 44.- (Modified by the Constitution of 1966). Subjection

to the surveillance of the high police gives the Government the right to determine certain places to which the convicted person may not appear until after having served his sentence. Criminal Code of the Dominican Republic 23

At least fifteen days before the convicted person obtains his freedom, he must declare the place where he is going to establish his residence; and if he does not do so, the Government will fix one for him. The individual condemned to the surveillance of the high police may not leave the residence that he has chosen or that has been indicated to him before six months, without the authorization of the Secretary of State for Interior and Police. However, the Provincial Governors may grant this authorization: 1o., in the case of simple relocation within the limits of their province; and 2o., in cases of urgency, but only on a provisional basis. After six months have elapsed, or earlier in the case of having obtained the competent authorization, the convicted person may move to any place where he is not prohibited from doing so, notifying the Governor or local authority eight days in advance. The stay of six months referred to in the article is mandatory for the convicted person in each of the places that he successively chooses during the time he is subject to the surveillance of the high police, unless he obtains special authorization granted, in accordance with the preceding provisions, by the Secretary of State for Interior and Police or by the Provincial Governors. The convicted person who returns to his residence shall obtain a route order that regulates the itinerary he must follow, from which he may not deviate; nor may he exceed the time set for his stay in transit locations. He shall be obliged to present himself within twenty-four hours of his arrival before the authority of the place where he is going to reside.

###### Art. 45.- In case of violation of the provisions prescribed

in the preceding article, the individual subject to the surveillance of the high police shall be sentenced by the correctional courts to imprisonment that may not exceed two years.

###### Art. 46.- (Modified by Laws 224 of June 26,

1984 and 46-99 of May 20, 1999). In no case may the duration of the sentence under the surveillance of the high police exceed five years. Those convicted of major imprisonment, 24 Criminal Code of the Dominican Republic detention and minor imprisonment, shall remain as of right, after having served their sentence and for five years, under the surveillance of the high police. However, the conviction may reduce this term, and even declare that the convicted person will not be subject to the surveillance of the high police. Any person convicted to the maximum sentence of major imprisonment who obtains commutation or remission of his sentence shall remain, as of right, subject to the surveillance of the high police for five years, unless otherwise resolved by the pardon decree.

###### Art. 47.- Surveillance under the high police may be pardoned

or reduced by pardon; and may be suspended by governmental provision.

###### Art. 48.- The prescription of the sentence does not relieve the convicted person

of the surveillance under the high police to which he is subject. In the case of prescription of the greater afflictive penalty, the convicted person shall be, as of right, under the surveillance of the high police for five years; and it shall not produce its effects except from the day on which the prescription is fulfilled.

###### Art. 49.- Individuals who have been convicted of

crimes or offenses against the internal or external security of the State must remain subject to the surveillance of the high police.

###### Art. 50.- Outside the cases determined by the articles

preceding, convicted persons shall not remain subject to the surveillance of the high police, except in the event that it is so established by a particular provision of the law.

###### Art. 51.- When restitution is required, the guilty party may

also be sentenced in favor of the aggrieved party, if the latter so requests, to compensation for the damages that the former has caused, which must be assessed by the court when the law has not determined them. In no case may the courts, even with the consent of the aggrieved party, allocate compensation to charitable works or any others.

###### Art. 52.- The execution of sentences to fines,

###### Art. 53.- When fines and costs are pronounced in

###### Art. 59.- Accomplices to a crime or an offense shall be im-

posed the penalty immediately inferior to that corresponding to the authors of this crime or offense; except in cases where the law provides otherwise.

###### Art. 60.- Those who by gifts, promises, threats, abuse of power or authority, guilty machinations or plots, provoke that action or give instructions to commit it will be punished as accomplices to an action qualified as a crime or delict: those who, knowingly, provide weapons or instruments, or facilitate the means that have served to execute the action. Those who, knowingly, have helped or assisted the author or authors of the action, in those acts that prepared or facilitated its realization, or in those that consummated it; without prejudice to the penalties that are specifically established in this code, against the authors of plots or provocations threatening the internal or external security of the State, even in the event that the crime that the conspirators or provocateurs intended to execute was not committed.

###### Art. 61.- Those who, knowing the criminal conduct of

malefactors who engage in raids or violence against the security of the State, public peace, persons or properties, habitually provide them with lodging, hiding place or meeting place, will be punished as their accomplices.

###### Art. 62.- Will also be considered as accomplices, and punished

as such, those who knowingly have concealed in whole or in part, things robbed, stolen, subtracted or acquired by means of crime or delict.

belong to him, becomes guilty of theft.

###### Art. 380.- Subtractions between spouses and those effected

by widows or widowers with respect to things that belonged to the deceased spouse shall not be considered thefts, and shall give rise only to civil indemnifications. ###### Art. 390.- Inhabited houses are considered to be buildings, dwellings, huts, shacks even if mobile, which, without being currently inhabited, are destined for habitation. Dependencies are also considered inhabited places, such as courtyards, corrals, granaries, stables and other buildings that are enclosed therein, whatever the use to which they are destined, and even when they have a particular enclosure within the general fence or circuit.

###### Art. 391.- Corrals, pigsties and pens, destined to enclose cattle large or small, whatever the material of which they are constructed, shall also be considered as dependencies of an inhabited house, when they depend on huts or other shelters for the guardians of said cattle.

###### Art. 392.- Land surrounded by ditches, stakes, brambles, platforms, palisades, living or dead hedges, or walls is considered enclosed, whatever the nature of the materials used in its construction, and also whatever its height and depth, and its state of deterioration or age, and even if there is no door that closes with a key or otherwise, or even if the door is a screen or is habitually open.

###### Art. 393.- The forcing, breaking, deterioration or demolition of walls, ceilings, floors, mezzanines, doors, windows, bolts, padlocks or other utensils or instruments that serve to close or prevent passage is qualified as breaking. The breaking of any other kind of enclosure, whatever it may be, is also qualified as breaking.

###### Art. 394.- Breakings are exterior or interior.

###### Art. 395.- Exterior breakings are those that an individual uses to penetrate houses, courtyards, enclosures or their dependencies, or dwellings or other inhabited places; and interior ones are those that, after the guilty party penetrates the places mentioned in the preceding paragraph, are made to interior doors, windows or hedges, as well as those that have the purpose of opening wardrobes and other closed furniture. ###### Art. 396.- Included in the categories of interior breakings is: the simple theft of boxes, small boxes, bales arranged with packaging, and other closed furniture that contains effects, whatever these may be, and even if the breaking does not occur in the place where the robbery was committed.

###### Art. 397.

Climbing is qualified as: entry into houses, courtyards, gardens, corrals and other enclosed buildings, effected over walls, doors or roofs, or overcoming any other fence. False keys are and are deemed to be: picks, skeleton keys, master keys and any others; and other instruments that the guilty party uses to open the bolts, padlocks or locks of doors, windows, wardrobes and other closed furniture, when those are not the ones that the owner, guest or tenant used for that purpose.

FIFTH WHEREAS: That in order to guarantee the inherent rights of human beings confined in the modern Correction and Rehabilitation Centers into which Dominican prisons must be transformed, a greater concentration of both economic and human resources is necessary for their strengthening and development; SIXTH WHEREAS: That what concerns the improvement of the prison system benefits Dominican society;

SEVENTH WHEREAS: That the fine penalty has a deterrent character and constitutes a fiscal measure that aspires to procure for the State the resources for the combat and prevention of crime.

IN VIEW OF: The Constitution of the Republic.

IN VIEW OF: The Penal Code of the Dominican Republic. IN VIEW OF: The Criminal Procedure Code of the Dominican Republic.

IN VIEW OF: Law No. 821 on Judicial Organization.

HAS PASSED THE FOLLOWING LAW:

###### Article 1. It is established that fines or pecuniary sanctions

for different infractions, whether crimes or felonies, whose amount is less than one-third of the minimum wage of the public sector, shall henceforth be raised to said amount.

###### Article 2. Fines or pecuniary sanctions for cases

of contraventions shall be established by the competent court in the amount comprised between one-fifth and one-third of the minimum wage of the public sector.

Penal Code of the Dominican Republic 1

###### Article 3. A special fund is created for the implementation and

development of the New Penitentiary Model, under the responsibility of the Attorney General's Office of the Republic, to administer all collections obtained from fines collected by said institution, through the application of this Law.

###### Article 4. All articles in the current Penal Code and in

any other special legislation of a criminal nature that establish fines or pecuniary sanctions for different infractions that are contrary to this Law are hereby amended.
