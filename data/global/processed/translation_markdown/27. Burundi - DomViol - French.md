# Burundi

Burundi

Law No. 1/27 of December 29, 2017

[NB ‐ Law No. 1/27 of December 29, 2017 revising the Penal Code]

## Book 1 ‐ Offenses and Repression in General

### Title 1 ‐ General Provisions

#### Chapter 1 ‐ Offense in General

###### Art.1.‐ An offense is an action or omission that manifests itself as an infringement

on the social order and which the law sanctions with a penalty.

###### Art.2.‐ Only the law defines the constituent elements of the offense and determines the applicable

penalties.

###### Art.3.‐ Criminal law is to be strictly interpreted.

###### Art.4.‐ No offense may be punished by penalties that were not provided for by law

before the offense was committed.

However, in case of conflict between two criminal laws, one former under whose authority the offense was committed and the other promulgated since the offense, and before a final judgment has been rendered, the new law alone must be applied if it provides for a less severe penalty.

###### Art.5.‐ Immediately applicable to offenses committed before their entry into

force are:

1° Laws on jurisdiction and judicial organization, as long as a judgment on the merits has not been rendered at first instance;

2° Laws establishing the modalities of prosecution and the forms of procedure;

3° Laws relating to regimes for the application and execution of penalties insofar as they provide for more favorable conditions for the convicted person;

4° Laws relating to the limitation period for public prosecution and penalties if they provide for shorter time limits;

5° Laws having as their object the decriminalization of facts.

###### Art.6.‐ Remedies are undertaken and procedural time limits are counted

according to the laws in force at the time the relevant acts are performed.

###### Art.7.‐ The immediate application of the new law has no effect on the validity of acts

accomplished in accordance with the former law.

###### Art.8.‐ Any offense committed on the territory of Burundi by Burundians or

foreigners is, subject to international conventions on diplomatic and consular immunities, punished in accordance with the criminal law of Burundi. Diplomatic or consular immunities do not apply in case of the crime of genocide, crime against humanity or war crime.

###### Art.9.‐ Offenses committed on board boats, ships, trains or aircraft

registered in Burundi or abroad and carrying out their activity in Burundi or against them are punished in accordance with the criminal law of Burundi.

###### Art.10.‐ Any misdemeanor or crime committed outside the national territory by a Burundian or a

foreigner is, subject to extradition conventions, punished by the criminal law of Burundi if the perpetrator is in Burundi or if the victim has Burundian nationality and the act is punished by the legislation of the country where the offense was committed.

In offenses other than those relating to the counterfeiting of State seals and national currencies, those relating to acts of torture, terrorism, genocide, crimes against humanity and war crimes, the prosecution and trial of offenses committed abroad are subject to the filing of a complaint by the injured party or to the official denunciation by the authority of the country where the offense was committed.

The jurisdiction of Burundian courts, with regard to the crime of genocide, crime against humanity and war crimes, is not subject to the requirement that these crimes be punished by the legislation of the country where they were committed nor to extradition conventions.

###### Art.11.‐ When the offense was committed abroad, no prosecution takes place if

the accused proves that he has been definitively tried and in case of conviction, that he has served his sentence or it is time-barred, obtained pardon or benefited from amnesty.

#### Chapter 2 ‐ Classification of Offenses

###### Art.12.

‐ According to their degree of severity, offenses are classified as crimes, misdemeanors, or contraventions.

Offenses punishable by at most two months of penal servitude are contraventions.

Offenses whose penalty is between two months and five years of penal servitude are misdemeanors.

Offenses punishable by more than five years of penal servitude are crimes.

###### Art.13.‐ When the penalty for an offense is expressed by a minimum and a

maximum, only the latter is taken into consideration for the application of the provisions of the preceding article.

When the penalty for an offense is increased by the effect of aggravating circumstances, the maximum of the aggravated penalty alone is taken into consideration for the application of the provisions of the preceding article.

When the penalty incurred by the perpetrator of the offense is increased by the effect of recidivism, this increase is not taken into consideration for the application of the provisions of the preceding article.

#### Chapter 3 ‐ Attempt

###### Art.14.‐ There is punishable attempt when the resolution to commit the offense has been

manifested by external acts that form the beginning of execution of this offense and which were suspended or failed to have their effect only due to circumstances independent of the will of the perpetrator.

###### Art.15.‐ The attempt of an offense is punished by half the penalty of the consummated misdemeanor or crime

.

If the consummated offense is punished by life penal servitude the perpetrator of the attempt is punished by fifteen years of penal servitude.

###### Art.16.‐ There is impossible attempt when a potential offender has done everything

within his power to commit an offense, when it could not be realized due to an impossibility of which he was unaware.

The impossible attempt is punished by one quarter of the penalty of the failed offense. If the offense is punished by life penal servitude, the perpetrator of the impossible attempt is punished by ten years of penal servitude.

###### Art.17.‐ The attempt of a contravention is punishable only in cases determined by

the law.

#### Chapter 4 ‐ Criminal Liability

##### Section 1 ‐ Principle

###### Art.18.‐ Criminal liability is personal: no one is punishable except by reason of

his own act, without prejudice to the particular provisions contained in this

Code.

###### Art.19.‐ The perpetrator of the offense is the one who personally commits the different

material and intellectual elements as defined by law.

###### Art.20.‐ The intellectual author is the one who conceives the offense and has all or

certain material acts carried out by a third party.

###### Art.21.‐ With the exception of those cited in Article 24, legal persons are

criminally liable for offenses committed by their directors or legal representatives acting on behalf of these persons or in the defense of their interests or on the occasion of any other act closely related to their corporate purpose.

###### Art.22.‐ The criminal liability of legal persons does not exclude that of

natural persons who are authors or accomplices of the same acts.

###### Art.23.‐ Assimilated to the legal persons referred to in Article 21 are:

1° Temporary associations;

2° Civil or commercial companies in formation;

3° Non-profit or mutualist associations in formation;

4° De facto associations.

###### Art.24.‐ Cannot be considered as legal persons for the application of

Article 21: the State, Communes and Public Establishments of a commercial, industrial, administrative and/or scientific nature.

##### Section 2 ‐ Subjective Causes of Criminal Irresponsibility or Mitigation of the

Penalty

###### Art.25.‐ Not punishable is one who suffered from an illness or mental deficiency

that deprived them of the ability to understand the criminal nature or the nature of their behavior, or to control it to conform to the requirements of the law.

###### Art.26.‐ However, one who voluntarily deprived themselves of the use of their mental

faculties at the time of the offense remains criminally responsible, even if this deprivation was not caused for the purpose of committing the offense.

###### Art.27.‐ Not punishable is the person who acted under the constraint of a force

to which they could not resist.

However, constraint can never be used as an argument by the defense in cases of genocide, crimes against humanity, war crimes and other crimes falling under international law, but it may only be taken into

###### Art.28.

‐ Minors under fifteen years of age are criminally irresponsible. Offenses committed by them give rise only to civil reparations.

###### Art.29.‐ When the perpetrator or accomplice of an offense is a minor of fifteen years

of age or over and under eighteen years at the time of the offense, the penalties are pronounced as follows:

1° If he should incur the penalty of penal servitude for life, he is sentenced to a penalty of five to ten years of principal penal servitude;

2° If he has incurred a sentence for a fixed term or a fine, the penalties that may be pronounced against him cannot exceed four years.

###### Art.30.‐ The protection, education and supervision measures that may be

pronounced against a minor are as follows:

1° Warning;

2° Reminder of the law;

3° Return to parents, guardian or a trusted person;

4° Educational assistance;

5° Placement in a social institution, educational establishment or other authorized educational institution.

At the same time as pronouncing a principal penalty other than penal servitude, the judge seized of the case may grant the minor the benefit of educational assistance or order placement in a foster family or in an authorized institution which it determines.

The judge seized of the case may at any time, either on its own motion, or at the request of the Public Prosecutor, parents or legal representatives, or on the report of the social worker, modify the protection, supervision or education measures taken with regard to the minor or terminate them.

##### Section 3 ‐ Objective causes of criminal irresponsibility

###### Art.31.‐ There is no offense:

1° When the act was ordered or authorized by law or commanded by legitimate authority, unless the act was manifestly illegal.

However, hierarchical order can never be used as an argument by the defense in cases of genocide, crimes against humanity, war crimes and other crimes falling under international law, but it may only be taken into 2° In case of state of necessity, which is the position of one who, placed before a serious and imminent danger for himself, others or property, or in order to interrupt a crime or an offense, commits an act that falls under criminal law in order to ensure the safeguarding of an interest superior to that sacrificed. The means employed for this purpose must be proportional to the seriousness of the threat.

However, state of necessity can never be used as an argument by the defense in cases of genocide, crimes against humanity, war crimes and other crimes falling under international law, but it may only be taken into The offenses provided for under the chapter on voluntary homicides are not concerned by the content of point 2°.

3° In case of self-defense which is the reaction of one who, facing an unjustified aggression towards themselves, or others, accomplishes an act that falls under criminal law, provided that the means used are proportional to the seriousness of the aggression.

##### Section 4 ‐ Excuses

###### Art.32.‐ No crime or offense may be excused except in cases determined by

law.

###### Art.33.‐ Legal excuses leave the offense and responsibility intact, but

ensure for offenders, either impunity when they are absolutory, or a moderation of the penalty when they are mitigating.

#### Chapitre 5 ‐ Mitigating circumstances

###### Art.34.‐ The judge sovereignly assesses the circumstances which, prior,

concurrent or subsequent to the offense, mitigate the guilt of its perpetrator. However, decisions concerning the reduction of the penalty must not be taken at the entire discretion of judges but only in accordance with appropriate factors, taking into account mitigating circumstances.

###### Art.35.‐ The decision that admits mitigating circumstances indicates them, enumerates them and

provides reasons for them.

###### Art.36.‐ If mitigating circumstances exist, the penalties of penal servitude and

fine may be reduced to the extent determined by the judge.

#### Chapitre 6 ‐ Participation in the offense

###### Art.37.‐ Are considered as perpetrators:

1° Those who, personally, took part directly in the execution of the offense or directly cooperated in its execution;

2° Those who, by any act, provided such assistance for the execution that, without their assistance, the offense could not have been committed.

###### Art.38.‐ Are considered as accomplices to an offense, those who, without direct participation

in it and without their contribution being indispensable, have:

1° Provoked to action by gift, promise, threats, abuse of authority and power, machinations or culpable artifices or given instructions to commit it; 2° Procured weapons, instruments or any other means that served the action knowing that it was to be used for it;

3° With knowledge, aided by any means or assisted the perpetrator or perpetrators of the action in the acts that prepared or facilitated it or in those that consummated it; 4° With knowledge of their criminal conduct, habitually provided lodging, place of retreat or meeting to one or more wrongdoers;

5° Either by incitement to commit the offense through speeches given in meetings or in public places, or by writings or printed matter sold or distributed, placed for sale or displayed in public places or meetings or by placards or posters displayed to public view, directly provoked the perpetrator or perpetrators to commit this action;

6°, Concealed or aided wrongdoers under the conditions provided for in article 319.

###### Art.39.‐ One who, intentionally, has caused a person to commit an

offense incurs, if it has been committed, the penalty applicable to the perpetrator of the offense.

###### Art.40.‐ When the offense has not been committed solely by reason of the voluntary abstention

of the one who was to commit it, the instigator incurs half of the penalty provided for that offense.

###### Art.41.‐ Except for specific provisions establishing other penalties, co-perpetrators and

accomplices are punished as follows:

1° Co-perpetrators, with the penalty established by law for perpetrators;

2° Accomplices, with a penalty that does not exceed half of that which they would have incurred if they had themselves been perpetrators;

3° When the penalty provided by law is penal servitude for life, the penalty applicable to the accomplice is twenty years of penal servitude.

###### Art.42.‐ Personal circumstances from which result the aggravation, mitigation or

exemption from penalty, have effect only with regard to the sole participant to whom they relate.

###### Art.43.‐ Objective circumstances inherent to the offense that aggravate or

diminish the penalty of those who participated in that offense have effect against them or in their favor depending on whether they had knowledge of them or not.

### Title 2 ‐ Penalties in General

#### Chapter 1 ‐ Classification of Penalties

##### Section 1 ‐ Penalties Applicable to Natural Persons

###### Paragraph 1 ‐ Principal Penalties

###### Art.44.‐ The principal penalties applicable to natural persons are:

 1° penal servitude;

 2° fine;

 3° community service.

1. Penal Servitude

###### Art.45.

‐ The duration of principal penal servitude is either perpetual or temporary.

###### Art.46.‐ The duration of principal temporary penal servitude is a minimum of one day and

a maximum of thirty years according to the cases specified by law, except in cases of recidivism or others where the law has determined other limits.

It is calculated by day, month and year of the Gregorian calendar. The penalty of one day is twenty-four hours. That of one month is thirty days.

###### Art.47.‐ Those sentenced to penal servitude serve their sentence in prisons

determined by the Minister responsible for Justice. They are employed either inside these establishments or outside, in one of the works authorized by the establishment's regulations unless they are exempted from it, in exceptional cases, by the Minister responsible for Justice.

###### Art.48.‐ Any detention undergone before irrevocable conviction as a result of

the offense that gave rise to that conviction is credited in full against the entire duration of the penal servitude sentence pronounced.

2. Fine

###### Art.49.‐ A fine is a pecuniary penalty that consists in the obligation to pay a

sum of money to the public treasury. It is at least one thousand Burundian francs.

###### Art.50.‐ A fine is pronounced individually against each of the convicted persons in

respect of the same offense. There is no collective fine.

###### Art.51.‐ In the absence of payment within eight days following the conviction

becoming irrevocable and in the case of an immediately enforceable judgment, within eight days following the pronouncement of the judgment or decision, the fine may be replaced by penal servitude the duration of which is fixed by the judgment of conviction according to the circumstances or the amount of the fine imposed on the convicted person.

###### Art.52.‐ The duration of subsidiary penal servitude in case of fine cannot exceed

twelve months. In all cases, the convicted person may free himself from this penal servitude by paying the fine. He cannot avoid proceedings against assets by offering to undergo penal servitude.

3. Community Service

###### Art.53.‐ Community service consists in the conviction as principal of a misdemeanor or

petty offense to personally perform unpaid work for the benefit of a legal person under public law or an association authorized to implement community service.

###### Art.54.‐ In the event that the judge believes he can pronounce a penal servitude sentence the

duration of which does not exceed two years, it may be waived and substituted with a conviction to community service the duration of which does not exceed two thousand eight hundred and twenty hours. In the application of this article, the penalty of one month of penal servitude corresponds to one hundred and twenty hours of community service.

The judgment specifies the penal servitude and/or fine penalty undergone by the convicted person who evades the execution of community service.

The penalty of community service and the penalty of fine or penal servitude cannot be pronounced cumulatively.

###### Art.55.‐ In choosing the nature of community service, the judge takes into account the

social environment, age as well as any other vulnerability criterion of the convicted person.

###### Art.56.‐ For each convicted person, the institution responsible for having community service

executed informs the territorially competent Prosecutor of the official designated to ensure supervision of the execution.

###### Art.57.‐ The designated official reports to the Prosecutor on the progress of

the execution of the work as well as all incidents that occur during its execution.

In the event that the perpetrator of the offense is a minor under eighteen years of age, the Prosecutor of the Republic transmits a copy of said report to the judge seized of the file.

###### Art.58.‐ Upon expiration of the period specified in the judgment, the institution for the benefit of which

the community service was performed sends a final report to the Prosecutor of the Republic, establishing the manner in which the work was executed.

In the event that the perpetrator of the offense is a minor under eighteen years of age, the Prosecutor of the Republic transmits a copy of said report to the judge seized of the file.

###### Art.59.‐ The nature and terms of execution of community service are specified

in the judgment.

###### Paragraph 2 ‐ Complementary Penalties Applicable to Natural Persons

###### Art.60.‐ The complementary penalties applicable to natural persons are:

 1° special confiscation;

 2° prohibition;

 3° socio-judicial monitoring;

 4° placement at the disposal of the Government;

 5° closure of establishment;

 6° publication of the conviction unless the perpetrator of the offense is a minor under eighteen years of age;

 7° temporary or permanent withdrawal of driving license;

 8° presentation of the convicted person to the public.

1. Special Confiscation

###### Art.61.‐ In case of crime or misdemeanor, the special confiscation of property that forms the

corpus of the offense or that was used or intended to commit it, or that was the product thereof may be pronounced in addition to the principal penalty, when the ownership of said property belongs to the convicted person.

When the ownership of the property described above does not belong to the convicted person, as well as in matters of petty offenses, special confiscation may only be pronounced in cases provided for by law.

Special confiscation applies to the thing that was used or intended to commit the offense or to the thing that is the product thereof with the exception of objects subject to restitution.

###### Art.62.‐ When the thing to be confiscated has not been seized or cannot be produced, the

confiscation is ordered in value. For the recovery of the sum representing the value of the thing to be confiscated, the provisions relating to constraint by detention are applicable.

The confiscated thing is, except for particular contrary provisions providing for its destruction or allocation, devolved to the State; it remains nevertheless encumbered, up to its value, with real rights lawfully constituted for the benefit of third parties.

###### Art.63.‐ The penalty of confiscation is pronounced automatically for objects that the judge

deems dangerous or harmful to public order and security.

###### Art.64.‐ General confiscation covering the totality of the present and future patrimony

of the convicted person is prohibited.

2. Prohibition

###### Art.65.‐ In cases determined by law, the following prohibitions may be

pronounced

1° Prohibition of civic, civil and family rights;

2° Prohibition from exercising a public function, a professional or social activity; 3° Exclusion from public contracts;

4° Prohibition from issuing cheques;

5° Prohibition from using payment cards;

6° Prohibition from leaving the territory;

7° Prohibition from residence and assignment to residence;

8° Prohibition from the territory;

9° Prohibition from access to the marital home for a spouse convicted of serious violence against their spouse;

10° Prohibition of the right to frequent certain places of public gathering, places of childcare and education of children.

###### Art.66.‐ The prohibition of civic, civil and family rights relates to:

1° The right to vote;

2° Eligibility;

3° The right to exercise a judicial function or to be an expert before a court, to represent or assist a party before justice;

4° The right to testify in court other than to make simple declarations; 5° The right to be a guardian or curator except for one's own children, after opinion of the Family Council;

6° The right to bear arms;

7° The right to wear any decoration.

The prohibition of civic, civil and family rights may not exceed a period of five years.

###### Art.67.‐ The prohibition of civic, civil and family rights pronounced by the courts and

tribunals has the effect of depriving the convicted person of one or more rights listed in the preceding article without it being able to apply to all of these rights. The prohibition of civic, civil and family rights may only be pronounced as a complement to a sentence of penal servitude exceeding ten years.

However, in cases expressly provided for by law, the prohibition of civic, civil and family rights may be total or partial. It may be suspended during execution under the same conditions as penal servitude.

It may be reduced or erased following the rehabilitation procedure, after a term and the fulfillment of conditions left to the discretion of the jurisdiction that pronounced the sentence.

###### Art.68.‐ The prohibition from exercising a public function, a professional or

social activity may relate either to the professional or social activity in the exercise of which or on the occasion of which the offence was committed, or to any other professional or social activity defined by the law that punishes the offence. The prohibition from exercising a public function or from exercising a professional or social activity may not exceed a period of twenty years.

###### Art.69.‐ Exclusion from public contracts consists in the prohibition from participating

directly or indirectly in any contract concluded by the State and public establishments, local authorities as well as by companies granted concessions or controlled by the State or by local authorities.

Exclusion from public contracts may not exceed five years.

###### Art.70.‐ The prohibition from issuing cheques entails for the convicted person an injunction

to return to the banker who had issued them the forms in their possession and in that of their agents.

The prohibition from issuing cheques may not exceed a period of five years.

###### Art.71.

‐ The prohibition from using payment cards consists, for the convicted person, in the injunction to return to the banker the cards in their possession and in that of their agents.

The prohibition from using payment cards may not exceed a period of five years.

###### Art.72.‐ The prohibition from leaving the territory entails a prohibition for the convicted person

from leaving the territory of Burundi before the final execution of their obligations arising from the judgment or decision.

###### Art.73.‐ In cases provided for by law, the sentence of prohibition from Burundian territory

may be pronounced definitively or for a period of ten years at most against a foreigner guilty of a crime or an offence.

The prohibition from the territory automatically entails the escorting of the convicted person to the border, where applicable, upon expiry of the principal sentence.

###### Art.74.‐ The prohibition from residence consists in the prohibition made to a convicted person

from appearing in certain places determined by the judge.

###### Art.75.‐ Assignment to residence consists in the obligation made to the convicted person to

reside in certain places determined by the judgment. The duration of the prohibition from residence may not exceed one year.

###### Art.76.‐ The prohibition from residence and assignment to residence may be pronounced:

1° Against any person convicted of having committed an offence punishable by a principal sentence of penal servitude of six months at most or when the applicable sentence must not exceed six months by reason of the circumstances;

2° Against whoever has committed, within ten years, at least two offences that have each resulted in penal servitude of at least two months.

###### Art.77.‐ The judgment that sentences to the penalties of prohibition from residence and assignment

to residence determines the date on which these take effect.

3. Socio-judicial supervision

###### Art.78.‐ In cases provided for by law, the courts and tribunals may order socio-

judicial supervision. Socio-judicial supervision entails, for the convicted person, the obligation to submit, under the control of the Officer of the Public Prosecutor and for a period determined by the trial court, to surveillance and assistance measures intended to prevent recidivism. The duration of socio-judicial supervision may not exceed five years in case of conviction for offences or ten years in case of conviction for crime.

The conviction decision also sets the maximum duration of incarceration of the convicted person in case of non-observance of the obligations imposed upon them. This incarceration may not exceed two years in case of conviction for an offence and five years in case of conviction for a crime.

###### Art.79.‐ Socio-judicial supervision may include an injunction to undergo care or

training. The injunction to undergo care may be pronounced by the trial court, if it is established, after medical examination, ordered under the conditions provided for by the Code of Criminal Procedure, that the person prosecuted is likely to be subject to treatment.

In case of refusal of the care or training offered to them, the incarceration pronounced pursuant to paragraph two of the preceding article is put into execution.

###### Art.80.‐ When socio-judicial supervision accompanies a custodial sentence without

suspension, it applies from the day of deprivation of liberty.

When the measures prescribed in the framework of socio-judicial supervision cannot be executed in a detention house, any incarceration of the convicted person suspends the legal period of socio-judicial supervision of the latter.

The imprisonment ordered by reason of non-observance of the obligations resulting from socio-judicial supervision is cumulated with the custodial sentences pronounced for offences committed during the execution of the measure.

###### Art.81.‐ The surveillance measures applicable to the person subject to socio-judicial supervision are those provided for in Article 123.

He may also be subject to one or more of the following obligations:

1° To refrain from appearing in any place or any category of specially designated places, and in particular places usually accommodating minors;

2° To refrain from frequenting or entering into contact with certain persons or certain categories of persons and in particular minors, with the exception, where applicable, of those designated by the court;

3° Not to engage in any professional or volunteer activity involving regular contact with minors.

4. Placement at the disposal of the Government

###### Art.82.‐ Anyone who has committed, within ten years, at least three offenses each of which has resulted in penal servitude of at least six months and who demonstrates a persistent tendency towards delinquency may, by order or judgment of conviction, be placed at the disposal of the Government for a period not exceeding ten years after expiry of the sentence of penal servitude.

###### Art.83.‐ The proceedings relating to the convictions forming the basis for placement at the disposal of the Government are attached to the prosecution file and the grounds for the decision are specified therein by indicating the circumstances that establish the persistent tendency towards delinquency.

###### Art.84.‐ When a convicted person has been placed at the disposal of the Government by two successive decisions for non-concurrent offenses, if the first decision has not reached its term at the expiry of the principal sentence of penal servitude pronounced by the second decision, the second placement at the disposal of the Government only takes effect after the expiry of the first.

###### Art.85.‐ When the convicted person is conditionally released, the sentence of placement at the disposal of the Government takes effect from the date of conditional release. Its execution is suspended in case of revocation of conditional release, from the time of arrest.

###### Art.86.‐ When during the execution of placement at the disposal of the Government, the convicted person is arrested, even preventively, pursuant to a judicial decision, the execution of the sentence of placement at the disposal of the Government is suspended for the duration of the detention.

###### Art.87.‐ The convicted person placed at the disposal of the Government is interned, if necessary, in an establishment designated by the Minister having Justice in his attributions.

###### Art.88.‐ At the expiry of the principal sentence, the Minister having Justice in his attributions decides whether he is released or interned. If the convicted person is released, he may, at any time, by reason of misconduct, be interned by decision of the Public Prosecutor of the jurisdiction where the misconduct occurred.

The convicted person may lodge an appeal against the decision of the Prosecutor with the Prosecutor General at the Court of Appeal.

###### Art.89.‐ The convicted person placed at the disposal of the Government may request to be relieved from the effects of this conviction. The request is addressed to the Prosecutor General at the Court of Appeal having jurisdiction over the court that pronounced the placement at the disposal of the Government.

The Prosecutor General at the Court of Appeal investigates the application and refers it, through his requisitions, to the court that convicted; the latter rules by reasoned decision, the convicted person having been duly summoned and heard.

In case of rejection, a new application may not be submitted before the expiry of a period of one year.

5. Closure of establishment

###### Art.90.‐ When the offense is committed in the context of commercial, artisanal or industrial activities by business heads and in all cases expressly provided for by law, the courts may, in addition to principal sentences, order the closure of the convicted person's establishment for a period of up to two years. In this case, the convicted person may transfer all or part of his stock, in particular perishable goods, to another professional. The transfer price may not be paid without the agreement of the treasury, which enjoys a special privilege over this price, for the payment of criminal or tax fines chargeable to the convicted person.

6. Publication of the conviction

###### Art.91.‐ In cases determined by law, at the request of an interested party or ex officio at the court's discretion, the judge may order that the decision of conviction be published in full or in extracts in the Official Bulletin of Burundi, in one or more other press publications or in one or more audiovisual communication services or by posting in places he determines, all at the expense of the convicted person.

The publication costs may not exceed the sum fixed for this purpose by the decision of conviction, without the duration of posting being able to exceed one month.

In case of inability of the convicted person to pay the publication costs, it is done at the initiative of the Public Prosecutor.

7. Temporary or permanent withdrawal of driving license

###### Art.92.‐ Temporary withdrawal of driving license as a complementary penalty is a measure resulting in the cancellation of the driving license for repetitive failures to comply with obligations contained in the Road Traffic Code. The holder of the driving license subject to temporary withdrawal must surrender his driving license within ten days and may not begin the procedure for obtaining a new driving license until after a period of one year. Both theoretical and practical tests must be retaken.

###### Art.93.‐ Permanent withdrawal of driving license as a complementary penalty is a measure resulting in the permanent withdrawal of the right to drive by reason of serious and repetitive failures to comply with obligations contained in the Road Traffic Code and their consequences.

8. Presentation of the convicted person to the public

###### Art.94.‐ In cases expressly determined by law, the penalty of presentation to the public may be pronounced against a convicted person in addition to penal servitude of at least ten years.

The penalty of presentation of the convicted person to the public may only be executed when the judgment of conviction has become res judicata.

The judgment of conviction determines the place where the convicted person is presented to the public. The presentation of the convicted person to the public is done at the initiative of the Prosecutor in collaboration with the Communal Administrator of the convicted person's place of residence.

###### Paragraphe 3 ‐ Restitutions and damages

###### Art.95.‐ Any criminal conviction is pronounced without prejudice to restitutions and damages that may be owed to the parties, at their request or at the request of the Public Prosecutor.

###### Art.96.‐ The court may fix the amount of damages and order ex officio restitutions and damages that are owed by virtue of law or local customs.

###### Art.97.‐ The execution of convictions for restitutions, damages and costs may be pursued by means of imprisonment for debt.

###### Art.98.‐ Imprisonment for debt is treated for its execution as penal servitude; its duration does not release from payment.

###### Art.99.‐ The duration of imprisonment for debt is proportional to the amount of sums owed at the rate of six months per bracket or part of bracket of one hundred thousand francs.

###### Art.100.‐ The convicted person who proves his insolvency is released after this finding.

###### Art.101.‐ Imprisonment for debt is neither exercised nor maintained against convicted persons who have reached their sixtieth year or who are suffering from an incurable disease at a very advanced stage established by an ad hoc Medical Commission.

###### Art.102.‐ When the property of the convicted person is insufficient to cover the

sentences to fine, restitution and damages, the latter two sentences have preference.

###### Art.103.‐ In case of competition between the fine and court costs owed to the State, the

payments made by convicted persons are applied first to these costs.

###### Paragraphe 4 ‐ Protection and safeguard measures applicable to minor children aged

15 to 18 years

###### Art.104.‐ At the same time as pronouncing a principal sentence other than penal

servitude, the judge may order the placement of the child in conflict with the law in a foster family or in a specialized institution that he determines.

###### Art.105.‐ In the case where the judge could pronounce a sentence of penal servitude less

than or equal to two years, it may be derogated from and substituted with community service work whose duration does not exceed two hundred forty hours.

The judgment specifies the sentence of penal servitude and/or fine that the convicted person who evades the execution of the community service work undergoes.

The sentence of community service work and the sentence of fine or penal servitude cannot be pronounced cumulatively.

###### Art.106.‐ At the same time as sentencing to community service work, the judge may

prescribe the socio-judicial monitoring of the minor in conflict with the law. The judgment specifies the authority responsible for ensuring this monitoring.

##### Section 2 ‐ Sentences applicable to legal persons

###### Art.107.‐ The sentences incurred by legal persons are the fine as well as one or more

complementary sentences listed in Article 108.

###### Art.108.‐ For legal persons having the status of commercial company, the judge applies

the following sentences:

1° When the law provides for the offense a sentence of life imprisonment, the judge applies a fine equal to half of the turnover of the previous fiscal year. 2° When the law provides for the offense a sentence of temporary imprisonment, the judge sentences to a minimum fine equal to one-twentieth of the turnover of the previous fiscal year while the maximum amounts to a fine equal to one quarter of the turnover of the previous fiscal year.

###### Art.109.

‐ Without prejudice to the relevant provisions on complementary sentences, for non-profit legal persons, the judge applies the following sentences: 1° When the law provides for an offense a sentence of life imprisonment, the judge applies a fine equal to one million Burundian francs at minimum and a fine of fifty million Burundian francs at maximum;

2° When the law provides for a felony a sentence of temporary imprisonment, the judge applies a fine of five hundred thousand Burundian francs at minimum and a fine of twenty million Burundian francs at maximum;

3° When the offense constitutes a misdemeanor, the judge applies a fine of one hundred thousand to five million Burundian francs;

4° When the offense constitutes a petty offense, the judge applies a fine that cannot exceed one hundred thousand Burundian francs.

###### Art.110.‐ When the law provides for it, the Courts and Tribunals may pronounce one or

more of the following sentences:

1° Dissolution;

2° The prohibition, permanently or for a period of five years at most, from exercising directly or indirectly one or more professional or social activities; 3° The permanent closure or for a period of five years at most of the establishments or of one or more establishments of the enterprise having committed the offense within the meaning of Article 21;

4° Exclusion from public procurement either permanently or for a period not exceeding five years

5° The prohibition, for a period of five years at most, from making public appeals for savings; 6° The prohibition, for a period of one year at most, from issuing checks or using payment cards, 7° The confiscation of the thing that was used or was intended to commit the offense or of the thing that is the product thereof;

8° The publication of the conviction.

###### Art.111.‐ The sentences provided for in the preceding article are not applicable to the

State, to local authorities as well as to any legal person of public law.

#### Chapitre 2 ‐ Sentences applicable in case of concurrent offenses

###### Art.112.‐ There is a concurrence of offenses when several offenses have been committed

by the same perpetrator without a final conviction having occurred for at least one of them.

###### Art.113.‐ There is ideal concurrence:

1° When the fact unique from a material point of view is susceptible to several qualifications;

2° When the action includes facts that, constituting distinct offenses, are united among themselves as proceeding from a single criminal intent or as being aggravating circumstances of each other.

In either case, only the strongest sentence is pronounced.

###### Art.114.‐ There is real concurrence when the facts, distinct from a material point of view,

have succeeded each other and have constituted distinct offenses. In this case sentences are pronounced for each offense and the pronounced sentences are cumulated subject to the following provisions:

1° Life penal servitude absorbs by right any sentence of deprivation of liberty; 2° The total of cumulated sentences of temporary penal servitude and fines cannot exceed double the maximum of the strongest sentences provided for by one or the other offense retained against the convicted person;

3° The total of sentences of prohibition of residence and house arrest cannot exceed twenty years;

4° The total of sentences of placement at the disposal of the Government absorbs by right the sentences of prohibition of residence and house arrest;

5° The total of sentences of temporary prohibition of civic, civil and family rights cannot exceed twenty years.

###### Art.115.

‐ The strongest sentence is the one whose maximum is the highest. If two sentences have the same maximum, the strongest sentence is the one whose minimum is the highest. If two sentences have the same maximum and the same minimum, the strongest sentence is the one accompanied by a fine sentence.

###### Art.116.‐ A fine sentence is always less strong than a penal servitude sentence.

#### Chapitre 3 ‐ Recidivism

###### Art.117.‐ Whoever having been, by final decision, convicted for an offense to a sentence

greater than or equal to one year of penal servitude, has committed, within a period of five years after the expiration of this sentence or its prescription, an offense that must be punished by penal servitude of more than two months, is sentenced to double the sentence provided by law.

###### Art.118.‐ If the first conviction was life penal servitude and the second offense is

punishable by the same sentence, the convicted person may only claim conditional release after a safety period of thirty years.

###### Art.119.‐ There is no recidivism when the sentence pronounced for the first offense has

been erased by amnesty or if the convicted person has been irrevocably rehabilitated.

###### Art.120.‐ One who has been convicted by a military tribunal is, in case of subsequent

offense, subject to the sentences for recidivism only if the first conviction was pronounced for an offense punishable under common law.

#### Chapter 4 ‐ Conditional sentencing

###### Art.121.‐ Courts and Tribunals, when sentencing to one or more main or additional penal servitude sentences, may order by reasoned decision that the execution of the judgment or decision is suspended with respect to that sentence or those sentences, for a period the duration of which they determine from the date of the pronouncement of the judgment or decision but which cannot exceed five years.

###### Art.122.‐ The granting of suspension is subject to the following conditions:

1° That no main penal servitude sentence exceeding two years or a main fine of one hundred thousand francs be pronounced against the convicted person; 2° That the convicted person has not previously incurred during the last five years any conviction to penal servitude or for an offense committed in Burundi, punishable, independently of the fine, by penal servitude of more than six months. However, this restriction does not apply to minors.

3° That the convicted person has fully restituted the sums or any other property obtained through the offense.

###### Art.123.‐ In case of conviction to a penal servitude sentence with suspension, the

trial court may specifically impose on the convicted person the observance of one or more of the following obligations:

1° To respond to summons from the Public Prosecutor;

2° To follow education or vocational training;

3° To submit to measures of medical examination, treatment or care even under hospitalization;

4° To refrain from driving certain vehicles determined by the license categories provided for by the Road Code;

5° Not to engage in the professional activity in the exercise of or on the occasion of which the offense was committed;

6° To refrain from appearing in any specifically designated place;

7° Not to frequent drinking establishments;

8° To refrain from entering into relations with certain persons, notably the victim of the offense;

9° Not to possess or carry a weapon;

10° To submit to one or more obligations provided for by the Code of Criminal Procedure regarding provisional release.

###### Art.124.‐ Non-observance of any of the obligations stated in the preceding article

entails automatic revocation of the suspension.

###### Art.125.‐ The judgment or decision imposing conviction is not executed, with respect

to the penal servitude sentence or sentences, if, during the fixed period, the convicted person does not incur new convictions for offenses punishable, independently of the fine, by penal servitude of more than six months.

In the contrary case, the sentences for which suspension was granted and those which were the subject of the new conviction are cumulated.

###### Art.126.‐ In case of suspension applicable to subsidiary penal servitude, the execution

extends to the fine.

###### Art.127.

‐ In case of conviction for rape, torture, crime of genocide, crime against humanity, war crime, or in case of conviction for attempt or complicity in war crime, crime against humanity or crime of genocide, courts and tribunals cannot grant suspension.

###### Art.128.‐ The conviction for crime or offense accompanied by suspension is deemed null

and void if the convicted person who benefits from it has not committed, within five years from that date, a crime or ordinary offense followed by a new conviction without suspension that entails revocation.

### Title 3 ‐ Certain modalities of execution of the penal servitude sentence

#### Chapter 1 ‐ Conditional release

###### Art.129.‐ Convicted persons who have to serve one or more sentences involving deprivation

of liberty may be released conditionally when they have completed one quarter of these sentences, provided that the duration of incarceration already served exceeds three months.

Persons sentenced to life imprisonment may be released conditionally when the duration of incarceration already served exceeds ten years.

The duration of incarceration already prescribed in the two preceding paragraphs may be reduced when the convicted person has already reached the age of seventy years or if, in the opinion of a College of three medical experts appointed by the Minister of Justice, prolonged incarceration may endanger the life of the convicted person.

###### Art.130.‐ Conditional release can only occur in favor of convicted persons

after having repaired the damages caused by the offense.

###### Art.131.‐ Release may always be revoked for misconduct or

violations of the conditions stated in the release order.

###### Art.132.‐ Definitive release is acquired by the convicted person if revocation has not

occurred before the expiration of a period equal to twice the term of incarceration that the person still had to serve on the date on which release was ordered in their favor.

###### Art.133.‐ Conditional release is ordered by the Minister responsible for Justice

after consultation with the Public Prosecutor and the Prison Director. It is revoked by the same Minister at the initiative of the Public Prosecutor.

The provisional re-arrest of the conditionally released person may be ordered by the Prosecutor General of the Republic or one of their Deputy Prosecutors General with the obligation to immediately notify the Minister responsible for Justice.

###### Art.134.‐ Reintegration takes place, by virtue of the revocation order, for

the completion of the term of incarceration that the execution of the sentence still entailed on the date of release.

###### Art.135.‐ The prescription of sentences does not run while the convicted person is in

conditional release by virtue of a release order that has not been revoked.

###### Art.136.‐ The Minister responsible for Justice determines the form of

release permits, the conditions to which release may be subject and the mode of supervision of conditionally released persons.

#### Chapter 2 ‐ Incompressible sentences

###### Art.137.‐ A sentence is said to be incompressible when the convicted person is required to execute the

entirety of their sentence without being able to benefit from any measure of reduction.

###### Art.138.

‐ In case of conviction for crimes of genocide, crime against humanity, war crime, voluntary homicide, sexual assault, torture and armed robbery, the convicted person executes the entirety of the sentence without being able to benefit from the application of provisions on conditional sentencing and conditional release.

### Title 4 ‐ Extinction of public prosecution, sentences and expungement of

convictions

#### Chapter 1 ‐ General provisions

###### Art.139.‐ Public prosecution is extinguished by the death of the accused, the dissolution of the legal

person, the repeal of the criminal law, res judicata, amnesty or prescription. The dissolution of the legal person does not prejudice criminal proceedings directed against the directors of the dissolved company.

###### Art.140.‐ Public prosecution may also be extinguished by settlement and by withdrawal of

the complaint when the law expressly so provides.

###### Art.141.

‐ The sentence is extinguished by its execution, by the death of the convicted person, pardon, amnesty, prescription or the dissolution of the legal person.

However, recovery of the fine and court costs may be proceeded with as well as the execution of confiscation after the dissolution of the legal person until the closure of liquidation operations.

###### Art.142.‐ The sentence may also be modified or expunged by pardon, conditional

release or rehabilitation.

#### Chapter 2 ‐ Withdrawal of Complaint

###### Art.143.‐ For offenses that can only be prosecuted upon complaint by the injured

party, withdrawal by the latter extinguishes the public prosecution.

Withdrawal is only admissible if it extends to all those who participated in the perpetration of the offense.

###### Art.144.

‐ Withdrawal may be judicial or extrajudicial. It is tacit when the complainant has performed acts incompatible with the intention to persist in their complaint.

###### Art.145.‐ Express or tacit withdrawal cannot be revoked.

###### Art.146.‐ To produce its effects, withdrawal must, except in cases where the law

provides otherwise, occur before the conviction becomes final.

###### Art.147.‐ If the complaint has been filed by several victims in connection with the same

offense, the public prosecution is only extinguished if all complainants have withdrawn.

#### Chapter 3 ‐ Limitation Period for Public Prosecution

###### Art.148.‐ The public prosecution resulting from an offense is time-barred:

1° After one year has elapsed if the offense committed constitutes a petty offense;

2° After three years have elapsed if the offense committed constitutes a misdemeanor;

3° After ten years if the offense committed constitutes a felony punishable by five to ten years of penal servitude;

4° After twenty years if the offense committed constitutes a felony punishable by more than ten years of penal servitude;

5° After thirty years if the offense committed constitutes a felony punishable by penal servitude

###### Art.149.‐ The limitation period begins to run on the day when all constituent elements of

the offense are completed in case of instantaneous offenses; it runs from the day the criminal state ceased in matters of continuous or continuing offenses.

###### Art.150.‐ The limitation period is interrupted by acts of investigation or prosecution

carried out within the periods of one year, three years or ten years, twenty years or thirty years from the day the offense was committed.

###### Art.151.‐ The limitation period for public prosecution of crimes committed against

minors only begins to run from the civil majority of the latter.

###### Art.152.‐ Public prosecution for crimes of genocide, crimes against humanity and

war crimes is not subject to any limitation period.

###### Art.153.

‐ The civil action arising from an offense is time-barred according to the rules of civil law. However, if the limitation period for the civil action was acquired while that of the public prosecution is not yet completed, the civil action is only time-barred according to the rules relating to the public prosecution.

#### Chapter 4 ‐ Limitation Period for Penalties

###### Art.154.‐ Fine penalties of less than five thousand Burundian francs are time-barred

after two years have elapsed, penalties of five thousand to one hundred thousand Burundian francs are time-barred after four years have elapsed, penalties of more than one hundred thousand to one million Burundian francs after ten years have elapsed and penalties of more than one million Burundian francs after thirty years.

###### Art.155.‐ Penal servitude penalties are time-barred after two years or five years have elapsed

depending on whether they concern petty offense or misdemeanor matters.

###### Art.156.‐ Penal servitude penalties in criminal matters are time-barred by a

period equal to double the sentence imposed without this period exceeding twenty years.

Life sentences are time-barred after thirty years.

###### Art.157.‐ Penalties imposed for crimes of genocide, crimes against

humanity and war crimes are not subject to any limitation period.

###### Art.158.‐ The periods in the provisions of this chapter run from the date of the day when the

judgment or ruling rendered is final and has acquired the force of res judicata.

###### Art.159.‐ The supplementary penalty is time-barred within the same period as the penalty of which it

is accessory.

###### Art.160.‐ The escaped convict's sentence is time-barred after a period equal to triple the sentence

not yet served. This period begins to run from the day of escape.

###### Art.161.

‐ The limitation period for the penalty is interrupted by the arrest of the convict; their detention results in the suspension of the limitation period with regard to supplementary penalties.

###### Art.162.‐ Civil judgments rendered by criminal courts are

time-barred according to the rules of the Civil Code.

#### Chapter 5 ‐ Pardon

###### Art.163.‐ Pardon consists in the total or partial remission by the executive power of

the execution of sentences imposed or in their commutation into other less serious penalties determined by law.

###### Art.164.

‐ Pardon may apply to all principal or supplementary penalties. It does not apply to civil imprisonment exercised for the recovery of fines nor to legal costs nor to civil damages.

###### Art.165.‐ Only enforceable penalties resulting from a final conviction may be the subject

of a pardon measure.

###### Art.166.‐ A suspended sentence cannot be the subject of a pardon as long as the suspension

is not revoked.

###### Art.167.‐ Pardon may be either unconditional or subject to the execution of a

sentence stated by the pardon decision. If this condition is not fulfilled, the revocation of the pardon occurs by operation of law and the sentence is brought back to execution. In this case, the limitation period for the penalty is suspended between the notification and the revocation of the pardon.

###### Art.168.‐ Pardon does not extinguish supplementary penalties not covered by the pardon decision

nor the effects of the conviction, in particular those relating to recidivism, to the application of suspension in case of subsequent prosecution and to civil judgments such as restitutions and damages.

###### Art.169.‐ Applications for pardon are investigated by the Public Prosecutor at the

court that pronounced the conviction.

They may also be investigated by the Public Prosecutor's Office in whose jurisdiction the applicant resides or of the place of their detention.

###### Art.170.‐ When it is general, pardon is proposed at the initiative of the Minister having

Justice in their attributions; the application is presented by the convict or any interested party acting on their behalf when it is individual.

###### Art.171.‐ After investigation, pardon files are sent to the Minister having

Justice in their attributions who presents a report to the Head of State for discretionary decision.

###### Art.172.‐ Pardon does not extinguish penalties imposed for genocide, crimes

against humanity and war crimes.

#### Chapter 6 ‐ Amnesty

###### Art.173.‐ Amnesty is the act by which the legislative power prohibits the exercise or

continuation of criminal prosecutions and erases convictions pronounced.

Genocide, crime against humanity and war crime cannot be the subject of any amnesty law.

###### Art.174.

‐ Amnesty is in principle general; however, it may be limited to certain categories of offenses.

###### Art.175.‐ Amnesty is of public order: it is acquired by operation of law and without the knowledge of and

despite those who benefit from it.

###### Art.176.‐ Amnesty sometimes erases certain specified offenses independently

of the sentence imposed, sometimes it is based solely on the amount of sentences imposed.

###### Art.177.‐ The power to interpret amnesty laws belongs to the judicial power and

more precisely to the court that pronounced the conviction.

The execution of amnesty laws is entrusted to a Commission established by the

Minister having Justice in their attributions.

###### Art.178.

‐ Amnesty extinguishes the public prosecution; erases or reduces any conviction of a criminal nature but leaves in place provisions that are not punitive in nature; it cannot be set against the rights of the State and third parties. Fines already collected and costs paid remain acquired by the treasury.

#### Chapter 7 ‐ On Pardoning Amnesty

###### Art.179.‐ Pardoning amnesty is the combination of pardon and amnesty to which

the legislator resorts to introduce more justice in the application of amnesty.

###### Art.180.‐ The legislator sets in an amnesty law the criminal acts to which the

measure of leniency must extend, but leaves to the Head of State the responsibility of determining subsequently, by way of individual pardon, which are, among the perpetrators of the acts provided for, the sole beneficiaries of amnesty.

###### Art.181.

‐ Pardoning amnesty erases or reduces criminal convictions; it leaves in place the other effects of the public prosecution or of the conviction.

#### Chapter 8 ‐ On the Erasure of Sentences and the Rehabilitation of Convicted Persons

##### Section 1 ‐ On the Erasure of Sentences

###### Art.182.‐ Criminal sanctions pronounced as a final resort are recorded on the

criminal record of the convicted person.

The erasure of these convictions is granted automatically to the natural person convicted who has not, within the time periods determined hereafter, received any new conviction for a criminal or misdemeanor sentence:

1° For conviction to a fine, after a period of three years from the day of payment of the fine, the expiration of the subsidiary penal servitude or the completed prescription;

2° For conviction to a misdemeanor sentence not exceeding one year, the erasure is granted after a period of five years from the execution of the sentence or its prescription.

###### Art.183.‐ In case of conviction to one or more additional sentences,

the erasure can only occur after having executed the latter.

##### Section 2 ‐ On Rehabilitation

###### Art.184.‐ Rehabilitation is an act of the judicial power which restores to the convicted person the

lost rights and causes the effects resulting from the conviction to cease for the future without prejudice to the rights of third parties.

###### Art.185.‐ Any person convicted for an offense committed in Burundi may

be rehabilitated.

###### Art.186.

‐ Rehabilitation can only be requested in court, during the lifetime of the convicted person, by the latter, or if he is prohibited, by his legal representative; in case of death and if the legal conditions are met, the request may be made by his spouse or by his ascendants or descendants, but within the period of five years from the date of death.

###### Art.187.‐ The request must cover all the convictions pronounced that

have not been erased by a prior rehabilitation.

###### Art.188.‐ Rehabilitation is subject to the following conditions:

1° The pecuniary or custodial sentence must have been served or remitted by virtue of the right of pardon or be considered as null and void as a result of the conditional conviction;

2° The request for rehabilitation can only be made after a period of five years for those convicted to a misdemeanor sentence and ten years for those convicted to a criminal sentence

This period runs, for those convicted to a fine from the day the conviction became final and, for those convicted to a custodial sentence, from the day of their final release or from the day of conditional release if it has not been followed by revocation. This period is fifteen years for the recidivist and the one who has prescribed his sentence.

3° During this period, the convicted person must have been of good conduct and have had a certain residence;

4° He must not have already enjoyed the benefit of rehabilitation;

5° He must justify, except in the case of prescription, the payment of legal costs, the fine and damages or the remission granted to him.

In the absence of this justification, he must establish that he has served the time of bodily constraint determined by law, or that the treasury or the victims of the offense have waived this means of execution.

If he is convicted for bankruptcy, he must justify the payment of the liabilities of the bankruptcy in capital, interest and costs or the remission granted to him.

However, if the convicted person justifies that he is absolutely unable to discharge the pecuniary convictions imposed on him, he may be rehabilitated, even if these convictions have not been paid or have only been paid partially.

###### Art.189.‐ If the injured party cannot be found or if it refuses to receive the sum

due, it is deposited in a public fund.

If the party does not present itself within a period of five years to be awarded the deposited sum, this sum is paid to the treasury at the diligence of the judge who pronounced the conviction.

###### Art.190.‐ The convicted person addresses the request for rehabilitation to the Public Prosecution Officer

of his residence. This request specifies the date of the conviction and the places where the convicted person has resided since his release.

The Public Prosecution Officer conducts a morality investigation on the convicted person. He has delivered to him a copy of the conviction judgments, an extract from the register of places of detention where the sentence was served and noting what the conduct of the convicted person has been as well as a criminal record bulletin. He transmits the documents with his opinion to the General Prosecutor at the Court of Appeal.

###### Art.191.‐ The Court is seized by the General Prosecutor and rules within two months

on the requisitions of the latter, the party or his counsel heard or duly summoned.

###### Art.192.‐ In case of rejection of the request, a new request cannot be introduced

before the expiration of a period of two years, unless the rejection of the first was motivated by the insufficiency of the probationary period; in this case, the request may be renewed upon the expiration of this period.

###### Art.193.‐ Mention of the judgment pronouncing the rehabilitation is made in the margin of the conviction judgment

and on the criminal record.

###### Art.194.‐ Rehabilitation erases the mention of the conviction from the criminal record of the

rehabilitated person.

###### Art.195.‐ Rehabilitation is revoked by operation of law if the rehabilitated convicted person

commits, within five years, an offense punishable by a penal servitude equal to or greater than five years, and followed by a conviction to imprisonment; to this effect, the Public Prosecution at the jurisdiction which pronounced the conviction to imprisonment must inform the General Prosecutor, who himself seizes the Court of Appeal for the purpose of having the revocation of rehabilitation noted, the party or his counsel being duly summoned.

In case of revocation, the rehabilitation is considered as never having been granted.

###### Art.196.‐ The costs of the rehabilitation procedure are borne by the applicant.

## Book 2 ‐ On Offenses and Their Punishment in Particular

### Title 1 ‐ On Offenses Against Persons

#### Chapter 1 ‐ On Genocide, Crimes Against Humanity and War Crimes

###### Art.197.‐ The crime of genocide means any of the following acts committed

with intent to destroy, in whole or in part, a national, ethnic, racial or religious group, as such:

1° Killing members of the group;

2° Causing serious bodily or mental harm to members of the group;

3° Deliberately inflicting on the group conditions of life calculated to bring about its physical destruction in whole or in part;

4° Imposing measures intended to prevent births within the group;

5° Forcibly transferring children of the group to another group.

###### Art.198.‐ A crime against Humanity means any of the following acts

when committed as part of a widespread or systematic attack directed against any civilian population and with knowledge of the attack:

1° Murder;

2° Extermination;

3° Enslavement;

4° Deportation or forcible transfer of population;

5° Imprisonment or other severe deprivation of physical liberty in violation of fundamental rules of international law;

6° Torture;

7° Rape, sexual slavery, enforced prostitution, forced pregnancy, enforced sterilization, or any other form of sexual violence of comparable gravity;

8° Persecution of any identifiable group or collectivity on political, racial, national, ethnic, cultural, religious or gender grounds as defined in article 199, 10°, or other grounds that are universally recognized as impermissible under international law within the jurisdiction of the Court;

9° Enforced disappearance of persons;

10° Crimes of apartheid;

11° Other inhumane acts of a similar character intentionally causing great suffering, or serious injury to body or to mental or physical health.

###### Art.199.‐ For the purposes of the preceding article:

1° "Attack directed against any civilian population" means a course of conduct involving the multiple commission of acts referred to in the preceding article against any civilian population, pursuant to or in furtherance of a State or organizational policy to commit such attack;

2° "Extermination" includes in particular the intentional infliction of conditions of life, inter alia the deprivation of access to food and medicine, calculated to bring about the destruction of part of a population;

3°. "Enslavement" means the exercise of any or all of the powers attaching to the right of ownership over a person and includes the exercise of such power in the course of trafficking in persons, in particular women and children; 4° "Deportation or forcible transfer of population" means the forced displacement of the persons concerned by expulsion or other coercive acts from the area in which they are lawfully present, without grounds permitted under international law;

5° "Torture" means the intentional infliction of severe pain or suffering, whether physical or mental, upon a person in the custody or under the control of the accused; except that torture shall not include pain or suffering arising only from, inherent in or incidental to, lawful sanctions;

6° "Forced pregnancy" means the unlawful confinement of a woman forcibly made pregnant, with the intent of affecting the ethnic composition of any population or carrying out other grave violations of international law;

7°. "Persecution" means the intentional and severe deprivation of fundamental rights contrary to international law by reason of the identity of the group or collectivity;

8° "Crime of apartheid" means inhumane acts of a character similar to those referred to in the preceding article, committed in the context of an institutionalized regime of systematic oppression and domination by one racial group over any other racial group or groups and committed with the intention of maintaining that regime;

9° "Enforced disappearance of persons" means the arrest, detention or abduction of persons by, or with the authorization, support or acquiescence of, a State or a political organization, followed by a refusal to acknowledge that deprivation of freedom or to give information on the fate or whereabouts of those persons, with the intention of removing them from the protection of the law for a prolonged period of time;

10° The term "gender" refers to the two sexes, male and female, within the context of society. It does not indicate any meaning different from the above.

###### Art.200.‐ "War crimes" means crimes that fall within the framework

of a plan or policy or when they are part of a series of similar crimes committed on a large scale, in particular:

- 1) Any of the grave breaches of the Geneva Conventions of 12 August 1949 as follows:

 1° wilful killing;

 2° torture or inhuman treatment, including biological experiments;

 3° wilfully causing great suffering, or serious injury to body or health;

 4° extensive destruction and appropriation of property, not justified by military necessity and carried out unlawfully and wantonly on a large scale;

 5° compelling a prisoner of war or other protected person to serve in the forces of a hostile Power;

 6° wilfully depriving a prisoner of war or other protected person of the rights of fair and regular trial;

 7° unlawful deportation or transfer or unlawful confinement;

 8° taking of hostages.

2) Other serious violations of the laws and customs applicable to international armed conflicts within the established framework of International Law, namely, any of the following acts:

 1° intentionally directing attacks against the civilian population as such or against civilians not taking direct part in hostilities;

 2° intentionally directing attacks against civilian objects, that is to say, objects which are not military objectives;

 3° intentionally directing attacks against personnel, installations, material, units or vehicles involved in a humanitarian assistance or peacekeeping mission in accordance with the Charter of the United Nations, as long as they are entitled to the protection given to civilians and civilian objects under International Law of armed conflicts;

 4° intentionally launching an attack in the knowledge that such attack will cause incidental loss of life or injury to civilians, damage to civilian objects or widespread, long-term and severe damage to the natural environment which would be clearly excessive in relation to the concrete and direct overall military advantage anticipated;

 5° attacking or bombarding, by whatever means, towns, villages, dwellings or buildings which are undefended and which are not military objectives;

 6° killing or wounding a combatant who, having laid down his arms or having no longer means of defence, has surrendered at discretion;

 7° making improper use of a flag of truce, of the flag or of the military insignia and uniform of the enemy or of the United Nations, as well as of the distinctive emblems of the Geneva Conventions, resulting in death or serious personal injury;

 8° the transfer, directly or indirectly, by the Occupying Power of parts of its own civilian population into the territory it occupies, or the deportation or transfer of all or parts of the population of the occupied territory within or outside this territory;

 9° intentionally directing attacks against buildings dedicated to religion, education, art, science or charitable purposes, historic monuments, hospitals and places where the sick and wounded are collected, provided they are not military objectives;

 10° subjecting persons who are in the power of an adverse party to physical mutilation or to medical or scientific experiments of any kind which are neither justified by the medical, dental or hospital treatment of the person concerned nor carried out in his interest, and which cause death to or seriously endanger the health of such person or persons;

 11° killing or wounding treacherously individuals belonging to the hostile nation or army;

 12° declaring that no quarter will be given;

 13° destroying or seizing the enemy's property, unless such destruction or seizure be imperatively demanded by the necessities of war;

 14° declaring abolished, suspended or inadmissible in a court of law the rights and actions of nationals of the hostile party;

 15° compelling the nationals of the hostile party to take part in the operations of war directed against their own country, even if they were in the belligerent's service before the commencement of the war;

 16° pillaging a town or place, even when taken by assault;

 17° employing poison or poisoned weapons;

 18° employing asphyxiating, poisonous or other gases, and all analogous liquids, materials or devices;

 19° employing bullets which expand or flatten easily in the human body, such as bullets with a hard envelope which does not entirely cover the core or is pierced with incisions;

 20° employing weapons, projectiles and material and methods of warfare of a nature to cause superfluous injury or unnecessary suffering or to strike indiscriminately in violation of the International Law of armed conflict, provided that such weapons, projectiles and material and methods of warfare are the subject of a comprehensive prohibition;

 21° outrages upon personal dignity, in particular humiliating and degrading treatment;

 22° rape, sexual slavery, enforced prostitution, forced pregnancy, as defined in article 199, 6°, enforced sterilization, or any other form of sexual violence constituting a grave breach of the Geneva Conventions;

 23° utilizing the presence of a civilian or other protected person to render certain points, areas or military forces immune from military operations;

 24° intentionally directing attacks against buildings, material, medical units and transport, and personnel using, in conformity with International Law, the distinctive emblems of the Geneva Conventions;

 25° intentionally using starvation of civilians as a method of warfare by depriving them of objects indispensable to their survival, including wilfully impeding relief supplies as provided for under the Geneva Conventions;

 26° intentionally using starvation of civilians as a method of warfare by depriving them of objects indispensable to their survival, including wilfully impeding relief supplies as provided for under the Geneva Conventions;

 27° conscripting or enlisting children under the age of 15 years into the national armed forces or using them to participate actively in hostilities.

3) In the case of an armed conflict not of an international character, serious violations of article 3 common to the four Geneva Conventions of 12 August 1949, namely, any of the following acts committed against persons taking no active part in the hostilities, including members of armed forces who have laid down their arms and those placed hors de combat by sickness, wounds, detention or any other cause:

 1° violence to life and person, in particular murder of all kinds, mutilation, cruel treatment and torture;

 2° outrages upon personal dignity, in particular humiliating and degrading treatment;

 3° taking of hostages;

 4° the passing of sentences and the carrying out of executions without previous judgment pronounced by a regularly constituted court, affording all judicial guarantees which are generally recognized as indispensable.

4) Point 3 applies to armed conflicts not of an international character and does not apply to situations of internal disturbances and tensions, such as riots, isolated and sporadic acts of violence or other acts of a similar nature;

5) Other serious violations of the laws and customs applicable to armed conflicts not of an international character, within the established framework of international law, namely any of the following acts:

 1° intentionally directing attacks against the civilian population as such or against individual civilians not taking direct part in hostilities;

 2° intentionally directing attacks against buildings, material, medical units and transport, and personnel using, in conformity with International Law, the distinctive emblems of the Geneva Conventions;

 3° intentionally directing attacks against personnel, installations, material, units or vehicles involved in a humanitarian assistance or peacekeeping mission in accordance with the Charter of the United Nations, as long as they are entitled to the protection given to civilians and civilian objects under international law of armed conflicts;

 4° intentionally directing attacks against buildings dedicated to religion, education, art, science or charitable purposes, historic monuments, hospitals and places where the sick and wounded are collected, provided they are not military objectives;

 5° pillaging a town or place, even when taken by assault;

 6° rape, sexual slavery, enforced prostitution, forced pregnancy, as defined in article 197, 6°, enforced sterilization, or any other form of sexual violence also constituting a serious violation of the Geneva Conventions;

 7° conscripting or enlisting children under the age of 15 years into armed forces or groups or using them to participate actively in hostilities;

 8° ordering the displacement of the civilian population for reasons related to the conflict, unless the security of the civilians involved or imperative military reasons so demand;

 9° killing or wounding treacherously a combatant adversary;

 10° declaring that no quarter will be given;

 11° subjecting persons who are in the power of another party to the conflict to physical mutilation or to medical or scientific experiments of any kind which are neither justified by the medical, dental or hospital treatment of the person concerned nor carried out in his or her interest, and which cause death to or seriously endanger the health of such person or persons;

 12° destroying or seizing the property of an adversary unless such destruction or seizure be imperatively demanded by the necessities of the conflict.

6) Point 5 applies to armed conflicts not of an international character and does not therefore apply to situations of internal disturbances and tensions, such as riots, isolated and sporadic acts of violence or other acts of a similar nature. It applies to armed conflicts that take place in the territory of a State when there is protracted armed conflict between the authorities of the Government of that State and organized armed groups or between such groups.

#### Chapitre 2 ‐ Torture and other cruel, inhuman or degrading treatment

###### Art.206.‐ Torture means any act by which severe pain or suffering, whether physical or

mental, is intentionally inflicted on a person for such purposes as obtaining from him or a third person information or a confession, punishing him for an act he or a third person has committed or is suspected of having committed, or intimidating or coercing him or a third person, or for any reason based on discrimination of any kind, when such pain or suffering is inflicted by or at the instigation of or with the consent or acquiescence of a public official or other person acting in an official capacity.

This term does not extend to pain or suffering arising only from, inherent in or incidental to lawful sanctions.

###### Art.207.

‐ Whoever subjects a person to torture or other cruel, inhuman or degrading treatment is punished by penal servitude of ten to fifteen years and a fine of one hundred thousand to one million Burundian francs.

###### Art.208.‐ The offence is punished by penal servitude of twenty years when it is

committed:

1° Against a minor under eighteen years of age;

2° Against a person vulnerable by reason of age, state of health, infirmity, physical or mental deficiency or state of pregnancy;

3° Against a witness, victim or civil party, either to prevent him from reporting the facts, filing a complaint or testifying in court, or by reason of his report, complaint or testimony;

4° By several persons acting as perpetrators or accomplices;

5° With use or threat of a weapon.

###### Art.209.‐ The offender is punished by twenty years of penal servitude when the torture

and other cruel, inhuman or degrading punishment or treatment has resulted in mutilation or permanent infirmity or when it is accompanied by sexual assault.

He is punished by penal servitude for life when it has resulted in the death of the victim.

###### Art.210.‐ No exceptional circumstances whatsoever, whether a state of war or threat of

war, internal political instability or any other state of emergency, may be invoked as a justification of torture and other cruel, inhuman or degrading punishment or treatment.

The order of a superior or a public authority may not be invoked as a justification of torture.

###### Art.211.

‐ The penalties provided for in articles 207, 208, and 209 are incompressible. The judge shall pronounce, in addition to the principal penalties, the prohibition from exercising the function in the course of which the torture was practiced, without prejudice to other complementary penalties provided for by this Code.

##### Section 3 ‐ Misappropriation of Pledge or Seized Object

###### Art.276.‐ The act by a debtor, borrower or third party pledgor of

destroying or misappropriating the object constituted as pledge is punished by two to five years of penal servitude and a fine of fifty thousand to one hundred thousand Burundian francs or only one of these penalties.

###### Art.277.‐ The act by the person whose property is seized of

destroying or misappropriating a seized object placed in his hands as security for the rights of a creditor and entrusted to his custody or that of a third party is punished by the penalties provided for in the preceding article.

##### Section 4 ‐ Extortion

###### Art.278.‐ Any person who has extorted, by means of violence or threats, either funds, valuables, movable objects, obligations, notes, promises, receipts, or the signature or delivery of any document containing or creating an obligation, disposition or discharge, is punished by penal servitude of six months to five years and a fine of

fifty thousand francs to one hundred thousand Burundian francs.

Any public representative, civil servant, magistrate or member of the defense and security forces who carries out a seizure not recorded by an official report in accordance with the relevant provisions of the Code of Criminal Procedure or by a discharge document serving as such is punished by the same penalties.

###### Art.279.‐ The penalty provided for in the preceding article is increased to ten years:

1° When the extortion is preceded, accompanied or followed by violence against others resulting in total incapacity for work for at least eight days; 2° When the extortion is committed against a vulnerable person due to their age, illness, infirmity, physical or mental deficiency or a state of pregnancy that is apparent or known to its perpetrator;

3° When the extortion is committed by a member of the defense and security forces, a public representative, a civil servant or a magistrate.

###### Art.280.‐ Extortion is punished by twenty years of penal servitude and a fine of one hundred

thousand francs to five hundred thousand Burundian francs when it is preceded, accompanied or followed by violence against others resulting in incapacity, mutilation or permanent infirmity.

###### Art.281.‐ Extortion is punished by twenty years of penal servitude and a fine of one hundred

thousand to five hundred thousand Burundian francs when it is committed with use or threat of any weapon.

###### Art.282.‐ Extortion by an organized gang is punished by twenty years of penal servitude and

a fine of one hundred thousand francs to five hundred thousand francs.

The penalty is increased to thirty years of penal servitude when the extortion is preceded, accompanied or followed by violence against others, resulting in mutilation or permanent infirmity.

Extortion by an organized gang is punished by thirty years of penal servitude when it is committed with use or threat of any weapon.

###### Art.283.‐ Extortion is punished by life imprisonment and a fine of one hundred thousand to five

hundred thousand Burundian francs, when it is preceded, accompanied or followed by violence resulting in death.

##### Section 5 ‐ Blackmail

###### Art.284.‐ Blackmail is the act of obtaining, by threatening to reveal or impute facts

likely to harm honor or reputation, either a signature, commitment or waiver, or the disclosure of a secret, or the delivery of funds, valuables or any property.

Blackmail is punished by one to five years of penal servitude and a fine of The penalty may be increased to ten years when the perpetrator has carried out his threat.

##### Section 6 ‐ Meaning of Terms Used in This Title

###### Paragraph 1 ‐ Theft Committed at Night

###### Art.285.‐ Theft committed during the night is theft committed between sunset and

sunrise.

###### Paragraph 2 ‐ Inhabited House

###### Art.286.

‐ Any building, any apartment, any dwelling, any lodge, any cabin, even mobile, or other place used for habitation is deemed an inhabited house.

###### Paragraph 3 ‐ Dependencies of an Inhabited House

###### Art.287.

‐ Courtyards, farmyards, gardens and all other enclosed land, as well as barns, stables, stalls and all other buildings enclosed therein, whatever their use, even if they form a separate enclosure within the main enclosure, are deemed dependencies of an inhabited house.

###### Art.288.‐ Mobile pens intended to contain livestock in the countryside, however

they are made, are deemed dependencies of an inhabited house when they are established on the same piece of land, with mobile cabins or other shelters intended for the guards.

###### Paragraph 4 ‐ Weapons

###### Art.289.

‐ The word "weapon" includes any machine, instrument, utensil or other cutting, piercing or blunt object, which one has seized to kill, injure or strike, even if one has not used it.

###### Paragraph 5 ‐ Violence and Threats

###### Art.290.‐ By "violence", the law means acts of physical constraint exercised on

persons.

By "threat", the law means all means of moral constraint through fear of imminent harm.

###### Paragraph 6 ‐ Breaking and Entering

###### Art.291.

‐ Breaking and entering consists of forcing, breaking, degrading, demolishing or removing any closing device of a house, building, any construction or its dependencies, of a boat, a railway car, a vehicle, forcing locked cupboards or furniture, intended to remain in place and to protect the effects they contain.

###### Art.292.‐ Are assimilated to theft with breaking and entering the removal of movable property referred to in

the preceding article, theft committed by means of breaking seals.

###### Paragraph 7 ‐ On scaling

###### Art.293.

‐ Is qualified as "scaling" any entry into houses, buildings, courtyards, farmyards, any edifices, gardens, parks, enclosures, executed over walls, doors, roofs or any other type of enclosure; entry through an opening other than that which has been established to serve as an entrance.

###### Paragraph 8 ‐ On false keys

###### Art.294.‐ Are qualified as "false keys":

1. All hooks, lockpicks, skeleton keys, imitated, counterfeited or altered keys;

2. Keys which have not been intended by the owner, tenant, innkeeper or landlord for the locks, padlocks or any closures for the opening of which the perpetrator has used them;

3. Lost or stolen keys which have been used to commit the theft.

However, the use of false keys constitutes an aggravating circumstance only if it took place to open objects whose breaking and entering has resulted in an aggravation of the sentence.

#### Chapter 2 ‐ On frauds

##### Section 1 ‐ On bankruptcy

###### Art.296.‐ Is punished by penal servitude from one year to five years and a fine of one hundred

thousand to five hundred thousand Burundian francs, the merchant declared bankrupt who, fraudulently:

1° Has diverted or concealed part of his assets or has acknowledged himself debtor of sums which he did not owe;

2° Has removed his books or has removed from them; erased or altered the content.

###### Art.297.‐ Is punished by penal servitude from three months to one year and a fine of

fifty thousand to two hundred thousand Burundian francs, the merchant declared bankrupt who:

1° After cessation of payments, has favored a creditor to the detriment of the estate; 2° Has, for his personal needs or those of his household, incurred excessive expenses; 3° Has consumed large sums in gambling, in operations of pure chance or fictitious operations;

4° Has, with the intention of delaying his bankruptcy, made purchases to resell above market price or, in the same situation, has engaged in borrowing, circulation of bills and other ruinous means of procuring funds.

###### Art.298.‐ Are punished by the penalties provided for in the preceding article, persons who have

supposed expenses or losses or have not been able to justify the existence or use of all or part of the assets as they appear in the accounting documents and books at the date of cessation of payments and of all property, of whatever nature, obtained subsequently.

###### Art.299.‐ Is punished by penal servitude from three months to one year and a fine of

fifty thousand to two hundred thousand Burundian francs, the merchant declared bankrupt: 1° Who has not kept the commercial books or made the inventories prescribed by legal and regulatory provisions;

2° Whose books or inventories are incomplete, irregular or written in a language other than that whose use, in this matter, is prescribed by law;

3°. Whose books or inventories do not show his true active and passive situation, without there being fraud nevertheless;

4° Who has contracted, without receiving value in exchange, commitments deemed too considerable, having regard to his situation when he contracted them;

5° Who without being unfortunate and in good faith, has already been previously bankrupt; 6° Who, following a previous bankruptcy, has not fulfilled all the obligations of a concordat in progress or against whom the resolution of the concordat has. been pronounced;

7° Who has not made the admission of cessation of payments under the conditions and within the time limits provided for by the legislation on bankruptcy;

8° Who, without legitimate cause, has absented himself without the authorization of the judge or has not appeared in person at the summonses made to him by the judge or the trustee.

##### Section 2 ‐ On cases assimilated to bankruptcy

###### Art.300.‐ Are punished by penal servitude from one year to five years and a fine of one hundred

thousand to five hundred thousand Burundian francs, the presidents, administrators, directors, managers or liquidators of a commercial company declared bankrupt and, generally speaking, any person having, in law or in fact, directly or through an intermediary, administered, managed or liquidated such a company under cover of or in place of its legal representatives who, fraudulently:

1° Have diverted or concealed part of the assets or acknowledged the company as debtor of sums which they did not owe;

2° Have removed the books of the company or have removed, erased or altered the content thereof; 3° Have omitted to publish the articles of association or the amending acts thereof in the forms and time limits provided for by law;

4° Have, in these acts, made statements contrary to the truth;

5° Have caused the bankruptcy of the company.

###### Art.301.‐ Are punished by penal servitude from one year to five years and a fine of one hundred

thousand to five hundred thousand Burundian francs, the presidents, administrators, directors, managers or liquidators of a commercial company declared bankrupt and, generally speaking, any person having, in law or in fact, directly or through an intermediary, administered, managed or liquidated such a company under cover of or in place of its legal representatives who, fraudulently:

1° After cessation of payments of the company, have favored a creditor to the detriment of the estate;

2° Have committed the company to excessive expenses or costs;

3° Have, on behalf of the company, consumed large sums in gambling, or who have carried out for it operations of pure chance or fictitious operations;

4° Have, with the intention of delaying the bankruptcy of the company, made purchases to resell below market price, or, with the same intention, have engaged in borrowing, circulation of bills and other ruinous means of procuring funds;

5° Have supposed expenses or losses, or do not justify the existence or use of the assets of the last inventory of the company and of the monies, securities, movable property and effects, of whatever nature they may be, which would have subsequently come to the company; 6° Have carried out the distribution among the members of the company of dividends not taken from actual profits.

###### Art.302.‐ Are punished by the penalties provided for in the preceding article, the presidents,

administrators, directors, managers or liquidators of a commercial company declared bankrupt and, generally speaking, any person having, in law or in fact, directly or through an intermediary, administered, managed or liquidated such a company under cover of or in place of its legal representatives when, through their fault: 1° The books provided for by legislation on commercial matters have not been kept, the inventories provided for by the same legislation have not been made; that they have been written in a language other than that whose use, in this matter, is prescribed by law; that they are incomplete or irregular, that the same books and inventories do not show the true active and passive situation of the company, without there having been fraud nevertheless; 2° The admission of cessation of payment of the company has not been made under the conditions and time limits provided for by the legislation on bankruptcies.

###### Art.303.

‐ Are punished by the penalties provided for in Article 301 the presidents, directors, managers, representatives of creditors or liquidators of a commercial company declared bankrupt, and in general, any person having, in law or in fact, directly or through an intermediary, administered, managed or liquidated such a company under cover or in place of its legal representatives:

1° Have not provided the information requested from them, either by the judge or by the curator, or who have given inaccurate information;

2° Have willfully harmed the interests of creditors or the debtor, either by using for their profit sums received in the performance of their mission, or by attributing to themselves advantages they knew were not due;

3° Have made, in their interest, use of the powers at their disposal that they knew to be contrary to the interests of creditors or the debtor;

4° Have acquired the debtor's property or used it for their profit; 5° Have not appeared in person at the summons of the judge or curator without legitimate impediment.

###### Art.304.‐ Is punished by penal servitude of two months to one year and a fine of

twenty thousand to fifty thousand francs any person who, having participated in any capacity in the proceedings, acquires, directly or indirectly, property of the debtor or uses it for their profit.

###### Art.305.‐ Are punished by penal servitude of one to five years and a fine of one hundred

thousand francs to five hundred thousand francs:

1° Those who, in the interest of the bankrupt, have removed, concealed or hidden all or part of their property

2° Those who, fraudulently, have presented in the bankruptcy false or exaggerated claims;

3° The curator who has been guilty of embezzlement in their management.

###### Art.306.‐ Are punished by the penalties provided for in the preceding article, those who have stipulated, either

with the bankrupt or with any other persons, particular advantages by reason of their vote in the declaration of bankruptcy, or who have made a particular agreement from which would result, in their favor, an advantage at the expense of the estate.

##### Section 3 ‐ Fraudulent organization of insolvency

###### Art.307.

‐ The act, by a debtor, even before the judicial decision establishing their debt, of organizing or aggravating their insolvency, either by increasing the liabilities or decreasing the assets of their estate, or by decreasing or concealing all or part of their income, or by concealing certain of their property, with a view to evading the execution of a judgment of a patrimonial nature pronounced by a criminal court or, in matters of tort, quasi-tort or maintenance, pronounced by a civil court, is punished by penal servitude of one year to three years and a fine of five hundred thousand to one million Burundian francs.

Is punished by the same penalties the joint and several co-debtor who organizes their insolvency by any means whatsoever with a view to imposing their obligations on other debtors.

##### Section 4 ‐ Breach of trust

###### Art.308.‐ Whoever has fraudulently, either misappropriated or wasted to the prejudice

of others, effects, money, merchandise, bills, receipts, writings of any nature containing or operating obligation, disposition or discharge, and which had been entrusted to them on condition of returning them or making a determined use or employment of them, is punished by penal servitude of two months to three years and a fine of fifty thousand francs to two hundred thousand francs or one of these penalties only.

###### Art.309.‐ The penalties may be increased from two years to five years of penal servitude and

a fine of five hundred thousand to one million Burundian francs or one of these penalties only when the breach of trust is committed:

1° By a person who appeals to the public in order to obtain the delivery of funds or securities, either for their own account or as director or agent de jure or de facto of an industrial or commercial enterprise;

2° By any other person who, habitually, engages in or lends their assistance, even on an ancillary basis, to operations relating to the property of third parties on whose behalf they collect funds or securities.

###### Art.310.‐ The penalties may be increased to ten years of penal servitude and a fine

of one hundred thousand to five hundred thousand Burundian francs or one of these penalties only when the breach of trust is committed by a judicial representative or by a public or ministerial officer, either in the exercise or on the occasion of the exercise of their functions, or by reason of their capacity.

##### Section 5 ‐ Stellionate and similar cases

###### Art.311.‐ Is punished by penal servitude of six months to five years and a fine of five

hundred thousand to one million Burundian francs or one of these penalties only, whoever has sold or mortgaged an immovable property that does not belong to them.

###### Art.312.‐ Is punished by penal servitude of two months to five years and a fine of

fifty thousand to five hundred thousand Burundian francs or one of these penalties only, whoever has sold or pledged the same movable property or any right to two or more persons.

##### Section 5 ‐ Usury

###### Art.313.‐ Is punished by penal servitude of three months to two years and a fine of

ten thousand to fifty thousand Burundian francs or one of these penalties only, whoever, abusing the weaknesses, passions, needs or ignorance of the debtor, causes to be promised, by reason of a credit operation, a loan contract or any other contract indicating a delivery of movable value, whatever the apparent form of the contract, for themselves or for another, an interest or other advantages manifestly exceeding the normal interest.

##### Section 6 ‐ Misappropriation of labor

###### Art.314.‐ Is punished by penal servitude of six months to five years and a fine of one hundred

thousand to five hundred thousand Burundian francs, whoever has fraudulently used for their profit or for the profit of a third party, the services of workers placed under their orders by the master with a view to work to be performed for the latter or for another.

##### Section 7 ‐ Fraud and deception

###### Paragraph 1 ‐ Fraud

###### Art.315.‐ Whoever, with the intention of appropriating a thing belonging to another, has

had delivered or issued to them funds, movables, obligations, receipts, either by using false names or false qualities, or by employing fraudulent maneuvers to persuade the existence of false enterprises, of an imaginary power or credit, to create hope or fear of a success, an accident or any other chimerical event, to otherwise abuse confidence or credulity, is punished by penal servitude of two months to five years and a fine of fifty thousand to two hundred thousand Burundian francs or one of these penalties only.

###### Art.316.‐ The penalties are two years to five years of penal servitude and a fine of

one hundred thousand to one million Burundian francs or one of these penalties only when the fraud is committed:

1° By a person who appeals to the public with a view to the issuance of securities or with a view to the collection of funds for humanitarian or social mutual aid purposes;

2° By a person holding public authority or charged with a public service mission, in the exercise of their functions or mission;

3° By a person who unduly appropriates the capacity of a person holding public authority or charged with a public service mission;

4° To the prejudice of a vulnerable person by reason of their age, illness, infirmity, physical or mental deficiency apparent or known to its perpetrator; 5° In an organized gang.

###### Paragraphe 2 ‐ On deception

###### Art.317.‐ Is punished with three months to two years of penal servitude and a fine of

fifty thousand to one hundred thousand Burundian francs or one of these penalties only, whoever has deceived the buyer:

1° On the identity of the thing sold, by fraudulently delivering a thing other than the determined object on which the transaction was based;

2° On the nature or origin of the thing sold, by selling or fraudulently delivering a thing which, similar in appearance to that which he purchased or believed he was purchasing, deceives the buyer in what he principally sought.

###### Art.318.‐ Is punished with the penalties provided for in the preceding article whoever, through

fraudulent maneuvers, has deceived:

1° The buyer or the seller on the quality of the things sold;

2° The buyer or the seller on the quantity of the things sold;

3° The parties engaged in a contract for work, or one of them, on the elements that must be used to calculate the salary.

##### Section 8 ‐ On the concealment of objects obtained by means of an offense

###### Art.319.‐ Whoever has concealed in whole or in part things obtained by means of an

offense is punished with penal servitude of six months to five years and a fine of twenty thousand to one hundred thousand Burundian francs or one of these penalties only.

##### Section 9 ‐ On fraudulent concealment

###### Art.320.‐ Are punished with penal servitude of eight days to two years and a fine of

ten thousand to fifty thousand Burundian francs or one of these penalties only, those who, having found a movable thing belonging to another, or having obtained possession of it by chance, have fraudulently concealed it or delivered it to third parties.

##### Section 10 ‐ On obtaining services by deception

###### Art.321.‐ Is punished with penal servitude of eight days to two years and a fine of

twenty thousand to one hundred thousand Burundian francs or one of these penalties only whoever, knowing that he is in absolute impossibility of paying:

1° Has had served to him, in an establishment intended for this purpose, drinks or food that he has consumed there in whole or in part;

2° Has obtained lodging in a hotel where he presented himself as a traveler; 3° Has rented a hired vehicle;

4° Has himself transported in a bus or any other means of public transport providing paid transportation of persons;

5° Has had fuel or lubricant supplied to him;

6° Has telephone, fax, Internet or secretarial services provided to him;

7° Has water and electricity provided to him.

###### Art.322.‐ The offenses provided for in the preceding article can only be prosecuted

upon complaint of the injured party. Payment by the perpetrator of the price and costs advanced before judgment by the complaining party or the withdrawal by the latter extinguishes the public action.

##### Section 11 ‐ On special provisions

###### Art.323.

‐ Are not punishable and can only give rise to civil reparations, thefts committed:

1° By ascendants to the prejudice of their children and other descendants; 2° By descendants to the prejudice of their ascendants;

3° By one spouse to the prejudice of the other spouse, except in cases of divorce or separation proceedings.

###### Art.324.‐ Thefts committed between relatives, collaterals or relatives by marriage up to the fourth degree

inclusively, can only be prosecuted upon complaint of the injured person. Withdrawal of the complaint ends the prosecution.

###### Art.325.‐ The provisions of the two preceding articles do not apply to any

other persons who may have participated in the theft or concealment of stolen objects.

##### Section 12 ‐ On instruments without funds

###### Art.326.‐ Is punished with penal servitude of one month to two years and a fine

equivalent to twenty percent of the amount shown on the drawn instrument or one of these penalties only, whoever has fraudulently obtained funds, securities or discharge by means of an instrument drawn either on a person who does not exist, or a person who had not authorized him to draw on her and whom he knew not to be his debtor or not to be obliged to be so at maturity.

###### Art.327.‐ Is punished with penal servitude of three months to five years and a fine

equivalent to twenty percent of the amount shown on the check or one of these penalties only:

1° Whoever, in bad faith, issues a check without prior and available funds or with funds less than the amount of the check;

2° Whoever, except for lawful stop payment in case of loss or his incapacity to receive, withdraws, after issuance, all or part of the funds or orders the drawee not to pay; 3° Whoever transfers a check knowing that there are no funds, or that the funds are insufficient or that they are not available;

4° Whoever, knowingly, agrees to receive a check issued under the conditions provided for in the preceding paragraphs.

###### Art.328.‐ In the cases referred to in the two preceding articles, the applicable penalty does not exceed

one quarter of the maximum imprisonment and fine provided for by these articles or one of these penalties only, if the holder has been compensated before the court has been seized.

The judge at the same time orders the withdrawal of the checkbook from the issuer for a period not exceeding one year.

#### Chapter 3 ‐ On destruction, damage, and harm

##### Section 1 ‐ On arson

###### Art.329.‐ Are punished with penal servitude of five to ten years and a fine of one

hundred thousand to five hundred thousand Burundian francs, those who have intentionally set fire to buildings or any other buildings whatsoever belonging to another but uninhabited at the time of the fire.

If the fire has reached buildings which, to the knowledge of the perpetrator, housed livestock, a stock of food or movable property, the penalty is increased to fifteen to twenty years.

###### Art.330.‐ Are punished with penal servitude for life, those who have set fire either to

buildings, ships, warehouses, or any other places whatsoever used for habitation or to any places, even uninhabited, and containing one or more persons at the time of the fire, if, according to the circumstances, the perpetrator should have presumed that one or more persons were there at the time of the offense.

###### Art.331.‐ When the fire has caused the death of one or more persons who, to the

knowledge of the perpetrator, were in the burned places at the time of the offense, and if death must be considered a necessary or probable consequence thereof, the guilty party is punished with penal servitude for life.

###### Art.332.‐ Are punished with penal servitude of ten to twenty years and a fine of one

hundred thousand to five hundred thousand Burundian francs, those who, outside the cases covered by the regulations on burning grass and standing vegetation, have set fire to. forests, woods and standing or cut crops.

###### Art.333.‐ Are punished with penal servitude of ten to twenty years and a fine of one

hundred thousand to five hundred thousand Burundian francs, the exclusive owners of the things designated in the two preceding articles who have set fire to them with malicious or fraudulent intent.

Whoever, with the intention of committing one of the acts provided for in the preceding articles, has set fire to any objects placed so as to communicate it to the thing he wanted to destroy is punished as if he had directly set fire to this latter thing.

###### Art.334.‐ Are punished with penal servitude of two months to one year and a fine of

fifty thousand to two hundred thousand Burundian francs or one of these penalties only, those who, without prejudice to the provisions relating to the regulations on burning grass and standing vegetation, have, through lack of foresight and precaution, set fire to forests, woods and standing or cut crops.

###### Art.335.‐ Is punished by penal servitude of eight days to three months and a fine of

ten thousand to one hundred thousand Burundian francs, or only one of these penalties, the fire of movable or immovable property of others that was caused by lack of foresight or precaution.

##### Section 2 ‐ Of the destruction of constructions, machines and other monuments

###### Art.336.

‐ Whoever has destroyed, knocked down or damaged, by whatever means, in whole or in part, buildings, bridges, dikes, roads, railways, telegraphic or telephonic devices, or other constructions belonging to others, is punished by penal servitude of one month to five years and a fine of ten thousand to one hundred thousand

###### Art.337.‐ Is punished by penal servitude of one year to five years and a fine of ten

thousand to one hundred thousand Burundian francs or only one of these penalties, whoever has destroyed or damaged commemorative signs of monuments, statues or other objects intended for public utility or decoration.

##### Section 3 ‐ Of the destruction and damage of trees, crops or other

properties

###### Art.338.‐ Are punished by penal servitude of one month to one year and a fine of

fifty thousand to one hundred thousand Burundian francs, those who, in enclosed or unenclosed places, have maliciously destroyed or damaged trees, crops, agricultural instruments or other property, movable or immovable, belonging to others.

###### Art.339.

‐ Whoever has, even without malicious intent, destroyed or damaged, without title or right, trees, crops, agricultural instruments or other property, movable or immovable, is punished by penal servitude of one month to one year and a fine of ten thousand to one hundred thousand Burundian francs or only one of these penalties.

##### Section 4 ‐ Of the destruction of animals

###### Art.340.‐ Is punished by a penalty of penal servitude of one year to five years and a fine

of fifty to one hundred thousand Burundian francs, or only one of these penalties, whoever has maliciously and without necessity, killed or seriously injured livestock or domestic animals belonging to others.

###### Art.341.‐ Is punished by penal servitude of one year to five years and a fine of one

hundred thousand to one million Burundian francs, whoever undertakes without prior authorization any activity likely to harm rare, threatened or endangered animal species, as well as their natural habitat.

##### Section 5 ‐ Of the removal, displacement or exceeding of boundary markers

###### Art.342.‐ Are punished by penal servitude of six months to five years and a fine of

ten thousand francs to one hundred thousand francs or only one of these penalties, those who, without being validly authorized, have removed, displaced or exceeded and those who have maliciously damaged boundary markers, signals or geodetic markers, have modified their appearance, indications or inscriptions.

The penalties are one year to five years and a fine of fifty thousand to two hundred thousand Burundian francs or only one of these penalties if the boundary markers in question were fixed by a judicial authority.

### Title 3 ‐ Of offenses against public trust

#### Chapter 1 ‐ Of counterfeiting, falsification and imitation of monetary

signs

###### Art.343.‐ Are punished by penal servitude of five to twenty years and a fine of one

hundred thousand to one million Burundian francs, those who have counterfeited or fraudulently altered or falsified metallic coins or bearer notes having legal tender in Burundi or abroad, and those who have introduced or issued on the territory of Burundi counterfeit, falsified or fraudulently altered coins or bearer notes.

###### Art.344.‐ Are punished by penal servitude of five to ten years and a fine of

fifty thousand to five hundred thousand Burundian francs, those who, without being guilty of participation, have obtained, with knowledge, metallic coins or bearer notes referred to in the preceding article, have put them in circulation.

Are punished by penal servitude of one year to five years and a fine of fifty thousand to one hundred thousand Burundian francs, those who, with the purpose of putting them in circulation, have received or obtained metallic coins or bearer notes referred to in the preceding article.

###### Art.345.‐ Are punished by penal servitude of one year to five years and a fine of

fifty thousand to one hundred thousand Burundian francs, those who, having received as good metallic coins or bearer notes having legal tender in Burundi or abroad, counterfeit or falsified, have put them in circulation with knowledge of the defects.

###### Art.346.‐ Are punished by penal servitude of two to ten years and a fine of one

hundred thousand to five hundred thousand Burundian francs, those who have manufactured, distributed or put into circulation, either tokens, medals or metallic pieces, or printed matter or forms, obtained by any process whatsoever and presenting by their external form, with coins or bearer notes having legal tender in Burundi or abroad, a resemblance having the purpose of facilitating their acceptance in place of the imitated values.

###### Art.347.

‐ Are punished as guilty of deception as provided for in Article 317, those who have given or offered in payment tokens, medals, metallic pieces, printed matter or forms, obtained by any process whatsoever and presenting by their external form with coins or bearer notes having legal tender in Burundi or abroad, a resemblance of a nature to facilitate their acceptance in place of the imitated values.

#### Chapter 2 ‐ Of counterfeiting or falsification of seals, stamps, punches

or other marks of authority

###### Art.348.‐ Are punished by penal servitude of five to fifteen years and a fine of

one hundred thousand to five hundred thousand Burundian francs or only one of these penalties:
1° Those who have counterfeited or falsified the seals, stamps, punches or other marks of Burundi, a foreign State or national or international public administrations;

2° Those who have fraudulently made use of these counterfeit or falsified objects;
3° Those who have knowingly displayed or offered them for sale.

###### Art.349.‐ Are punished by the penalties provided for in the preceding article:

1° Those who have received or unduly obtained the true seals, stamps, punches or other marks of the State, or of a foreign State, national or international public administrations, have made an application or use thereof detrimental to the rights and interests of the State, any authority or even an individual;
2° Those who have manufactured or falsified papers or printed matter with official letterheads in use in the assemblies established by the Constitution, public administrations, prosecutors' offices or courts;

3° Those who have offered for sale, distributed or made any use of such papers with letterheads thus manufactured or falsified.

###### Art.350.‐ Are punished by a penalty of five to ten years of penal servitude and a fine

of one hundred thousand to five hundred thousand Burundian francs or only one of these penalties:
1° Those who have counterfeited or falsified the seals, stamps or other mark of any authority, either of a private establishment, bank, industry or commerce, or of an individual;

2° Those who have made use of such seals, stamps or other marks of authority thus counterfeited or falsified.

#### Chapitre 5 ‐ Forgeries committed in writing and in electronic dispatches

##### Section 1 ‐ Definition

###### Art.357.‐ Constitutes the offence of forgery, any alteration of the truth likely to cause

prejudice to another and committed with criminal intent:

1° Either by distorting the substance or circumstances of a deed;

2° Either by writing agreements other than those drawn up by the parties; 3° Either by recording as true facts that are false or as acknowledged facts that were not;

4° Either by fabrication of all or part of a document;

5° Either by counterfeiting or alteration of writings or signatures;

6° Either by false signature;

7° Either by substitution of persons;

8° Either by addition, removal or alteration of clauses, declarations or facts that any deed was intended to receive or record.

###### Art.402.‐ The following acts are punished by one to three years of penal servitude and a fine of fifty

#### Chapitre 1 ‐ Of offenses against freedom of worship

### Titre 6 ‐ Of crimes and offenses of an economic nature and against the public interest

#### Chapitre 1 ‐ Of offenses against the proper functioning of the national economy

###### Art.428.‐ Is punishable by six months to two years of penal servitude and a fine of

fifty thousand to one hundred thousand Burundian francs anyone who, by means of threats, violence, assault or fraudulent maneuvers, has brought about or maintained a concerted work stoppage with the aim of forcing the increase or decrease of wages or of infringing upon the free exercise of industry or labor.

###### Art.429.‐ Are punishable by six months to two years of penal servitude and a fine of one hundred

thousand to one million Burundian francs, workers and management agents who, without authorization, communicate manufacturing secrets of their company to persons outside it.

###### Art.430.‐ Is punishable by one year to five years of penal servitude and a fine of one hundred thousand

to one million Burundian francs or one of these penalties only, anyone who, by concerted action, by making use of inaccurate or misleading information or by making use of threats, assault, or by concealing stocks of commodities or materials which he holds or causes to be held, has obstructed free commercial competition or the normal supply of retail traders or the public.

###### Art.431.‐ The penalties provided for in the preceding article are applicable to those who, in

public auctions or in public procurement awards, have hindered or disturbed the freedom of bidding or submissions by assault, threats, promises, false news, price agreements or any fraudulent concerted action.

###### Art.432.‐ Are punished by five to ten years of penal servitude and a fine of one hundred

thousand to five million Burundian francs:

1° Any person who, acting on behalf of the State, public administrations, local authorities, public institutions, parastatal or mixed economy enterprises, banking organizations, self-managed units of consumption, industrial or agricultural production, any private law entity ensuring the management of a public service, enters into, for personal purposes, a contract, agreement or contract that they know to be contrary to the fundamental economic interests of the Nation;

2° Without prior authorization, any merchant, industrialist, artisan, contractor, or, in general any person, who enters into, even on an occasional basis, a contract or agreement with the State or one of its organizations referred to in the preceding paragraph, by taking advantage of the authority or influence of the agents of the aforementioned organizations to increase the prices they normally and habitually charge or to modify to their advantage the quality of goods or delivery times;

3° Without prior authorization, any unauthorized intermediary who, without real needs corresponding to the necessities of the distribution system, contributes to the artificial increase of prices, modifies to their advantage the quality of goods or disrupts delivery times.

###### Art.433.‐ Commits an offense against exchange regulations whoever:

1° Violates an obligation or prohibition relating to transfers of funds, declarations of assets, the holding or trade of precious metals or precious stones; 2° Offers to sell or purchase currencies, cash, securities, even when such offers are not accompanied by any delivery or presentation;

3° Offers their services as an intermediary, either to bring together sellers and buyers, or to facilitate negotiations even when such intermediation is not remunerated.

###### Art.434.‐ Is punished by two months to two years of penal servitude and a fine equal

to the legal value of the corpus delicti that was the subject of the offense, any person who commits one of the offenses against exchange regulations referred to in the preceding article. In case of recidivism, the penalty of penal servitude is fixed according to article 117.

###### Art.435.‐ Independently of the penalties provided for in the preceding article, the

confiscation of the corpus delicti shall be carried out.

When, for any reason, the corpus delicti could not be seized or is not produced, the offender is sentenced to a fine in an amount equal to the value of the corpus delicti.

#### Chapter 2 ‐ Corruption and related offenses

##### Section 1 ‐ Corruption

Subsection 1 ‐ Corruption of public officials

###### Paragraph 1 ‐ Passive corruption

###### Art.436.‐ Is punished by penal servitude of five to ten years and a fine ranging from

single to double the value of the illicit profit acquired, any person holding public authority, charged with a public service mission or invested with an elective public mandate, who has solicited or accepted, without right, at any time, directly or indirectly, for their own account or for the account of another, offers, gifts, presents or any advantages whatsoever to perform or have performed, to refrain or have refrained from performing an act of their function or mandate or facilitated by their function, mission or mandate.

###### Art.437.‐ Is punished by penal servitude of ten to fifteen years and a fine ranging

from single to double the value of the illicit profit acquired, any person holding public authority, charged with a public service mission or invested with an elective public mandate, who has solicited or accepted, without right, at any time, directly or indirectly, for their own account or for the account of another, offers, gifts, presents or any advantages whatsoever to perform or have performed an unjust act.

###### Art.438.‐ Without prejudice to the application of other criminal provisions, the offender is

punished by penal servitude of fifteen to twenty years and a fine ranging from single to double the value of the illicit profit acquired, if they have solicited or accepted, without right, at any time, directly or indirectly, for their own account or for the account of another, offers, gifts, presents or any advantages whatsoever, to commit or have committed in the exercise of their function, employment or mission any offense whatsoever.

Is punished by the same penalties, any judge or arbitrator who is guilty of corruption during a dispute they are settling. The same applies to any public agent charged with combating corruption or any public authority who allows themselves to be corrupted in the context of their functions.

###### Art.439.‐ Is punished by penal servitude of twelve to fifteen years and a fine

ranging from single to double the value of the illicit profit acquired, any agent of the judicial order, any Officer of the Public Prosecutor's Office or the Judicial Police who receives or agrees to receive offers or promises for the purpose of having a decision made that should not have been made.

###### Art.440.‐ Is punished by penal servitude of twelve to fifteen years and a fine of

two hundred thousand to five hundred thousand Burundian francs, anyone mentioned in articles 436 to 439 of this Code who has, explicitly or implicitly, at any time, demanded, benefited from, inflicted acts of a sexual nature or accepted the promise thereof in order to perform or have performed, refrain or have refrained from performing an act that falls within their responsibilities.

The penalty is increased to servitude of fifteen to twenty years and a fine of five hundred thousand to one million Burundian francs if the offender is a male or female teacher who has committed these acts with regard to their schoolchild, pupil or student regardless of their sex.

###### Art.441.‐ Is punished by penal servitude of one to three years and a fine of two

hundred thousand to five hundred thousand Burundian francs, any public agent or any public authority who accepts gifts from a person or any advantage likely to have influenced or to influence the processing of a procedure or transaction related to their functions.

The same penalties are applicable to anyone who grants gifts or any advantage to a public agent or any public authority for having provided them with any public service whatsoever.

###### Paragraph 2 ‐ Active corruption

###### Art.442.‐ Is punished by penal servitude of five to ten years and a fine ranging from

single to double the value of the illicit profit acquired, anyone who has proposed, without right, at any time, directly or indirectly, offers, promises, gifts, presents or any advantages whatsoever to obtain from a person holding elective public office, that they perform or have performed, that they refrain or have refrained from performing an act of their function, mission or mandate or facilitated by their function or mandate.

Is punished by the same penalties the act of yielding to a person holding public authority, charged with a public service mission or invested with an elective public mandate, who solicits, without right, at any time, directly or indirectly, offers, promises, gifts, presents or any advantages whatsoever to perform or have performed, to refrain or have refrained from performing an act referred to in the preceding paragraph.

Subsection 2 ‐ Corruption of persons not exercising a public function

###### Art.443.‐ Is punished with penal servitude of two to five years and a fine from

simple to double the value of the illicit profit acquired, any person who has proposed, at any time, directly or indirectly, offers, promises, gifts, presents or any advantages whatsoever to obtain from a person, who without being a holder of public authority or charged with a public service mission, exercises in the context of a professional or social activity, a management function or work for a natural or legal person, or any organization, that they accomplish or have accomplished, abstain or have abstained from accomplishing an act of their activity or function or facilitated by their activity or function, in violation of their legal, contractual or professional obligations.

###### Art.444.‐ Is punished with the same penalties, the one who has yielded to a person referred to in the

previous article who solicits, at any time, directly or indirectly, offers, gifts, presents or any advantages whatsoever to accomplish or have accomplished, to abstain or have abstained from accomplishing an act referred to in the same article, in violation of their legal, contractual or professional obligations.

###### Art.445.‐ Is punished with penal servitude of two to five years and a fine from

simple to double the value of the illicit profit acquired, any person who, without being a holder of public authority or charged with a public service mission, exercises in the context of a professional or social activity, a management function or work for a natural or legal person, or any organization, has solicited or accepted, at any time, directly or indirectly, offers, promises, gifts, presents or any advantages whatsoever to accomplish or have accomplished, to abstain or have abstained from accomplishing an act of their activity or function, or facilitated by their activity or function, in violation of their legal, contractual or professional obligations.

###### Art.446.‐ Is punished with penal servitude of twelve to fifteen years and a fine of

two hundred thousand to five hundred thousand Burundian francs, any person who, without being a holder of public authority or charged with a public service mission, exercises in the context of a professional or social activity, a management function or work for a natural or legal person, or any organization has, explicitly or implicitly, at any time, demanded, benefited from, subjected to acts of a sexual nature or has accepted the promise thereof in order to perform or have performed, abstain or have abstained from performing an act within their attributions.

##### Section 2 ‐ Repression of offenses related to corruption

###### Paragraphe 1 ‐ Concussion

###### Art.447.‐ Is punished with penal servitude of five to twenty years and a fine of

fifty thousand to five hundred thousand Burundian francs, any person holding public authority or charged with a public service mission, who receives, demands or orders the collection of, as rights or contributions, taxes or duties, fine or security, revenues or interests, a sum that they know is not due, or exceeds what is due.

Is punished with the same penalties, the act, by the same persons, of granting in any form and for any reason whatsoever an exemption or franchise from rights, contribution, taxes or duties, fine or security, revenues or interests in violation of legal and regulatory texts and of carrying out, gratuitously or at a low price, the delivery of public goods.

The quality of the persons referred to in the first paragraph of this article is assessed both during the exercise of functions by the interested parties and after their cessation.

###### Paragraphe 2 ‐ Trading in influence

###### Art.448.‐ Is punished with penal servitude of five to ten years and a fine of

fifty thousand to five hundred thousand Burundian francs, any person holding an elective public office, who has solicited or accepted, without right, at any time, directly or indirectly, for their own account or for the account of others, offers, promises, gifts, presents or any advantages whatsoever to use or have used their real or supposed influence with a view to obtaining from an authority or public administration distinctions, employment, contracts or any other favorable decision.

###### Art.449.

‐ Is punished with the penalties provided for in the previous article, the one who proposes, without right, at any time, directly or indirectly, offers, promises, gifts, presents or any advantages whatsoever to obtain from a person holding an elective public office, that they use or have used their real or supposed influence with a view to obtaining from an authority or public administration distinctions, employment, contracts or any other favorable decision.

###### Art.450.‐ Is punished with the same penalties provided for in article 448, the one who has yielded to a

person holding public authority, charged with a public service mission or invested with an elective public mandate who solicits, without right, at any time, directly or indirectly, offers, promises, gifts, presents or any advantages whatsoever to use or have used their influence under the conditions referred to in the previous article.

###### Art.451.‐ Is punished with penal servitude of three to five years and a fine of

fifty thousand to five hundred thousand Burundian francs, whoever solicits or accepts, without right, at any time, directly or indirectly, offers, gifts, presents or any advantages whatsoever to use or have used their real or supposed influence with a view to obtaining from an authority or public administration distinctions, employment, contracts or any other favorable decision.

Is punished with the same penalties the act of yielding to the solicitations provided for in the previous paragraph, or of proposing without right, at any time, directly or indirectly, offers, promises, gifts, presents or any advantages whatsoever for a person to use or have used their real or supposed influence with a view to obtaining from an authority or public administration distinctions, employment, contracts or any other favorable decision.

###### Paragraphe 3 ‐ Misappropriation and embezzlement of property

###### Art.452.‐ Is punished with penal servitude of ten to twenty years and a fine of

fifty thousand to one million Burundian francs, any person holding public authority, charged with a public service mission, any public accountant, any public depositary or one of their subordinates, who destroys, embezzles or misappropriates an act or title, public or private funds, effects, documents or titles in lieu thereof, or any other object which has been entrusted to them by reason of their functions or mission.

###### Art.453.‐ When the destruction, embezzlement or misappropriation by a third party of the

property referred to in the previous article results from the negligence of a person holding public authority or charged with a public service mission, a public accountant or a public depositary, the latter is punished with penal servitude of one year to two years and a fine of ten thousand to fifty thousand Burundian francs.

###### Paragraphe 4 ‐ Fraudulent management

###### Art.454.‐ Any person holding an elected public office, any public accountant, any public depositary or one of their subordinates, who fraudulently commits irregularities in the execution of accounts and budgets of the State, public administrations, local authorities, public institutions, companies with public participation or mixed economy companies, banking organizations, self-managed units of consumption, industrial or agricultural production, or any private law organization ensuring the management of a public service, shall be punished with penal servitude of ten to twenty years and a fine of

fifty thousand to one million Burundian francs. Prosecutions shall be initiated against all those who took part in the offense with knowledge of the inauthenticity of the monies or securities.

###### Paragraphe 5 ‐ Favoritism

###### Art.455.‐ Any person holding public authority, charged with a public service mission or invested with an elected public mandate or exercising specific functions in state structures, public administrations, local authorities, public institutions, companies with public participation or mixed economy companies, banking organizations, self-managed units of consumption, industrial or agricultural production, or any private law organization ensuring the management of a public service, who has procured for another an unjustified advantage by an act contrary to legislative and regulatory provisions intended to guarantee equal access and equality of candidates to public functions, in public procurement and contractual transfers of public service management, shall be punished with penal servitude of one year to three years and a fine of

fifty thousand to one hundred thousand Burundian francs.

###### Paragraphe 6 ‐ Illegal taking of interest

###### Art.456.‐ Any person holding public authority, or charged with a public service mission or a person invested with an elected public mandate, who takes, receives or retains, directly or indirectly, any interest whatsoever in an enterprise or in an operation of which they have, at the time of the act, in whole or in part, the responsibility to ensure supervision, administration, liquidation or payment, shall be punished with penal servitude of five to ten years and a fine of five

hundred thousand to one million Burundian francs.

Any person having been charged, as a public official or employee or agent of a public administration charged, by reason of their very function, with exercising supervision or direct control of a private enterprise, or with expressing their opinion on operations carried out by a private enterprise and who, either on leave, placed on availability, or after admission to retirement, or after resignation, dismissal or revocation and for a period of three years from the cessation of the function, exercises in that enterprise any mandate whatsoever or a remunerated activity of any kind whatsoever, shall be punished with penal servitude of two to five years and a fine of two hundred thousand Burundian francs.

###### Paragraphe 7 ‐ Money laundering

###### Art.457.‐ Commits the offense of money laundering and shall be punished with a sentence of penal

servitude of ten to fifteen years and a fine of up to ten times the value of the property subject to money laundering, whoever proceeds with:

1° The conversion, transfer or assignment of property in full knowledge that they are the proceeds of an offense with a view to concealing or disguising the illicit origin of said property or helping any person involved in the commission of the offense to escape the consequences of their action;

2° The concealment or disguise of the true nature, origin, location, disposition, movement or ownership of property or rights, proceeds of an offense;

3° The acquisition, possession or use of property whose origin, at the time of acquisition, possession or use, is the proceeds of an offense; 4° Participation in one of the acts referred to in the three preceding points, association to commit the act, aiding, inciting or advising someone to commit it or facilitating its execution.

###### Paragraphe 8 ‐ Active corruption of foreign public officials, officials

of public international organizations and non-governmental organizations

###### Art.458.‐ Whoever has proposed, offered or granted, without right, directly or indirectly, offers, promises, gifts, presents or any advantages whatsoever to obtain from a foreign public official, an official of a public international organization or a non-governmental organization, that they perform or refrain from performing an act in the exercise of their official functions or to retain a contract or other advantage in connection with international trade activities, shall be punished with penal servitude of five to ten years and a fine ranging from

the simple amount up to twice the value of the illicit profit acquired.

Any person having served as an intermediary in the commission of the offense referred to in this article shall be punished with the same penalties.

###### Paragraphe 9 ‐ Complementary penalties

###### Art.459.‐ Natural persons guilty of one of the offenses provided for in this

chapter may also incur at least one of the following complementary penalties:

1° Confiscation as provided for by the relevant provisions of this Code;

2° Permanent ban from Burundian territory for a specified period which may not be less than five years for any foreigner;

3° Prohibition for a period of up to ten years from exercising a public function or exercising the professional or social function in the exercise of which or on the occasion of the exercise of which the offense was committed;

4° Prohibition of civic, civil, political and family rights as determined by law;

5° Posting or dissemination of the pronounced decision.

###### Art.460.‐ Legal persons guilty of one of the offenses provided for in this

chapter may also incur at least one of the following complementary penalties:

1° Special confiscation as provided for by this Code;

2° For a maximum period of five years:

 prohibition from exercising directly or indirectly the professional or social activity in which or on the occasion of the exercise of which the offense was committed;

 closure of the establishments of the enterprise having been used to commit the incriminated acts;

 exclusion from public procurement;

3° Posting or dissemination of the pronounced decision.

###### Art.461.‐ Those convicted of offenses provided for in this chapter may not

benefit from pardon, nor from amnesty, nor from amnesty pardon as long as they have not executed the civil reparations pronounced by the trial court.

###### Paragraphe 10 ‐ Exemptions and mitigation of penalties

###### Art.462.‐ Except in case of recidivism in matters of corruption, is exempt from penalty any

person, author or accomplice of active corruption who, before any prosecution, has revealed the offense to the administrative or judicial authority and allowed the identification of other persons implicated.

Except for the case provided in the preceding paragraph, the maximum penalty incurred by any person, author or accomplice of one of the corruption offenses who, after the initiation of prosecution, has allowed or facilitated the arrest of other persons involved, is reduced by half.

In addition, they are exempt from the complementary penalties provided by this Code. In all cases, restitution is never made to the corruptor of things delivered by them. They are confiscated for the benefit of the Treasury.

#### Chapitre 3 ‐ Games of chance, lotteries and prediction contests

##### Section 1 ‐ Games of chance

###### Art.463.‐ Games of chance are those in which chance is the essential and

predominant element and prevails over the skill, equality or combinations of players who engage in them, in the hope of achieving an appreciable gain, relatively considerable sums of money in view of their contributory capacity.

The holding of games of chance consists in the fact of organizing or facilitating the passion of others for these games, with a view to deriving personal pecuniary profit from them.

Games of chance are prohibited in public places or places open to the public, in all unenclosed places where the public can have direct view, as well as in all other places, even private ones, where anyone wishing to engage in gambling is freely admitted to enter.

Games of chance are prohibited to minors under eighteen years of age.

###### Art.464.‐ Is punished by one to two years of penal servitude and a fine of fifty

thousand francs at most or one of these penalties only:

1° Whoever has held games of chance in one of the places and under the conditions referred to in the preceding article;

2° Whoever has played games of chance in these same places and conditions.

##### Section 2 ‐ Lotteries

###### Art.465.‐ Lotteries are prohibited.

Are deemed lotteries, all operations offered to the public under any denomination whatsoever and intended to give rise to the hope of a gain which would be acquired by chance.

###### Art.466.

‐ The authors, entrepreneurs, administrators, employees or lottery agents are punished by one to two years of penal servitude and a fine of fifty thousand Burundian francs at most.

Are confiscated, movable objects put in the lottery and those used or intended for its service.

When an immovable has been put in the lottery, the special confiscation is replaced by a fine of fifty thousand to two hundred thousand Burundian francs.

###### Art.467.‐ Are punished by the same penalties:

1° Those who have placed, peddled or distributed lottery tickets;

2° Those who, through notices, announcements, posters, or by any other means of publication, have made known the existence of lotteries or facilitated the issuance of their tickets. In all cases, tickets, notices, announcements or posters are seized and destroyed.

###### Art.468.‐ Are exempt from the penalties provided by the preceding article, town criers and

bill posters who have made known the person from whom they hold these tickets or the writings mentioned above.

###### Art.469.‐ Are exempt from these provisions, lotteries and games of chance

organized or authorized by public authorities and exclusively intended for acts of charity, for the encouragement of industry, arts or sports or for any other purpose of public utility, when they have been authorized:

1° By the Minister of the Interior, if the issuance of tickets is made and announced or published in more than one province;

2° By the Provincial Governor, if the issuance of tickets is made and announced or published only in one province.

###### Art.470.‐ Are also exempt:

1° Financial operations of the State, made with premiums or reimbursable by chance;

2° Financial operations of the same nature made by foreign powers, when the issuance of securities relating to these operations has been authorized by the President of the Republic or his delegate;

3° Financial operations of the same nature made by municipalities, as well as operations of companies making incidentally reimbursements with premiums by chance, when they have been authorized by the President of the Republic or his delegate.

##### Section 3 ‐ Prediction contests

###### Art.471.‐ Is punished by eight days to three months of penal servitude and a fine of

two hundred thousand Burundian francs at most, or one of these penalties only:

1° Whoever, for the purpose of profit, has organized or operated for his own account or for the account of others prediction contests;

2° Whoever, free of charge or for remuneration, has served as intermediary in a prohibited prediction contest, either by transferring funds, or by distributing bulletins or advertisements of the enterprise that organizes or operates this contest.

###### Art.472.‐ In all cases, the funds at stake, bulletins, advertisements and equipment

for operation are confiscated.

###### Art.473.‐ Are exempt from these provisions, prediction contests

organized under the conditions provided for in article 469.

#### Chapitre 4 ‐ Offenses relating to public and private companies

##### Section 1 ‐ Offenses in connection with the formation of companies

###### Art.474.‐ Are punished by penal servitude of one to five years and a fine of one hundred

thousand to one million Burundian francs the partners and founders of companies who, knowingly, have made in the articles of association, at the time of incorporation or capital increase, a false declaration concerning the distribution of shares and stocks as well as their payment.

###### Art.475.‐ Are punished by penal servitude of six months to five years and a fine of

one hundred thousand to five hundred thousand Burundian francs or one of these penalties only:

1° Those who have solicited, either subscriptions or payments, or purchases of shares, bonds or other company securities:

 by simulation of subscription or payments to a company;

 by publication of subscription or payment that they know does not exist;

 by publication of names of persons designated as being or to be attached to the company in any capacity when they know these designations to be contrary to the truth;

 by publication of any other facts that they knew to be false.

2° Those who, directly or through an intermediary have opened a public subscription to shares, to the sale of bonds or other securities of a company which is not authorized to make a public call for savings.

##### Section 2 ‐ Offences relating to the operation of companies

###### Art.476.‐ Are punished by a fine of fifty thousand to five hundred thousand Burundian francs, the

managers, managing directors, directors, members of the management board or supervisory board, or company directors who:

1° Have not prepared for each financial year the balance sheet and other required accounting documents and a report on the operations of the financial year;

2° Have refused or neglected to communicate to the partners and shareholders before the general meeting the balance sheet and other required documents to assess their management;

3° Have refused or neglected to prepare the minutes of the proceedings of a general meeting; have failed to publish within the prescribed time limit the resolutions resulting in amendments to the articles of association, to the powers of managers, managing directors, directors, members of the management board or supervisory board, or company directors, or to the share capital with respect to capital companies.

###### Art.477.‐ Are punished by penal servitude of one year to five years and a fine of one hundred

thousand to one million Burundian francs, managers, managing directors, directors, members of the management board or supervisory board, or company directors who:

1° Fraudulently, have caused a valuation higher than its real value to be attributed to a contribution in kind;

2° In the absence of accounts or by means of fraudulent accounts, have knowingly made the distribution of fictitious dividends among the partners;

3° Even in the absence of any dividend distribution, have knowingly presented to the partners and shareholders an inaccurate balance sheet in order to conceal the true situation of the company;

4° In bad faith, have made use of the assets or credits of the company, the powers they possessed or the votes they had, in a manner they knew to be contrary to the interest of the company, for personal purposes or to favor another company or enterprise in which they had a direct or indirect interest.

###### Art.478.‐ Are punished by penal servitude of three months to two years and a fine of

fifty thousand to three hundred thousand Burundian francs:

1° Those who, by presenting themselves as owners of shares or bonds that do not belong to them, have taken part in voting at a meeting of shareholders or bondholders;

2° Those who have handed over the shares or bonds for the above-mentioned use.

###### Art.479.

‐ The provisions of Articles 476 and 477 are applicable to any person who, directly or through an intermediary, has in fact exercised the management of a limited liability company under the cover of or in place of its legal representatives.

##### Section 3 ‐ Offences relating to the auditing of companies

###### Art.480.‐ Is punished by three months to two years of penal servitude and fifty thousand to

one hundred thousand Burundian francs fine, any auditor, any independent auditor, who has knowingly given or confirmed false information about the situation of the company.

Half of the penalties provided for in the preceding paragraph are applicable to managers, managing directors, directors, members of the management board or supervisory board, or company directors who have knowingly obstructed the verifications and audits of members of state audit institutions, auditors and independent auditors or who have refused to provide them on site with all documents useful for the exercise of their mission, in particular contracts, books, accounting documents and registers and minutes.

##### Section 4 ‐ Misuse of company assets

###### Art.481.‐ Is punished by penal servitude of two to five years and a fine of

fifty thousand to one hundred thousand Burundian francs, any manager or agent of a public or private company who makes use of its assets in a manner he knew to be contrary to the interests of the company.

##### Section 5 ‐ Offences relating to the liquidation of companies

###### Art.482.‐ The penalties provided for in Article 477 are applicable to the liquidator of the company

who refuses or neglects to communicate the liquidation accounts to the partners, to convene the general meeting.

The penalties of Article 480 are applicable to the liquidator of the company who, in bad faith:

1° Has made use of the assets or credit of the company in liquidation in a manner he knew to be contrary to its interest, for personal purposes or to favor another company or enterprise in which he had a direct or indirect interest;

2° Has sold all or part of the assets of the company in liquidation below their market value;

3° Has knowingly given or confirmed false information in the liquidation accounts or report causing prejudice to the rights of the partners.

#### Chapitre 5 ‐ Computer crime

##### Section 1 ‐ Computer forgery

###### Art.483.‐ Anyone who commits a forgery, by introducing into a computer system, by

modifying or deleting data, that is stored, processed or transmitted by a computer system, or by modifying by any technological means the possible use of data in a computer system, and thereby modifies the legal scope of such data, is punished by penal servitude of five to twenty years and a fine of Anyone who makes use of the data thus obtained, while knowing that it is false, is punished as if he were the author of the forgery.

##### Section 2 ‐ Computer fraud

###### Art.484.‐ Anyone who obtains, for himself or for another, a fraudulent patrimonial advantage

### Titre 8 ‐ Offenses against public security

#### Chapitre 1 ‐ Association formed for the purpose of attacking persons and

property

###### Art.487.‐ Any association of wrongdoers constitutes any grouping formed or understanding

established with a view to the preparation characterized by one or more material facts, of one or more crimes or one or more offenses punishable by at least five years of penal servitude.

###### Art.488.‐ Any association formed for the purpose of attacking persons or

property is an offense that exists by the mere fact of the organization of the gang. If the association has as its purpose the perpetration of an offense punishable by at least ten years of penal servitude, the instigators of this association, the leaders of this gang and those having exercised any command are punished by ten to twenty years of penal servitude.

###### Art.489.‐ The same persons are punished by five to ten years of penal servitude if

the association was formed only with a view to perpetrating offenses punishable by less than ten years of penal servitude.

###### Art.490.‐ Anyone who, knowingly and voluntarily, provided the gang or

association with arms and ammunition, vehicles, is punished by five to ten years of penal servitude.

###### Art.491.‐ Any other person being part of the association or one who knowingly

and voluntarily provided the gang with information, material, places of retreat or meeting or any other assistance useful to the perpetration and consummation of the offenses that are the object of the association, is sentenced to penal servitude of two to five years.

###### Art.492.‐ Those offenders who, before any attempted offense that is the object of the association and before any prosecution, revealed to the public authorities the existence of these gangs and the names of their leaders or those responsible are exempted from the penalties provided for in the preceding article.

#### Chapitre 2 ‐ Threats of attack against persons or against property

###### Art.493.‐ Anyone who, by anonymous or signed writing, threatened with order or under condition, an attack against persons or property punishable by at least five years of penal servitude is sentenced to penal servitude of two to five years and to a fine of

ten thousand to one hundred thousand francs or by only one of these penalties. The verbal threat made with order or under condition, or the threat by gestures or emblems of an attack against persons or property punishable by at least five years of penal servitude, is punished by three months to two years and a fine of ten thousand francs to fifty thousand francs or by only one of these penalties.

###### Art.494.‐ Anyone who intentionally caused alarm among the population by the threat

or false announcement of a danger to life, health or property is punished by three months to one year of penal servitude and a fine of twenty thousand francs to one hundred thousand francs or by only one of these penalties.

#### Chapitre 3 ‐ Endangering public security by means of weapons

###### Art.495.‐ The different types of weapons or any other device classified as such in application of laws and regulations, in particular on the regime of firearms and their ammunition, constitute weapons within the meaning of the provisions of this chapter.

###### Art.496.

‐ Whoever imports, acquires, possesses, transfers, rents or arranges rental, abandons, manufactures, repairs, transits or exports weapons or ammunition in violation of the laws and regulations in force on the regime of firearms is liable to penal servitude of two to ten years and a fine of one hundred thousand to five million Burundian francs. The penalty is doubled when the acts provided for in the preceding paragraph are committed by a public official, a civil servant, a magistrate or a member of the defense and security forces.

The penalty of penal servitude for life may be pronounced when the facts mentioned in the first paragraph of this article are connected to a collective enterprise aimed at overthrowing the constitutional order.

Other offenses against the laws and regulations on the regime of firearms and its implementing measures are punishable by penal servitude of up to five years and a fine of fifty thousand to five hundred thousand Burundian francs or one of these Are also liable to the penalties mentioned in the preceding paragraph of this article persons who, through their negligence or lack of precautions in the custody of weapons or ammunition they possess, have made their disappearance possible. In all cases, special confiscation of weapons and ammunition may be pronounced. It is pronounced in the case of prohibited firearms, bladed weapons and special devices.

#### Chapitre 4 ‐ Breaches of public solidarity

###### Art.497.‐ Whoever, having knowledge of an offense punishable by more than five years

of penal servitude, already attempted or completed, has not immediately informed the public authorities when it was still possible to prevent or limit its effects, is punished by one quarter of the penal servitude that he himself would incur if he were the perpetrator of the offense.

When the offense was punishable by penal servitude for life, the perpetrator of the offense provided for in the preceding paragraph is punished by penal servitude of ten to twenty years.

###### Art.498.‐ Whoever, being able to prevent by his immediate action, without risk to himself

or to third parties, either an offense against persons or an offense against property, voluntarily refrains from doing so is punished by penal servitude of one to three years and a fine of twenty thousand to one hundred thousand Burundian francs, or one of these Is punished by the same penalties, whoever voluntarily refrains from providing to a person in danger the assistance that, without risk to himself or to third parties, he could provide, either by his personal action or by calling for help.

#### Chapitre 5 ‐ Escape of detainees and breach of ban

##### Section 1 ‐ Escape of detainees

###### Art.499.‐ Is considered a detainee any person:

1° Who is placed in police custody;

2° Who is pending or in the process of being presented to the judicial authority at the end of police custody or in execution of a warrant to bring or arrest;

3° Who has been notified of a committal warrant or an arrest warrant continuing to produce effect;

4° Who is serving a custodial sentence or who has been arrested to serve this sentence

5° Who is placed under extradition detention;

6° Who, for health reasons, is placed in a health or hospital establishment

7° Who, having been convicted, evades the control to which she is subject when she had benefited from a relaxation measure;

8° Who, having been convicted, refuses to return to the penitentiary establishment at the end of a leave of absence;

9° Who, having been convicted, is subject to a security measure.

###### Art.500.‐ The act for a detainee of evading the custody to which he is subject, by

violence, threats, breaking and entering or corruption is punished by penal servitude of two to five years and a fine of five thousand to twenty thousand Burundian francs.

If the offense provided for in the preceding paragraph is committed by a convicted person serving a sentence of penal servitude for life, the judge pronounces the measure of non-reduction of the sentence being served.

###### Art.501.‐ Those who have procured or facilitated the escape of a detainee are punished by half

of the penalty incurred by the escapee for the offense on the basis of which he was prosecuted.

###### Art.502.‐ If the offense was committed by a person assigned to the supervision or

custody of detainees, the penalty is penal servitude of two to five years and a fine of ten thousand to fifty thousand Burundian francs.

###### Art.503.‐ If the escape took place with violence, threats or breaking and entering, the penalties against

those who favored it, either by their cooperation or by providing instruments or weapons suitable for operating, are penal servitude of three to ten years and a fine of ten thousand to fifty thousand Burundian francs.

##### Section 2 ‐ Breach of ban

###### Art.504.‐ The convicted person who contravenes the obligation to stay away from certain places or

a certain region or to reside in a specified place provided for in Articles 74 to 77, is punished by penal servitude of a maximum of six months.

#### Chapitre 6 ‐ Other acts liable to harm public security

##### Section 1 ‐ Narcotics

###### Art.505.‐ Constitute narcotics within the meaning of the provisions of this section the

substances or plants classified as such in application of the provisions of the order of the Minister having public health in his attributions.

###### Art.506.

‐ The cultivation, sale, transport, possession and consumption of narcotics are prohibited except in the cases and conditions determined by the order of the Minister having public health in his attributions.

###### Art.507.‐ Are punished by penal servitude of one to five years and a fine of one hundred

thousand to two hundred and fifty thousand Burundian francs or one of these penalties only, those who have contravened the regulatory provisions concerning substances classified as narcotics.

###### Art.508.‐ Whoever has illegally produced, imported, manufactured or exported

narcotics is punished by ten to fifteen years of penal servitude and a fine of up to one million Burundian francs.

The penalties provided for in the first paragraph are doubled when the acts are committed by an organized gang.

The penalties are incurred even when the various acts constituting the elements of the offense were accomplished in different countries.

###### Art.509.‐ Are punished by penal servitude of one to five years and a fine of

fifty thousand to one million Burundian francs those who have facilitated the use of narcotics by others, for a fee or free of charge, either by providing premises for this purpose or by any other means.

The guilty parties are punished by double the penalties stated in the preceding paragraph if the perpetrators are affiliated with an organized gang.

The same penalties are pronounced against those who, by means of fictitious prescriptions or prescriptions of complacency, have had said substances delivered to them and against those who, knowing the fictitious or complacent nature of these prescriptions, have, upon presentation of these prescriptions, delivered narcotics.

If the use or delivery of these narcotics was made to minors under eighteen years of age, the penalty of penal servitude is increased from ten to twenty years.

###### Art.510.‐ Are punished by penal servitude of one to five years, and a fine of

fifty thousand to one hundred thousand Burundian francs, or one of these penalties only, those who have, in an illicit manner, consumed or possessed one of the substances classified as narcotics.

###### Art.511.‐ Are punished with the penalties provided by the preceding article:

1° Those who, by any means, have incited one of the offenses punished by articles 504 to 508 even though this incitement has not been followed by effect, or who have presented them in a favorable light;

2° Those who, by any means, have incited, even though this incitement has not been followed by effect, the use of substances presented as having the effects of narcotic substances or plants.

###### Art.512.‐ In all cases provided for in articles 506 to 510, the court orders the

confiscation of substances or plants classified as narcotics and the confiscation of materials and installations used for the consumption, manufacture and transport of said substances or plants.

The court orders the destruction of the crops and confiscated substances or plants. The authority provides ex officio, at the expense of the offenders, for the destruction of these crops.

###### Art.513.‐ For any offense of consumption of narcotics, the judge orders the

socio-judicial monitoring as a complementary penalty in accordance with the provisions of articles 78 to 81.

##### Section 2 ‐ Public intoxication and nocturnal disturbance

###### Art.514.‐ Is punished with a fine of ten thousand to twenty thousand Burundian francs, whoever has

been found in a manifest state of intoxication in streets, squares, paths, drinking establishments, theaters or other public places, as well as in unfenced places on which the public can have direct view.

###### Art.515.‐ Are punished with a fine of twenty thousand to fifty thousand Burundian francs, the

sellers of beverages as well as their employees who have served drinks to manifestly intoxicated persons or who have received them in their establishments.

###### Art.516.‐ Are punished with one month to two months and a fine of fifty thousand to one hundred

Are punished with the same penalties, nightclub operators who admit into their establishments minors under eighteen years of age not accompanied by their parents or guardians.

###### Art.517.‐ Sellers of beverages who have violated the regulatory provisions

in this matter are punished with a fine of twenty thousand to one hundred thousand Burundian francs.

###### Art.518.‐ In case of recidivism, the court may order the temporary or

permanent closure of the establishment.

###### Art.519.‐ Is punished with penal servitude of one month maximum and a fine of ten

thousand to fifty thousand Burundian francs or only one of these penalties, whoever has made himself guilty of noise and nocturnal disturbance likely to disturb the tranquility of the inhabitants.

##### Section 3 ‐ Prohibited beverages

###### Art.520.‐ Constitute prohibited beverages within the meaning of the provisions of this

section alcoholic or non-alcoholic beverages manufactured artisanally at the local level or imported, classified as such in application of the provisions of the order of the Minister responsible for public health.

###### Art.521.‐ The importation or manufacture, purchase, possession or circulation

with a view to sale, the sale or free offer of beverages specified in the preceding article is punished with penal servitude of one year to two years and a fine of five hundred thousand to two million Burundian francs.

The products and equipment used to commit the offense as well as the beverages already manufactured are seized and immediately destroyed. The record of destruction is filed in the case file.

In case of recidivism, an imprisonment of four years may additionally be pronounced.

###### Art.522.‐ The consumers of beverages specified in article520 are punished with

penal servitude of three months to six months and a fine of ten thousand to one hundred thousand Burundian francs.

###### Art.523.‐ The penalty is doubled if the offenses provided for in articles 521 and 522

are committed in the exercise or on the occasion of the exercise of their functions by a person holding public authority or entrusted with a public service mission.

##### Section 4 ‐ Vagrancy and exploitation of begging

###### Art.524.‐ Is a vagrant, any person who wanders without exercising a profession or

trade, without possessing means of subsistence and who does not provide proof of a certain domicile.

The vagrant is punished with penal servitude of fourteen days to two months and a fine of five to ten thousand Burundian francs or only one of these penalties.

###### Art.525.‐ Any able-bodied person who exploits charity as a professional beggar

or one who, through idleness, drunkenness or moral dissoluteness, lives in a habitual state of begging, is punished with fourteen days to two months and a fine of five to ten thousand

###### Art.526.‐ The exploitation of begging is the act of anyone in whatever manner

whatsoever:

1° Organizing the begging of others for profit;

2° Drawing profit from the begging of others, deriving benefits from it or receiving subsidies from a person habitually engaged in begging;

3° Hiring, enticing or diverting a person and delivering them to begging or exerting pressure on them to beg or continue to do so.

###### Art.527.‐ The exploitation of begging is punished with three to five years of penal servitude

and a fine of one hundred thousand to five hundred thousand Burundian francs or only one of these penalties.

The exploitation of begging is punished with five to ten years of penal servitude and a fine of one hundred thousand to five hundred thousand Burundian francs when it is committed: 1° Against a minor;

2° A person whose particular vulnerability, due to their age, an illness, an infirmity, a physical or mental deficiency or a state of pregnancy, is apparent or known to its perpetrator;

3° Against several persons;

4° Against a person who has been incited to engage in begging either outside the territory of the Republic, or upon their arrival on the territory of the Republic; 5° By a legitimate, natural or adoptive ascendant of the person who begs or by a person who has authority over them or abuses the authority conferred upon them by their functions; 6° With the use of coercion, violence or fraudulent maneuvers on the person engaged in begging, on the family or on the person being in habitual relationship with them;

7° By several persons acting as perpetrators or accomplices, without constituting an organized gang.

The exploitation of the begging of others is punished with a penalty of penal servitude of ten to fifteen years and a fine of one hundred thousand to five hundred thousand Burundian francs when it is committed by an organized gang.

### Titre 8 ‐ Offenses against the family and public morality

#### Chapitre 1 ‐ Offenses against the order of families

##### Section 1 ‐ Abortion

###### Art.528.

‐ Whoever, through food, beverages, medications, has, intentionally, caused a woman to abort, outside the cases provided for by law, is punished with penal servitude of one year to two years and a fine of twenty thousand to fifty thousand Burundian francs.

###### Art.529.

‐ When the abortion has been caused by violence exercised voluntarily, but without intention to produce it, the guilty party is punished with six months to two years of penal servitude and a fine of fifty thousand to one hundred thousand Burundian francs. If the violence has been committed with premeditation and with knowledge of the victim's condition, the penalty is five to ten years of penal servitude and a fine of fifty thousand to one hundred thousand Burundian francs.

###### Art.530.‐ Anyone who has incited a pregnant woman to terminate her pregnancy shall be punished with penal servitude of two months to one year and a fine of ten thousand to fifty thousand Burundian francs.

###### Art.531.‐ If the offenders practice a medical or paramedical profession or are studying to obtain the diploma granting the right to practice such a profession, they shall be punished with penal servitude of two to five years and a fine of fifty thousand to five hundred thousand Burundian francs.

###### Art.532.‐ If the abortive procedures have caused the death of the woman, the persons cited in the preceding article shall be punished with twenty years of penal servitude.

###### Art.533.

‐ A woman who has voluntarily procured an abortion for herself shall be punished with penal servitude of one year to two years and a fine of twenty thousand to fifty thousand Burundian francs.

###### Art.534.‐ The penal sanctions provided for in the preceding articles shall not be applicable when the pregnancy has been terminated by a qualified physician, with the written consent of the pregnant person and upon the conforming opinion of a second qualified physician, with a view to averting a danger impossible to avert otherwise and threatening the life of the mother or seriously threatening her health with a serious and permanent injury.

If the pregnant person is incapable of expressing her will, the written consent of her legal representative is required.

In the exercise of public prosecution and upon any conviction pursuant to the provisions of articles 531 to 533, account shall be taken of the social requirements of the environment in which the act was committed.

There is no offense when the pregnancy has been terminated by decision of two physicians in case of emergency or impossibility of obtaining the expression of the will of the pregnant person or of the person authorized to represent her.

##### Section 2 ‐ Offenses against the child

###### Art.535.‐ For the purposes of this section, the term child means any person under eighteen years of age.

###### Art.536.‐ Those who have exposed, caused to be exposed, abandoned or caused to be abandoned a child or an incapable person unable to protect himself by reason of his physical or mental condition, shall be punished for that fact alone:

1° With two months to one year of penal servitude and a fine of twenty thousand Burundian francs if the act took place in a non-isolated place;

2° With one year to three years of penal servitude and a fine of fifty thousand Burundian francs if the act took place in an isolated place.

These penalties shall be doubled if the offenders are the ascendants or are legally charged with the custody of the child or the incapable person.

Exposure or abandonment that has resulted in mutilation or disability shall be punished with ten years of penal servitude.

The offender shall be punished with twenty years of penal servitude if the exposure or abandonment has been followed by death.

###### Art.537.‐ Anyone who has abducted or caused to be abducted, diverted or caused to be diverted, removed or caused to be removed children from the places where they had been placed by those having parental authority over them or by persons to whom they had been entrusted, shall be punished with one year to five years of penal servitude and a fine of ten thousand to one hundred thousand Burundian francs.

The penal servitude shall be increased to five to ten years if the acts were committed with violence, fraud or threats.

It shall be increased to ten years to twenty years if the offenders acted with the purpose of obtaining a ransom or obtaining the execution of an order or a condition.

If the abduction has been followed by the death of the child, the offenders shall be punished with servitude The father or mother who has abducted or caused to be abducted, removed or caused to be removed a child without the consent of the other shall be punished with the same penalties.

The father or any other person who abducts or causes to be abducted, removes or causes to be removed a child still at the mother's breast, without the consent of the child's mother, shall be punished with five to ten years of penal servitude.

###### Art.538.‐ The act, by a legitimate, natural or adoptive father or mother, of evading his legal obligations shall be punished with six months to one year of penal servitude and a fine of one hundred thousand to two hundred thousand Burundian francs or one of these penalties only.

The penal servitude shall be doubled if the act of evading his obligations had compromised the health, safety, morality or education of his child.

###### Art.539.‐ Anyone who has, with fraudulent intent, obtained for himself an adoption contravening the provisions of the law on adoption shall be punished with penal servitude of two to five years and a fine of one hundred thousand to five hundred thousand Burundian francs.

###### Art.540.‐ Any person who has intervened as an intermediary in obtaining an adoption for another without being a member of an organization previously approved for this purpose, has obtained an adoption contravening the provisions of the law on the matter, shall be punished with the same penalties.

###### Art.541.‐ The act of directly inciting a child to commit an unlawful act or likely to compromise his health or his morality or his development shall be punished with two to five years of penal servitude and a fine of ten thousand to twenty thousand Burundian francs.

###### Art.542.‐ Anyone who has used, recruited or offered a child for purposes of prostitution, production of pornographic material or shows shall be punished with penal servitude of three years to five years and a fine of one hundred thousand to five hundred thousand Burundian francs.

###### Art.543.‐ Any person who is guilty of an act or a transaction concerning the transfer of a child to someone in exchange for remuneration or any other advantage shall be punished with five to ten years of penal servitude and a fine of twenty thousand to fifty thousand Burundian francs.

###### Art.544.‐ Any person who has used a child for sexual activities in exchange for remuneration or any other form of advantage shall be punished with the penalties provided for in the preceding article.

###### Art.545.‐ Anyone who has used a child for work which, by its nature or the conditions under which it is carried out, is likely to harm his health, his safety or his morality, shall be punished with penal servitude of three to five years and a fine of fifty thousand to one hundred thousand Burundian francs.

###### Art.546.‐ The penalties provided for in articles 542 to 545 of this section shall be doubled:

1° When the offense is committed by a legitimate, natural or adoptive ascendant or by any other person having authority over the victim;

2° When the offense is committed by a person who abuses the authority conferred upon him by his functions;

3° When the offense is committed by several persons acting as principals or accomplices.

###### Art.547.‐ When a decision has been made regarding the custody of a child by enforceable court decision, the father, mother or any other person who does not present the child to those who have the right to claim him, or who removes him from those to whom his custody has been entrusted or from the places where they had placed him, shall be punished with one month to one year of penal servitude and a fine of twenty thousand to fifty thousand Burundian francs or one of these

###### Art.548.‐ Are punished with one to five years of penal servitude, those who have attributed to a

woman who was not pregnant the child born of another woman, to make her obtain civil status to which she had no right.

Are punished with the same penalties, those who have substituted one child for another or who have tried to prevent proof of civil status of the child, have concealed the birth of a child or have passed it off as dead.

The same penalties are applied to those who have given the assignment to commit the acts mentioned under this article insofar as this assignment has been executed or failed through the intervention of a third person.

The penalties provided in the preceding paragraphs are doubled, if the person has acted on the occasion of the exercise of their functions.

###### Paragraphe 1 ‐ Adultery

###### Art.549.‐ Is qualified as adultery, the sexual union of a person legally married and

whose marriage is not dissolved, with a person other than their spouse.

###### Art.550.‐ The spouse convicted of adultery is punished with a fine of twenty thousand to one hundred

###### Art.551.‐ The penalty set forth in the preceding article is applied to the accomplice.

###### Art.552.‐ The prosecution or conviction for adultery can only take place upon complaint

###### Paragraphe 2 ‐ Polygamy and polyandry

###### Art.553.‐ Whoever, being engaged in the bonds of marriage, has contracted one or

more others, before the dissolution of the previous one, shall be punished for polygamy or polyandry, with penal servitude of six months to two years and a fine of twenty thousand to one hundred thousand Burundian francs.

###### Art.554.‐ The spouse convicted of having maintained a male concubine or a female concubine is

The prosecution or conviction can only take place upon complaint of the spouse claiming to be offended. The complainant may in any case, by withdrawal of their complaint, stop the proceedings.

##### Section 4 ‐ Offences against family morality

###### Paragraphe 1 ‐ Incest

###### Art.555.‐ Are considered as incest and punished with penal servitude of two to

five years, sexual relations between:

1° Relatives in direct descending and ascending line, whether the bonds of kinship are legitimate, natural or adoptive;

2° Brothers and sisters of the whole blood, of the half blood through the father or through the mother;

3° A person and a child of their brothers or sisters of the whole blood, of the half blood through the father or through the mother, or with a descendant thereof;

4° The stepfather or stepmother and the descendant of the other spouse.

In all cases, if incest is committed by an adult with a minor under eighteen years of age, the penalty incurred by the perpetrator is that provided for rape with violence.

The conviction pronounced against the perpetrator of the offence includes:

1° Loss of parental authority or legal guardianship;

2° Prohibition of civic, civil and family rights;

3° Publication of the conviction;

4° Presentation of the convicted person to the public.

###### Paragraphe 2 ‐ Abandonment of family

###### Art.556.‐ Are punished with penal servitude not exceeding two months and a fine

of twenty thousand to fifty thousand Burundian francs or one of these penalties only:

1° The father or mother of a family who abandons without serious reason, for more than two months, the family residence and evades all or part of the moral or material obligations resulting from parental authority or legal guardianship; the period of two months can only be interrupted by a return to the home implying the will to definitively resume family life;

2° The spouse who, without valid reason, voluntarily abandons for more than two months their spouse and who refuses to provide for the maintenance and assistance that spouses owe each other according to the relevant provisions of the Code of Persons and Family;

3° The husband who, without serious reason, voluntarily abandons for more than two months his wife, knowing her to be pregnant;

4° The father or mother, whether or not forfeiture of parental authority is pronounced against them, who compromises by ill-treatment, by pernicious examples of habitual drunkenness or notorious misconduct, by lack of care or by lack of necessary direction, either the health, or the safety, or the morality of their children or one or more of them.

###### Art.557.‐ Is punished with two months to six months of penal servitude and a fine of ten

thousand to fifty thousand Burundian francs or one of these penalties only, whoever, having been convicted by a judicial decision with force of res judicata, to provide maintenance to their spouse, descendants or ascendants has voluntarily remained more than two months without paying the installments.

Failure to pay is presumed voluntary until proven otherwise. Insolvency resulting from habitual misconduct, laziness, drunkenness or commitments made after seizing the court is in no case a valid ground of excuse for the debtor.

##### Section 5 ‐ Domestic violence

###### Art.558.‐ Whoever subjects their spouse, child or any other person living under

the same roof to cruel, inhuman or degrading treatment is punished with penal servitude of three years to five years and a fine of fifty thousand Burundian francs.

###### Art.559.‐ Whoever compels a woman to conceive and to carry a pregnancy to term

is punished with penal servitude of three to five years. Is punished with the same penalties whoever forces a woman to abort.

###### Art.560.‐ Is punished with penal servitude of one year to two years any person who expels from

the family home their spouse, child or any person in their custody who is not able to take care of themselves.

#### Chapitre 2 ‐ Offences against public morals

##### Section 1 ‐ Prostitution

###### Art.561.‐ Constitutes an act of prostitution the act of giving one's body to the pleasure of others

and making it a profession.

###### Paragraphe 1 ‐ Incitement to debauchery and prostitution

###### Art.562.‐ Is punished with one year to five years of penal servitude and a fine of fifty

thousand to two hundred thousand Burundian francs whoever has committed an offence against morals by inciting, facilitating or favoring, to satisfy the passions of others, the debauchery, corruption, prostitution of persons of either sex aged or apparently aged over twenty-one years.

The penalty is increased to five to ten years, if the person who has been subjected to debauchery, corruption or prostitution is aged or apparently aged under twenty-one years. The age of the victim may be determined by medical examination in the absence of civil status.

###### Art.563.‐ The penalties set forth in the first paragraph of the preceding article are applicable to:

1° Whoever has recruited, enticed or diverted, with a view to debauchery or prostitution, another person of legal age or minor, even consenting;

2° Whoever maintains, for the same purposes, a person of legal age or minor, even consenting.

###### Art.564.‐ Whoever, by threat, pressure, maneuver or any other means, obstructs the action of prevention, assistance or rehabilitation undertaken by qualified organizations in favor of persons engaging in prostitution or at risk of prostitution, is punished by six months to five years of penal servitude and a fine of fifty thousand to two hundred thousand Burundian francs.

###### Paragraphe 2 ‐ Procuring

###### Art.565.‐ Whoever, directly or through an intermediary, directs, manages, or knowingly finances or contributes to financing a house of prostitution, is punished by two to five years of penal servitude and a fine of one hundred thousand to one million Burundian francs.

###### Paragraphe 3 ‐ Facilitation of prostitution

###### Art.566.‐ Any person who, in any form, derives profit from prostitution, shares the proceeds of prostitution of an adult or minor, even if the latter is consenting, or receives subsidies from a person habitually engaging in prostitution, is punished by one year to five years of penal servitude and a fine of twenty thousand to two hundred thousand Burundian francs.

###### Art.567.‐ Any person who, in any manner, knowingly aids, assists or protects the prostitution of another, or soliciting for prostitution, is punished by penal servitude of one year to three years and a fine of twenty thousand to fifty thousand Burundian francs.

###### Art.568.

‐ The penalties provided for in the preceding article are applicable to any person who acts as an intermediary, in any capacity, between persons habitually engaging in prostitution and individuals who exploit or remunerate the prostitution of another.

###### Art.569.‐ Whoever knowingly gives or takes on lease, in whole or in part, any building or premises for the purposes of the prostitution of another is punished by two years to five years of penal servitude and a fine of fifty thousand to one million francs

###### Art.570.‐ The penalties provided for by the provisions of this section are doubled when one of the following circumstances is established in the case:

1° The offense was committed against a person under eighteen years of age;
2° The offense was committed against a non-consenting person;

3° The offense was committed against a person whose particular vulnerability due to their age, an illness, an infirmity, a physical or mental deficiency or a state of pregnancy, is apparent or known to its perpetrator;

4° The offense was committed by an organized gang or by several persons acting as perpetrators, co-perpetrators or accomplices;

5° The perpetrator of the offense acted by deceit, threats, or violence;

6° The offense was committed by an ascendant of the victim;

7° The offense was committed by a person having authority over the victim;
8° The offense was committed by a servant of the victim;

9° The offense was committed by a public official or a Minister of religion.

###### Paragraphe 4 ‐ Soliciting

###### Art.571.‐ Constitutes an act of soliciting, the fact for a person engaging in prostitution to accost clients or to engage in, in public, acts of any nature whatsoever, with a view to attracting clients.

It is punished by a penalty of penal servitude of one month to six months and a fine of five thousand to twenty thousand Burundian francs or one of these penalties only.

##### Section 2 ‐ Indecent assault

###### Art.572.‐ Constitutes an act of indecent assault, any act of a sexual nature contrary to Burundian morals exercised intentionally and directly on a person.

The assault exists as soon as there is commencement of execution.

###### Art.573.‐ Indecent assault committed without violence, deceit or threats on persons of either sex is punished by penal servitude of six months to two years and a fine of twenty thousand to fifty thousand Burundian francs.

###### Art.574.‐ Indecent assault committed with violence, deceit or threats on persons of either sex is punished by penal servitude of one year to five years and a fine of fifty thousand to one hundred thousand Burundian francs.

###### Art.575.‐ Any indecent assault committed without violence, deceit or threats on the person or with the aid of the person of a child aged or apparently aged under eighteen years, is punished by penal servitude of five to fifteen years and a fine of fifty thousand to two hundred thousand Burundian francs.

The age of the child may be determined in particular by medical examination in the absence of civil status records.

If the assault was committed with violence, deceit or threats, the penalty of penal servitude is five years to twenty years.

###### Art.576.‐ The minimum of the penalties imposed by the articles of this section is doubled:

1° When the assault is committed by a legitimate, natural or adoptive ascendant or descendant of the victim;

2° When the assault is committed by a person having authority over the victim;
3° When the assault is committed by a person who has abused the authority conferred by their functions;

4° When the assault was committed by doctors, surgeons, midwives, towards persons entrusted to their care;

5° When the indecent assault is committed on a vulnerable person by reason of an illness, an infirmity or a physical or mental deficiency or a state of pregnancy,
6° When the indecent assault is committed under threat of a weapon;

7° When the assault is committed by a Minister of religion.

##### Section 3 ‐ Rape

###### Art.577.‐ Is deemed rape with violence any act of sexual penetration, of whatever nature and by whatever means, committed by a criminally responsible person on a minor under eighteen years of age even if consenting.

Is also deemed rape with violence, the mere fact of carnal intercourse committed on a minor under eighteen years of age, even if consenting.

Domestic rape is punished by penal servitude of eight days and a fine of ten thousand to fifty thousand Burundian francs or one of these penalties only.

###### Art.578.‐ Commits rape, either by means of violence or serious threats or by constraint against a person, directly or through an intermediary, or by surprise, by psychological pressure, or on the occasion of a coercive environment, or by abusing a person who, by reason of an illness, by the alteration of their faculties or by any other accidental cause would have lost the use of their senses or would have been deprived thereof by some artifice, and even if the victim is the spouse of that person:

1° Any man, whatever his age, who introduces his sexual organ, even superficially into that of a woman or any woman, whatever her age, who has compelled a man to introduce, even superficially, his sexual organ into hers;
2° Any man who has caused to penetrate, even superficially, by the anal route, the mouth or any other orifice of the body of a woman or a man his sexual organ, any other part of the body or any other object whatsoever;

3° Any person who introduces, even superficially, any other part of the body or any object whatsoever into the female sex;

4° Any person who compels a man or woman to penetrate, even superficially, their anal orifice, their mouth with a sexual organ;

Is punished by five years to fifteen years of penal servitude and a fine of fifty thousand francs to one hundred thousand francs.

###### Art.579.‐ Rape is punished by fifteen to twenty‐five years of penal servitude and a

fine of fifty thousand to two hundred thousand Burundian francs:

1° When it is committed against a minor from fifteen to eighteen years;

2° When it is committed by a legitimate, natural or adoptive ascendant or descendant, by a brother or sister, by a stepfather or stepmother of the victim;

3° When it is committed by a person in the service of the victim;

4° When it is committed by a person who abuses the authority conferred by their functions;

5° When it is committed by an educator;

6° When rape is committed by a Minister of religion;

7° When it is committed by doctors, surgeons, obstetricians or other medical personnel towards persons entrusted to their care;

8° When it is committed against a vulnerable person due to their age, an illness, an infirmity, a physical or mental deficiency or a state of pregnancy, which is apparent or known to the perpetrator.

###### Art.580.‐ Rape is punished by twenty to thirty years and a fine of one hundred thousand to five hundred

thousand Burundian francs:

1° When it is committed by several persons acting as perpetrators or accomplices;

2° When the perpetrator is carrying a weapon;

3° When it has caused the victim serious impairment of their health and/or left serious physical and/or psychological sequelae, notably mutilation, permanent disability or transmission of a disease;

4° When it is committed with use or threat of a weapon;

5° When rape has been committed against a child from twelve to fifteen years; 6° When it is committed in public.

###### Art.581.‐ Rape is punished by life penal servitude:

1° When the perpetrator knew they were carrying a sexually transmitted disease known to be incurable;

2° When rape has resulted in the death of the victim;

3° When rape has been committed against a child under 12 years;

4° When rape has been preceded, accompanied or followed by acts of torture or barbarity.

###### Art.582.‐ The penalties provided for by the provisions of this section are

non-reducible, imprescriptible, non-amnistiable and non-pardonable.

###### Art.583.‐ The official capacity of the perpetrator of an offence relating to sexual violence

cannot in any case exempt them from responsibility or constitute a ground for reduction of the sentence.

###### Art.584.‐ Hierarchical order or command from a legitimate civil or

military authority in no way exempts the perpetrator of an offence relating to sexual violence from their responsibility.

###### Art.585.‐ With regard to offences of indecent assault and rape, the judge

shall impose, in addition to the principal sentence, at least one of the following complementary sentences:

1° Publication of the conviction;

2° Presentation of the convicted person to the public;

3° Prohibition from exercising civic, civil and family rights;

4° Prohibition from residence;

5° Socio‐judicial monitoring.

If the judge imposes the sentence provided for in point 1°, the identity of the victim is neither published nor disclosed to the public.

##### Section 4 ‐ Sexual harassment

###### Art.586.‐ Constitutes an act of sexual harassment the act of using against another person

orders, threats or physical or psychological coercion, or serious pressure, with the aim of obtaining favours of a sexual nature, by abusing the authority conferred by one's functions.

It is punished by one month to two years of penal servitude and one hundred thousand to five hundred thousand Burundian francs fine.

If the victim of harassment is a minor under eighteen years the sentences are doubled.

##### Section 5 ‐ Public outrages to good morals, zoophilia and

homosexuality

###### Paragraph 1 ‐ Public outrages to good morals

###### Art.587.‐ Whoever has displayed, sold or distributed songs, pamphlets or other

writings, printed or not, figures, images, emblems or other objects contrary to good morals, is sentenced to a fine of fifty thousand to one hundred thousand Burundian francs.

Is punished by the same sentences, whoever has, with a view to trade or distribution, held, imported or caused to be imported, transported or caused to be transported, handed over to a transport or distribution agent, advertised by any means of publicity songs, pamphlets, writings, figures, images, emblems or objects contrary to good morals.

In the cases provided for by the preceding paragraphs, the author of the writing, figure, image, whoever printed or reproduced them, the manufacturers of the emblem or object are punished by a fine of fifty thousand to one hundred thousand Burundian francs.

Whoever has sung, read, recited, made heard or uttered obscenities in meetings or public places before several persons and in a manner to be heard by these persons, is punished by a fine of ten thousand to twenty thousand Burundian francs.

###### Art.588.‐ Whoever has publicly outraged morals by actions that offend

decency is punished by a fine of fifty thousand francs to one hundred thousand francs.

###### Paragraph 2 ‐ Zoophilia

###### Art.589.‐ Whoever has voluntarily had sexual relations with an animal is punished

by a sentence of penal servitude of one year to three years and a fine of fifty thousand to Is punished by a sentence of penal servitude of five years to ten years and a fine of twenty thousand to fifty thousand Burundian francs whoever has forced a person to have sexual relations with an animal. The sentence is doubled if the person forced is a minor.

###### Paragraph 3 ‐ Homosexuality

###### Art.590.‐ Whoever has sexual relations with a person of the same sex is

punished by penal servitude of three months to two years and a fine of fifty thousand to one hundred thousand Burundian francs or one of these sentences only.

### Title 9 ‐ Offences against State security

#### Chapter 1 ‐ Offences against the external security of the State

##### Section 1 ‐ Treason and espionage

###### Art.591.‐ Is guilty of treason and punished by life penal servitude, any

Burundian who bears arms against Burundi.

###### Art.592.‐ Is guilty of treason and punished by life penal servitude, any

Burundian who:

1° Maintains intelligence with a foreign power or with its agents, to engage this power to undertake hostilities against Burundi or to provide it with the means;

2° Delivers to a foreign power or to its agents defence works, posts, ports, warehouses, materials, munitions, vessels, buildings or aircraft belonging to Burundi;

3° With a view to harming national defence, destroys or damages a ship, an aircraft, materials, supplies, construction or any installation, or who, with the same aim, introduces into them either before or after their completion, defects of a nature to damage them or to cause an accident.

###### Art.593.‐ Is guilty of treason and punished by life penal servitude, any

Burundian who, in time of war:

1° Incites military personnel or sailors to enter the service of a foreign power, facilitates the means for them or makes enlistments for a power at war with Burundi;

2° Maintains intelligence with a foreign power or with agents with a view to favouring the undertakings of this power against Burundi;

3° Knowingly participates in an undertaking to demoralize the Army or the Nation having the object of harming national defence.

###### Art.594.‐ Is guilty of treason and punished with penal servitude for life, any

Burundian who, in time of war:

1° Delivers to a foreign power or its agents, in any form and by any means whatsoever, information, object, document or process that must be kept secret in the interest of national defense;

2° Secures, by any means whatsoever, possession of such information, object, document or process, with a view to delivering it to a foreign power or its agents; 3° Destroys or allows to be destroyed such information, object, document or process with a view to favoring a foreign power.

###### Art.595.

‐ Is guilty of espionage and punished with penal servitude for life, any foreigner who commits one of the acts referred to in the two preceding articles.

###### Art.596.‐ Without prejudice to the application of articles 37 and 38, are punished with

penal servitude of one to five years and a fine of fifty thousand to one hundred thousand Burundian francs:

1° The offer or proposal to commit one of the offenses provided for in articles 592 to 593

2° The acceptance of such offer or proposal.

##### Section 2 ‐ Other offenses against the external security of the State

###### Art.597.‐ Is punished with penal servitude of two to ten years and a fine of

fifty thousand to two hundred thousand Burundian francs, anyone who, without intent of treason or espionage:

1° Secures, being without authority, possession of information, object, document or process that must be kept secret in the interest of national defense or whose knowledge may lead to the discovery of a national defense secret; 2° Destroys, removes, allows to be destroyed or removed, or allows to be reproduced such information, object, document or process;

3° Brings or allows to be brought to the knowledge of an unauthorized person or the public such information, object, document or process, or has extended its disclosure.

###### Art.598.‐ Is punished with penal servitude of six months to one year and a fine of

fifty thousand to one hundred thousand Burundian francs, anyone who, without intent of treason or espionage, has brought to the knowledge of an unauthorized person or the public, military information not made public by the competent authority and whose disclosure is manifestly of a nature to harm national defense.

###### Art.599.‐ Is punished with penal servitude of one to five years and a fine of

fifty thousand to one hundred thousand Burundian francs, anyone who:

1° Enters under disguise or a false name, or by concealing their status or nationality, into a defense work, post, depot or military warehouse, into a warship or commercial vessel employed for national defense, into a military establishment or into an establishment or shipyard of interest to national defense;

2° Even without disguising themselves, or without concealing their name, status or nationality, has organized in a clandestine manner any means of correspondence or remote transmission of a nature to harm national defense.

###### Art.600.‐ Is punished with penal servitude of one to five years and a fine of

fifty thousand to one hundred thousand Burundian francs, anyone who maintains with the agents of a foreign power, communications of a nature to harm the military, political or economic situation of Burundi.

###### Art.601.‐ Is punished with penal servitude of one to five years and a fine of

fifty thousand francs to one hundred thousand francs, anyone who, in time of war: 1° Maintains, without Government authorization, correspondence or relations with the agents of an enemy power;

2° Conducts directly or through intermediaries, acts of trade with the subjects or agents of an enemy power, in defiance of the prohibitions enacted.

###### Art.602.‐ Is punished with penal servitude of one to five years and a fine of

fifty thousand to one hundred thousand Burundian francs, anyone who has, through hostile acts not approved by the Government, exposed Burundi to hostilities on the part of a foreign power.

If hostilities have ensued, the penal servitude is five to twenty years.

###### Art.603.‐ The penalties provided for in articles 593 to 596, 600 and 601, 1° are doubled

if the perpetrator of the offense is a Burundian.

The penalty provided for in article 597 is penal servitude of thirty years if the perpetrator of the offense is a Burundian.

##### Section 3 ‐ Mercenarism

###### Art.604.‐ The term mercenary means any person:

1° Who is specially recruited within the country or abroad to fight in an armed conflict;

2° Who, in fact, takes a direct part in the hostilities;

3° Who takes part in the hostilities with a view to obtaining a personal advantage and who is effectively promised, by a party to the conflict or on its behalf, material remuneration;

4° Who is neither a national of a party to the conflict nor a resident of the territory controlled by a party to the conflict;

5° Who is not a member of the armed forces of a party to the conflict;

6° Who has not been sent by a State other than a party to the conflict on official mission as a member of the armed forces of the said State.

###### Art.605.

‐ Is guilty of mercenarism and is punished with five to twenty years of penal servitude and a fine of fifty thousand to two hundred thousand Burundian francs, anyone who, with the aim of opposing armed violence to a process of self-determination, to the stability or to the territorial integrity of another State, has, on the national territory: 1° Sheltered, organized, financed, assisted, equipped, trained, supported or employed, in any way whatsoever, bands of mercenaries;

2° Enlisted, engaged in the said bands.

###### Art.606.

‐ The penalty of penal servitude for life is pronounced against any person who has assumed command of mercenaries, against one who has given them orders, or against one guilty of the crime of mercenarism directed against Burundi.

#### Chapter 2 ‐ Offenses against the internal security of the State

##### Section 1 ‐ Attacks and conspiracies against the Head of State

###### Art.607.‐ An attack against the life or against the person of the Head of State is punished with

If it has caused neither bloodshed, nor injury, nor illness, the attack against their person is punished with penal servitude of thirty years.

###### Art.608.‐ A conspiracy against the life or against the person of the Head of State is punished with

penal servitude of ten to fifteen years and a fine of two million to five million Burundian francs if any act has been committed to prepare its execution.

If there has been a proposal made and not accepted to form a conspiracy against the life or against the person of the Head of State, the one who made such a proposal is punished with penal servitude of one to five years and a fine of five hundred thousand to one million

##### Section 2 ‐ Attacks, conspiracies and other offenses against the authority of the State and

the integrity of the territory

###### Art.609.‐ An attack whose purpose has been either to destroy or change the constitutional

regime, or to incite citizens or inhabitants to arm themselves against the authority of the State or to arm themselves against each other, or to undermine the integrity of the national territory, is punished with penal servitude of thirty years.

###### Art.610.‐ A conspiracy formed for one of the purposes mentioned in the preceding article is punished

with penal servitude of ten to fifteen years and a fine of two million to five million Burundian francs if any act has been committed or begun to prepare its execution.

If there has been an unaccepted proposal to form a conspiracy to achieve one of the purposes mentioned in article 609, the one who made such a proposal is punished with penal servitude of one to five years and a fine of one million to two million Burundian francs,

###### Art.611.‐ Whoever, except in cases provided for in articles 613 and 614, has undertaken by any means whatsoever, to undermine the integrity of the national territory, is punished by penal servitude of five to ten years and a fine of one million to three million

###### Art.612.‐ Are punished by penal servitude of five to twenty years and a fine of one

million to five million Burundian francs, those who have raised or caused to be raised armed troops, engaged or enlisted, caused to be engaged or enlisted soldiers, or have provided them with arms or munitions, without order or authorization from the Government.

###### Art.613.‐ Are punished by penal servitude of five to twenty years and a fine of one

million to five million Burundian francs:

1° Those who, without right or legitimate reason, have taken any military command;

2° Those who, against the order of the Government, have retained such command; 3° Those who have kept their army or troop assembled after dismissal or separation thereof has been ordered.

##### Section 3 ‐ Attacks and conspiracies tending to bring about massacre, devastation and

pillage

###### Art.614.‐ The attack whose purpose has been to bring about massacre is punished by penal servitude

for life.

The attack whose purpose has been to bring about devastation or pillage is punished by penal servitude of fifteen to twenty years.

###### Art.615.‐ The conspiracy formed for one of the purposes mentioned in the preceding article is punished

by penal servitude of ten to fifteen years and a fine of one million to five million Burundian francs, if any act has been committed or begun to prepare its execution.

If there has been a proposal made and not accepted to form a conspiracy to achieve one of the ends mentioned in article 611, the one who made such proposal is punished by penal servitude of five to ten years and a fine of one million to two million

##### Section 4 ‐ Participation in armed bands

###### Art.616.‐ Is punished by the penalty of penal servitude for life, whoever, with a view to

disturbing the State by one of the attacks provided for in articles 609 and 611, by invasion, or the division of public or private properties, or by making attack or resistance against the public force acting against the perpetrators of these offenses, has placed himself at the head of armed bands or has exercised any command therein.

The same penalty is applied to those who have directed the association, raised or caused to be raised, organized or caused to be organized the bands.

###### Art.617.‐ Individuals forming part of the bands referred to in the preceding article, without

exercising any command therein and who have been apprehended at the location of the seditious gathering, are punished by penal servitude of ten to fifteen years and a fine of fifty thousand to two hundred thousand Burundian francs.

###### Art.618.‐ In the case where one of the attacks provided for in articles 609 and 612 has been committed

by an armed band, the penalty of penal servitude for life is applied without distinction of rank, to all individuals forming part of the band and who have been apprehended at the location.

Is punished by the same penalty, although not apprehended at the location, whoever has directed the sedition, or has exercised any command in the band.

###### Art.619.

‐ No penalty is pronounced, for the fact of sedition, against those who, having formed part of an armed band without exercising any command therein, withdrew at the first warning from civil or military authorities, or even thereafter, when they have been apprehended only outside the location of the seditious gathering, without offering resistance and without weapons.

They are punished in this case, only for the particular offenses they would have personally committed.

##### Section 5 ‐ Participation in an insurrectional movement

###### Art.620.‐ Are punished by penal servitude of two to ten years and a fine of

fifty thousand to one hundred thousand Burundian francs, individuals who, in an insurrectional movement:

1° Have made or helped to make barricades, entrenchments or any other works having the purpose of hindering or stopping the exercise of the public force;

2° Have prevented, by means of violence or threats, the intervention or gathering of the public force or who have provoked or facilitated the gathering of insurgents, either by the distribution of orders or proclamations, or by the carrying of flags or other rallying signs, or by any other means of appeal;

3° Have, for the purpose of making attack or resistance against the public force, invaded or occupied buildings, posts or other public establishments, inhabited or uninhabited houses. The penalty is the same with regard to the owner or tenant who, knowing the purpose of the insurgents, has provided them without constraint entry into said houses.

###### Art.621.‐ Are punished by penal servitude of five to twenty years and a fine of

fifty thousand to two hundred thousand Burundian francs, individuals who, in an insurrectional movement:

1° Have seized arms, munitions or materials of all kinds, either by means of violence or threats, or by the pillage of shops or public establishments, or by the disarming of agents of the public force;

2° Have carried apparent weapons or maintained caches of munitions.

Individuals who have made use of their weapons are punished by the penalty of servitude

###### Art.622.‐ Are punished by the penalty of penal servitude for life, those who have directed or

organized an insurrectional movement having made use of weapons.

##### Section 6 ‐ Other threats to the internal security of the State

###### Art.623.‐ Is punished by penal servitude of two to five years and a fine of

fifty thousand to two hundred thousand Burundian francs, the one who, for propaganda purposes, has distributed, put into circulation or exposed to public view, leaflets, bulletins or flags of foreign origin or inspiration of a nature to harm the national interest. Is punished by the same penalties, the one who has possessed such bulletins or flags with a view to distribution, circulation or display for propaganda purposes.

###### Art.624.‐ Is punished by penal servitude of one year to five years and a fine of one hundred

thousand to five hundred thousand Burundian francs, whoever receives from a foreign person or organization, directly or indirectly, in whatever form and under whatever title, gifts, presents, loans or other advantages, intended or used in whole or in part to conduct or remunerate in Burundi an activity or propaganda of a nature to undermine the integrity or independence of Burundi, or to shake the loyalty that citizens owe to the State and the Institutions of Burundi.

###### Art.625.‐ Is punished by penal servitude of one year to three years and a fine of

fifty thousand to two hundred thousand Burundian francs or one of these penalties only 1° The one who has publicly attacked the binding force of laws or directly provoked disobedience thereto;

2° The one who has knowingly spread false rumors of a nature to alarm the populations or to incite them against the public authorities or to civil war;

3° The one who, with a view to disturbing the public peace, has knowingly contributed to the publication, dissemination or reproduction by any means whatsoever, of false news or fabricated, falsified or falsely attributed documents to third parties; 4° The one who has displayed or caused to be displayed, in public places or places open to the public, drawings, posters, engravings, paintings, photographs, any objects or images of a nature to disturb the public peace.

###### Art.626.‐ Is punished with penal servitude of ten to twenty years and a fine of

fifty thousand to two hundred thousand Burundian francs anyone who has harmed the economy or national security by stealing, destroying, overturning or damaging, by any means whatsoever, in whole or in part, buildings, bridges, dikes, roads, railways, telegraph or telephone equipment or other constructions belonging to the State or to other state or para-state organs.

##### Section 7 ‐ Definitions relating to the provisions of sections 1 to 6

###### Art.627.‐ An attack exists as soon as there is a punishable attempt.

###### Art.628.‐ There is conspiracy as soon as the resolution to act has been decided between two or more

persons.

###### Art.629.‐ Included in the word "weapons" are all machines, instruments,

utensils or other sharp, piercing or blunt objects that one has seized to kill, injure or strike, even if one has not used them.

###### Art.630.‐ By "insurrectional movement" means a collective movement

that manifests itself, either by acts harming established powers and institutions, or by assaults against persons, devastation or pillage.

#### Chapitre 3 ‐ Common provisions to the two preceding chapters

###### Art.631.‐ Is punished with penal servitude of three months to two years and a fine of

fifty thousand to one hundred thousand Burundian francs, or only one of these penalties, anyone who, having knowledge of plans or acts of treason, espionage or other activities likely to harm national defense, attacks or conspiracies against the internal security of the State, does not report them to the military, administrative or judicial authorities from the moment they became aware of them.

###### Art.632.‐ In addition to the persons designated in Article 38, is punished as an accomplice

anyone, other than the perpetrator or accomplice:

1° Who provides without coercion and with knowledge of their intentions, subsidies, means of subsistence, accommodation, place of retreat or meeting to the perpetrators of offenses against the security of the State;

2° Who knowingly carries the correspondence of the perpetrators of such offenses, or knowingly facilitates in any way whatsoever, the search, concealment, transport or transmission of the object of the offense.

###### Art.633.‐ In addition to the persons designated in Article 320, is punished as a receiver

anyone, other than the perpetrator or accomplice of an offense against the security of the State:

1° Who knowingly conceals objects or instruments having served or intended to serve in committing the offense or objects, materials or documents obtained through the offense;

2° Who destroys, removes, conceals, hides or knowingly alters a public or private document likely to facilitate the investigation of the offense, the discovery of evidence or the punishment of its perpetrators.

In the case provided for in this article, the court may exempt from the penalty incurred the relatives or in-laws of the guilty party up to and including the fourth degree.

###### Art.634.‐ Is exempt from the penalty incurred anyone who, before execution or attempt of an

offense against the security of the State, first gives notice of it to the administrative or judicial authorities.

Exemption from penalty is also optional if the denunciation occurs after the commission or attempt of the offense but before the opening of proceedings.

Exemption from penalty is also optional with regard to the guilty party who, after the opening of proceedings, allows the arrest of the perpetrators and accomplices of the same offense, or other offenses of the same nature or gravity.

###### Art.635.‐ Confiscation of the object of the offense and of objects used to

commit it is always ordered.

Property that does not belong to the perpetrator of the offense is confiscated in case of proven complicity of the owner.

The remuneration received by the guilty party, or the amount of its value when the remuneration could not be seized, is declared acquired by the Treasury.

###### Art.636.‐ Any person guilty of treason, attack or conspiracy against the internal security

of the State is deprived for a period of five years of the right to vote and the right to stand for election.

#### Chapitre 4 ‐ Acts of terrorism and bioterrorism

###### Art.637.‐ Constitute acts of terrorism, the following intentional acts committed within

the framework of an individual or collective enterprise aimed at seriously disturbing public order by intimidation or terror:

1° Voluntary attacks on the life and integrity of the person, abduction and sequestration as well as the hijacking of aircraft, ship or any other means of transport;

2° Thefts, extortions, destructions, degradations and deteriorations;

3° The manufacture or possession of machines, murderous or explosive devices;

4° The production, sale, import or export of explosive substances;

5° The acquisition, possession, transport or illegitimate carrying of explosive substances or devices manufactured using said substances.

###### Art.638.‐ Also constitute acts of terrorism:

1° The act of introducing into the atmosphere, on the ground, in the subsoil or in the waters, of rivers or the lake and belonging to the territory of Burundi, a substance likely to endanger the health of human populations or animals, with the aim of seriously disturbing public order by intimidation and terror;

2° The act of participating in a group formed or in an agreement established with a view to preparation, characterized by one or more material acts, of one of the acts of terrorism listed in Article 637.

###### Art.639.‐ Is also qualified as an act of terrorism the act of financing a terrorist

enterprise, by providing, collecting or managing funds, securities or any property whatsoever or by giving advice for this purpose, with the intention of seeing these funds, securities or property used or knowing that they are intended to be used, in whole or in part, with a view to committing any one of the acts of terrorism provided for under this chapter, regardless of the possible occurrence of such an act.

###### Art.640.

‐ All acts of terrorism mentioned above, all acts of import, export, acquisition, possession, transfer, abandonment, manufacture, transport, transit and/or use of biological agents, chemical, radiological, nuclear and explosive weapons or materials with the aim of harming or killing human beings, animals or plants or with the intention to intimidate or frighten constitute acts of terrorism.

Biological agents include bacteria, viruses, molds, fungi, rickettsiae, toxins.

Radiological and nuclear elements include Alpha particles, Beta particles, Gamma radiation, neutrons.

###### Art.641.‐ An act of terrorism is punished with a penalty of penal servitude of ten to twenty

years and a fine of two hundred thousand to one million Burundian francs.

When this act has resulted in the death of one or more persons, it is punished with the penalty of life penal servitude.

The penalties provided for above are incompressible.

###### Art.642.‐ Natural or legal persons guilty of the offenses provided for by

this chapter also incur one of the following complementary penalties:

1° Deprivation of civil rights;

2° Prohibition from exercising a public function or from exercising a professional or social activity in the exercise or on the occasion of which the offense was committed;

3° Prohibition from residing when the perpetrator is a foreigner.

### Title 10 ‐ Transitional and Final Provisions

###### Art.643.‐ Regulatory acts adopted in execution of the law, regulations

of administration and police of the public authority and local authorities may not establish sanctions other than administrative fines:

1° Not exceeding a fine of fifty thousand Burundian francs for decrees; 2° Not exceeding a fine of twenty thousand Burundian francs, for ordinances; 3° Not exceeding a fine of five thousand Burundian francs, for acts of local authorities.

An exception is made to the preceding provisions with regard to administrative fines for offenses in the tax and customs domains, as well as in foreign exchange regulation or of an economic nature.

###### Art.644.‐ The penalties provided for by regulatory acts and regulations

of administration and police enacted prior to the entry into force of this Code are reduced, where necessary, to the maxima set by the preceding article.

###### Art.645.

‐ Misdemeanors and violations of decrees, orders, ordinances, decisions, regulations of administration and police, for which the law does not determine specific penalties are punished ex officio by penalties not exceeding the maxima of those provided for in Article 643 according to the distinctions made therein.

###### Art.646.‐ Specific laws of which certain criminal provisions have been incorporated

in this Code remain in force insofar as they are not contrary to this law.

###### Art.647.

‐ Prior provisions contrary to this law are repealed.

###### Art.648.‐ This law enters into force on the day of its promulgation.
