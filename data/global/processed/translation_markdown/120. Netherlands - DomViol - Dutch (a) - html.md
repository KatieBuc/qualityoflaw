Original title and preamble

Act of 9 October 2008, containing rules aimed at imposing a temporary house ban on persons who pose a serious threat of domestic violence

(Temporary House Ban Act)

We Beatrix, by the grace of God, Queen of the Netherlands, Princess of Orange-Nassau, etc. etc. etc.

All those who shall see or hear this read, greetings! do make known:

Whereas We have taken into consideration that it is desirable to introduce a measure whereby a person who poses a serious and immediate danger to the safety of one or more persons with whom that person shares a household can be temporarily imposed a house ban in order to safeguard the safety of these persons and to create a period in which measures can be taken to eliminate the threat of domestic violence;

So it is that We, having heard the Council of State, and in consultation with the States General, have approved and understood, as We approve and understand hereby:

## Article 1

In this Act, the following definitions apply: a. police officer: the police officer referred to in Article 2(a) of the Police Act 2012

, and the police officer referred to in Article 2(c) of that Act, insofar as he is charged with the performance of the police task; b. house ban: decision containing an order to immediately leave a specified dwelling and a prohibition to enter, stay at or be present in that dwelling and a prohibition to make contact with those who live in the same dwelling with the person to whom the decision is addressed or who stay there other than incidentally; c. person placed out of home: the person to whom a house ban has been imposed.

## Article 2

Create a permanent link

Show legislative technical information

Print the regulation section

Save the regulation section

The mayor may impose a house ban on a person if facts or circumstances show that that person's presence in the dwelling poses a serious and immediate danger to the safety of one or more persons who live with him in the dwelling or who stay there other than incidentally, or if on the basis of facts or circumstances there is a serious suspicion of this danger. The prohibition applies for a period of ten days, subject to extension in accordance with Article 9

. Further rules shall be laid down by or pursuant to general administrative order concerning the nature of the facts and circumstances that may give rise to imposing a house ban.

A house ban may only be imposed on an adult.

If the mayor intends to impose the house ban on account of child abuse or a serious suspicion thereof, he shall contact the advice and reporting centre for domestic violence and child abuse referred to in Article 4.1.1 of the Social Support Act 2015 in order to consult about the intention to impose a house ban.

The house ban shall contain at least: a. a description of the place and the duration for which it applies; b. the facts and circumstances that have given rise to imposing the house ban, and c. the names of the persons with respect to whom the prohibition to make contact applies. Further rules may be laid down by ministerial regulation with respect to the house ban.

The person placed out of home shall indicate where or in what manner he can be reached. If the person placed out of home cannot provide this immediately, he shall provide this within 24 hours after the house ban has been imposed to the mayor.

If the situation is so urgent that the house ban cannot be put in writing in advance, the house ban may be announced orally. The mayor shall nevertheless ensure that it is put in writing and made public promptly. If the place of residence of the person placed out of home is not known, publication may take place by depositing the house ban at the municipal secretariat.

The mayor shall immediately communicate the content of the house ban and the consequences of non-compliance for the person placed out of home to the person with whom the person placed out of home shares a household. The mayor shall also communicate the content of the house ban to the institution designated by the mayor for advice or assistance, and if the house ban is imposed on account of child abuse or a serious suspicion thereof to the foundation referred to in the third paragraph.

The mayor may in any case revoke the house ban if the person placed out of home has accepted an offer of assistance and this has been confirmed by the institution for advice or assistance designated pursuant to the eighth paragraph, and this acceptance also entails that the person placed out of home will not obstruct assistance to one or more persons who live in the dwelling with the person placed out of home or who stay there other than incidentally and will cooperate if this is requested of him by the institution for advice or assistance.

The mayor may grant a mandate or authorization respectively with respect to the powers and tasks referred to in Article 2, first, third, seventh and eighth paragraphs

, and Article 5, first paragraph

, to the assistant public prosecutor.

Article 10:9, first paragraph, of the General Administrative Law Act

The assistant public prosecutor shall immediately notify the mayor of a house ban imposed by him.

If the danger referred to in Article 2, paragraph 1

occurs, the mayor or a police officer is authorised, with a view to imposing the house ban, to enter the dwelling without the permission of the resident, insofar as this is reasonably necessary for the performance of his duties. The police officer demands the house key or house keys from the person removed from the house and is authorised, if necessary after having searched the person removed from the house for this purpose on their clothing, to take them from the person removed from the house in exchange for issuing a receipt of receipt. For the purpose of monitoring compliance with the house ban, police officers are authorised to enter any place where the person removed from the house is not allowed to be pursuant to the house ban, with the exception of a dwelling without the permission of the resident.

If the person removed from the house does not have a lawyer, and requests legal assistance upon request, the mayor shall notify the board of the legal aid council thereof, which shall appoint a lawyer for the person removed from the house.

If a request for interim relief as referred to in Article 8:81 of the General Administrative Law Act

is made against the house ban: a. in derogation from Article 8:83, paragraph 1, of that Act

, the person removed from the house shall be heard by the preliminary relief judge within three days; b. the preliminary relief judge shall, in derogation from Article 8:84, paragraph 1, of that Act, give judgment immediately after hearing the parties unless there are special circumstances, in which case the preliminary relief judge shall give judgment within 24 hours after the hearing of the case.

The court shall also take into account in its assessment of the house ban the facts and circumstances that have occurred after the imposition of the house ban.

In derogation from Articles 8:41, paragraph 1

, and

8:82, paragraph 1, of the General Administrative Law Act, no court fees shall be charged by the clerk.

The court shall give minors who belong to the household of the person removed from the house and who have reached the age of twelve years the opportunity to make their opinion known to him, unless the expeditious hearing of the case precludes this. He may give minors who have not yet reached the age of twelve years the opportunity to make their opinion known to him in a manner to be determined by him.

The clerk shall send a copy of the judgment or of the minutes of the oral judgment of the preliminary relief judge without delay, and a copy of the judgment or of the minutes of the oral judgment of the court within two weeks of the date of the judgment free of charge to: a. the spouse, registered partner, other cohabiting partner or other adult person with whom the person removed from the house shares the household; b. the police of the municipality where the house ban was imposed; c. if minors are involved: the advice and reporting centre for domestic violence and child abuse, referred to in Article 4.1.1 of the Social Support Act 2015

The clerk shall inform the person removed from the house, the lawyer of the person removed from the house and the mayor without delay orally of the judgment. The mayor shall inform the persons referred to in paragraph 1, under a, insofar as they were not present at the hearing at which the judgment was given, and the police without delay orally of the judgment.

The mayor may extend a house ban to a maximum of four weeks after it has been imposed if the threat of danger, or the serious suspicion thereof, continues. Articles 2, paragraph 4

, and

6 to 8 inclusive shall apply mutatis mutandis.

The appeal or higher appeal against the house ban shall also relate to a decision to extend the house ban as referred to in paragraph 1, insofar as the interested party contests this decision.

On appeal or higher appeal, the interested party shall, if possible, submit a copy of the decision he contests.

Paragraphs 2 and 3 shall apply mutatis mutandis to a request for interim relief.

9, paragraph 1

The person removed from the house who acts in contravention of a house ban given pursuant to Article 2, paragraph 1

, or Article 9, paragraph 1

, shall be punished with imprisonment of not more than two years or a fine of the fourth category.

The offences made punishable in paragraph 1 are crimes.
