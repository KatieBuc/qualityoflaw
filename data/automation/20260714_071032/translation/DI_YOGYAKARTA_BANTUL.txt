REGENT OF BANTUL
REGIONAL REGULATION OF BANTUL REGENCY
NUMBER 15 OF 2013
CONCERNING
PROTECTION OF CHILDREN AND WOMEN VICTIMS OF VIOLENCE BY THE GRACE OF GOD ALMIGHTY

REGENT OF BANTUL,

Considering : a. that every child and woman has the right to be free from torture or treatment that degrades human dignity and has the right to obtain a sense of security, free from all forms of violence, discrimination and violations of the rights of women and children;
b. that acts of violence against children and women continue to increase, so that comprehensive and maximum protection efforts are needed, concrete actions, formal institutional support, regulations that can guarantee implementation from the Regional Government in increasing broad community participation;
c. that based on the considerations as referred to in letter a, letter b, it is necessary to establish a Regional Regulation on the Protection of Children and Women Victims of Violence;

In View of : 1. Article 18 paragraph (6) of the 1945 Constitution of the Republic of Indonesia;

CHAPTER I
GENERAL PROVISIONS
Article 1
In this Regional Regulation, what is meant by:
1. Government is the Central Government.
2. Region is Bantul Regency.
3. Regional Government is the Regent and Regional Apparatus as elements of regional government administration.
4. Regional House of Representatives hereinafter abbreviated as DPRD is the Regional House of Representatives of Bantul Regency as an element of Regional Government administration.
5. Regent is the Regent of Bantul.
6. Regional Apparatus Work Unit hereinafter abbreviated as SKPD is the Regional Apparatus Work Unit of Bantul Regency.
7. Woman is a person who is female by sex and a person who is legally recognized as a woman.
8. Child is a person who has not reached 18 years of age, including a child still in the womb.
9. Violence is any act that results in or may result in misery or suffering whether physical, sexual, economic, social, and psychological, threats of certain actions, coercion or arbitrary deprivation of freedom, whether occurring in public or in private life against victims.
10. Violence against women is any action that results in or may result in misery or suffering of women physically, sexually, economically, socially, psychologically, including threats of certain actions, coercion or deprivation of freedom, whether occurring in public or private life.
11. Violence against children is any action that results in or may result in suffering of children physically, psychologically, sexually, neglect, exploitation, economically, and other violence.
12. Physical Violence is any act that causes pain, injury, wounds or disability to a person's body, miscarriage, fainting and or causes death.
13. Psychological Violence is any act that causes fear, loss of self-confidence, loss of ability to act, feelings of helplessness and or severe psychological suffering to a person.
14. Sexual Violence is any act in the form of sexual harassment, forced sexual intercourse, forced unnatural or unwanted sexual intercourse, forced sexual intercourse with others for commercial purposes and or certain purposes.
15. Economic Violence is any act that neglects family members in the form of not providing a proper living, care or maintenance.
16. Exploitation of Children is any act involving children in activities that may harm their welfare and development or endanger the safety of children with the aim of enabling others to obtain economic, sexual, social, or political benefits, including if there are restrictions or elimination of opportunities for children to obtain their rights.
17. Sexual harassment is any action with sexual nuances conveyed through physical or non-physical contact that targets sexual body parts or a person's sexuality, including by using whistles, winking, comments or sexually nuanced speech, displaying pornographic materials and sexual desires, pinching or touching body parts, movements or gestures that are sexual in nature resulting in discomfort, feeling offended, feeling degraded in dignity, and possibly causing health and safety problems.
18. Victim is a Child and Woman who experiences misery and or suffering either directly or indirectly as a result of violence occurring in the area of Bantul Regency.
19. Prevention is direct efforts carried out by the Regional Government to prevent acts of violence against women and children.
20. Protection is all service actions to guarantee and protect the rights of victims of violence organized by the Integrated Service Center.
21. Protection of women is all activities aimed at providing a sense of security for women carried out by family members, the police, prosecutors, courts, social institutions, or other parties who know or hear that violence against women will, is, or has occurred.
22. Protection of children is all activities to guarantee and protect children and their rights so they can live, grow, develop and participate optimally in accordance with human dignity and receive protection from violence and discrimination.
23. Service is action taken as soon as possible to victims when seeing, hearing and knowing that violence against victims will, is, or has occurred.
24. Women's Empowerment is any effort to improve physical, mental, spiritual, social capacity, knowledge, and skills so that women are ready to be utilized according to their respective abilities.
25. Companion is a person or representative from an institution who has expertise in accompanying victims to conduct counseling, therapy and advocacy for strengthening and recovery of violence victims.
26. Spiritual guidance is accompaniment in the form of services for spiritual strengthening so that sick people can rise to be closer to God so that a sense of calm, security arises, and mental health is achieved.
27. Judicial body is a general court that has the authority to receive, examine, and adjudicate, as well as resolve every case submitted to it, to realize law enforcement and justice.
28. Prosecution is the action of a public prosecutor to transfer a case to the competent District Court in the manner and according to the procedures regulated in the Law with a request to be examined and decided by a judge in a court session.
29. Lawsuit is a notification accompanied by a request by an interested party to a judge to act according to law against a person who has harmed them in a civil manner.
30. Institution is an agency/office/body/office within the scope of Regional Government and/or Non-Governmental Organization (NGO) that provides assistance to victims of violence.
31. Integrated Service Center hereinafter abbreviated as PPT is a functional work unit that organizes integrated services for victims of violence based in hospitals, and is managed jointly in the form of medical services (including medico-legal), psychosocial and legal services.
32. Regional Action Plan is the foundation and guideline for relevant agencies, vertical agencies, and the community, in order to carry out activities for the implementation of prevention and protection of children and women victims of violence.
33. Safe House is a shelter for victims, during the accompaniment process, for the safety and comfort of victims from threats and dangers of perpetrators in accordance with predetermined standards.
34. Social Reintegration is the process of preparing the community and victims that supports the reunification of victims into the family environment, family substitutes who can provide protection and fulfillment of victims' needs.
35. Standard Operating Procedure hereinafter abbreviated as SOP is standard steps that must be taken in protecting victims starting from complaints/identification, rehabilitation, health, social rehabilitation, legal services up to repatriation and social reintegration of witnesses and/or victims.
36. Family is the smallest unit in society consisting of husband and wife, or husband-wife and their children, or father and children, or mother and children, or blood relatives in a straight line upward or downward up to the third degree.
37. Household is husband, wife, and children, people who have family relationships due to blood relations, marriage, breastfeeding, foster care, and representation, and/or domestic workers and reside in that household.
38. Community is individuals, families, groups, social organizations and/or community organizations.
39. Regional Revenue and Expenditure Budget hereinafter abbreviated as APBD is the Regional Revenue and Expenditure Budget of Bantul Regency
40. Gender responsive budget is a budget that provides and accommodates justice and equality for women and men to access and obtain access, as well as benefits from development programs and participate in decision-making processes and have control over resources.
41. Forum for Protection of Violence Victims hereinafter abbreviated as FPKK is a coordination forum for handling Child and Women victims of violence whose implementation is carried out in a networked manner.

Article 2
The principles of implementing protection for Children and Women from acts of violence are:
a. respect and fulfillment of victims' rights;
b. justice and gender equality;
c. non-discrimination; and
d. the best empowerment for victims.

Article 3
(1) The objectives of the protection of Children and Women from acts of violence are: a. to prevent and eliminate all forms of acts of violence and exploitation against children and women that occur in the family environment and society;
b. to pursue recovery and social reintegration for children and women victims of acts of violence;
c. to provide services to children and women victims of acts of violence, reporters, and witnesses;
d. to carry out empowerment for women victims of violence; and e. to increase community participation.
(2) The implementation of protection of children and women from acts of violence as referred to in paragraph (1), includes aspects of:
a. prevention;
b. services and assistance;
c. social reintegration; and
d. empowerment.

CHAPTER II
RIGHTS OF VICTIMS
Article 4
Every victim of acts of violence shall receive the following rights: a. the right to have their dignity and worth as a human being respected; b. the right to health and psychological recovery from the suffering experienced by the victim;
c. the right to determine their own decisions;
d. the right to obtain information;
e. the right to confidentiality of their identity;
f. the right to optimal services;
g. the right to compensation;
h. the right to continuous handling up to the stage of social rehabilitation; i. the right to complaint handling;
j. the right to obtain facilitation in the judicial process; k. the right to psychological and legal assistance; and
l. the right to legal protection and services.

Article 5
Child victims of violence, in addition to obtaining the rights as referred to in Article 4, also obtain the following rights:
a. the right to respect for survival, growth and development;
b. the right to basic services in the field of education;
c. the right to basic services in the field of health;
d. the right to equal protection;
e. the right to be free from various stigma; and
f. the right to obtain freedom.

BAB III
OBLIGATIONS AND RESPONSIBILITIES
Article 6
Obligations and responsibilities in the implementation of protection for children and women victims of violence are:
a. Regional Government;
b. community;
c. family; and
d. parents.

Article 7
(1) To implement the provisions as referred to in Article 6 letter a, the Regional Government shall carry out victim protection efforts, in the form of:
a. formulating policies regarding the protection of Children and Women from all forms of violence and discrimination;
b. preparing program and activity planning;
c. providing support for facilities and infrastructure;
d. allocating special gender-responsive budgets; e. establishing PPT and/or Safe Houses;
f. establishing and facilitating FPKK; and
g. conducting monitoring and evaluation.
(2) In the implementation of the Regional Government's obligations as referred to in paragraph (1), the Regional Government shall prepare a Regional Action Plan as a basis for SKPD in implementing protection for victims.
(3) The Regional Action Plan as referred to in paragraph (2), constitutes part of the Regional Medium-Term Development Plan (RPJMD).
(4) Further provisions regarding the Regional Action Plan as referred to in paragraph (2), shall be further regulated by Regent Regulation.

Article 8
The Regional Government provides services for victims in the form of:
a. facilitating the establishment of PPT;
b. facilitating PPT facilities and infrastructure according to capacity; and
c. facilitating FPKK as a forum for victim handling networks.

Article 9
Regional Government has the obligation and responsibility to: a. supervise the implementation of services to victims in accordance with minimum service standards; and
b. provide funds for victim protection through the APBD. Article 10 In carrying out the obligations and responsibilities of the Regional Government as referred to in Article 7, Article 8, and Article 9, the Regent grants authority to the SKPD that has duties and functions in the field of women's empowerment and child protection.

Article 11
The obligations and responsibilities of the community towards the protection of children and Women Victims of violence are:
a. preventing the occurrence of violence against children and women; b. providing education on peace culture and gender equality; c. providing information and/or reporting if acts of violence against children and women occur to law enforcement officials or authorized parties;
d. protecting victims; and
e. providing emergency assistance.

Article 12
The obligations of the family and/or parents as referred to in Article 6 letter c and letter d, legally have full responsibility to prevent all forms of acts of violence and protect children and women as family members.

CHAPTER IV
IMPLEMENTATION OF PROTECTION
Part One
Institutional Arrangements

Article 13
(1) In order to provide services and protection to victims of acts of violence, the Regional Government establishes PPT.
(2) PPT as referred to in paragraph (1) has the duties, principal tasks, and functions of providing protection to Children and Women from acts of violence, which consists of fields.
(3) PPT as referred to in paragraph (2), shall be determined by Regent's Decree.
(4) PPT as referred to in paragraph (2) is tasked with providing services and temporary protection in the form of a safe house for victims of violence.
(5) In the event that PPT does not have a safe house (shelter) as referred to in paragraph (3), then victims of violence shall be referred to other institutions.

Article 14
(1) The organizational structure of the PPT formed by the Regent as referred to in Article 12 paragraph (1) consists of:
a. Chairperson or other equivalent title;
b. Secretary;
c. Treasurer; and
d. Executive Chairperson who oversees the divisions.
(2) The Divisions as referred to in paragraph (1) letter d, at minimum include:
a. complaints service division;
b. health service division;
c. social rehabilitation service division;
d. repatriation and social reintegration division;
e. legal aid service division;
f. spiritual division; and
g. psychology division

Article 15
The complaint service sector as referred to in Article 14 paragraph (2) letter a has the following duties:
a. conducting interviews and observation of the victim's condition;
b. making recommendations for follow-up services;
c. conducting coordination and referrals to follow-up services and related parties; and
d. carrying out administration of the complaint process.

Article 16
The health service sector as referred to in Article 14 paragraph (2) letter b has the following duties:
a. to conduct examination, treatment, and follow-up care for victims;
b. to coordinate the implementation of health rehabilitation and medico-legal services;
c. to conduct medico-legal examination including collection of evidence from victims, preparation of visum et repertum and visum et psikiatrikum;
d. to conduct supporting examinations and laboratory tests on evidence;
e. to conduct consultation with specialist doctors or make referrals; and
f. to prepare case reports.

Article 17
The field of social rehabilitation services as referred to in Article 14 paragraph (2) letter c has the duties of:
a. providing assistance during the case handling process;
b. conducting counseling; and
c. implementing social functions.

Article 18
The field of repatriation and social reintegration as referred to in Article 14 paragraph (2) letter d has the following duties:
a. to coordinate with relevant agencies for the repatriation of victims; b. to prepare progress reports on the process of accompanying repatriation and social rehabilitation; and
c. to conduct monitoring for a maximum of 3 (three) months after the victim is returned to their family.

Article 19
The field of legal aid services as referred to in Article 14 paragraph (2) letter e has the duties:
a. to accompany and defend in every legal process; and
b. to prepare reports on the progress of legal case handling.

Article 20
The spiritual field as referred to in Article 14 paragraph (2) letter f has the following duties:
a. providing spiritual guidance to victims;
b. coordinating with related parties; and
c. preparing reports and recommendations for follow-up services;

Article 21
The field of psychology as referred to in article 14 paragraph (2) letter g has the following duties:
a. provide psychological guidance to victims;
b. make recommendations for follow-up services; and
c. conduct coordination and referrals to follow-up services

Article 22
(1) PPT must have at least 5 (five) counselors.
(2) Counselors as referred to in paragraph (1) comprise:
a. counselor in the field of law;
b. counselor in the field of health;
c. counselor in the field of psychology;
d. counselor in the field of social affairs; and
e. counselor in the field of spirituality.

Article 23
The organizational structure of the PPT formed by the Regent involves relevant SKPD with community institutions adjusted to needs.

Article 24
Further provisions regarding the development, mechanisms and networked working relationships of PPT shall be further regulated by Regent Regulation.

BAB V
FORM AND MECHANISM OF PROTECTION
First Part
Prevention

Article 25
(1) Efforts to prevent violence against Children and Women shall be carried out in an integrated manner by the Regional Government coordinated by the agency having the main duties and functions of coordination in the field of women's empowerment and child protection.
(2) Prevention efforts as referred to in paragraph (1) shall be implemented by:
a. establishing a working network in efforts to prevent violence and handle acts of violence through coordination, integration, synchronization of violence prevention based on partnership patterns;
b. establishing a violence prevention system, mapping of locations or areas prone to acts of violence;
c. organizing communication, information, and critical education about the rights of children and women for the community;
d. conducting socialization regarding laws and regulations related to the protection of children and women; and
e. prevention efforts as referred to in paragraph (1) shall be carried out in an integrated manner coordinated by the SKPD responsible for women's empowerment and child protection.

Article 26
Prevention efforts are also carried out by:
a. the community;
b. the family; and
c. parents.

Second Part
Services

Article 27
(1) The provision of services to victims is carried out in an integrated manner by PPT.
(2) PPT as referred to in paragraph (1) may receive and send case referrals from or to other service units in a networked manner.

Article 28
The provision of services to victims is implemented with:
a. no charge;
b. promptness;
c. safety and comfort;
d. empathy;
e. non-discrimination;
f. integration;
g. easy accessibility; and
h. guaranteed confidentiality.

Article 29
Forms of services for victims include:
a. complaint, consultation, and counseling services;
b. assistance services;
c. health services;
d. social rehabilitation services;
e. legal services;
f. repatriation and social reintegration services; and
g. psychological services.

Article 30
Complaint, consultation, and counseling services as referred to in Article 29 letter a include:
a. identification or initial recording of victims; and
b. consent for action to be taken.

Article 31
Accompaniment services as referred to in Article 29 letter b include:
a. accompanying victims during the examination process and health recovery;
b. accompanying victims during the medicolegal process;
c. accompanying victims during the examination process at the Police, Prosecutor's Office and court;
d. monitoring the interests and rights of victims in the examination process at the Police, Prosecutor's Office and Court;
e. maintaining the privacy and confidentiality of victims from all parties who have no interest, including reporting by mass media;
f. coordinating with other companions; and g. providing continuous handling up to the rehabilitation stage.

Article 32
Health services as referred to in Article 29 letter c include: a. first aid to victims;
b. treatment and recovery of physical injuries aimed at restoring the physical condition of victims carried out by medical personnel and paramedics; and c. referral to health services.

Article 33
Social rehabilitation services as referred to in Article 29 letter d constitute services provided by companions in order to restore the traumatic condition of victims, including the provision of safe houses to protect victims from various threats and intimidation against victims and to provide social support so that victims have self-confidence, strength, and independence in resolving their problems, by means of:
a. providing spiritual guidance to victims; and
b. psychological recovery of victims.

Article 34
Legal services as referred to in Article 29 letter e to assist victims in undergoing the judicial process by means of:
a. providing legal consultation that includes information regarding the rights of victims and the judicial process;
b. accompanying victims at the investigation, prosecution, and examination levels in court proceedings and assisting victims to comprehensively describe the violence they have experienced; and
c. conducting coordination with fellow law enforcers, volunteer assistants, and social workers so that the judicial process proceeds as it should.

Article 35
(1) Repatriation and social reintegration services as referred to in Article 29 letter f aim to return victims to their families and social environment.
(2) Repatriation and social reintegration services as referred to in paragraph (1) shall be carried out by the Regional Government in coordination with: a. other Regional Governments; and
b. related government and non-government agencies and institutions.

Article 36
Psychological services as referred to in Article 29 letter g aim to:
a. provide assistance and psychological services; and b. provide psychological recovery of victims from trauma.

Third Part
Empowerment

Paragraph 1
Empowerment of Women Victims of Violence

Article 37
Forms of empowerment for women victims of violence include:
a. job training, comprising:
1. skills training;
2. field work practice; and
3. apprenticeship.
b. productive economic enterprises and joint business groups, comprising:
1. entrepreneurial skills training;
2. facilitation of joint business group formation; and
3. business implementation assistance.
c. capital assistance, comprising:
1. work facilities and infrastructure assistance; and
2. working capital assistance facilitation.

Paragraph 2
Protection and Fulfillment of Children's Rights

Article 38
(1) Protection for children in conflict with the law is implemented through:
a. treatment of children in a humane manner in accordance with the dignity and rights of children;
b. provision of special accompanying officers for children from an early stage; c. provision of special facilities and infrastructure;
d. imposition of appropriate sanctions for the best interests of the child; e. continuous monitoring and recording of the development of children in conflict with the law.
f. provision of guarantees to maintain relationships with parents or family; and
g. protection from identity disclosure through mass media and to avoid labeling.
(2) Forms of fulfillment of the rights of child victims of violence, include:
a. rehabilitation efforts both within institutions and outside institutions; b. provision of safety guarantees for victim witnesses and expert witnesses both physical, mental, and social; and
c. provision of accessibility to obtain information regarding case developments.
(3) PPT and the community are obligated to fulfill the rights of child victims of violence.

Article 39
(1) In an effort to guarantee the fulfillment of children's rights, the Regional Government cooperates with the Children's Forum that has been established.
(2) The Children's Forum is an organization whose members are children who become administrators of children's organizations, studios or groups of children's activities and the like which are generally based on the development of talents, interests, abilities and utilization of free time.
(3) The Children's Forum has the following duties:
a. to conduct socialization about acts of violence to peers; b. to be actively involved in the planning of policies, programs and activities related to the protection of children from acts of violence;
c. to become a source of information on incidents of violence in their environment; and
d. to consult the needs and desires of child victims of violence to partners from related sectors.

Fourth Part
Coordination of Services and Protection of Victims

Article 40
(1) In an effort to provide and organize handling of services and protection for victims, PPT establishes FPKK.
(2) FPKK as referred to in paragraph (1) aims to: a. coordinate and synchronize the handling of PPT services;
b. maintain and develop networks and referral systems; and c. collect, compile and present reports on violence.
(3) The management and membership of FPKK as referred to in paragraph (1) shall be determined by Decision of the Chairperson of PTT.
(4) The membership of FPKK as referred to in paragraph (3) shall be grouped in the following roles:
a. health role;
b. psychology role;
c. legal role;
d. social role; and
e. economic role.
(5) Further provisions regarding the establishment, main duties, and functions as well as membership of FPKK as referred to in paragraph (1) shall be further regulated by Regent Regulation.

CHAPTER VI
COOPERATION AND PARTNERSHIP
Part One
Cooperation

Article 41
(1) In order to achieve the objectives of protecting children and women from acts of violence as referred to in Article 3, the Regional Government cooperates with:
a. the Government;
b. the Provincial Government;
c. other Regencies/Cities; and
d. non-Governmental institutions.
(2) Cooperation as referred to in paragraph (1) includes: a. exchange of data and information;
b. rehabilitation of victims of acts of violence;
c. repatriation and social reintegration; and
d. provision of evidence and witnesses, and followed up in accordance with applicable law.
(3) Cooperation as referred to in paragraph (1) and paragraph (2) shall be set forth in the form of a Joint Agreement.

Second Part
Partnership

Article 42
(1) The Regional Government establishes partnerships with the business sector in the protection of children and women from acts of violence.
(2) Partnerships as referred to in paragraph (1) are carried out through: a. employment opportunity information system for women victims of acts of violence;
b. education and training for children and women victims of acts of violence;
c. educational assistance for children and women victims of acts of violence who are vulnerable to dropping out of school; and
d. fostering and increasing economic independence of women victims of acts of violence.
(3) Partnerships as referred to in paragraph (1) and paragraph (2) are set forth in the form of a joint agreement.

CHAPTER VII
GUIDANCE AND SUPERVISION
Article 44
(1) Regional Government conducts guidance and supervision on the implementation of PPT Minimum Service Standards.
(2) Guidance and Supervision as referred to in paragraph (1) includes:
a. coordination;
b. guidance;
c. education and training; and
d. monitoring and evaluation.
(3) Coordination as referred to in paragraph (2) letter a covers aspects related to planning and implementation.
(4) Guidance as referred to in paragraph (2) letter b covers aspects related to planning, implementation, procedures, funding, quality, control and supervision.
(5) Education and training as referred to in paragraph (2) letter c shall be conducted by SKPD responsible for women's empowerment and child protection.
(6) Monitoring and evaluation as referred to in paragraph (2) letter d shall be conducted periodically.

CHAPTER VIII
REPORTING
Article 43
(1) PPT and FPKK shall prepare reports on the protection of Children and Women victims of acts of violence to the Regent.
(2) Reporting as referred to in paragraph (1) shall be submitted in writing, covering:
a. administration;
b. finance;
c. services; and
d. performance.
(3) Submission of written reports as referred to in paragraph (2), shall be carried out at least once every 3 (three) months and shall be conducted in accordance with the provisions of laws and regulations.

BAB IX
FUNDING
Article 45
(1) Funding is carried out by the Regional Government for protection activities for victims of acts of violence charged to the APBD.
(2) Management of funding allocation as referred to in paragraph (1) is adjusted to the needs of the PPT by coordinating between SKPDs.

CHAPTER X
COMMUNITY PARTICIPATION
Article 46
(1) The community participates in the implementation of protection for victims of acts of violence.
(2) Community participation as referred to in paragraph (1) shall be in the form of: a. establishing family partners at the kelurahan/village level by the community; b. establishing Child and Women protection units within community organizations;
c. conducting socialization of the rights of Children and Women independently; d. providing first aid to victims; and
e. reporting to the competent authority if violence against victims occurs in their environment.
(3) Community participation as referred to in paragraph (1) shall be carried out by individuals, social community institutions, non-governmental organizations, educational institutions, religious institutions, the private sector, and mass media.

CHAPTER XI
CLOSING PROVISIONS
Article 47
This Regional Regulation shall come into force on the date of promulgation.
In order that every person shall know of it, the promulgation of this Regional Regulation is ordered by placement in the Regional Gazette of Bantul Regency.

I. GENERAL
One of the objectives of the establishment of the state is to provide protection to every citizen with a sense of security and comfort, this is explicitly stated in the Preamble of the Constitution of the Republic of Indonesia Fourth Paragraph. It is stated that the national objective of the Indonesian nation is to protect all Indonesian people and all Indonesian territory, to advance general welfare, to educate the life of the nation and to participate in implementing world order based on independence, eternal peace and social justice.
To achieve this, sustainable development efforts have been carried out which constitute a comprehensive, directed and integrated series of development, including development in promoting gender equality and women's empowerment. This is as affirmed in the Millennium Development Goals (MDGs). The implementation of gender equality and women's empowerment has become a priority that is continuously improved from year to year, especially since 2000, when Presidential Instruction Number 9 Year 2000 concerning Gender Mainstreaming (PUG) was issued at the urging of the State Minister for Women's Empowerment (Meneg PP) at that time.
This Presidential Instruction affirms that all Ministries and non-Ministerial government institutions in the National Government, Provinces, and Regencies/Cities must conduct gender mainstreaming in planning, implementation, monitoring, and evaluation of development policies and programs. This is a manifestation of efforts to fulfill the human rights of every Indonesian citizen, also intended so that gender equality and women's empowerment in Indonesia will increasingly improve.
Indonesia as a country that has ratified the International Convention, namely the Women's Convention; Convention of Elimination and Discrimination Against Women (CEDAW) in the form of Law Number 7 Year 1984 concerning Ratification of the Convention on the Elimination of All Forms of Discrimination Against Women, has thereby bound itself to guarantee through legislation, policies, programs and temporary special measures (affirmative action), to realize equality and justice between women and men, as well as the elimination of all forms of discrimination against women, including acts of violence against women and children.
It is a breath of fresh air for Indonesian women, when clearly explained in the Women's Convention which establishes principles and provisions to eliminate gaps, subordination and actions that violate women's rights and harm the position of women in law, family and society. These principles are clearly contained in the substantive articles of the Women's Convention (articles 1-16).
Through Law Number 23 Year 2002 concerning Child Protection, the Government recognizes the existence of children's rights. The inherent human rights of children include basic rights as human beings, namely the Right to Life, the Right to Growth and Development, the Right to Protection and the Right to Participation.
To fulfill the Right to life, children need food in sufficient quantities, healthy and nutritious, as well as optimal health access. Protection from violence that threatens their safety and health must be provided from an early age. Protection is needed to prevent physical, mental and sexual violence and exploitation. To fulfill the right to growth and development, children need space to play, exercise, education that is appropriate to their physical and psychological development. In fulfilling children's rights, every government administrator, community and parents are obliged to understand and care about children's rights. The existence of legal instruments and law enforcement officials who defend the interests of children is needed for this protection effort. Legislation that is principled on defending the best interests of children is needed to realize protection that is legal in nature. Furthermore, it is not uncommon to find cases of women and children who experience problems of physical, psychological, sexual and social violence. For example, women and children victims of abuse, neglect, eviction and mistreatment by parents or others. For women and children who experience these problems, the government must provide a Safe House or Integrated Service Center (PPT) for women and children victims of violence. The Safe House or PPT is a safe and friendly place for women and children. The PPT is equipped with companions from educators, doctors, psychologists, religious leaders/clergy and networked with lawyers for women and children as well as RPK Units at the Police.
The fate of women and children victims of violence must be given attention by the government. The high number of cases of violence against women and children is often caused by factors that develop in society, such as low levels of economy, education, environments in the industrial sector. Therefore, victims of violence like this need to receive protection in accordance with the principles of justice, truth, legal certainty, equality and upholding human rights.
For the empowerment of victims of violence, local governments can establish an Integrated Service Center and Violence Victim Protection Forum (FPKK) as a center for empowerment of women victims of violence which specifically has the main duties and functions for empowerment of children and women victims of violence.
From the above framework, the Government is responsible for taking actions both legally, politically, economically and socially to prevent, suppress, reduce, and eliminate all forms of violence against women and children. From these various efforts, a legal framework will be realized in the implementation of protection for children and women from acts of violence.

Article 1

Article 2
Letter a What is meant by "respect and fulfillment of victims' rights" is a series of actions to respect, appreciate and ensure the fulfillment of victims' rights.
Letter b What is meant by "justice and gender equality" is a process to be fair to men and women and equality of conditions for men and women to obtain opportunities and their rights as human beings, so that they are able to play a role and participate in political, economic, social and cultural activities, National defense and security and equality in enjoying the results of such development.
Letter c What is meant by "non-discrimination" is an attitude and treatment toward victims by not making distinctions on the basis of age, sex, race, ethnicity, religion and between groups.
Letter d What is meant by "the best empowerment for victims" is all recovery, rehabilitation, reunification actions concerning victims carried out by the government, society, legislative bodies and judicial bodies, must be the primary consideration.

Article 3
Paragraph (1) Sufficiently clear Paragraph (2) Letter a What is meant by the "prevention" aspect is a protection strategy effort through:
a. Primary prevention, all people, families, communities and the state in efforts to increase the capacity of knowledge, understanding and ensure that violence against women and children does not occur, including policy socialization, adequate services, supportive workplace policies, and life skills training for women and children. What is meant by life skills training includes conflict resolution without violence, stress management skills, resource management, effective decision making, effective interpersonal communication, guidance on the psychosocial development of women and children.
b. Secondary prevention, aimed at community groups with high risk in efforts to increase skills, including training and victim services to ensure that violence against women and children does not occur in the next generation. Activities carried out here include conducting home visits for parents who have just had children to conduct self assessment whether they are at risk of committing violence against children in the future.
c. Tertiary prevention, intended to increase parenting capacity that ensures violence against women and children is not repeated, what is done here is integrated services for women and children who are victims of violence, through counseling, stress management training.
Letter b What is meant by the "service and assistance" aspect is immediate activities and actions carried out by professionals and assistants in accordance with their respective professions in the form of counseling, therapy and advocacy for the strengthening and recovery of victims of violence.
Letter c What is meant by the "empowerment" aspect is the process of increasing the role of the community to participate in the implementation of protection for women and children victims of acts of violence which aims to obtain access and participation in the process of protection of women and children broadly.

Article 4
Letter a What is meant by "the right to have one's dignity respected as a human being" is upholding human rights.
Letter b What is meant by "health and psychological recovery from the suffering experienced by the victim" is assistance provided by health experts (doctors) for physical disorders suffered and from psychologists to victims suffering from trauma/other mental problems to restore the victim's mental condition.
Letter c Sufficiently clear Letter d What is meant by "obtaining information" is access and information about the whereabouts of complaint centers, PPT, and all matters related to the fulfillment of their rights and involvement in the decision-making process related to assistance and case developments that contain values, meanings, and messages, whether data, facts or explanations that can be seen, heard, and read presented in various packages and formats in accordance with the development of information and communication technology either electronically or non-electronically related to acts of violence.
Letter e What is meant by "confidentiality of identity" is an effort to guarantee certainty for victims that their identity, medical care and legal handling will not be disseminated.
Letter f What is meant by "optimal service" is service that includes medical, extensive medicolegal, psychosocial and legal.
Letter g What is meant by "the right to compensation" includes: economic empowerment, repatriation costs, health insurance, and education or skills. Letter h What is meant by "continuous handling up to the rehabilitation stage" is handling that does not stop at physical and psychological healing, legal assistance to restore civil rights, but until the victim can resume life in society including restoration of good name and citizenship.
Letter i What is meant by "the right to complaint handling" is the availability of a special integrated service unit by officers.
Letter j Sufficiently clear Letter k What is meant by "the right to assistance" includes among others: psychologists, psychiatrists, health experts, religious counselors, advocates, and family members. Meanwhile, legal assistance is assistance provided by persons and/or legal aid institutions to victims at every level of examination and throughout the legal process.
