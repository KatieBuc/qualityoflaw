CHAPTER I
GENERAL PROVISIONS
Article 1
In this Regional Regulation, what is meant by:
1.
Regency Region is the Regency Region of Bekasi.
2. Regent is the Regent of Bekasi.
3. Regency Regional Government is the Regent as an element of regional government administration who leads the implementation of government affairs that are the authority of the autonomous region.
4. Regional Secretary is the Regional Secretary of Bekasi Regency
5. Regional Apparatus is an auxiliary element of the Regent and Regional House of Representatives in government affairs that are the authority of the region.
6. Woman is a person of female sex starting from infants, adolescents, adults up to the elderly, who has been recognized by law.
7. Protection is all efforts aimed at providing a sense of security to women carried out by family, advocates, social institutions, police, prosecutors, courts or other parties either temporarily or based on court determination.
8. Protection of Women is all activities aimed at providing a sense of security carried out by the Regional Government, community, and/or other parties who know or hear that violence against women will or has occurred.
9. Discrimination is any restriction, harassment or exclusion that is directly or indirectly based on human differentiation on the basis of religion, ethnicity, race, ethnic group, group, class, social status, economic status, gender, language, political beliefs, which results in the reduction, deviation or elimination of recognition, implementation or use of human rights and fundamental freedoms in life both individually and collectively in the political, economic, legal, educational, social, cultural and other aspects of life.
10. Family is the smallest unit in society consisting of husband, wife, or husband, wife and their child, or father and his child, or mother and her child, or blood relatives in a straight line upward or downward up to the third degree.
11. Household is husband, wife, children, people who have family relationships due to blood relations, marriage, breastfeeding, care, and representation, and/or domestic workers and reside in that household.
12. Victim is a woman who experiences misery and/or suffering either directly or indirectly as a result of violence and life vulnerability.
13. Violence is any act that results in or can result in misery or suffering whether physical, psychological, sexual, economic and social as well as discrimination against victims that poses danger to life, body or results in the deprivation of a person's freedom.
14. Violence Against Women is any action that results in or may result in misery or suffering whether physical, psychological, sexual, socio-economic and discrimination, including threats of certain actions, coercion or deprivation of freedom, whether occurring in public or private life.
15. Domestic Violence is any act against a person, especially women, that results in misery or suffering physically, sexually, psychologically and/or threats of household neglect including threats to commit acts of coercion or deprivation of freedom unlawfully within the household scope.
16. Physical Violence is any act that causes pain, injury, wounds or disability to a person's body, miscarriage, fainting and or causes death.
17. Psychological Violence is an act that causes fear, loss of self-confidence, loss of ability to act, feelings of helplessness and/or other psychological suffering to a person
18. Sexual Violence is any act in the form of sexual harassment, forced sexual intercourse, forced sexual intercourse in an unnatural or unwanted manner, forced sexual intercourse with others for commercial purposes and or certain other purposes.
19. Women's Rights are a set of rights inherent in a woman or group of people not to experience discrimination, misery or suffering either directly or indirectly committed by state apparatus or by the state or by regional government apparatus or individuals which are natural rights given by God Almighty.
20. Service is an action taken as soon as possible to victims when seeing, hearing and or knowing that violence against victims will, is or has occurred.
21. Integrated Service is a series of activities to provide protection for witnesses and victims of violence carried out jointly by relevant agencies or institutions as a unified implementation of health rehabilitation, social rehabilitation, repatriation, social reintegration, and legal assistance.
22. Disability is an individual who has physical or mental limitations.
23. Complaint is a series of actions taken by the organizer of the Integrated Service Center to follow up on reports of acts of violence against victims of violence.
24. Health Rehabilitation is the recovery of victims of violence from the health disorders they suffer.
25. Social Rehabilitation is a service aimed at restoring the psychological condition and developing the abilities of a person experiencing social dysfunction so that they can carry out their social functions properly.
26. Legal Assistance is legal services provided by companions, law enforcement officers which include providing legal consultation, exercising power of attorney, representing, accompanying, defending and taking other legal actions for victims of violence.
27. Repatriation is an effort to return victims of violence from the receiving area/area where violence occurred to the area of origin or family.
28. Social Reintegration is an effort to reunite victims of violence with family, community, institutions or other social environments that can provide protection.
29. Regional Technical Implementation Unit for the Protection of Women and Children, hereinafter abbreviated as UPTD PPA, is an institution established by the Regional Government to provide services to women and children experiencing criminal and civil problems.
30. Safe House is a shelter for victims during the assistance process, for the safety and comfort of victims from threats and dangers of perpetrators.
31. Standard Operating Procedure, hereinafter abbreviated as SOP, is standard steps that must be taken in protecting victims starting from complaints/identification, health rehabilitation, social rehabilitation, legal services up to repatriation and social reintegration of witnesses and/or victims.

CHAPTER II
WOMEN'S RIGHTS
Part One
Fundamental Rights of Women

Article 2
(1) Women have the right:
a. to life;
b. to form a family and continue descendants;
c. to self-development;
d. to obtain justice;
e. to personal freedom;
f. to a sense of security;
g. to welfare; and
h. to participate in government.
(2) Every woman has the right:
a. to live peacefully, safely, in peace, happily, prosperously both physically and spiritually;
b. to be free from all threats of violence and/or exploitation;
c. to live in a family within the bonds of a legal marriage;
d. to sustain life and improve her standard of living;
e. to have work that she likes and is also entitled to fair employment conditions;
f. to obtain education and teaching in all types, levels and pathways of education in accordance with the requirements determined;
g. to a healthy and clean environment;
h. to receive fair wages in accordance with her work that is comparable and commensurate with human dignity;
i. to elect and/or be elected in general elections, regional head elections, village head elections and/or other political position elections based on equal rights through direct, general, free, secret, honest and fair voting according to the applicable laws and regulations;
j. to obtain special protection in the implementation of work or profession against matters that may threaten safety and/or health related to women's reproductive function; and
k. special rights inherent to women due to their reproductive function are guaranteed and protected by law.
l. to obtain legal protection and assistance for problems faced in accordance with the provisions of laws and regulations
m. other rights in accordance with the provisions of laws and regulations.
(3) Every woman in conditions of disability, old age, pregnancy has the right to obtain facilities and special treatment.
(4) Every elderly woman, with physical and/or mental disability, has the right to obtain care, education, training and special assistance from the Regional Government to ensure a decent life in accordance with human dignity.
(5) The Regional Government endeavors to establish an old age protection guarantee system for elderly women.

Second Part
Rights of Women with Disabilities

Article 3
Every Woman with disability has the right:
a. to reproductive health;
b. to accept or refuse the use of contraceptives;
c. to obtain greater Protection from multilayered Discrimination;
d. to obtain greater Protection from acts of violence, including sexual violence and exploitation;
e. to obtain education at all units, pathways, types and levels of education;
f. to obtain work and decent livelihood in accordance with the type and degree of disability, education and ability;
g. to obtain accessibility in the context of independence;
h. to obtain rehabilitation, social assistance and maintenance of social welfare standards; and
i. to obtain equal rights to develop talents, abilities and social life.

Third Part
Rights of Women Victims of Violence

Article 4
Every Female victim of violence has the right to:
a. obtain protection;
b. obtain determine her own decisions;
c. obtain information;
d. obtain complaint handling;
e. obtain assistance;
f. obtain continuous handling up to the rehabilitation stage; and
g. obtain confidential handling both from individuals, groups or institutions both government and non-government.

Article 5
Women victims of acts of violence have the right to obtain protection, information, integrated services, continuous handling up to the rehabilitation stage and confidential handling both from individuals, groups or institutions whether government or non-government.

CHAPTER III
FORMS OF VIOLENCE
Article 8
Forms of violence include:
a. Physical Violence;
b. Psychological Violence;
c. Sexual Violence; and
d. Domestic Violence;

Article 9
Physical Violence as referred to in Article 8 letter a is caused by acts that result in pain, bruising, injury, wounds or disability to a person's body, miscarriage, fainting and/or causing death.

Article 10
Psychological Violence as referred to in Article 8 letter b is caused by acts that result in fear, loss of self-confidence, loss of ability to act, feelings of helplessness and/or severe psychological suffering in a person.

Article 11
(1) Sexual Violence as referred to in Article 8 letter c is caused by:
a. acts in the form of sexual harassment;
b. sexual abuse;
c. coercion of unnatural or unwanted sexual relations;
d. coercion of sexual relations with others and/or exploitation of women for commercial purposes and other specific purposes.
(2) Coercion of sexual relations as referred to in article 11 paragraph (1) letter c is excluded for persons who have mental disorders or sexual disorders in marital relations.

Article 12
Domestic Violence as referred to in Article 8 letter d is caused by:
a. acts that neglect women within the household scope by a person who is obligated to provide life, care or maintenance to such woman;
b. acts that result in economic dependence by means of restricting and/or prohibiting decent work inside or outside the home so that the victim is under the control of a person; and
c. acts committed by family members that result in pain, bruises, injury, wounds or disability to a person's body, miscarriage, fainting and/or causing death.

BAB IV
PREVENTION, PROTECTION SERVICES, AND EMPOWERMENT Part One Prevention of Violence Against Women

Article 13
(1) Regional Governments undertake integrated efforts to prevent violence against women in the Region.
(2) The integrated efforts to prevent violence against women as referred to in paragraph (1) shall be coordinated by the Regional Apparatus responsible for the protection and empowerment of women.
(3) The prevention efforts as referred to in paragraph (1) and paragraph (2) shall be implemented by:
a. conducting coordination, integration, synchronization of violence prevention based on partnership patterns that may involve community organizations, universities, professionals as well as customary heads and/or community leaders;
b. conducting socialization regarding laws and regulations related to the protection of the rights of women victims of violence;
c. establishing an integrated violence prevention system;
d. providing ease of access to obtain civil rights, education, health, employment, training and funding, welfare improvement and social services;
e. providing education regarding women's rights for the community;
f. fostering and developing community participation and concern for the protection of women from acts of violence;
g. building and providing a complete and easily accessible information system; and
h. establishing a cooperation network in prevention efforts from acts of violence with law enforcement apparatus, government apparatus, universities and various non-governmental organizations that work and/or care about women.

Article 14
(1) Prevention of the occurrence of acts of violence against women as referred to in Article 13, shall be implemented by Regional Apparatus that handle affairs of:
a. health;
b. education;
c. manpower;
d. socio-political;
e. protection and empowerment of women; and
f. peace and order;
(2) Prevention of acts of violence by Regional Apparatus as referred to in paragraph (1), shall be implemented in an integrated and continuous manner based on the Regional Action Plan with the Regional Apparatus that handles Protection and Empowerment of Women.

CHAPTER VII
MONITORING, EVALUATION AND REPORTING
Part One
Monitoring

Article 34
(1) To ensure synergy, continuity and effectiveness in the implementation of policies, programs and activities for the empowerment and protection of women, the Regional Government conducts monitoring.
(2) The monitoring as referred to in paragraph (1) is intended to determine the progress and obstacles in the implementation of policies, programs and activities for the empowerment and protection of women in the Region.
(3) The monitoring as referred to in paragraph (2) is conducted periodically starting from the planning stage up to the policies, programs and activities for the empowerment and protection of women for the current year.
(4) The results of the monitoring as referred to in paragraph (3) shall be reported to the Regent periodically.

Second Part
Evaluation

Article 35
(1) Evaluation of the implementation of policies, programs and activities for the protection of women shall be conducted at the end of each fiscal year.
(2) The results of the evaluation of the implementation of policies, programs and activities for the protection of women shall be used as input material for the formulation of policies, programs and activities for the following year.

CHAPTER VIII
COMMUNITY PARTICIPATION
Article 38

(2) Participation as referred to in paragraph (1) shall be carried out by individuals, groups or social community organizations, non-governmental organizations, educational institutions, religious institutions, business entities and mass media.
(3) Community participation as referred to in paragraph (1) may take the form of: (4)
a. providing information or reporting to the competent authorities if they become aware of acts of violence against women;
b. taking preventive action against violence towards women;
c. conducting skills training for women; and
d. establishing social community institutions that handle the guidance and empowerment of women.
Further provisions regarding community participation as referred to in paragraph (2) shall be regulated by Regent Regulation.

CHAPTER IX
FINANCING
Article 39
Financing for the implementation of women's protection in the Region is sourced from the regional revenue and expenditure budget and other legitimate and non-binding sources of funds.

CHAPTER X
ADMINISTRATIVE SANCTIONS
Article 40
(1) Any person or institution or entity that deliberately fails to provide protection to women thereby causing the occurrence of acts of violence against women, allows the occurrence of violence, and/or does not report to the relevant agency may be subject to administrative sanctions.
(2) Business Entities or non-governmental organizations are obligated to provide Protection to Women and violence in their work environment.
(3) Business Entities or non-governmental organizations are obligated to report acts of violence in their work environment to the relevant agency.
(4) Violations as referred to in paragraph (1), paragraph (2) and paragraph (3) may be subject to administrative sanctions in the form of:
a. verbal warning;
b. written warning;
c. temporary suspension from activities;
d. termination of cooperation;
e. revocation of registered certificate;
f. revocation of operational permit;
g. sealing;
h. withdrawal of facilities; and/or
i. administrative fine of at least Rp.10,000,000,- (Ten Million Rupiah) and at most Rp. 50,000,000,- (Fifty Million Rupiah) (5) Provisions regarding the procedures for implementing administrative sanctions as referred to in paragraph (4) are regulated in the Regent's Regulation.
