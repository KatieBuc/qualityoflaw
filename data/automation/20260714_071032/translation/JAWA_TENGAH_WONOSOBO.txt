BAB II
SCOPE
Article 2
The scope of social protection includes: women and children with social problems.

Article 3
Women and children with social problems as referred to in Article 2, include:
a. Neglected children;
b. Children who are victims of gender-based violence and/or maltreatment;
c. Delinquent children;
d. Street children;
e. Children with disabilities;
f. Women vulnerable to socio-economic problems;
g. Women who are victims of gender-based violence and/or maltreatment;
h. women and children victims of human trafficking
i. children with special needs
j. children in emergency situations

Article 4
Social protection for women and children in the form of: a. Social assistance;
b. Social advocacy; and/or
c. legal assistance.

CHAPTER III
PRINCIPLES, FUNCTIONS AND OBJECTIVES
Article 5
Social protection for women and children is based on the principles of:
a. humanity;
b. justice and gender equality;
c. protection;
d. best interests of women and children;
e. non-discrimination;
f. participation;
g. professionalism; and
h. partnership.

Article 6
Social protection for women and children functions to:
a. prevent the emergence, growth and development of social welfare problems.
b. restore social functions in achieving independence.
c. enhance community social resilience in preventing and handling social welfare problems.
d. develop social potential.
e. empower social resources.
f. prevent social vulnerability.

Article 7
Social protection for women and children aims to:
a. eliminate all forms of violence against women and children
b. protect, provide a sense of security for women and children
c. improve the standard of welfare, quality, and continuity of life.
d. restore social functions in order to achieve independence.
e. increase social resilience of the community in preventing and addressing social welfare issues.
f. increase awareness, capability, concern and social responsibility of the business sector in the implementation of social welfare in an institutionalized and sustainable manner.
g. increase awareness, capability, concern and social responsibility of the business sector and community.

CHAPTER IV
IMPLEMENTATION
Part One
General

Article 8
(1) Social protection for women and children is a joint obligation between the Regional Government, community, social organizations and business sector which is implemented in an integrated and sustainable manner.
(2) Social protection for women and children as referred to in paragraph (1) is prioritized for women and children who have lives that are not humanely decent and have social problem criteria:
a. poverty
b. neglect;
c. disability
d. social disability and behavioral deviation; and/or
e. victims of gender-based violence and children and/or mistreatment, as well as victims of human trafficking;
f. victims of social conflict.
(3) Social protection for women and children, as referred to in paragraph (1) is implemented through:
a. prevention efforts;
b. handling efforts;
c. recovery efforts;
d. protection and support efforts.

Article 9
Prevention Efforts as referred to in Article 8 paragraph (3) letter a, can be carried out among others through efforts of:
a. social counseling and guidance;
b. Social assistance;
c. improvement of health and education levels;
d. improvement of accessibility to resources;
e. empowerment.

Article 10
(1) Handling Efforts as referred to in Article 8 paragraph (3) letter b, may be carried out among others through efforts of:
a. outreach (raid operations);
b. identification;
c. selection;
d. social motivation;
e. social guidance and assistance.
(2) In the framework of handling efforts as referred to in paragraph (1), further actions may be taken consisting of:
a. referral to Temporary Shelter;
b. referral to Social Rehabilitation Institution/Center;
c. return to parents/guardians/family/place of origin;
d. provision of health services.

Article 11
(1) Recovery Efforts as referred to in Article 8 paragraph (3) letter c, can be carried out among others through efforts of:
a. Initial motivation from outreach results (raid operations);
b. Identification;
c. Selection;
d. Social motivation;
e. Distribution/referral to rehabilitation centers;
f. Return to place of origin;
g. Social guidance and skills training;
h. Stimulant assistance;
i. Supervision.
(2) Handling of rehabilitative efforts can be carried out in centers located in the region and outside the region.
(3) In the implementation of rehabilitative efforts as referred to in paragraph (1), it can be provided through guidance, education, training both physical, mental, social, medical rehabilitation, work skills in accordance with their talents and abilities, social assistance, distribution and follow-up guidance.

Article 12
Protection and supporting efforts as referred to in Article 8 paragraph (3) letter d, may be carried out through the provision of facilities which include:
a. Child Care Social Institutions that can receive referrals from private institutions and are equipped with workshop facilities;
b. Women's Work Centers;
c. Provision of facilities/accessibility for persons with disabilities in buildings and public facilities.

Article 13
The technical implementation of social protection and the administration of social welfare for women and children as referred to in Article 8 paragraph (2) shall be further regulated by Regent Regulation.

Second Part
Social Protection and Implementation of Social Welfare for Women

Article 14
Every Woman has the right to obtain social protection in the form of equal treatment and opportunity in education and training, employment, protection of safety and/or health in relation to women's reproductive function in accordance with decent norms of life and livelihood.

Article 15
Every woman has the obligation to maintain her dignity and honor and that of her family as well as her nature as a woman by paying attention to her function and role in family life and/or society in accordance with her capabilities.

Article 16
(1) Regional Government and/or community provide social protection and implementation of social welfare to women who are unable, neglected, exploited, trafficked and prostitutes.
(2) Social protection and implementation of social welfare for women as referred to in paragraph (1), through:
a. education and training services;
b. opportunities to work and do business;
c. physical, religious, mental, and social guidance;
d. health services;
e. legal assistance;
f. other special social protection.
(3) Social protection and implementation of social welfare for women as referred to in paragraph (2) shall be the responsibility of SKPD/UKPD in accordance with their duties and functions.
(4) Social protection and implementation of social welfare for women as referred to in paragraph (2), may be organized by the community, social organizations, and the business sector.

Article 17
Every institution, both government and private, that provides public services or employs women is obligated to provide adequate facilities for the interests of women.

Third Part
Social Protection And Implementation of Social Welfare For Children

Article 18
(1) Every child has the right to obtain social protection and the implementation of social welfare from parents, family, community, and regional government to grow and develop properly.
(2) Every child has the right to obtain social protection and the implementation of social welfare to develop their abilities and social life in accordance with the culture and personality of the nation to become a good and useful member of society.
(3) Every child has the right to obtain maintenance of the child's welfare level and protection from environments that endanger and/or hinder their growth and development properly.
(4) Every child has the right to obtain first aid, assistance and protection in dangerous situations. every child has the right to receive protection from parents against all forms of physical and mental violence, neglect, maltreatment, exploitation and sexual abuse, as well as the right to care, religious guidance, and social mental development.

Article 19
(1) Every person is prohibited from neglecting, committing acts of violence and/or exploitation against children.
(2) Every person is prohibited from employing children below the working age as regulated in the provisions of applicable laws and regulations.

Article 20
Regional government and/or the community may provide social protection and implementation of social welfare for children for the purpose of improving child welfare carried out in accordance with the provisions of laws and regulations.

Article 21
(1) Social protection and implementation of social welfare for children as referred to in Article 18, in the form of:
a. Care and upbringing
b. Health services and nutritional improvement;
c. Education and recreation services;
d. Religious, mental and social guidance;
e. Social rehabilitation;
f. Social assistance;
g. Family reunification;
h. Population administration and civil registration services; i. Funeral services
j. Legal aid services;
k. Maintenance of social welfare standards
l. Provision of childcare facilities at the workplace
m. Other special social protection.
(2) For children with disabilities, in addition to receiving social protection and implementation of social welfare for children as referred to in paragraph (1), they also receive accessibility services.
(3) Social protection and implementation of social welfare for children as referred to in paragraph (1), shall be the responsibility of SKPD/UKPD in accordance with their duties and functions.
(4) Social protection and implementation of social welfare for children as referred to in paragraph (1) may be implemented by the community, social organizations and the business sector.
(5) Further provisions regarding the forms of social protection and implementation of social welfare for children as referred to in paragraph (1), shall be regulated by Regent Regulation.

Responsibility of Regional Government

f. facilitating participation from the community, social organizations and/or the business sector in social protection and the implementation of social welfare for women and children.

I. GENERAL ELUCIDATION

The target of development in the field of social welfare regulated in this Regional Regulation is directed towards Women and Children. Especially programs for improving the quality of life and protection of women and children. This is adjusted to the objectives and functions of a Regional Regulation to regulate certain matters in accordance with the conditions and needs of a region.

This Regional Regulation on Social Protection for Women and Children aims to provide a legal basis for efforts of Social Protection for Women and Children as well as to provide studies within the philosophical, sociological and juridical framework regarding the necessity of a Regional Regulation that becomes the legal foundation for handling social problems in Wonosobo Regency, especially related to Women and Children.

Substantively, that in efforts to overcome the problems of Women and Children in Wonosobo Regency, it is necessary to formulate a Regional Regulation concerning Social Protection for Women and Children which essentially constitutes action that is rooted in or has a strong foundation, namely in good governance, and so that it is formulated with correct principles.

The regulation of the field of Social Protection for Women and Children must pay attention to environmental aspects and social aspects, so that the social conditions of the community are controlled. The regulation of Social Protection for Women and Children through Regional Regulation can be viewed as a solution as well as an effort to prevent negative impacts that potentially harm the interests of the community.

The absence of a Regional Regulation that specifically regulates Social Protection for Women and Children in Wonosobo Regency makes the urgency of this Regional Regulation indeed necessary as a legal umbrella for the creation of legal certainty in matters of Social Protection for Women and Children, which is an obligation of the Regional Government, specifically the Wonosobo Regional Government, to provide Social Protection for Women and Children of Wonosobo Regency.

II. EXPLANATION ARTICLE BY ARTICLE
Article 1
Sufficiently clear.

Article 2
Sufficiently clear.

Article 3
a. A neglected child is a child aged 0-18 years who, due to certain reasons (poverty/inability, one or both parents/guardians being ill or deceased, family disharmony), cannot have their basic needs properly fulfilled, whether physically, spiritually or socially.
b. A child who is a victim of gender-based violence or maltreatment is a child aged 0-18 years who is threatened physically and non-physically due to acts of violence, maltreatment or improper treatment within the family environment or immediate social environment, so that their basic needs cannot be properly fulfilled, whether physically, spiritually or socially.
c. A delinquent child is a child aged 0-18 years who behaves in deviation from the norms and customs prevailing in society and their environment, thereby harming themselves, their family and others, and will disturb public order, but due to age cannot yet be prosecuted legally.
d. A street child is a child aged 0-18 years who spends most of their time seeking livelihood and/or wandering on the streets or in public places.
e. A child with disabilities is a child aged 0-18 years who has physical and/or mental abnormalities, which can interfere with or constitute obstacles and barriers for them to carry out activities normally, consisting of those with physical disabilities, those with mental disabilities, those with physical and mental disabilities.
f. A woman vulnerable to socio-economic conditions is an adult woman aged 18-59 years, unmarried or widowed, who does not have sufficient income to meet daily basic needs.
g. A woman who is a victim of gender-based violence and/or maltreatment is a woman aged 18-59 years who is threatened physically or non-physically (psychologically) due to acts of violence, maltreatment or improper treatment within the family environment or immediate social environment.
h. Trafficking of women and children is the transfer, transportation, mobilization, sending of children and women within a country or abroad illegally and unlawfully, with or without the consent of the person concerned, to then be employed exploitatively, especially in the prostitution environment

Article 5
a. What is meant by "the principle of solidarity" is that in the implementation of social welfare it must be based on social care to help people who need assistance with empathy and compassion.
b. What is meant by "the principle of justice" is that in the implementation of social welfare it must emphasize aspects of equity, non-discrimination and balance between rights and obligations.
c. What is meant by "the principle of benefit" is that in the implementation of social welfare it must provide benefits for improving the quality of life of citizens.
d. What is meant by "the principle of integration" is that in the implementation of social welfare it must integrate various related components so that they can run in a coordinated and synergistic manner.
e. What is meant by "the principle of partnership" is that in handling social welfare issues partnership is required between the Government and the community, the Government as the responsible party and the community as the Government's partner in handling social welfare problems and improving social welfare.
f. What is meant by "the principle of transparency" is providing the widest possible access to the community to obtain information related to the implementation of social welfare.
g. What is meant by "the principle of accountability" is that in every implementation of social welfare it must be accountable in accordance with the provisions of laws and regulations.
h. What is meant by "the principle of participation" is that in every implementation of social welfare it must involve all components of society.
i. What is meant by "the principle of professionalism" is that in every implementation of social welfare to the community it should be based on professionalism in accordance with the scope of duties and carried out as optimally as possible.
j. What is meant by "the principle of sustainability" is that in implementing social welfare it is carried out continuously, so that independence is achieved.
k. What is meant by "the principle of non-discrimination" is treatment that does not distinguish between ethnicity, religion, race, group, gender, ethnicity, culture and language, legal status of children, birth order of children, and physical and mental conditions.
l. What is meant by "gender equality" is equal conditions for men and women to obtain opportunities and their rights as human beings, so that they are able to play a role and participate in political, legal, economic, socio-cultural, educational and national defense and security (hankamnas) activities, as well as equality in enjoying the results of such development.
