# CONCERNING

IMPLEMENTATION OF EMPOWERMENT AND PROTECTION OF WOMEN AND CHILDREN

# BY THE GRACE OF ALMIGHTY GOD

# THE REGENT OF SAROLANGUN,

Considering : a. that women and children are national assets who play a role in the process of continuation and creation of quality generations and need to receive guarantees for the fulfillment of their rights and protection from acts of violence and discrimination in order to build society, nation and state;

   - b. that along with the increasing violence against women and children in Sarolangun Regency, which constitutes acts that degrade human dignity and honor, the role of the Sarolangun Regency government is required so that women and children are protected from violations of rights guaranteed by the Constitution;
   - c. that based on the considerations as referred to in letter a and letter b, it is necessary to establish a Regional Regulation on the Implementation of Empowerment and Protection of Women and Children;

# CHAPTER I

## GENERAL PROVISIONS

#### Article 1

In this Regional Regulation, what is meant by:

1. Region is Sarolangun Regency.
2. Regional Government is the administration of governmental affairs by the Regional Government and the Regional House of Representatives according to the principles of autonomy and assistance tasks with the principle of the widest possible autonomy within the system and principles of the Unitary State of the Republic of Indonesia as referred to in the 1945 Constitution of the Republic of Indonesia.
3. Regional Government is the Regent as an element of the organizer of Regional Government who leads the implementation of governmental affairs that are the authority of the autonomous region.
4. Regent is the Regent of Sarolangun.
5. Regional House of Representatives hereinafter abbreviated as DPRD is a regional people's representative institution that is positioned as an element of the organizer of Regional Government.
6. Women's Empowerment is every effort to improve physical, mental, spiritual, social capabilities, knowledge, and skills so that women are ready to be utilized according to their respective capabilities.
7. Women's Protection is all efforts aimed at protecting women and providing a sense of security in the fulfillment of their rights by providing consistent and systematic attention aimed at achieving gender equality.
8. Discrimination Against Women is any distinction, exclusion or restriction made on the basis of sex which has the effect or purpose of impairing or nullifying the recognition, enjoyment or exercise of human rights and fundamental freedoms in the political, economic, social, cultural, civil or any other field by women, regardless of their marital status, on the basis of equality between men and women.
9. Gender is a concept that refers to the differentiation of roles and responsibilities of men and women that occur as a result of social and cultural conditions of society.
10. Gender Equality is the equality of conditions for men and women to obtain opportunities and their rights as human beings so that they are able to play a role and participate in political, economic, social, cultural, defense and security activities, and equality in enjoying the results of development.
11. Gender mainstreaming is a strategy built to integrate gender into an integral dimension of planning, formulation, implementation, monitoring, and evaluation of national development policy programs.
12. Child is a person who has not reached the age of 18 (eighteen) years, including a child who is still in the womb.
13. Parents are biological father and/or mother, or step father and/or mother, or adoptive father and/or mother.
14. Guardian is a person or body who in reality exercises parental authority over a child.
15. Family is the smallest unit in society consisting of husband, wife and their children, or father and his children, or mother and her children, or blood relatives in direct lineage upward or downward up to the third degree.
16. Community is individuals, families, communities, religious leaders, community leaders, traditional leaders, business world and corporations, community organizations, social organizations, women's and gender study centers, mass media or other similar entities.
17. Child Protection is all activities to guarantee and protect children and their rights so that they can live, grow and develop and participate optimally in accordance with human dignity and receive protection from violence, exploitation, discrimination, and neglect.
18. Special protection is protection given to children in emergency situations, children in conflict with the law, children from minority and isolated groups, children who are exploited economically and/or sexually, children who are trafficked, children who are victims of narcotics, alcohol, psychotropic, and other addictive substances (napza) abuse, children who are victims of kidnapping, sale, trafficking, children who are victims of violence both physical and/or mental, children with disabilities, and children who are victims of maltreatment and neglect.
19. Safe house is a shelter or temporary protection house for victims during the assistance process to ensure the safety and comfort of victims from threats and dangers of perpetrators and is only known to interested parties for reasons of victim safety and security.
20. Child-friendly regency is a regency that has a child rights-based development system through the integration of commitments and resources of government, community, business world, and media that are planned comprehensively and sustainably in policies, programs and activities to ensure the fulfillment of children's rights and special protection of children.

#### Article 2

Empowerment and Protection of Women and Children is organized based on the principles of:

   - a. respect for human rights;
   - b. gender equality;
   - c. non-discrimination;
   - d. justice and legal certainty;
   - e. benefit;
   - f. participatory;
   - g. best interests of the child;
   - h. accountability; and
      - i. gender responsiveness.

#### Article 3

The regulation regarding the Empowerment and Protection of Women and Children aims to:

   - a. guarantee the fulfillment of the rights of every woman and child to survival, growth and development;
   - b. improve the quality of life of women, children and the quality of families;
   - c. increase the institutional capacity for women's empowerment and child protection in the regions, including the development of gender and child data systems; and
   - d. provide protection of women's rights and fulfillment of children's rights including special protection for children from various forms of violence and other discriminatory treatment.

#### Article 4

The scope of regulation for Women and Children Empowerment and Protection includes:

   - a. fulfillment of women's rights;
   - b. women's empowerment;
   - c. women's protection;
   - d. children's protection;
   - e. fulfillment of children's rights;
   - f. safe houses;
   - g. child-friendly regency;
   - h. guidance and supervision;
      - i. community participation;
   - j. awards; and
   - k. financing.

# CHAPTER II

## PROTECTION OF WOMEN

## Part One

Fulfillment of Women's Rights

#### Article 5

1. Every woman has the right to:
   - a. live and maintain her life and improve her standard of living;
   - b. fulfill her basic needs to grow and develop properly, form a family and continue her lineage;
   - c. develop her personality, obtain education, and improve her quality of life in order to become a human being who is faithful, pious, responsible, of noble character, happy, and prosperous in accordance with human rights;
   - d. obtain justice, a sense of security, and freedom to express opinions without discrimination;
   - e. be involved in every stage of the development process;
   - f. be free from slavery or enslavement and threats;
   - g. obtain protection of her person, family, honor, dignity and property rights;
   - h. obtain welfare and a decent life;
      - i. participate in politics and obtain equal opportunities in government;
   - j. perform legal acts; and
   - k. freely choose a partner in her marriage.
2. In addition to the rights as referred to in paragraph (1), specifically women with disabilities have the right to obtain facilitation and special treatment in public services.

#### Article 6

1. Every wife during the marriage bond has equal rights and responsibilities with her husband over all matters concerning their marital life, relationship with their children, and ownership as well as management of joint property in accordance with applicable laws and regulations.
2. After the dissolution of marriage, a woman has equal rights and responsibilities with her former husband over all matters concerning the children with due regard to the best interests of the children.
3. After the dissolution of marriage, a woman has equal rights with her former husband over all matters concerning joint property without prejudice to the rights of the children, in accordance with the provisions of laws and regulations.

#### Article 7

1. The Regional Government has the obligation and responsibility for the empowerment and protection of women.
2. The efforts for empowerment and protection of women as referred to in paragraph (1) include:
   - a. determination of policies, programs, and activities for improving the quality of life of women related to the fields of development, education, health, economy, law, Human Rights and politics, environment and socio-culture;
   - b. provision of services to women especially to victims of violence, female workers, elderly women, and women with disabilities, victims of conflict and victims affected by disasters; and
   - c. implementation of gender mainstreaming in government institutions, women's study centers, research and development institutions, non-governmental institutions.
3. The efforts for empowerment and protection of women as referred to in paragraph (2) shall be carried out in an integrated manner in accordance with the prevailing laws and regulations.
4. The empowerment and protection of women shall be coordinated by the Regional Government Work Unit/Regional Apparatus Organization that administers regional government affairs in the field of women's empowerment and child protection.

Second Part Women's Empowerment

#### Article 8

The empowerment of women is directed towards obtaining opportunities and rights as human beings so that they are able to play a role and participate in the fields of:

   - a. economy;
   - b. social culture;
   - c. politics; and
   - d. law.

#### Article 9

Women's empowerment in the economic field as referred to in Article 8 letter a is implemented through:

   - a. provision of skills and job training;
   - b. facilitation of the formation of productive economic business groups;
   - c. facilitation of strengthening and development of productive economic business groups;
   - d. facilitation and capital assistance; and
   - e. facilitation of marketing network development.

#### Article 10

Empowerment of women in the social and cultural field as referred to in Article 8 letter b shall be implemented through:

   - a. improvement of knowledge, attitudes and skills to encourage the fulfillment of tiered education in accordance with potential to improve social status;
   - b. improvement of awareness, willingness and ability to overcome health problems through quality promotive, preventive, curative, and rehabilitative efforts primarily in the field of reproductive health;
   - c. improvement of awareness and knowledge about independent, healthy and prosperous family planning including access to consultation services and marriage registration; and
   - d. facilitation and efforts to preserve customs and the development of science, technology, arts and culture for the advancement of women.

#### Article 11

1. Empowerment of women in the political field as referred to in Article 8 letter c includes:
   - a. involvement of women in decision-making at various levels;
   - b. provision of opportunities for women to hold public office;
   - c. participation in general elections; and
   - d. self-development through organizations to associate, assemble and express opinions.
2. Empowerment of women in the political field as referred to in paragraph (1) shall be implemented in accordance with the prevailing laws and regulations.

#### Article 12

1. Empowerment of women in the field of law as referred to in Article 8 letter d includes:
   - a. increasing awareness and knowledge in the field of law through communication, information and education services; and
   - b. facilitation of access and legal consultation services.
2. Empowerment of women in the field of law as referred to in paragraph (1) shall be implemented in accordance with the applicable laws and regulations.

Third Part Protection of Women

#### Article 13

1. Protection of women includes:
   - a. social protection;
   - b. economic protection; and
   - c. legal protection.
2. Social protection as referred to in paragraph (1) letter a is to ensure the survival of women in the form of social protection in order to ensure the fulfillment of decent living needs.
3. Economic protection as referred to in paragraph (1) letter b is to prevent and address the risk of vulnerability resulting from shocks and social vulnerability of a person, family, group and/or community for the survival of women.
4. Legal protection as referred to in paragraph (1) letter c is implemented in terms of providing protection to women who become witnesses and victims of acts of violence and discrimination.

# CHAPTER III

## CHILD PROTECTION

## Part One

General

#### Article 14

Every Child has basic rights which include:

   - a. civil rights and freedoms;
   - b. family environment and alternative care;
   - c. basic health and welfare;
   - d. education, utilization of leisure time and cultural activities; and
   - e. special protection.

## Part Two

Civil Rights and Freedoms

#### Article 15

Every Child has the right to obtain Civil Rights and Freedoms as referred to in Article 14 letter a in the form of:

   - a. birth certificate;
   - b. child identity card;
   - c. expressing opinions in accordance with their age and level of intelligence;
   - d. obtaining healthy and safe information;
   - e. freedom of assembly and organization that is appropriate for them;
   - f. protection of reputation and not being exploited to the public without the consent of the child; and/or
   - g. participating in development through children's forums at the village/kelurahan level, sub-district level and even Regency level.

#### Article 16

In fulfilling the Civil Rights and Freedoms of the Child, the Family:

   - a. processes the child's birth certificate after the child is born;
   - b. endeavors to obtain an identity card for the child;
   - c. provides space to gather and to associate/organize, and listens to the child so that the child can express their opinion;
   - d. supervises the child in accessing various information and provides healthy and safe information; and
   - e. protects the child's private life and does not expose the child without the child's permission.

#### Article 17

The Government provides facilities for the fulfillment of the Civil Rights and Freedoms of Children, by:

   - a. organizing fast and easy services in the making of birth certificates;
   - b. involving children through children's forums at the village/kelurahan level, sub-district and city in development planning deliberations or other similar forums;
   - c. providing children's call centers as a means of interactive communication or complaints related to the interests of children;
   - d. providing healthy and safe information facilities by conducting supervision of internet service providers;
   - e. providing children's identity cards; and
   - f. providing reading rooms or reading corners in public spaces.

Third Part Rights in the Family Environment and Alternative Care

#### Article 18

Every Child has the right to obtain rights in the family environment and alternative care as referred to in Article 14 letter b in the form of:

   - a. receiving priority to be raised by their own parents;
   - b. not being separated from their parents, except when such separation is in the best interest of the child;
   - c. receiving good, polite and loving parenting patterns;
   - d. receiving balanced parenting patterns from both parents;
   - e. receiving welfare support even though their parents are unable to provide;
   - f. receiving alternative care in the event that both parents have died or suffer from an illness that makes it impossible to care for the child; and
   - g. receiving family harmony.

#### Article 19

In fulfilling rights within the family environment and alternative care, the Family:

   - a. provides parenting, affection, attention, protection, facilities, and maintains family harmony, always considering the best interests of the child, in accordance with the conditions and capabilities of the parents;
   - b. prevents marriage at child age and safeguards the child from being trapped in promiscuity and a permissive culture, and ensures the child avoids narcotics/psychotropics/addictive substances (NAPZA), HIV and AIDS, and is protected from pornography;
   - c. provides religious education and instills noble values from an early age in the child;
   - d. provides national insight, heroism, and national defense from an early age to the child; and
   - e. provides welfare support.

#### Article 20

The Regional Government provides facilities for the fulfillment of children's rights in the family environment and alternative care environment, by: a. providing or facilitating alternative care for children whose parents have died or are ill such that they are unable to care for the child;

   - b. providing welfare support to all children; c. fulfilling the rights of children's growth and development and protecting children with social welfare problems and children in conflict with the law; d. protecting children from cruel, inhuman and degrading treatment;
   - e. conducting training for parents on good parenting patterns or establishing consultation institutions for families;
   - f. playing an active role in assisting families in carrying out their obligations;
   - g. preventing the occurrence of marriage at child age; and h. providing child-friendly infrastructure, child-friendly play spaces and safe routes to and from school.

Fourth Part Basic Health Rights and Welfare

#### Article 21

Every Child has the right to obtain basic health rights and welfare, as referred to in Article 14 letter c, in the form of: a. not to be aborted unless endangering the safety of the mother; b. good nutrition since in the womb;

   - c. breast milk until the age of two Years;
   - d. complete basic immunization;
   - e. periodic health examinations for children under five;
   - f. smoke-free environment;
   - g. availability of clean water;
   - h. access to social security; and
      - i. protection and rehabilitation from narcotics, psychotropics and other addictive substances, HIV and AIDS.

#### Article 22

In fulfilling the basic health rights and welfare of children, the family:

   - a. provides good nutrition from the womb;
   - b. provides breast milk until the age of two years;
   - c. provides complete basic immunization;
   - d. conducts regular health examinations for toddlers;
   - e. frees children from cigarette smoke;
   - f. fulfills the need for clean water;
   - g. seeks social security; and
   - h. prevents children from Narcotics, Psychotropics, and Addictive Substances (NAPZA), HIV and AIDS.

#### Article 23

The Regional Government provides facilities to fulfill basic health rights and welfare, by:

   - a. provision of child-friendly community health centers;
   - b. provision of breastfeeding rooms in Government and private offices;
   - c. organization and facilitation of facilities and infrastructure for Posyandu in every banjar dinas/neighborhood;
   - d. provision of clean water;
   - e. arrangement of green open spaces and a clean, healthy and comfortable environment;
   - f. provision of smoke-free public spaces; and
   - g. facilities aimed at reducing maternal mortality rates and child mortality rates as well as increasing life expectancy, nutritional standards and health standards.

## Part Five

Right to Education, Utilization of Leisure Time and Cultural Activities

#### Article 24

Every Child has the right to obtain the right to education, utilization of leisure time and cultural activities, as referred to in Article 14 letter d, in the form of:

   - a. participating in early childhood education;
   - b. obtaining the widest possible opportunity to obtain education;
   - c. the right to develop talents, interests and creative abilities;
   - d. recreation; and
   - e. having leisure time to rest and engage in various artistic, cultural and sports activities.

#### Article 25

In fulfilling the right to education, leisure time utilization and cultural activities, the family:

   - a. guarantees the continuity of the child's education from an early age;
   - b. provides the widest possible opportunity for the child to obtain education and builds communication related to teaching and learning activities at school with the school;
   - c. directs and provides opportunities for the child to develop interests, talents and creativity;
   - d. provides leisure time to rest and engage in various arts, cultural and sports activities; and
   - e. sets aside time for recreation together with children according to the situation and conditions of the parents.

#### Article 26

Regional Government provides facilities to fulfill the rights to Education, Leisure Time Utilization and Cultural Activities, by:

   - a. providing the widest possible opportunity for children to obtain education by ensuring the implementation of a 12 (twelve) year compulsory education program for all children free of charge;
   - b. providing opportunities and accessibility for children with disabilities to obtain education;
   - c. organizing inclusive education for children with disabilities;
   - d. optimizing skills education;
   - e. providing city parks, playgrounds, art buildings and sports arenas as a forum to hone and develop children's talents, interests and creativity in the fields of art, culture and sports;
   - f. realizing child-friendly schools at every level of education;
   - g. providing scholarships for students from underprivileged families;
   - h. facilitating school dropouts at Open Schools or Community Learning Activity Centers;
      - i. providing awards to students who excel in academic, art, culture and sports fields;
   - j. issuing policies for education providers not to expel students from educational institutions, by conducting guidance, except if involved in criminal acts;
   - k. organizing student orientation periods that are educational and without violence; and
   - l. establishing children's creativity centers.

## Part Six

Special Protection

#### Article 27

1. Regional Governments are responsible for realizing Child Protection in the Region in the form of coordinating the implementation of the administration of Child Protection in the Region.
2. Coordination of the implementation of the administration of Child Protection in the Region as referred to in paragraph (1) is in order to fulfill the Rights of Children requiring Special Protection.
3. Children requiring Special Protection as referred to in paragraph (1) include:
   - a. children in emergency situations;
   - b. children in conflict with the law;
   - c. children from minority and isolated groups;
   - d. children who are exploited economically and/or sexually;
   - e. children who are victims of narcotics, alcohol, psychotropic substances, and other addictive substances abuse;
   - f. children who are victims of pornography;
   - g. children who are perpetrators of pornography;
   - h. children with human immunodeficiency virus/ acquired immuno deficiency syndrome;
      - i. children who are victims of abduction, sale, and/or trafficking;
   - j. children who are victims of physical and/or psychological violence;
   - k. children who are victims of sexual crimes;
   - l. children who are victims of terrorism networks;
   - m. children who are perpetrators of terrorism networks;
   - n. children with disabilities;
   - o. children who are victims of mistreatment and neglect;
   - p. children with deviant social behavior; and
   - q. children who are victims of stigmatization from labeling related to their parents' condition.

#### Article 28

Every family shall carry out its functions and responsibilities in the optimal fulfillment of children's rights so that children can avoid the special conditions as referred to in Article 27 paragraph (3).

#### Article 29

Regional Government shall endeavor to fulfill the Right to Special Protection for children by:

   - a. formulating programs to prevent children from being involved in situations and conditions as referred to in Article 27 paragraph (3);
   - b. providing access to public services and social security for children with disabilities; and
   - c. providing 24 (twenty-four) hour complaint service facilities, Shelters, rehabilitation centers and orphanages.

# CHAPTER IV

## SAFE HOUSE

#### Article 30

1. Women and child victims of violence who require ongoing assistance have the right to stay in a safe house.
2. The safe house as referred to in paragraph (1) provides a temporary safe place or shelter for victims of violence until their rights are restored.
3. The organization and management of the safe house as referred to in paragraph (1) shall be carried out by the regional apparatus that performs functions in the field of women and child empowerment.
4. Further provisions regarding the organization of the safe house as referred to in paragraph (1) shall be regulated by the Regent's Regulation.

# CHAPTER V

## CHILD-FRIENDLY REGENCY

#### Article 31

1. In the framework of implementing the fulfillment of children's rights and various laws and regulations as well as policies related to the fulfillment of children's rights and special protection of children, the Regional Government, the Private Sector and Community Organizations make efforts to realize a Child-Friendly Regency.
2. To realize a Child-Friendly Regency as referred to in paragraph (1), the Regional Government, the Private Sector, and Community Organizations shall pay attention to children's rights related to the right to life, growth and development, protection, and participation.
3. The Regional Government, the Private Sector, and Community Organizations in realizing a Child-Friendly Regency are incorporated in the Child-Friendly Regency task force.
4. The Child-Friendly Regency task force as referred to in paragraph (3) is responsible for the fulfillment of children's rights related to:
   - a. civil rights and freedoms;
   - b. family environment and alternative care;
   - c. basic health and welfare;
   - d. education, utilization of leisure time, and arts and cultural activities; and
   - e. special protection of children.
5. Further provisions regarding the Child-Friendly Regency as referred to in paragraph (1) shall be regulated by a Regent Regulation.

# CHAPTER VI

## GUIDANCE AND SUPERVISION

#### Article 32

1. The Regent implements guidance and supervision of the implementation of women's empowerment and child protection in the Region. (2) Guidance as referred to in paragraph (1) is implemented by the Regional Apparatus that handles affairs of women's empowerment and child protection.
3. The Regional Apparatus that handles affairs of women's empowerment and child protection as referred to in paragraph (2) serves as the coordinator for the implementation of women's empowerment and child protection in the Region.

#### Article 33

1. The form of guidance as referred to in Article 32 may be implemented through activities:
   - a. providing socialization to the community and business sector regarding policies on women's empowerment and child protection;
   - b. providing books, leaflets, brochures regarding women's empowerment and child protection, and other related issues as well as disseminating them to the community;
   - c. facilitation of capacity building for women's empowerment and child protection institutions;
   - d. facilitation of women's participation forums and women's and children's organizations;
   - e. facilitation of rehabilitation and reintegration services for women and children victims of discrimination, violence and disasters;
   - f. coordinating the participation of the business sector and other institutions, including international assistance for women's empowerment and child protection; and
   - g. providing awards to the community and business sector both individually and institutionally who have made efforts in women's empowerment and child protection.
2. The form of supervision as referred to in Article 32 may be in the form of monitoring and evaluation activities on the implementation of policies, programs and activities for women's empowerment and child protection.

# CHAPTER VII

## COMMUNITY PARTICIPATION

#### Article 34

1. The Regional Government has the obligation to strengthen community participation in the development of women's empowerment and Child Protection.
2. Community participation as referred to in paragraph (1) may be conducted in the form of:
   - a. socialization;
   - b. advocacy;
   - c. counseling;
   - d. guidance;
   - e. supervision;
   - f. facilitation;
   - g. services;
   - h. studies;
      - i. training;
   - j. capital provision; and
   - k. provision of facilities and infrastructure.
3. Strengthening of community participation as referred to in paragraph (1) may be conducted through:
   - a. strengthening cooperation;
   - b. strengthening institutional capacity for prevention and services from the community, Islamic boarding schools and the business sector; and
   - c. financing support.

#### Article 35

1. The business sector has responsibility in the implementation of women's empowerment and child protection.
2. The form of business sector responsibility as referred to in paragraph (4) shall be carried out in the form of:
   - a. financial assistance to service institutions;
   - b. empowerment;
   - c. capacity building;
   - d. publication and information media;
   - e. provision of facilities and infrastructure;
   - f. establishment of special institutions for prevention and complaint services regarding violence; and
   - g. non-binding social assistance.

# CHAPTER VIII

## AWARDS

#### Article 36

The Regent may grant awards to educational institutions, religious institutions, health service provider institutions, professional institutions and the business sector, service institutions, Islamic boarding schools, youth organizations, legal aid organizations, communities, media that have rendered meritorious service in the implementation of women's empowerment and child protection.

# CHAPTER IX

## FINANCING

#### Article 37

1. Financing for the implementation of women's empowerment and child protection is sourced from the Regional Revenue and Expenditure Budget. (2) In addition to being sourced from the Regional Revenue and Expenditure Budget, financing for the implementation of women's empowerment and child protection may be sourced from other legitimate and non-binding sources of financing.

##### CONCERNING

THE IMPLEMENTATION OF EMPOWERMENT AND PROTECTION OF WOMEN AND CHILDREN

##### A. GENERAL EXPLANATION

Pancasila and the 1945 Constitution of the Republic of Indonesia have firmly and clearly provided protection for Human Rights, including the rights of women and children. Every person has equal dignity and worth not only guaranteed by the 1945 Constitution of the Republic of Indonesia, but also internationally recognized through the Universal Declaration of Human Rights (UDHR) of 1948 which explicitly regulates the importance of protection of Human Rights including protection of women and children.

To guarantee the fulfillment of the rights of women and children, regional governments have the obligation and responsibility to implement and support national policies in the administration of governmental affairs in the field of empowerment and protection of women and children in the regions. In addition, regional governments are also given the obligation to conduct supervision over the administration of empowerment and protection of women and children.

The administration of empowerment and protection of women and children is an effort to realize capable women as well as an effort to protect women and children from all acts of violence in order to achieve the goal of welfare. The empowerment and protection of women and children that has been implemented so far has not been optimal and comprehensive in reaching the problems faced by women and children. In addition, public awareness of efforts to empower and protect women and children is still very lacking. To achieve these objectives and to implement the authority of regional governments as outlined in Law Number 23 of 2014 concerning Regional Government in the sub-affairs of empowerment and protection of women and children, a regulation is needed that can serve as a legal umbrella for regional governments in the administration of governmental affairs in the field of women's empowerment and child protection in the regions.

In formulating policies related to women's empowerment and child protection, it is necessary to pay attention to the principles of good legislation formation as regulated in Law Number 12 of 2011 concerning the Formation of Legislation, including:

   - r. clarity of purpose;
   - s. appropriate institution or forming official;
   - t. conformity between type, hierarchy, and material content;
   - u. implementability;
   - v. utility and effectiveness;
   - w. clarity of formulation; and
   - x. openness.

In addition to the principles of legislation formation, it is also necessary to pay attention to the principles of material content which include:

   - a. protection;
   - b. humanity;
   - c. nationality;
   - d. kinship;
   - e. archipelagic principle;
   - f. unity in diversity;
   - g. justice;
   - h. equality of position in law and government;
      - i. order and legal certainty; and/or
   - j. balance, harmony, and congruence.

The administration of governmental affairs in the field of Women's Empowerment and Child Protection aims to increase efforts for the protection of women's rights, fulfillment of children's rights and Special Protection for Children and to increase the role of Regional Governments and the business sector, media and society in efforts for Women's Empowerment and Child Protection. The principal matters contained in the body of the Regional Regulation concerning the Administration of Empowerment and Protection of Women and Children include:

   - a. fulfillment of women's rights;
   - b. empowerment of women;
   - c. protection of women;
   - d. protection of children;
   - e. fulfillment of children's rights;
   - f. integrated service center;
   - g. safe house;
   - h. child-friendly regency;
      - i. guidance and supervision;
   - j. community participation;
   - k. awards; and
   - l. financing.
