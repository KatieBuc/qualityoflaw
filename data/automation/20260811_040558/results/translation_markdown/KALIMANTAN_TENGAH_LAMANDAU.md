# INTEGRATED SERVICES FOR WOMEN AND CHILDREN EMPOWERMENT

# BY THE GRACE OF ALMIGHTY GOD

# REGENT OF LAMANDAU,

Considering : a. that acts of violence against women and children that can cause physical and psychological victims constitute violations of human rights, discrimination against women and children and crimes against human dignity thus hindering the creation of justice and gender equality;

   - b. that protection for women and children victims of violence is an effort to provide a sense of security and freedom from all acts of violence guaranteed by laws and regulations;
   - c. that the regional government is obligated to regulate and manage the handling and provide services for women and children victims of violence which include aspects of law enforcement, legal protection, health services, and spiritual;
   - d. that to improve protection for women and children victims of violence it is necessary to establish a vehicle that can provide integrated services;
   - e. that based on the considerations as stated in letter a, letter b, letter c and letter d, it is necessary to establish a Regional Regulation of Lamandau Regency concerning Integrated Services for Women and Children Empowerment.

# CHAPTER I

## GENERAL PROVISIONS

#### Article 1

In this Regional Regulation, what is meant by:

1. Region is Lamandau Regency.
2. Regional Governance is the administration of Government affairs by the Regional Government and the Regional House of Representatives according to the principles of Autonomy and Co-Administration (Assistance Tasks) with the principle of the widest possible autonomy within the system and principles of the Unitary State of the Republic of Indonesia as referred to in the 1945 Constitution.
3. Regional Government is the Regent together with Regional Apparatus as Elements of Regional Government Administration.
4. Regent is the Regent of Lamandau.
5. Regional House of Representatives is the Regional House of Representatives of Lamandau Regency.
6. Regional Secretary is the Regional Secretary of Lamandau Regency.
7. Child is a person who has not reached the age of 18 (eighteen) years, and is not yet married, including a child who is still in the womb.
8. Woman is every person who is physically born as a woman.
9. Victim is a woman and/or child who experiences misery and/or suffering either directly or indirectly as a result of an act of violence.
10. Prevention is all efforts carried out by government or non-government institutions aimed at providing a sense of security to women and children.
11. Protection is all efforts aimed at providing a sense of security to victims carried out by the government, family, advocates, social institutions or other community institutions, police, prosecutors, courts or other parties either temporarily or based on court decisions or determinations, in connection with acts of violence against children and women.
12. Child Protection is all activities carried out to guarantee and protect children and their rights so that they can live, grow, develop, and participate optimally in accordance with human rights and dignity, as well as receive protection from violence and discrimination.
13. Women's Protection is all activities carried out to guarantee and protect women and their rights so that they can live, grow, develop, and participate optimally in accordance with human rights and dignity, as well as receive protection from violence and discrimination.
14. Misery is any act that results in or may result in suffering, whether psychological, physical, sexual, economic, or social, to the victim.
15. Physical violence is any act that causes pain, injury, wounds or disability to a person's body, miscarriage, fainting and/or causes death.
16. Psychological violence is an act that causes fear, loss of self-confidence, loss of ability to act, feelings of helplessness and/or severe psychological suffering to women and/or children.
17. Sexual violence is any act in the form of sexual harassment, forced sexual relations with others for commercial purposes and/or certain purposes.
18. Service is action taken as soon as possible to the victim, when seeing, hearing, and knowing that violence against the victim will occur, is occurring, or has occurred.
19. Companion is a person or institution that has the ability to provide assistance to victims to conduct counseling, therapy, legal services and advocacy, for the purpose of strengthening victim recovery.
20. Safe House is a temporary residence used to provide protection for women and children victims in accordance with the standard operating procedures, or abbreviated as SOP, as determined.
21. Community is individuals, families, groups, social organizations and/or other community organizations.
22. Integrated Service Center for Women and Children Empowerment, hereinafter abbreviated as P2TP2A, is one form of service facility for women and children in an effort to fulfill information and needs in the fields of education, health, economy, politics, law, protection and prevention of acts of violence and trafficking against women and children.

# CHAPTER II

## PRINCIPLES AND OBJECTIVES

#### Article 2

Integrated services for the empowerment of women and children are conducted based on Pancasila, the 1945 Constitution of the Republic of Indonesia, as well as the basic principles and rights as stipulated in the Conventions on women and children, which include:

   - a. Respect for human rights.
   - b. Justice and gender equality.
   - c. Non-discrimination;
   - d. The best interests of women and or children;
   - e. The right to life, survival, and development;
   - f. Respect for the opinions of women and children;
   - g. Justice and gender equality; and
   - h. Protection of victims.

#### Article 3

The objectives of implementing integrated services for women's and children's empowerment are:

   - a. To guarantee the fulfillment of the rights of women and children in accordance with applicable laws and regulations.
   - b. To prevent and protect women and children from violence.
   - c. To encourage the community to carry out its obligations in the prevention and protection of women and children in accordance with applicable laws and regulations.
   - d. To restore victims of acts of violence they have experienced.
   - e. To take action against perpetrators of domestic violence.
   - f. To provide sex-disaggregated data and information on issues of empowerment and protection of women and children for the community that needs it.
   - g. To encourage the provision of facilities, infrastructure and various types of services in various areas of life for women and children such as data and information centers, counseling, psychological and medical therapy, assistance, education and training, referral centers, skills training and so forth as an effort to improve the quality of life of women, welfare and protection of children managed by the community independently.

# CHAPTER III

## RIGHTS AND OBLIGATIONS

#### Article 4

The rights of children are as follows:

   - a. Every child has the right to live, grow, develop and participate properly in accordance with human dignity and worth, and to receive protection from violence and discrimination;
   - b. Every child has the right to a name as identity and citizenship status;
   - c. Every child has the right to worship according to their religion, to think and express themselves in accordance with their level of intelligence and age, under the guidance of parents;
   - d. Every child has the right to know their parents, to be raised, and to be cared for by their own parents;
   - e. In the event that for any reason the parents cannot guarantee the child's growth and development, or the child is in a state of neglect, then the child has the right to be cared for and adopted as a foster child or adopted child by other persons in accordance with the provisions of applicable laws and regulations;
   - f. Every child has the right to obtain health services and social security in accordance with their physical, mental, spiritual and social needs;
   - g. Every child has the right to obtain education and teaching in the context of developing their personality and level of intelligence in accordance with their interests and talents;
   - h. Specifically for children with disabilities, they have the right to obtain special/extraordinary education, while children who have excellence have the right to receive special education;
      - i. Every child has the right to express and have their opinion heard, to receive, seek, and provide information in accordance with their level of intelligence and age for their self-development in accordance with the values of decency and propriety;
   - j. Every child has the right to rest and utilize leisure time, to associate with peers, to play, to recreate, and to create in accordance with their interests, talents, and level of intelligence for self-development;
   - k. Every child with disabilities has the right to obtain rehabilitation, social assistance, and maintenance of social welfare standards; and
   - l. Every child while in the care of parents, guardians, or any other party responsible for care, has the right to receive protection from treatment of: - Discrimination;

- Exploitation, both economic and sexual;

- Neglect;

- Cruelty, violence, and abuse;

- Injustice; and - Other maltreatment.

   - m. Every child has the right to be cared for by their own parents, unless there are valid reasons and/or legal rules showing that such separation is in the best interests of the child and is a last consideration.
   - n. Every child has the right to obtain protection from: - Abuse in political activities;

- Involvement in armed conflicts;

- Involvement in social unrest;

- Involvement in events containing elements of violence; and - Involvement in warfare.

   - o. Every child has the right to obtain protection from being targets of persecution, torture, or imposition of inhuman punishment.
   - p. Every child has the right to obtain freedom in accordance with the law.
   - q. Arrest, detention, or criminal imprisonment of children shall only be carried out if in accordance with applicable law and can only be carried out as a last resort.
   - r. Every child who is deprived of their freedom has the right to: - Receive humane treatment and placement separated from adults;

- Obtain legal assistance or other assistance effectively at every stage of applicable legal remedies; and - Defend themselves and obtain justice before an objective and impartial juvenile court in a hearing closed to the public.

   - s. Every child who becomes a victim or perpetrator of sexual violence or who is in conflict with the law has the right to confidentiality.
   - t. Every child who becomes a victim or perpetrator of a criminal act has the right to receive legal assistance and other assistance.

#### Article 5

Victims of domestic violence have the right to obtain: a. Protection from family members, the police, the prosecutor's office, the court, legal aid, social institutions, or other parties either temporarily or based on the determination of a protection order from the court; b. Health services in accordance with medical needs;

   - c. Special handling related to the confidentiality of the victim; d. Assistance by social workers and legal aid at every level of the examination process in accordance with the provisions of laws and regulations; and
   - e. Spiritual guidance services.

#### Article 6

Every child has the obligation to:

   - a. Respect parents, guardians, and teachers;
   - b. Love family, society, and care for friends;
   - c. Love the homeland, nation and state;
   - d. Perform worship in accordance with the teachings of their religion; and
   - e. Practice noble ethics and morals.

#### Article 7

Every person is prohibited from committing domestic violence against persons within the scope of their household, by means of:

   - a. Physical violence;
   - b. Psychological violence;
   - c. Sexual violence; and
   - d. Household neglect.

#### Article 8

1. Physical violence is an act that results in pain, falling ill, or serious injury.
2. Psychological violence is an act that results in fear, loss of self-confidence, loss of ability to act, a sense of helplessness, and/or severe psychological suffering in a person.
3. Sexual violence includes:
   - a. Forced sexual relations committed against a person residing within the scope of the household.
   - b. Forced sexual relations against a person within the scope of the household with another person for commercial purposes and/or certain purposes.
4. Household neglect is an act of not providing livelihood, not providing care, or not providing maintenance to a person within the scope of the household.
5. Neglect also applies to any person who causes economic dependency by means of restricting and/or prohibiting decent work inside or outside the home so that the victim is under the control of that person.

#### Article 9

1. The state, government, society, family, and parents have the obligation and responsibility for the implementation of child protection. (2) The government is responsible for efforts to prevent domestic violence.

#### Article 10

1. The state, government, society, family, and parents have the obligation and responsibility for the implementation of child protection.
2. The government is responsible for efforts to prevent domestic violence.
3. Every person who hears, sees or knows of the occurrence of domestic violence is obligated to make efforts in accordance with their capabilities to:
   - a. Prevent the continuation of criminal acts;
   - b. Provide protection to victims;
   - c. Provide emergency assistance; and
   - d. Assist in the process of submitting applications for protection orders.

# CHAPTER IV

IMPLEMENTATION OF PROTECTION FOR CHILDREN AND VICTIMS OF DOMESTIC VIOLENCE

#### Article 11

1. Every child receives protection to worship according to their religion. (2) Before the child can determine their choice, the religion embraced by the child follows the religion of their parents.
3. The state, government, society, family, parents, guardians, and social institutions guarantee the protection of children in embracing their religion. (4) Protection of children in embracing their religion includes development, guidance, and practice of religious teachings for children.

#### Article 12

1. The Government is obligated to provide facilities and organize comprehensive health efforts for children, so that every child obtains an optimal degree of health from the time they are in the womb.
2. Comprehensive health efforts include promotive, preventive, curative and rehabilitative both for basic health services and referral.
3. Parents and family are responsible for maintaining the health of children and caring for children from the time they are in the womb.
4. The State, government, family and parents are obligated to protect children from acts of:
   - a. Removal of children's organs and/or children's body tissue without regard to the health of the child;
   - b. Buying and selling of organs and/or children's body tissue; and
   - c. Health research that uses children as research objects without the permission of parents and does not prioritize the best interests of the child.

#### Article 13

1. The local government is obligated to provide basic education for a minimum of 9 (nine) years for all children.
2. The state, government, family and parents are obligated to provide the widest possible opportunity to children to obtain education.
3. Education is directed toward:
   - a. Development of the child's attitude and personality capabilities, talents, mental and physical abilities to reach their optimal potential;
   - b. Development of respect for human rights and fundamental freedoms;
   - c. Development of respect for parents, cultural identity, language and their own values, national values where the child resides, from where the child originates, and civilizations that differ from one's own civilization;
   - d. Preparation of the child for a responsible life; and
   - e. Development of respect and love for the environment.
4. Children with physical and/or mental disabilities are given equal opportunity and accessibility to obtain regular education and special education.
5. Children in and around schools must be protected from acts of violence committed by teachers, school administrators or their peers within the school concerned or other educational institutions.

#### Article 14

1. The Government is obligated to provide care and maintenance for neglected children, both in institutions and outside social institutions.
2. The Government in providing care and maintenance is obligated to endeavor and assist children, so that children can:
   - a. Participate;
   - b. Freely express opinions and think in accordance with their conscience and religion;
   - c. Freely receive oral or written information in accordance with the age stage and development of the child;
   - d. Freely associate and assemble;
   - e. Freely rest, play, recreate, create and engage in cultural arts; and
   - f. Obtain play facilities that meet health and safety requirements.

#### Article 15

The Government and other state institutions are obligated and responsible to provide special protection to children in emergency situations, children in conflict with the law, children from minority and isolated groups, children exploited economically and/or sexually, children who are trafficked, children who are victims of narcotics abuse, alcohol, psychotropic substances, and other addictive substances (napza), children who are victims of kidnapping, sale and trafficking, children who are victims of violence both physical and/or mental, children with disabilities, and children who are victims of maltreatment and neglect.

#### Article 16

1. Within 1x24 (one time twenty-four) hours calculated from the time of knowing or receiving a report of domestic violence, the police are obligated to immediately provide temporary protection to the victim.
2. Temporary protection is given for a maximum of 7 (seven) days from the time the victim is received or handled.
3. Within 1x24 (one time twenty-four) hours calculated from the provision of protection, the police are obligated to request a letter of determination of a protection order from the court.

#### Article 17

In providing temporary protection, the police may cooperate with health workers, social workers, support volunteers, and/or spiritual advisors to accompany victims.

#### Article 18

The Police are obligated to provide information to victims regarding the victims' rights to receive services and assistance.

#### Article 19

The Police are obligated to immediately conduct an investigation after learning of or receiving a report regarding the occurrence of domestic violence.

#### Article 20

The Police shall immediately inform the victim about:

   - a. The identity of the officers for introduction to the victim;
   - b. Domestic violence is a crime against human dignity; and
   - c. The obligation of the police to protect the victim.

#### Article 21

In providing health services to victims, health workers must:

   - a. Examine the health of victims in accordance with their professional standards;
   - b. Prepare a written report on the results of the examination of victims and a visum et repertum at the request of police investigators, or a medical certificate that has the same legal force as evidence.

#### Article 22

In providing services, social workers must:

   - a. Conduct counseling to strengthen and provide a sense of security for victims;
   - b. Provide information regarding the rights of victims to obtain protection from the police and the issuance of protection orders from the court;
   - c. Escort victims to safe houses or alternative residences; and
   - d. Carry out integrated coordination in providing services to victims with the police, social services, social institutions needed by victims.

#### Article 23

In providing services, volunteer assistants may:

   - a. Inform the victim of their right to obtain one or several assistants;
   - b. Accompany the victim at the investigation level, prosecution level or court examination level by guiding the victim to objectively and completely describe the domestic violence they have experienced;
   - c. Listen empathetically to all statements of the victim so that the victim feels safe being accompanied by the assistant; and
   - d. Actively provide psychological and physical reinforcement to the victim.

#### Article 24

In providing services, spiritual counselors must provide explanations regarding the rights and obligations of the victim and provide strengthening of faith and piety to the victim.

#### Article 25

In providing protection and services, advocates are obligated to:

   - a. Provide legal consultation that includes information regarding the rights of victims and the judicial process;
   - b. Accompany victims at the investigation, prosecution, and court examination levels and assist victims to comprehensively describe the domestic violence they have experienced; and
   - c. Coordinate with fellow law enforcers, volunteer companions, and social workers so that the judicial process proceeds as it should.

#### Article 26

1. Victims have the right to directly report domestic violence to the police either at the place where the victim is located or at the scene of the incident.
2. Victims may grant power of attorney to family members or other persons to report domestic violence to the police either at the place where the victim is located or at the scene of the incident.
3. In the event that the victim is a child, the report may be made by the parent, guardian, caregiver, or the child concerned, which shall be carried out in accordance with the provisions of the applicable laws and regulations.

#### Article 27

The head of the court within a period of 7 (seven) days from the receipt of the application must issue a letter of determination containing a protection order for the victim and other family members, unless there are justifiable reasons.

#### Article 28

An application to obtain a protection order may be submitted by:

   - a. The victim or the victim's family;
   - b. A friend of the victim;
   - c. The Police;
   - d. An accompanying volunteer; and
   - e. A spiritual advisor.

#### Article 29 (1) An application for a protection order shall be submitted in oral or written form.

2. In the event the application is submitted orally, the clerk of the local district court shall record the application.
3. In the event the application for a protection order is submitted by family, a friend of the victim, the police, an accompanying volunteer, or a spiritual advisor, the victim must give consent. In certain circumstances, the application may be submitted without the victim's consent.

#### Article 30

Upon application by the victim or their representative, the court may consider to:

   - a. Establish a special condition;
   - b. Modify or revoke a special condition of the protection order.

#### Article 31

1. A protection order may be granted for a maximum period of 1 (one) year.
2. A protection order may be extended by court determination.
3. An application for extension of a protection order shall be submitted 7 (seven) days before the expiration of its validity period.

#### Article 32

1. The court may declare one or more additional protection orders.
2. In granting additional protection orders, the court must consider statements from the victim, health workers, social workers, support volunteers, and/or spiritual counselors.

#### Article 33

1. Based on consideration of potential dangers that may arise, the court may stipulate one or more additional conditions in the protection order.
2. In granting additional conditions in the protection order, the court must consider statements from the victim, health workers, social workers, accompanying volunteers, and/or spiritual advisors.

#### Article 34

1. The Police may arrest and subsequently detain without a warrant the perpetrator who is believed to have violated a protection order, even though the violation was not committed at the place where the police officer is on duty.
2. The arrest and detention as referred to in paragraph (1) must be provided with an arrest and detention warrant after 1x24 (one time twenty-four) hours.
3. Suspension of detention does not apply to detention as referred to in paragraph (1) and paragraph (2).

#### Article 35

1. To provide protection to victims, the police may arrest the perpetrator with sufficient preliminary evidence for having violated the protection order.
2. The arrest as referred to in paragraph (1) may be followed by detention accompanied by a detention order within 1x24 (one time twenty-four) hours.

#### Article 36

1. The victim, police or accompanying volunteer may submit a written report regarding an alleged violation of a protection order.
2. In the event the court receives a written report as referred to in paragraph (1), the perpetrator is ordered to appear within 3x24 (three times twenty-four) hours for examination.
3. The examination as referred to in paragraph (2) is conducted by the court at the place where the perpetrator previously lived together with the victim at the time the violation is alleged to have occurred.

#### Article 37

1. If the court knows that the perpetrator has violated the protection order and is suspected of committing further violations, then the court may require the perpetrator to make a written statement containing a commitment to comply with the protection order.
2. If the perpetrator still disregards the written statement as referred to in paragraph (1), the court may detain the perpetrator for a maximum of 30 days.
3. Detention as referred to in paragraph (2), shall be accompanied by a detention order.

# CHAPTER V

## VICTIM RECOVERY

#### Article 38

For the purpose of recovery, victims may obtain services from:

   - a. Health workers;
   - b. Social workers;
   - c. Volunteer companions; and
   - d. Spiritual counselors.

#### Article 39

1. Health workers are obligated to examine victims in accordance with their professional standards. (2) In the event that victims require treatment, health workers are obligated to restore and rehabilitate the health of victims.

#### Article 40

Social workers, volunteer assistants, and/or spiritual counselors are obligated to provide services to victims in the form of counseling to strengthen and/or provide a sense of security for victims.

# CHAPTER VI

## INSTITUTIONAL ARRANGEMENTS

#### Article 41

1. In the context of providing protection services to women and children who are victims of violence, the Regional Government establishes P2TP2A.
2. The membership of P2TP2A as referred to in (1), consists of: a. regional government elements;
   - b. community elements; and
   - c. academic elements.
3. The membership of P2TP2A as referred to in paragraph (2), shall be determined by Regent's Decree.

#### Article 42

1. The Integrated Service Center for Women and Children Empowerment (P2TP2A) facilitates the provision of various services for the community, both physical and non-physical, which include information, referral, consultation/counseling, skills training and other activities.
2. The Integrated Service Center for Women and Children Empowerment (P2TP2A) can also serve as a place to conduct training for cadres who have great commitment and concern for women's and children's issues in all fields (health, education, economy, politics, law as well as protection of women and children from acts of violence and human trafficking) so that they can then work together and participate in providing services to the community in a forum for improving the quality of life and protection for women and children.

#### Article 43

Programs that can be implemented by the Integrated Service Center for the Empowerment of Women and Children (P2TP2A) include: a. Women's empowerment.

   - b. Protection of women and children from acts of violence and human trafficking.
   - c. Communication, information and education.
   - d. Increasing community member participation.
   - e. Increasing manager capacity.

#### Article 44

The success indicators of the Integrated Service Center for Women and Children Empowerment (P2TP2A) are as follows:

   - a. The establishment and functioning of the Integrated Service Center for Women and Children Empowerment (P2TP2A) that provides women empowerment services, protection of women and children in the fields of law, politics, education, health, economy, acts of violence and human trafficking.
   - b. An increase in the number of women and children utilizing the Integrated Service Center for Women and Children Empowerment (P2TP2A).
   - c. An increase in the number of women and children to obtain training.
   - d. The functioning of dialogue and communication mechanisms between the government, the community, and the business sector.
   - e. The existence of cooperation between the Integrated Service Center for Women and Children Empowerment (P2TP2A) and the government and the business sector.

# CHAPTER VII

## REPORTING

#### Article 45

1. The Integrated Service Center for Women and Children Empowerment (P2TP2A) is obligated to report the implementation of activities to the Regent.
2. The submission of written reports as referred to in paragraph (1), shall be carried out at least once every 3 (three) months.

# CHAPTER VIII

#### Article 46

Financing for the implementation of Integrated Services for Women's and Children's Empowerment is sourced from:

   - a. Regional revenue and expenditure budget of Lamandau Regency.
   - b. Other legal and non-binding sources.

##### I. GENERAL ELUCIDATION

All forms of violence in general and acts of violence against women and/or children are a form of human rights violation and constitute a form of crime against human dignity as well as discriminatory treatment; therefore, it is the obligation of the Government and the Regional Government to realize guarantees of security/protection for honor, dignity, property, freedom from torture, or treatment that degrades human dignity and discriminatory treatment.

Nowadays, it turns out that acts of violence against women and/or children still frequently occur within the family environment as well as within society which clearly result in misery and suffering of women and/or children physically, psychologically, or sexually, including threats of certain actions, coercion, or deprivation of rights.

Services and handling, guidance as well as empowerment of women and/or children have not been optimal so far. Various causes mentioned above are that most women and/or children victims of acts of violence choose to remain silent and their problems end with the collapse of women and children into powerlessness.

This regional regulation is intended as an effort by the Lamandau Regency Government in meeting the demands of society by providing services for the rights of women and children victims of acts of violence in a comprehensive, clear and firm manner to protect or side with victims.
