7. Office of Population Control, Family Planning, Women's Empowerment and Child Protection, hereinafter abbreviated as DPPKBPPPA, is the Office of Population Control, Family Planning, Women's Empowerment and Child Protection of the City of Prabumulih.
8. Regional General Hospital, hereinafter abbreviated as RSUD, is the Regional General Hospital (RSUD) of the City of Prabumulih.
9. Integrated Service Center for Women's Empowerment and Child Protection, hereinafter abbreviated as P2TP2A, is the Integrated Service Center for Women's Empowerment and Child Protection of the City of Prabumulih.
10. Community-Based Integrated Protection, hereinafter abbreviated as PATBM, is Community-Based Integrated Protection (PATBM) of the City of Prabumulih.
11. Community Health Center, hereinafter abbreviated as Puskesmas, is the Community Health Center (Puskesmas) of the City of Prabumulih.
12. Regional Technical Implementation Unit, hereinafter abbreviated as UPTD, is the Technical Implementation element of the Office that carries out Technical Operational activities and/or certain Technical Supporting Activities.
13. Private is part of the economy outside Government ownership, consisting of Individuals or groups of Legal Entity Individuals who carry out Public services in the City of Prabumulih.
14. Women's Empowerment is an effort of Protection, strengthening of human rights, improving quality of life, and increasing women's participation in development.
15. Child Protection is all activities to guarantee and protect Children and their rights so they can live, grow, develop, and participate optimally in accordance with human dignity and worth, and receive Protection from Violence and discrimination.
16. Child is a person who has not reached the age of 18 (eighteen) years, including a Child still in the womb and not yet married.
17. Prevention is direct efforts carried out by the Regional Government, community and parents and/or family to prevent violations of the rights of women and Children.
18. Violence is any act that results in misery or suffering either physically, sexually, psychologically and/or domestic neglect including threats to commit acts, coercion, or unlawful deprivation of a person's freedom.
19. Children's Forum is a forum for Children's participation formed with the aim of facilitating Children's involvement in decision-making processes and enjoying changes concerning their lives either directly or indirectly, which is carried out with the consent and will of all Children based on awareness and understanding, in accordance with age and level of thinking maturity.
20. Child-Friendly City, hereinafter abbreviated as KLA, is a City that has a Child rights-based development system through the integration of commitments and resources of government, community and business world that are planned comprehensively and sustainably in policies, programs and activities to ensure the fulfillment of Children's rights.
21. Gender Mainstreaming is a strategy built to integrate gender into an integral dimension of planning, formulation, implementation, monitoring, and evaluation of policies, programs, and development activities in the Region.
22. Community is individuals, families, groups and social organizations and/or community organizations.
23. Women Victims of Violence are women who experience actions that result in misery or suffering physically, sexually or psychologically, including threats of certain actions, coercion or arbitrary deprivation of freedom, whether occurring in the public sphere or in private life.
24. Child Victims of Violence are Children who experience actions that result in misery or suffering physically, mentally, sexually, psychologically or neglect that threatens life, body and degrades human dignity.
25. Human trafficking is the act of recruitment, transportation, harboring, sending, transfer, or receipt of a person by threat of Violence, use of Violence, kidnapping, confinement, forgery, fraud, abuse of power or vulnerable position, debt bondage or giving payment or benefits, so as to obtain consent from the person holding control over that other person, whether carried out within the Country or between Countries, for the purpose of exploitation or resulting in the person being exploited.
26. Complaint handling is a series of actions carried out by integrated service providers to follow up on reports of acts of Violence against women and Children, submitted by victims, families or the community.
27. Social rehabilitation is a service aimed at restoring and developing the ability of a person experiencing social dysfunction so they can carry out their social functions normally.
28. Law enforcement is the action of officials authorized by the state to implement laws and regulations.
29. Legal aid is legal services provided by legal assistants and advocates to carry out the process of assisting witnesses and/or victims of Violence against women and Children that is gender sensitive.
30. Repatriation is an effort to return women and Child victims of Violence from abroad to the debarkation point/entry point, or from the receiving Region to the Region of origin.
31. Social reintegration is an effort to reunite victims with family, substitute family, or community that can provide Protection and fulfillment of needs for victims.
32. Safe house is a temporary shelter that aims to provide Protection and a sense of security to victims of Violence.
33. Shelter house is a forum for guidance and welfare services for street Children that carries out social assistance/guidance activities, maintenance, care, education, and health, skills guidance in order to ensure that Children do not carry out activities on the streets so they can grow and develop normally.
34. Spiritual Guidance is an effort to restore the psyche through strengthening the religion adhered to by the victim.
35. Public service provider is every state organizing institution, corporation, independent institution formed based on Law for public service activities, and other legal entities formed solely for public service activities.
36. Community Organization is an organization established and formed by the community voluntarily based on common aspirations, will, needs, interests, activities and goals to participate in development for the achievement of the goals of the Unitary State of the Republic of Indonesia based on Pancasila.
37. Parents are biological father and/or mother, or step father and/or mother, or adoptive father and/or mother.
38. Family is the smallest unit in society consisting of husband and wife or husband, wife and their Child, or father and his Child, or mother and her Child, or blood family in direct line upward or downward up to the third degree.
39. Customs are eternal and hereditary codes of conduct from one generation to another generation as heritage, so strong is its integration with community behavior patterns.

Article The Implementation of Women's Empowerment and Child Protection is based on the principles of:

   - a. best interests of women and Children,
   - b. gender justice and gender equality,
   - c. local wisdom,
   - d. enforcement and legal certainty,
   - e. non-discrimination,
   - f. right to life, survival, and development,
   - g. respect for Children's opinions.

Article This Regional Regulation aims to ensure the fulfillment of women's empowerment and child protection in the City:

   - a. as a guide in planning, policies and strategies for Women's Empowerment and Child Protection;
   - b. protecting and preventing women and Children from actions, decisions and policies that violate the rights of women and Children, increasing the role and value of local wisdom and the role of customs in Women's Empowerment and Child Protection;
   - c. and as a legal umbrella for PD within the Government of the City of Prabumulih that oversees women's empowerment and child protection in order to provide assistance and support to women and child victims starting from investigation at the police, prosecution to trial in court.

Article The scope of regulation of Women's Empowerment and Child Protection includes:

   - a. protection of women;
   - b. protection of Children;
   - c. fulfillment of Children's rights;
   - d. improvement of quality of life of women and children;
   - e. community participation; and
   - f. guidance, supervision, assistance.
   - g. gender and child data system, disaggregated data on women and children.

# CHAPTER III

## PROTECTION OF WOMEN AND CHILDREN

## Part One

General

#### Article 16

1. The City Government in efforts to Protect women and children is responsible for:
   - a. establishing and implementing policies, programs and activities for the protection of women and children,
   - b. making efforts to prevent acts of Violence against women and children including criminal acts of human trafficking,
   - c. improving the effectiveness of services for women and children victims of acts of Violence,
   - d. increasing efforts to Protect female workers,
   - e. strengthening and developing institutions providing Protection services for women and children,
   - f. conducting cooperation with the Government, other Regional Governments, private parties, business sectors and/or NGOs in efforts to prevent acts of Violence against women and children, and
   - g. involving the active role of families, customary leaders, religious leaders, communities, and institutions in the field of Protection of women and children.
2. The responsibility of the City Government in efforts to Protect women as referred to in paragraph (1) is implemented by PD in accordance with their duties and functions and may coordinate with relevant agencies.

## Part Two

Prevention of Acts of Violence against Women and Children

#### Article 17

In order to prevent acts of Violence against women and children, the City Government: conducts socialization regarding the prevention of Violence against:

   - a. women and children by optimizing the role of customary institutions, education, culture, religion and community organizations, building networks and cooperation with law enforcement apparatus;
   - b. government apparatus, universities and various non-governmental organizations that work and/or care for women and children victims of Violence, providing public facilities that are comfortable and safe for women and children, and establishing an Integrated Service Center for Women and Children Empowerment (P2TP2A) established by decree of the Mayor as well as providing;
   - d. a Secretariat for the Integrated Service Center for Women and Children Empowerment.

Third Part Effectiveness of Services for Women and Children Victims of Violence First Part General

#### Article 18

Protection of women and children victims of acts of Violence (1) is implemented through integrated services.

The provision of integrated services to women and children victims of acts (2) of Violence as referred to in paragraph (1) uses the principles of:

   - a. gender responsive,
   - b. non-discrimination,
   - c. equal and respectful relations,
   - d. maintaining confidentiality, providing a sense of security and comfort,
   - e. respecting individual differences, non-judgmental,
   - f. respecting the choices and decisions of victims,
   - h. sensitive to the background and condition of victims and the use of language that is appropriate and understood by victims,
   - j. fast and simple,
   - k. empathy, and
   - l. fulfillment of the rights of women and children.

(121

#### Article 19

The forms of integrated services for women and children victims of acts of Violence include:

   - a. handling of complaints, health rehabilitation;
   - b. social rehabilitation, victim assistance, law enforcement and legal aid;
   - c. repatriation and social reintegration.

Second Part Handling of Complaints

#### Article 20

Integrated services for handling complaints as referred to in Article 19 letter are implemented by:

   - a. hospitals;
   - b. community health centers;
   - c. women and children service units;
   - d. shelter (house of protection) and trauma center;
   - e. women crisis centre;
   - f. advisory board for marriage guidance and preservation;
   - g. police;
   - h. prosecutor's office;
      - i. courts;
   - j. ministry of religious affairs; and/or
   - k. legal aid institutions.

#### Article 21

Complaints regarding acts of violence against women and children may be submitted by the following reporters:

   - a. the victim;
   - b. family;
   - c. the community; and/or
   - d. community organizations in the field of the protection of women and children.

Reporters as referred to in paragraph (1) may submit complaints:

   - a. directly;
   - b. by telephone; and/or
   - c. by letter.

In addition to handling complaints submitted by reporters, the agency/department in charge of the protection of women and children, as well as officers at service institutions as referred to in Article 20, are also authorized to provide assistance in handling victims of acts of violence against women and children obtained through other information.

Further provisions regarding the procedures for complaint-handling services as referred to in paragraph (1) and paragraph (2) shall be regulated by Mayor Regulation.

Third Part Health Rehabilitation

#### Article 22

1. Health rehabilitation as referred to in Article 19 letter d constitutes efforts that include aspects of:
   - a. promotive;
   - b. preventive;
   - c. curative; and
   - d. rehabilitative.
2. Health rehabilitation as referred to in paragraph (1) is conducted comprehensively, can be accessed 24 (twenty-four) hours, is of quality, and is carried out in accordance with health service standards.
3. Women and child victims of acts of Violence and children in conflict with the law have the right to free health services, including:
   - a. medical services;
   - b. medicolegal services;
   - c. psychosocial counseling; and
   - d. visum.
4. Further provisions regarding the procedures for implementing health services as referred to in paragraph (3) shall be regulated by Mayoral Regulation.

## Part Four Social Rehabilitation

#### Article 23

Social rehabilitation as referred to in Article 19 letter (1) is intended to restore and develop the capabilities of women and children victims of acts of Violence who experience social dysfunction so that they can carry out their social functions in society properly.

Social rehabilitation as referred to in paragraph (1) includes (2) services: psychosocial motivation and diagnosis,

   - a. care and nurturing,
   - b. vocational training and entrepreneurship development,
   - c. mental and spiritual guidance,
   - d. physical guidance,
   - e. social guidance and psychosocial counseling,
   - f. accessibility services,
   - g. social assistance and aid,
   - h. resocialization guidance,
      - i. follow-up guidance, and/or
   - k. referral.
3. The implementation of services as referred to in paragraph (2) shall be organized by:
   - a. Regional Apparatus that has the duties and functions in the social field,
   - b. Regional Apparatus that oversees Women's and Children's Empowerment, and may cooperate with integrated service centers, women crisis centre(s) or existing social community organizations, or
   - c. the Ministry of Religious Affairs.

#### Article 24

1. Counseling services as referred to in Article 23 paragraph (2) letter l aim to:
   - a. assist women and children victims of acts of violence in recognizing their problems and finding effective ways to overcome them themselves;
   - b. empower women and children victims of acts of Violence to determine their future;
   - c. provide moral support to women and children victims of acts of Violence in facing the process they are undergoing; and
   - d. make women and children victims of acts of Violence feel accepted in the community environment.
2. Counseling services are carried out by social rehabilitation officers who are gender responsive, consisting of:
   - a. counselors;
   - b. psychologists; and
   - c. psychiatrists.
3. Counseling services are carried out in places that guarantee a sense of security, comfort, and confidentiality of information from women and children victims of acts of Violence.
4. Stages of counseling services include:
   - a. initial counseling;
   - b. follow-up counseling;
   - c. case discussion; and
   - d. site visits.

#### Article 25

1. The City Government may provide safe houses for women and child victims of acts of Violence.
2. The provision of safe house services as referred to in paragraph (1) aims to protect women and child victims of acts of Violence from intimidation by perpetrators or other parties.
3. The location of safe houses as referred to in paragraph (1) must be kept confidential.
4. The criteria for women and child victims of acts of Violence who require safe houses include:
   - a. their lives are threatened,
   - b. receiving rejection from family or community,
   - c. requiring intensive services but their residence is relatively far away, and/or
   - d. abandoned if not placed in a safe house.
5. The implementation of safe house services is carried out by social rehabilitation officers who have sensitivity towards women's empowerment and child protection and/or gender awareness, consisting of:

   - c. community leaders.

   - b. leaving the final decision in the hands of the victim (self determination).

## Part Five

Enforcement and Legal Assistance

#### Article 27

The enforcement services and legal assistance as referred to in (1)

#### Article 19 letter [specify letter] constitutes an action by law enforcement officials to implement statutory regulations in the context of handling cases of violence against women and children, especially to impose sanctions on perpetrators and provide Protection for witnesses and/or victims. Law enforcement services and legal assistance as referred to in paragraph (1) shall be carried out by prioritizing the best interests of the victim.

#### Article 28

Law enforcement and legal aid services are aimed at providing (1) assistance and Protection for women and children victims of acts of Violence and witnesses with a gender-responsive approach so that victims obtain justice and legal certainty.

Types of legal aid services include: (2)

   - a. legal aid in the reporting process and preparation of official examination reports,
   - b. legal aid in the prosecution process at the prosecutor's office,
   - c. legal aid in the examination process at court,
   - d. legal aid in the court hearing decision process until there is a final legal decision, and
   - e. legal aid in the mediation process.
3. The City Government provides legal aid to women and children victims of acts of Violence based on the provisions of laws and regulations.

In providing legal aid as referred to in paragraph (3) (4) the Regional Government may cooperate with legal aid institutions, women crisis centers, advocates and/or other legal assistants who are gender sensitive.

#### Article 29

Repatriation is intended to return women and children victims of acts of Violence to their region of origin.

1. The repatriation of women and children victims of acts of Violence is accompanied by social reintegration efforts.

#### Article 30

   - e. lactation rooms in every public facility.

#### Article 40

Communication, information and education as referred to in Article 39 letter are communication activities to increase knowledge and improve the attitudes and behavior of families and the community in the population and family planning program.

#### Article 41

1. In addition to the City Government, private parties that organize Public services are obligated to provide a lactation room.
2. The lactation room as referred to in paragraph (1) shall be a proper and adequate room.
3. Private parties that do not provide a lactation room as referred to in paragraph (2) shall be subject to administrative sanctions in the form of:
   - a. written warning,
   - b. limitation of business activities,
   - c. suspension of business activities,
   - d. administrative fine, and/or
   - e. revocation of permit.

##### PROTECTION AND FULFILLMENT OF CHILDREN'S RIGHTS

## Part One

General

#### Article 42

1. The City Government, in efforts to protect and fulfill the rights of Children, is responsible for:
   - a. establishing and implementing policies and programs/activities for the protection and fulfillment of Children's rights,
   - b. respecting and guaranteeing the human rights of every Child without distinguishing ethnicity, religion, race, class, gender, ethnic group, culture and language, the legal status of the Child, the birth order of the Child, and physical and/or mental condition,
   - c. strengthening and developing service provider institutions for improving family quality in realizing Children's rights,
   - d. guaranteeing the Protection, care, and welfare of Children with due regard to the rights and obligations of parents, guardians, or other persons legally responsible for the Child,
   - e. providing facilities and infrastructure in the implementation of the fulfillment of Children's rights,
   - f. guaranteeing the right of Children to express opinions in accordance with the age and level of intelligence of the Child,
   - g. conducting supervision and guidance in the context of implementation, and
   - h. conducting cooperation with private parties in the implementation of Child Protection.
2. The responsibility of the City Government in efforts to Protect Children as referred to in paragraph (1) is implemented by PD in accordance with its duties and functions.

Second Part Birth Registration

#### Article 43

1. The City Government is obligated to conduct the registration of the birth of a Child by issuing a birth certificate.
2. The birth certificate as referred to in paragraph (1) shall be issued free of charge.

## Part Three Education

#### Article 44

The City Government guarantees the implementation of a compulsory education program of (nine) years for Children aged (seven) to 15 (fifteen) years.

#### Article 45

1. Every education unit organizer must provide opportunities for a Child to obtain educational services without discrimination. (2) Every education unit organizer is prohibited from expelling a Child from an educational institution unless there is a guarantee for the continuity of the Child's education.
3. In the event that a Child victim of Violence experiences dropping out of school, the education unit organizer must coordinate with relevant agencies.

#### Article 46

1. Every Child has the right to obtain education and teaching in the context of developing their personality and level of intelligence in accordance with their interests and talents.
2. Every Child has the right to receive Protection in educational units from sexual crimes and Violence committed by educators, educational personnel, fellow students, and/or other parties.

#### Article 47

1. Children with disabilities and Children with special needs are given equal opportunities to obtain formal, non-formal and informal education services, including inclusive education programs.
2. Regional governments provide educators for inclusive education at every education unit.

Fourth Part Health and Social

#### Article 48

1. The City Government is obligated to provide comprehensive health services for Children, so that every Child obtains optimal health status from the time they are in the womb.
2. Comprehensive health services as referred to in paragraph (1) include:
   - a. promotive, preventive, curative and rehabilitative efforts for basic health services; and
   - b. exemption from cost burden for malnourished Children, Children with disabilities, Children with special needs, Children infected with HIV/AIDS, child workers, Children who are victims of narcotics abuse, alcohol, psychotropic substances and other addictive substances, Children who become victims of Violence, and Children who are victims of human trafficking.
3. The City Government, community, family and parents are obligated to make efforts so that Children who are born are protected from diseases that threaten survival and/or cause disability.

#### Article 49

1. Promotive efforts for basic health services as referred to in Article 48 paragraph (2) letter [letter] are aimed at improving health status more optimally.
2. Promotive efforts as referred to in paragraph (1) are carried out by providing socialization or counseling to the community regarding healthy lifestyle patterns which include:
   - a. the importance of exclusive breastfeeding for infants,
   - b. the importance of immunization for toddlers,
   - c. clean living patterns,
   - d. the importance of nutritious food, and
   - e. the dangers of drugs and smoking.
3. In addition to promotive efforts as referred to in paragraph (2), the City Government may carry out other activities in the context of the implementation of health.

#### Article 50

1. Preventive efforts for basic health services as referred to in Article 48 paragraph (2) letter ... are aimed at preventing the occurrence of disease or health problems.
2. The efforts as referred to in paragraph (1) are carried out by: a. requiring mothers to provide exclusive breastfeeding to infants up to the age of (six) months,
   - b. providing free immunization and vitamins to infants and toddlers, c. providing nutritious food to toddlers from underprivileged families, d. optimizing the role of posyandu for health services for mothers and toddlers,
   - e. conducting periodic health examinations, and
   - f. providing sanitation facilities and clean water in every school.

#### Article 51

1. Curative efforts of basic health services as referred to in Article 48 paragraph (2) letter are aimed at preventing diseases from becoming more severe through treatment.
2. Treatment as referred to in paragraph (1) is implemented free of charge for Children who come from underprivileged families.

#### Article 52

1. Rehabilitative efforts of basic health services as referred to in Article 48 paragraph (2) letter [specify letter] are aimed at maintaining and restoring conditions after illness.
2. Rehabilitative efforts as referred to in paragraph (1) are carried out through periodic health examinations.

#### Article 53

1. The implementation of social welfare is directed towards:
   - a. individuals,
   - b. families,
   - c. groups; and/or
   - d. communities.
2. The implementation of social welfare as referred to in paragraph (1) is prioritized for those who have lives that are not decent from a humanitarian perspective and have social problem criteria:
   - a. poverty,
   - b. neglect,
   - c. disability,
   - d. remoteness,
   - e. social dysfunction and behavioral deviation,
   - f. disaster victims; and/or
   - g. victims of violence, exploitation and discrimination.
3. Social welfare as referred to in paragraph (1) may be in the form of service provision:
   - a. health,
   - b. education,
   - c. legal aid,
   - d. assistance,
   - e. social, mental and spiritual guidance,
   - f. social rehabilitation; and/or
   - g. reintegration.

Fifth Part Employment

#### Article 54

The Regional Government conducts periodic supervision of every person/entity that employs Children in an effort to protect child workers.

#### Article 55

Every person/entity that employs a Child must observe the requirements:

   - a. minimum age of 15 (fifteen) years;
   - b. must obtain written consent from the parent/guardian of the Child worker;
   - c. have a written employment agreement between the employer and the parent/guardian of the Child worker and obtain validation from the authorized agency;
   - d. not employ the Child at night; and
   - e. maximum working hours of (three) hours per day.

#### Article 56

In the event that a Child is employed together with adult workers, then the workplace of the Child must be separated from the workplace of adult workers.

#### Article 57

1. Every person/entity is prohibited from employing and involving Children in the worst forms of work.
2. The worst forms of work referred to in paragraph (1) include:
   - a. all work in the form of slavery or the like;
   - b. all work that exploits, provides, or offers Children for prostitution, pornography production, pornographic performances, or gambling;
   - c. all work that exploits, provides, or involves Children for the production and trade of alcoholic beverages, narcotics, psychotropic substances, and other addictive substances; and/or
   - d. all work that endangers the health, safety, or morals of Children.

(231 Sixth Part Facilitation of the Juvenile Justice System

#### Article 58

1. Facilitation of the Child justice system includes criminal and civil justice. (2) Facilitation of the criminal justice system as referred to in paragraph (1) includes
   - a. provision of companions or Child volunteers at the time of examination by investigators,
   - b. provision of legal and psychological assistance services,
   - c. involving members/investigators of the local police in efforts to prevent criminal acts committed by Children as well as Children as victims,
   - d. realizing restorative justice processes for Children in conflict with the law, and
   - e. rehabilitation and social reintegration services.
3. Fulfillment of Children's rights in the civil justice system as referred to in paragraph (1) includes the provision and delivery of consultation and legal assistance services during the judicial process.
4. In facilitating the Child justice system as referred to in paragraph (1) and paragraph (2), the authorized PD is the PD responsible for women and Child empowerment and Protection and has the obligation to accompany child victims and child perpetrators, in police examinations, at the prosecutor's office, and in court.

Seventh Part Children's Forum

#### Article 59

1. In organizing the fulfillment of the Child's right to express opinions and participate, the City Government facilitates the establishment of a Children's Forum.
2. The Children's Forum as referred to in paragraph (1) constitutes a representation of Children in the City, including representation of Children's geographical domicile, components of Children's socio-cultural groups, and Children's educational background.
3. In every formulation of policies related to Children, the City Government is obligated to consider and accommodate the opinions of Children conveyed through the Children's participation Forum.
4. Further provisions regarding the establishment of the Children's Forum as referred to in paragraph (1) through paragraph (3) shall be stipulated by a Mayor's Regulation.

Eighth Part Child-Friendly City

#### Article 60

1. In order to fulfill the rights of Children in an integrated, systematic, and sustainable manner, the City Government establishes a policy for the Development of KLA.
2. The KLA Development Policy contains:
   - a. the KLA concept concerning the objectives, strategies, and roles of the related parties;
   - b. the rights of Children; and
   - c. the KLA development approach.

#### Article 61

The policy for developing KLA is directed towards the fulfillment of Children's rights which are divided into five groups, comprising:

   - a. civil rights and freedoms;
   - b. family environment and alternative care;
   - c. basic health and welfare;
   - d. education, utilization of leisure time, and cultural activities; and
   - e. special protection.

Further provisions regarding the policy for developing KLA as referred to in paragraph (1) shall be regulated by Mayoral Regulation.

#### Article 62

In order to ensure the effectiveness of the implementation of the Child-Friendly City (KLA) policy in the Region, a (1) KLA Task Force is established.

The establishment and membership composition of the KLA Task Force is stipulated (2) by the Mayor's Decision.

The KLA Task Force is domiciled at the DPPKBPPPA Office.

3. The KLA Task Force as referred to in paragraph (1), has the principal tasks (4):
   - a. coordinating the implementation of the KLA policy and development,
   - b. conducting socialization, advocacy and communication of information and education on the KLA policy,
   - c. collecting baseline data for KLA development,
   - d. conducting needs analysis sourced from baseline data,
   - e. conducting dissemination of baseline data,
   - f. determining the focus and priority of programs in realizing KLA, which are adjusted to regional potential,
   - g. preparing a (five) year Child-Friendly City regional action plan and work mechanism, and
   - h. conducting monitoring, evaluation and reporting at least (once) a year.

#### Article 63

In carrying out its duties, the KLA Task Force may coordinate and cooperate with other institutions that provide Child Protection services.

# CHAPTER VI

## CHILD PROTECTION

#### Article 64

The City Government, in the framework of providing protection to Children: a. implements and supports national policies in the administration of Child Protection in the City,

##### (2SI

   - b. guarantee the Child to use their right in expressing opinions in accordance with the Child's age and level of intelligence;
   - c. conduct prevention of Violence against Children by optimizing the role of customary institutions,
   - d. conduct socialization, seminars and counseling regarding Protection and rights of Children,
   - e. develop child-friendly school models,
   - f. provide public facilities that are friendly and safe for Children, and
   - g. encourage public awareness of the importance of Protection of Children.

#### Article 65

Service efforts carried out by the City Government for child victims of acts of Violence as referred to in Article 42 through Article 64 shall apply mutatis mutandis to service efforts for Child victims of acts of Violence.

# CHAPTER VII

## PARTICIPATION OF THE COMMUNITY, AND PARENTS AND/OR FAMILY

## Part One

Community

#### Article 66

1. The role of community participation in efforts to Protect women and Children:
   - a. participate in prevention and provide support for the development of programs to prevent violations of the rights of women and Children:
   - b. provide information and/or report violations of the rights of women and Children to the competent authorities; and
   - c. supervise the law enforcement process regarding cases of violations of the rights of women and Children.
2. The community, parents and families are responsible for protecting and preventing violations of the rights of women and Children.
3. Community participation as referred to in paragraph (1) and paragraph (2) is based on laws and regulations and the values of local wisdom of the local community.

#### Article 67

1. In the framework of preventing violations of women's rights, the community:
   - a. monitors various conditions occurring in their environment that may give rise to violations of women's rights;
   - b. increases awareness of violations of women's rights;
   - c. strengthens the role of community organizations, religious organizations and the family environment; and
   - d. conducts supervision of the implementation of Women's Empowerment and Child Protection organized by the City Government (261) through mechanisms for conveying aspirations to the Mayor or the Regional House of Representatives.
2. In the framework of preventing violations of children's rights, the community:
   - a. monitors various conditions occurring in their environment that give rise to violations of children's rights;
   - b. increases awareness of violations of children's rights; and
   - c. strengthens the role of community organizations, religious organizations and the family environment, and prevents the occurrence of marriage at an early age.

Second Part Parents and/or Family

#### Article 68

The family, in efforts to protect the rights of women, has the role of: protecting women and children from conflict and violence in the household that degrades human dignity, providing equal opportunities to women and children

   - a. to develop themselves in the fields of education, social, economic, political and cultural matters; and
   - b. preventing violations of the rights of women and children in the household.

#### Article 69

Parents in the effort of Protection of the rights of Children have the role of: (1)

   - a. caring for, maintaining, educating, and protecting Children,
   - b. developing Children according to their abilities, talents, and interests,
   - c. providing formal and non-formal education,
   - d. providing character education and instilling moral values in Children,
   - e. guiding Children to behave in accordance with religion, ethics and morals as well as customs, and
   - f. preventing the occurrence of marriage at an early age.

In the event that parents do not exist, or their whereabouts are unknown, or (2) due to a certain reason, are unable to carry out their obligations and responsibilities, then the obligations and responsibilities as referred to in paragraph (1) may be transferred to the family.

# CHAPTER VIII

## GUIDANCE AND SUPERVISION

#### Article 70

1. The City Government is obligated to conduct guidance and supervision of the implementation of Women's Empowerment and the Protection of Child Victims of Violence.
2. Guidance as referred to in paragraph (1), includes:
   - a. guidelines and fulfillment standards,
   - b. technical guidance and training,
   - c. provision of facilities,
   - d. monitoring, and
   - e. evaluation.
3. Guidance as referred to in paragraph (2) is in order to realize the objectives of empowerment and the Protection of women and Child victims of Violence in accordance with minimum service standards.

#### Article 71

Supervision of the implementation of women's empowerment and Child Protection as regulated in this Regional Regulation is carried out by a Supervisory institution specifically established for that purpose.

In carrying out supervision, the Institution referred to is authorized to recommend cases to law enforcement officials, government agencies and other community institutions as regulated in the Law.

Recommendations submitted must be followed up by law enforcement officials, government agencies, community institutions and other related individuals.

# CHAPTER IX

## FINANCING

#### Article 72

Financing for the implementation of programs and activities for Women's Empowerment and Child Protection is sourced from the regional revenue and expenditure budget, and

   - a. other legitimate sources in accordance with the provisions of laws and regulations.

##### SANCTIONS

#### Article 73

Every person who intentionally fails to provide protection for women and Children resulting in the occurrence of violence, fails to report and fails to provide protection to victims, shall be subject to sanctions as stipulated in the applicable laws and regulations.

If officials appointed to implement protection fail to carry out their obligations and responsibilities, they shall be subject to sanctions in accordance with the applicable laws and regulations.

UPT managers who carry out service duties but violate service principles, may be subject to sanctions in accordance with the applicable mechanisms.

Private parties that carry out Public services and fail to carry out their obligations and responsibilities in accordance with these regulations shall be subject to sanctions in accordance with the applicable laws and regulations.
