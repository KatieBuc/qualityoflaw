# WOMEN'S EMPOWERMENT AND CHILD PROTECTION

# BY THE GRACE OF ALMIGHTY GOD

# THE REGENT OF TASIKMALAYA,

Considering : a. that every citizen has the same and equal rights to live a dignified life in accordance with the principles of humanity, equality and justice;

   - b. that women and children are groups of society who are vulnerable to acts of injustice and inequality as well as acts of violence that can harm their rights and dignity as human beings;
   - c. that the rampant cases of violence against women and children in Tasikmalaya Regency require handling and concrete action from the Regional Government to eliminate all forms of violence against women and children;
   - d. that based on the considerations as referred to in letter a, letter b, and letter c, it is necessary to establish a Regional Regulation on Women's Empowerment and Child Protection;

# CHAPTER I

## GENERAL PROVISIONS

#### Article 1

In this Regional Regulation, what is meant by:

1. Regent is the Regent of Tasikmalaya.
2. Regency Area is the Regency Area of Tasikmalaya.
3. Regency Regional Government is the Regent as an element of regional government administration who leads the implementation of government affairs that are the authority of the autonomous region.
4. Regional House of Representatives hereinafter abbreviated as DPRD is a regional people's representative institution positioned as an element of regional government administration.
5. Regency Regional Apparatus is an auxiliary element of the Regent and Regency DPRD in administering government affairs that are the authority of the Regency area.
6. Women's Empowerment is every effort to improve physical, mental, spiritual, social capacity, knowledge, and skills so that women are ready to be utilized according to their respective capabilities.
7. Protection is a series of fulfillment of the rights of women and children victims of violence and discrimination starting from prevention efforts, services and social rehabilitation.
8. Child is a person who has not yet reached the age of 18 (eighteen) years, including a child still in the womb.
9. Women's Protection is all efforts aimed at protecting women and providing a sense of security in the fulfillment of their rights by providing consistent and systematic attention aimed at achieving gender equality.
10. Child Protection is all activities to guarantee and protect children and their rights so they can live, grow, develop and participate optimally in accordance with human dignity and receive protection from violence and discrimination.
11. Violence is any act that results in misery or suffering physically, psychologically, sexually, and/or neglect, including threats to commit acts, coercion, or unlawful deprivation of liberty.
12. Violence Against Women is any action based on gender differences that results in or may result in misery or suffering of women physically, sexually or psychologically, including threats of certain actions, coercion or arbitrary deprivation of liberty, whether occurring in the public sphere or in private life.
13. Violence Against Children is any act against a child that results in misery or suffering physically, mentally, sexually, psychologically, including neglect and maltreatment that threatens bodily integrity and degrades the dignity of the child.
14. Special Protection is a form of protection received by Children in certain situations and conditions to obtain a guarantee of security against threats that endanger themselves and their lives in their growth and development.
15. Domestic Violence hereinafter referred to as KDRT is any act against a person, especially women, that results in misery or suffering physically, sexually, psychologically, and/or household neglect including threats to commit acts of coercion, or unlawful deprivation of liberty within the scope of the household.
16. Parents are biological father and/or mother, or stepfather and/or stepmother, or adoptive father and/or adoptive mother.
17. Guardian is a person or body who in fact exercises custody as a Parent over a Child.
18. Family is the smallest unit in society consisting of husband and wife, or husband-wife and their children, or father and his children, or mother and her children, or blood relatives in direct line upward or downward up to the third degree.
19. Community is individuals, families, groups, and/or community organizations.
20. Non-Governmental Organization, hereinafter referred to as LSM is an organization/institution formed by citizens of the Republic of Indonesia voluntarily of their own will and interested in and engaged in the field of social welfare efforts as determined by the organization/institution as a manifestation of community participation in efforts to improve community welfare that emphasizes voluntary service.
21. Victim is a woman and child who experiences misery and or suffering either directly or indirectly as a result of violence occurring in the Tasikmalaya Regency area.
22. Prevention is an effort to develop the capacity and mechanisms of the Regional Government and community in creating conditions that can prevent the occurrence of violence, maltreatment, KDRT, exploitation and/or neglect.
23. Handling is action that includes identification, rescue, rehabilitation and reintegration of women and children who are victims of violence, ill-treatment, KDRT, exploitation, or neglect.
24. Repatriation is an effort to return women and child victims of violence from abroad to the debarkation point/entry point, or from the receiving area to the area of origin.
25. Service is action taken as soon as possible to victims when seeing, hearing and knowing of the impending, ongoing or having occurred violence against victims.
26. Assistance is an effort made by a person or representative from an institution that has expertise in assisting victims to conduct counseling, therapy and advocacy for strengthening and recovery of violence victims.
27. Rehabilitation is recovery from disturbances to physical, psychological, and social conditions in order to carry out their role again properly both in the family and in society.
28. Social Rehabilitation is a service aimed at restoring and developing the ability of a person experiencing social dysfunction to be able to carry out social functions properly.
29. Social Reintegration is an effort to reunite victims with family, substitute family, or community that can provide protection and fulfillment of needs for victims.
30. Regional Technical Implementation Unit for Women and Child Protection hereinafter abbreviated as UPTD PPPA is a technical implementer to carry out operational technical activities in its working area in providing services for women and children experiencing problems of violence, discrimination, special protection and other problems;
31. Legal Aid is legal services provided by legal assistants and advocates to conduct the process of assisting witnesses and/or victims of violence against women and children that is gender sensitive.
32. Integrated Service Center for Women's Empowerment and Child Protection, hereinafter abbreviated as P2TP2A, is an integrated service center in efforts to empower women in various fields of development, as well as protection of women and children from various types of discrimination and acts of violence, including trafficking in persons, established by the Regional Government or community-based, which includes: referral centers, business consultation centers, reproductive health consultation centers, legal consultation centers, Integrated Crisis Centers (PKT), Integrated Service Centers (PPT), trauma recovery centers (trauma centers), women's crisis handling centers (women crisis centers), training centers, Science and Technology Information Centers (PIPTEK), safe houses (shelters), transit houses, or other forms.
33. Women and Child Protection Task Force is a functional Work Unit that organizes protection services and handling of cases of violence against children and women in an integrated manner at the Sub-district level.
34. Women and Child Protection Task Unit is a functional Work Unit that organizes protection services and handling of cases of violence against children and women in an integrated manner at the Village level.
35. Child-Friendly Regency is a Regency that has a child rights-based development system through the integration of commitments and resources of government, community and business world that are planned comprehensively and sustainably in policies, programs and activities to ensure the fulfillment of children's rights.
36. Child-Friendly Regency Task Force is a coordinative institution at the regency level that coordinates policy efforts, programs and activities to realize a child-friendly regency.
37. Regional Action Plan for Child-Friendly Regency Development is a document containing policies, programs and activities to realize a child-friendly regency.

# CHAPTER II

## WOMEN'S EMPOWERMENT

#### Article 2

1. The Regional Government has the obligation and responsibility for the implementation of women's empowerment.
2. Women's empowerment efforts as referred to in paragraph (1) include:
   - a. determination of policies, budgets, programs, and activities;
   - b. determination of implementation guidelines;
   - c. provision of services; and
   - d. coordination of policies, programs and activities.

#### Article 3

The empowerment of women is directed towards obtaining opportunities and rights as human beings so that they are able to play a role and participate in the fields of:

   - a. economy;
   - b. social culture;
   - c. politics; and
   - d. law.

#### Article 4

The implementation of women's empowerment in the economic field as referred to in Article 3 letter a shall be carried out through:

   - a. provision of skills and job training;
   - b. facilitation of the formation, strengthening and development of productive economic business groups;
   - c. facilitation and capital assistance;
   - d. facilitation of marketing network development; and
   - e. Economic Empowerment of Women Heads of Households (PEKKA).

#### Article 5

Empowerment of women in the social and cultural field as referred to in Article 3 letter b shall be implemented through:

   - a. enhancement of knowledge, attitudes and skills to encourage the fulfillment of the right to education in stages according to potential to improve the social status of women;
   - b. enhancement of awareness, willingness and ability to overcome health problems through promotional, preventive, curative, and rehabilitative efforts that are affordable and of quality, primarily in the field of reproductive health;
   - c. enhancement of awareness and knowledge about independent, healthy and prosperous family planning including access to consultation services and marriage registration; and
   - d. facilitation and efforts to preserve customs and traditions and the development of science, technology, arts and culture for the advancement of women.

#### Article 6

The implementation of women's empowerment in the political field as referred to in Article 3 letter c includes:

   - a. political education for women;
   - b. involvement of women in decision-making at various levels of government;
   - c. provision of opportunities for women to hold public office;
   - d. participation in general elections; and
   - e. self-development through organizations to associate, assemble and express opinions.

#### Article 7

The implementation of women's empowerment in the field of law as referred to in Article 3 letter d includes:

   - a. enhancement of knowledge and awareness in the field of law through communication, information and education services; and
   - b. facilitation of access and consultation services as well as free legal aid.

# CHAPTER III

## PROTECTION OF WOMEN

#### Article 8

1. Women have the right to obtain protection from acts of domestic violence, protection of women's rights in employment, protection of women in emergency situations and special conditions as well as protection of women's rights from the criminal act of human trafficking.
2. Protection of women's rights from acts of domestic violence is in the form of prevention of physical and psychological violence, sexual violence and neglect, as well as the handling, services and empowerment of victims of domestic violence.
3. Protection of women's rights in employment, whether within or outside the country, includes protection in the prevention and handling of cases of neglect of women's rights in the context of employment.
4. Protection of women's rights in emergency conditions and special conditions includes protection of women in emergency situations, in conflict areas, and in disaster areas; protection of the rights of women with disabilities; and protection of the rights of elderly women who are persons with social welfare problems.
5. Protection of women's rights from human trafficking includes prevention and handling, services, and empowerment of victims of the criminal act of human trafficking.

#### Article 9

1. The Regional Government is obligated to provide protection to women so that they can realize and enjoy a reasonable standard of living.
2. Provisions regarding Protection as referred to in paragraph (1) shall be regulated by a Regent Regulation.

# CHAPTER IV

## CHILD PROTECTION

## Part One

General Child Protection

#### Article 10

Child Protection covers the fields of:

   - a. Child Protection in the field of Religion and Belief;
   - b. Child Protection in the field of Civil and Liberties;
   - c. Child Protection in the field of Health;
   - d. Child Protection in the field of Education;
   - e. Child Protection in the social field; and
   - f. Child Protection in the field of Special Protection.

Second Part Protection of Children in the Field of Religion and Belief

#### Article 11

1. Every child receives protection to worship according to their respective religion and belief.
2. Before a child can determine their choice, the religion embraced by the child follows the religion of their parents.
3. In the case of child adoption, prospective adoptive parents must be of the same religion as the religion adhered to by the prospective adopted child.
4. If the origin of the child as referred to in paragraph (3) is not known, then the religion of the child shall be adjusted to the religion of the majority of the local population.

Third Part Protection of Children in the Civil Sphere and Freedoms

#### Article 12

Protection of children in the field of civil affairs as referred to in Article 10 letter b includes:

   - a. a guarantee that every child has the right to obtain a name and personal identity from birth in the form of a birth certificate;
   - b. the issuance/making of birth certificates is the responsibility of the Government;
   - c. the issuance/making of birth certificates is not subject to any fee and is borne by the Regional Government; and
   - d. provisions regarding the issuance/making of birth certificates are further regulated in a Regent Regulation.

#### Article 13

1. Child protection in the civil sector and freedom assists children so that children can:
   - a. participate in all fields;
   - b. freely express opinions and think in accordance with conscience and religion in accordance with the norms applicable in the region;
   - c. freely receive oral or written information in accordance with the child's age stage and development;
   - d. freely associate and assemble in accordance with noble norms and ethics;
   - e. freely rest, play, recreate, and create cultural arts without neglecting obligations as a child; and
   - f. obtain play facilities that meet health and safety requirements.
2. Efforts as referred to in paragraph (1), are developed and adjusted to the age, level of ability of the child, and their environment so as not to hinder and disturb the child's development.

Fourth Part Protection of Children in the Field of Health

#### Article 14

Protection of children in the field of Health as referred to in Article 10 letter c, includes:

   - a. providing facilities and organizing comprehensive health efforts for children, so that every child obtains an optimal degree of health from the time they are in the womb, carried out by the Regional Government;
   - b. organizing comprehensive and free health efforts for all children including those from poor families;
   - c. providing health insurance for child victims of violence, exploitation, neglect and maltreatment;
   - d. being responsible for maintaining the health and caring for children from the time they are in the womb, carried out by the family and parents; and
   - e. endeavoring so that children who are born are protected from diseases that threaten the continuity of life and or cause disability, carried out by the Regional Government, community, family and parents.

Fifth Part Protection of Children in the Field of Education

#### Article 15

The implementation of child protection in the field of education as referred to in Article 10 letter d, includes:

   - a. providing the widest possible opportunity for children to obtain education carried out by the Regional Government, family and parents;
   - b. every education provider is prohibited from expelling children from Educational Institutions without guarantees for the continuity of the child's education;
   - c. implementation of a 12 (twelve) year compulsory education program, regulated in a separate Regional Regulation;
   - d. providing equal opportunity and accessibility to obtain regular education and special education for children with disabilities; and
   - e. protecting their rights to obtain education for child victims of violence, exploitation, neglect and maltreatment.

Sixth Part Protection of Children in the Social Sector

#### Article 16

The implementation of child protection in the social sector as referred to in Article 10 letter e shall be carried out in the form of:

   - a. Prevention services, including:
1. Formulating policies on child protection from all forms of violence, exploitation, neglect and maltreatment in all aspects of children's lives;
2. Formulating policies on social welfare guarantees for children who experience violence, exploitation, neglect and maltreatment; and
3. Organizing training to increase capacity for family welfare consultation institutions.
   - b. Risk Reduction Services, including:
1. Conducting early identification, complaint services and assessment of children and families who are victims of violence, exploitation, neglect and maltreatment;
2. Providing services for children and families who are victims of violence, exploitation, neglect and maltreatment including:
   - a. guidance and counseling on parenting patterns and behavior change;
   - b. mediation to related parties;
   - c. psychosocial services;
   - d. economic assistance and life skills;
   - e. temporary shelter;
   - f. preparation of social reintegration for child victims of violence, exploitation, neglect and maltreatment; and
   - g. protecting the identity of victims to prevent negative stigma against children.
   - c. Case Handling Services, including:
1. Conducting immediate rescue efforts for victims who are in dangerous conditions for their safety;
2. Providing referrals according to the needs of victims;
3. Providing counseling and support from families;
4. Providing rehabilitation and social reintegration services;
5. Conducting monitoring of the condition of children in alternative care in the long term; and
6. Creating a database on alternative care facilities for children.

Seventh Part Protection of Children in the Field of Special Protection

#### Article 17

Protection of Children in the field of special Protection as referred to in Article 10 letter f, includes:

   - a. children in emergency situations;
   - b. children in conflict with the law (ABH);
   - c. children exploited economically and or sexually;
   - d. children who are victims of narcotics, alcohol, psychotropic, and other addictive substances (NAPZA) abuse;
   - e. children victims of kidnapping, sale and trafficking;
   - f. children victims of violence both physical and/or mental, and children with disabilities; and/or
   - g. children victims of maltreatment and neglect.

### Paragraph 1

Child Protection in Emergency Situations

#### Article 18

1. Children in emergency situations as referred to in Article 17 letter a, consist of:
   - a. children who become refugees;
   - b. children victims of riots;
   - c. children victims of natural disasters; and
   - d. children in armed conflict situations.
2. The implementation of child protection in emergency situations as referred to in paragraph (1) is carried out in the form of:
   - a. Prevention Services, including:
1. Making policies for child protection from all forms of violence, exploitation, neglect and maltreatment in emergency situations;
2. Making policies for fulfillment of basic needs consisting of food, clothing, shelter, education, health, learning and recreation, security guarantees, and equal treatment for children who become refugees, victims of violence, victims of natural disasters and children in armed conflict situations; and
3. Making policies and disaster management mechanisms that take into account the interests of children.
   - b. Risk Reduction Services, including:
1. Conducting early identification, complaint services and assessment of children and families who are victims of violence, exploitation, neglect and maltreatment.
2. Providing services for children and families who are victims of violence, exploitation, neglect and maltreatment, which include:
   - a. legal consultation;
   - b. mediation to related parties;
   - c. psychosocial services;
   - d. economic assistance and life skills;
   - e. safe houses; and
   - f. temporary shelters.
   - c. Case Handling Services, including:
1. Conducting immediate rescue efforts for victims who are in dangerous conditions for their safety;
2. Providing referrals according to the needs of victims;
3. Providing counseling and support from families;
4. Treating children in emergency situations humanely in accordance with the dignity and rights of children;
5. Providing a sense of security and protection for children who become refugees, victims of violence, victims of natural disasters and children in armed conflict situations; and
6. Fulfilling special needs for children in emergency situations who experience psychosocial disorders.

### Paragraph 2

Protection for ABH

#### Article 19

1. Special protection for ABH as referred to in Article 17 letter b includes children in conflict with the law and child victims of criminal acts, constituting the obligation and responsibility of the Regional Government and society.
2. The implementation of special protection for ABH as referred to in paragraph (1) is carried out in the form of:
   - a. Prevention services, including:
1. Creating policies regarding education guarantees for ABH;
2. Providing socialization and raising awareness of parents and/or society, the press, educators, government, law enforcement officials regarding the protection of ABH identity to avoid negative stigma toward children;
3. Providing socialization and raising awareness of parents/guardians and/or society, the press, educators, Regional Government and law enforcement officials regarding alternative forms of enforcing discipline without physical and psychological punishment;
4. Providing socialization to society regarding understanding of restorative justice or justice that restores;
5. Organizing training to increase the capacity of society in conducting advocacy to law enforcement institutions to make distinctions in handling issues related to ABH;
6. Organizing training to increase the capacity of society in conducting advocacy for the juvenile court process to provide alternative punishments in the form of actions; and
7. Creating a complaint mechanism for handling issues related to ABH.
   - b. Risk Reduction Services, including:
1. Conducting early identification, complaint services and assessment of children and families at risk;
2. Providing services for children and families such as:
   - a. guidance and counseling regarding parenting patterns and behavior change;
   - b. legal consultation;
   - c. mediation to related parties;
   - d. psychosocial services; and
   - e. protecting the identity of victims.
   - c. Case Handling Services, including:
1. Providing counseling and the need for family support;
2. Treatment of children in a humane manner in accordance with the dignity and rights of children;
3. Providing guarantees of legal assistance/special accompaniment for children in conflict with the law;
4. Providing a sense of security and protection from reporting regarding ABH identity to avoid negative stigma toward children;
5. Providing special facilities and infrastructure for ABH;
6. Providing accessibility to obtain information regarding case developments;
7. Providing rehabilitation and social reintegration services; and
8. Conducting continuous monitoring and recording of the development of children in conflict with the law.

### Paragraph 3

Protection of Children Exploited Economically and/or Sexually

#### Article 20

1. Special protection for children who are exploited economically and/or sexually as referred to in Article 17 letter c, constitutes the obligation and responsibility of the Regional Government and society.
2. The implementation of special protection for children who are exploited economically and or sexually as referred to in paragraph (1) is carried out in the form of:
   - a. Prevention Services, including:
1. Creating policies for the protection of children from all forms of violence, exploitation, and maltreatment in all situations of children's lives;
2. Creating policies for the fulfillment of special needs for children with disabilities and children experiencing psychosocial disorders who are victims of economic and/or sexual exploitation;
3. Providing protection guarantees for child victims of economic and/or sexual exploitation;
4. Providing protection guarantees from all forms of violence, exploitation, neglect and maltreatment for children who are exploited economically and/or sexually;
5. Providing socialization and raising awareness of parents and/or society, the press, educators, by the government and law enforcement officials regarding the adverse impacts of violence and economic and/or sexual exploitation;
6. Providing socialization and raising awareness of parents and/or society, the press, educators, by the government and law enforcement officials regarding the protection of the identity of child victims of economic and/or sexual exploitation; and
7. Providing socialization regarding the provisions of laws and regulations related to the protection of children who are exploited economically and/or sexually.
   - b. Risk Reduction Services, which include:
1. Conducting early identification, complaint services and assessment of children and families at risk of experiencing economic and/or sexual exploitation;
2. Providing services for children and families who are victims of violence, and economic and/or sexual exploitation including:
   - a. legal consultation;
   - b. legal assistance to victims of sexual violence;
   - c. mediation to related parties;
   - d. psychosocial services;
   - e. economic assistance and life skills;
   - f. temporary shelter; and
   - g. protecting the identity of victims.
   - c. Case Handling Services, including:
1. Conducting immediate rescue efforts for victims who are in dangerous conditions for their safety;
2. Providing referrals according to the needs of victims;
3. Providing counseling and support from family;
4. Treating child victims of economic and/or sexual exploitation humanely in accordance with the dignity and rights of children;
5. Providing a sense of security and protection for child victims of economic and/or sexual exploitation from media coverage; and
6. Providing rehabilitation and social reintegration services.

### Paragraph 4

Protection of Children who Become Victims of Narcotics, Alcohol, Psychotropic Substances, and Other Addictive Substances (NAPZA) Abuse

#### Article 21

1. Supervision, prevention, care, and rehabilitation of children who are victims of abuse of narcotics, alcohol, psychotropic substances, and other addictive substances (NAPZA) as referred to in Article 17 letter d, and children involved in their production and distribution, shall be the obligation and responsibility of the Regional Government and the community.
2. The implementation of protection for children who are victims of narcotics, alcohol, psychotropic substances, and other addictive substances (NAPZA) abuse as referred to in paragraph (1) shall be carried out in the form of:
   - a. Prevention Services, including:
1. Creating policies for the protection of children who are victims of narcotics, alcohol, psychotropic substances and other addictive substances (NAPZA) abuse;
2. Creating special protection policies for children who are victims of narcotics, alcohol, psychotropic substances, and other addictive substances (NAPZA) abuse; and
3. Providing socialization and raising awareness of parents and/or the community and children about the adverse effects of narcotics, alcohol, psychotropic substances and other addictive substances (NAPZA) abuse.
   - b. Risk Reduction Services, which include:
1. Conducting early identification, complaint services and assessment of children and families at risk;
2. Providing services for children and families who are victims of narcotics, alcohol, psychotropic substances, and other addictive substances (NAPZA) abuse including:
   - a. guidance and counseling on parenting patterns and behavior change;
   - b. legal consultation; and
   - c. psychosocial services.
   - c. Case Handling Services, including:
1. Conducting immediate rescue efforts for victims whose safety is in danger;
2. Providing referrals according to the needs of victims of narcotics, alcohol, psychotropic substances and other addictive substances (NAPZA) abuse;
3. Conducting counseling and providing support from the family;
4. Treating children who are victims of narcotics, alcohol, psychotropic substances, and other addictive substances (NAPZA) abuse in a humane manner in accordance with the dignity and rights of children; and
5. Providing rehabilitation and social reintegration services.

### Paragraph 5

Protection of Child Victims of Abduction, Sale and Trafficking

#### Article 22

1. Special Protection for child victims of abduction, sale and trafficking as referred to in Article 17 letter e is carried out through supervision, protection, prevention, care and rehabilitation efforts by the Regional Government and the community.
2. The implementation of special protection for children who are victims of abduction, sale and trafficking as referred to in paragraph (1) is carried out in the form of:
   - a. Prevention Services, including:
1. Providing protection guarantees for child victims of abduction, sale and trafficking; and
2. Providing complaint mechanisms and case handling.
   - b. Risk Reduction Services, including:
1. Conducting early identification, complaint services and assessment of children and families at risk; and
2. Providing services for children who are victims of abduction, sale and trafficking which include:
   - a. guidance and counseling on parenting patterns and behavior change;
   - b. legal consultation;
   - c. mediation to related parties;
   - d. psychosocial services;
   - e. economic assistance and life skills;
   - f. temporary shelter; and
   - g. protecting the identity of victims.
   - c. Case Handling Services, including:
1. Conducting immediate rescue efforts for victims who are in dangerous conditions for their safety;
2. Providing referrals according to the needs of victims;
3. Conducting counseling and having support from the family;
4. Treating child victims of abduction, sale and trafficking in a humane manner in accordance with the dignity and rights of children;
5. Providing accessibility to obtain information regarding case developments;
6. Providing rehabilitation and social reintegration services; and
7. Creating a database of child victims of abduction, sale and trafficking.

### Paragraph 6

Protection of Child Victims of Violence both Physical and/or Mental and Children with Disabilities

#### Article 23

1. Special Protection for child victims of violence, whether physical and/or mental, and children with disabilities as referred to in Article 17 letter f is carried out through the following efforts:
   - a. dissemination and socialization of the provisions of laws and regulations that protect children who become victims of violence;
   - b. obtaining equal treatment with other children to achieve social integration as fully as possible and individual development; and
   - c. fulfillment of special needs.
2. The implementation of special protection for children who become victims of violence, whether physical and/or mental, and children with disabilities as referred to in paragraph (1) is carried out in the form of:
   - a. Prevention Services, which include:
1. making policies for the fulfillment of special needs for children with disabilities and children experiencing psychosocial disorders;
2. providing education guarantees for child victims of violence, whether physical and/or mental, and children with disabilities;
3. providing protection guarantees from all forms of violence, exploitation, neglect and mistreatment for children with disabilities;
4. providing socialization and raising awareness of parents and/or the community, the press, and educators, by the government and law enforcement officials, about the adverse impacts of violence;
5. providing socialization and raising awareness of parents and/or the community, the press, and educators, by the government, about the protection of the identity of children who become victims of violence, whether physical and/or mental, and children with disabilities from negative stigma; and
6. establishing complaint mechanisms for case handling.
   - b. Risk Reduction Services, including:
1. conducting early identification, complaint services and assessment of children and families at risk;
2. providing services for children and families who are victims of violence which include:
   - a. guidance and counseling on parenting patterns and behavior change;
   - b. legal consultation;
   - c. mediation to related parties;
   - d. psychosocial services;
   - e. economic assistance and life skills;
   - f. temporary shelter; and
   - g. protecting the identity of victims.
   - c. Case Handling Services, which include:
1. conducting immediate rescue efforts for victims who are in dangerous conditions for their safety;
2. providing referrals according to the needs of victims;
3. conducting counseling and support from the family;
4. treatment of children in a humane manner in accordance with the dignity and rights of children;
5. providing a sense of security and protection from news coverage to avoid negative stigma;
6. providing special facilities and infrastructure for children with disabilities;
7. providing rehabilitation and social reintegration services; and
8. creating a database of child victims of violence, whether physical and/or mental, and children with disabilities.

### Paragraph 7

Protection of Child Victims of Abuse and Neglect

#### Article 24

1. Protection of child victims of maltreatment and neglect as referred to in Article 17 letter g is carried out through supervision, prevention, care and rehabilitation efforts by the Regional Government and the community.
2. The implementation of protection for children who are victims of maltreatment and neglect as referred to in paragraph (1) is carried out in the form of:
   - a. Prevention Services, including:
1. providing protection guarantees for child victims of maltreatment and neglect;
2. providing protection guarantees from all forms of neglect and maltreatment for children;
3. providing socialization and raising awareness of parents and/or the community, press, educators, government, law enforcement officials about the adverse effects of maltreatment and neglect; and
4. establishing complaint mechanisms for case handling.
   - b. Risk Reduction Services, including:
1. conducting early identification, complaint services and assessment of children and families at risk;
2. providing services for children and families who are victims of neglect and maltreatment including:
   - a. guidance and counseling on parenting patterns and behavior change;
   - b. legal consultation;
   - c. mediation to related parties;
   - d. psychosocial services; and
   - e. temporary shelter.
   - c. Case Handling Services, including:
1. conducting immediate rescue efforts for victims in conditions dangerous to their safety;
2. providing referrals according to the needs of victims;
3. conducting counseling and support from families;
4. treatment of children in a humane manner in accordance with the dignity and rights of children; and
5. conducting continuous monitoring and recording of the development of child victims of maltreatment and neglect.
3. The procedures, mechanisms and service standards for special protection for child laborers, child workers and working children as referred to in paragraph (1) shall be further regulated by Regent Regulation.

# CHAPTER V

## FORMS OF VIOLENCE AGAINST WOMEN AND CHILDREN

#### Article 25

Forms of violence against women and children, include: a. physical violence;

   - b. psychological violence;
   - c. sexual violence;
   - d. neglect;
   - e. exploitation; and/or
   - f. other violence.

#### Article 26

1. Physical violence as referred to in Article 25 letter a constitutes an act that results in pain accompanied by injury, wounds or disability to the body, miscarriage, unconsciousness and/or causes death of women and/or children.
2. Psychological violence as referred to in Article 25 letter b constitutes an act that results in fear, loss of self-confidence, loss of ability to act, feelings of helplessness and/or psychological suffering of women and/or children.
3. Sexual violence as referred to in Article 25 letter c, includes:
   - a. acts in the form of sexual harassment, consisting of:
1. physical harassment;
2. verbal harassment;
3. gestural harassment;
4. written or pictorial harassment; and
5. psychological/emotional harassment.
   - b. forced sexual intercourse;
   - c. forced sexual intercourse in an unnatural or unwanted manner; and/or
   - d. forced sexual intercourse with others for commercial purposes and/or certain purposes.
4. Neglect as referred to in Article 25 letter d, includes:
   - a. acts that result in the failure to fulfill the needs of children properly, whether physical, mental, spiritual or social, committed by parents, guardians, or any other party responsible for their care;
   - b. acts of deliberately neglecting to maintain, care for, or look after children as appropriate, committed by parents, guardians, or any other party responsible for their care; and/or
   - c. acts of deliberately neglecting women within the scope of their household, whereas according to the law applicable to them or due to agreement or contract they are obligated to provide livelihood, care, or maintenance to such women.
5. Exploitation as referred to in Article 25 letter e, includes:
   - a. acts of economic or sexual exploitation with the intention of benefiting oneself or others;
   - b. acts with or without the consent of the victim which include but are not limited to prostitution, forced labor or services, slavery or similar practices, oppression, extortion, utilization of physical, sexual, reproductive organs, or unlawfully transferring or transplanting organs and/or body tissues or utilizing the labor or ability of a person by other parties to obtain benefits both material and immaterial; and
   - c. all forms of utilization of sexual organs or other body organs of the victim to obtain benefits, but not limited to prostitution or sexual abuse activities.
6. Other violence as referred to in Article 25 letter f constitutes threats of violence and coercion.
7. Threats of violence as referred to in paragraph (6) include any unlawful act in the form of speech, writing, images, symbols, or body movements, whether with or without using means that cause fear or restrict a person's fundamental freedom.
8. Coercion as referred to in paragraph (6) includes a situation where a person or victim is ordered to do something in such a way that the person does something contrary to their own will.

# CHAPTER VI

## RIGHTS OF VICTIMS OF VIOLENCE

#### Article 27

Women and children victims of violence shall receive the following rights: a. the right to have their dignity and worth as human beings respected; b. the right to health and psychological recovery from the suffering experienced by the victim;

   - c. the right to determine their own decisions;
   - d. the right to obtain information;
   - e. the right to confidentiality;
   - f. the right to compensation;
   - g. the right to social rehabilitation;
   - h. the right to complaint handling;
      - i. the right to assistance and legal aid;
   - j. the right to obtain visum et repertum free of charge; and k. the right of victims and their families to obtain facilitation in the judicial process.

#### Article 28

Child victims of violence, in addition to obtaining the rights as referred to in Article 27, are granted special rights, as follows:

   - a. the right to respect and full enjoyment for survival, growth and development;
   - b. the right to basic services;
   - c. the right to equal protection;
   - d. the right to be free from various stigma;
   - e. the right to obtain freedom; and
   - f. the right to be tried based on the principle of restorative justice.

# CHAPTER VII

## OBLIGATIONS AND RESPONSIBILITIES

## Part One

General

#### Article 29

The obligations and responsibilities in implementing protection for women and children are the shared responsibility of:

   - a. Regional Government;
   - b. village government;
   - c. Community; and
   - d. parents, Guardians and/or Family.

Second Part Regional Government

#### Article 30

1. Regional Government as referred to in Article 29 letter a, has the obligation and responsibility to:
   - a. respect, protect and fulfill the rights of victims and the special rights of child victims of violence;
   - b. formulate and implement programs and activities related to the protection of women and children;
   - c. implement protection of women and children from violence;
   - d. supervise services for victims of violence based on minimum service standards in accordance with the provisions of laws and regulations;
   - e. allocate budget for protection of women and children from violence according to regional financial capacity and budgeted in each fiscal year; and
   - f. realize Child-Friendly Regency.
2. In the framework of formulating and implementing effective women and children protection programs as referred to in paragraph (1) letter b, Regional Government shall organize:
   - a. disaggregated gender and child data system; and
   - b. gender-responsive and child-friendly planning and budgeting.
3. Regional Government may establish Regional Technical Implementation Unit for Women's Empowerment and Child Protection to reach services in the Region.

Third Part Village Government

#### Article 31

1. The Village Government as referred to in Article 29 letter b, has the obligation and responsibility to:
   - a. prepare and implement programs and activities related to the protection of women and children;
   - b. implement the protection of women and children from violence;
   - c. realize a Child-Friendly Village; and
   - d. allocate budget for the protection of women and children from violence in accordance with the village's financial capacity in each fiscal year.
2. In order to carry out the obligations and responsibilities as referred to in paragraph (1), the village government shall prepare programs and activities for the protection of women and children in the village action plan which is part of the village medium-term development plan.

Fourth Part Society

#### Article 32

1. The community as referred to in Article 29 letter c has the obligation and responsibility for the protection of women and children through community participation activities in the protection of women and children.
2. Forms of community participation as referred to in paragraph (1), include:
   - a. preventing the occurrence of acts of violence against women and children;
   - b. providing information and/or reporting acts of violence against women and children to law enforcement or authorized parties; and
   - c. participating in the handling of victims of acts of violence.

## Part Five

Parents, Guardians and/or Family

#### Article 33

1. Parents, guardians and/or family as referred to in Article 29 letter d legally have full responsibility to prevent all forms of violence and to report if violence occurs and to protect victims.
2. In the event that the parents, guardians and/or family as referred to in paragraph (1) are not present or their whereabouts are unknown or, for a certain reason, cannot carry out their obligations and responsibilities, then the obligations and responsibilities may be transferred to the Regional Government and other family members, to be carried out in accordance with the provisions of laws and regulations.

# CHAPTER VIII

## IMPLEMENTATION OF PROTECTION FOR WOMEN AND CHILDREN

## Part One

General

#### Article 34

Regional Governments carry out activities for the implementation of women's protection and child protection, which include:

   - a. prevention;
   - b. services; and
   - c. social rehabilitation.

## Part Two

Prevention

#### Article 35

1. Prevention as referred to in Article 34 letter a, shall be implemented by means of:
   - a. establishing a working network in efforts to prevent violence;
   - b. conducting coordination, integration, synchronization of violence prevention based on partnership patterns;
   - c. establishing a violence prevention system;
   - d. conducting socialization regarding laws and regulations related to the protection of women and children; and
   - e. providing knowledge about prevention and mechanisms for handling violence against women and children.
2. Prevention as referred to in paragraph (1) shall be implemented in an integrated manner by Regional Apparatus of the Regency and related agencies that have principal tasks and functions in the administration of affairs of:
   - a. women's empowerment and child protection;
   - b. education;
   - c. health;
   - d. social affairs;
   - e. manpower;
   - f. youth and sports; and
   - g. mental and spiritual.
3. In addition to being implemented by Regional Apparatus of the Regency and related agencies as referred to in paragraph (2), prevention shall also be implemented by:
   - a. family and/or closest relatives;
   - b. community, NGOs, community organizations, social organizations and religious organizations; and
   - c. business sector.

Third Part Services

#### Article 36

Services as referred to in Article 34 letter b, shall take into account: a. service principles; and

   - b. forms of services for victims.

#### Article 37

The service principles as referred to in Article 36 letter a, consist of:

   - a. fast;
   - b. safe and comfortable;
   - c. sense of empathy;
   - d. non-discrimination;
   - e. easily accessible;
   - f. not subject to fees; and
   - g. guaranteed confidentiality.

#### Article 38

Forms of services for victims as referred to in Article 36 letter b, consist of:

   - a. services for women and children victims of acts of violence, including:
1. complaint, consultation and counseling services;
2. assistance services;
3. health services;
4. legal aid services; and
5. repatriation and social reintegration services.
   - b. special services for women victims of acts of domestic violence or human trafficking, including:
1. complaint services;
2. pick-up services;
3. medical and psychological referral services;
4. shelter services;
5. assistance and legal aid services; and
6. repatriation and social reintegration services.

#### Article 39

Complaint, consultation and counseling services as referred to in Article 38 letter a number 1, include:

   - a. identification or initial recording of victims; and
   - b. consent to carry out an action (informed consent).

#### Article 40

Assistance services as referred to in Article 38 letter a number 2 include:

   - a. accompanying victims during the examination and health recovery process;
   - b. accompanying victims during the psychological recovery process;
   - c. accompanying victims during the medicolegal process;
   - d. accompanying victims during the examination process at the police, prosecutor's office and court;
   - e. monitoring the interests and rights of victims in the examination process at the police, prosecutor's office and court;
   - f. protecting the privacy and confidentiality of victims from all parties who have no interest, including reporting by mass media;
   - g. coordinating with other assistants; and
   - h. providing continuous treatment up to the rehabilitation stage.

#### Article 41

Health services as referred to in Article 38 letter a number 3 include:

   - a. first aid to victims by competent officers; b. treatment and recovery of physical injuries aimed at restoring the physical condition of victims carried out by medical and paramedical personnel; and c. referral to health services according to needs.

#### Article 42

1. Legal aid services as referred to in Article 38 letter a number 4 are to assist victims in undergoing the judicial process. (2) Legal aid services as referred to in paragraph (1) are carried out by:
   - a. providing legal consultation that includes information regarding the rights of victims and the judicial process;
   - b. accompanying victims at the investigation, prosecution, and examination levels in court hearings and assisting victims to comprehensively explain the violence they have experienced; and
   - c. coordinating with fellow law enforcers, volunteer companions, and social workers so that the judicial process proceeds as it should.

#### Article 43

1. Repatriation and social reintegration services as referred to in Article 38 letter a number 5 are conducted to return victims to their families and social environment.
2. Repatriation and social reintegration services as referred to in paragraph (1) are conducted by the Regional Government in coordination with:
   - a. Regency/City Government within one Provincial territory or outside the Province; and/or
   - b. related agencies and institutions both Government and non-Government.
3. Forms of services as referred to in Article 38, are implemented in accordance with minimum service standards established by the Government/Regional Government and implemented by Regency Regional Apparatus that have duties and functions in administering affairs of:
   - a. women's empowerment and child protection;
   - b. social;
   - c. manpower;
   - d. health;
   - e. education;
   - f. security and order; and
   - g. mental and spiritual.
4. In carrying out duties and functions as referred to in paragraph (1) the Regional Government cooperates with the Government, Provincial Government, other Regency/City Governments, and the community.

Fourth Part Social Rehabilitation

#### Article 44

1. Social rehabilitation as referred to in Article 34 letter c constitutes services provided by companions to restore the traumatic condition of victims.
2. Social rehabilitation as referred to in paragraph (1) is carried out by means of:
   - a. providing guidance and counseling to victims and parents of victims;
   - b. psychological recovery of victims;
   - c. accompanying victims and parents of victims in the family and community environment; and
   - d. providing safe houses for victims of violence who require continuous handling.
3. Special social rehabilitation for female victims of domestic violence and human trafficking is carried out by means of:
   - a. providing guidance and counseling;
   - b. psychological recovery of victims; and
   - c. accompanying victims in the family and community environment.
4. Social rehabilitation as referred to in paragraph (1) is implemented by the Regency Regional Apparatus having the principal tasks and functions in administering affairs of:
   - a. social;
   - b. women's empowerment and child protection;
   - c. health; and
   - d. mental and spiritual.
5. In addition to being implemented by the Regency Regional Apparatus as referred to in paragraph (4), social rehabilitation may also be implemented by the community or social service institutions.

Village Level Task Force for the Protection of Women and Children

#### Article 49

1. The Village Level Women and Children Protection Task Force as referred to in Article 45 letter d constitutes a functional work unit that organizes integrated protection services and handling of cases of violence against children and women at the Village level.

   - c. facilitating women and children who are victims of violence in resolving their problems;
   - d. conducting outreach to the community regarding the protection of women and children; and
   - e. providing assistance for victims or referring them to the Sub-district level women and children protection task force.
4. Further provisions regarding the establishment, duties and functions of the village level women and children protection task force as referred to in paragraph (1) shall be stipulated by a Village Head Decree.

Sixth Part Regional Indonesian Child Protection Commission

#### Article 50

1. The Regional Indonesian Child Protection Commission as referred to in Article 45 letter e constitutes a partner of the Government in the endeavor to fulfill children's rights and child protection and is positioned at the Regency level.
2. The Regional Indonesian Child Protection Commission is established by decree of the Regent.
3. The membership of the Regional Indonesian Child Protection Commission consists of 1 (one) chairperson, 1 (one) vice chairperson, and 7 (seven) members.
4. The membership of the Regional Indonesian Child Protection Commission comes from elements of the Regional Government, religious leaders, community leaders, social organizations, the business sector, and community groups concerned with child protection.

#### Article 51

1. The Regional Indonesian Child Protection Commission as referred to in Article 45 letter e has detailed duties including:
   - a. conducting supervision of the implementation of protection and fulfillment of children's rights;
   - b. providing input and proposals in the formulation of policies regarding the implementation of child protection;
   - c. collecting data and information regarding child protection;
   - d. receiving and conducting examination of public complaints regarding violations of children's rights;
   - e. conducting mediation on disputes regarding violations of children's rights;
   - f. conducting cooperation with institutions established by the community in the field of child protection; and
   - g. other duties in accordance with the provisions of laws and regulations.
2. In the event of changes to laws and regulations concerning the Regional Indonesian Child Protection Commission, the regulation regarding the Regional Indonesian Child Protection Commission shall be guided by the laws and regulations, the further provisions of which are regulated by a Regent Regulation.

Seventh Part Regional Children's Forum

#### Article 52

1. The Regional Children's Forum as referred to in Article 45 letter f is an organization or social institution used as a forum or mechanism for participation for children under the age of 18 (eighteen) years where its members are representatives from children's groups or children's activity groups that are managed by children and fostered by the government as a medium to hear and fulfill the aspirations, voices, opinions, desires and needs of children in the development process.
2. The Regional Children's Forum as referred to in paragraph (1) is formed in a tiered manner at all levels, namely:
   - a. Regency Level;
   - b. Sub-district Level; and
   - c. Village Level.

### Paragraph 1

District Level Children's Forum

#### Article 53

1. The District Level Children's Forum as referred to in Article 52 paragraph (2) letter a constitutes a forum for children's participation within the District area with membership consisting of representatives from the Sub-district Level Children's Forum of at least 1 (one) female child and 1 (one) male child.
2. The District Level Children's Forum shall be established by Regent's Decree with a management term of 2 (two) years.
3. In order for the District Level Children's Forum to function properly, a secretariat must be formed.
4. The formation of the secretariat as referred to in paragraph (3) may involve at most 40% (forty percent) adults who are unmarried.
5. The involvement of adults as referred to in paragraph (4) is intended to guide and foster new members of the District Level Children's Forum.
6. The District Regional Apparatus responsible for women's empowerment and child protection as the Supervisor of the District Level Children's Forum shall facilitate the establishment of the District Level Children's Forum from the selection stage, ratification of management and coaching in capacity building of members of the District Level Children's Forum.

### Paragraph 2

Sub-District Level Children's Forum

#### Article 54

1. The Sub-District Level Children's Forum as referred to in Article 52 paragraph (2) letter b constitutes a forum for children's participation within the sub-district area with membership consisting of representatives from the Village Level Children's Forum of at least 1 (one) female child and 1 (one) male child.
2. The Sub-District Level Children's Forum is established by Decree of the Sub-District Head with a management term of 2 (two) years.
3. The Sub-District Government as Supervisor of the Sub-District Level Children's Forum facilitates the formation of the Sub-District Level Children's Forum from the election stage, ratification of management and guidance in capacity building of members of the Sub-District Level Children's Forum.

### Paragraph 3

Village Level Children's Forum

#### Article 55

1. The Village Level Children's Forum as referred to in Article 52 paragraph (2) letter c constitutes a forum for children's participation in the Village area with membership consisting of representatives from Neighborhood Associations and Community Associations of at least 1 (one) female child and 1 (one) male child.
2. The Village Level Children's Forum is established by Decree of the Village Head with a term of office of 2 (two) years.
3. The Village Government as Supervisor of the Village Level Children's Forum facilitates the formation of the Village Level Children's Forum starting from the selection stage, validation of management and guidance in capacity building of members of the Village Level Children's Forum.
4. Community institutions existing in the Village have the obligation to accommodate and foster children's participation in development as well as in advocating for children's rights.

# CHAPTER X

## CHILD-FRIENDLY REGENCY

#### Article 56

1. The Regional Government is obligated to implement a Child-Friendly Regency implementation system in order to fulfill children's rights.
2. The development of a Child-Friendly Regency is carried out based on the following principles:
   - a. good governance, namely transparency, accountability, participation, information openness, and rule of law;
   - b. non-discrimination, namely not differentiating ethnicity, race, religion, gender, language, political views, national origin, economic status, physical or psychological condition of the child, or other factors;
   - c. the best interests of the child, namely making what is best for the child the primary consideration in every policy, program, and activity;
   - d. the right to life, survival, and development of the child, namely guaranteeing the right to life, survival and development of the child to the maximum extent possible; and
   - e. respect for the views of the child, namely recognizing and ensuring that every child who has the ability to express their opinion is given the opportunity to express their views freely on all matters affecting them.
3. The implementation instruments of the Child-Friendly Regency as referred to in paragraph (1) include:
   - a. development system and public services;
   - b. Child-Friendly Health Services;
   - c. Child-Friendly Family;
   - d. Child-Friendly School; and
   - e. Child-Friendly Environment;
   - f. Child-Friendly Islamic Boarding School.
4. The Regional Government in implementing the Child-Friendly Regency as referred to in paragraph (1) is obligated to consider the Child-Friendly Regency Indicators which include:
   - a. institutional; and
   - b. child rights clusters.
5. Institutional strengthening as referred to in paragraph (4) letter a includes:
   - a. the existence of laws and regulations and policies for the fulfillment of children's rights;
   - b. percentage of budget for the fulfillment of children's rights, including budget for institutional strengthening;
   - c. number of laws and regulations, policies, programs and activities that receive input from the Children's Forum and other children's groups;
   - d. availability of Human Resources (HR) trained in the Children's Rights Conference (KHA) and capable of applying children's rights into policies, programs, and activities;
   - e. availability of disaggregated child data by gender, age, and sub-district;
   - f. involvement of community institutions in the fulfillment of children's rights; and
   - g. involvement of the business sector in the fulfillment of children's rights.

#### Article 57

The cluster of children's rights as referred to in Article 56 paragraph (4) letter b includes:

   - a. Civil rights and freedoms, which include:
1. the right of the child to identity;
2. the right to protection of identity;
3. the right to express and voice opinions;
4. the right to think, have a conscience, practice religion, and have beliefs;
5. the right to organize and assemble peacefully;
6. the right to protection of private life;
7. the right to access appropriate information; and
8. the right to be free from torture and other cruel, inhuman, or degrading punishment or treatment.
   - b. The right to a family environment and care, which includes:
1. parental guidance and responsibility;
2. children separated from parents;
3. reunification;
4. illegal transfer of children;
5. welfare support;
6. children who are forced to be separated from the family environment;
7. child adoption;
8. periodic review of placement; and
9. violence and neglect.
   - c. The right of the child to basic health and welfare, which includes:
   - a. children with disabilities obtain access to health services and their welfare;
   - b. health and health services;
   - c. social security health services and facilities; and
   - d. standard of living.
   - d. The right of the child to education, use of leisure time, and cultural activities, which includes:
1. education;
2. objectives of education; and
3. holiday activities, cultural activities, and sports.
   - e. Protection of children under special protection, which includes:
1. children in emergency situations;
2. children in conflict with the law;
3. children in situations of exploitation; and
4. children who are part of minority and isolated groups.

#### Article 58

1. To make effective the development of Child-Friendly Regency, a Child-Friendly Regency Task Force shall be established as determined by Regent Decree.
2. The membership of the Child-Friendly Regency Task Force as referred to in paragraph (1) may come from the Regional Government, child representatives, business sector, religious leaders, community leaders and other parties as needed.
3. In carrying out the main duties, members of the Child-Friendly Regency Task Force as referred to in paragraph (2) have detailed tasks including:
   - a. coordinating various efforts for the development of Child-Friendly Regency;
   - b. formulating the Regional Action Plan for Child-Friendly Regency Development;
   - c. implementing socialization, advocacy and communication for the development of Child-Friendly Regency;
   - d. conducting monitoring of the implementation of policies, programs and activities in the Regional Action Plan for Child-Friendly Regency Development;
   - e. conducting evaluation at the end of each year on the implementation of policies, programs and activities in the Regional Action Plan for Child-Friendly Regency Development; and
   - f. submitting reports to the Regent.

#### Article 59

1. The Regional Action Plan as referred to in Article 58 paragraph (3) letter b is prepared for a period of 5 (five) years or according to needs, integrated with the regional medium-term development plan and the regional apparatus work plan.
2. The Regional Action Plan has an annual program focus that refers to the stages of achieving the Child-Friendly Regency indicators.
3. The Child-Friendly Regency Regional Action Plan must be based on problems in the field and their comprehensive resolution.
4. The Regional Action Plan is disseminated to all Regional Apparatus, child stakeholders, families, and the community.
5. Further provisions regarding the Child-Friendly Regency Regional Action Plan are stipulated by a Regent Regulation.

# CHAPTER XI

## MONITORING, EVALUATION, AND REPORTING

#### Article 60

Monitoring is conducted periodically and in an integrated manner every 6 (six) months by the Regional Apparatus that administers governmental affairs in the field of women's empowerment and child protection in coordination with Regional Apparatus or other units that handle women's empowerment and child protection.

#### Article 61

Evaluation of the implementation of women's empowerment and child protection is conducted by the Regional Apparatus that administers governmental affairs in the field of women's empowerment and child protection at least 2 (two) times in 1 (one) year.

#### Article 62

1. Reporting on the implementation of women's empowerment and child protection shall be conducted by:
   - a. P2TP2A to the Regent;
   - b. Task Force for the Protection of Women and Children at the Sub-district Level to the Sub-district Head;
   - c. Task Unit for the Protection of Women and Children at the Village Level to the Village Head; and
   - d. Regional Indonesian Child Protection Commission to the Regent through the Head of the Regional Apparatus that administers governmental affairs in the field of women's empowerment and child protection.
2. Reporting as referred to in paragraph (1) shall be submitted in writing, covering:
   - a. administration;
   - b. finance;
   - c. services; and
   - d. performance.
3. Submission of written reports as referred to in paragraph (2) shall be carried out at least once every 6 (six) months.

# CHAPTER XII

## FUNDING

#### Article 63

Funding for women's empowerment and child protection is sourced from: a. regional revenue and expenditure budget;

   - b. village revenue and expenditure budget; and
   - c. other legitimate sources in accordance with the provisions of laws and regulations.

# CHAPTER XIII

## SANCTIONS

#### Article 64

1. Every person who intentionally violates the provisions of Article 10 or does not provide protection against forms of violence against women and children as referred to in Article 25 and Article 26, as well as does not provide the rights of women and children as referred to in Article 27 and Article 28, thereby causing the occurrence of violence, allowing the occurrence of violence, and/or not reporting and not providing protection to victims, shall be subject to sanctions as stipulated in the laws and regulations.
2. Regency Regional Apparatus that has the duties and functions of implementing women's empowerment and child protection that does not carry out its obligations and responsibilities shall be subject to administrative sanctions.
3. The Management of P2TP2A, Task Force for the Protection of Women and Children at the Sub-district Level, Task Unit for the Protection of Women and Children at the Village Level and other institutions or organizations in accordance with the provisions of laws and regulations that carry out protection duties, if they violate protection principles, may be subject to sanctions in accordance with the mechanisms applicable within the internal institutions or respective organizations.

# CHAPTER XIV

## CLOSING PROVISIONS

#### Article 65

This Regional Regulation shall come into force on the date of promulgation. In order that everyone may know of it, the promulgation of this Regional Regulation is ordered by placing it in the Regional Gazette of Tasikmalaya Regency.
