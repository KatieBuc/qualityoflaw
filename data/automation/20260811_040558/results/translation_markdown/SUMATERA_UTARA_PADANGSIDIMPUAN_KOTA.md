# THE MAYOR OF PADANG SIDEMPUAN,

   - a. that women and children are creatures of Almighty God who have human rights guaranteed by the 1945 Constitution of the Republic of Indonesia, therefore women and children must be protected from all forms of violence and discrimination;
   - b. that violence against women and children in the Region tends to increase, therefore the Regional Government and/or community need to play an active role optimally to provide protection, so that women and children are prevented and free from violence or threats of violence, torture, or treatment that degrades human dignity and worth;
   - c. that protection of women and children is a mandatory concurrent affair which is the authority, obligation, and responsibility of the Regional Government, thus requiring regulation as the basis for implementing comprehensive and integrated protection of women and children;
   - d. that based on the considerations as referred to in letter a, letter b and letter c, it is necessary to establish a Regional Regulation on Women's Empowerment and Child Protection in Padang Sidempuan City;

# CHAPTER I

## GENERAL PROVISIONS

#### Article 1

In this Regional Regulation, what is meant by:

1. Region is the City of Padang Sidempuan.
2. Regional Government is the administration of governmental affairs by the Regional Government and the Regional House of Representatives according to the principles of autonomy and assistance tasks with the principle of the widest possible autonomy within the system and principles of the Unitary State of the Republic of Indonesia as referred to in the 1945 Constitution of the Republic of Indonesia.
3. Regional Government is the Mayor as an element of the Regional Government administration who leads the implementation of governmental affairs that are the authority of the autonomous region.
4. Mayor is the Mayor of Padang Sidempuan.
5. Regional House of Representatives hereinafter abbreviated as DPRD of Padang Sidempuan City is a Regional People's Representative Institution positioned as an element of Regional Government Administration.
6. Regional Apparatus is an auxiliary element of the Mayor and the Regional House of Representatives in administering governmental affairs that are the authority of the region.
7. Child is a person who has not reached the age of 18 (eighteen) years, including a child still in the womb.
8. Women Empowerment and Child Protection System is the entire process of implementing women empowerment and child protection carried out comprehensively, inclusively, and integratively starting from the stage of report/complaint handling services, health services, social rehabilitation, law enforcement and legal assistance, up to repatriation and social reintegration for women and children victims of crime and violence to their social environment.
9. Child Protection is all activities to guarantee and protect children and their rights so they can live, grow, develop and participate optimally in accordance with human dignity and receive protection from violence and discrimination.
10. Gender is a concept that refers to the roles and responsibilities of men and women that occur as a result of and can be changed by the social and cultural conditions of society.
11. Victim is a person who experiences physical, mental suffering, and/or economic loss caused by a criminal act.
12. Violence is any act that causes injury or death to another person or causes physical damage or damage to another person's property.
13. Violence against women is any action based on gender differences that results or may result in misery or suffering of women physically, sexually, economically, socially, psychologically, including threats of certain actions, coercion or deprivation of freedom, whether occurring in public or private life.
14. Violence against children is any action that results or may result in suffering of children physically, sexually, economically, socially and psychologically.
15. Victim is a woman and child who experiences violence and/or suffering as a result of violence and/or threat of violence.
16. Community is individuals, families, groups, social organizations and/or community organizations.
17. Family is the smallest unit in society consisting of husband and wife, or husband and wife and their children, or father and children, and mother and children and among people who are their dependents.
18. Social Rehabilitation is a service aimed at restoring and developing the ability of a person experiencing social dysfunction so they can carry out their social functions properly.
19. Repatriation is an effort to return victims of violence from a regional area to their area of origin.
20. Social Reintegration is an effort to reunite victims with family, substitute family, or community that can provide protection and fulfillment of needs for victims.
21. Prevention is a direct effort carried out by the regional government to prevent acts of violence against women and children.
22. Companion is a person or representative from an institution who has expertise in accompanying victims to conduct counseling, therapy and advocacy for strengthening and recovery of violence victims.
23. Safe house is a temporary residence used to provide protection to victims in accordance with determined operational standards.
24. Child Friendly City hereinafter abbreviated as KLA is a city that has a child rights-based development system through the integration of commitments and resources of government, community and business world that are planned comprehensively and sustainably in policies, programs and activities to ensure the fulfillment of children's rights.
25. Child friendly city task force hereinafter abbreviated as KLA task force is a coordinative institution at the city level that coordinates policies, programs and activities to realize KLA.
26. Business Entity is an institution that daily carries out activities seeking profit and gain, but has a commitment to the protection of women and children.
27. Social welfare is a condition of fulfillment of material, spiritual, and social needs of citizens so they can live properly and are able to develop themselves, so they can carry out their social functions.
28. Gender mainstreaming hereinafter abbreviated as PUG is a strategy built to integrate gender into an integral dimension of planning, formulation, implementation, monitoring, and evaluation of development program policies.
29. Integrated Service Center for Women and Child Protection hereinafter abbreviated as P2TP2A is an integrated service center in efforts to empower women in various fields of development, as well as protection of women and children from various types of discrimination and acts of violence, including trafficking in persons, established by the regional government or community-based, which includes: referral center, business consultation center, reproductive health consultation center, legal consultation center, integrated crisis center (PKT), integrated service center (PPT), trauma recovery center (trauma center), women crisis center, training center, science and technology information center (PIPTEK), safe house (shelter), transit house, children's friend telephone (TESA) 129, or other forms.

# CHAPTER II

## PRINCIPLES

#### Article 2

The implementation of Protection of Women and Children shall be carried out based on the principles of:

   - a. respect and fulfillment of the rights of victims;
   - b. justice and gender equality;
   - c. non-discrimination;
   - d. best interests of victims;
   - e. legal certainty;
   - f. legal protection;
   - g. empowerment.

# CHAPTER III

## PURPOSE AND OBJECTIVES

#### Article 3

1. The purpose of the establishment of this Regional Regulation is to provide a foundation of legal certainty in the activities of Women's Empowerment and Child Protection in the Region.
2. The implementation of Women's and Children's Protection aims to:
   - a. improve the quality of life of women, children, and the quality of families;
   - b. increase the institutional capacity of women's empowerment and child protection including the development of gender and child data systems;
   - c. provide protection of women's rights and fulfillment of children's rights including special protection for children from various forms of violence and other discriminatory treatment;
   - d. provide guidance to regional governments in planning policy strategies for the protection of women and children; and
   - e. prevent violations of the rights of women and children.

# CHAPTER IV

   - a. rights and obligations of women and children;

   - c. prevention;
   - d. services for acts of violence;
   - e. implementation of protection of women and children;
   - f. information system data on women and children;

      - i. Child-Friendly City;

   - n. criminal provisions; and
   - o. Closing provisions.

# CHAPTER V

## RIGHTS AND OBLIGATIONS

## Part One

Rights of Women and Children

#### Article 5

1. Every woman has the right to:
   - a. obtain protection from discriminatory actions;
   - b. obtain protection from economic exploitation and sexual exploitation;
   - c. obtain protection from physical, psychological, sexual violence, and neglect;
   - d. obtain decent work in accordance with her capabilities;
   - e. obtain work leave while receiving wages/salary in accordance with statutory regulations;
   - f. obtain education and teaching in all types, levels and pathways of education;
   - g. obtain special protection in the implementation of work or profession against matters that may threaten safety and or health in relation to women's reproductive function; and
   - h. ownership and management of joint assets in marriage in accordance with statutory regulations.
2. Every child has the right to:
   - a. live, grow, develop and participate reasonably in accordance with human dignity;
   - b. obtain protection from discriminatory actions;
   - c. obtain protection from economic exploitation and sexual exploitation;
   - d. obtain protection from physical, psychological, sexual violence, and neglect;
   - e. to a name as personal identity and citizenship status;
   - f. obtain a birth certificate free of charge;
   - g. to embrace a religion and worship according to the teachings of their religion with the guidance of parents/guardians;
   - h. to not be forced to change religion by foster parents, adoptive parents and by anyone;
      - i. to be recognized and know their parents, be raised and cared for by their own parents;
   - j. obtain health services and social security in accordance with physical, mental, spiritual, and social needs;
   - k. obtain education and teaching in accordance with their interests and talents;
   - l. obtain care, education, training, self-improvement and the ability to participate in community and state life for children with physical, mental disabilities and special needs;
   - m. express and have their opinion heard; and
   - n. rest and utilize leisure time for self-development.

Second Part Obligations of Regional Government

#### Article 6

1. The obligations of Regional Government in efforts to protect women include:
   - a. establishing and implementing policies, programs and activities for the protection of women's rights;
   - b. conducting cooperation with the community and/or Community Organizations in the implementation of women's protection in accordance with laws and regulations;
   - c. conducting rehabilitation and empowerment of women who are victims of violence;
   - d. providing special protection to women victims of violence and human trafficking; and
   - e. taking preventive action in order to prevent violations of women's rights.
2. The obligations of Regional Government in efforts to protect children include:
   - a. being responsible for respecting the fulfillment of children's rights without distinguishing ethnicity, religion, race, class, gender, ethnicity, culture and language, legal status, birth order, and physical and/or mental condition;
   - b. having the obligation and responsibility to implement and support national policies in the implementation of child protection in the region;
   - c. the policy as referred to in letter b can be realized through regional efforts to build child-friendly regencies/cities;
   - d. having the obligation and responsibility to provide support for facilities, infrastructure, and availability of human resources in the implementation of child protection;
   - e. guaranteeing the protection, care, and welfare of children with due regard to the rights and obligations of parents, guardians, or other persons who are legally responsible for the child;
   - f. supervising the implementation of child protection;
   - g. guaranteeing children to exercise their rights in expressing opinions in accordance with the age and level of intelligence of the child.

Third Part Obligations of the Community

#### Article 7

1. Obligations of the community in efforts to protect women and children:
   - a. participate in prevention and provide support for the development of prevention programs against violations of the rights of women and children;
   - b. provide information and/or report violations of the rights of women and children to the competent authorities; and
   - c. supervise the law enforcement process regarding cases of violations of the rights of women and children.
2. The obligations of the community as referred to in paragraph (1) are based on laws and regulations and the values of local wisdom of the local community.
3. The obligations and responsibilities of the community towards child protection are implemented through community role activities in the implementation of child protection.
4. The obligations and responsibilities of the community towards child protection are implemented through community role activities in the implementation of child protection.
5. The obligations and responsibilities of the community as referred to in paragraph (4) are implemented by involving community organizations, academics, and child welfare observers.

#### Article 8

The family, in the effort to protect the rights of women, is obligated to:

   - a. protect women from conflict and violence in the household that degrades the dignity of women;
   - b. provide equal opportunities for women to develop themselves in the fields of education, social, economic, political, and cultural affairs; and
   - c. prevent the occurrence of violations of the rights of women in the household.

#### Article 9

1. The family, in efforts to protect the rights of children, has the obligation to:
   - a. care for, maintain, educate, and protect children;
   - b. develop children in accordance with their abilities, talents, and interests;
   - c. prevent marriage at a child's age;
   - d. provide character education and instill moral values in children.
2. In the event that parents do not exist, or their whereabouts are unknown, or due to a certain reason, cannot carry out their obligations and responsibilities, then the obligations and responsibilities as referred to in paragraph (1) may be transferred to the family.

# CHAPTER VI

## FORMS OF VIOLENCE

#### Article 10

Forms of violence include:

   - a. physical violence;
   - b. psychological violence;
   - c. sexual violence; and/or
   - d. domestic neglect.

#### Article 11

Physical violence as referred to in Article 10 letter a results in:

   - a. the victim falling ill or being seriously injured;
   - b. the death of the victim; and
   - c. causing illness or impediment to carrying out work duties or livelihood or daily activities.

#### Article 12

Psychological violence as referred to in Article 10 letter b results in fear, loss of self-confidence, loss of the ability to act, feelings of helplessness and/or severe psychological suffering in a person.

2021, No. 3 -82 

#### Article 13

Sexual violence as referred to in Article 10 letter c in the form of:

   - a. acts of sexual harassment;
   - b. coercion of sexual relations in an unnatural or unwanted manner; and/or
   - c. coercion of sexual relations with another person for commercial purposes and/or certain purposes.

#### Article 14

Household neglect as referred to in Article 10 letter d is caused by:

   - a. acts that result in the failure to fulfill the needs of a child properly, whether physical, mental, spiritual or social, committed by parents, guardians, or any other party responsible for their care;
   - b. acts of deliberately neglecting to maintain, care for, or look after a child as appropriate, committed by parents, guardians, or any other party responsible for their care;
   - c. acts that neglect a person within the household scope, whereas according to the law applicable to them or due to an agreement or contract they are obligated to provide livelihood, care, or maintenance to that person; and/or
   - d. acts that result in economic dependency by means of limiting and/or prohibiting proper work inside or outside the home so that the victim is under the control of that person.

# CHAPTER VII

## PREVENTION

#### Article 15

1. Prevention efforts against violence toward women and children are carried out in an integrated manner by the Regional Government coordinated by the PPPA Office.
2. Prevention efforts as referred to in paragraph (1) are implemented by means of:
   - a. building networks and collaboration with law enforcement apparatus, government apparatus, as well as educational institutions, health facilities, hospitals, various NGOs that care about women and children;
   - b. building community and family participation and awareness toward prevention and protection of women and children from acts of violence;
   - c. establishing a violence prevention system that provides a complete and easily accessible information system;
   - d. conducting socialization regarding laws and regulations related to the protection of women and children victims of violence;
   - e. providing critical education about the rights of women and children as victims of violence to the community; and
   - f. opening complaint posts for the protection of women and children from acts of violence.

#### Article 16

1. Prevention of acts of violence against women and children as referred to in Article 15, shall be implemented by the PPPA Office and related regional apparatus/institutions in the fields of;
   - a. education;
   - b. employment;
   - c. health;
   - d. social affairs;
   - e. women's empowerment and child protection;
   - f. mental and spiritual; and
   - g. public peace and order.
2. Prevention of acts of violence by the PPPA Office and related regional apparatus/institutions as referred to in paragraph (1), shall be implemented in an integrated and sustainable manner.

# CHAPTER VIII

## SERVICE KOREAN ACTS OF VIOLENCE

#### Article 17

Implementation of Victim Recovery Activities Includes:

   - a. health services;
   - b. victim assistance;
   - c. counseling;
   - d. spiritual guidance; and
   - e. resocialization;

#### Article 18

1. Health Services are carried out by health workers at health facilities owned by the government, regional government, and the community, including the private sector by providing treatment services and health recovery for victims.
2. Victim assistance is carried out by health workers, social workers, volunteer assistants, and/or spiritual guides by providing counseling, spiritual guidance and advocacy for the strengthening and self-recovery of the victim.
3. Provision of counseling is carried out by social workers, volunteer assistants, by listening empathetically and exploring problems for the psychological strengthening of victims.
4. Spiritual guidance is carried out by spiritual guides by providing explanations regarding their rights and obligations, as well as strengthening faith and piety in accordance with the religion and beliefs they adhere to.
5. Resocialization of victims is carried out by social agencies and social institutions so that victims can return to carrying out their social functions in society.

##### BABX

##### INFORMATION SYSTEM FOR WOMEN AND CHILDREN DATA

#### Article 20

1. Information system is a system to collect, prepare, store, process, analyze and/or disseminate information.
2. Gender and Children Data System is the institutionalization of gender and children data administration consisting of components-components-components of regulations, institutions, and mechanisms in ministries/agencies and regions in the framework of planning, implementation, evaluation, and reporting of results of policies/programs/activities of development that is gender responsive and child sensitive.
3. Gender Data is data regarding relational relationships in status, roles and conditions between men and women.
4. Disaggregated Data is data disaggregated by sex, age, status and conditions of women in all fields of development including health, education, economy and employment, political field and decision making, legal and socio-cultural fields and violence.
5. Types of gender and children data:
   - a. gender data:
1. gender data in economic field including among others gender issues in labor, KUKM, industry and trade, agriculture, forestry, fisheries and marine affairs, transportation, science and technology and infrastructure;
2. gender data in political, social and legal fields;
3. women's rights protection data; and
4. family welfare and resilience development data.
   - b. children data:
1. civil rights and freedoms;
2. family environment and alternative care;
3. basic health and welfare;
4. education, leisure time utilization and cultural activities; and
5. special protection.
6. Gender analysis is a process of analyzing disaggregated data by sex conducted systematically with the purpose of identifying gender issues and factors that can influence development, particularly related to gender equality issues which are the objective of development.

#### Article 21

1. The Guidelines for the Implementation of the Gender and Child Data System are intended as a reference for ministries/agencies and regions in providing and utilizing disaggregated data for planning, formulation, implementation, monitoring, evaluation and reporting of gender-responsive and child-sensitive development policies/programs/activities.
2. To increase the availability and utilization of gender and child data and information in the formulation, planning, implementation, monitoring and evaluation of development programs/activities.

#### Article 22

1. The Guidelines for the Implementation of the Gender and Child Data System aim to:
   - a. strengthen and encourage institutional arrangements (regulations, institutions, mechanisms) of the data system disaggregated by sex and age in ministries/agencies and regions, which are reliable, can be presented quickly, accurately, comprehensively, and up-to-date;
   - b. establish or strengthen coordination mechanisms among ministries/agencies and regions in the implementation of disaggregated data collection;
   - c. increase the availability and utilization of disaggregated data for planning, implementation, monitoring, evaluation, and reporting of results of gender-responsive and child-sensitive development policies/programs/activities in ministries/agencies and regions.
2. The Implementation of the Electronic-Based Gender and Child Data System of Padang Sidempuan City includes the transmission, verification, data entry and publication of data.
3. The Utilization of the Gender and Child Data System of Padang Sidempuan City consists of:
   - a. regional apparatus within the Padang Sidempuan City Government;
   - b. agencies domiciled in Padang Sidempuan City; and
   - c. the general public/public.

#### Article 23

1. Gender Mainstreaming or abbreviated as PUG is a strategy carried out rationally and systematically to achieve and realize gender equality and gender justice in a number of aspects of human life (household, society and State), through policies and programs that take into account the experiences and aspirations, needs and problems of women and men in the planning, implementation, monitoring and evaluation of all policies in various aspects of life and development.
2. In an effort to accelerate the institutionalization of gender mainstreaming in all SKPD, a PUG Working Group is established.
3. Members of the PUG Working Group are all heads/leaders of SKPD.
4. The Mayor appoints the head of Bapeda as chairman of the PUG Working Group and the head of the SKPD responsible for women's empowerment as Secretary of the PUG Working Group.
5. The establishment of the PUG Working Group is determined by Decree of the Mayor.

#### Article 24

The PUG Working Group as referred to in article 23 has the following duties:

   - a. promoting and facilitating PUG to each SKPD;
   - b. conducting socialization and advocacy of PUG to Sub-district Heads, Village Heads and Urban Village Heads;
   - c. preparing work programs every year;
   - d. encouraging the realization of gender responsive planning and budgeting;
   - e. preparing the work plan of the PUG Working Group every year;
   - f. being responsible to the Mayor through the Deputy Mayor;
   - g. formulating policy recommendations to the Mayor;
   - h. preparing the Gender Profile;
      - i. conducting monitoring of PUG implementation in each agency;
   - j. establishing a technical team to conduct analysis of the regional budget;
   - k. preparing the Regional Action Plan (RANDA) for PUG and;
   - l. encouraging the implementation of selection and appointment of Focal Points in each SKPD.

#### Article 25

1. The PUG Focal Point in each SKPD consists of officials and/or staff in charge of planning and/or program duties.
2. The PUG Focal Point as referred to in paragraph (1), has the following duties:
   - a. promoting gender mainstreaming in the work unit;
   - b. facilitating the preparation of work plans and SKPD budgeting that is Gender responsive;
   - c. implementing training, socialization, advocacy for gender mainstreaming to all officials and staff within the SKPD;
   - d. encouraging the implementation of gender analysis on policies, programs, and activities in the work unit; and
   - e. facilitating the preparation of gender data in each SKPD;
3. The PUG Focal Point as referred to in paragraph (1), is selected and determined by the head/leader of the SKPD.

#### Article 26

1. Regional Apparatus/Agencies shall submit reports on the women and children data information system to the Mayor through the Office of Women's Empowerment and Child Protection of Padang Sidempuan City.
2. Reporting of implementation as referred to in paragraph (1) shall be conducted annually and/or at any time when necessary.

#### Article 27

1. Every person, community institution, university and business entity has the right to utilize the women and children data information system of Padang Sidempuan City by citing the data.
2. Every person, community institution, university and business entity may partner as a provider or user of gender and child-related data and information to the administrator of the women and children data information system of Padang Sidempuan City for subsequent verification, data entry, and publication.

# CHAPTER XI

## CHILDREN'S FORUM

#### Article 28

1. The Regional Government is obligated to establish a children's forum at the Padang Sidempuan City level as participation through the children's forum.
2. The children's forum as referred to in paragraph (1) constitutes a representation of children, both representation of children's geographical domicile, components of children's socio-cultural groups and children's educational background as well as accommodating the interests of children with special needs.
3. In every formulation of policies related to children, the Regional Government must consider and accommodate the opinions of children conveyed through the children's forum.
4. The establishment of the children's forum as referred to in paragraph (1) shall be stipulated by Mayor's Decree.
5. Sources of financing for the implementation of children's forum activities may come from:
   - a. contributions from members of the children's forum;
   - b. donations from the community/private parties that are legal and non-binding in nature;
   - c. assistance from the City Government; and/or
   - d. other sources of financing in accordance with laws and regulations.

#### Article 29

1. Formation of the Children's Forum of Padang Sidempuan City: participants in the meeting for the formation of the district/city children's forum are delegates or representatives of the sub-district children's forum, at least one male child and one female child. The number of representatives is adjusted to the needs and the number of sub-districts in the area of Padang Sidempuan City.
2. Formation of the Sub-district Children's Forum: participants in the meeting for the formation of the Sub-district Children's Forum are delegates or representatives of the village/urban village children's forum, at least one male child and one female child. The number of representatives is adjusted to the needs and the number of villages/urban villages in the area of that sub-district.
3. Formation of the Village/Urban Village Children's Forum: children are invited representing their respective activity groups, at least one male child and one female child from each children's activity group; furthermore, the child representatives from these activity groups become the main participants in the formation of the Village/Urban Village Children's Forum.

# CHAPTER XII

INSTITUTIONAL FRAMEWORK FOR THE IMPLEMENTATION OF PROTECTION

## OF WOMEN AND CHILDREN

In the context of implementing the Protection of Women and Children, the following shall be established:

   - a. Integrated Service Center for the Empowerment of Women and Children (P2TP2A);
   - b. Family Welfare Consultation Institution; and
   - c. Other institutions in accordance with needs and/or provisions of laws and regulations.

#### Article 31

1. In the context of handling family psychosocial problems, recovery of family psychosocial conditions and strengthening family resilience, a Family Welfare Consultation Institution shall be established in the region.
2. With regard to the Family Welfare Consultation Institution, the Mayor has the following authorities:
   - a. implementing policies for the organization of the Family Welfare Consultation Institution;
   - b. implementing norms, standards, procedures, and criteria regarding the Family Welfare Consultation Institution;
   - c. implementing service standards of the Family Welfare Consultation Institution;
   - d. implementing and managing institutional data of the Family Welfare Consultation Institution;
   - e. implementing capacity strengthening of human resources and institutions of the Family Welfare Consultation Institution;
   - f. providing facilitation of facilities and infrastructure for services of the Family Welfare Consultation Institution;
   - g. implementing family welfare cooperation;
   - h. implementing coordination with relevant agencies, the business sector and the community; and/or
1. implementing monitoring and evaluation.

#### Article 32

1. Services of the Family Welfare Consultation Institution are organized by prioritizing strategies to build networks and partnerships.
2. Types of services of the Family Welfare Consultation Institution consist of:
   - a. consultation;
   - b. counseling;
   - c. information;
   - d. advocacy;
   - e. referral; and
   - f. outreach.
3. Networks and partnerships as referred to in paragraph (1) consist of:
   - a. potential and sources of social welfare;
   - b. Hospitals;
   - c. Indonesian National Police;
   - d. Bureau/Institution/Legal Aid Post;
   - e. Trauma Center Shelter;
   - f. Integrated Service Center for Women and Children Empowerment;
   - g. other public services that can serve as networks and partnerships of the Family Welfare Consultation Institution.
4. Networks and partnerships as referred to in paragraph (1) are conducted through:
   - a. cooperation;
   - b. coordination; and/or
   - c. collaboration.
5. Further provisions regarding the Family Welfare Consultation Institution are regulated by Mayor Regulation.

#### Article 33

1. The Integrated Service Center for Women's Empowerment and Child Protection (P2TP2A) is under the coordination of regional apparatus work units (SKPD) or other units that handle women's empowerment and child protection.
2. The Integrated Service Center for Women's Empowerment and Child Protection (P2TP2A) as referred to in paragraph (1) does not have authority in the formulation of policies related to women's empowerment and child protection.

#### Article 34

Integrated Service Center for Women's Empowerment and Child Protection (P2TP2A) which functions as:

   - a. information center for women and children;
   - b. service center for women and victims of violence; and
   - c. empowerment center for women and children.

#### Article 35

1. The Integrated Service Center for Women's Empowerment and Child Protection (P2TP2A) at City Level shall be established based on a Mayor's Decree and a District Head's Decree for the sub-district level.
2. Membership of the Integrated Service Center for Women's Empowerment and Children (P2TP2A) may consist of elements from regional apparatus, professional organizations, academics, community leaders and other elements as needed.
3. Further provisions regarding P2TP2A shall be regulated by a Mayor Regulation.

#### Article 36

The Family Learning Center (PUSPAGA) is a form of preventive service under the coordination of the Office of Women's Empowerment and Child Protection as a manifestation of state concern in improving family life and family resilience through education or parenting programs, parenting skills, child protection skills, ability to increase child participation in the family as well as counseling program services for children and families.

# CHAPTER XIII

## CHILD-FRIENDLY CITY

## Part One

Principles and Objectives

#### Article 37

To guarantee the fulfillment of children's rights so they can live, grow, develop, and participate optimally in accordance with human dignity and receive protection from violence and discrimination, real integrated efforts are needed between the Regional Government, community and business sector through the development of Child-Friendly Cities.

#### Article 38

The development of Child-Friendly Cities as referred to in Article 37 shall be implemented based on the following principles:

   - a. good governance, namely transparency, accountability, participation, information disclosure, and rule of law;
   - b. non-discrimination, namely not differentiating based on ethnicity, race, religion, gender, language, political views, national origin, economic status, physical or psychological condition of the child, or other factors;
   - c. the best interests of the child, namely making what is best for the child as the primary consideration in every policy, program, and activity;
   - d. the right to life, survival, and development of the child, namely guaranteeing the right to life, survival and development of the child to the maximum extent possible; and
   - e. respect for the views of the child, namely recognizing and ensuring that every child who has the ability to express their opinion, is given the opportunity to express their views freely on all matters that affect them.

#### Article 39

The Development of Child-Friendly Districts/Regencies is directed toward the fulfillment of children's rights which include:

   - a. civil rights and freedoms;
   - b. family environment and alternative care;
   - c. basic health and welfare;
   - d. education, utilization of leisure time, and cultural activities; and
   - e. special protection.

#### Article 40

1. In order to realize a Child-Friendly City, stages of Child-Friendly City development are arranged which include:
   - a. preparation;
   - b. planning;
   - c. implementation;
   - d. monitoring;
   - e. evaluation; and
   - f. reporting.
2. In every stage of Child-Friendly City development, it is mandatory to consider the views of children obtained through child consultation.

#### Article 41

1. To make effective the development of the Child-Friendly City (Kota Layak Anak), a Child-Friendly City (Kota Layak Anak) Task Force shall be established as determined by the Mayor's Decree.
2. Membership of the Child-Friendly City (Kota Layak Anak) Task Force may come from the Regional Government, child representatives, the business sector, religious leaders, community leaders, and other parties as needed.

#### Article 42

1. The main duties of the Child-Friendly City Task Force are as follows:
   - a. coordinating various efforts for the development of a Child-Friendly City;
   - b. preparing the Regional Action Plan for Child-Friendly City Development;
   - c. implementing socialization, advocacy and communication for the development of a Child-Friendly City;
   - d. conducting monitoring of the implementation of policies, programs and activities in the Regional Action Plan for Child-Friendly City Development;
   - e. conducting evaluation at the end of each year on the implementation of policies, programs and activities in the Regional Action Plan for Child-Friendly City Development; and
   - f. submitting reports to the Mayor.
2. Further provisions regarding the Child-Friendly City Task Force shall be regulated by Mayor Regulation.

# CHAPTER XIV

## FUNDING

#### Article 43

1. Regional Governments are obligated to provide budget for the implementation of women's empowerment and child protection programs and activities.
2. Financing for the implementation of women's empowerment and child protection activities as referred to in paragraph (1) is sourced from:
   - a. Regional Revenue and Expenditure Budget (APBD); and
   - b. other legal and non-binding sources based on the provisions of laws and regulations.

# CHAPTER XV

## COMMUNITY PARTICIPATION

#### Article 44

1. The community may participate in the implementation of women's empowerment activities.
2. Community participation as referred to in paragraph (1) may be carried out by individuals, community groups and community organizations.
3. Community participation as referred to in paragraph (1) may take the form of:
   - a. cooperation;
   - b. an active role in policy formulation; and
   - c. complaints/reports.
4. The procedures for community participation as referred to in paragraph (3) shall be regulated by a Mayor's Regulation.

# CHAPTER XVI

## COOPERATION

#### Article 45

1. Regional cooperation is joint efforts between regions with other regions, between regions and third parties, and/or between regions and institutions or regional governments abroad based on the balance of efficiency and effectiveness of public services and mutual benefit.
2. Regional cooperation with third parties, hereinafter abbreviated as KSDPK, is joint efforts undertaken by regions with third parties in the context of implementing governmental affairs that are regional authority to improve community welfare and accelerate the fulfillment of public services.
3. In order to achieve the objective of protecting the rights of women and children from acts of violence, regional governments cooperate with:
   - a. other provinces;
   - b. regencies/cities; and
   - c. non-governmental institutions (4) Cooperation as referred to in paragraph (3) includes:
   - a. exchange of data and information
   - b. rehabilitation of victims of acts of violence
   - c. repatriation and social reintegration, and
   - d. provision of physical evidence and witnesses which are followed up in accordance with applicable law.

#### Article 46

Regional governments provide services for victims in the form of;

   - a. facilitating the establishment of UPT P2TP2A in each district/city;
   - b. facilitating facilities and infrastructure for UPT P3TP2A;
   - c. facilitating the Victims of Violence Protection Coordination Forum as a forum for a network for handling victims.

# CHAPTER XVII

## GUIDANCE AND SUPERVISION

#### Article 47 (1) Regional Government is obligated to conduct guidance and supervision of Women's Empowerment and Child Protection.

2. Guidance as referred to in paragraph (1), includes:
   - a. formulation of policies and regulations to be used as guidelines and standards in the implementation of women's empowerment and child protection;
   - b. technical guidance and training, including:
1. providing training for government officials and law enforcement regarding prevention and handling of violence;
2. providing training for religious leaders, community leaders, traditional leaders, educators, journalists and media managers in order to prevent violence against women and children;
3. providing training to health workers who provide health services to women and children victims of violence at community health centers, integrated service units and general hospitals;
4. providing training to social rehabilitation officers and spiritual guidance officers in order to protect women and children victims of violence;
5. providing training to PUG working groups and focal points of each Regional Apparatus for the preparation of activity programs based on gender responsive planning and budgeting.
   - c. provision of facilities and infrastructure in implementing women's empowerment and child protection.
   - d. facilitation of the establishment of nagari that care for women and children;
   - e. provision of information through print and electronic media related to the protection of women and children.
3. Supervision as referred to in paragraph (1), includes:
   - a. monitoring implemented in the form of submission of reports on the implementation of women's empowerment and child protection programs by the Head of Regional Apparatus to the Mayor periodically 1 (one) time every 3 (three) months; and
   - b. evaluation of the implementation of women's empowerment and child protection periodically 1 (one) time every 3 (three) months conducted by the relevant Regional Apparatus.
4. The results of supervision as referred to in paragraph (3) constitute evaluation material for the policy of women's empowerment and child protection programs.
5. Further provisions regarding guidance and supervision as referred to in paragraph (1) shall be regulated by Mayor Regulation.
