# CHAPTER I

## GENERAL PROVISIONS

#### Article 1

In this Regional Regulation, what is meant by:

1. Region is Banyumas Regency.
2. Regional Government is the Regent and Regional Apparatus as elements of the Regional Government administration.
3. Regent is the Regent of Banyumas.
4. Regional Apparatus Work Unit, hereinafter abbreviated as SKPD, is a regional apparatus assigned with specific principal duties and authorities in the region.
5. Child is a person who has not yet reached the age of 18 (eighteen) years, including a child still in the womb.
6. Gender is a concept that refers to the roles and responsibilities of men and women that occur as a result of and can be changed by the social and cultural conditions of society.
7. Child Protection is all activities to guarantee and protect children and their rights so that they can live, grow, develop, and participate optimally in accordance with human dignity and worth, as well as receive protection from violence and discrimination.
8. Protection Implementation is a series of activities aimed at preventing the occurrence of violence, providing protection and services for victims of gender-based violence and children, conducting coordination and cooperation, and increasing community participation carried out by the Integrated Service Center for the Protection of Victims of Gender-Based Violence and Children.
9. Gender-Based Violence is any form of restriction, exclusion, differentiation and all forms of treatment carried out on the basis of gender and aimed at reducing or eliminating the recognition, enjoyment or exercise of human rights, the consequences of which include but are not limited to physical, sexual, psychological and economic violence.
10. Violence Against Children is any form of restriction, differentiation, exclusion and all forms of treatment carried out against children, the consequences of which include but are not limited to physical, sexual, psychological, and economic violence.
11. Victim of Gender-Based Violence is a person who, because of their gender, experiences physical, psychological, economic, social, sexual suffering, and other losses resulting from state policies, acts of violence and/or threats of violence within the scope of the household and community.
12. Child Victim of Violence is a child who receives harsh treatment either physically, psychologically, economically, socially, sexually, and other losses resulting from state policies, acts of violence and/or threats of violence within the scope of the household and community.
13. Victim Recovery is all efforts to strengthen victims of gender-based violence and children so that they are more capable, whether physically, psychologically, socially, economically or sexually.
14. Companion is a social worker who has professional competence in their field.
15. Assistance is all actions in the form of counseling, psychological therapy, advocacy, and spiritual guidance, for the purpose of strengthening the victim of gender-based violence and children to resolve the problems they face.
16. Social Reintegration is the process of preparing victims and communities that support the reunification of victims into a family environment or family substitute that can provide protection and fulfillment of the victim's needs.
17. Safe House is a temporary residence used to provide protection for victims of gender-based violence and children in accordance with determined operational standards.
18. Integrated Service Center for the Protection of Victims of Gender-Based Violence and Children, hereinafter abbreviated as PPT PKBGA, is a special service place for victims of gender-based violence and children carried out in a cross-functional and cross-sectoral manner as one unified implementation so that victims receive maximum service in an integrated and comprehensive manner.
19. Sub-District Level Complaint Service Institution, hereinafter abbreviated as LPPTK, is a special service place for victims of gender-based violence and children located at the sub-district level so that victims receive maximum service in an integrated and comprehensive manner.

# CHAPTER II

## PRINCIPLES, OBJECTIVES AND SCOPE

#### Article 2

The Principles of Implementation of Protection for Victims of Gender-Based Violence and Children are:

   - a. non-discrimination;
   - b. best interests of victims;
   - c. gender justice and equality;
   - d. victim protection;
   - e. maternal survival;
   - f. survival, growth, and development of children;
   - g. respect for the views of children;
   - h. transparency;
      - i. integration;
   - j. not blaming victims;
   - k. empowerment;
   - l. victim confidentiality;
   - m. decision-making in the hands of victims.

#### Article 3

The Objectives of Victim Protection are to:

   - a. prevent all forms of gender-based violence and violence against children, occurring within the household and/or community;
   - b. provide protection in the form of complaint services, health services, social rehabilitation services, legal aid and law enforcement services, as well as return and social reintegration services;
   - c. endeavor to increase community participation.

#### Article 4

The scope of protection for Victims includes prevention efforts, services and empowerment for victims of violence.

# CHAPTER III

## FORMS OF VIOLENCE

#### Article 5

Forms of violence are:

   - a. physical violence;
   - b. psychological violence;
   - c. sexual violence;
   - d. neglect;
   - e. exploitation; and/or
   - f. other violence.

#### Article 6

Physical violence as referred to in Article 5 letter a is caused by acts that result in pain, injury, wounds or disability to a person's body, miscarriage, loss of consciousness and/or cause death.

#### Article 7

Psychological violence as referred to in Article 5 letter b is caused by acts that result in fear, loss of self-confidence, loss of ability to act, feelings of helplessness and/or severe psychological suffering in a person.

#### Article 8

Sexual violence as referred to in Article 5 letter c occurs due to:

   - a. acts in the form of sexual harassment;
   - b. forced sexual intercourse;
   - c. forced sexual intercourse in an unnatural or unwanted manner; and/or
   - d. forced sexual intercourse with another person for commercial purposes and/or specific purposes.

#### Article 9

Neglect as referred to in Article 5 letter d is caused by:

   - a. acts that result in the failure to fulfill the needs of a child properly, whether physical, mental, spiritual or social, committed by parents, guardians, or any other party responsible for their care;
   - b. acts of deliberately neglecting to maintain, care for, or look after a child as appropriate, committed by parents, guardians, or any other party responsible for their care;
   - c. acts that neglect a person within the household scope; and/or
   - d. acts that result in economic dependency by means of restricting and/or prohibiting the victim from performing decent work inside or outside the home so that the victim is under the control of that person.

#### Article 10

Exploitation as referred to in Article 5 letter e is caused by:

   - a. acts that exploit economically or sexually with the intention of benefiting oneself or others;
   - b. acts with or without the consent of the victim which include but are not limited to prostitution, forced labor or services, slavery or similar practices, oppression, extortion, exploitation of physical, sexual, reproductive organs, or unlawfully removing or transplanting organs and/or body tissues or exploiting the labor or capabilities of a person by other parties to obtain benefits both material and immaterial;
   - c. all forms of exploitation of sexual organs or other body organs of the victim to obtain benefits, including but not limited to all activities of prostitution or sexual abuse.

#### Article 11

Other violence as referred to in Article 5 letter f is caused by:

   - a. threat of violence includes any unlawful act in the form of speech, writing, image, symbol, or body movement, whether with or without using means that cause fear or restrict a person's fundamental freedom; and
   - b. coercion, includes a situation where a person/victim is ordered to do something in such a way that the person does something that is contrary to their own will.

# CHAPTER IV

## OBLIGATIONS AND RESPONSIBILITIES

## Part One

Regional Government

#### Article 12

1. The Regional Government has the obligation and responsibility to implement efforts to protect victims of violence in the form of:
   - a. preventing the occurrence of all forms of gender-based and child violence;
   - b. providing protection by providing complaint services, health services, social rehabilitation services, legal aid and enforcement services, repatriation and social reintegration services, seeking to increase community participation, as well as monitoring and reporting;
   - c. encouraging and increasing community participation;
   - d. cooperating with service providers in efforts to prevent, protect and recover victims of violence.
2. To carry out the obligations as referred to in paragraph (1), the Regional Government has the authority to:
   - a. formulate policies and programs on the elimination of gender-based and child violence;
   - b. establish an integrated service center;
   - c. establish complaint service institutions at the sub-district level;
   - d. facilitate the implementation of integrated service centers and activities of complaint service institutions at the sub-district level;
   - e. provide facilities and infrastructure;
   - f. increase the capacity of service provider institutions;
   - g. conduct coordination and cooperation in the implementation of protection for victims of gender-based and child violence;
   - h. encourage and increase community participation;
      - i. conduct monitoring and evaluation.
3. In the implementation of its obligations, responsibilities and authorities, the Regent may delegate to the relevant SKPD.
4. The relevant SKPD receiving delegation from the Regent as referred to in paragraph (3) shall in its implementation prepare a Regional Action Plan for the protection of victims.

Second Part Society, Family and Parents

#### Article 13

In the Effort to Provide protection to victims of violence, the Community, Family and Parents have the obligation and responsibility: a. to prevent the occurrence of violence against women and children; b. to report if violence occurs;

   - c. to protect victims; and
   - d. to provide emergency assistance.

Fourth Part Rights of Victims of Violence

#### Article 32

1. Every victim of gender-based violence and every child victim has the right to: a. obtain protection for personal, family and property security, and freedom from threats related to testimony that will be, is being, or has been given; b. participate in the process of selecting and determining the form of protection and security support;
   - c. freedom from ensnaring questions;
   - d. obtain information regarding case developments and court decisions;
   - e. obtain complaint services, health services, social rehabilitation services, legal aid and enforcement services, as well as repatriation and social reintegration services.
2. The rights of victims as referred to in paragraph (1) shall be implemented in accordance with the provisions of laws and regulations.

##### I. GENERAL

The right to a decent life, self-respect, and the dignity and worth of women and children should properly receive protection in accordance with their nature and disposition, without discrimination and against the possibility of acts of violence.

The policy for protection services for victims of gender-based violence and children has been normatively regulated in the Regulation of the State Minister for Women's Empowerment and Child Protection of the Republic of Indonesia Number 01 year 2010 concerning Minimum Service Standards in the field of Integrated Services for women and children victims of violence.

Concrete steps that can be taken by the Regional Government so that women and children are protected from acts of violence, threats of violence, torture or treatment that degrades human dignity and worth, can be done by implementing one legal rule embodied in the form of a regional regulation. The formulation of norms concerning the protection of women and children victims of violence in the form of a regional regulation needs to be done, the enactment of a regional regulation is in order to reinforce institutional support.

This Regional Regulation regulates protection efforts for victims of violence particularly in terms of prevention, services and empowerment of women and children victims of violence, community participation, implementation mechanisms, development, funding as well as development and supervision.

##### II. ARTICLE BY ARTICLE

#### Article 2

Letter a What is meant by "non-discrimination" is protection for all victims of gender-based violence and child victims without distinguishing ethnicity, religion, race, group, sex, ethnic group, culture and language, legal status, and physical or mental condition.

Letter b What is meant by "the best interests of the victim" is all actions concerning victims carried out by the government, the community, and judicial bodies; therefore, the best interests of the victim must be the primary consideration.

Letter c What is meant by "gender justice" is fair treatment given to women and men.

What is meant by "gender equality" is a condition and position that describes harmonious, aligned, and balanced relations between men and women in obtaining opportunities/chances in access, participation, control, and benefits in the implementation of development as well as enjoying the results of development in family life, as well as in community, national, and state life.

Letter d What is meant by "victim protection" is providing a sense of security to victims carried out by family, advocates, social institutions, the police, the prosecutor's office, the courts, or other parties, either temporarily or based on a court determination.

Letter e What is meant by "maternal survival" is ensuring that a mother does not experience death occurring during pregnancy, childbirth, and postpartum, whether caused by physical or non-physical conditions.

Letter f What is meant by "children's growth and development" is as stated in the principles of the Convention on the Rights of the Child, including the right to education, the right to play, the right to create, and the right to recreation.

What is meant by "children's survival" is as stated in the principles of the Convention on the Rights of the Child, including the right to identity and the right to enjoy the highest attainable standard of health.

Letter g What is meant by "respect for the views of the child" is respect for children's rights to participate and express their opinions in decision-making, especially if it concerns matters that influence/affect their lives.

Letter h What is meant by "openness" is that in the implementation of protection for victims of gender-based violence and children, it is transparent among service providers.

Letter i What is meant by "integration" is that in the implementation of protection for victims of gender-based violence and children, it is carried out by building coordination among service providers, including medical services, legal assistance/advocates, psychologists, clergy/religious counselors, social workers, and the police.

Letter j What is meant by "not blaming the victim" is an attitude and treatment of not blaming the victim for the occurrence of violence experienced by the victim.

Letter k What is meant by "empowerment" is that every effort provided must be able to strengthen victims, whether physically, psychologically, socially, or economically.

Letter l What is meant by "victim confidentiality" is every action taken to ensure victims are in a safe condition from threats or other actions that threaten the life and psychological condition of the victim.

Letter m What is meant by "decision-making in the hands of the victim" is the victim's right to determine the best choice in resolving their problem.
