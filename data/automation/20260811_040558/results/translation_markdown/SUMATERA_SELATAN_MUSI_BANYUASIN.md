REGIONAL REGULATION OF MUSI BANYUASIN REGENCY NUMBER 11 YEAR 2018

# CONCERNING

# PROTECTION OF WOMEN AND CHILDREN

# BY THE GRACE OF GOD ALMIGHTY

# REGENT OF MUSI BANYUASIN,

Considering:

   - a. that the government has the obligation to give respect, protection and fulfillment of Human Rights of every citizen including the Rights of Women and Children;
   - b. that Women and Children are among vulnerable groups who tend to experience violence and discrimination so they need to receive optimal protection;
   - c. that the fulfillment of constitutional rights of Women and Children as well as the improvement of quality of life of Women and Children, is one of the mandatory affairs that becomes the duty, authority and responsibility of the Regency Government;
   - d. that based on the considerations as referred to in letter a, letter b and letter c, it is necessary to establish a Regional Regulation concerning Protection of Women and Children;

In view of:

1. Article 18 paragraph (6) of the 1945 Constitution of the Republic of Indonesia;
2. Law Number 28 Year 1959 concerning Establishment of Level II Regions and Municipalities in South Sumatra (State Gazette of the Republic of Indonesia Year 1959 Number 73, Supplement to the State Gazette of the Republic of Indonesia Number 1821);
3. Law Number 23 Year 2002 concerning Child Protection (State Gazette of the Republic of Indonesia Year 2002 Number 109, Supplement to the State Gazette of the Republic of Indonesia Number 4235), as amended by Law Number 35 Year 2014 concerning Amendment to Law Number 23 Year 2002 concerning Child Protection (State Gazette of the Republic of Indonesia Year 2014 Number 297, Supplement to the State Gazette of the Republic of Indonesia Number 5606);
4. Law Number 23 Year 2014 concerning Regional Government (State Gazette of the Republic of Indonesia Year 2014 Number 244, Supplement to the State Gazette of the Republic of Indonesia Number 5587), as has been amended several times, most recently by Law Number 9 Year 2015 concerning Second Amendment to Law Number 23 Year 2014 concerning Regional Government (State Gazette of the Republic of Indonesia Year 2015 Number 58, Supplement to the State Gazette of the Republic of Indonesia Number 5679);

# CHAPTER I

## GENERAL PROVISIONS

First Section Definitions, Meanings and Terminology Article 1 In this Regional Regulation, what is meant by:

1. Regency is Musi Banyuasin Regency.
2. Regency Government is the Government of Musi Banyuasin Regency.
3. Regent is the Regent of Musi Banyuasin.
4. Regional Apparatus hereinafter abbreviated as PD is the Regional Apparatus within the Government of Musi Banyuasin Regency.
5. Office of Women's Empowerment and Child Protection hereinafter abbreviated as DPPPA is the Regional Apparatus that administers the affairs of Women's Empowerment and Child Protection in Musi Banyuasin Regency.
6. Protection of Women is all efforts to guarantee and protect the rights of Women so that they can live healthily and participate optimally in accordance with human dignity and obtain protection from neglect, violence and discrimination, whether in the form of preventive, repressive or post-remedial protection (assistance and treatment).
7. Child Protection is all activities to guarantee and protect Children and their rights so that they can live, grow, develop, and participate optimally in accordance with human dignity, and receive protection from violence and discrimination.
8. Child is a person who has not yet reached the age of 18 (eighteen) years, including a Child who is still in the womb.
9. Child with Disability is a Child who has physical, mental, intellectual, or sensory limitations for a long period of time who in interacting with the environment and community attitudes may encounter obstacles that make it difficult to participate fully and effectively based on equal rights.
10. Child with Excellence is a Child who has extraordinary intelligence or has potential and/or special talents not limited to intellectual abilities, but also in other fields.
11. Prevention is direct efforts carried out by the Regency Government, community and parents and/or family to prevent violations of Women's rights and Children's Rights.
12. Children's Forum is a platform for Children's participation formed with the aim of facilitating Children's involvement in decision-making processes and enjoying changes relating to their lives either directly or indirectly, which is implemented with the consent and will of all Children based on awareness and understanding, in accordance with age and level of thinking maturity.
13. Child-Friendly Regency hereinafter abbreviated as KLA is a Regency/City that has a Child rights-based development system through the integration of government, community and business commitments and resources that are planned comprehensively and sustainably in policies, programs and activities to ensure the fulfillment of Children's rights.
14. Neglected Child is a Child whose needs are not properly fulfilled, whether physical, mental, spiritual, or social.
15. Women victims of violence are Women who experience actions that result in misery or suffering physically, sexually or psychologically, including threats of certain actions, coercion or arbitrary deprivation of freedom, whether occurring in the public sphere or in private life.
16. Child victims of violence are Children who experience actions that result in misery or suffering physically, mentally, sexually, psychologically or neglect that threatens life, body and degrades human dignity.
17. Human trafficking is the act of recruiting, transporting, harboring, sending, transferring, or receiving a person by threat of violence, use of violence, kidnapping, confinement, forgery, fraud, abuse of power or position of vulnerability, debt bondage or giving payment or benefits, so as to obtain consent from the person holding control over that other person, whether carried out within the Country or between Countries, for the purpose of exploitation or resulting in the person being exploited.
18. Complaint handling is a series of actions carried out by integrated service providers to follow up on reports of acts of violence against Women and Children, submitted by victims, families or the community.
19. Social rehabilitation is a service aimed at restoring and developing the abilities of a person experiencing social dysfunction so that they can carry out their social functions normally.
20. Law enforcement is action by officials authorized by the state to implement statutory regulations.
21. Legal aid is legal services provided by legal assistants and advocates to carry out the process of assisting witnesses and/or victims of violence against Women and Children that is gender-sensitive.
22. Repatriation is an effort to return Women and Child victims of violence from abroad to the debarkation point/entry point, or from the receiving area to the area of origin.
23. Social reintegration is an effort to reunite victims with family, substitute family, or community that can provide protection and fulfillment of needs for victims.
24. Safe house is a temporary shelter that aims to provide protection and a sense of security to victims of violence according to established standards.
25. Shelter house is a forum for guidance and welfare services for street Children that carries out mentoring/social guidance activities, maintenance, care, education, and health, skills guidance in order to ensure that Children do not carry out activities on the streets so that they can grow and develop normally.
26. Spiritual guidance is an effort to restore the psyche through strengthening the religion adhered to by the victim.
27. Gender responsive is consistent and systematic attention to the differences between Women and men in society accompanied by efforts to eliminate structural and cultural barriers in achieving gender equality.
28. Gender Sensitivity or Gender Awareness is the ability to recognize power relationship gaps between Women and men in the family and in the community, the impact of gender-based division of labor on Women and men, and that the experiences, problems, needs, interests and aspirations of Women and men are different.
29. Public service providers are every state organizing institution, corporation, independent institution formed based on Law for public service activities, and other legal entities formed solely for public service activities.
30. Community Organization is an organization established and formed by the community voluntarily based on common aspirations, will, needs, interests, activities and goals to participate in development for the achievement of the goals of the Unitary State of the Republic of Indonesia based on Pancasila.
31. Parents are biological father and/or mother, or step father and/or mother, or adoptive father and/or mother.
32. Family is the smallest unit in society consisting of husband and wife or husband, wife and their Child, or father and his Child, or mother and her Child, or blood relatives in direct line upward or downward up to the third degree.
33. Integrated Service Center for Women's Empowerment and Children, hereinafter abbreviated as P2TP2A is an integrated service center in efforts to empower Women in various fields of development, as well as protection of Women and Children from various types of discrimination and acts of violence, including human trafficking, which is formed by the government or community-based.

## Part Two

Definitions, Understanding and Terminology

#### Article 2

1. The implementation of Women's Protection is based on the principles of:
   - a. the best interests of Women;
   - b. gender justice and gender equality;
   - c. local wisdom; and
   - d. legal certainty.
2. The implementation of Child Protection is based on the principles of:
   - a. non-discrimination;
   - b. the best interests of the Child;
   - c. the right to life, survival, and development;
   - d. respect for the views of the Child; and
   - e. legal certainty.

#### Article 3

The regulation of Women's and Child Protection aims to:

   - a. serve as a guideline for the Regency Government in planning, policies and strategies for Women's and Child Protection;
   - b. protect Women and Children from actions, decisions and policies that violate the rights of Women and Children;
   - c. prevent the occurrence of violations of Women's and Children's Rights;
   - d. increase empowerment of Women and Children;
   - e. increase the role and value of local wisdom in Women's and Child Protection; and
   - f. increase the role of Regional Apparatus and non-Government entities in the Regency in efforts for Women's and Child Protection.

#### Article 4

The scope of regulation of Women's and Child Protection includes:

   - a. prevention;
   - b. implementation of Women's and Child Rights Protection;
   - c. handling;
   - d. empowerment; and
   - e. guidance and supervision.

# CHAPTER II

## WOMEN'S RIGHTS AND CHILDREN'S RIGHTS

## Part One

Women's Rights Article 5 Every Woman has the right to:

   - a. obtain protection from discriminatory actions;
   - b. obtain protection from economic exploitation and sexual exploitation;
   - c. obtain protection from physical, psychological, sexual violence, and neglect;
   - d. obtain decent work in accordance with her capabilities;
   - e. obtain work leave while receiving wages/salary in accordance with statutory regulations;
   - f. obtain education and teaching in all types, levels and pathways of education;
   - g. obtain special protection in the implementation of her work or profession against matters that may threaten safety and/or health in relation to Women's reproductive function; and
   - h. ownership and management of joint assets in marriage in accordance with the provisions of Statutory Regulations.

## Part Two

Rights of the Child

#### Article 6

1. Every Child has the right:
   - a. to live, grow, develop, and participate reasonably in accordance with human dignity and worth, and to receive protection from violence and discrimination;
   - b. to obtain a name as personal identity and citizenship status;
   - c. to worship according to their religion, to think, and to express themselves in accordance with their level of intelligence and age under the guidance of Parents or Guardians;
   - d. to obtain education and teaching in the context of personal development and their level of intelligence in accordance with their interests and talents and to receive protection in educational units from sexual crimes and Violence committed by educators, educational personnel, fellow students, and/or other parties;
   - e. to obtain protection from being targets of persecution, torture, or imposition of inhumane punishment;
   - f. to obtain freedom in accordance with law and arrest, detention, or criminal imprisonment of a Child shall only be carried out if in accordance with applicable law and can only be carried out as a last resort;
   - g. to be cared for by their own Parents, unless there are reasons and/or valid legal rules showing that the separation is in the best interests of the Child and is a last consideration;
   - h. to obtain protection from abuse in political activities, involvement in armed conflict, involvement in social unrest, involvement in events containing elements of Violence, involvement in warfare and, sexual crimes;
      - i. to participate, express opinions and think in accordance with their conscience and religion;
   - j. to receive oral or written information in accordance with the Child's age stage and development;
   - k. to associate and assemble; and
   - l. to rest, play, recreate, create, and engage in cultural arts, to obtain play facilities that meet health and safety requirements and are adapted to the Child's age, the Child's level of ability, and their environment so as not to hinder and disturb.
2. Every child who is deprived of liberty has the right to:
   - a. receive humane treatment;
   - b. placement separated from adults;
   - c. obtain legal assistance or other assistance effectively at every stage of applicable legal remedies; and
   - d. defend themselves and obtain justice before an objective and impartial Children's Court in a closed session to the public.
3. Every child who becomes a victim or perpetrator of sexual violence or who is in conflict with the law has the right to confidentiality.
4. Every Child with Disabilities in addition to obtaining the rights as referred to in paragraph (1) is also entitled to obtain:
   - a. special education/special needs education;
   - b. rehabilitation;
   - c. social assistance; and
   - d. maintenance of social welfare standards.
5. Every Child who has excellence has the right to receive special education.

# CHAPTER III

## OBLIGATIONS AND RESPONSIBILITIES

## Part One

Regency Government

#### Article 7

1. The Regency Government in efforts to protect Women is obligated and responsible to:
   - a. guarantee the Rights of Women without discrimination in accordance with the provisions of Laws and Regulations;
   - b. establish and implement policy programs and activities for the protection of Women;
   - c. conduct cooperation with the community and/or Community Organizations in the implementation of Women's Protection in accordance with the provisions of Laws and Regulations;
   - d. conduct rehabilitation and empowerment of Women who are victims of acts of violence;
   - e. provide special protection to Women victims of acts of violence and human trafficking; and
   - f. take preventive actions in order to prevent violations of the rights of Women.
2. The responsibility of the Regency Government in efforts to protect the Rights of Women as referred to in paragraph (1) shall be implemented by DPPPA.

#### Article 8

1. The Regency Government in efforts to protect Children is obligated and responsible to:
   - a. respect the fulfillment of the rights of Children without distinguishing ethnicity, religion, race, class, gender, ethnicity, culture and language, legal status, birth order, and physical and/or mental condition;
   - b. provide support for facilities, infrastructure, and availability of human resources in the implementation of Child Protection;
   - c. guarantee the protection, care, and welfare of Children by taking into account the rights and obligations of Parents, Guardians, or other persons who are legally responsible for Children;
   - d. guarantee Children to convey their right to express opinions in accordance with the age and level of intelligence of the Child;
   - e. establish and implement policies and programs/activities for the protection of the rights of Children;
   - f. conduct cooperation with the community and/or Community Organizations in the implementation of Child protection in accordance with the provisions of Laws and Regulations;
   - g. conduct rehabilitation of Children who are victims of acts of violence;
   - h. provide special protection to Children:
1. in emergency situations victims of disasters and conflicts;
2. who are in conflict with the law;
3. victims of acts of violence, neglect and human trafficking;
4. from minority and isolated groups;
5. who are exploited economically and/or sexually/victims of pornography;
6. who are victims of narcotics, alcohol, psychotropic abuse; and
7. identified with HIV/AIDS;
   - i. organize inclusive education for Children with special needs;
   - j. take preventive actions in order to prevent violations against Children; and
   - k. implement and support National policies in the implementation of Child Protection in the Regency conducted through efforts to realize Child-Friendly Regency, Child-Friendly Sub-district and Child-Friendly Village;
2. The responsibility of the Regency Government in efforts to protect Children as referred to in paragraph (1) shall be implemented by DPPPA.

#### Article 9

1. The Regency Government in order to carry out its responsibilities, establishes programs and activities for the protection of Women and Children with the Regional Action Plan as the basis for Regional Apparatus in implementing the protection of Women and Children.
2. Further provisions regarding the Regional Action Plan as referred to in paragraph (1) shall be regulated by Regent Regulation.

## Part Two

Community

#### Article 10

1. The community is obligated and responsible in implementing the protection of Women and Children including:
   - a. participation in prevention and providing support for the development of prevention programs against violations of the rights of Women and Children;
   - b. providing information and/or reporting violations of the rights of Women and Children to the competent authorities; and
   - c. supervising the law enforcement process regarding cases of violations of the rights of Women and Children.
2. The responsibility of the community as referred to in paragraph (1) is in accordance with the provisions of laws and regulations and the values of local wisdom of the local community.

Third Part Parents and/or Family Paragraf 1 Responsibility Towards the Protection of Women's Rights Article 11 The responsibility of the family in efforts to protect women includes: a. protecting women from conflict and violence in the household that degrades the dignity of women;

   - b. providing equal opportunities for women to develop themselves in the fields of:
1. education;
2. social;
3. economy; and
4. politics and culture.
   - c. preventing violations of women's rights in the household.

Paragraf 2 Responsibility Towards the Protection of Children's Rights Article 12 (1) Parents in efforts to protect Children are responsible for: a. caring for, maintaining, educating, and protecting Children; b. developing Children according to their abilities, talents, and interests;

   - c. providing formal and non-formal education;
   - d. guiding Children to behave in accordance with religion, ethics and morals as well as customs; and
   - e. preventing marriage at a child's age.
2. In the event that parents do not exist, or their whereabouts are unknown, or due to a certain reason, cannot carry out their obligations and responsibilities, then the obligations and responsibilities as referred to in paragraph (1) may be transferred to the family.

# CHAPTER IV

## PREVENTION

## Part One

Regency Government

#### Article 13

1. Efforts undertaken by the Regency Government in order to prevent violations of Women's rights include:
   - a. conducting socialization regarding prevention of violence against Women by optimizing the role of customary, cultural, religious institutions and community organizations;
   - b. provision of guidelines on handling acts of violence against Women;
   - c. involving community participation in recovery and reintegration;
   - d. development of an integrated database and information system on acts of violence against Women;
   - e. development of family empowerment programs;
   - f. strengthening the function of community organizations engaged in Women's empowerment; and
   - g. provision of public facilities that are comfortable and safe for Women.
2. Efforts undertaken by the Regency Government in order to prevent violations of Children's rights include:
   - a. prevention of acts of violence against Children by optimizing the role of customary, cultural, religious institutions and community organizations;
   - b. development of child-friendly schools;
   - c. encouraging increased participation of children through the establishment of Children's Forums;
   - d. provision of guidelines on handling acts of violence against Children;
   - e. involving community participation in recovery and reintegration services for child victims of violence;
   - f. development of an integrated database and information system on acts of violence against Children;
   - g. strengthening the function of community organizations engaged in Child protection; and
   - h. encouraging the establishment of a Child-Friendly Regency.

## Part Two

Community

#### Article 14

1. Efforts made by the community in order to prevent violations of Women's rights include:
   - a. monitoring various conditions occurring in their environment that may lead to violations of Women's rights;
   - b. strengthening the role of community organizations, religious organizations and the family environment; and
   - c. prevention of unregistered marriages/siri marriages.
2. Efforts made in order to prevent violations of Children's rights include:
   - a. monitoring various conditions occurring in their environment that lead to violations of Children's rights;
   - b. strengthening the role of community organizations, religious organizations and the family environment; and
   - c. prevention of marriage at a child's age.

## Part Three

Parents and/or Family Article 15 (1) Efforts made by the family in order to prevent the occurrence of violations against the rights of Women include:

   - a. protection of women from various violations of rights within the household; and
   - b. encouraging the formation of a harmonious, educative and democratic family;
2. Efforts made by parents in order to prevent the occurrence of violations against the rights of Children include:
   - a. providing good example to children in accordance with religious and customary teachings;
   - b. supervision of children in their activities inside or outside the home;
   - c. providing opportunities for children to engage in dialogue and express opinions democratically; and
   - d. protection of children from various acts of violation of Children's rights within the household.

# CHAPTER V

## IMPLEMENTATION OF PROTECTION FOR WOMEN AND CHILDREN

## Part One

Implementation of Protection for Women

### Paragraph 1

General Article 16 The Regency Government organizes Protection of Women by involving:

   - a. family;
   - b. community leaders;
   - c. religious leaders;
   - d. community;
   - e. community organizations in the field of Women's protection.

### Paragraph 2 Marriage Article 17 During the marriage bond or after divorce, a Woman has the same rights as the man who is her husband/former husband over all matters related to the consequences of marriage and/or divorce.

### Paragraph 3 Health Article 18 (1) The Regency Government provides adequate health and maternity facilities for pregnant and nursing mothers.

2. Public service providers are obligated to provide special facilities for nursing mothers.

### Paragraph 4 Employment Article 19 (1) Employers who employ Women are obligated to maintain decency, safety and security while in the workplace.

2. Employers are obligated to place Women workers in places that are safe for Women's reproductive health.
3. Employers are obligated to fulfill the rights of Women workers to receive wages in accordance with the provisions of laws and regulations.
4. Employers provide special facilities for nursing mothers in their companies.

#### Article 20 (1) Women workers/laborers aged at most 18 (eighteen) years are prohibited from being employed between 23.00 WIB until 07.00 WIB.

2. Employers are prohibited from employing pregnant Women workers/laborers who according to a doctor's statement are dangerous to the health and safety of their pregnancy or themselves during working hours from 23.00 WIB until 07.00 WIB.
3. Employers who employ Women workers/laborers between 23.00 WIB until 07.00 WIB, are obligated to:
   - a. provide nutritious food and beverages; and
   - b. maintain decency and security while in the workplace.
4. Employers are obligated to provide shuttle transportation for Women workers/laborers who depart and return from work between 23.00 WIB until 05.00 WIB.

### Paragraph 5 Education

#### Article 21

Women are guaranteed the right to obtain education at every level of formal and non-formal education.

### Paragraph 6 Social

#### Article 22

1. The Regency Government shall organize empowerment of Women experiencing socio-economic vulnerability through productive economic business activities.
2. Community Organizations may conduct empowerment for Women experiencing socio-economic vulnerability as referred to in paragraph (1) in accordance with the provisions of laws and regulations.

Second Part Implementation of Protection of Children's Rights

### Paragraph 1

Health Article 23 (1) The District Government's efforts to provide protection for Children in the health sector include: a. free health services for Children from poor families;

   - b. the provision of optimal health care for Children so as to obtain a good degree of health since in the womb;
   - c. ensuring that infants receive exclusive breastfeeding from their mothers properly and reasonably;
   - d. ensuring Children receive adequate nutrition;
   - e. ensuring newborn Children undergo screening; and
   - f. ensuring the provision of growth and development monitoring for toddlers and pre-school age children.
2. The District Government, families, and parents protect children from efforts to transplant their body organs to other parties.

### Paragraph 2 Education Article 24 Children in formal and non-formal educational institutions must be protected from acts of violence.

#### Article 25 Every Child aged 7 (seven) to 18 (eighteen) years who has not completed education, and who is more than 3 (three) years beyond the age limit for formal education, may pursue education through non-formal education units:

   - a. Package A equivalency program equivalent to Elementary School and Madrasah Ibtidaiyah;
   - b. Package B equivalency program equivalent to Junior High School and Madrasah Tsanawiyah; and
   - c. Package C equivalency program equivalent to Senior High School, Madrasah Aliyah and Vocational High School.

#### Article 26 (1) Children with special needs and/or who have potential intelligence and/or special talents are given equal opportunities and facilities to obtain regular education or inclusive education.

2. Inclusive education as referred to in paragraph (1) is organized by the District Government and the community.

#### Article 27 Every organizer of formal education is prohibited from expelling Children without guarantees for the continuity of the Child's education.

#### Article 28 (1) The District Government provides scholarships and/or educational assistance for:

   - a. children from poor families;
   - b. neglected children; and
   - c. children residing in remote areas.
2. The implementation of scholarship programs and/or educational assistance as referred to in paragraph (1) is supported by community participation and community organizations.
3. Further provisions regarding the provision of scholarships and or educational assistance as referred to in paragraph (1) are regulated by Regent Regulation.

#### Article 29 Children in conflict with the law, Child victims of violence and sexual abuse and Child victims of HIV/AIDS transmission are protected in their rights to obtain education.

#### Article 30

The Regency Government and the community shall organize Early Childhood Education (PAUD).

### Paragraph 3

Employment

#### Article 31

Employers are prohibited from employing Children.

#### Article 32

1. The provisions as referred to in Article 31 may be exempted for Children aged between 13 (thirteen) years to 15 (fifteen) years, to perform light work as long as it does not interfere with physical, mental, and social development and health.
2. Employers who employ Children in light work as referred to in paragraph (1) must fulfill the following requirements:
   - a. written permission from parents or guardians;
   - b. work agreement between the employer and parents or guardians;
   - c. maximum working time of 3 (three) hours;
   - d. performed during the day and does not interfere with school time;
   - e. occupational safety and health;
   - f. clear employment relationship; and
   - g. receive wages in accordance with the provisions of laws and regulations.
3. The provisions as referred to in paragraph (2) letter a, letter b, letter f, and letter g are exempted for Children who work in family businesses.

#### Article 33

In the event that a company employs Children as referred to in Article 32, then the workplace for Children must be separated from the workplace of adult workers.

#### Article 34

1. Children may perform work in a workplace that is part of an educational or training curriculum validated by the authorized official.
2. Children as referred to in paragraph (1) must be at least 14 (fourteen) years old.

#### Article 35

1. Every person is prohibited from employing and involving Children in the worst forms of work.
2. The worst forms of work as referred to in paragraph (1) include:
   - a. all work in the form of slavery or similar practices;
   - b. all work that exploits, provides, or offers Children for:
1. prostitution;
2. pornography production;
3. pornographic performances;
4. gambling;
5. production and trade of alcoholic beverages;
6. narcotics;
7. psychotropic substances; and
8. other addictive substances; and/or
   - c. all work that endangers the health, safety, or morals of Children.

### Paragraph 4

Social

#### Article 36

1. The Regency Government is obligated to provide care for neglected Children.
2. The care as referred to in paragraph (1) may be carried out by Community Organizations in the social field.
3. In the event that a Child is neglected because for some reason the parents neglect their obligations, then the Family or Regency Government or Community Organizations as referred to in paragraph (2) may submit an application to the court to determine the Child as a neglected Child.
4. The court determination as referred to in paragraph (3) shall simultaneously determine the shelter and care for the neglected Child concerned.

#### Article 37

1. The Regency Government shall organize skills training for street Children and neglected Children.
2. Skills training as referred to in paragraph (1) shall be organized by Regional Apparatus whose duties and functions are in the social and employment fields and may cooperate with Community Organizations.

#### Article 38

1. The Regency Government shall provide shelters to guide street Children and social homes for neglected Children.
2. Shelters as referred to in paragraph (1) may be organized in cooperation with Community Organizations.
3. Further provisions regarding shelters as referred to in paragraph (1) shall be regulated by Regent Regulation.

#### Article 39

The implementation of protection for Women and Children shall be carried out by Regional Apparatus whose duties and functions are related to the protection of Women and Children.

# CHAPTER VI

## HANDLING

## Part One

Handling of Women and Children Victims of Violence Article 40 The implementation of handling of Women and Children victims of violence includes: a. handling of complaints from victims of violence;

   - b. health services;
   - c. social rehabilitation;
   - d. law enforcement and legal assistance; and
   - e. repatriation and social reintegration.

#### Article 41 (1) The implementation of handling of Women and Children victims of violence as referred to in Article 40 is carried out through integrated services.

2. Integrated services for Women and Children victims of violence as referred to in paragraph (1) use the principles of:
   - a. gender responsive;
   - b. non-discrimination;
   - c. equal and respectful relations;
   - d. maintaining confidentiality;
   - e. providing a sense of security and comfort;
   - f. respecting individual differences;
   - g. non-judgmental;
   - h. respecting the choices and decisions of victims;
      - i. sensitive to the background and conditions of victims and the use of appropriate language understood by victims;
   - j. fast and simple;
   - k. empathy;
   - l. fulfillment of the rights of Women and Children; and
   - m. free of charge.

#### Article 42 Complaint handling services for victims of violence against Women and Children are carried out by:

   - a. hospitals;
   - b. community health centers;
   - c. P2TP2A;
   - d. Women and Children Service Units (UPPA);
   - e. Trauma Center Shelter Houses (RPTC);
   - f. Children's Social Shelter Houses (RPSA);
   - g. Women Crisis Centre (WCC)
   - h. Advisory Board for Marriage Guidance and Preservation (BP4);
      - i. Police;
   - j. Prosecutor's Office;
   - k. Courts;
   - l. Legal Aid Institutions (LBH); and
   - m. Child Protection Institutions (LPA).

#### Article 43 (1) Complaints of acts of violence against Women and Children as referred to in Article 42 may be made by reporters consisting of:

   - a. Victims;
   - b. Family;
   - c. Community; and/or
   - d. community organizations in the field of protection of Women and Children.
2. Reporters as referred to in paragraph (1) may file complaints: a. directly;
   - b. by telephone; and
   - c. by letter.
3. In addition to handling complaints submitted by reporters, officers at service institutions as referred to in Article 42 are authorized to handle Women and Children victims of violence obtained through other information.
4. Further provisions regarding the procedures for complaint handling services as referred to in paragraph (1) and paragraph (2) are regulated by Regent Regulation

## Part Two

Health Services for Women and Children Victims of Violence

#### Article 44

1. Health services for Women and Children victims of violence include aspects of:
   - a. Promotive;
   - b. Preventive;
   - c. Curative;
   - d. Rehabilitative.
2. Health services for Women and Children victims of violence are conducted in a manner that is:
   - a. Comprehensive;
   - b. accessible 24 (twenty-four) hours;
   - c. quality; and
   - d. in accordance with health service standards.
3. Special health services for Children victims of violence must prioritize the best interests of the Child.
4. Women and Children victims of violence are entitled to health services, including:
   - a. medical services;
   - b. medicolegal services; and
   - c. psychosocial counseling.
5. Further provisions regarding the procedures for implementing health services as referred to in paragraph (4) shall be regulated by a Regent Regulation.

## Part Three

Social Rehabilitation Services for Women and Children Victims of Violence

#### Article 45

1. Social rehabilitation services for Women and Children victims of violence are aimed at restoring and developing the capabilities of victims who experience social dysfunction so that they can carry out their social functions in society properly.
2. Social rehabilitation services as referred to in paragraph (1) include:
   - a. counseling services;
   - b. provision of safe houses;
   - c. spiritual guidance.
3. The implementation of services as referred to in paragraph (2) is carried out by:
   - a. Regional Apparatus having duties and functions in the social field;
   - b. DPPPA;
   - c. P2TP2A;
   - d. Women Crisis Centre (WeC);
   - e. social community organizations; and/or
   - f. religious institutions.

#### Article 46

1. Counseling services as referred to in Article 45 paragraph (2) letter a aim to:
   - a. assist victims in recognizing their problems and finding effective ways to overcome them independently;
   - b. empower victims to determine their future;
   - c. provide moral support for victims in facing the process they are undergoing; and
   - d. make victims feel accepted in the community environment.
2. Counseling services are conducted by gender-responsive social rehabilitation officers, consisting of:
   - a. counselors;
   - b. psychologists; and
   - c. psychiatrists.
3. Counseling services are conducted in a place that guarantees a sense of security, comfort and confidentiality of information from victims.
4. The stages of counseling services include:
   - a. initial counseling;
   - b. follow-up counseling;
   - c. case conference; and
   - d. home visit.

#### Article 47

1. The Regency Government may provide safe houses for Women and Children victims of violence.
2. Safe houses as referred to in paragraph (1) aim to protect Women and Children victims of violence from intimidation by perpetrators or other parties.
3. The location of safe houses as referred to in paragraph (1) must be kept confidential.
4. The criteria for Women and Children victims of violence who require safe houses include:
   - a. their life is threatened;
   - b. receiving rejection from family or community;
   - c. requiring intensive services but their residence is relatively far; and/or
   - d. abandoned if not placed in a safe house.
5. The implementation of safe house services is conducted by social rehabilitation officers who have gender sensitivity or gender awareness, consisting of:
   - a. social workers/volunteers;
   - b. security officers; and
   - c. community leaders.
6. Further provisions regarding the implementation of safe house services are regulated by Regent Regulation.

#### Article 48

1. Every Woman and Child victim of violence is provided with spiritual guidance aimed at psychological recovery.
2. Spiritual guidance as referred to in paragraph (1) is implemented based on the principles of:
   - a. not imposing any religion or belief; and
   - b. leaving the final decision in the hands of the victim (self determination).
3. Spiritual guidance as referred to in paragraph (2) is conducted by spiritual guidance officers.
4. Spiritual guidance officers as referred to in paragraph (3) consist of:
   - a. consultants of the Marriage Advisory, Development and Preservation Agency (BP4);
   - b. Officers of the Office of Religious Affairs (KUA);
   - c. Ustad or Ulama;
   - d. Pastor;
   - e. Minister; and
   - f. other religious leaders who have gender sensitivity or gender awareness.

## Part Four

Law Enforcement and Legal Aid Services I

#### Article 49

1. Law enforcement and legal aid services constitute actions by law enforcement officials provided to Women and Children victims of violence and/or witnesses.
2. Law enforcement and legal aid services as referred to in paragraph (1) shall be conducted by prioritizing the best interests of the victims.

#### Article 50

1. Law enforcement and legal aid services as referred to in Article 49 are intended to provide assistance and protection to Women and Children victims of violence and witnesses with a gender-responsive approach so that victims obtain justice and legal certainty of rights.
2. Types of legal aid services as referred to in paragraph (1) include:
   - a. legal aid in the reporting process and preparation of BAP;
   - b. legal aid in the prosecution process at the prosecutor's office;
   - c. legal aid in the examination process at the court;
   - d. legal aid in the court hearing decision process until a final legal decision is reached; and
   - e. legal aid in the mediation process.
3. The Regency Government shall provide legal aid to Children and Women victims of violence free of charge.
4. In providing legal aid as referred to in paragraph (3), the Regency Government may cooperate with:
   - a. Legal Aid Institutions (LBH);
   - b. Women Crisis Center (WCC);
   - c. Advocates; and/or
   - d. other legal assistants who are gender-sensitive.

## Part Five

Repatriation and Social Reintegration Services Article 51 (1) Repatriation of victims of violence is intended to return Women and Children victims of violence to their area of origin.

2. Repatriation of Women and Children victims of violence as referred to in paragraph (1) shall be accompanied by social reintegration efforts.
3. The Regency Government shall implement repatriation and social reintegration of Women and Children victims of violence in accordance with the provisions of Laws and Regulations.

# CHAPTER VII

## WOMEN'S EMPOWERMENT

#### Article 52 (1) The Regency Government guarantees the implementation of empowerment for Women.

2. The implementation of Women's empowerment as referred to in paragraph (1) is supported by community participation and Community Organizations.

#### Article 53 (1) The Regency Government provides skills training for capacity building of Women.

2. In providing training as referred to in paragraph (1) the Regency Government may cooperate with training institutions organized by community organizations.
3. The Regency Government expands access and opportunities for Women in obtaining skills and employment.

#### Article 54 (1) The Regency Government facilitates:

   - a. provision of business capital assistance for Women with weak economy;
   - b. marketing of products resulting from Women's businesses;
   - c. implementation of training for community capacity development through economic empowerment of pre-prosperous families;
   - d. expansion of access, information and providing knowledge to Women regarding marketing efforts for products produced; and
   - e. providing entrepreneurship training to Women comprehensively.
2. The Regency Government in facilitating activities as referred to in paragraph (1) cooperates with the community and/or community organizations.

# CHAPTER VIII

## CHILD PARTICIPATION

#### Article 55 (1) Every Child has the right to participate in expressing opinions, ideas, and concepts related to life based on awareness and understanding, in accordance with age and level of thinking maturity.

2. Child Participation as referred to in paragraph (1) may be conducted through Child Forums starting from the village level and the sub-district level within the regency.
3. Child Forums as referred to in paragraph (2) represent Children, both geographical domicile representation, socio-cultural groups and Children's educational backgrounds.
4. The establishment of Child Forums as referred to in paragraph (2) shall be facilitated by the Regency Government.
5. The establishment of Child Forums as referred to in paragraph (4) shall be determined by Regent's Decree.

#### Article 56 (1) In every formulation of policies related to Children, the Regency Government shall consider and accommodate the Opinions of Children conveyed through Child Forums.

2. The Regency Government shall include representatives of Child Forums in the Regency Development Planning Deliberation.

# CHAPTER IX

## CHILD-FRIENDLY REGENCY

#### Article 57 (1) The Regency Government conducts guidance to the Sub-district Government in the development of a Child-Friendly Regency.

2. The guidance as referred to in paragraph (1) includes: a. coordination;
   - b. facilitation;
   - c. mentoring;
   - d. supervision;
   - e. consultation; and
   - f. education and training.
3. Provisions regarding Child-Friendly Regency shall be further regulated by Regent Regulation.

# CHAPTER X

## DEVELOPMENT AND SUPERVISION

#### Article 58 (1) The Regent conducts development and supervision of the implementation of Protection of Women and Children.

2. Development as referred to in paragraph (1) may be conducted in the form of:
   - a. training for government apparatus and law enforcers regarding prevention and handling of violence;
   - b. training for religious leaders, community leaders, traditional leaders, educators, journalists and media managers in order to prevent violence against Women and Children;
   - c. training for health workers who provide health services to Women and Children victims of violence at community health centers, integrated service units, and general hospitals;
   - d. training for social rehabilitation officers and spiritual guidance officers in order to protect Women and Children victims of violence; and
   - f. improvement of professionalism of officers, managers of violence case data against Women and Children at community health centers, integrated service units and general hospitals.
3. The Regent in conducting development and supervision as referred to in paragraph (1) may delegate to the Head of Agency.

#### Article 59 (1) The Head of DPPPA is obligated to submit a report on the implementation of the Women and Children protection program to the Regent.

2. The report as referred to in paragraph (1) shall be submitted periodically 1 (one) time every 3 (three) months.

#### Article 60 (1) The Regent conducts supervision and evaluation of the implementation of Protection of Women and Children periodically 1 (one) time every 3 (three) months.

2. The evaluation results as referred to in paragraph (1) constitute evaluation material for the Women and Children protection program policy in the Regency.

# CHAPTER XI

## FINANCING.

#### Article 61 Financing for the implementation of protection of Women and Children is sourced from:

   - a. Regional Revenue and Expenditure Budget;
   - b. State Revenue and Expenditure Budget; and
   - c. other legitimate sources in accordance with the provisions of Laws and Regulations.

# CHAPTER XII

## CLOSING PROVISIONS

#### Article 62 The implementing regulations of this Regional Regulation shall be stipulated no later than 1 (one) year from the promulgation of this Regional Regulation.

#### Article 63 This Regional Regulation shall come into force on the date of promulgation. So that everyone may know of it, the promulgation of this Regional Regulation is ordered by placing it in the Regional Gazette of Musi Banyuasin Regency.
