# REGENT OF TANGGAMUS,

Considering : a. that in order to protect the constitutional rights of women and children in accordance with the 1945 Constitution of the Republic of Indonesia, the Regional Government is obligated to be responsible in organizing the protection of women and children against acts of violence;

   - b. that to fulfill the indicators of the Implementation of Child-Friendly Regency, concerning protection services, prevention of violence against women and children, regulations are needed to govern the various roles of regional apparatus institutions and other institutions;
   - c. that based on the considerations as referred to in letter a, and letter b, it is necessary to establish a Regional Regulation on the Organization of Protection of Women and Children;

# CHAPTER I

## GENERAL PROVISIONS

#### Article 1

In this Regional Regulation, what is meant by:

1. Region is a legal community unit that has territorial boundaries authorized to regulate and manage government affairs and the interests of the local community on its own initiative based on community aspirations within the system of the Unitary State of the Republic of Indonesia.
2. Regional Government is the administration of government affairs by the regional government and DPRD according to the principles of autonomy and assistance tasks with the principle of the widest possible autonomy within the system and principles of the Unitary State of the Republic of Indonesia as referred to in the 1945 Constitution of the Republic of Indonesia;
3. Regional Government is the Regent and Regional Apparatus as elements of the regional government administration of Tanggamus Regency.
4. Regent is the Regent of Tanggamus.
5. Regional Representative Council hereinafter abbreviated as DPRD is the Regional People's Representative Council of Tanggamus Regency.
6. Regional Apparatus abbreviated as PD is an auxiliary element of the Regent and Regional People's Representative Council of Tanggamus Regency in the administration of Government Affairs that are the authority of Tanggamus Regency.
7. Regional Planning, Development, Research and Innovation Agency hereinafter abbreviated as Bapperida is the Regional Planning, Development, Research and Innovation Agency in Tanggamus Regency.
8. Office of Women's Empowerment, Child Protection, Population Control and Family Planning hereinafter abbreviated as DPPPA DALDUK and KB is the Office of Women's Empowerment, Child Protection, Population Control and Family Planning of Tanggamus Regency.
9. Sub-district is the working area of the sub-district head as a regional apparatus of Tanggamus regency.
10. Village or called Pekon, hereinafter referred to as village/pekon, is a legal community unit that has territorial boundaries authorized to regulate and manage the interests of the local community, based on origins and customs that are recognized and respected in the government system of the Unitary State of the Republic of Indonesia.
11. Child is a person who is not yet 18 (eighteen) years old, including a child still in the womb.
12. Child's rights are part of human rights that must be guaranteed, protected, and fulfilled by parents, family, community, government, and the State.
13. Family is the smallest unit in society consisting of husband and wife, husband-wife and their children, or father and children, or mother and children, or blood relatives in direct line upward or downward up to the third degree.
14. Parents are biological father and/or mother, or step father and/or mother, or adoptive father and/or mother.
15. Guardian is a parent or body that in fact exercises custody power as a parent over a child.
16. Woman is a human being who can menstruate, become pregnant, give birth and breastfeed.
17. Child Marriage is a marriage conducted through civil, religious or customary law with or without registration or official consent where one or both partners are children under the age of 18 years.
18. Children in Need of Special Protection hereinafter abbreviated as AMPK are Children in need of special protection.
19. Worst Forms of Child Labor hereinafter referred to as BPTA is the worst forms of child labor.
20. Neglected child is a child whose needs are not properly fulfilled, whether physical, mental, spiritual or social.
21. Street child is a child whose life is irregular, spending most of their time outside the home to earn a living on the streets or in public places.
22. Child with disability is a child who experiences physical and/or mental obstacles that interfere with their growth and development normally.
23. Gifted child is a child who has extraordinary intelligence or has special potential and/or talents not limited to intellectual abilities, but also in other fields.
24. Child in conflict with the law is a child who conflicts with the law, a child who becomes a victim of a criminal act, and a child who becomes a witness to a criminal act.
25. Adopted Child is a Child whose rights are transferred from the environment of the authority of the Family of Parents, legal Guardian, or other person responsible for the care, education and raising of that Child into the environment of the Family of adoptive Parents based on a court decision or determination.
26. Foster Child is a Child who is cared for by a person or institution to be provided with guidance, maintenance, care, education, and health because the Parents or one of the Parents is unable to guarantee the normal growth and development of the Child.
27. Economically exploited child is an action with or without the consent of the child who becomes a victim which includes but is not limited to prostitution, forced labor or service, slavery or practices similar to slavery, oppression, extortion, physical exploitation, sexual, reproductive organs, or unlawfully transferring or transplanting organs and/or body tissues or exploiting the labor or ability of a child by another party to obtain material benefits.
28. Sexually exploited child is any form of exploitation of sexual organs or other body organs of a child to obtain benefits, including but not limited to all activities of prostitution and sexual abuse.
29. Child labor is children both male and female who are involved in economic activities that interfere with or hinder the growth and development process and are dangerous to the physical and mental health of children.
30. Custody is the power of Parents to care for, educate, maintain, foster, protect and develop Children in accordance with their religion and in accordance with their abilities, talents, and interests.
31. Child's Rights are part of human rights that must be guaranteed, protected, and fulfilled by Parents, Family, community, state, government and regional government.
32. Community is individuals, families, groups, and social organizations and/or community organizations.
33. Human trafficking is the act of recruiting, transporting, harboring, sending, transferring, or receiving a person by threat of violence, use of violence, kidnapping, confinement, forgery, fraud, abuse of power or vulnerable position, debt bondage or giving payment or benefits, so as to obtain consent from the person who holds control over that other person, whether carried out within the country or between countries, for the purpose of exploitation or resulting in the person being exploited.
34. Protection is all efforts aimed at providing a sense of security to victims carried out by family, advocates, social institutions, police, prosecutors, courts, or other parties either temporarily or based on court determination.
35. Protection of women is all efforts made to ensure the fulfillment of women's rights as a form of respect for human rights.
36. Implementation of Child Protection is a series of activities carried out by the Regional Government, community, and parents, aimed at preventing, reducing risks, and handling victims of acts of violence, exploitation, abuse and neglect of children.
37. Child Protection is all activities to guarantee and protect children and their rights so they can live, be healthy, intelligent, grow and develop and participate optimally in accordance with human dignity and receive protection from neglect, violence and discrimination.
38. Violence Against Women is any action based on gender differences that results in or may result in misery or suffering of women physically, sexually or psychologically, economic exploitation, social including threats of certain actions, coercion or arbitrary deprivation of freedom, whether occurring in the public sphere or in private life.
39. Violence Against Children is any act against a child that results in misery or suffering physically, mentally, sexually, and psychologically.
40. Violence Against Women and Children in the Household is any act against a woman and child that results in misery or suffering physically, sexually, psychologically, economically and/or household neglect including threats to commit acts of coercion or deprivation of freedom unlawfully within the household sphere.
41. Exploitation of Children is any act involving children in activities that can harm welfare and growth and development or endanger the safety of children with the aim of enabling others to obtain economic, sexual, social, or political benefits, including if there is restriction or elimination of opportunities for children to obtain their rights.
42. Child Abuse is any action against a child, including placing a child in a situation that can cause adverse impacts on the welfare, safety, dignity and development of the child.
43. Assistance is social work that has professional competence in its field.
44. Child Neglect is any act of neglecting the fulfillment of basic needs, care, and maintenance that interferes with or hinders child development, including leaving a child in a dangerous situation.
45. Prevention is the effort of the Regional Government and community in creating conditions that can prevent the occurrence of violence, abuse, exploitation and neglect of children.
46. Risk Reduction is early action toward children and their families who are in vulnerable situations or at risk of experiencing various forms of violence, abuse, exploitation, and neglect.
47. Handling is action that includes identification, rescue, rehabilitation and reintegration of children who become victims of acts of violence, abuse, exploitation, and or neglect.
48. Care Environment is care by parents and care outside parental care. Care outside parental care consists of care by foster parents or adoptive parents as well as care in institutions such as orphanages or social care institutions for children or other similar names.
49. PATBM is a movement of networks or citizen groups at the community level working in a coordinated manner to achieve child protection goals.
50. Regional Long-Term Development Plan hereinafter abbreviated as RPJPD is a regional development planning document for a period of 20 (twenty) years.
51. Regional Medium-Term Development Plan hereinafter referred to as RPJMD is a regional development planning document for a period of 5 (five) years.
52. Safe house is a temporary residence used to provide protection to victims in accordance with determined operational standards.
53. Child Participation Forum / Regional child forum is an organization that accommodates the aspirations of children and/or groups of children in Tanggamus Regency.
54. Repatriation is an Effort to return Women and Child Victims of Violence from abroad to the debarkation point/entry point or receiving area to the area of origin.
55. Social reintegration is an Effort to reunite witnesses and/or victims with family, substitute family, or community that can provide protection and fulfillment of needs for witnesses and/or victims.
56. Women's Protection Service Provider Institution is a government-owned institution or legal community institution that makes efforts to Protect Women from violence, both in policy implementation, activities, and administration in service delivery.
57. Child Social Welfare Institution is a social organization or social association that implements Child Social Welfare Administration formed by the community, whether legally incorporated or not legally incorporated.
58. Alternative care is child care carried out by Child Social Welfare Institutions.
59. Child participation is the involvement of children in the decision-making process and enjoying changes relating to their lives either directly or indirectly carried out with the consent and will of the child based on awareness and understanding.
60. Child Data Information System is the collection, management, and utilization of child data which is one of the requirements in the Implementation of Child Protection.
61. Task Force for Prevention and Handling of Criminal Acts of Human Trafficking hereinafter referred to as the Task Force is a coordinating institution tasked with coordinating prevention and handling efforts for criminal acts of human trafficking in Tanggamus Regency.
62. Child-Friendly Regency/City (KLA) is a Regency/City that has a Child Rights-based Development system through the Integration of Commitment and Resources of Government, Community and Business World that is planned comprehensively and sustainably in policies, programs and Activities to ensure the fulfillment of Child Rights, up to the sub-district and pekon level.
63. Parahita Ekapraya Award (APE) is an award given by the central government to Ministries or Institutions, Provincial and Regency/City Regional Governments for their commitment in efforts to realize gender equality, women's empowerment and child protection.
64. Gender mainstreaming working group hereinafter referred to as Pokja PUG is a consultation forum for implementers and movers of gender mainstreaming from various agencies/institutions in the region.
65. Technical Implementation Unit of the Office, hereinafter referred to as UPTD is an organization that carries out operational technical activities and/or certain supporting technical activities at the Office of Women's Empowerment, Child Protection, Population Control and Family Planning of Tanggamus Regency.
66. Technical Implementation Unit of the Office of Integrated Service Center for Women and Child Protection hereinafter referred to as UPTD PPA is the Technical Implementation Unit of the Office of Integrated Service Center for Women and Child Protection.
67. Private sector is Non-Governmental Institutions, Business World and Mass Media.
68. Child-Friendly Health Center is a health center that provides services to children completely and in an integrated manner while paying attention to children's needs.
69. Child-Friendly School (SRA) is a formal, non-formal, and informal education unit that seeks to guarantee, fulfill, and respect rights, provide protection, and support child participation.
70. Child-friendly Islamic boarding school is an effort to create Islamic boarding schools and their surrounding environment so that children can be comfortable, clean, feel at home, worship solemnly, enjoy learning, playing and interacting.
71. Child-friendly place of worship is a place of worship that provides a comfortable, safe environment, and is appropriate to children's needs.
72. Women-Friendly and Child-Caring Village (DRPPA) is a village that integrates gender perspective and child rights in the governance of village government administration, village development, as well as community guidance and empowerment, which is carried out in a planned, comprehensive, sustainable manner, in accordance with the vision of Indonesian development.
73. Integrated Service and Referral System hereinafter abbreviated as SLRT is a service system that helps identify the needs of poor and vulnerable communities based on profiles in the Beneficiary List and connects them with social protection and poverty alleviation programs carried out by the central, provincial and regency/city governments according to their needs.
74. Team for Violence against Women and Children or abbreviated as (Tim KtP/A) regarding Criminal Acts of Sexual Violence (TPKS) is the health sector that plays an important role in efforts to fulfill the right to health services for victims of Violence Against Women and Children (KtP/A), especially in recognizing or identifying cases of KtP/A, providing comprehensive services to victims of KtP/A, including making medical, social, and legal referrals.

# CHAPTER II

## PURPOSE, OBJECTIVES AND SCOPE

#### Article 2

The Purpose and Objectives of the protection of women and children from acts of violence are:

   - a. To prevent all forms of violence against women and children;
   - b. To provide protection and services to women and children victims of gender-based violence;
   - c. To provide a sense of security to women and children victims of violence;
   - d. To restore the physical, psychological and economic condition of women and children victims of violence;
   - e. To ensure the best interests of women and children victims of violence occurring in the domestic household and/or in public; and
   - f. To prosecute perpetrators of violence against women and children.

# CHAPTER III

## SCOPE

#### Article 3

The Scope in this Regional Regulation is as follows: a. Forms of Violence against Women and Children b. Rights of Victims of Acts of Violence Against Women and Children c. Obligations and Responsibilities

   - d. Protection of Women and Children
   - e. Cooperation, Partnership and Empowerment
   - f. Guidance and Supervision
   - g. Child-Friendly Regency (KLA) and Parahita Ekapraya Award (APE)

# CHAPTER IV

## FORMS OF VIOLENCE AGAINST WOMEN AND CHILDREN

#### Article 4

Forms of violence against women and children, among others: a. Physical violence;

   - b. Psychological violence;
   - c. Sexual violence;
   - d. Neglect;
   - e. Exploitation;
   - f. Other violence, namely:
1. Victims of pornography;
2. Victims of disaster and conflict;
3. Victims of narcotics;
4. Victims infected with HIV/AIDS;
5. Victims of terrorism;
6. Victims of Stigmatization;
7. Worst Forms of Child Labor (WFCL);
8. Street children and deviant behavior;
9. Persons with disabilities and/or;
10. Children in Need of Other Special Protection.

#### Article 5

Physical violence as referred to in Article 4 letter a constitutes an act that results in pain accompanied by injury, wounds or disability to the body, termination/loss of pregnancy, loss of consciousness and/or causes the death of women and/or children.

#### Article 6

Psychological violence as referred to in Article 4 letter b constitutes acts that result in fear, loss of self-confidence, loss of ability to act, feelings of helplessness and/or psychological suffering in women and/or children.

#### Article 7

Sexual violence as referred to in Article 4 letter c includes: a. Acts constituting sexual harassment;

   - b. Forced sexual intercourse;
   - c. Forced unnatural sexual intercourse; and/or d. Forced sexual intercourse with another person for commercial purposes and/or specific purposes.

#### Article 8

Neglect as referred to in Article 4 letter d includes: a. Acts of deliberately neglecting a woman within the household sphere, whereas according to the law applicable to the person concerned or due to agreement or contract he/she is obligated to provide livelihood and care to said woman.

   - b. Acts that result in the unfulfillment of a child's needs in a proper manner, whether physical, spiritual or social, committed by parents, guardians, or other parties responsible for his/her care.
   - c. Acts of deliberately neglecting to care for or look after a child as appropriate, committed by parents, guardians, or any other party responsible for his/her care.

#### Article 9

Exploitation as referred to in Article 4 letter e includes: a. Acts of economic or sexual exploitation with the intention of benefiting oneself or others;

   - b. Acts with or without the consent of the victim which include but are not limited to prostitution, forced labor or services, slavery or similar practices, oppression, extortion, exploitation of the physical body, sexual exploitation, exploitation of reproductive organs, or unlawfully removing or transplanting organs and/or body tissues, or exploiting the labor or capabilities of a person by other parties to obtain benefits both material and immaterial.

#### Article 10

1. Other violence as referred to in Article 4 letter f constitutes threat of violence and coercion.
2. Threat of violence as referred to in paragraph (1) includes any unlawful act in the form of speech, writing, images, symbols, or body movements, whether or not using means that cause fear or restrict a person's fundamental freedom.
3. Coercion as referred to in paragraph (1) includes a situation where a person/victim is ordered to do something in such a way that the person does something contrary to their own will.

# CHAPTER V

RIGHTS OF VICTIMS OF ACTS OF VIOLENCE AGAINST WOMEN AND CHILDREN

#### Article 11

Women and children victims of acts of violence shall receive the following rights:

   - a. The right to have their dignity and worth as human beings respected;
   - b. The right to recovery;
   - c. The right to determine their own decisions;
   - d. The right to obtain information;
   - e. The right to confidentiality;
   - f. The right to social rehabilitation and social reintegration;
   - g. The right to complaint handling that is appropriate, comfortable, and in accordance with their needs;
   - h. The right of victims and their families to obtain facilitation in the judicial process;
      - i. The right to assistance; and
   - j. To receive continuous handling up to the rehabilitation stage.

#### Article 12

Child victims of acts of violence, in addition to obtaining the rights as referred to in Article 11, also obtain special rights, as follows: a. Right to survival, growth, and development; b. Right to basic population services;

   - c. Right to equal protection;
   - d. Right to be free from various stigma; and
   - e. Right to obtain freedom.

# CHAPTER VI

## OBLIGATIONS AND RESPONSIBILITIES

#### Article 13

Obligations and responsibilities in providing protection and Empowerment of women and children, among others:

   - a. Regional Government;
   - b. Private Sector and Community Institutions;
   - c. Community;
   - d. Family and/or parents;
   - e. Business Sector; and
   - f. Mass Media.

First Part Regional Government

#### Article 14

1. The obligations and responsibilities of the Regional Government as referred to in Article 13 letter a, include:
   - a. Establishing policies for the protection and empowerment of women and children;
   - b. Implementing policies, protection, and empowerment of women and children from acts of violence in accordance with the provisions of laws and regulations;
   - c. Conducting cooperation in the implementation of the protection and empowerment of women and children;
   - d. Providing support for facilities and infrastructure for the implementation of the protection and empowerment of women and children;
   - e. Allocating a budget for the organization/implementation of the protection and empowerment of women and children;
   - f. Fostering and supervising the implementation of the protection and empowerment of women and children; and
   - g. Realizing a Child-Friendly Regency.
2. In order to carry out the obligations and responsibilities as referred to in paragraph (1), the Regent together with the DPRD shall establish programs and action activities for the protection and empowerment of women and children in the Regional Medium-Term Development Plan (RPJMD).
3. The RPJMD as referred to in paragraph (2) shall also contain the Regional Action Plan (RAD) on the protection of women and children.
4. Further provisions regarding the Regional Action Plan as referred to in paragraph (3) shall be regulated by a Regent Regulation.

Second Part Private Sector and Community Institutions

#### Article 15

1. The obligations and responsibilities of the private sector and the community as referred to in Article 13 letter b and letter c, shall be implemented in the form of participation of the private sector and the community.
2. The form of participation of the private sector and the community as referred to in paragraph (1), includes:
   - a. Preventing the occurrence of acts of violence against women and children;
   - b. Providing information and/or reporting acts of violence against women and children to law enforcement or the authorized parties; and
   - c. Participating in the handling of victims of violence.
3. The form of participation of the private sector and the community as referred to in paragraph (2), shall be implemented responsibly in accordance with the provisions of laws and regulations.

Third Part Family and/or Parents

#### Article 16

The obligations of the family and/or parents as referred to in Article 13 letter d, who legally have full responsibility to prevent all forms of violence and to protect women and children as members of the family.

Fourth Part Business World

#### Article 17

Obligations and Responsibilities of the Business Sector, as referred to in Article 13 letter e, include: (1) Company policies with a child- and women-oriented perspective in order to provide protection;

2. Products intended for Children must be safe for Children and/or in accordance with the Indonesian National Standard (SNI);
3. Contributing to the fulfillment of Children's Rights through corporate social responsibility.

## Part Five

Mass Media

#### Article 18

The Obligations of Mass Media as referred to in Article 13 letter f, shall be carried out through the dissemination of information and educational materials that are beneficial from the social, cultural, educational, religious, and health aspects of Children by taking into account the best interests of women and Children.

# CHAPTER VII

## PROTECTION OF WOMEN AND CHILDREN

## Part One

Prevention of violence against women and children

#### Article 19

1. To prevent the occurrence of acts of violence against women and children, the Regional Government conducts empowerment and awareness raising to families, parents, children and the community by providing information, guidance and/or counseling, trainings and realizing a Child-Friendly Regency.
2. In addition to empowerment and awareness raising as referred to in paragraph (1), the Regional Government undertakes efforts as follows:
   - a. Providing material on the prevention of acts of violence against women and children in formal, non-formal, and informal education;
   - b. Provision of accessibility to obtain education, training, funding, income enhancement and social services;
   - c. Creating a friendly and safe work environment atmosphere for women;
   - d. Building community participation and concern towards the prevention and protection of women and children from acts of violence;
   - e. Building and providing an information system that is easily accessible;
   - f. Building networks and cooperation with law enforcement apparatus, government apparatus, universities and various non-governmental organizations that work and/or care about women and children.

#### Article 20

1. Prevention of acts of violence against women and children as referred to in Article 19 shall be implemented by Regional Apparatus whose duties and functions are in the fields of:
   - a. Regional planning and innovation;
   - b. Social affairs;
   - c. Health;
   - d. Education;
   - e. Manpower;
   - f. Ministry of Religious Affairs;
   - g. Religious Courts;
   - h. Population and civil registration;
      - i. Ministry of Law and Human Rights;
   - j. Women's empowerment and child protection;
   - k. Family planning;
   - l. Public Works and Public Housing;
   - m. Tourism;
   - n. Cooperatives and micro enterprises;
   - o. Environment;
   - p. Village community empowerment;
   - q. Regional disaster management;
   - r. Police;
   - s. Civil service (public order officers).
2. Prevention of acts of violence by Regional Apparatus as referred to in paragraph (1), shall be implemented in an integrated and sustainable manner based on RPJPD and RPJMD.

Second Part Forms and Mechanisms of Services for Violence Against Women and Children

#### Article 21

1. In the context of providing protection services to women and children from acts of violence, the Regional Government establishes the Regional Technical Implementation Unit for the Protection of Women and Children (UPTD PPA), the Integrated Referral Service System (SLRT), and the Task Force on Violence Against Women/Children (Tim KTP/A) at Puskesmas (community health centers), hospitals, schools, Islamic boarding schools (Pondok Pesantren), and Women-Friendly and Child-Caring Villages (DRPPA).
2. The Regional Government through UPTD PPA as referred to in paragraph (1), functions to implement services:
   - a. Public complaints;
   - b. Victim outreach;
   - c. Case management;
   - d. Temporary shelter;
   - e. Mediation;
   - f. Victim assistance; and
   - g. Return of victims.
3. Further provisions regarding the organization and working procedures of UPTD PPA as referred to in paragraph (1) shall be stipulated by Regent Regulation.
4. To assist in the protection of women and children, the Regional Government establishes:
   - a. Task Force for the Prevention and Handling of Criminal Acts of Human Trafficking;
   - b. Task Force for the Prevention of Child Marriage;
   - c. Task Force for Child-Friendly Regency;
   - d. Regional Action Committee for the elimination of the worst forms of child labor or Worst Forms of Child Labor (BPTA);
   - e. Regional Children's Forum (FAD);
   - f. Community-Based Integrated Child Protection (PATBM) PENA BISMA;
   - g. Domestic Violence Forum (KDRT);
   - h. Task Force for the Protection of Women and Children (Satgas PPA);
      - i. Women-Friendly and Child-Caring Villages (DRPPA);
   - j. Tim KTP/A at hospitals, Puskesmas (community health centers), schools, and Islamic boarding schools (Pondok Pesantren).
5. Further provisions regarding the establishment, duties, and functions of the task forces as referred to in paragraph (4) shall be stipulated by Regent Regulation.

#### Article 22

1. The form of Regional Government services to victims implemented by UPTD PPA and others as referred to in Article 21 includes: a. Medical services;
   - b. Medicolegal services (visum);
   - c. Psychosocial services;
   - d. Legal services;
   - e. Economic independence services;
   - f. Psychological services; and
   - g. Psychiatrist services.
2. Further provisions regarding service mechanisms according to standard operational procedures are regulated in the Regent's Decree.

#### Article 23

1. The Implementation of Assistance as referred to in Article 21 includes all integrated efforts to restore and strengthen the condition of victims, carried out by health workers, education workers, police, prosecutors, courts, advocates, social workers, assistance volunteers and spiritual advisors.
2. Assistance as referred to in paragraph (1) shall be in accordance with the provisions of laws and regulations.

Third Part Service Principles

#### Article 24

The provision of services to victims is carried out with principles, among others: no fees charged, fast, safe, empathy, non-discriminatory, easily accessible and confidentiality.

# CHAPTER VIII

## COOPERATION, PARTNERSHIP AND EMPOWERMENT

## Part One

Cooperation

#### Article 25

1. In order to achieve the objectives of protection of women and children, cooperation may be carried out with:
   - a. Central Government;
   - b. Provincial Government;
   - c. Other Regency/City Governments;
   - d. Non-Governmental Institutions;
   - e. Community;
   - f. Higher Education Institutions;
   - g. Business Sector;
   - h. Mass Media;
2. Cooperation as referred to in paragraph (1) includes:
   - a. Exchange of data and information;
   - b. Recovery of victims of violence;
   - c. Repatriation and social reintegration;
   - d. Provision of evidence and witnesses; and
   - e. Followed up in accordance with applicable law.
3. Cooperation as referred to in paragraph (1) and paragraph (2) shall be set forth in the form of a Cooperation Agreement in accordance with the provisions of laws and regulations.

Second Part Partnership

#### Article 26

1. Regional Government establishes partnerships with the business sector and mass media in the protection of women and children from acts of violence in accordance with the provisions of laws and regulations.
2. Partnership with the business sector as referred to in paragraph (1) is carried out through:
   - a. Notification of employment opportunity information for women and children victims of acts of violence;
   - b. Education and training for women and children victims of acts of violence.
3. Partnership with mass media as referred to in paragraph (1) is carried out through the dissemination of information and educational materials that are beneficial from social, cultural, educational, religious, and health aspects of women and children by taking into account the best interests of women and children victims of violence.

Third Part Empowerment

#### Article 27

Strengthening and development of Women's Empowerment Service Provider Institutions, is implemented through:

   - a. capacity building of Women's Empowerment Service Provider Institutions;
   - b. capacity building of human resources managing Women's Empowerment Service Provider Institutions;
   - c. assistance in the implementation of Women's Empowerment services;
   - d. strengthening networks among Women's Empowerment Service Provider Institutions at the district level;
   - e. facilitation of efforts to fulfill the standards of Women's Empowerment Service Provider Institutions; and
   - f. development of communication, information, and education about Women's Empowerment services

# CHAPTER IX

## GUIDANCE AND SUPERVISION

#### Article 28

1. The regional government carries out Guidance and Supervision by means of:
   - a. Coordination
   - b. Guidance
   - c. Education and Training
   - d. Monitoring and Evaluation (2) The implementation of coordination as referred to in paragraph (1) letter a is carried out among stakeholders cross-sectorally with Regional Apparatus and community organizations/institutions in accordance with the provisions of laws and regulations.
3. The implementation of Guidance as referred to in paragraph (1) letter b for women and children who become victims is carried out through guidance on religious values and social values, counseling, social rehabilitation, and social assistance by the competent Regional Apparatus together with other related agencies.
4. The implementation of education and training as referred to in paragraph (1) letter c is provided to women and children who become victims as an effort to improve the physical and mental condition of women and children in fulfilling their rights and life needs by the competent Regional Apparatus, Work Training Centers, and other related agencies.
5. The implementation of guidance and supervision for women and children who become victims as referred to in paragraph (2), paragraph (3), and paragraph (4) is carried out through monitoring and evaluation of the targets to be achieved.

# CHAPTER X

## CHILD-FRIENDLY REGENCY (KLA)

## AND PARAHITA EKAPRAYA AWARD (APE)

#### Article 29

1. To realize a Child-Friendly Regency (KLA) and Parahita Ekapraya Award (APE), the Regional Government, Private Sector and Community Institutions shall pay attention to the rights of children and women related to the rights to life, growth and development, protection and participation.
2. The Regional Government, Private Sector and Community Institutions in realizing a Child-Friendly Regency are incorporated in the Child-Friendly Regency Task Force and PUG Working Group.
3. The Child-Friendly Regency Task Force as referred to in paragraph (2) is responsible for the fulfillment of children's rights related to:
   - a. Civil rights and freedoms;
   - b. Family environment and alternative care;
   - c. Basic health and welfare;
   - d. Education, use of leisure time and arts and cultural activities;
   - e. Special protection of children;
   - f. As well as institutionalization;
5. The PUG Working Group as referred to in paragraph (2), is responsible for the fulfillment of women's rights with a gender perspective.
6. Provisions related to Child-Friendly Regency (KLA) and PUG are regulated in Laws and Regulations.

# CHAPTER XI

## FUNDING

#### Article 30

Financing for the implementation of protection of women and children is sourced from:

   - a. Regional Revenue and Expenditure Budget (APBD); and
   - b. Other legal and non-binding sources.

#### Article 31

1. Funding for the protection of women and children victims of violence includes:
   - a. Medical services, which include doctor's examinations, procedure costs, hospital costs, medication costs, and supporting medical costs;
   - b. Medicolegal services, which include examinations for Visum et Repertum and Visum et Psikiatrikum;
   - c. Psychosocial services, which consist of counseling and psychological therapy as well as safe houses (shelter);
   - d. Legal services;
   - e. Economic empowerment, in the form of services for skills training and providing economic access.
2. Funding management is carried out through technical institutions appointed by the Regional Government, conducted transparently and legally accountable.

##### CONCERNING

##### THE IMPLEMENTATION OF PROTECTION OF WOMEN AND CHILDREN

##### I. GENERAL.

Acts of violence against women and children are experiencing an increasingly rising trend. Triggering factors such as economic, social, cultural and social environment are becoming more complex along with the influence of technological development. Concrete steps are needed to provide protection by all elements of citizens as a unity of society, as well as the role of the Regional Government as the protector of its citizens with various programs to prevent acts of violence against women and children. One of the efforts to prevent violence against women is carried out through women's empowerment and optimization of previously possessed potential, so that it is expected to be able to improve and develop themselves to play a role and be protected from potential acts of violence. This condition will strengthen the form of appreciation and respect for women and maintain their role and nature as a woman to play a role in educating and supporting the welfare and happiness of their family.

Efforts to prevent violence against children are a shared obligation of parents, families, communities and the private sector in a holistic manner and inseparable from one another. Community-based services are the frontline when potential violence against children occurs. The mechanism for preventing violence and protecting child victims of violence involves multi-sectors coordinated by the Regional Government. The intended efforts are carried out with the direction of providing social rehabilitation for child victims of violence, reuniting child victims of violence with their families and/or environment, and increasing the empowerment of child victims of violence.

In connection with this matter, to realize the provision of protection efforts for women and child victims of violence as well as for legal certainty in the implementation of women's empowerment and protection of child victims of violence in the regional area, it is necessary to regulate this in a Regional Regulation.
