# CONCERNING

# WOMEN'S EMPOWERMENT AND CHILD PROTECTION

# BY THE GRACE OF ALMIGHTY GOD

# THE REGENT OF DHARMASRAYA,

Considering : a. that women and children have the right to obtain a sense of security and freedom from all forms of torture or treatment that degrades human dignity; b. that women and children are vulnerable groups who tend to experience violence, therefore they need to obtain optimal protection;

   - c. that the fulfillment of constitutional rights of women and children as well as the improvement of the quality of life of women and children, is one of the mandatory affairs that becomes the duty, authority and responsibility of the Regional Government; d. that based on the considerations as referred to in letter a, letter b and letter c, it is necessary to establish a Regional Regulation concerning Women's Empowerment and Child Protection;

In View of : 1. Article 18 paragraph (6) of the 1945 Constitution of the Republic of Indonesia;

2. 3. Law Number 38 of 2003 concerning the Establishment of Dharmasraya Regency, South Solok Regency and West Pasaman Regency in West Sumatra Province (State Gazette of the Republic of Indonesia of 2003 Number 153, Supplement to the State Gazette of the Republic of Indonesia Number
4348. ;
4. Law Number 23 of 2004 concerning the Elimination of Domestic Violence (State Gazette of the Republic of Indonesia of 2004 Number 95, Supplement to the State Gazette of the Republic of Indonesia Number
4419. ;

# CHAPTER I

## GENERAL PROVISIONS

## Part One

Definitions

#### Article 1

In this Regional Regulation, what is meant by:

1. Region is Dharmasraya Regency.
2. Regional Government is the Regent and regional apparatus as elements of the Regional Government administration of Dharmasraya Regency.
3. Regent is the Regent of Dharmasraya.
4. Regional Apparatus is the Regional Apparatus within the Government of Dharmasraya Regency.
5. Office of Social Affairs, Women's Empowerment, Child Protection, Population Control and Family Planning, hereinafter referred to as the Office, is the Regional Apparatus that handles affairs in the Field of Implementation of Women's and Children's Protection.
6. Women's Protection is all efforts to guarantee and protect the rights of women so that they can live healthily and participate optimally in accordance with human dignity and obtain protection from neglect, violence and discrimination, whether in the form of preventive, repressive or post-remedial protection (assistance and treatment).
7. Gender Mainstreaming is a strategy carried out rationally and systematically to achieve and realize gender equality and justice in a number of aspects of human life, through policies and programs that take into account the experiences, aspirations, needs and problems of women and men in the planning, implementation, monitoring and evaluation of all policies and programs in various fields of life and development.
8. Gender Mainstreaming Working Group, hereinafter referred to as Pokja Gender Mainstreaming, is a consultation forum for implementers and mobilizers of gender mainstreaming from various agencies/institutions in the region.
9. Gender Mainstreaming Focal Point is an apparatus in the Regional Apparatus who has the ability to carry out gender mainstreaming in their respective work units.
10. Child is a person who has not yet reached 18 (eighteen) years of age, including children still in the womb.
11. Child protection is all activities to guarantee and protect children and their rights so that they can live, grow, develop, and participate optimally in accordance with human dignity, and receive protection from violence and discrimination.
12. Prevention is direct efforts made by the Regional Government, community and parents and/or family to prevent violations of the rights of women and children.
13. Children's Forum is a forum for children's participation formed with the aim of facilitating children's involvement in the decision-making process and enjoying changes concerning their lives either directly or indirectly, which is carried out with the consent and will of all children based on awareness and understanding, in accordance with age and level of maturity of thinking.
14. Child-Friendly Regency, hereinafter abbreviated as KLA, is a Regency that has a child rights-based development system through the integration of commitments and resources of government, community and business world that are planned comprehensively and sustainably in policies, programs and activities to ensure the fulfillment of children's rights.
15. Women victims of violence are women who experience actions that result in misery or suffering physically, sexually or psychologically, including threats of certain actions, coercion or deprivation of freedom arbitrarily, whether occurring in the public sphere or in private life.
16. Child victims of violence are children who experience actions that result in misery or suffering physically, mentally, sexually, psychologically or neglect that threatens life, body and degrades human dignity.
17. Human trafficking is the act of recruitment, transportation, harboring, sending, transfer, or receipt of a person by threat of violence, use of violence, abduction, confinement, forgery, fraud, abuse of power or position of vulnerability, debt bondage or giving payment or benefits, so as to obtain consent from the person holding control over that other person, whether carried out within the country or between countries, for the purpose of exploitation or resulting in the person being exploited.
18. Complaint handling is a series of actions taken by integrated service providers to follow up on reports of acts of violence against women and children, submitted by victims, families or the community.
19. Social rehabilitation is a service aimed at restoring and developing the ability of a person experiencing social dysfunction so that they can carry out their social functions properly.
20. Law enforcement is action by apparatus authorized by the state to implement laws and regulations.
21. Legal aid is legal services provided by legal assistants and advocates to carry out the process of assisting witnesses and/or victims of violence against women and children that is gender sensitive.
22. Repatriation is an effort to return women and child victims of violence from abroad to the debarkation point/entry point, or from the receiving area to the area of origin.
23. Social reintegration is an effort to reunite victims with family, substitute family, or community that can provide protection and fulfillment of needs for victims.
24. Safe house is a temporary shelter that aims to provide protection and a sense of security to victims of violence.
25. Spiritual guidance is an effort to restore the soul through strengthening the religion adhered to by the victim.
26. Gender responsive is consistent and systematic attention to the differences between women and men in society accompanied by efforts to eliminate structural and cultural barriers in achieving gender equality.
27. Parents are biological father and/or mother, or stepfather and/or stepmother, or adoptive father and/or mother.
28. Family is the smallest unit in society consisting of husband and wife or husband, wife and their children, or father and his children, or mother and her children, or blood relatives in a straight line upward or downward up to the third degree.
29. Customs are eternal and hereditary codes of conduct from one generation to another generation as heritage, so that they are strongly integrated with the behavioral patterns of society.

## Part Two

Principles, Objectives and Scope

#### Article 2

1. The implementation of protection for women is based on the principles of:
   - a. the best interests of women;
   - b. gender justice and gender equality;
   - c. local wisdom; and
   - d. legal certainty.
2. The implementation of protection for children is based on the principles of:
   - a. non-discrimination;
   - b. the best interests of the child;
   - c. the right to life, survival, and development;
   - d. respect for the views of the child; and
   - e. legal certainty.

#### Article 3

Women's Empowerment and Child Protection aims to:

   - a. serve as a guideline for local government in planning, policies and strategies for the protection of women and children;
   - b. protect women and children from actions, decisions and policies that violate the rights of women and children;
   - c. prevent violations of the rights of women and children;
   - d. increase empowerment of women and children;
   - e. enhance the role and value of local wisdom and the role of customs in the protection of women and children; and
   - f. enhance the role of government and non-government institutions in the region in efforts to protect women and children.

#### Article 4

The scope of regulation for the protection of women and children includes:

   - a. rights and obligations;
   - b. improvement of women's quality of life;
   - c. protection of women and children;
   - d. improvement of family quality;
   - e. gender and child data system;
   - f. fulfillment of children's rights;
   - g. child-friendly regency; and
   - h. guidance and supervision.

# CHAPTER II

## RIGHTS AND OBLIGATIONS

## Part One

Rights of Women and Children

### Paragraph 1

Women's Rights

#### Article 5

Every woman has the right to:

   - a. obtain protection from discriminatory actions;
   - b. obtain protection from economic exploitation and sexual exploitation;
   - c. obtain protection from physical, psychological, sexual violence, and neglect;
   - d. obtain decent work in accordance with her capabilities;
   - e. obtain work leave while receiving wages/salary in accordance with statutory regulations;
   - f. obtain education and teaching in all types, levels and pathways of education;
   - g. obtain special protection in the implementation of her work or profession against matters that could threaten her safety and or health in relation to women's reproductive function; and
   - h. ownership and management of joint property in marriage in accordance with statutory regulations.

### Paragraph 2

Rights of the Child

#### Article 6

Every child has the right:

   - a. to live, grow, develop and participate reasonably in accordance with human dignity;
   - b. to obtain protection from acts of discrimination;
   - c. to obtain protection from economic exploitation and sexual exploitation;
   - d. to obtain protection from physical, psychological, sexual violence, and neglect;
   - e. to a name as personal identity and citizenship status;
   - f. to obtain a birth certificate free of charge;
   - g. to embrace a religion and worship according to the teachings of their religion with the guidance of parents/guardians;
   - h. not to be forced to change religion by foster parents, adoptive parents, or anyone;
      - i. to be recognized and to know their parents, and to be raised and cared for by their own parents;
   - j. to obtain health services and social security in accordance with physical, mental, spiritual, and social needs;
   - k. to obtain education and teaching in accordance with their interests and talents;
   - l. to obtain care, education, training, self-improvement and the ability to participate in community and state life for children with physical and mental disabilities and special needs;
   - m. to express and have their opinion heard; and
   - n. to rest and utilize leisure time for self-development.

Second Part Obligations of Regional Government

#### Article 7

The obligations of the Regional Government in efforts to protect women include:

   - a. establishing and implementing policies, programs and activities for the protection of women's rights;
   - b. conducting cooperation with the community and/or Community Organizations in the implementation of women's protection in accordance with laws and regulations;
   - c. conducting rehabilitation and empowerment of women who are victims of acts of violence;
   - d. providing special protection to women victims of acts of violence and human trafficking; and
   - e. taking preventive action in order to prevent violations of women's rights.

#### Article 8

The obligations of the Regional Government in efforts to protect children include:

   - a. guaranteeing the human rights of every child without discrimination; b. providing support for facilities and infrastructure in the implementation of child protection;
   - c. guaranteeing the right of children to express their opinions in accordance with the age and intelligence level of the child; d. establishing and implementing policies and programs/activities for the protection of children's rights; e. cooperating with the community and/or Community Organizations in the implementation of child protection in accordance with laws and regulations; f. conducting rehabilitation for children who are victims of acts of violence;
   - g. providing special protection to children in emergency situations, children in conflict with the law, children who are victims of acts of violence and human trafficking, children from minority and isolated groups, children who are economically and/or sexually exploited, children who are victims of narcotics, alcohol, psychotropic substance abuse and children with HIV/AIDS;
   - h. organizing inclusive education for children with special needs; and
      - i. taking preventive action in order to prevent violations of children's rights.

Third Part Obligations of the Community

#### Article 9

1. Obligations of the community in efforts to protect women and children:
   - a. participate in prevention and provide support for the development of programs to prevent violations of the rights of women and children;
   - b. provide information and/or report violations of the rights of women and children to the competent authorities; and
   - c. supervise the law enforcement process regarding cases of violations of the rights of women and children.
2. Ninik mamak, alim ulama and cadiak pandai as well as bundo kanduang are responsible for protecting and preventing violations of the rights of women and children.
3. The obligations of the community as referred to in paragraph (1) and paragraph (2) are based on laws and regulations and the values of local wisdom of the local community.

Fourth Part Obligations of Family and/or Parents

### Paragraph 1

Obligations Towards the Protection of Women

#### Article 10

The family, in efforts to protect the rights of women, is obligated to:

   - a. protect women from conflict and domestic violence that degrades the dignity of women;
   - b. provide equal opportunities for women to develop themselves in the fields of education, social, economic, political, and cultural affairs; and
   - c. prevent the occurrence of violations of women's rights within the household.

### Paragraph 2

Obligations Towards Child Protection

#### Article 11

1. Parents, in efforts to protect the rights of children, are obligated to:
   - a. care for, maintain, educate, and protect children;
   - b. develop children in accordance with their abilities, talents, and interests;
   - c. provide formal and non-formal education;
   - d. guide children to behave in accordance with religion, ethics and morals as well as customs; and
   - e. prevent the occurrence of marriage at a child's age.
2. In the event that parents do not exist, or their whereabouts are unknown, or due to a reason cannot carry out their obligations and responsibilities, then the obligations and responsibilities as referred to in paragraph (1) may transfer to the family.

# CHAPTER III

## IMPROVEMENT OF WOMEN'S QUALITY OF LIFE

## Part One

General

#### Article 12

1. In order to improve the quality of life of women, the Regional Government is responsible for:
   - a. respecting and guaranteeing the rights of women without discrimination in accordance with human dignity and worth;
   - b. implementing Gender Mainstreaming in the Region;
   - c. establishing a Gender Mainstreaming working group in the Region;
   - d. increasing the empowerment of Women in terms of role, quality and capacity in the fields of:
1. politics;
2. law;
3. social; and
4. economy.
   - e. strengthening and developing service provider institutions for women's empowerment in the region.
2. The responsibility of the regional government in efforts to improve the quality of life of women as referred to in paragraph (1) shall be implemented by the relevant Regional Apparatus.

Second Part Implementation of Gender Mainstreaming in the Regions

#### Article 13

1. The Regent is responsible for the implementation of Gender mainstreaming in the Region.
2. The responsibility of the Regent as referred to in paragraph (1) may be delegated to the Vice Regent.

#### Article 14

In implementing Gender Mainstreaming in the region, the Regent establishes the Regional Apparatus responsible for Women's Empowerment as the coordinator for the implementation of Gender Mainstreaming.

Third Part Formation of the Gender Mainstreaming Working Group

#### Article 15

1. In the effort to accelerate the institutionalization of Gender Mainstreaming in all Regional Apparatus, a Gender Mainstreaming Working Group is established.
2. The Gender Mainstreaming Working Group as referred to in paragraph (1) consists of:
   - a. chairperson: Head of Bapppeda;
   - b. secretary: Head of the Regional Apparatus in charge of Women's Empowerment; and
   - c. members: all heads/leaders of Regional Apparatus.
3. The establishment of the Gender Mainstreaming Working Group as referred to in paragraph (1) is stipulated by a Decree of the Regent.

#### Article 16

The Regional Gender Mainstreaming Working Group as referred to in Article 15 paragraph (1) has the following duties:

   - a. promoting and facilitating Gender Mainstreaming to each Regional Apparatus;
   - b. implementing socialization and advocacy of Gender Mainstreaming to Sub-district Heads and Wali Nagari;
   - c. preparing work programs every year;
   - d. encouraging the realization of gender-responsive planning and budgeting;
   - e. preparing the work plan of the Gender Mainstreaming working group every year;
   - f. being responsible to the Regent through the Vice Regent;
   - g. formulating Gender Mainstreaming policy recommendations to the Regent;
   - h. preparing the Regional Gender profile;
      - i. conducting monitoring of Gender Mainstreaming implementation in each Regional Apparatus;
   - j. establishing a technical team to conduct analysis of the gender-responsive regional budget;
   - k. preparing a regional action plan on Gender Mainstreaming in the region; and
   - l. encouraging the implementation of selection and determination of focal points in each Regional Apparatus.

#### Article 17

1. The technical team as referred to in Article 16 letter j shall consist of apparatus who understand gender responsive budget analysis.
2. The regional action plan for Regional Gender Mainstreaming as referred to in Article 16 letter k shall contain: a. gender mainstreaming in laws and regulations in the region;
   - b. gender mainstreaming in the development cycle in the region;
   - c. institutional strengthening of Gender Mainstreaming in the region; and
   - d. strengthening of community participation in the region

#### Article 18

1. The Gender Mainstreaming Focal Point in each Regional Apparatus as referred to in Article 16 letter l consists of officials and/or staff in charge of planning and/or programs.
2. The Gender Mainstreaming Focal Point as referred to in paragraph (1) has the following duties:
   - a. promoting Gender Mainstreaming in work units;
   - b. facilitating the preparation of work plans and budgeting of Regional Apparatus that are gender responsive;
   - c. conducting training, socialization, and advocacy of Gender Mainstreaming to all officials and staff within the Regional Apparatus;
   - d. reporting the implementation of Gender Mainstreaming to the leadership of the Regional Apparatus;
   - e. encouraging the implementation of gender analysis on policies, programs, and activities in work units; and
   - f. facilitating the preparation of gender data in each Regional Apparatus.
3. The Gender Mainstreaming Focal Point as referred to in paragraph (1) shall be selected and determined by the leadership of the Regional Apparatus.

Fourth Part Enhancement of the Role, Quality, and Capability of Women in the Political Field

#### Article 19

Efforts to increase the role, quality, and capability of women in the political field include:

   - a. conducting outreach to increase women's political participation in general elections;
   - b. implementing programs that encourage the level of women's involvement in the political process and public positions;
   - c. facilitating women's self-development in organizing, associating, and gathering;
   - d. involving women in decision-making concerning women's interests;

## Part Five

Enhancement of the Role, Quality, and Capability of Women in the Legal Field

#### Article 20

Efforts to increase the role, quality and capability of women in the field of law include: a. conducting socialization to increase women's legal knowledge and awareness; b. conducting mapping and reviewing gender-biased policies; c. involving women's organizations in the process of drafting and discussing Regional legal products related to women's participation;

Sixth Part Enhancement of the Role, Quality, and Capability of Women in the Social Sector

#### Article 21

Efforts to increase the role, quality and capability of women in the social field include:

   - a. increasing knowledge and skills to encourage the fulfillment of education in accordance with the potential possessed;
   - b. increasing and fulfilling the quality of health for women;
   - c. increasing awareness and knowledge about independent, healthy and prosperous family planning including access to consultation services and marriage registration;
   - d. increasing women's awareness of the impact of early marriage;
   - e. facilitating and endeavoring to preserve customs and traditions and the development of science, technology, arts and culture for the advancement of women;
   - f. increasing women's awareness and knowledge of independent, healthy and prosperous family planning;
   - g. implementing programs to reduce maternal mortality rates;
   - h. encouraging the establishment of women's organizations in the Region;

Seventh Part Enhancement of the Role, Quality, and Capability of Women in the Economic Field

#### Article 22

Efforts to increase the role, quality and capability of women in the field include:

   - a. implementing programs, activities, and training to improve women's skills and expertise in order to realize an improvement in women's welfare;
   - b. facilitating the strengthening of business capital for women with weak economic conditions;
   - c. marketing products resulting from the businesses of women with weak economic conditions;
   - d. training to develop women's capabilities through the economic empowerment of pre-prosperous families;
   - e. comprehensive entrepreneurship training for women.

# CHAPTER IV

## PROTECTION OF WOMEN AND CHILDREN

First Part Prevention of Acts of Violence against Women and Children

#### Article 23

1. The responsibility of the Regional Government to prevent acts of violence against women and children includes:
   - a. conducting socialization regarding the prevention of violence against women and children by optimizing the role of customary, cultural, religious institutions and community organizations;
   - b. providing guidelines on handling acts of violence against women and children;
   - c. involving community participation in recovery and reintegration;
   - d. developing an integrated database and information system on acts of violence against women and children;
   - e. developing family empowerment programs;
   - f. strengthening the function of community organizations engaged in women's empowerment and child protection; and
   - g. providing public facilities that are comfortable and safe for women and children.
2. Prevention of acts of violence against women and children as referred to in paragraph (1), shall be implemented by Regional Apparatus whose duties and functions are in the fields of:
   - a. social affairs;
   - b. health;
   - c. education;
   - d. manpower;
   - e. women's empowerment and child protection;
   - f. mental and spiritual;
   - g. informatics and communication; and
   - h. public peace and order.
3. Prevention as referred to in paragraph (1), shall be implemented by Regional Apparatus in accordance with their respective fields of duty in an integrated and sustainable manner based on the Regional Action Plan.

#### Article 24

The responsibility of the community to prevent the occurrence of acts of violence against women and children includes:

   - a. monitoring various conditions that occur in their environment that may give rise to violations of the rights of women and children;
   - b. increasing awareness of violations of the rights of women and children;
   - c. strengthening the role of community organizations, religious organizations and the family environment; and
   - d. preventing unregistered marriages and marriage at the age of a child.

#### Article 25

The responsibility of parents and family to prevent the occurrence of acts of violence against women and children includes:

   - a. protecting women and children from various violations of rights in the household;
   - b. encouraging the formation of a harmonious, educational and democratic family;
   - c. providing good examples to children in accordance with religious and customary teachings;
   - d. supervising children in their activities inside or outside the home; and
   - e. providing opportunities for children to dialogue and express opinions democratically.

Third Part Effectiveness of Services for Women and Children

### Paragraph 1 General

#### Article 26

1. In order to implement the Protection of Women and Children in the Region, the Regional Government establishes UPTD PPA in accordance with the provisions of laws and regulations.
2. UPTD PPA as referred to in paragraph (1) functions to provide services to women and Children who experience criminal and/or civil problems.
3. The scope of services of UPTD PPA as referred to in paragraph (2) includes:
   - a. complaint services;
   - b. health rehabilitation;
   - c. social rehabilitation;
   - d. assistance;
   - e. enforcement and legal aid; and
   - f. repatriation and social reintegration.
4. In carrying out its functions, UPTD PPA is obligated to:
   - a. provide services and handling as quickly as possible to women and children who experience problems;
   - b. provide convenience, comfort, safety and free of charge to women and children who experience problems;
   - c. maintain the confidentiality of women and children who experience problems;
   - d. guarantee justice and legal certainty for women and children who experience problems.
5. UPTD PPA is responsible to the Regional Apparatus that administers governmental affairs in the field of Women's Empowerment and Child Protection.

#### Article 27

1. The implementation of UPTD PPA services is integrative in nature, which is carried out under one roof or networked to provide optimal services to women and children experiencing problems.
2. In the event that the implementation of UPTD PPA services as referred to in paragraph (1) is carried out under one roof, UPTD PPA is responsible for implementing the entire process in one unit to provide the services needed by women and children experiencing problems.
3. In the event that the implementation of UPTD PPA services as referred to in paragraph (1) is carried out networked, UPTD PPA is responsible for the entire referral process of services needed by women and children experiencing problems.
4. Every element within the UPTD PPA environment in carrying out its duties must apply the principles of coordination, integration, synchronization, accountability and transparency, both within the UPTD PPA environment, as well as in relations between Regional Apparatus, related agencies, and community elements.

#### Article 28

Further provisions regarding the organization and working procedures of the UPTDPPA as referred to in Article 26 and Article 27 shall be stipulated by a Regent’s Regulation in accordance with the provisions of laws and regulations.

### Paragraph 2

Complaint Services

#### Article 29

Complaint services as referred to in Article 26 paragraph (3) letter a may be coordinated by UPTD PPA with related agencies consisting of:

   - a. hospitals;
   - b. public health centers;
   - c. integrated service centers for women and children empowerment;
   - d. trauma center protection houses/shelters;
   - e. women crisis centres;
   - f. advisory board for marriage guidance and preservation;
   - g. police;
   - h. prosecutor's office;
      - i. courts;
   - j. ministry of religious affairs; and/or legal aid organizations;

#### Article 30

1. Complaints of acts of violence against women and children may be made by reporters consisting of victims, victims' families, the community and/or community organizations in the field of protection of women and children.
2. Reporters as referred to in paragraph (1) may file complaints:
   - a. directly;
   - b. by telephone; and/or
   - c. by letter.
3. Further provisions regarding procedures for complaint handling services as referred to in paragraph (1) and paragraph (2) shall be regulated by the Regent's Regulation.

### Paragraph 3

Health Rehabilitation

#### Article 31

1. Health rehabilitation as referred to in Article 26 paragraph (3) letter b constitutes efforts that include the following aspects:
   - a. promotive, improving so that health status increasingly improves;
   - b. preventive, preventing so as not to become ill;
   - c. curative, curing a person from illness physically and psychologically;
   - d. rehabilitative, maintaining so that a person who has not fully recovered can return to being fit as before.
2. Health rehabilitation as referred to in paragraph (1) shall be conducted comprehensively, accessible 24 (twenty-four) hours, with quality and conducted in accordance with health service standards.
3. Women and Children victims of acts of violence have the right to health services free of charge, including:
   - a. medical services;
   - b. medicolegal services;
   - c. psychosocial counseling; and
   - d. Visum et Repertum.
4. Further provisions regarding the procedures for implementing health services as referred to in paragraph (3) shall be regulated by a Regent Regulation.

### Paragraph 4

Social Rehabilitation

#### Article 32

1. Social rehabilitation as referred to in Article 26 paragraph (3) letter c is intended to restore and develop the capabilities of women and children who experience social dysfunction due to criminal and/or civil matters so that they can carry out their social functions in society properly.
2. Social rehabilitation as referred to in paragraph (1) includes services:
   - a. counseling;
   - b. provision of safe houses; and
   - c. spiritual guidance.
3. In implementing services as referred to in paragraph (2), UPTD PPA may cooperate with women crisis centers and/or existing social community organizations.

#### Article 33

1. Counseling services as referred to in Article 32 paragraph (2) letter a aim to:
   - a. assist women and children victims of acts of violence in recognizing their problems and finding effective ways to overcome them independently;
   - b. empower women and children experiencing criminal and/or civil problems to determine their future;
   - c. provide moral support to women and children experiencing criminal and/or civil problems in facing the process they are undergoing; and
   - d. make women and children experiencing criminal and/or civil problems feel accepted in the community environment.
2. Counseling services are carried out by gender-responsive social rehabilitation officers, consisting of:
   - a. counselors;
   - b. psychologists; and/or
   - c. psychiatrists.
3. Counseling services are carried out in a place that guarantees a sense of security, comfort and confidentiality of information from women victims of acts of violence.
4. The stages of counseling services as referred to in paragraph (4) include:
   - a. initial counseling;
   - b. follow-up counseling;
   - c. case discussion; and
   - d. site visits.

#### Article 34

1. The provision of safe houses as referred to in Article 32 paragraph (2) letter b may be provided by the Regional Government for women and children who experience criminal and/or civil problems.
2. The service of providing safe houses as referred to in paragraph (1) aims to protect women and children as victims of criminal and/or civil problems from intimidation by perpetrators or other parties.
3. The location of safe houses as referred to in paragraph (1) must be kept confidential.
4. The criteria for women and children as victims of criminal and/or civil problems who require safe houses include:
   - a. their life is threatened;
   - b. receiving rejection from family or community;
   - c. requiring intensive services but their residence is relatively far; and/or
   - d. abandoned if not placed in a safe house.

#### Article 35

1. Spiritual guidance as referred to in Article 32 paragraph (2) letter c aims for psychological recovery.
2. Spiritual guidance as referred to in paragraph (1) is implemented based on the principles of:
   - a. not imposing any religion or belief; and
   - b. leaving the final decision in the hands of the victim (selfdetermination).
3. Spiritual guidance is conducted by spiritual guidance officers.

### Paragraph 5

Assistance

#### Article 36

1. Accompaniment services as referred to in Article 26 paragraph (3) letter d are conducted for the strengthening and recovery of victims of violence.
2. In providing accompaniment services as referred to in paragraph (1) P2TP2A may cooperate with persons or institutions that have expertise to conduct counseling, therapy and advocacy.

### Paragraph 6

Enforcement and Legal Assistance

#### Article 37

1. Law enforcement and legal assistance services as referred to in Article 26 paragraph (3) letter e constitute actions by law enforcement apparatus to implement laws and regulations in order to handle criminal and/or civil problems against women and children, especially to impose sanctions on perpetrators and provide protection for witnesses and/or victims.
2. Law enforcement and legal assistance services as referred to in paragraph (1) shall be carried out by prioritizing the best interests of victims.
3. Types of legal assistance services as referred to in paragraph (1) include:
   - a. legal assistance in the reporting process and preparation of examination reports;
   - b. legal assistance in the prosecution process at the prosecutor's office;
   - c. legal assistance in the examination process at court;
   - d. legal assistance in the court hearing decision process until there is a final legal decision; and
   - e. legal assistance in the mediation process.
4. The Regional Government shall provide legal assistance to women and children as victims of criminal and/or civil problems based on the provisions of laws and regulations.
5. In providing legal assistance as referred to in paragraph (3), UPTD PPA may cooperate with legal aid institutions, women crisis centers, advocates and/or other legal assistants who are gender sensitive.

### Paragraph 7

Repatriation and Social Reintegration

#### Article 38

1. Repatriation as referred to in Article 26 paragraph (3) letter f is intended to return women and children as victims of criminal and/or civil problems to their place of origin.
2. Repatriation of women and children as victims of criminal and/or civil problems is accompanied by social reintegration efforts.

#### Article 39

6. Social reintegration as referred to in Article 38 paragraph (2) is carried out in the context of reuniting women and children as victims of criminal and/or civil problems with their family, substitute family, or community that can provide protection and fulfillment of needs for the victims.
7. Social reintegration as referred to in paragraph (6) aims to enable women and children as victims to resume their social life and to have a decent life.
8. Social reintegration as referred to in paragraph (6) is carried out after the victims receive social rehabilitation services.

#### Article 40

The repatriation and social reintegration of women and children as victims of criminal and/or civil matters as referred to in Article 38 and Article 39 shall be implemented by UPTDPPA in accordance with the laws and regulations.

## IMPROVEMENT OF FAMILY QUALITY

   - a. growth and development of children under five years old;

   - c. maturation of marriage age;

   - b. conducting premarital counseling; and
   - c. facilitating the implementation of family development;

# CHAPTER VII

## FULFILLMENT OF CHILDREN'S RIGHTS

## Part One

General

#### Article 52

1. Regional Government in the effort to fulfill the rights of Children is responsible for:
   - a. establishing and implementing policies and programs/activities for the fulfillment of Children's rights;
   - b. respecting and guaranteeing the human rights of every Child without distinguishing ethnicity, religion, race, class, gender, ethnic group, culture and language, legal status of the Child, birth order of the Child, and physical and/or mental condition;
   - c. strengthening and developing service provider institutions for improving family quality in realizing Children's rights;
   - d. guaranteeing the Protection, care, and welfare of Children by taking into account the rights and obligations of parents, guardians, or other persons who are legally responsible for the Child;
   - e. providing facilities and infrastructure in the implementation of the fulfillment of Children's rights;
   - f. guaranteeing the right of Children to express opinions in accordance with the age and level of intelligence of the Child;
   - g. conducting supervision and guidance in the framework of implementing the fulfillment of Children's rights; and
   - h. conducting cooperation with private parties and/or NGOs in the implementation of Child Protection;
2. The responsibilities as referred to in paragraph (1) shall be implemented by Regional Apparatus in accordance with their duties and functions.

Second Part Fulfillment of Children's Rights in the field of Birth Registration and Child Identity

#### Article 53

1. Regional Governments are obligated to administer the registration of the birth of Children with the issuance of birth certificates. (2) Birth certificates as referred to in paragraph (1) shall be administered free of charge.

#### Article 54

1. To encourage improvement in data collection, protection and public services as well as to realize the best interests of the child, every child under the age of 17 years must have a child identity card.
2. The child identity card is issued by the Regional Authority responsible for population and civil registration.

Third Part Fulfillment of Children's Rights in the field of education

#### Article 55

The Regional Government guarantees the implementation of compulsory education programs for Children in accordance with the provisions of laws and regulations.

#### Article 56

1. Every organizer of an educational unit is obligated to provide opportunities to Children to obtain educational services without discrimination.
2. Every organizer of an educational unit is prohibited from expelling Children from educational institutions unless there is a guarantee for the continuity of the Child's education.
3. In the event that a Child victim of an act of Violence experiences dropping out of school, then the child victim of violence shall be provided formal or non-formal education; in this case, it shall be carried out by the organizer of the educational unit and the organizer shall coordinate with the Women and Child Empowerment Unit.

#### Article 57

1. Every Child has the right to obtain education and teaching in the context of developing their personality and level of intelligence in accordance with their interests and talents.
2. Every Child has the right to receive Protection in educational units from sexual crimes and Violence committed by educators, educational personnel, fellow students, and/or other parties.

#### Article 58

1. Children with disabilities and Children with special needs are given equal opportunities to obtain formal, nonformal and informal education services, including inclusive education programs.
2. The local government provides teaching staff for inclusive education at every education unit.

Fourth Part Fulfillment of Children's Rights in the field of Health and Social

#### Article 59

1. Regional Governments are obligated to provide comprehensive health services for Children, so that every Child obtains optimal health status from the time in the womb.
2. Comprehensive health services as referred to in paragraph (1) include:
   - a. promotive, preventive, curative and rehabilitative efforts for basic health services;
   - b. exemption from cost burden for malnourished children, children with disabilities, children with special needs, children infected with HIV/AIDS, child workers, children who are victims of narcotics abuse, alcohol, psychotropic substances and other addictive substances, children who become victims of violence, and children who are victims of human trafficking;
3. Regional Governments, communities, families and parents are obligated to endeavor to ensure that Children who are born are protected from diseases that threaten survival and/or cause disability.

#### Article 60

1. Promotive efforts for basic health services as referred to in Article 59 paragraph (2) letter a are aimed at improving health status in a more optimal manner.
2. Promotive efforts as referred to in paragraph (1) are carried out by providing socialization or education to the community regarding healthy lifestyle patterns which include:
   - a. the importance of exclusive breast milk for infants;
   - b. the importance of immunization for toddlers;
   - c. clean living patterns;
   - d. the importance of nutritious food; and
   - e. the dangers of narcotics and smoking.
3. In addition to promotive efforts as referred to in paragraph (2), the Regional Government may carry out other activities in the context of the implementation of health.

#### Article 61

1. Preventive efforts for basic health services as referred to in Article 59 paragraph (2) letter a are aimed at preventing the occurrence of disease or health problems.
2. The efforts as referred to in paragraph (1) are carried out by:
   - a. obligating mothers to provide exclusive breastfeeding to infants up to 6 (six) months of age;
   - b. providing free immunization and vitamins to infants and toddlers;
   - c. providing nutritious food to toddlers from underprivileged families;
   - d. optimizing the role of posyandu for health services for mothers and toddlers;
   - e. conducting periodic health examinations;
   - f. providing sanitation facilities and clean water at every school; and
   - g. providing special breastfeeding facilities or breastfeeding corner rooms at government agencies or business entities.

#### Article 62

1. Curative efforts for basic health services as referred to in Article 59 paragraph (2) letter a are intended to prevent diseases from becoming more severe through treatment.
2. Treatment as referred to in paragraph (1) is implemented free of charge for children from underprivileged families.

#### Article 63

1. Rehabilitative efforts for basic health services as referred to in Article 59 paragraph (2) letter a are aimed at maintaining and restoring conditions after illness.
2. Rehabilitative efforts as referred to in paragraph (1) are carried out through periodic health examinations.

#### Article 64

The Regional Government is obligated to provide Social Welfare for:

   - a. children in conflict with the law; b. child victims of violence who are economically exploited; c. children who do not have parents and are cared for by parties or families who are unable to provide;
   - d. child victims of natural disasters or social disasters; and e. children with disabilities.

Fifth Part Fulfillment of Children's Rights in the Field of Manpower

#### Article 65

The Regional Government conducts periodic supervision of every person/entity that employs Children in efforts to Protect child workers.

#### Article 66

1. Every person/entity is prohibited from employing and involving Children in the worst forms of work.
2. The worst forms of work referred to in paragraph (1) include:
   - a. all work in the form of slavery or similar practices;
   - b. all work that utilizes, provides, or offers Children for prostitution, pornography production, pornographic performances, or gambling;
   - c. all work that utilizes, provides, or involves Children for the production and trade of alcoholic beverages, narcotics, psychotropic substances, and other addictive substances; and/or
   - d. all work that endangers the health, safety, or morals of Children.

## Part Six

Children's Forum

#### Article 67

1. In implementing the fulfillment of children's rights to express opinions and participate, the Regional Government facilitates the establishment of a Children's Forum.
2. The Children's Forum as referred to in paragraph (1) represents Children in the Region.
3. The representation as referred to in paragraph (2) consists of the geographical domicile of Children, components of Children's socio-cultural groups and Children's educational background.
4. The Children's Forum as referred to in paragraph (1) may be established at the Regional, Sub-district and Nagari levels.
5. In every formulation of policies related to Children, the Regional Government must consider and accommodate the opinions of Children conveyed through the Children's Forum.
6. The establishment of the Children's Forum as referred to in paragraph (1) shall be stipulated by Regent's Decree.

   - a. the concept of KLA concerning objectives, strategies and roles of related parties;
   - b. Children's rights; and
   - c. the approach to KLA development.

   - a. civil rights and freedoms;
   - b. family environment and alternative care;
   - c. basic health and welfare;
   - d. education, utilization of leisure time, and cultural activities; and
   - e. special protection.

#### Article 70

1. In order to ensure the effectiveness of the implementation of the KLA policy in the Region, a KLA Task Force is established.
2. The KLA Task Force as referred to in paragraph (1), has the following main duties:
   - a. coordinating the implementation of policies and development of KLA;
   - b. conducting socialization, advocacy and communication of information and education on KLA policy;
   - c. collecting baseline data for KLA development;
   - d. conducting needs analysis based on baseline data;
   - e. conducting dissemination of baseline data;
   - f. determining the focus and priority of programs in realizing KLA, which are adjusted to regional potential;
   - g. preparing a 5 (five) year KLA (Child-Friendly City) regional action plan and work mechanism; and
   - h. conducting monitoring, evaluation and reporting at least 1 (one) time per year.
3. The establishment of the KLA Task Force as referred to in paragraph (1) shall be stipulated by Regent's Decree.

#### Article 71

In carrying out its duties, the KLA Task Force as referred to in Article 70 paragraph (1) may coordinate and cooperate with UPTD PPA and/or other institutions that provide Women and Child Protection services.

##### WOMEN'S EMPOWERMENT AND CHILD PROTECTION

##### I. GENERAL

Children are a trust as well as a gift from God Almighty, which we must always protect because inherent in them are dignity, honor, and rights as human beings that must be upheld. Children's human rights are part of human rights contained in the 1945 Constitution and the United Nations Convention on the Rights of the Child. From the perspective of national and state life, children are the future of the nation and the next generation of the nation's ideals, so that every child has the right to survival, growth, and development, to participate and has the right to protection from acts of violence and discrimination as well as civil rights and freedoms.

Likewise with women, from women are expected to be born children who are healthy, strong, resilient, of noble character, potential and quality. Through the role of women in the family, society and state will be born resilient generations who will continue the ideals of the nation. As citizens of Indonesia, women also have the right to improve quality and develop themselves in accordance with their abilities, dignity and honor.

Women and children need to be protected from acts of discrimination and acts of violence whether physical, psychological, mental, sexual and neglect. Because of their physical weakness compared to men and adults and because of the nature of women themselves, women and children often become objects of venting desires, anger, economic exploitation, violence (physical, psychological and sexual), cruelty, neglect, and so forth. As assets and hope of the nation and state, children need to be given protection and safeguarded from all forms of acts of violence that will hinder their growth and development.

To realize women's empowerment and child protection, support from laws and regulations is needed that can guarantee its implementation.

Although the protection of the rights of women and children has been regulated in national level laws and regulations, the fulfillment of the constitutional rights of women and children as well as the improvement of the quality of life of women and children is one of the mandatory affairs that is the duty, authority and responsibility of the Regional Government, therefore in providing protection to women and children, the Regional Government needs to establish a Regional Regulation concerning Women's Empowerment and Child Protection.

In this Regional Regulation concerning Women's Empowerment and Child Protection, the scope is regulated as follows:

   - a. rights of women and children;
   - b. obligations;
   - c. improvement of women's quality of life;
   - d. protection of women and children;
   - e. improvement of family quality;
   - f. gender and child data system;
   - g. fulfillment of children's rights;
   - h. child-friendly regency; and
      - i. guidance and supervision.
