# CHAPTER I

## GENERAL PROVISIONS

#### Article 1

In this Regional Regulation, what is meant by:

1. Region is the Regency Region of Nunukan.
2. Regional Government is the Regional Government of Nunukan Regency.
3. Regent is the Regent of Nunukan.
4. Regional Work Unit, hereinafter abbreviated as SKPD, is an auxiliary element of the Regent and DPRD in the implementation of Government Affairs that are the authority of the Region.
5. Technical Regional Work Unit, hereinafter referred to as technical SKPD, is a Regional Work Unit whose scope of duties and responsibilities includes the affairs of Women's Empowerment and/or Child Protection.
6. Child is a person who has not yet reached the age of 18 (eighteen) years, including a child still in the womb.
7. Woman is a human being of female sex and a person who is legally recognized as a woman.
8. Women's Empowerment is every effort to improve physical, mental spiritual, social abilities, knowledge, and skills so that women are ready to be utilized according to their respective abilities.
9. Fulfillment of Children's Rights is all activities to guarantee and protect children and their rights so that they can live, grow, develop and participate optimally in accordance with human dignity and receive protection from violence and discrimination.
10. Violence is any act that results in misery or suffering whether physical, sexual, psychological, and/or domestic neglect including threats to commit acts, coercion, or unlawful deprivation of a person's freedom.
11. Victim is a woman and/or child who experiences misery and/or suffering either directly or indirectly as a result of violence occurring in the Nunukan Regency area.
12. Protection is all preventive actions, services and empowerment to protect and guarantee the rights of women and children from acts of violence.
13. Prevention is activities carried out to prevent the occurrence of acts of violence against women and children.
14. Service is action taken as soon as possible to victims when seeing, hearing and knowing that violence against victims will, is or has occurred.
15. Empowerment is the strengthening of women victims of violence to be able to work and work independently after they are recovered and provided with health and social rehabilitation services.
16. Health Rehabilitation is an effort to restore the condition of victims including physical, psychological and social health so that victims can carry out their roles properly and normally both in the family and in society.
17. Social rehabilitation is a service aimed at restoring and developing the ability of a person who experiences social dysfunction so that they can carry out their social functions normally.
18. Repatriation is an effort to return victims of violence from the regional area to their area of origin.
19. Social reintegration is an effort to reunite victims with family, substitute family or community that can provide protection and fulfillment of needs for victims.
20. Integrated Service Center, hereinafter abbreviated as PPT, is a unit that organizes integrated services for victims of violence and is integrated with PPT that organizes integrated services for witnesses and/or victims of criminal acts of human trafficking.

# CHAPTER II

## PRINCIPLES AND OBJECTIVES

#### Article 2

The Protection of Women and Children is implemented based on the principles of:

   - a. respect and protection of Human Rights;
   - b. justice and gender equality;
   - c. non-discrimination;
   - d. best interests of the Child;
   - e. right to life, survival, and development;
   - f. empowerment; and
   - g. legal certainty.

#### Article 3

This Regional Regulation aims to:

   - a. guarantee the fulfillment of the rights of women and children;
   - b. maintain the integrity of households in order to realize harmonious families;
   - c. prevent violence against women and children;
   - d. protect and provide a sense of security for women and children;
   - e. provide services to women and children victims of acts of violence;
   - f. provide protection to reporters and witnesses; and
   - g. carry out empowerment of women victims of violence.

# BAB III

## PROTECTION OF WOMEN AND CHILDREN

First Section Women's Empowerment

### Paragraph 1

Rights and Obligations of Women

#### Article 4

Every Woman has the right:

   - a. to life and the right to maintain her life and existence;
   - b. to form a family and continue descendants through legal marriage;
   - c. to develop herself through the fulfillment of her basic needs;
   - d. to obtain education and benefit from science and technology, arts and culture, in order to improve the quality of her life and for the welfare of humankind;
   - e. to advance herself in fighting for her rights collectively to build society, nation and state;
   - f. to recognition, guarantee, protection and fair legal certainty as well as equal treatment before the law;
   - g. to work and receive fair and proper remuneration and treatment in employment relations;
   - h. to obtain equal opportunities in government;
      - i. to freedom of association, assembly and expression of opinion;
   - j. to protection of her person, family, honor, dignity and property under her control, and the right to a sense of security and protection from threats of fear to do or not do something which is a basic right;
   - k. to be free from torture or treatment that degrades human dignity and the right to obtain political asylum from other countries;
   - l. to live in physical and spiritual prosperity, to reside and obtain a good and healthy living environment, and the right to obtain health services;
   - m. to social security that enables her full development as a dignified human being;
   - n. to equality of position in law and government;
   - o. to work and a livelihood worthy of humanity;
   - p. in efforts to defend the state;
   - q. to communicate and obtain information to develop her personal and social environment, and the right to seek, obtain, possess, store, process, and convey information using all types of available channels;
   - r. to embrace her respective religion and to worship according to her religion and beliefs; and
   - s. to obtain education.

### Paragraph 2

Forms of Women's Empowerment

#### Article 5

1. In order to enhance the position, role, and quality of women, as well as efforts to realize gender equality and justice in family life, society, nation, and state, women must be empowered.
2. The empowerment of women as referred to in paragraph (1) is directed towards obtaining opportunities and their rights as human beings, so that they are able to play a role and participate in economic, socio-cultural, political, and legal activities.

#### Article 6

1. Regional Governments have the obligation and responsibility in efforts to empower women.
2. Efforts to empower women as referred to in paragraph (1) shall be carried out in an integrated manner in accordance with the prevailing laws and regulations.

#### Article 7

The implementation of women's empowerment in the economic field as referred to in Article 5 paragraph (2) is carried out through:

   - a. provision of skills and job training;
   - b. facilitation of the formation of productive economic business groups;
   - c. facilitation of strengthening and development of productive economic business groups;
   - d. facilitation and capital assistance; and
   - e. facilitation of marketing network development.

#### Article 8

Empowerment of women in the social and cultural field as referred to in Article 5 paragraph (2) shall be implemented through:

   - a. enhancement of knowledge, attitudes and skills to encourage the fulfillment of tiered education in accordance with potential to improve social status;
   - b. enhancement of awareness, willingness and ability to overcome health problems through quality promotive, preventive, curative, and rehabilitative efforts primarily in the field of reproductive health;
   - c. enhancement of awareness and knowledge about independent, healthy and prosperous family planning including access to consultation services and marriage registration; and
   - d. facilitation and efforts to preserve customs and traditions and development of science, technology, arts and culture for the advancement of women.

#### Article 9

1. The implementation of women's empowerment in the political field as referred to in Article 5 paragraph (2) includes:
   - a. the involvement of women in decision-making at various levels;
   - b. providing opportunities for women to hold public office;
   - c. participation in general elections; and
   - d. self-development through organizations to associate, assemble, and express opinions.
2. The implementation of women's empowerment in the political field as referred to in paragraph (1) shall be carried out in accordance with the applicable laws and regulations.

#### Article 10

1. The implementation of women's empowerment in the field of law as referred to in Article 5 paragraph (2) includes:
   - a. increasing awareness and knowledge in the field of law through communication, information, and education services; and
   - b. facilitating access to and legal consultation services.
2. The implementation of women's empowerment in the field of law as referred to in paragraph (1) shall be carried out in accordance with the applicable laws and regulations.

#### Article 11

1. To implement Women's Empowerment as referred to in Article 6, the Regional Government shall organize gender mainstreaming including:
   - a. establishing the implementation of gender mainstreaming;
   - b. coordinating, facilitating, and mediating gender mainstreaming;
   - c. facilitating institutional strengthening and development of gender mainstreaming mechanisms in government institutions, women's study centers, research and development institutions, non-governmental institutions;
   - d. implementing gender mainstreaming related to the fields of development, education, health, economy, law, Human Rights and politics, environment and socio-culture;
   - e. improving the quality of life of women related to the fields of development, education, health, economy, law, Human Rights and politics, environment and socio-culture;
   - f. integrating efforts to improve the quality of life of women related to the fields of development, education, health, economy, law, Human Rights and politics, environment and socio-culture;
   - g. coordinating the implementation of improving the quality of life of women related to the fields of development, education, health, economy, law, Human Rights and politics, environment and socio-culture;
   - h. organizing the protection of women especially protection for victims of violence, female workers, elderly women, and women with disabilities, in conflict areas and disaster-affected areas;
      - i. facilitating the integration of protection of women especially protection for victims of violence, female workers, elderly women and women with disabilities, in conflict areas and disaster-affected areas;
   - j. coordinating the protection of women especially protection for victims of violence, female workers, elderly women and women with disabilities;
   - k. facilitating the strengthening and development of networks of community institutions or organizations and the business sector for the implementation of gender mainstreaming;
   - l. developing a gender-based information system; and
   - m. providing and organizing integrated or non-integrated services for women victims of violence, female workers, elderly women, and women with disabilities.
2. Further provisions regarding the forms of women's empowerment and gender mainstreaming mechanisms as referred to in paragraph (1) shall be regulated by Regent Regulation.

### Paragraph 3

Community Participation

#### Article 12

1. To carry out the empowerment of women as referred to in Article 6, the Regional Government may involve community participation.
2. Community participation as referred to in paragraph (1) shall be carried out by individuals, Community Social Institutions, religious institutions and/or other legitimate institutions.

Second Part Child Protection

### Paragraph 1

Rights and Obligations of Children

#### Article 13

Every child has the right to obtain:

   - a. birth registration;
   - b. health;
   - c. education; and
   - d. social welfare.

#### Article 14

Every child has the obligation to:

   - a. respect parents, guardians and teachers;
   - b. love family, society and care for friends;
   - c. love the homeland, nation and state;
   - d. perform worship in accordance with their religion and belief;
   - e. study and develop themselves in accordance with their abilities and talents, interests; and
   - f. have noble character.

### Paragraph 2

Birth Registration

#### Article 15

The Implementation of Fulfillment of Children's Rights in the field of birth registration, as referred to in Article 13 letter a, the Regional Government is obligated to administer the registration of children's births with the issuance of birth certificates in accordance with the provisions of laws and regulations.

### Paragraph 3

Health

#### Article 16

1. The Implementation of Fulfillment of Children's Rights in the health sector as referred to in Article 13 letter b, the Regional Government is obligated to provide facilities and organize comprehensive health services for children, so that every child obtains an optimal degree of health from the womb which is carried out in stages adjusted to the regional financial capacity and supported by community participation.
2. Comprehensive health services as referred to in paragraph (1) include:
   - a. promotive, preventive, curative and rehabilitative efforts for basic health services;
   - b. exemption from the burden of costs for malnourished children; children undergoing dialysis due to kidney failure; children suffering from thalassemia major; children with disabilities; children with special needs; street children; children infected with HIV/AIDS; child workers; children who are victims of abuse of narcotics, alcohol, psychotropic substances and other addictive substances (NAPZA); and children who are victims of violence, such as neglect, economic exploitation, sexual exploitation, and victims of human trafficking.
3. Children as referred to in paragraph (2) are children from poor families except as otherwise regulated in accordance with the health financing program established by the government.

#### Article 17

The Regional Government, the community, the family, and parents are obligated to make efforts so that children who are born are protected from diseases that threaten survival and/or cause disability.

### Paragraph 4

Education

#### Article 18

1. The Implementation of Fulfillment of Children's Rights in the field of education as referred to in Article 13 letter c, the Regional Government guarantees the implementation of a 12 (twelve) year compulsory education program for all children.
2. Every organizer of an education unit is obligated to provide opportunities to children to obtain educational services without discrimination.

#### Article 19

Every organizer of an educational unit is prohibited from removing a child from an educational institution unless there is a guarantee for the continuity of the child's education.

#### Article 20

Every education unit organizer is obligated to coordinate with relevant child protection agencies if they find a child dropping out of school due to being a victim of violence.

#### Article 21

Children with disabilities and children with special needs are given equal opportunities to obtain formal, non-formal and informal education services, including special education and inclusion.

#### Article 22

Early Childhood Education for children aged 0 (zero) to 3 (three) years and 4 (four) to 6 (six) years may be organized by Posyandu institutions, Taman Posyandu, Early Childhood Education Posts and similar Early Childhood Education unit institutions.

#### Article 23

For children who do not pursue formal education, they may pursue education through nonformal education units, including:

   - a. For children who are 14 (fourteen) years of age and above and do not pursue formal elementary school (SD) education or equivalent, they may pursue nonformal education Package A or equivalent to Elementary School (SD)/Madrasah Ibtidaiyah (MI);
   - b. For children who are 17 (seventeen) years of age and above and do not pursue formal junior high school (SMP) education or equivalent, they may pursue nonformal education Package B or equivalent to Junior High School (SMP)/Madrasah Tsanawiyah (MTs); or
   - c. For children who are 21 (twenty one) years of age and above and do not pursue formal senior high school (SMA) education or equivalent, they may pursue nonformal education Package C or equivalent to Senior High School (SMA)/Vocational High School (SMK)/Madrasah Aliyah/Madrasah Aliyah Kejuruan (MAK);

### Paragraph 5

Social Welfare

#### Article 24

1. The implementation of Protection and empowerment of children in the field of social welfare as referred to in Article 13 letter d, the Regional Government is obligated to provide social welfare for:
   - a. children in conflict with the law;
   - b. child victims of violence who are exploited economically and sexually, neglected children, child victims of kidnapping and child victims of human trafficking;
   - c. child victims of narcotics abuse, alcohol, psychotropic substances and other addictive substances (NAPZA);
   - d. children infected with HIV/AIDS;
   - e. children who do not have parents and are cared for by other parties or families who are unable;
   - f. street children;
   - g. child victims of natural disasters or social disasters;
   - h. children with disabilities;
      - i. children of migrant worker families;
   - j. children living in or around prostitution locations; and
   - k. child victims of other maltreatment.
2. Social welfare as referred to in paragraph (1) may be in the form of provision of services:
   - a. health;
   - b. education;
   - c. legal aid;
   - d. assistance;
   - e. social, mental and spiritual guidance;
   - f. social rehabilitation;
   - g. empowerment;
   - h. social assistance; and/or
      - i. reintegration.

#### Article 25

1. Regional Government is obligated to protect the fulfillment of the rights of child workers in the informal sector.
2. Child Workers in the informal sector as referred to in paragraph (1) include: a domestic workers;

b shoe shiners;

c street vendors;

d scavengers;

e parking attendants;

f children who work in the field of agriculture, livestock and plantations;

g children who work in the field of mining; and h other informal sector work that employs children.

3. Every person who employs children in the informal sector is obligated to observe the requirements: a minimum age of 15 (fifteen) years, except for the category of Worst Forms of Child Labor minimum 18 (eighteen) years;

b obtain written consent from the parent/guardian of the child worker;

c have a written work agreement between the employer and the parent/guardian of the child worker and obtain validation from the authorized agency;

d not employed at night;

e working time of maximum 3 (three) hours per day;

f not employed in a place or environment that can disrupt the child's growth and development process, whether physical, mental, moral and intellectual or the child's health;

g provide opportunities to obtain education in accordance with their talents and interests;

h employ for light types of work; and i provide one day off per week.

#### Article 26

Fulfillment of Children's Rights as referred to in Article 25 shall be in the form of:

   - a. counseling to stakeholders regarding children's rights;
   - b. assistance with psychological, medical and legal services and socio-economic reintegration;
   - c. family empowerment through provision of training, business capital stimulants and mentoring;
   - d. scholarships to continue formal education to higher levels; and
   - e. non-formal education and skills training for those not pursuing formal education.

Third Part Forms of Violence

#### Article 27

Forms of violence include:

   - a. physical violence;
   - b. psychological violence;
   - c. sexual violence;
   - d. neglect;
   - e. exploitation; and/or
   - f. other violence.

#### Article 28

Physical violence as referred to in Article 27 letter a is caused by acts that result in pain, injury, wounds or disability to a person's body, miscarriage, loss of consciousness and/or causing death.

#### Article 29

Psychological violence as referred to in Article 27 letter b is caused by acts that result in fear, loss of self-confidence, loss of the ability to act, feelings of helplessness and/or severe psychological suffering in a person.

#### Article 30

Sexual violence as referred to in Article 27 letter c is caused by:

   - a. acts in the form of sexual harassment;
   - b. forced sexual intercourse;
   - c. forced sexual intercourse in an unnatural or unwanted manner; and/or
   - d. forced sexual intercourse with another person for commercial purposes and/or specific purposes.

#### Article 31

Neglect as referred to in Article 27 point d is caused by:

   - a. acts that result in the failure to fulfill the needs of a child properly, whether physical, mental, spiritual or social, committed by parents, guardians, or any other party responsible for their care;
   - b. acts of deliberately neglecting to maintain, care for, or look after a child as appropriate, committed by parents, guardians, or other parties responsible for their care;
   - c. acts of neglecting a person within the scope of their household, whereas according to the law applicable to them or due to an agreement or contract they are obligated to provide livelihood, care, or maintenance to that person; and/or
   - d. acts that result in economic dependence by means of restricting and/or prohibiting the victim from working properly inside or outside the home so that the victim is under the control of that person.

#### Article 32

Exploitation as referred to in Article 27 letter e occurs due to:

   - a. acts that exploit economically or sexually with the intention of benefiting oneself or others;
   - b. acts which, with or without the consent of the victim, include among others prostitution, forced labor or services, slavery or similar practices, oppression, extortion, exploitation of the physical body, sexual organs, reproductive organs, or unlawfully removing or transplanting organs and/or body tissues, or exploiting the labor or capabilities of a person by other parties to obtain material or immaterial benefits; and/or
   - c. all forms of exploitation of the victim’s sexual organs or other body organs to obtain benefits, including but not limited to all activities of prostitution or indecent acts.

#### Article 33

Other violence as referred to in Article 27 letter f is caused by:

   - a. threat of violence includes: any unlawful act in the form of speech, writing, images, symbols, or body movements, whether with or without using means that cause fear or restrict a person's fundamental freedom; and
   - b. coercion, includes: a situation where a person/victim is ordered to do something in such a way that the person does something contrary to their own will.

## Part Four

Prevention of Violence against Women and Children

#### Article 34

1. Efforts to prevent violence against women and children shall be carried out in an integrated manner by the Regional Government and coordinated by the technical SKPD.
2. Prevention efforts as referred to in paragraph (1) shall be implemented by:
   - a. conducting guidance towards harmonious household integrity;
   - b. establishing a working network in efforts to prevent violence;
   - c. conducting coordination, integration, synchronization of violence prevention based on partnership patterns;
   - d. establishing a violence prevention system;
   - e. conducting socialization regarding laws and regulations related to the protection of women and children victims of violence; and
   - f. providing critical education regarding the rights of women and children for the community.

#### Article 35

In addition to Regional Governments, prevention efforts as referred to in Article 34 paragraph (1) shall be carried out by:

   - a. family and/or closest relatives;
   - b. community;
   - c. educational institutions; and
   - d. Non-Governmental Organizations.

Fifth Part Handling of Women and Children Victims of Violence

#### Article 36

The implementation of handling for women and children who are victims of violence is carried out in a manner that is:

   - a. prompt;
   - b. safe and comfortable;
   - c. with a sense of empathy;
   - d. non-discriminatory;
   - e. easily accessible;
   - f. free of charge; and
   - g. with confidentiality guaranteed.

#### Article 37

Forms of handling for women and children victims of violence include:

   - a. complaint services, consultation, and counseling;
   - b. assistance services;
   - c. medical rehabilitation services;
   - d. social rehabilitation services;
   - e. legal services; and
   - f. social reintegration services.

#### Article 38

Complaint, consultation, and counseling services as referred to in Article 37 letter a include:

   - a. identification or initial recording of victims;
   - b. Case identification; and
   - c. consent for action to be taken.

#### Article 39

Assistance services as referred to in Article 37 letter b include:

   - a. accompanying victims during the examination and health recovery process;
   - b. accompanying victims during the examination process at the police, prosecutor's office and court;
   - c. monitoring the interests and rights of victims in the examination process at the police, prosecutor's office and court;
   - d. protecting the privacy and confidentiality of victims from all parties who have no interest, including reporting by mass media;
   - e. providing a sense of security to victims; and
   - f. providing continuous treatment up to the rehabilitation stage.

#### Article 40

Medical rehabilitation services as referred to in Article 37 letter c include:

   - a. first aid to victims;
   - b. treatment and recovery of physical injuries aimed at restoring the physical condition of victims carried out by medical personnel and paramedics; and
   - c. referral to health services.

#### Article 41

1. Social rehabilitation services as referred to in Article 37 letter d constitute services provided in order to restore the traumatic condition of victims.
2. The form of services as referred to in paragraph (1) is through social support.
3. Social support as referred to in paragraph (2) is conducted through:
   - a. spiritual guidance to victims; and
   - b. psychological recovery of victims.

#### Article 42

Legal services as referred to in Article 37 letter e are to assist victims in undergoing the judicial process by:

   - a. providing legal consultation that includes information regarding the rights of victims and the judicial process;
   - b. accompanying victims at the investigation, prosecution, and examination levels in court proceedings and assisting victims to comprehensively describe the violence they have experienced; and
   - c. coordinating with fellow law enforcers, volunteer assistants, and social workers so that the judicial process proceeds as it should.

#### Article 43

1. Social reintegration services as referred to in Article 37 letter f aim to return victims to their families and social environment.
2. Social reintegration services as referred to in paragraph (1) are carried out by the Regional Government in coordination with: a. related agencies and institutions both government and non-government; and
   - b. village community institutions and/or local community leaders.

# CHAPTER IV

## INTEGRATED SERVICE CENTER

#### Article 44

1. The provision of services to women and children victims of violence shall be carried out in an integrated manner by the PPT.
2. The PPT as referred to in paragraph (1) is an integrated part and constitutes one entity with the PPT that provides integrated services for witnesses and/or victims of criminal acts of human trafficking.
3. The provision of services to women and children victims of violence as referred to in paragraph (1) includes: a. providing fast and free services to victims; b. providing protection and fulfillment of victims' rights to health rehabilitation, social rehabilitation, repatriation, social reintegration and legal assistance;
   - c. cooperating with certain institutions in providing translators and volunteer assistants required for victims;
   - d. establishing networks with government or private hospitals for treatment and health recovery of victims and cooperating with witness and victim protection institutions, social protection houses or trauma centers owned by the government, community or other institutions for the health recovery of victims;
   - e. providing convenience, comfort and safety for victims;
   - f. maintaining the confidentiality of victims; and
   - g. providing fulfillment of legal assistance for victims.
4. Further provisions regarding the procedures and mechanisms for implementing the PPT shall be regulated by Regent Regulation.

# CHAPTER V

## RIGHTS OF WOMEN AND CHILDREN VICTIMS OF VIOLENCE

#### Article 45

Every Woman and Child victim of violence receives the following rights:

   - a. the right to complaint handling;
   - b. the right to have their dignity and worth as human beings respected;
   - c. the right to obtain protection from family, community, regional government and/or other parties either temporarily or based on a protection order from the court;
   - d. the right to health and psychological recovery from the suffering experienced by the victim;
   - e. the right to determine their own decisions;
   - f. the right to special handling related to the confidentiality of the victim;
   - g. the right to obtain information;
   - h. the right to confidentiality of their identity;
      - i. the right to social rehabilitation;
   - j. the right to assistance by social workers and legal aid at every level of the examination process in accordance with statutory regulations; and
   - k. the right to spiritual guidance services.

#### Article 46

Women and children victims of violence, in addition to receiving the rights as referred to in Article 45, also receive special rights, as follows:

   - a. right to survival;
   - b. right to grow and develop;
   - c. right to receive equal protection; and
   - d. right to participate in community life.

# CHAPTER VI

## PROHIBITIONS

#### Article 47

1. Every business operation whose activities may disrupt the growth and development of children is prohibited from accepting child visitors.
2. Business operations as referred to in paragraph (1) include:
   - a. discotheque;
   - b. nightclub;
   - c. bar;
   - d. adult karaoke;
   - e. pub;
   - f. massage parlor;
   - g. steam bath/sauna parlor; and
   - h. other similar business fields.

#### Article 48

Every operator of hotels, motels, inns, tourism guesthouse businesses and similar business activities is prohibited from renting rooms to children without being accompanied by parents or adult family members or supervising teachers/persons in charge in the context of carrying out school activities or other activities.

#### Article 49

1. Every operator of internet service business, play station and types of children's games based on other information and communication technology is obligated to manage and design a child-friendly place.
2. Every business operator as referred to in paragraph (1) must: a arrange rooms/booths with sufficient lighting and not closed off;

b not accept children wearing uniforms and/or during school hours;

c locally block sites containing elements of pornography and pornographic acts; and d limit visiting hours and supervise the use of existing facilities.

# CHAPTER VII

## MONITORING AND EVALUATION

#### Article 50

1. To ensure synergy, balance and effectiveness of integrated measures in the implementation of policies, programs, activities, protection of Women and children, the Government conducts monitoring.
2. Monitoring as referred to in paragraph (1) shall be implemented by the Technical SKPD and PPT.
3. Monitoring as referred to in paragraph (1) is to determine the progress and obstacles in the implementation of policies, programs and activities for the Protection of Women and children.
4. Monitoring shall be conducted periodically through coordination and direct monitoring of SKPDs that implement policies, activities for the protection of Women and children victims of violence.
5. Monitoring shall be conducted from planning through to implementation of policies, programs and activities for the protection of women and children every year.

#### Article 51

1. Evaluation of the implementation of policies, programs and activities for the Protection of Women and children victims of violence shall be conducted at the end of each Fiscal year or if necessary according to needs.
2. The results of the evaluation of the implementation of policies, programs and activities for the protection of Women and children victims of violence shall be used as input material for the formulation of policies, programs and activities for the protection of Women and children victims of violence for the following year.
3. Evaluation as referred to in paragraph (1) shall be implemented in accordance with the provisions of laws and regulations.

# CHAPTER VIII

## FINANCING

#### Article 52

Funding for the implementation of Protection for Women and children, is sourced from:

   - a. Regional revenue and expenditure budget; and/or
   - b. Other legal and non-binding sources in accordance with the provisions of laws and regulations.

# CHAPTER IX

## GUIDANCE AND SUPERVISION

#### Article 53

1. The Regent conducts guidance and supervision over the implementation of this regional regulation.
2. Guidance and supervision as referred to in paragraph (1) includes providing implementation instructions, guidance, supervision, monitoring and evaluation of the Implementation of Protection of Women and Children.

# CHAPTER X

## COMMUNITY PARTICIPATION

#### Article 54

1. In the implementation of Protection for Women and children, the community may:
   - a. establish family partners at the Kelurahan/Village level by the community;
   - b. conduct socialization of women's and children's rights independently;
   - c. provide first aid to victims; and
   - d. report to the authorized agency if violence against victims occurs in their environment.
2. Community participation as referred to in paragraph (1) shall be carried out by individuals, community institutions, non-governmental organizations, educational institutions, religious institutions, private sector and/or mass media.

# CHAPTER XI

## ADMINISTRATIVE SANCTIONS

#### Article 55

1. Every person or business entity that violates the provisions as referred to in Article 47, Article 48, Article 49 shall be subject to administrative sanctions in the form of: a Written warning;

b Suspension of permit;

c Administrative fine; and d Revocation of permit.

2. Written warning as referred to in paragraph (1) letter a shall be given 3 (three) times with a grace period between one warning and another warning of at least 1 (one) month.
3. Suspension of Permit as referred to in paragraph (1) letter b shall be imposed if written warnings have been given as referred to in paragraph (2).
4. Suspension of Permit as referred to in paragraph (1) letter b may be withdrawn if the person or business entity committing the violation pays an administrative fine of Rp. 30,000,000,- (thirty million rupiah).
5. If within the period specified in the suspension of permit, the administrative fine is not paid, then revocation of permit shall be carried out.

# CHAPTER XII

## CLOSING PROVISIONS

#### Article 56

At the latest 1 (one) year, calculated from the effective date of this Regional Regulation, the Implementing Regulations of this Regional Regulation must have been stipulated.

#### Article 57

This Regional Regulation shall come into force on the date of promulgation.

In order that every person may know of it, the promulgation of this Regional Regulation is ordered by its placement in the Regional Gazette of Nunukan Regency.
