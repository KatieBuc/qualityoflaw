# CONCERNING

# WOMEN'S EMPOWERMENT AND CHILD PROTECTION

# BY THE GRACE OF ALMIGHTY GOD

# THE REGENT OF BUOL,

Considering : a. that every citizen has equal rights to live a dignified life in accordance with the principles of humanity, equality, and justice;

   - b. that to guarantee women's empowerment and child protection in accordance with human dignity and to receive protection from violence, discrimination and other violations, women's empowerment and child protection must be carried out in an integrated and sustainable manner through the acceleration of fulfillment and protection of the rights of women and children in their personal lives, family, community, nation and state;
   - c. that in accordance with the provisions of Article 12 paragraph (2) letter b of Law Number 23 Year 2014 concerning Regional Government as has been amended several times most recently by Law Number 9 Year 2015 concerning the Second Amendment to Law Number 23 Year 2014 concerning Regional Government, women's empowerment and child protection is one of the mandatory government affairs that is the authority of the Regional Government;
   - d. that based on the considerations as referred to in letter a, letter b, and letter c, it is necessary to establish a Regional Regulation concerning Women's Empowerment and Child Protection;

# CHAPTER I GENERAL PROVISIONS

#### Article 1

In this Regional Regulation, what is meant by:

1. Region is Buol Regency.
2. Regional Government is the head of the Region as an element of the Regional Government administrator who leads the implementation of Government Affairs that are the authority of the autonomous Region.
3. Regent is the Regent of Buol Regency.
4. Regional Apparatus hereinafter abbreviated as PD is the Regional Apparatus within the Government of Buol Regency.
5. Women's empowerment is an effort to enable women to obtain access and control over resources, economic, political, social, and cultural so that women can manage themselves and increase self-confidence to be able to play a role and participate actively in solving problems, so as to be able to build capacity and self-concept.
6. Women's protection is all efforts to guarantee and protect women's rights so that they can live healthy and participate optimally in accordance with human dignity and receive protection from neglect, violence, and discrimination, whether in the form of preventive, repressive or post-remedial protection (assistance and treatment).
7. Child protection is all activities to guarantee and protect children and their rights so that they can live, grow, develop and participate, optimally in accordance with human dignity, and receive protection from violence and discrimination.
8. Child is a person who has not reached the age of 18 (eighteen) years, including children who are still in the womb and are not married.
9. Prevention is direct efforts made by the Regional Government, community and parents and/or family to prevent violations of the rights of women and children.
10. Violence is any act that results in misery or suffering physically, sexually, psychologically and/or domestic neglect including threats to commit acts, coercion, or deprivation of a person's freedom unlawfully.
11. Children's forum is a forum for children's participation formed with the aim of facilitating children's involvement in the decision-making process and enjoying changes relating to their lives either directly or indirectly, which is carried out with the consent and will of all children based on awareness and understanding, in accordance with age and level of thinking maturity.
12. Child-Friendly Regency hereinafter abbreviated as KLA is a Regency that has a child rights-based development system through the integration of commitment and resources of government, community and business world that are planned comprehensively and sustainably in policies, programs and activities to ensure the fulfillment of children's rights.
13. Neglected child is a child whose needs are not properly fulfilled, whether physical, mental, spiritual or social.
14. Social welfare is a condition of fulfillment of material, spiritual, and social needs of citizens so that they can live properly and are able to develop themselves, so that they can carry out their social functions.
15. Gender mainstreaming hereinafter abbreviated as PUG is a strategy built to integrate gender into an integral dimension of planning, formulation, implementation, monitoring and evaluation of national development program policies.
16. Community is individuals, families, groups and social organizations and/or community organizations.
17. Women victims of violence are women who experience actions that result in misery or suffering physically, sexually or psychologically, including threats of certain actions, coercion or arbitrary deprivation of freedom, whether occurring in the public sphere or in private life.
18. Child victims of violence are children who experience actions that result in misery or suffering physically, mentally, sexually, psychologically or suffering that threatens life, body and degrades human dignity.
19. Human trafficking is the act of recruitment, transportation, harboring, sending, transfer, or receipt of a person by threat of violence, use of violence, kidnapping, confinement, forgery, fraud, abuse of power or vulnerable position, debt bondage or giving payment or benefits, so as to obtain consent from the person who holds control over that other person, whether carried out within the Country or between Countries.
20. Complaint handling is a series of actions taken by integrated service providers to follow up on reports of acts of violence against women and children, submitted by victims, families or the community.
21. Social rehabilitation is a service aimed at restoring and developing the ability of a person experiencing social dysfunction so that they can carry out their social functions properly.
22. Law enforcement is the action of apparatus authorized by the state to implement statutory regulations.
23. Focal Point PUG is OPD apparatus who have the ability to carry out gender mainstreaming in their respective work units.
24. Legal aid is legal services provided by legal assistants and advocates to carry out the process of providing assistance to witnesses and/or victims of violence against women and children that is gender sensitive.
25. Repatriation is an effort to return women and child victims of violence from abroad to the debarkation point/entry point, or from the receiving Region to the Region of origin.
26. Social reintegration is an effort to reunite victims with family, substitute family, or community that can provide protection and fulfillment of needs for victims.
27. Safe house is a temporary shelter that aims to provide protection and a sense of security to victims of violence.
28. Shelter home is a forum for guidance and welfare services for street children that carries out skills assistance/guidance activities to ensure that children do not carry out activities on the streets so that they can grow and develop normally.
29. Spiritual guidance is an effort for psychological and systematic recovery.
30. Gender responsive is consistent and systematic attention to the differences between women and men in society accompanied by efforts to eliminate structural and cultural barriers in achieving gender equality.
31. Gender sensitivity or gender awareness is the ability to identify gaps in power relations between women and men in the family and in the community, the impact of gender-based division of labor on women and men, and the experiences, problems, needs, interests and aspirations of women and men are different.
32. Public service provider is every state organizing institution, corporation, independent institution formed based on Law for public service activities, and other legal entities formed solely for public service activities.
33. Integrated Service Center for Women's Empowerment and Child Protection hereinafter abbreviated as P2TP2A is an integrated service center in efforts to empower women in various fields of development, as well as protection of women and children from various types of discrimination and acts of violence, including human trafficking, which is formed by the government or community-based, which includes: referral center, business consultation center, reproductive health consultation center, integrated crisis center (PKT), trauma recovery center (trauma center), women's crisis handling center (women crisis center), training center, science and technology information center (PIPTEK), safe house (shelter), shelter home, children's friend telephone (TESSA) 129 or other forms.
34. Community Organization is an organization established and formed by the community voluntarily based on common aspirations, will, needs, interests, activities and goals to participate in development for the achievement of the goals of the Unitary State of the Republic of Indonesia based on Pancasila.
35. Parents are biological father and/or mother, or step father and/or mother, or adoptive father and/or mother.
36. Family is the smallest unit in society consisting of husband and wife or husband, wife and their children, or father and his children, or mother and her children, or blood relatives in a straight line upward or downward up to the third degree.
37. Customs are local wisdom values that apply and are still applied from generation to generation in the community environment.
38. Child-Friendly Regency Task Force is a coordinating institution at the regency level that coordinates policy efforts, programs and activities to realize a child-friendly regency.
39. Regional Action Plan for Child-Friendly Regency Development is a document containing policies, programs and activities to realize a Child-Friendly Regency.

# CHAPTER II

## RIGHTS OF WOMEN AND CHILDREN

#### Article 2

Every woman and child has basic rights as a human being that must be protected, promoted, upheld and fulfilled in accordance with the provisions of laws and regulations.

#### Article 3

The rights of women as referred to in Article 2 include:

   - a. to live peacefully, safely, tranquilly, happily, prosperously both physically and spiritually;
   - b. to enjoy a good and healthy living environment;
   - c. to live in a family within the bonds of a legal marriage based on the provisions of laws and regulations;
   - d. to obtain work in accordance with ability, requirements and fair and proper wages;
   - e. specifically for women with disabilities, the right to obtain facilities and special treatment in public services;
   - f. to play an active role in the field of politics and government in accordance with their potential;
   - g. to obtain legal information and services;
   - h. to obtain services to increase their income;
      - i. to obtain other rights in accordance with their humanitarian benefits based on the provisions of laws and regulations;

#### Article 4

The rights of children as referred to in Article 2 include:

   - a. obtaining education and teaching in accordance with age level, physical and mental condition, intelligence as well as interests and talents;
   - b. living, growing, developing, and participating reasonably in accordance with human dignity and receiving protection from acts of violence, exploitation, discrimination and neglect, cruelty, violence and persecution, injustice and mistreatment;
   - c. having needs fulfilled by the family;
   - d. receiving religious guidance;
   - e. obtaining their identity;
   - f. obtaining other rights in accordance with the provisions of laws and regulations;
   - g. specifically for children with disabilities, having the right to receive facilities and special treatment in their services;

#### Article 5

In addition to the rights of women and children as referred to in Article 3 and Article 4, women and children victims of acts of violence have the right to:

   - a. have their dignity respected as human beings;
   - b. the right to file complaints and receive fast and affordable services;
   - c. obtain information and legal certainty;
   - d. confidentiality regarding the case they are undergoing;
   - e. compensation for the case they are undergoing;
   - f. obtain facilitation in the judicial process;
   - g. obtain assistance;
   - h. obtain physical health recovery and oshis from the suffering experienced by the victim;
      - i. determine their own decisions;
   - j. obtain rehabilitation and social reintegration;
   - k. obtain respect and services for survival, growth and development;
   - l. obtain basic services;
   - m. be free from negative stigma; and
   - n. obtain freedom to determine choices and decide for themselves following their parents' divorce;

#### Article 6

The implementation of women's empowerment and child protection is based on the principles of:

   - a. the best interests of women and children;
   - b. gender justice and gender equality;
   - c. local wisdom;
   - d. law enforcement and legal certainty;
   - e. non-discrimination;
   - f. the right to life, survival, and development;
   - g. respect for the views of the child; and

# CHAPTER III

## IMPROVING THE QUALITY OF LIFE OF WOMEN

## Part One

General

#### Article 7

1. In order to improve the quality of life of women, the regional government is responsible for:
   - a. respecting and guaranteeing the rights of women without discrimination in accordance with human dignity and worth;
   - b. implementing gender mainstreaming;
   - c. establishing a gender mainstreaming working group;
   - d. enhancing the empowerment of women in terms of role, quality and capacity in the fields of:
1. politics;
2. law;
3. social; and
4. economy.
   - e. strengthening and developing institutions providing women's empowerment services in the Region;
2. The responsibility of the Regional Government in efforts to improve the quality of life of women as referred to in paragraph (1) shall be carried out by the Regional Apparatus (PD) that administers governmental affairs in the field of women's empowerment and child protection.

Second Part Implementation of Gender Mainstreaming

#### Article 8

1. The Regent is responsible for the implementation of gender mainstreaming.
2. The responsibility of the Regent as referred to in paragraph (1) may be delegated to the Vice Regent.

#### Article 9

In implementing gender mainstreaming as referred to in Article 7 paragraph (1) letter b, the Regent shall designate the PD that administers governmental affairs in the field of women's and children's empowerment as the coordinator for the implementation of gender mainstreaming.

Third Part Formation of the Gender Mainstreaming Working Group

#### Article 10

1. In the effort to accelerate the institutionalization of gender mainstreaming, working groups for gender mainstreaming shall be formed in all PD.
2. Members of the gender mainstreaming working group are all heads/leaders of PD.
3. The Regent shall appoint the Head of PD who administers Government Affairs in the field of Regional Development Planning as Chair of the gender mainstreaming Working Group and the Head of PD who administers government affairs in the field of women's empowerment and Child Protection as Head of the Secretariat of the gender mainstreaming Working Group.
4. The formation of the gender mainstreaming working group as referred to in paragraph (1) shall be stipulated by a Regent's Decree.

#### Article 11

The gender mainstreaming working group as referred to in Article 10 has the following duties:

   - a. promoting and facilitating gender mainstreaming to each PD;
   - b. conducting socialization and advocacy of gender mainstreaming to Sub-district Heads and Village Heads;
   - c. preparing annual programs;
   - d. encouraging the realization of gender-responsive planning and budgeting;
   - e. preparing the work plan of the gender mainstreaming working group every year;
   - f. being responsible to the Regent through the Vice Regent;
   - g. formulating policy recommendations to the Regent;
   - h. preparing the Regional Gender Profile;
      - i. conducting monitoring of gender mainstreaming in each agency;
   - j. establishing a technical team to conduct analysis of the regional budget;
   - k. preparing a regional action plan for gender mainstreaming; and
   - l. encouraging the implementation of selection and determination of gender mainstreaming focal points in each PD.

#### Article 12

1. The Technical Team as referred to in Article 11 letter j shall consist of officials who understand gender-responsive budget analysis.
2. The Regional Action Plan for Gender Mainstreaming as referred to in Article 11 letter k shall contain:
   - a. gender mainstreaming in laws and regulations;
   - b. gender mainstreaming in the development cycle;
   - c. strengthening of gender mainstreaming institutions; and
   - d. strengthening of community participation.

#### Article 13

1. The gender mainstreaming focal point in each PD as referred to in Article 11 letter 1 consists of officials and/or staff in charge of planning and/or program duties.
2. The gender mainstreaming focal point as referred to in paragraph (1), has the following duties:
   - a. promoting gender mainstreaming in the work unit;
   - b. facilitating the preparation of work plans and budgeting of PD that are gender responsive;
   - c. implementing training, socialization, advocacy of gender mainstreaming to all officials and staff within the PD;
   - d. reporting the implementation of gender mainstreaming to the leadership of PD;
   - e. encouraging the implementation of gender analysis on policies, programs, and activities in the work unit; and
   - f. facilitating the preparation of gender data in each PD.
3. The gender mainstreaming focal point as referred to in paragraph (1), shall be selected and determined by the PD.

Fourth Part Enhancement of the Role, Quality, and Capability of Women

### Paragraph 1

Political Sector

#### Article 14

Efforts to increase the role, quality, and capability of women in the political field as referred to in Article 7 paragraph (1) letter d number 1 include:

   - a. conducting outreach/dissemination of information to increase women's political participation in general elections;
   - b. implementing programs that encourage the level of women's involvement in political processes and public office;
   - c. facilitating women's self-development in organizing, forming associations, and gathering;
   - d. involving women in decision-making concerning women's interests;

### Paragraph 2

Legal Field

#### Article 15

Efforts to enhance the role, quality and capability of women in the field of law as referred to in Article 7 paragraph (1) letter d number 2 include:

   - a. conducting socialization to increase legal knowledge and awareness of women;
   - b. conducting mapping and reviewing gender-biased policies;
   - c. involving women's organizations in the process of formulating and discussing regional legal products related to women's participation.

### Paragraph 3

Social Sector

#### Article 16

Efforts to increase the role, quality and capability of women in the social field as referred to in Article 7 paragraph (1) letter d number 3 include:

   - a. improvement of knowledge and skills to encourage the fulfillment of education in accordance with existing potential;
   - b. improvement and fulfillment of health quality for women;
   - c. improvement of awareness and knowledge about independent, healthy and prosperous family planning including access to consultation services and marriage registration;
   - d. improvement of women's awareness of the impact of early marriage;
   - e. facilitating and endeavoring to preserve customs and the development of science, technology, arts and culture for the advancement of women;
   - f. conducting improvement of women's awareness and knowledge regarding independent, healthy and prosperous family planning;
   - g. conducting programs to reduce maternal mortality rates;
   - h. encouraging the establishment of women's organizations.

### Paragraph 4

Economic Sector

#### Article 17

Efforts to increase the role, quality and capability of women in the economic field as referred to in Article 7 paragraph (1) letter d number 4 include:

   - a. implementing programs, activities, and training to improve women's skills and expertise in order to realize an improvement in women's welfare;
   - b. facilitating the strengthening of business capital for women with weak economic conditions;
   - c. marketing products resulting from the businesses of women with weak economic conditions;
   - d. training to develop women's capabilities through economic empowerment of pre-welfare (underprivileged) families;
   - e. comprehensive entrepreneurship training for women.

# CHAPTER IV

## PROTECTION OF WOMEN

## Part One

General

#### Article 18

1. Regional Governments in the effort to protect women are responsible for:
   - a. establishing and implementing policies, programs and activities for the protection of women;
   - b. undertaking prevention efforts against the occurrence of acts of violence against women including criminal acts of human trafficking;
   - c. increasing the effectiveness of services for women victims of acts of violence;
   - d. increasing protection efforts for women workers;
   - e. strengthening and developing institutions providing women protection services;
   - f. conducting cooperation with the Government, other Regional Governments, private parties, the business sector and/or community non-governmental organizations in efforts to prevent the occurrence of acts of violence against women; and
   - g. involving the active role of families, customary leaders, religious leaders, the community and community social institutions in the field of women protection.
2. The responsibility of Regional Governments in the effort to protect women as referred to in paragraph (1) shall be implemented by the Regional Apparatus that administers Government Affairs in the field of women and child empowerment and in its implementation may coordinate with related agencies.

## Part Two

Prevention of Acts of Violence Against Women

#### Article 19

In order to prevent the occurrence of acts of violence against women, Regional Governments are obligated to:

   - a. conduct socialization regarding the prevention of violence against women by optimizing the role of customary, cultural, religious institutions, and community organizations;
   - b. provide guidelines on handling acts of violence against women;
   - c. involve community participation in recovery and reintegration;
   - d. develop an integrated database and information system on acts of violence against women;
   - e. strengthen the function of community organizations engaged in the empowerment of women and children; and
   - f. provide public facilities that are comfortable and safe for women.

Third Part Effectiveness of Services for Women Victims of Violence

### Paragraph 1

General

#### Article 20

1. Protection of women victims of acts of violence is implemented through integrated services.
2. The provision of integrated services to women victims of acts of violence as referred to in paragraph (1) uses the principles of:
   - a. gender responsiveness;
   - b. non-discrimination;
   - c. equal and respectful relationships;
   - d. maintaining confidentiality;
   - e. providing a sense of security and comfort;
   - f. respecting individual differences;
   - g. non-judgmental;
   - h. respecting the choices and decisions of victims;
      - i. sensitivity to the background and conditions of victims and the use of language that is appropriate and understood by victims;
   - j. fast and simple;
   - k. empathy; and
   - l. fulfillment of women's rights.

#### Article 21

The form of integrated services for women victims of acts of violence includes:

   - a. complaint handling;
   - b. health rehabilitation;
   - c. social rehabilitation;
   - d. law enforcement and legal assistance; and
   - e. repatriation and social reintegration.

### Paragraph 2

Complaint Handling

#### Article 22

Integrated complaint handling services as referred to in Article 21 letter a shall be implemented by:

   - a. hospitals;
   - b. community health centers;
   - c. women's service units;
   - d. trauma center shelters;
   - e. women crisis centers;
   - f. advisory board for marriage guidance and preservation;
   - g. police;
   - h. prosecutor's office;
      - i. courts;
   - j. ministry of religious affairs; and/or
   - k. legal aid institutions.
1. Complaints regarding acts of violence against women may be filed by reporters consisting of victims, families, communities and/or community organizations in the field of protection of women and children.
2. Reporters as referred to in paragraph (1) may file complaints:
   - a. directly;
   - b. by telephone; and/or
   - c. by letter.
3. In addition to handling complaints submitted by reporters, officers at service institutions as referred to in Article 22 are also authorized to handle victims of acts of violence against women based on other information.
4. Further provisions regarding procedures for complaint handling services as referred to in paragraph (1) and paragraph (2) shall be regulated in Regent Regulation.

### Paragraph 3

Health Rehabilitation

#### Article 24

1. Health rehabilitation as referred to in Article 21 letter b constitutes efforts that include aspects of:
   - a. promotive;
   - b. preventive;
   - c. curative; and
   - d. rehabilitative.
2. Health rehabilitation as referred to in paragraph (1) shall be conducted comprehensively, accessible 24 (twenty-four) hours at the Regional Public Hospital, with quality and conducted in accordance with health service standards.
3. Women victims of acts of violence have the right to free health services, including:
   - a. medical services;
   - b. medicolegal services; and
   - c. visum.
4. Further provisions regarding the procedures for implementing health services as referred to in paragraph (3) shall be regulated in a Regent Regulation.

### Paragraph 4

Social Rehabilitation

#### Article 25

1. Social rehabilitation as referred to in Article 21 letter c is aimed at restoring and developing the capabilities of women victims of violence who experience social dysfunction to be able to carry out their social functions in society properly.
2. Social rehabilitation as referred to in paragraph (1) includes services:
   - a. counseling;
   - b. provision of safe houses; and
   - c. spiritual guidance.
3. The implementation of services as referred to in paragraph (2) is carried out by:
   - a. Regional Apparatus that administers governmental affairs in the social sector;
   - b. Regional Apparatus that administers governmental affairs in the sector of women's and children's empowerment, and may cooperate with integrated service centers, women crisis centers or existing social community organizations; or
   - c. religious institutions and/or organizations.

#### Article 26

1. Counseling services as referred to in Article 25 paragraph (2) letter a aim to:
   - a. assist women victims of violence in recognizing their problems and finding effective ways to overcome them independently;
   - b. empower women victims of violence to determine their future;
   - c. provide moral support to women victims of violence in facing the process they are undergoing; and
   - d. make women victims of violence feel accepted in the community environment.
2. Counseling services are conducted by gender-responsive social rehabilitation officers, consisting of:
   - a. counselors;
   - b. psychologists; and
   - c. psychiatrists.
3. Counseling services are conducted in a place that guarantees a sense of security, comfort and confidentiality of information of women victims of violence.
4. Stages of counseling services include:
   - a. initial counseling;
   - b. follow-up counseling;
   - c. case discussion; and
   - d. site visits.

#### Article 27

1. The Regional Government may provide safe houses for women victims of acts of violence.
2. The provision of safe house services as referred to in Article 25 paragraph (2) letter b aims to protect women victims of acts of violence from intimidation by perpetrators or other parties.
3. The location of safe houses as referred to in paragraph (1) must be kept confidential.
4. The criteria for women victims of acts of violence who require safe houses include:
   - a. their life is threatened;
   - b. receiving rejection from family or the community;
   - c. requiring intensive services but their residence is relatively far; and/or
   - d. abandoned if not placed in a safe house.
5. The implementation of safe house services is carried out by social rehabilitation officers who have gender sensitivity or gender awareness, consisting of:
   - a. social workers/volunteers;
   - b. security officers; and/or
   - c. community leaders.
6. Further provisions regarding the implementation of safe house services are regulated in a Regent's Regulation.

#### Article 28

1. Spiritual guidance as referred to in Article 25 paragraph (2) letter c aims for psychological recovery.
2. Spiritual guidance as referred to in paragraph (1) is implemented based on the principles of:
   - a. not imposing any religion or belief; and
   - b. leaving the final decision in the hands of the victim.
3. Spiritual guidance is conducted by spiritual guidance officers.
4. Spiritual guidance officers as referred to in paragraph (3) consist of consultants from the Marriage Advisory, Development and Preservation Board, Officers of the Religious Affairs Office, religious leaders, who have gender sensitivity or gender awareness.

### Paragraph 5

Enforcement and Legal Assistance

#### Article 29

1. Law enforcement and legal aid services as referred to in Article 21 letter d constitute actions by law enforcement officials to implement laws and regulations in handling cases of violence against women, especially to impose sanctions on perpetrators and provide protection for witnesses and/or victims.
2. Law enforcement and legal aid services as referred to in paragraph (1) shall be carried out by prioritizing the best interests of the victim.

#### Article 30

1. Law enforcement and legal aid services are intended to provide assistance and protection to women victims of violence and witnesses with a gender-responsive approach so that victims obtain justice and legal certainty.
2. Types of legal aid services include:
   - a. legal aid in the reporting process and preparation of official examination reports;
   - b. legal aid in the prosecution process at the prosecutor's office;
   - c. legal aid in the examination process at court;
   - d. legal aid in the court hearing decision process until a final legal decision is reached; and
   - e. legal aid in the mediation process.
3. The Regional Government provides legal aid to women victims of violence based on the provisions of laws and regulations.
4. In providing legal aid as referred to in paragraph (3), the Regional Government may cooperate with legal aid institutions, women crisis centers, advocates and/or other legal assistants who are gender sensitive.

### Paragraph 6

Repatriation and Social Reintegration

#### Article 31

1. Repatriation is intended to return women victims of acts of violence to their Region of origin.
2. Repatriation of women victims of acts of violence is accompanied by social reintegration efforts.

#### Article 32

1. Social reintegration is carried out in the context of reuniting female victims of acts of violence with their family, substitute family, or community that can provide protection and fulfillment of needs for the victim.
2. Social reintegration is aimed so that:
   - a. female victims of acts of violence can resume their social life; and
   - b. female victims of acts of violence can have a decent life.
3. Social reintegration as referred to in paragraph (1) is carried out after the victim receives social rehabilitation services.

#### Article 33

Regional Governments implement repatriation and social reintegration of women victims of acts of violence in accordance with laws and regulations.

Fourth Part Employment Protection

#### Article 34

1. Employers who employ women are obligated to maintain decency, safety and security while in the workplace.
2. Employers are obligated to place female workers in places that are safe for women's reproductive health.
3. Employers are obligated to fulfill the rights of female workers to receive wages in accordance with the provisions of laws and regulations.
4. Employers are obligated to provide special breastfeeding facilities at the company.

#### Article 35

1. Female workers/laborers who are less than 18 (eighteen) years old are prohibited from being employed between 23.00 WIB until 07.00 WIB.
2. Employers are prohibited from employing pregnant female workers/laborers who according to a doctor's statement would endanger the health and safety of their pregnancy.
3. Employers who employ female workers/laborers between 23.00 WIB until 07.00 WIB, are obligated to:
   - a. provide food and nutritious beverages; and
   - b. maintain decency and security while at the workplace.
4. Employers are obligated to provide shuttle transportation for female workers/laborers who depart for and return from work between 23.00 WIB until 05.00 WIB.

# CHAPTER VII

## FULFILLMENT OF CHILDREN'S RIGHTS

## Part One

General

#### Article 47

1. The Regional Government, in efforts to fulfill children's rights, is responsible for:
   - a. establishing and implementing policies and programs/activities for the fulfillment of children's rights;
   - b. respecting and guaranteeing the human rights of every child without distinguishing ethnicity, religion, race, group, sex, ethnic group, culture and language, the child's legal status, the child's birth order, and physical and/or mental condition;
   - c. strengthening and developing service-provider institutions for improving family quality in realizing children's rights;
   - d. guaranteeing the protection, care, and welfare of children by taking into account the rights and obligations of parents, guardians, or other persons who are legally responsible for the child;
   - e. providing facilities and infrastructure in the implementation of the fulfillment of children's rights;
   - f. guaranteeing the right of children to express opinions in accordance with the age and level of intelligence of the child;
   - g. conducting supervision and guidance in the context of implementing the fulfillment of children's rights;
   - h. cooperating with private parties and/or NGOs in the implementation of child protection;
2. The responsibility of the Government as referred to in the implementation of Institutional and Government Protection in the field of Child Empowerment.

Second Part Registration of Birth and Education

#### Article 48

1. Regional Governments are obligated to administer the registration of child births with the issuance of birth certificates.
2. Birth certificates as referred to in paragraph (1) shall be administered free of charge.

#### Article 49

The Regional Government guarantees the implementation of a 9 (nine) year compulsory education program for children aged 7 (seven) to 15 (fifteen) years.

#### Article 50

1. Every organizer of an educational unit shall provide children with the opportunity to obtain educational services without discrimination.
2. Every organizer of an educational unit is prohibited from expelling children from educational institutions unless there is a guarantee for the continuity of the child's education.
3. In the event that a child victim of violence experiences school dropout, the organizer of the educational unit must coordinate with relevant agencies.

#### Article 51

1. Children with disabilities and children with special needs are given equal opportunities to obtain formal, non-formal and informal education services, including inclusive education programs.
2. Regional governments provide educators for inclusive education at every education unit.

#### Article 52

Children in conflict with the law, children who experience pregnancy outside of marriage and/or an unwanted pregnancy, as well as children who are victims of HIV/AIDS transmission, are protected in their right to obtain education.

Third Part Health and Social

#### Article 53

1. Regional Governments are obligated to provide comprehensive health services for children, so that every child obtains optimal health status from the time they are in the womb.
2. Comprehensive health services as referred to in paragraph (1) include:
   - a. promotive, preventive, curative and rehabilitative efforts for basic health services;
   - b. exemption from cost burden for malnourished children, children with disabilities, children with special needs, children infected with HIV/AIDS, child laborers, child victims of abuse of narcotics, alcohol, psychotropic substances and other addictive substances, children who are victims of violence, and child victims of human trafficking.
3. Regional Governments, communities, families and parents are obligated to endeavor to ensure that children who are born are protected from diseases that threaten survival and/or cause disability.

#### Article 54

1. Promotive efforts for basic health services as referred to in Article 53 paragraph (2) letter a are aimed at improving health status more optimally.
2. Promotive efforts as referred to in paragraph (1) are carried out by providing socialization or counseling to the community regarding healthy lifestyle patterns which include:
   - a. the importance of exclusive breastfeeding for infants;
   - b. the importance of immunization for toddlers;
   - c. clean living patterns;
   - d. the importance of nutritious food;
   - e. the dangers of drugs and smoking; and (3) In addition to promotive efforts as referred to in paragraph (2), the Regional Government may carry out other activities in the context of the administration of health.

#### Article 55

1. Preventive efforts for basic health services as referred to in Article 53 paragraph (2) letter a are aimed at preventing the occurrence of disease or health problems.
2. The efforts as referred to in paragraph (1) are carried out by:
   - a. requiring mothers to provide exclusive breastfeeding to infants up to 6 (six) months of age;
   - b. providing free immunization and vitamins to infants and toddlers;
   - c. providing nutritious food to toddlers from underprivileged families;
   - d. optimizing the role of posyandu for health services for mothers and toddlers;
   - e. conducting periodic health examinations; and
   - f. providing sanitation facilities and clean water at every school.

#### Article 56

1. Curative efforts for basic health services as referred to in Article 53 paragraph (2) letter a are aimed at preventing diseases from becoming more severe through treatment.
2. Treatment as referred to in paragraph (1) is implemented free of charge for children from underprivileged families.

#### Article 57

1. Rehabilitative efforts for basic health services as referred to in Article 53 paragraph (2) letter a are intended to maintain and restore conditions after illness.
2. Rehabilitative efforts as referred to in paragraph (1) are carried out through periodic health examinations.

#### Article 58

1. Regional Government is obligated to provide social welfare for:
   - a. children in conflict with the law;
   - b. child victims of violence who are exploited economically and sexually, neglected children, child victims of abduction and child victims of human trafficking;
   - c. children who do not have parents and are cared for by parties or families who are unable;
   - d. child victims of natural disasters or social disasters; and
   - e. children with disabilities;
2. Social welfare as referred to in paragraph (1) may be in the form of provision of services:
   - a. health;
   - b. education;
   - c. legal aid;
   - d. assistance;
   - e. social, mental and spiritual guidance;
   - f. social rehabilitation; and/or
   - g. reintegration.

Fourth Part Employment

#### Article 59

The Regional Government conducts periodic supervision of every person/entity that employs children in an effort to protect child workers.

#### Article 60

Every person/entity that employs children must observe the following requirements:

   - a. minimum age of 15 (fifteen) years;
   - b. obtain written consent from the parent/guardian of the child worker;
   - c. have a written employment agreement between the employer and the parent/guardian of the child worker and obtain validation from the authorized agency;
   - d. not employ children at night;
   - e. maximum working time of 3 (three) hours per day;
   - f. not be employed in a place or environment that can disrupt the child's growth and development process, whether physically, mentally, morally and intellectually or the child's health;
   - g. provide opportunities to receive education in accordance with their talents and interests;
   - h. employ for light types of work; and
      - i. provide one day off per week.

#### Article 61

In the event that children are employed together with adult workers, then the workplace for children must be separated from the workplace for adult workers.

#### Article 62

1. Every person/entity is prohibited from employing and involving children in the worst forms of work.
2. The worst forms of work referred to in paragraph (1) include:
   - a. all work in the form of slavery or similar practices;
   - b. all work that exploits, provides, or offers children for prostitution, pornography production, pornographic performances, or gambling;
   - c. all work that exploits, provides, or involves children for the production and trade of alcoholic beverages, narcotics, psychotropic substances, and other addictive substances; and/or
   - d. all work that endangers the health, safety, or morals of children.

   - e. rehabilitation and social reintegration services;

4. In facilitating the juvenile justice system as referred to in paragraph (1) and paragraph (2), the authorized PD is the PD that administers governmental affairs in the field of protection of women and children as well as social affairs.

#### Article 64

3. In every formulation of policies related to children, the Regional Government is obligated to consider and accommodate the opinions of children conveyed through the children's participation forum.

   - b. children's rights; and
   - c. the approach to developing a Child-Friendly Regency.

   - a. civil rights and freedoms;
   - b. family environment and alternative care;
   - c. basic health and welfare;
   - d. education, leisure time utilization, and cultural activities; and
   - e. special protection.

   - a. coordinating the implementation of policies and development of Child-Friendly Regency;
   - b. conducting socialization, advocacy and communication of information and education on Child-Friendly Regency policies;
   - c. collecting baseline data for Child-Friendly Regency development;
   - d. conducting needs analysis sourced from baseline data;
   - e. conducting dissemination of baseline data;
   - f. determining program focus and priorities in realizing a Child-Friendly Regency, adjusted to regional potential;
   - g. formulating a 5 (five) year Child-Friendly Regency regional action plan and work mechanism; and
   - h. conducting monitoring, evaluation and reporting at least once (one time) a year.

##### REGARDING WOMEN'S EMPOWERMENT AND CHILD PROTECTION

##### I. GENERAL

The 1945 Constitution as a constitutional foundation has explicitly regulated the importance of protection of Human Rights, including the rights of women and children. In addition to the guarantee from the 1945 Constitution of the Republic of Indonesia, the guarantee that every person must have equal dignity and degree, has also been recognized internationally. The Universal Declaration of Human Rights or UDHR of 1948 which underlies Human Rights in general has guaranteed that all people are born with freedom, having equal dignity and rights without exception.

The 1945 Constitution of the Republic of Indonesia, mandates the Regional Government to regulate and manage regional government affairs aimed at realizing community welfare, through improving services, protection and empowerment of the community. As a component of society that plays an important role in realizing a quality generation and as successors to the nation's struggle, women and children must receive guarantees for the fulfillment of their rights.

To ensure the implementation of women's empowerment and child protection in accordance with human dignity and in order to carry out mandatory affairs, regional governments need to provide legal certainty in the implementation of women's empowerment and child protection through the formulation of a policy. The formulation of such policy is a form of support and concrete effort from various parties, ranging from Regional Government, community and other parties in ensuring the fulfillment of the rights of women and children.

The Implementation of Women's Empowerment and Child Protection is carried out based on the principles of:

   - a. non-discrimination;
   - b. the best interests of women and children;
   - c. the right to life, survival, and development;
   - d. respect for the views of children;
   - e. Legal certainty;
   - f. local wisdom; and
   - g. gender justice and equality.

The formulation of Regional Regulations on Women's Empowerment and Child Protection aims to:

   - a. provide guidance in the planning and implementation of Women's Empowerment and Child Protection policies;
   - b. increase efforts for Women's Empowerment and fulfillment of children's rights in order to realize improved quality of life for women and children in the Region;
   - c. increase protection efforts for women and children from actions and policies that violate the rights of women and children;
   - d. increase the value of local wisdom and the role of customs in Women's Empowerment and Child Protection; and
   - e. increase the role of Regional Government and other community institutions in efforts for Women's Empowerment and Child Protection.

The Regional Regulation regarding Women and Child Protection regulates:

   - a. Improvement of women's quality of life which contains government responsibility, implementation of PUG, Efforts to increase the role, quality and capacity of women in the political, legal, social and economic fields;
   - b. Protection of women which contains regional government responsibility in women's protection, prevention of violence against women, protection for women victims of violence, as well as complaint handling;
   - c. Child protection which contains the responsibility of regional government, parents/family and community in child protection as well as efforts made by regional government in implementing child protection. In addition, the need to establish children's forums and Child-Friendly Regency as a form of appreciation in realizing protection and fulfillment of children's rights.

#### Article 20

### Paragraph (1) Sufficiently clear.

### Paragraph (2)

Point a What is meant by gender responsive is that all service officers must be gender sensitive when exploring problems experienced by victims and be able to empower victims.

Point b Sufficiently clear.

Point c What is meant by equal and respectful relations is that whoever the victim, the provision of services for victims must be carried out with respect for the victim without distinguishing their beliefs, values and social status.

Point d What is meant by maintaining confidentiality is that services must be provided in a place that guarantees the victim's privacy. Every piece of information revealed in the service provision process must be kept confidential and known only by people who are relevant in providing services. Officers must convey this principle to victims.

Point e What is meant by providing a sense of security and comfort is that service providers must ensure that victims are in a safe and comfortable state in telling their problems.

Point f What is meant by respecting individual differences (individual differences) is that each individual must be viewed as unique; each person has a different background, life experience and way of dealing with stress (coping mechanism), so that one victim must not be compared with another victim in any matter.

Point g What is meant by not judging is that service providers must ensure that whatever the victim's condition or information that comes from the victim will not be assessed or judged.

Point h What is meant by respecting the victim's choices and decisions is that service provision must be carried out with the victim's consent, starting from the interview process, data recording, to the handling/actions to be taken.

Point i What is meant by being sensitive to the victim's background and condition and using language that is appropriate and understood by the victim is that sometimes victims come from regions or economic, educational and environmental backgrounds that are not the same as the officers. It must be ensured that victims are served in language understood by the victim. Lately, more and more disabled/difable/disable women have become victims of violence, whether deaf, mute, blind or those experiencing mental retardation. For this category of victims, interpreters must also be provided who, for example, can be drawn from special school (SLB) teachers.

Point j What is meant by fast and simple is that service provision must be given immediately without delays due to the involvement of various parties; however, with the proactive nature of service officers, victims must be guaranteed to be able to go through everything with a simple process. If the victim comes on referral from another service provider, the receiving officer must first read the referral cover letter.
