# BY THE GRACE OF ALMIGHTY GOD

# MAYOR OF SINGKAWANG,

   - a. that girls as creations of Almighty God need to be protected and their honor, dignity, and self-esteem safeguarded from all forms of discrimination, acts of violence, and exploitation to improve the quality of life of children and women physically, spiritually, and socially in realizing an advanced, just, prosperous, prosperous, and democratic society based on Pancasila and the 1945 Constitution of the Republic of Indonesia;
   - b. that the fate of children and women in Singkawang City needs attention from the Regional Government and society because cases of violence against children and women still occur, and children in conflict with the law, thus requiring efforts to provide legal protection for children and women;
   - c. that to provide protection for children and women, also as implementation of higher Laws and Regulations and constituting one of the mandatory government affairs that is the authority of the Regional Government, the regulation of protection of children and women needs to be regulated in a Regional Regulation;
   - d. that based on the considerations as referred to in letter a, letter b, and letter c, it is necessary to establish a Regional Regulation concerning Protection of Children and Women;

# CHAPTER I

## GENERAL PROVISIONS

#### Article 1

In this Regional Regulation, what is meant by:

1. Region is the City of Singkawang.
2. Regional Government is the regional head as an element of the Regional Government administration who leads the implementation of governmental affairs that are the authority of the autonomous region.
3. Mayor is the Mayor of Singkawang City.
4. Child is a person who has not yet reached the age of 18 (eighteen) years, including those still in the womb.
5. Woman is an adult human being of female gender and a person who is legally recognized as a woman.
6. Victim is a child and woman who experiences suffering and/or hardship either directly or indirectly as a result of violence.
7. Protection of Women is all activities aimed at providing a sense of security carried out by the Regional Government, community, and/or other parties who know or hear that violence against women will occur, is occurring, or has occurred.
8. Child protection is all activities to guarantee and protect children and their rights so that they can live, grow, develop, and participate optimally in accordance with human dignity and receive protection from violence and discrimination.
9. Special protection is a form of protection received by children in certain situations and conditions to obtain a guarantee of security against threats that endanger themselves and their lives in their growth and development.
10. Violence against women and children, hereinafter referred to as violence, is any action based on gender differences that results in suffering or hardship physically, sexually, mentally, psychologically, including intimidation, forced eviction, threats of certain actions, coercion or deprivation of freedom, neglect, and preventing the ability of women and children to enjoy all their rights and freedoms.
11. Family is the smallest unit in society consisting of husband and wife, husband and wife and their children, or father and children, or mother and children, or blood relatives in a direct line upward or downward up to the third degree.
12. Parents are biological father and/or mother, or stepfather and/or stepmother, or adoptive father and/or adoptive mother.
13. Guardian is a person or body who in reality exercises parental authority as a parent over a child.
14. Every person is an individual or corporation, whether a legal entity or not a legal entity.
15. Service is an action taken as soon as possible to victims when seeing, hearing and/or knowing that violence against victims will occur, is occurring, or has occurred.
16. Assistance is all actions in the form of counseling, psychological therapy, advocacy, and spiritual guidance, for the purpose of strengthening victims of violence to resolve the problems they face.
17. Regional Apparatus Organization, hereinafter abbreviated as OPD, is a regional apparatus organization within the Singkawang City Government.
18. Prevention is an activity carried out to prevent acts of violence against children and women.
19. Repatriation is an effort to return women and children victims of violence to their families, substitute families, or communities that can provide protection and fulfill their needs.
20. Rehabilitation is recovery from disturbances to physical, psychological, and social conditions so that they can carry out their roles again properly both in the family and in society.
21. Reintegration is an effort to reunite victims with their families, communities, institutions, or other social environments that can provide protection.
22. Institution is an agency/office/body/bureau within the scope of the Regional Government and/or non-governmental organizations that provide assistance.
23. Agency is a regional apparatus work unit whose duties and responsibilities are to coordinate the handling of affairs in the field of organizing the protection of children and women.
24. Integrated Service Center, hereinafter abbreviated as PPT, is a functional work unit that organizes integrated services for victims of violence.
25. Integrated Service Center for Women and Children Empowerment, hereinafter abbreviated as P2TP2A, is an integrated, one-stop, and networked technical service established by the government, Regional Government, community, non-governmental organizations, donor institutions, and other institutions in efforts to empower women and protect women and children comprehensively from various types of discrimination and non-violence, including human trafficking.
26. Community is individuals, families, groups, and social organizations, and/or community organizations.
27. Regional Action Plan, hereinafter abbreviated as RAD, is the foundation and guideline for related agencies, vertical agencies, and the community, in carrying out activities for the implementation of prevention and protection of children and women victims of violence.
28. Task Force is a coordinative institution formed by the Regional Government that coordinates policies, programs, and activities for the protection of children and women in the region.
29. Child-Friendly City, hereinafter abbreviated as KLA, is a city that has a child rights-based development system through the integration of commitments and resources of the Regional Government, community, and business world that are planned comprehensively and sustainably in policies, programs, and activities to ensure the fulfillment of children's rights.

# CHAPTER II

## RIGHTS OF CHILDREN, WOMEN, AND VICTIMS

#### Article 2

1. Every child has the right:
   - a. to live, grow, develop, and participate reasonably in accordance with human dignity and honor, and to receive protection from criminal acts, violence, exploitation, and neglect;
   - b. to a name as personal identity and citizenship status;
   - c. to worship according to their religion under parental guidance and to practice the teachings of their religion;
   - d. to know their parents, be raised, and be cared for by their own parents;
   - e. to obtain health services and social security in accordance with physical, mental, spiritual, and social needs;
   - f. to obtain education and teaching in accordance with age level, physical and mental condition, intelligence, and interests and talents;
   - g. to express and have their opinion heard as well as to receive, seek, and provide information in accordance with the level of intelligence and age for self-development in accordance with moral values and propriety;
   - h. to rest and utilize leisure time, play, express themselves for self-development;
      - i. to receive proper care from parents/guardians or other parties responsible for their care;
   - j. to obtain protection from abuse in politics, involvement in armed disputes, involvement in social unrest, involvement in events containing elements of violence, involvement in warfare, being targets of persecution, torture or imposition of inhumane punishment, and involvement of children in the worst forms of work;
   - k. to obtain protection from the dangers of narcotics, pornography, and violent shows or other things that impact changes in child development;
   - l. to have contact with parents if separated; and
   - m. to obtain other rights in accordance with Laws and Regulations.
2. Every woman has the right:
   - a. to live, sustain life, and improve living standards;
   - b. to live peacefully, safely, in peace, happily, prosperously physically and mentally;
   - c. to enjoy a good and healthy living environment;
   - d. to live in a family within the bonds of lawful marriage based on the provisions of Laws and Regulations;
   - e. to obtain work according to ability, appropriate conditions and fair and just wages;
   - f. specifically for women with disabilities, the right to receive facilities and special treatment in public services;
   - g. to play an active role in the field of politics and government in accordance with the potential and capabilities possessed;
   - h. to obtain legal information and services; and
      - i. to obtain other rights in accordance with human dignity and based on the provisions of Laws and Regulations.
3. Every victim has the right to receive:
   - a. protection;
   - b. information;
   - c. optimal services;
   - d. continuous handling up to the rehabilitation stage;
   - e. confidential handling;
   - f. assistance in accordance with applicable provisions;
   - g. guarantee of rights related to the victim's status as a family member and community member;
   - h. to obtain restitution in accordance with the provisions of Laws and Regulations; and
      - i. other rights regulated in the provisions of Laws and Regulations.

#### Article 3

Child victims of violence, in addition to obtaining the rights as referred to in Article 2 paragraph (1) and paragraph (3), also obtain special rights as follows:

   - a. the right to respect and full fulfillment for survival, growth, and development;
   - b. the right to basic services;
   - c. the right to equal protection;
   - d. the right to be free from various stigma; and
   - e. the right to obtain freedom.

#### Article 4

Every victim has the right to report and to receive assistance, as well as to obtain a guarantee of their rights related to their status as a wife, mother, child, family member, or as a member of society.

#### Article 5

The rights as referred to in Article 2 paragraph (3) letter c, are also granted to the family of the witness and/or victim up to the second degree, in the event that the family of the witness and/or victim receives threats either physical or psychological from other persons in connection with the testimony of the witness and/or victim.

# CHAPTER III

## OBLIGATIONS OF CHILDREN

#### Article 6

Every child is obligated to:

   - a. respect parents, guardians, and teachers;
   - b. love family and the community, and care for friends;
   - c. love the homeland, the nation, and the state;
   - d. perform worship in accordance with the teachings of their religion; and
   - e. practice noble ethics and morals.

# CHAPTER IV

## OBLIGATIONS AND RESPONSIBILITIES

#### Article 7

The obligations and responsibilities of the organizers of child and women's protection are the joint responsibility of the Regional Government, the community, the family, and parents or guardians.

#### Article 8

1. The obligations and responsibilities of the Regional Government as referred to in Article 7 shall be implemented by:
   - a. formulating and implementing policies, programs, and activities for the protection of children and women;
   - b. ensuring the establishment and implementation of cooperation between various parties in the Region, with and between various parties in the regency/city and all related elements in the implementation of the protection of children and women;
   - c. providing support for facilities, infrastructure, and availability of human resources in the implementation of the protection of children and women;
   - d. fulfilling, protecting, and respecting the rights of children and ensuring children to exercise their rights in expressing opinions in accordance with the age and intelligence of the child; and
   - e. fostering and supervising the implementation of the protection of children and women.
2. The obligations and responsibilities of the Regional Government as referred to in paragraph (1) shall be coordinated by the OPD responsible for women's empowerment and child protection affairs.
3. In order to implement the obligations and responsibilities as referred to in paragraph (1), the Regional Government shall establish programs and action activities for the protection of children and women in 1 (one) RAD as a basis for OPD in implementing the protection of children and women in the region.
4. The RAD as referred to in paragraph (3) constitutes part of the Regional Medium-Term Development Plan which is regulated by Mayor Regulation.

#### Article 9

1. The obligations and responsibilities of the community as referred to in Article 7 are carried out in the form of community participation in the implementation of protection of children and women.
2. Community participation as referred to in paragraph (1), may be carried out in the form of:
   - a. preventing the occurrence of acts of violence against women and children;
   - b. submitting reports to the authorities if acts of violence against women and children occur;
   - c. providing assistance to victims; and
   - d. playing an active role in efforts to increase the fulfillment of children's rights and the empowerment of women.
3. Community participation as referred to in paragraph (2) is carried out by individuals, social community institutions, community organizations, professional organizations, educational institutions, religious institutions, the private sector, mass media, and the business sector.
4. The role of community organizations, professional organizations, and educational institutions is carried out by taking the necessary steps in accordance with their respective duties, functions, authorities, and competencies to assist in the implementation of protection of children and women.
5. The participation of mass media is carried out through the dissemination of information and educational materials related to the implementation of protection of children and women by taking into account the best interests of the child and/or victim.
6. The role of the business sector is carried out through:
   - a. company policies with a child and women's perspective;
   - b. products intended must be safe for children; and
   - c. contributing to the fulfillment of children's rights and the empowerment of women through corporate social responsibility.
7. The form of community participation as referred to in paragraph (2), is implemented responsibly in accordance with the provisions of Laws and Regulations.

#### Article 10

The obligations and responsibilities of the family as referred to in Article 7, legally have full responsibility to prevent all forms of violence and protect women and children as family members.

#### Article 11

1. The obligations and responsibilities of parents as referred to in Article 7, include:
   - a. nurturing, caring for, educating, providing a sense of security and not committing mistreatment and violence against children;
   - b. developing children in accordance with their abilities, talents, and interests;
   - c. reporting every birth of a child to the authorized agency to conduct birth registration;
   - d. preventing marriage at child age; and
   - e. providing character education and instilling moral values in children.
2. In the event that parents do not exist, or their whereabouts are unknown, or due to a certain reason cannot carry out their obligations and responsibilities, then the obligations and responsibilities as referred to in paragraph (1) may be transferred to the family, which shall be implemented in accordance with the provisions of Laws and Regulations.
3. In the event that the parents and family of the child cannot carry out their obligations and responsibilities, then the obligations and responsibilities as referred to in paragraph (1), and paragraph (2), shall be transferred to a person or legal entity that meets the requirements appointed as guardian of the child concerned.

# CHAPTER V

## STATUS OF THE CHILD

## Part One

Identity of the Child

#### Article 12

1. The identity of every child must be provided from birth. (2) The identity as referred to in paragraph (1) shall be set forth in a birth certificate.
3. The making of a birth certificate is based on a statement from a person who witnessed and/or assisted the birth process.
4. In the case of a child whose birth process is not known, and whose parents' whereabouts are not known, the making of a birth certificate for such child shall be based on a statement from the person who found the child and supplemented with minutes of police examination.

#### Article 13

1. The administration of the issuance of birth certificates is carried out by the OPD that administers population administration affairs.
2. Provisions regarding the procedures and requirements for issuing birth certificates as referred to in paragraph (1) shall be regulated by a Mayoral Regulation.

Second Part Children Born from Mixed Marriages

#### Article 14

1. If a mixed marriage occurs between a citizen of the Republic of Indonesia and a foreign citizen, a child born from that marriage has the right to obtain the citizenship of his or her father or mother in accordance with the provisions of Laws and Regulations.
2. In the event of a divorce from a marriage as referred to in paragraph (1), the child has the right to choose or based on a court decision, to be in the custody of one of his or her parents.
3. In the event of a divorce as referred to in paragraph (2), while the child is not yet able to make a choice, and his or her mother is a citizen of the Republic of Indonesia, in the best interests of the child or upon the request of his or her mother, the Regional Government is obligated to process the status of citizenship of the Republic of Indonesia for that child.

# CHAPTER VI

## CUSTODY

#### Article 15

1. In the event that parents as referred to in Article 11 paragraph (1) neglect their obligations, supervisory action may be taken against them or parental custody may be revoked.
2. Supervisory action against parents or revocation of custody as referred to in paragraph (1) shall be carried out through court determination.

#### Article 16

1. One of the parents, a full sibling, or family up to the third degree, may submit an application to the court to obtain a court determination regarding the revocation of parental custody authority or to take supervisory action if there are strong reasons for it as referred to in Article 15 paragraph (1).
2. If one of the parents, a full sibling, or family up to the third degree, cannot carry out their function, then the revocation of parental custody authority as referred to in paragraph (1) may also be submitted by an authorized official or another institution that has the authority to do so.
3. The court determination as referred to in paragraph (1) may appoint an individual or a government/community institution to become a guardian for the child concerned.
4. The individual who carries out the care of the child as referred to in paragraph (3) must be of the same religion as the religion professed by the child to be cared for.

# CHAPTER VII

## GUARDIANSHIP

#### Article 117 (1) In the event that a child is not capable of performing legal acts, while the parents or family's place of residence or whereabouts are unknown, then a person or legal entity that meets the requirements may be appointed as guardian of the child concerned.

2. To become a guardian of a child as referred to in paragraph (1) shall be conducted through a court determination.
3. The guardian appointed must have the same religion as the religion adhered to by the child.
4. The guardian appointed as referred to in paragraph (2) is responsible for the child and must manage the property belonging to the child concerned for the best interests of the child.
5. Provisions regarding the requirements and procedures for the appointment of guardians shall be implemented in accordance with the provisions of Laws and Regulations.

# CHAPTER VIII

## ADOPTION OF CHILDREN

#### Article 18

1. Adoption of children may only be carried out for the best interests of the child, and is carried out based on customary practices and in accordance with the prevailing laws and regulations.
2. Adoption of children does not sever the blood relationship between the adopted child and his/her biological parents.
3. Adoption of children must be recorded in the birth certificate without eliminating the child's original identity.
4. Prospective adoptive parents must be of the same religion as the religion adhered to by the prospective adopted child.
5. Adoption of children by foreign nationals may only be carried out as a last resort in accordance with the prevailing laws and regulations.
6. In the event that the child's origins are not known, then the child's religion shall be adjusted to the religion of the majority of the local population.

#### Article 19

1. Adoptive parents are obligated to inform their adopted child regarding their origin and biological parents.
2. The notification of origin and biological parents shall be carried out with due consideration to the child's mental readiness.

# CHAPTER IX

## IMPLEMENTATION OF PROTECTION

## Part One

General

#### Article 20

The implementation of protection for children and women is carried out through:

   - a. planning and development of information systems for children and women;
   - b. prevention and protection;
   - c. child protection;
   - d. special protection;
   - e. protection of women;
   - f. protection in the workplace;
   - g. handling and recovery of victims of violence;
   - h. rehabilitation, reintegration, and empowerment;
      - i. law enforcement services and legal assistance; and
   - j. facilitation of juvenile justice.

Second Part Planning and Development of the Children and Women Information System

#### Article 21

1. Planning for the protection of children and women is implemented by providing data and information related to the protection of children and women in the region.
2. Data and information as referred to in paragraph (1) concerns data on gender mainstreaming (PUG), data on child rights mainstreaming (PUHA), and evaluation of programs and data services.
3. Data and information as referred to in paragraph (2) is provided by the OPD that administers the affairs of child and women protection.
4. Data information as referred to in paragraph (3) is required for policy planning and program interventions that are coordinated by the Agency.

## Part Three

Prevention and Protection

#### Article 22

1. The Regional Government is obligated to undertake prevention and protection efforts against violent treatment, exploitation, discrimination, maltreatment, and neglect.
2. The implementation of prevention and protection as referred to in paragraph (1), may cooperate with educational institutions, religious institutions, mass media, professional organizations, business entities, and/or community institutions.
3. Prevention and protection efforts as referred to in paragraph (2) are implemented by:
   - a. forming networks;
   - b. conducting coordination, integration, and synchronization of prevention and protection based on partnership patterns;
   - c. establishing a system for prevention of violence and protection of children and women;
   - d. disseminating Laws and Regulations related to the protection of women victims of violence;
   - e. providing education for victims of violence and vulnerable groups;
   - f. organizing family support in the form of family parenting education, child parenting education, family mediation, and economic support;
   - g. increasing the capacity of protection services for women and children including institutional capacity development and enhancement of professionalism and competence of service providers;
   - h. increasing the ability of individuals and/or vulnerable groups to recognize risks and dangers from situations or actions that may cause violence, exploitation, maltreatment and neglect; and
      - i. increasing the utilization of communication, information, and education media.
4. Prevention and protection efforts as referred to in paragraph (3) are carried out in an integrated manner by the Regional Government coordinated by the OPD responsible for women's empowerment and child protection.

#### Article 23

1. Every Person and/or Institution is obligated to protect children and women from violence, exploitation, discrimination, mistreatment, and neglect.
2. Protection by Institutions as referred to in paragraph (1) shall be carried out by not implementing policies and actions that violate the rights of children and women.

Fourth Part Child Protection

### Paragraph 1

Ag am a

#### Article 24

1. Every child receives protection to worship according to their religion. (2) Before a child can determine their choice, the religion embraced by the child follows the religion of their parents.

#### Article 25

1. The Regional Government, the community, the family, parents, guardians, and social community institutions guarantee the protection of children in embracing their religion.
2. Protection of children in embracing their religion as referred to in paragraph (1) includes fostering, guidance, and the practice of religious teachings for children.

### Paragraph 2

Health

#### Article 26

1. Regional Governments are obligated to provide facilities and organize comprehensive health efforts for children, so that every child obtains an optimal degree of health from the time they are in the womb.
2. The provision of facilities and organization of comprehensive health efforts as referred to in paragraph (1), is supported by community participation.
3. Comprehensive health efforts as referred to in paragraph (1) include promotive, preventive, curative, and rehabilitative efforts, both for basic health services and referrals.
4. Comprehensive health efforts as referred to in paragraph (3) are organized free of charge for families who are unable to afford them.
5. The implementation of provisions as referred to in paragraph (1) through paragraph (4) is carried out in accordance with the provisions of Laws and Regulations.

#### Article 27

1. Parents and family are obligated to be responsible for maintaining the health of children and caring for children from the time they are in the womb.
2. In the event that parents and family are unable to carry out their responsibilities as referred to in paragraph (1), then the Regional Government is obligated to fulfill it.
3. The obligation as referred to in paragraph (2), is implemented in accordance with the provisions of Laws and Regulations.

#### Article 28

Every person is prohibited from performing an abortion on a child still in the womb, except for reasons and procedures that are justified in accordance with the provisions of the prevailing laws and regulations.

#### Article 29

The Regional Government, the community, the family, and parents or guardians are obligated to protect children from acts that disrupt the health and growth and development of children.

### Paragraph 3

Education

#### Article 30

1. Regional Governments are obligated to provide basic education for a minimum of 9 (nine) years for all children.
2. Parents are obligated to fulfill the child's right to obtain basic education for a minimum of 9 (nine) years.

#### Article 31

Regional Government, family, and parents or guardians are obligated to provide the widest possible opportunity to children to obtain education.

#### Article 32

Education as referred to in article 31 is directed towards:

   - a. the development of the child's personality, talents, mental and physical abilities to their fullest potential;
   - b. the development of respect for human rights and fundamental freedoms;
   - c. the development of respect for the child's parents, cultural identity, language, and values, the national values of the country in which the child is living, the country from which the child may originate, and the diverse customs of the child's own area of residence;
   - d. the preparation of the child for a responsible life; and
   - e. the development of respect and love for the environment.

#### Article 33

1. Children with disabilities are given opportunities and accessibility to obtain inclusive education and/or special education.
2. Children who have special talents/advantages are given opportunities and accessibility to obtain special education.
3. Children who receive special protection are given opportunities to obtain their right to education.

#### Article 34

1. Regional Governments are responsible for providing education costs and/or free services or special services for children from underprivileged families, neglected children, and children residing in remote areas.
2. The responsibility of Regional Governments as referred to in paragraph (1) also includes encouraging the community to play an active role.

#### Article 35

1. Children in educational units must receive protection from acts of physical violence, psychological violence, sexual crimes, and other crimes committed by educators, education personnel, fellow students and/or other parties.
2. Protection as referred to in paragraph (1) shall be carried out by educators, educational personnel, government officials and/or the community.

#### Article 36

1. The Regional Government is obligated to develop a child-friendly school model.
2. Child-friendly schools as referred to in paragraph (1) constitute a joint effort of members of the school community to:
   - a. create a safe and comfortable environment for children at school;
   - b. develop children's participation in accordance with their age and maturity; and
   - c. provide opportunities for children from poor families and/or persons with disabilities to receive equal and fair treatment in obtaining services and guidance at school.

### Paragraph 4

Social

#### Article 37

1. The Regional Government is obligated to organize the maintenance and care of neglected children, both within institutions and outside institutions.
2. The organization of maintenance as referred to in paragraph (1) may be carried out by institutions or the community.
3. To organize the maintenance and care of neglected children, institutions and the community, as referred to in paragraph (2), may establish cooperation with various related parties.
4. In the case of organizing maintenance and care as referred to in paragraph (3), supervision shall be conducted by the OPD that administers affairs in the social sector in accordance with the provisions of laws and regulations.

#### Article 38

## Part Seven Protection in the Workplace

#### Article 63

1. Each company is prohibited from employing underage child labor or exploiting children.
2. Companies must position female workers as work partners to develop the company.

#### Article 64

1. The provisions as referred to in Article 63 paragraph (1), may be exempted by taking into account the following requirements: a. age for child workers in informal sector work above 15 (fifteen) years;
   - b. obtain written consent from parents/guardians of child workers in informal sector work;
   - c. implementation must be set forth in a written work agreement between the company/employer and the parents/guardians of child workers in informal sector work;
   - d. not employed at night;
   - e. working time of at most 3 (three) hours per day;
   - f. not employed in a place or environment that can disrupt the child's growth and development process, whether physical, mental, moral and intellectual or the child's health;
   - g. provide opportunities to socialize with family and the surrounding environment;
   - h. provide opportunities to obtain education in accordance with their talents and interests;
   - t. employed for light types of work; and J. provide health protection for children and the opportunity for one day off per week.
2. Protection for child workers in informal sector work as referred to in paragraph (1), aims to: a. prevent all forms of exploitation, discrimination, harassment and violence against children; and
   - b. protect children from activities that can disrupt the child's growth and development process, whether physical, mental, moral and intellectual or the child's health.
3. Protection efforts for child workers in informal sector work as referred to in paragraph (2), include among others in the form of: a. providing education to the community about children's rights; b. providing assistance in the form of psychological, medical and legal services to child workers in informal sector work who experience exploitation, discrimination, harassment and violence; c. empowering families through the provision of skills training and expenditure reduction;
   - d. providing scholarships to child workers in informal sector work who have dropped out of school to continue formal education to a higher level; and e. providing non-formal education and skills training for child workers in informal sector work who do not pursue formal education.

#### Article 65

1. For female workers who work beyond working hours, the company is obligated to provide overtime pay in accordance with the provisions of Laws and Regulations.
2. The company is obligated to protect occupational health and safety, as well as grant maternity leave rights to female workers.
3. The company is prohibited from making policies that create discrimination against female workers.
4. Companies that violate the provisions as referred to in paragraph (1), paragraph (2), and paragraph (3), may be subject to administrative sanctions in the form of:
   - a. verbal warning;
   - b. written warning; and
   - c. other administrative sanctions in accordance with the provisions of Laws and Regulations.

#### Article 66

   - a. children outside the care of parents;
   - b. children in disaster emergency situations;
   - c. children in conflict with the law;
   - d. child victims of physical, mental and sexual violence;
   - e. children subjected to maltreatment and neglect;
   - f. child victims of economic and sexual exploitation;
   - g. children who become victims of narcotics, alcohol, psychotropic and other addictive substance abuse or matters that cause dependency on narcotics, alcohol, psychotropic and other addictive substances;
   - h. child victims of pornography;
      - i. children who have deviant social behavior;
   - j. children who experience stigmatization due to their parents' behavior;
   - k. children and women who become victims of human trafficking;
   - l. children and women who are in situations where the nature and conditions of the workplace can endanger health, safety and morals; and
   - m. women victims of physical, mental and sexual violence.
2. Handling as referred to in paragraph (1) must be carried out immediately including:
   - a. identification process and receipt of complaints/reports;
   - b. rescue actions;
   - c. placement of children and women in temporary shelters;
   - d. rehabilitation including:
1. health recovery services;
2. psychosocial recovery services; and
3. legal assistance support;
   - e. social reintegration.

#### Article 67

1. The identification and receipt of complaints/reports as referred to in Article 66 paragraph (2) letter a includes:
   - a. ensuring readiness to receive complaints regarding issues of protection of children and women;
   - b. following up on information or complaints/reports received concerning issues of protection of children and women; and
   - c. identifying the type of problem, needs, and handling plan.
2. The Regional Apparatus Organization responsible for women's empowerment and child protection affairs is obligated to identify problems and receive complaints/reports.

#### Article 68

1. Rescue action as referred to in Article 66 paragraph (2) letter b shall be carried out if, based on the results of identification, it is known that the safety of the child or female victim of violence is threatened.
2. The action as referred to in paragraph (1) shall be carried out by removing the child from the situation and environment that threaten.
3. The OPD responsible for women's empowerment and child protection is obligated to carry out rescue action and coordinate with police officers/authorities or the civil service police unit.

#### Article 69

1. Placement of child or female victims of acts of violence in temporary shelters as referred to in Article 66 paragraph (2) letter c shall be carried out if based on identification results:
   - a. there is a threat to safety;
   - b. the child has no family/caregiver or guardian; and/or
   - c. the child cannot be reunited with family/caregiver/guardian and/or the community.
2. Placement as referred to in paragraph (1) shall be carried out for a certain period of time while the child or victim receives recovery services and/or until the family/caregiver/guardian is assessed to have the readiness to care for and protect the child.
3. Services provided to children and/or women while in temporary shelters include, among others, services for fulfilling basic needs and psychosocial assistance.
4. The OPD responsible for social affairs is obligated to provide temporary shelters.
5. The OPD responsible for social affairs may cooperate with the civil service police or the police in efforts to maintain the security of children or women whose safety is threatened while in temporary shelters.

#### Article 70

   - d. deoxyribose nucleic acid verification services;
   - e. delivery process services for pregnancy as a result of sexual violence and/or rape;
   - f. services for the provision of further referrals in accordance with the circumstances and condition of the victim; and

#### Article 71

   - b. counseling;
   - c. psychological therapy and social rehabilitation programs;
   - d. mental and spiritual guidance;
   - e. assistance; and
   - f. provision of conducive and representative places for the implementation of psychosocial recovery service activities.

#### Article 72

Legal assistance services as referred to in Article 66 paragraph (2) letter d number 3 include: a. ensuring the child is accompanied by a legal counsel; and b. facilitating assistance to children and women victims of violence, both in the examination process during court proceedings and outside court proceedings.

   - c. the best interests of the child, namely making what is best for the child as the primary consideration in every policy, program, and activity;
   - d. the right to life, survival, and development of the child, namely guaranteeing the right to life, survival and development of the child to the maximum extent possible; and e. respect for the views of the child, namely recognizing and ensuring that every child who has the ability to express their opinion, is given the opportunity to express their views freely on all matters that affect them.

# CHAPTER XVI

## PROHIBITIONS

#### Article 98

Every person is prohibited from:

   - a. receiving child visitors and employing children in discotheque businesses, nightclubs, bars, karaoke, pubs/music houses, massage parlors and steam baths/saunas, and/or other worst forms of child labor places.
   - b. producing, selling and/or promoting cigarettes in the school environment.
   - c. selling and/or promoting cigarettes, gambling equipment, contraceptives and goods that can damage the morals of children.
   - d. broadcasting and showing pornographic acts in community activities that can be accessed by children.
   - e. receiving child visitors during school hours or allowing child visitors after nine o'clock at night to access the internet at internet cafes.
   - f. refusing complaints and/or collecting fees in providing services to victims at Protection Service Institutions.
   - g. treating children in a discriminatory manner that results in children experiencing losses both material and moral thereby hindering their social function;
   - h. treating children with disabilities in a discriminatory manner;
      - i. placing, allowing, involving, ordering the involvement of children in situations of maltreatment and neglect;
   - j. placing, allowing, committing, ordering to commit or participating in committing violence against children;
   - k. committing violence or threat of violence forcing children to have sexual intercourse with him/her or with another person;
   - l. committing violence or threat of violence, coercing, committing deceit, committing a series of lies, or persuading children to commit or allow obscene acts to be committed;
   - m. placing, allowing, committing, ordering to commit or participating in committing kidnapping, sale, and/or trafficking of children;
   - n. obstructing children from enjoying their own culture, acknowledging and practicing the teachings of their religion and/or using their own language without neglecting access to community and cultural development;
   - o. recruiting or exploiting children for military and/or other interests, and leaving children without protection of life;
   - p. placing, allowing, committing, ordering to commit, or participating in committing economic and/or sexual exploitation of children;
   - q. intentionally placing, allowing, involving, ordering the involvement of children in the abuse, as well as production and distribution of narcotics and/or psychotropic substances; and
   - r. intentionally placing, allowing, involving, ordering the involvement of children in the abuse, as well as production and distribution of alcohol and other addictive substances.

# CHAPTER XVII

## INVESTIGATION PROVISIONS

#### Article 99

1. Certain Civil Servant Officials within the Regional Government are given special authority as investigators to conduct investigations into criminal acts regulated in this Regional Regulation as referred to in the Criminal Procedure Code.
2. The investigative authority as referred to in paragraph (1), is as follows: a. to receive, search for, collect, and examine information or reports concerning criminal acts of child and women protection so that such information or reports become more complete and clear;
   - b. to examine, search for, and collect information regarding individuals or entities about the truth of acts committed in connection with criminal acts of child and women protection;
   - c. to request information and evidence from individuals or entities in connection with criminal acts of child and women protection;
   - d. to examine books, records, and other documents concerning criminal acts of child and women protection;
   - e. to conduct searches to obtain evidence of bookkeeping, records, and other documents, as well as to carry out seizure of such evidence;
   - f. to request assistance of experts in the framework of carrying out investigation duties of criminal acts of child and women protection;
   - g. to order to stop and/or prohibit a person from leaving a room or place when an examination is in progress and to examine the identity of persons, objects, and/or documents carried;
   - h. to photograph a person related to criminal acts of child and women protection;
      - i. to summon persons to be heard for their statements and examined as suspects or witnesses;
   - j. to terminate the investigation; and/or
   - k. to take other necessary actions for the smooth conduct of investigation of criminal acts of child and women protection in accordance with the provisions of Laws and Regulations.
3. In the event the investigation meets the elements as referred to in paragraph (1) the Investigator notifies the commencement of the investigation and submits the results of the investigation to the Public Prosecutor through the Investigator Official of the Police of the Republic of Indonesia, in accordance with the provisions stipulated in the Criminal Procedure Code.

# CHAPTER XVIII

## SANCTION PROVISIONS

#### Article 100

1. Institutions or bodies designated by the Mayor to carry out service duties that violate service principles may be subject to administrative sanctions.
2. Perpetrators of acts of violence shall be subject to criminal sanctions in accordance with the applicable Laws.
3. Any person who deliberately fails to provide protection to women and children resulting in the occurrence of violence, allows violence to occur, fails to report and fails to provide protection to victims, shall be subject to sanctions as stipulated in the applicable Laws and Regulations.

##### CONCERNING

##### PROTECTION OF CHILDREN AND WOMEN

##### I. GENERAL

The implementation of protection for children and women is carried out based on the principles of humanity, integration and justice. So that every person must receive their right to protection against a sense of security and freedom from torture or treatment that degrades human dignity which is contrary to what is aspired to in the Preamble of the 1945 Constitution of the Republic of Indonesia, namely the creation of a just and civilized humanity.

In Article 2 of Law Number 23 of 2002 concerning Child Protection that the implementation of Child Protection is based on Pancasila and grounded in the 1945 Constitution of the Republic of Indonesia as well as the basic principles of the Convention on the Rights of the Child including:

   - a. non-discrimination;
   - b. the best interests of the child;
   - c. the right to life, survival, and development; and
   - d. respect for the views of the child.

In Article 3 of Law Number 23 of 2004 concerning the Elimination of Domestic Violence that the elimination of domestic violence is implemented based on the principles of:

   - a. respect for human rights;
   - b. justice and gender equality;
   - c. non-discrimination; and
   - d. protection of victims.

In Article 3 of Law Number 23 of 2002 concerning Child Protection that Child Protection aims to ensure the fulfillment of children's rights so that they can live, grow, develop, and participate optimally in accordance with human worth and dignity, and receive protection from violence and discrimination, for the realization of quality Indonesian children who are noble in character and prosperous.

In Article 4 of Law Number 23 of 2004 concerning the Elimination of Domestic Violence that the elimination of domestic violence aims to:

   - a. prevent all forms of violence in the household;
   - b. protect victims of domestic violence;
   - c. take action against perpetrators of domestic violence; and
   - d. maintain the integrity of a harmonious and prosperous household.

To realize law enforcement and the fulfillment of the basic rights of children and women, the Government of Singkawang City is obligated to provide protection from acts of violence and discrimination as well as the fulfillment of civil rights and freedoms which are set forth in a policy at the regional level.

The establishment of the Regional Regulation concerning the Protection of Children and Women is an elaboration of the legislation above it which regulates the rights of children and women. In the Regional Regulation concerning the Protection of Children and Women, the norms formulated are efforts to overcome the problems faced in the implementation of the protection of children and women in Singkawang City. The implementation of the protection of children and women is carried out to realize the fulfillment of the rights of children and women in accordance with human worth and dignity, and to receive protection from violence, discrimination, exploitation, and neglect for the realization of human resource development in Singkawang City that is faithful, intelligent, quality, noble in character and prosperous.
