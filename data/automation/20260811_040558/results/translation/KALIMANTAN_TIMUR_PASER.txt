c.	that based on the considerations as referred to in letter a and letter b, it is necessary to establish a Regional Regulation concerning Protection of Women Against Acts of Violence.

CHAPTER I
GENERAL PROVISIONS
Article 1
In this Regional Regulation, what is meant by:
1. Regional Government is the Regent as an element of the Regional Government administration who leads the implementation of government affairs that are the authority of the autonomous region of Paser Regency.
2. Regent is the Regent of Paser.
3. Women are all women who are both inside and outside the household.
4. Violence is any act that results in or may result in misery or suffering whether physical, sexual, economic, social, or psychological to the victim.
5. Physical violence is an act that results in pain, injury, wounds or disability to a person's body, miscarriage, fainting and/or causing death.
6. Psychological violence is an act that results in fear, loss of self-confidence, loss of ability to act, feelings of helplessness and or severe psychological suffering to a person.
7. Sexual violence is any act in the form of sexual harassment, forced sexual intercourse, whether unnatural or unwanted with another person for commercial purposes and or certain purposes.
8. Household neglect is any person who neglects another person in their household, whereas according to the law applicable to them or due to an agreement or contract they are obligated to provide livelihood, care, or maintenance to that person.
9. Victim is a woman who experiences misery and/or suffering either directly or indirectly as a result of violence.
10. Protection is a form of service that must be implemented by law enforcement officers or security officers to provide a sense of security, both physical and mental to victims or witnesses from threats, disturbances, terror and violence from any party given at the investigation, inquiry, determination and examination stages in court hearings.
11. Protection of women is all efforts aimed at protecting women and providing a sense of security in fulfilling their rights by providing consistent and systematic attention aimed at achieving gender equality.
12. Service is an action that must be immediately taken for the victim when seeing, hearing and knowing of the impending, ongoing or occurred violence against the victim (women).
13. Companion is a person or representative from an institution who has the expertise to accompany victims to conduct counseling, therapy and advocacy for the strengthening and recovery of victims of violence.
14. Integrated Service Center, hereinafter abbreviated as PPT is an institution providing services to victims of violence, based in hospitals, managed jointly in the form of medical services (including medico-legal), psycho-social and legal services.
15. Safe house is a temporary residence used to provide protection to victims in accordance with determined operational standards.
16. Community is individuals, families, groups, social organizations and/or community organizations.
17. Family is the smallest unit in society consisting of husband-wife, or husband-wife and their children, or father and their children, and mother and their children.
18. Household is family members and relatives (grandchildren, nieces/nephews, older siblings, younger siblings, grandfathers, grandmothers, cousins and so on) and non-relatives (servants, drivers and so on) who live and eat from one kitchen and reside in one house.
19. Gender is a characteristic inherent to men and women that is socially and culturally constructed.
20. Gender injustice is a system and structure in which both men and women become victims of that system.
21. Gender justice is a state where fair treatment is created for men and women by considering experiences, needs, difficulties, concerns as women and also as men.
22. Gender equality is the equality of conditions for men and women to obtain opportunities and their rights as human beings, to be able to play a role and participate in political, legal, economic, socio-cultural, educational and defense and security activities, as well as equality in enjoying the results of development.
23. Gender mainstreaming (PUG) is a strategy built to integrate gender into an integral dimension of planning, formulation, implementation, monitoring, and evaluation of development policies and programs.

CHAPTER II
PRINCIPLES AND OBJECTIVES
Article 2
Protection of women is carried out with due regard to religion, customs, and the social-culture of the community while prioritizing the basic principles of women's rights.
Protection of women includes efforts to protect and provide a sense of security to women by fulfilling their rights, namely through empowerment to improve the quality of life of women through improvement of the physical and mental condition of women in the fulfillment of their rights and life needs as part of human rights.
The implementation of protection of women victims of violence is based on the principles of: Respect for the rights of victims;
Justice and gender equality;
Non-discrimination;
The best interests of victims.

Article 3
The objectives of implementing protection for women against acts of violence are: to prevent all forms of violence against women;
to take action against perpetrators of violence against women in accordance with applicable laws and regulations;
to provide integrated protection and services to women victims of gender-based violence occurring in households and/or public spaces in the Paser Regency area.

CHAPTER III
OBLIGATIONS OF REGIONAL GOVERNMENT
Article 4
Regional government in implementing protection of women from acts of violence has the following obligations: to formulate strategic plans for protection of women against acts of violence for the short, medium, and long term as an integrated part of the Regional Medium-Term Development Plan (RPJMD);
to prevent and handle in an integrated manner the occurrence of acts of violence against women;
to encourage the responsibility of police institutions, prosecutorial institutions, judicial institutions, non-governmental organizations, religious institutions, and the general public to conduct coordination and cooperation in preventing and handling the occurrence of acts of violence against women;
to optimize the role and function of institutions within the regional government environment related to preventing and handling protection of women from acts of violence;
to provide facilities and infrastructure;
to conduct guidance, supervision and evaluation.

CHAPTER IV
SCOPE
Article 5
The implementation of protection of women from acts of violence includes: prevention;
handling;
empowerment.

Article 6
Protection of women from violence includes protection of women in the household, in the community, in the workplace and in conflict and disaster areas.
Protection of women from violence as referred to in paragraph (1) includes women with disabilities, elderly women.

CHAPTER V
IMPLEMENTATION OF PROTECTION
First Part
Prevention

Article 7
The target of prevention as referred to in Article 5 letter a is every woman.

Article 8
(1) Prevention as referred to in Article 5 letter a includes:
   a. collecting data and information on women victims of violence as well as laws and regulations;
   b. conducting education on anti-violence values toward women;
   c. conducting dissemination/outreach of laws and regulations related to the implementation of protection for women victims of violence;
   d. establishing and facilitating the operation of integrated service institutions for women victims by involving elements of the community;
   e. encouraging public concern regarding the importance of protection for women victims of violence;
   f. increasing women’s awareness of the importance of complying with ethics, religious norms, and cultural norms in behavior.
(2) Prevention as referred to in paragraph (1) is carried out by the Regency Regional Government, police institutions, prosecutorial institutions, judicial institutions, non-governmental organizations, and the community in accordance with their duties, functions, and responsibilities.

Second Part
Handling

Article 9
The target of the handling as referred to in Article 5 letter b is every woman.

Article 10
Forms of services for women victims of violence that are provided include: medical services, in the form of treatment and recovery of physical injuries aimed at restoring the physical condition of victims carried out by medical personnel and paramedics;
medicolegal services constitute a form of medical service for the purposes of evidence in the legal field;
psychosocial services constitute services provided by companions in order to restore the traumatic condition of victims, including the provision of safe houses to protect victims from various threats and intimidation against victims and provide social support so that victims have a sense of confidence, strength, and independence in resolving their problems;
legal services to assist victims in undergoing the judicial process;
economic independence services in the form of services for skills training and providing economic access so that victims can be independent.
The service mechanism as referred to in paragraph (1) shall be organized according to standard operational procedures which will be further regulated by a Regent Regulation.

Article 11
The provision of integrated services to women victims of violence is carried out free of charge, quickly, safely, with empathy, non-discriminatory, easily accessible, and with a guarantee of confidentiality.

Article 12
The integrated service provider is obligated to provide services in accordance with the service principles as referred to in Article 11.

Third Part
Empowerment

Article 13
Women Victims of violence in the family environment, community including in conflict areas and in the workplace have the right to be empowered.
Empowerment as referred to in paragraph (1) is directed to create conditions that enable women's potential to develop, work and earn a living independently and not depend on others.

Article 14
The Manpower Office and the Cooperatives and Small and Medium Enterprises Office empower women victims of violence.
In empowering women as referred to in paragraph (1), implementation shall be carried out in accordance with the people's economic empowerment program.
The people's economic empowerment program as referred to in paragraph (2) shall be conducted through:
1. increasing the economic productivity of local women by providing entrepreneurship guidance for women in micro and small enterprises;
2. developing models for increasing women's economic productivity through partnerships between local government and the community.

Article 15
The Manpower Office and the Cooperatives and Small and Medium Enterprises Office, in conducting entrepreneurship development as referred to in Article 14 paragraph (3) letters a and b, shall be in the form of:
a. strengthening independence, namely increasing the knowledge, proficiency, and work skills of women victims of violence to more easily obtain employment;
b. productive economic business, namely fostering the entrepreneurial spirit and skills of women victims of violence in the economic field who are not accommodated to work in the formal sector.
Productive economic business as referred to in paragraph (1) letter b includes forms of business that can be carried out by women.

CHAPTER VI
ASSISTANCE
Article 16
Assistance is carried out by persons or institutions that have the expertise to conduct counseling, therapy and advocacy for the empowerment and recovery of women victims of violence and have cooperated with the organizer of integrated services.

CHAPTER VII
COMMUNITY PARTICIPATION
Article 17
(1) The community may participate in efforts to prevent and handle women victims of violence through individuals or institutions.
(2) Community participation as referred to in paragraph (1) is carried out through:
   a. providing information and/or reporting any potential violence and incidents of violence that come to their knowledge;
   b. preventing the occurrence of acts of violence against women;
   c. providing protection services to women who become victims of violence;
   d. assisting in the process of return, social rehabilitation, and social reintegration of women victims of acts of violence.

Article 18
The role of community participation as referred to in Article 17 paragraph (1) can be carried out by: individuals;
families;
non-governmental organizations;
social community organizations;
professional organizations;
business entities.

CHAPTER VIII
INSTITUTIONAL FRAMEWORK, GUIDANCE, AND SUPERVISION
Part One
Institutional Framework

Article 19
Handling as referred to in Article 9 is carried out in an integrated manner by related SKPD and/or service institutions that handle the protection of women from acts of violence.
The implementation of integrated services is coordinated by BPPKB or another name as further regulated by the Regent's Regulation.
The implementation of integrated services is in accordance with applicable laws and regulations.

Second Part
Guidance

Article 20
Regional governments are obligated to conduct guidance and supervision of the implementation of protection for women victims of violence.
Guidance as referred to in paragraph (1), includes: Guidelines and standards for fulfillment;
Technical guidance and training;
Provision of facilities;
Monitoring;
Evaluation.
Guidance as referred to in paragraph (2), is intended to realize the protection of women from acts of violence in accordance with minimum service standards implemented by SKPD and the community.
SKPD as referred to in paragraph (3) is the Regional Work Unit within the Regional Government of Paser Regency that is related to the protection of women against acts of violence.

Third Part
Supervision

Article 21
The regional government is obligated to conduct supervision of the implementation of the protection of women from acts of violence.
Supervision as referred to in paragraph (1) shall be conducted with the principles of being professional, transparent, and accountable.

Article 22
The community may supervise the implementation of the protection of women from acts of violence carried out by the regional government through a mechanism for submitting aspirations to the Regent or the DPRD.

CHAPTER IX
FINANCING
Article 23
Financing for the implementation of protection of women against acts of violence is sourced from: Regional Revenue and Expenditure Budget (APBD); and other lawful sources in accordance with the provisions of laws and regulations.

CHAPTER X
PROHIBITION OF VIOLENCE AGAINST WOMEN
Article 24
Every person is prohibited from committing violence against women either within the household and/or outside the household, by means of: physical violence;
psychological violence;
verbal violence;
sexual violence; or neglect.

Article 25
Physical violence as referred to in Article 24 letter a, is an act that results in pain, injury, wounds or disability to a person's body, miscarriage, fainting and/or causes death.

Article 26
Psychological violence as referred to in Article 24 point b, is an act that results in fear, loss of self-confidence, loss of the ability to act, feelings of helplessness and/or severe psychological suffering in a person.

Article 27
Verbal violence as referred to in Article 24 letter c, is an act using words that causes another person to feel insulted and humiliated.

Article 28
Sexual violence as referred to in Article 24 letter d, includes: forced sexual relations committed against women residing within the scope of the household or outside the household;
forced sexual relations against women within the scope of the household or outside the household with other persons for commercial purposes and/or certain purposes;

Article 29
Neglect as referred to in Article 21 letter e, includes: Every person is prohibited from neglecting people within their household scope, whereas according to the law applicable to them or due to agreement or contract they are obligated to provide livelihood, care, or maintenance to that person.
Neglect as referred to in paragraph (1) also applies to every person who causes economic dependence by means of limiting and/or prohibiting proper work inside or outside the home so that the victim is under the control of that person.

CHAPTER XI
RIGHTS OF VICTIMS
Article 30
Every female victim of acts of violence has the right to obtain protection, information, and integrated services.

Article 31
Integrated services as referred to in Article 30, include:
identification of victims;
legal assistance;
medical rehabilitation;
psychosocial rehabilitation;
social reintegration;
repatriation assistance;
empowerment.

Article 32
Every female victim has the right to receive assistance both psychologically and legally as well as to obtain guarantees for her rights related to her status as a wife, mother, child, family member or as a member of society.

CHAPTER XII
PROVISIONS ON SANCTIONS
Article 33
Every person who intentionally fails to provide protection to women thereby causing violence to occur, allows violence to occur, and/or fails to report and fails to provide protection to victims, shall be subject to sanctions as regulated in the applicable laws and regulations.
If officials appointed to provide protection fail to carry out their obligations and responsibilities, they shall be subject to sanctions in accordance with the applicable laws and regulations.
Service providers who carry out service duties in violation of service principles may be subject to sanctions in accordance with the mechanisms of the applicable laws and regulations.

CHAPTER XIII
OTHER PROVISIONS
Article 34
Matters not yet regulated in this Regional Regulation shall be further regulated by Regent Regulation.

CHAPTER XIV
CLOSING PROVISIONS
Article 35
This Regional Regulation shall come into force on the date of promulgation.

I. GENERAL
To prevent, protect victims, and take action against perpetrators of violence against women, the state and society are obligated to implement prevention, protection, and action against perpetrators in accordance with the Pancasila philosophy and the 1945 Constitution of the Republic of Indonesia. The state holds the view that all forms of violence, especially violence against women, constitute a violation of human rights and a crime against human dignity as well as a form of discrimination.

Such a view is based on Article 28 of the 1945 Constitution of the Republic of Indonesia, along with its amendments. Article 28G paragraph (1) of the 1945 Constitution of the Republic of Indonesia stipulates that "Every person shall have the right to protection of self, family, honor, dignity, and property under his/her control, and shall have the right to a sense of security and protection from the threat of fear to do or not do something which is a basic right". Article 28H paragraph (2) of the 1945 Constitution of the Republic of Indonesia stipulates that "Every person shall have the right to receive facilities and special treatment to obtain equal opportunities and benefits in order to achieve equality and justice".

Current developments show that acts of violence against women physically, psychologically, sexually, and through neglect in reality occur, thus requiring adequate legal instruments to eliminate violence against women.

The Government of Indonesia has signed the Universal Declaration of Human Rights (UDHR 1948) and ratified CEDAW (Law Number 7 of 1984 concerning the Elimination of all forms of Discrimination against women), therefore it is obligated to comply with these provisions. Likewise in the fourth amendment of the 1945 Constitution, Chapter XA of the 1945 Constitution concerning Human Rights, states that the enforcement and fulfillment of Human Rights is the responsibility of the state, especially the Government.

In the National Action Plan for the Elimination of Violence Against Women of 1999, it is stated that women as mothers of the nation and children as successors of the nation are creatures created by God Almighty; therefore, they must be protected and their honor, dignity, and self-worth must be safeguarded properly and proportionally, both legally, economically, politically, socially, and culturally, without distinguishing ethnicity, religion, race, and group/class.

Regional governments also have the obligation to prevent, protect victims, and take action against perpetrators of violence against women. For this purpose, regional governments require regional regulations that can be used as instruments to realize prevention, protection and action against perpetrators of violence against women.

The best interests of victims are all actions concerning victims carried out by the government, society, legislative bodies and judicial bodies, therefore the best interests of victims must be the primary consideration.

Article 31
Victim identification is the collection of data on women who are victims of violence. The data collection is carried out by SKPD and/or service institutions established by the regional government that are given the authority to carry out that function.
Legal assistance is legal services to help victims in undergoing the judicial process.
Medical rehabilitation is medical services, in the form of treatment and recovery of physical injuries that aim to restore the physical condition of victims carried out by medical personnel and paramedics.
Psychosocial rehabilitation is services provided by assistants/counselors in order to restore the traumatic condition of victims, including the provision of safe houses to protect victims from various threats and intimidation against victims and to provide social support so that victims have a sense of confidence, strength, and independence in resolving their problems. Social reintegration is an effort by the regional government through SKPD and/or service institutions together with the community to help women victims of violence in adapting and integrating in the community environment where they live.
Return assistance is an effort by the regional government through SKPD and/or service institutions to help return women victims of violence to their homes or other intended destinations safely.
Empowerment is the regional government through SKPD and/or service institutions providing assistance to women victims of violence in the form of training and opening access to capital with the aim that women victims of violence can be independent.
