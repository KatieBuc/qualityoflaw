REGENT OF PURWAKARTA
PROVINCE OF WEST JAVA
REGIONAL REGULATION OF PURWAKARTA REGENCY
NUMBER 6 OF 2021
CONCERNING
THE ADMINISTRATION OF THE PROTECTION OF WOMEN AND CHILDREN
BY THE GRACE OF THE ALMIGHTY GOD

REGENT OF PURWAKARTA,

Considering : a. that every citizen has the right to obtain a sense of security and freedom from all forms of Violence;
b. that violence against women and children in the Region tends to increase, therefore the Regional Government and/or community need to play an active role optimally to provide protection, so that women and children are prevented and freed from violence or threats of violence, torture, or treatment that degrades human dignity and worth;
c. that protection of women and children is a mandatory concurrent affair which is the authority, obligation, and responsibility of the Regional Government, therefore regulation is needed as a basis for implementing comprehensive and integrated protection of women and children;
d. that based on the considerations as referred to in letter a, letter b and letter c, it is necessary to establish a Regional Regulation on the Implementation of Protection of Women and Children;

To Establish: REGIONAL REGULATION ON THE IMPLEMENTATION OF PROTECTION FOR WOMEN AND CHILDREN

CHAPTER I
GENERAL PROVISIONS
Article 1
In this Regional Regulation, what is meant by:
1. Region is Purwakarta Regency.
2. Regional Government is the administration of governmental affairs by the Regional Government and DPRD according to the principles of autonomy and assistance tasks with the principle of the widest possible autonomy within the system and principles of the Unitary State of the Republic of Indonesia as referred to in the 1945 Constitution of the Republic of Indonesia.
3. Regional Government is the Regent as an element of regional government administration who leads the implementation of governmental affairs that are the authority of the autonomous region.
4. Regent is the Regent of Purwakarta.
5. Regional House of Representatives hereinafter abbreviated as DPRD is a regional people's representative institution that is positioned as an element of Regional Government administration.
6. Integrated Service Center for Women and Children Empowerment hereinafter abbreviated as P2TP2A is an integrated service center in efforts to empower women in various fields of development as well as protection of women and children from various types of discrimination and acts of violence, including trafficking in persons, which is established by the Regional Government or community-based.
7. Protection of Women and Children is all activities to protect the rights of women and children as well as provide a sense of security in the fulfillment of their rights without violence and discrimination in accordance with human dignity.
8. Woman is an adult human being of female sex and a person who is legally recognized as a woman.
9. Child is a person who has not yet reached the age of 18 (eighteen) years including a child who is still in the womb.
10. Child in contact with the law (ABH) is a child who is in conflict with the law, a child who becomes a victim of a criminal act, and a child who becomes a witness to a criminal act.
11. Violence is any act that results in misery or suffering physically, psychologically, sexually, and/or neglect, including threats to commit acts, coercion or deprivation of liberty unlawfully.
12. Discrimination is any restriction, harassment or exclusion that is directly or indirectly based on human differentiation on the basis of religion, tribe, race, ethnicity, group, class, social status, economic status.
13. Victim is a woman and child who experiences misery and/or physical, mental suffering and or economic loss either directly or indirectly as a result of violence and criminal acts.
14. Service is immediate activities and actions carried out by professional personnel in accordance with their respective professions in the form of counseling, therapy and advocacy for the strengthening and recovery of victims of violence.
15. Assistance is all actions in the form of counseling, psychological therapy, advocacy, and guidance and/or spiritual counseling, for the self-strengthening of victims of violence to resolve the problems they face.
16. Repatriation is an effort to return women and children victims of violence to their families, substitute families, or communities that can provide protection and fulfillment of their needs.
17. Rehabilitation is the recovery of victims from psychosocial disorders and the restoration of normal social functioning, both in the family and in society.
18. Safe House hereinafter referred to as Shelter is a temporary residence used to provide protection to victims in accordance with predetermined operational standards.
19. Community is individuals, families, groups, social organizations and/or community organizations.

CHAPTER II
PRINCIPLES AND OBJECTIVES
Article 2
The implementation of protection for women and children is based on the principles of:
a. respect and fulfillment of the rights of women and children;
b. justice and gender equality;
c. non-discrimination;
d. best interests of women and children victims of violence; and
e. legal certainty.

Article 3
The implementation of protection for women and children aims to:
a. guarantee the fulfillment of the rights of women and children so that they can participate optimally in society in accordance with human dignity and worth;
b. provide protection and provide a sense of security to women and children from all forms of violence and discrimination;
c. prevent and eliminate all forms of violence and exploitation against women and children including trafficking in persons;
d. provide services to women and children victims of violence, reporters, and witnesses; and
e. empower women and children victims of violence.

BAB III
OBLIGATIONS OF REGIONAL GOVERNMENT
Article 4
The Regional Government is obligated to provide protection for women and children.

Article 5
In the implementation of protection of women and children, the Regional Government is obligated to:
a. establish basic policies for the implementation of protection of women and children in the Regional Action Plan;
b. implement policies for the protection of women and children established by the government and provincial government;
c. coordinate the implementation of policies, programs, and activities for the protection of the rights of women and children among regional apparatus organizations;
d. cooperate with other regions for the implementation of policies, programs, and activities for the protection of the rights of women and children;
e. strengthen institutional Gender mainstreaming and children's rights mainstreaming for the implementation of policies, programs, and activities for the protection of the rights of women and children;
f. establish P2TP2A in the Region;
g. ensure the implementation of excellent service in the protection of women and children, including for women and children victims of violence;
h. formulate a data system for the protection of women and children, including data collection on violence against women and children;
i. establish UPTD PPA (Protection of Women and Children).

CHAPTER IV
PLANNING
Article 6
The Regent establishes basic policies for the implementation of protection of women and children in the Region.

Article 7
(1) The basic policy as referred to in Article 6 shall be elaborated in the Regional Action Plan.
(2) The Regional Action Plan as referred to in paragraph (1) shall be integrated with the Regional Long-Term Development Plan, Regional Medium-Term Development Plan and Regional Government Work Plan documents.
(3) The Regional Action Plan as referred to in paragraph (1) shall at minimum contain:
a. action programs and activities for the protection of women and children, including women and children victims of violence;
b. regional action programs and activities to prevent all forms of violence and discrimination against women and children;
c. action programs and activities for the improvement and enhancement of protection services for women and children victims of violence; and
d. action programs and activities for the empowerment of women and children victims of violence.
(4) Provisions regarding the Regional Action Plan as referred to in paragraph (1) shall be further regulated by Regent Regulation.

Article 8
The Regional Action Plan must be used as a guideline for the Regional Government in formulating policies related to the implementation of protection of women and children.

CHAPTER V
IMPLEMENTATION OF PROTECTION
Part One
General

Article 9
(1) The Regent is obligated to implement policies, programs and activities for the protection of women and children in an integrated manner in the context of fulfilling the rights of women and children in the region.
(2) In carrying out the duties as referred to in paragraph (1), the Agency coordinates with regional apparatus organizations, law enforcement officials and the community.

Second Part
Protection of Women and Children Victims of Violence

Paragraph I
Forms of Violence

Article 10
Violence against women and children can take the form of:
a. physical violence;
b. psychological violence;
c. sexual violence;
d. neglect; and
e. exploitation.

Article 11
Physical violence as referred to in Article 10 letter a is caused by acts that result in pain, injury, wounds or disability to a person's body, miscarriage, fainting and/or causing death.

Article 12
Psychological violence as referred to in Article 10 letter b is caused by acts that result in fear, loss of self-confidence, loss of the ability to act, feelings of helplessness and/or severe psychological suffering in a person.

Article 13
Sexual violence as referred to in Article 10 letter c is caused by:
a. acts in the form of sexual harassment;
b. forced sexual intercourse;
c. forced sexual intercourse in an unnatural or unwanted manner;
d. forced sexual intercourse with another person for commercial purposes and or certain purposes; and/or
e. persuasion, deception and a series of lies that result in sexual violence.

Article 14
Neglect as referred to in Article 10 letter d is caused by:
a. acts that result in the failure to fulfill the needs of children properly, whether physical, mental, spiritual or social, committed by parents, guardians, or other parties responsible for care;
b. acts of deliberately neglecting to maintain, care for, or look after children as appropriate, committed by parents, guardians or any other party responsible for their care;
c. acts of neglecting a person within the household scope, whereas according to the law applicable to them or due to an agreement or contract they are obligated to provide livelihood, care, or maintenance to that person; and/or;
d. acts that result in economic dependency by means of limiting and/or prohibiting proper work inside or outside the home so that the victim is under the control of that person.

Article 15
Exploitation as referred to in Article 10 letter e is caused by:
a. acts that exploit economically or sexually with the intention of benefiting oneself or others; and/or;
b. all forms of utilization of sexual organs or other body organs of the victim to obtain benefits, including but not limited to all activities of prostitution or sexual abuse.

Paragraph 2
Rights of Victims

Article 16
Every woman and child victim of violence has the right to obtain:
a. protection;
b. information;
c. excellent service;
d. continuous handling up to the rehabilitation stage;
e. confidential handling;
f. psychological and legal assistance; and
g. guarantee of rights related to status as a family member as well as a community member and the right to restitution.

Article 17
In addition to obtaining rights as referred to in Article 16, child victims of violence also obtain special rights in the form of:
a. the right to respect and full enjoyment for survival, growth and development;
b. the right to basic services;
c. the right to equal protection;
d. the right to be free from various stigma; and
e. the right to obtain freedom.

Article 18
(1) In the event that women and children victims of violence require continuous handling, then women and children victims of violence have the right to stay in a safe house or alternative residence.
(2) Continuous handling as referred to in paragraph (1) constitutes handling at the rehabilitation stage, whether conducted by P2TP2A, individuals, non-governmental organizations, or by the community.

Article 19
(1) Safe houses as referred to in Article 18 paragraph (1) provide temporary safe places and/or shelter for victims of violence until their rights are restored.
(2) The organization and/or management of safe houses as referred to in paragraph (1) shall be carried out by the relevant Service.
(3) Provisions regarding the organization of safe houses as referred to in paragraph (2) shall be further regulated by a Regent Regulation.

Paragraph 3
Rights of Witnesses and Perpetrators

Article 20
(1) Every woman and child who becomes a witness or perpetrator of violence has the right to obtain information, protection, rehabilitation, confidential handling, psychological and legal assistance, as well as the fulfillment of their rights related to their status as a wife, mother, child, family member or as a member of society.
(2) The fulfillment of rights as referred to in paragraph (1) shall be implemented in accordance with the provisions of laws and regulations.

Paragraph 4
Scope

Article 21
Protection of women and children victims of violence includes:
a. prevention;
b. services;
c. empowerment; and
d. social rehabilitation

Article 22
(1) Regional Government conducts prevention as referred to in Article 21 letter a by means of:
a. socializing laws and regulations related to the implementation of protection of women and children;
b. providing counseling/guidance;
c. providing education about the dangers of domestic violence;
d. establishing cooperation networks in violence prevention efforts;
e. conducting coordination, integration, and synchronization of violence prevention based on partnership patterns;
f. collecting data and information as well as establishing a violence prevention system, mapping of locations or areas prone to violence;
g. conducting monitoring and evaluation of the implementation of protection of women and children;
h. increasing public awareness in behaving in accordance with social norms.
(2) In addition to prevention efforts conducted by the Regional Government as referred to in paragraph (1), prevention efforts are also conducted by families and/or communities.
(3) Prevention as referred to in paragraph (1) is implemented in an integrated manner by P2TP2A.

Article 23
(1) Services as referred to in Article 21 letter b are implemented through activities:
a. complaint services;
b. health services;
c. counseling;
d. spiritual guidance;
e. social services;
f. legal aid and assistance services; and/or
g. repatriation and social reintegration.
(2) Services as referred to in paragraph (1) are implemented with:
a. fast and precise;
b. safe and comfortable;
c. sense of empathy;
d. non-discrimination;
e. easily accessible;
f. not charged; and
g. guaranteed confidentiality.
(3) Services as referred to in paragraph (1) are implemented in an integrated manner by P2TP2A.

Article 24
(1) Empowerment as referred to in Article 21 letter c shall be conducted through:
a. job training;
b. productive economic enterprises and joint business groups; and
c. capital assistance.
(2) Job training as referred to in paragraph (1) letter a shall include:
a. skills training;
b. field work practice; and
c. apprenticeship.
(3) Productive economic enterprises and joint business groups as referred to in paragraph (1) letter b shall include:
a. entrepreneurial skills training; and
b. business actor mentoring.
(4) Capital assistance as referred to in paragraph (1) letter c shall include:
a. facilities and infrastructure assistance; and
b. working/business capital assistance facilities.
(5) Empowerment as referred to in paragraph (1) shall be implemented by P2TP2A.

Third Part
Special Protection of Children

Article 25
(1) Regional Government is obligated and responsible to provide special protection for children.
(2) Special protection for children as referred to in paragraph (1) is provided to:
a. children in emergency situations;
b. children in conflict with the law;
c. children from minority and isolated groups;
d. children who are exploited economically and/or sexually;
e. children who are victims of narcotics, alcohol, psychotropic substances, and other addictive substances abuse;
f. children who are victims of pornography;
g. children with HIV/AIDS;
h. children who are victims of kidnapping, sale, and/or trafficking;
i. children who are victims of physical and/or psychological violence;
j. children who are victims of sexual crimes;
k. children who are victims of terrorist networks;
l. children with disabilities;
m. children who are victims of maltreatment and neglect;
n. children with deviant social behavior; and
o. children who are victims of stigmatization from labeling related to their parents' condition.
(3) Provisions regarding procedures for special protection of children as referred to in paragraph (2) shall be further regulated by Regent Regulation.

Fourth Part
Assistance

Article 26
(1) Assistance is carried out by individuals or institutions that have the expertise to conduct counseling, therapy and advocacy for the strengthening and recovery of victims of violence, perpetrators and witnesses.
(2) Further provisions regarding assistance as referred to in paragraph (1) shall be further regulated by a Regent Regulation.

Part Five
Recovery of Victims, Witnesses, and Perpetrators

Article 27
(1) For the purpose of recovery, women and child victims, witnesses and perpetrators of violence may obtain services from:
a. Social institutions (groups or institutions that have scope in women and children) and/or;
b. Social workers (Individuals who have competence to carry out recovery or rehabilitation) namely:
1. Nurses
2. Health workers
3. Counseling workers
(2) What is meant by providing recovery services carried out by social institutions as referred to in paragraph (1) letter a is as follows:
a. volunteer companions make efforts to:
1. build equal relationships with victims so they are willing to open up in expressing their problems;
2. empathize and not blame victims regarding or related to their problems;
3. convince victims that no one may commit acts of violence;
4. ask what they want to do and what assistance is needed;
5. provide information and connect with institutions or individuals who can help overcome their problems; and/or
6. help provide information about legal consultation services
b. spiritual counselors make efforts to:
1. gather information and listen to complaints from victims;
2. strengthen the faith and piety of victims and encourage them to carry out worship according to their respective religions and beliefs.
3. suggest solutions to domestic violence problems according to the respective religions and beliefs of victims.
4. provide understanding regarding equality between men and women.
(3) What is meant by providing recovery services carried out by social workers as referred to in paragraph (1) letter b has the following provisions:
a. explore victim problems to help solve their problems;
b. restore victims from traumatic conditions through psychosocial therapy;
c. make referrals to hospitals or safe houses or service centers or other alternative places according to victim needs;
d. accompany victims in recovery efforts through assistance and counseling; and/or
e. carry out resocialization so that victims can return to carrying out their social functions in society.

Article 28
(1) Women and children who are victims, witnesses and perpetrators of violence have the right to receive health examination services by Health Facilities in accordance with professional standards.
(2) In the event that women and children who are victims, witnesses, and perpetrators of violence require treatment, health workers are obligated to provide health recovery and rehabilitation services.
(3) The provision of health recovery and rehabilitation services as referred to in paragraph (2) shall be implemented in accordance with the provisions of laws and regulations.

Article 29
(1) Women and children who are Victims, witnesses and perpetrators of violence have the right to receive counseling services from social workers, volunteer companions, and/or spiritual advisors.
(2) Social workers, volunteer companions, and/or spiritual advisors as referred to in paragraph (1) are obligated to provide services to women and children who are victims, witnesses and perpetrators of violence in the form of providing counseling to strengthen, provide a sense of security and rehabilitation.

CHAPTER VI
ESTABLISHMENT OF UPTD PPA
Article 30
(1) In the context of implementing protection for women and children, the Regional Government establishes UPTD PPA (Protection of Women and Children) as a service center for the protection of women and children.
(2) UPTD PPA as referred to in paragraph (1) consists of elements of regional apparatus that oversee affairs of:
a. education;
b. youth;
c. social;
d. cooperatives;
e. industry;
f. manpower;
g. trade;
h. population and civil registration;
i. women's empowerment and child protection;
j. public peace and order;
k. regional general hospital;
l. health;
(3) UPTD PPA as referred to in paragraph (1) shall be stipulated by Regent Regulation.
(4) Provisions regarding the organizational structure, duties and functions of UPTD PPA shall be further regulated by Regent Regulation.

CHAPTER VII
COOPERATION
Article 31
(1) In the implementation of the protection of women and children, the Regional Government may cooperate with:
a. other regional governments;
b. commissions/agencies/government institutions in the field of protection of women and children;
c. the police;
d. advocates;
e. health workers;
f. the District Prosecutor's Office;
g. the District Court;
h. PK Bapas (Community Counselor of the Correctional Center);
i. social workers;
j. volunteer assistants;
k. spiritual advisors;
l. non-governmental organizations engaged in the field of protection of women and children; and
m. the community.
(2) Cooperation as referred to in paragraph (1) shall be in the form of:
a. dissemination/socialization;
b. provision of legal aid;
c. health services;
d. victim assistance;
e. counseling;
f. social rehabilitation;
g. spiritual guidance;
h. repatriation and social reintegration; and
i. Family Welfare Consultation Institution (LK3).
(3) Cooperation as referred to in paragraph (1) shall be implemented in accordance with laws and regulations.

CHAPTER VIII
COMMUNITY PARTICIPATION
Article 32
(1) The community may participate in the implementation of protection for women and children.
(2) Community participation as referred to in paragraph (1) shall be in the form of:
a. establishing family partners at the kelurahan/village level;
b. establishing non-governmental organizations whose field of work is in the protection of women and children;
c. conducting independent socialization of women's rights;
d. undertaking preventive measures if indications of violence against women and children are found in the community;
e. providing first aid to women and children victims of violence; and
f. reporting to the competent authorities if violence against women and children occurs in their environment.
(3) Community participation as referred to in paragraph (1) may be carried out by individuals, non-governmental organizations, educational institutions, religious institutions, and the business sector.

Article 33
In implementing the protection of women and children, individuals, non-governmental organizations, educational institutions, religious institutions, and the business sector shall coordinate with UPTD PPA.

Article 34
(1) The Regional Government encourages the business sector to participate in the protection of women and children.
(2) Participation as referred to in paragraph (1) may take the form of:
a. socialization to employees within the company environment regarding the rights of women and children;
b. socialization to employees within the company environment regarding the dangers of acts of violence against women and children;
c. providing assistance in the implementation of protection of women and children as a form of corporate social responsibility.

CHAPTER IX
GUIDANCE AND SUPERVISION
Article 35
(1) The Regent conducts guidance and supervision of the implementation of protection for women and children, both carried out by UPTD PPA and non-governmental organizations.
(2) The guidance and supervision as referred to in paragraph (1) is conducted in order to realize the objectives of implementing protection for women and children in accordance with the mechanisms and service procedures stipulated in laws and regulations.
(3) The guidance and supervision as referred to in paragraph (1) is implemented by the relevant Agency.

Article 36
(1) Supervision as referred to in Article 35 is carried out on:
a. the implementation of policies, programs and activities for the protection of women and children; and
b. the fulfillment of guidelines and minimum service standards in the implementation of prevention, services and empowerment of women and children victims of violence.
(2) Supervision is carried out periodically and the results are used as input material for the formulation of policies, programs, and activities for the protection of women and children for the following year.

Article 37
Provisions regarding the guidance and supervision of the implementation of protection for women and children shall be further regulated by a Regent's Regulation.

CHAPTER X
REPORTING
Article 38
(1) UPTD PPA is obligated to report the implementation of protection services for women and children to the Regent through the relevant Agency.
(2) Reporting as referred to in paragraph (1) shall be submitted in writing and/or through a reporting application, covering:
a. administration;
b. finance;
c. services; and
d. performance.
(3) Submission of written reports as referred to in paragraph (2) shall be carried out at least once every 3 (three) months.

CHAPTER XI
FUNDING
Article 39
Funds for the implementation of protection of women and children are sourced from:
a. Regional Revenue and Expenditure Budget;
b. other lawful sources in accordance with the provisions of laws and regulations.

Article 40
(1) Regional Governments may provide funding assistance to community organizations, social organizations, or non-governmental organizations and universities that implement protection for women and children.
(2) Funding assistance as referred to in paragraph (1) shall be provided in accordance with regional financial capacity and implemented in accordance with the provisions of laws and regulations.

CHAPTER XII
PROHIBITIONS
Article 41
Every person is prohibited from:
a. treating women and children in a discriminatory manner that results in women and children experiencing loss, both material and moral, thereby hindering their social function;
b. treating women and children with disabilities in a discriminatory manner;
c. placing, allowing, involving, ordering the involvement of children in situations of maltreatment and neglect;
d. placing, allowing, committing, ordering the commission of or participating in committing violence against women and children;
e. committing violence or threat of violence to force women and children to engage in sexual intercourse with him or with another person;
f. committing violence or threat of violence, coercion, deception, a series of lies, or persuading women and children to commit or allow obscene acts to be committed;
g. placing, allowing, committing, ordering the commission of or participating in committing kidnapping, sale and/or trafficking of women and children;
h. obstructing women and children from enjoying their own culture, acknowledging and practicing their religious teachings and/or using their own language without neglecting access to community and cultural development;
i. placing, allowing, committing, ordering the commission of or participating in committing economic and/or sexual exploitation of women and children;
j. intentionally placing, allowing, involving women and children in the abuse, as well as production and distribution of narcotics and/or psychotropic substances; and/or
k. intentionally placing, allowing, involving, ordering the involvement of women and children in the abuse, as well as production and distribution of alcohol and other addictive substances.

Article 42
UPTD PPA or safe houses are prohibited from collecting fees in providing services to women and children victims, witnesses and perpetrators of violence

CHAPTER XIII
ADMINISTRATIVE SANCTIONS
Article 43
(1) Health Workers who refuse or are unwilling to provide health examination services as referred to in Article 28 shall be subject to administrative sanctions in the form of:
a. written warning;
b. freezing (temporary suspension) of the practice license/work permit;
c. practice restrictions for Health Workers in independent practice;
(2) Social workers, volunteer companions, and/or spiritual counselors who refuse or are unwilling to provide counseling services as referred to in Article 29 shall be subject to administrative sanctions in the form of:
a. reprimand; or
b. written warning.
(3) Provisions regarding the mechanism for imposing sanctions as referred to in paragraph (1) and paragraph (2) shall be further regulated by a Regent's Regulation.

CHAPTER XIV
PROVISIONS ON INVESTIGATION
Article 44
(1) Civil Servant Investigators within the Regional Government are given special authority to conduct investigations of criminal acts as referred to in the applicable Criminal Procedure Code.
(2) The authority of investigators as referred to in paragraph (1) is:
a. to receive, search for, collect and examine information or reports concerning criminal acts;
b. to examine, search for and collect information or reports concerning criminal acts;
c. to request information and evidence from individuals or entities in connection with criminal acts;
d. to examine books, records and other documents concerning criminal acts;
e. to conduct searches to obtain evidence of bookkeeping, records and other documents as well as to carry out seizures of such evidence;
f. to request the assistance of experts in the framework of carrying out the duties of investigating criminal acts;
g. to order a stop, prohibit a person from leaving a room or place when an examination is in progress, and to examine the identity of the person and/or the documents carried, as referred to in letter e;
h. to photograph persons related to criminal acts;
i. to summon persons to be heard for their statements and examined as suspects or witnesses;
j. to terminate investigations;
k. to take other actions necessary for the smooth conduct of investigations of criminal acts according to applicable law and which can be accounted for.
(3) Investigators as referred to in paragraph (1) shall notify the commencement of the investigation and submit the results of their investigation to the public prosecutor through Investigators of the Indonesian National Police of the Republic of Indonesia, in accordance with the provisions stipulated in the Criminal Procedure Code.

CHAPTER XV
CRIMINAL PROVISIONS
Article 45
(1) Every person who violates the prohibition as referred to in Article 42 and Article 43 shall be subject to punishment in accordance with the provisions of legislation.
(2) The criminal act as referred to in paragraph (1) is a violation.

CHAPTER XVI
CLOSING PROVISIONS
Article 46

REGIONAL REGULATION OF PURWAKARTA REGENCY
NUMBER 6 OF 2021
CONCERNING
THE IMPLEMENTATION OF PROTECTION FOR WOMEN AND CHILDREN
I. GENERAL ELUCIDATION
That protection for women and children is a mandatory concurrent affair which becomes the authority, obligation, and responsibility of the Regional Government, therefore regulation is needed as the basis for implementing protection for women and children that is comprehensive and integrated in a Regional Regulation.
