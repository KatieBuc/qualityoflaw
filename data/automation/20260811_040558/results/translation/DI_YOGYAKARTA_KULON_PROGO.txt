CHAPTER II
FORMS OF VIOLENCE
Article 5
Forms of violence include:
a. physical violence;
b. psychological violence;
c. sexual violence;
d. neglect;

Neglect includes:
a. neglect committed by parents, guardians, or other parties responsible for the care;
b. the act of deliberately neglecting to maintain, care for, or look after a child by parents, guardians, or other parties who should be responsible for the care as they ought to;
c. the act of neglecting a person within the scope of their household, whereas according to the law applicable to them or due to an agreement or contract they are obliged to provide livelihood, care, or maintenance to that person; and/or
d. an act that results in dependency and/or by means of restricting the economy by prohibiting the victim from working properly inside or outside the home so that the victim is under the control of that person.

Article 10
Exploitation as referred to in Article 5 letter e is caused by:
a. acts that exploit economically or sexually with the intention of benefiting oneself or others;
b. acts that, with or without the consent of the victim, include practices of prostitution, slavery or forced labor, similar services, oppression, extortion, utilization of physical aspects, sexual aspects, reproductive organs, or unlawfully transferring or transplanting tissues and/or organs, and/or utilizing the body or capabilities of a person by other parties to obtain material or immaterial benefits.

Third Part
Form and Mechanism of Service

Article 36
(1) Forms of services provided by PPT to victims include:
a. medical services, in the form of treatment and recovery of physical injuries aimed at restoring the physical condition of victims, carried out by medical personnel and paramedics;
b. medicolegal services constitute a form of medical service for evidentiary purposes in the legal field;
c. psychosocial services constitute services provided by companions in the context of recovery from the traumatic condition of victims;
g. right to complaint handling;
h. right to obtain facilitation in the judicial process; and/or
i. right to assistance.
(2) Children who become victims of violence, in addition to obtaining the rights as referred to in paragraph (1), also obtain special rights, as follows:
a. right to respect for survival, growth and development;
b. right to basic services;
c. right to obtain spiritual guidance;
d. right to equal protection;
e. right to be free from various stigma; and/or
f. right to obtain freedom.

Article 2
Letter a What is meant by “respect for the rights of victims” is the guarantee of fulfillment to ensure that the basic rights of victims are met.
Letter b What is meant by “justice” and equality of women is a situation where gender is treated equally and obtains equal opportunities to gain opportunities (access), as well as welfare;
What is meant by “gender equality” is equality of opportunities, rights, benefits, and equality in decision-making between women and men, including in entering employment opportunities both in the formal and informal sectors.
Letter c What is meant by “non-discrimination” is an attitude and treatment toward victims without making distinctions based on sex, race, ethnicity, religion, and between groups.
Letter d What is meant by “the best interests of victims” is that all actions concerning victims are carried out by the Regional Government, society, legislative bodies and judicial bodies, so that the best interests of victims must be the primary consideration.
