a. that the government has the obligation to provide protection, advancement, enforcement and fulfillment of Human Rights of every citizen including the Rights of Women and Children;

## GENERAL PROVISIONS

## Part One

Definitions, Meanings and Terms

#### Article 1

In this Regional Regulation, what is meant by:

1. Regency is the Regency of Ogan Komering Ulu Selatan.  
2. Regency Government is the Government of Ogan Komering Ulu Selatan Regency.  
3. Regent is the Regent of Ogan Komering Ulu Selatan.  
4. Regional Apparatus hereinafter abbreviated as PD is the Regional Apparatus within the Government of Ogan Komering Ulu Selatan Regency.  

4 -

5. Office of Population Control Family Planning Women's Empowerment and Child Protection hereinafter abbreviated as DPPKBPP-PA is the Regional Apparatus that administers the affairs of Women's Empowerment and Child Protection in Ogan Komering Ulu Selatan.  
6. Protection of Women is all efforts to guarantee and protect the rights of Women so that they can live healthily and participate optimally in accordance with human dignity and obtain protection from neglect, violence and discrimination, whether in the form of preventive, repressive or post-remedial protection (assistance and treatment).  
7. Child Protection is all activities to guarantee and protect Children and their rights so that they can live, grow, develop, and participate optimally in accordance with human dignity, and receive protection from violence and discrimination.  
8. Child is a person who has not reached the age of 18 (eighteen) years, including a Child who is still in the womb.  
9. Child with Disability is a Child who has physical, mental, intellectual, or sensory limitations for a long period of time who in interacting with the environment and community attitudes may encounter obstacles that make it difficult to participate fully and effectively based on equal rights.  
10. Child with Excellence is a Child who has extraordinary intelligence or has special potential and/or talent not limited to intellectual ability, but also in other fields.  
11. Prevention is a direct effort carried out by the Regency Government, community and parents and/or family to prevent violations of Women's rights and Children's Rights.  
12. Children's Forum is a forum for Children's participation formed with the aim of facilitating Children's involvement in decision-making processes and enjoying changes relating to their lives either directly or indirectly, which is carried out with the consent and will of all Children based on awareness and understanding, in accordance with age and level of maturity of thinking.  
13. Child-Friendly Regency hereinafter abbreviated as KLA is a Regency that has a Child rights-based development system through - 5 - the integration of commitment and resources of government, community and business world that are planned comprehensively and sustainably in policies, programs and activities to ensure the fulfillment of Children's rights.  
14. Neglected Child is a Child whose needs are not fulfilled properly, whether physical, mental, spiritual, or social.  
15. Women victim of violence is a Woman who experiences actions that result in misery or suffering physically, sexually or psychologically, including threats of certain actions, coercion or deprivation of freedom arbitrarily, whether occurring in the public sphere or in private life.  
16. Child victim of violence is a Child who experiences actions that result in misery or suffering physically, mentally, sexually, psychologically or neglect that threatens life, body and degrades human dignity.  
17. Human trafficking is the act of recruitment, transportation, harboring, sending, transfer, or receipt of a person by threat of violence, use of violence, kidnapping, confinement, forgery, fraud, abuse of power or vulnerable position, debt bondage or giving payment or benefits, so as to obtain consent from the person holding control over that other person, whether carried out within the Country or between Countries, for the purpose of exploitation or resulting in the person being exploited.  
18. Complaint handling is a series of actions carried out by integrated service providers to follow up on reports of acts of violence against Women and Children, submitted by victims, families or the community.  
19. Social rehabilitation is a service aimed at restoring and developing the ability of a person experiencing social dysfunction so that they can carry out their social functions properly.  
20. Law enforcement is the action of apparatus authorized by the state to implement laws and regulations.  
21. Legal aid is legal services provided by legal assistants and advocates to carry out the process of assisting witnesses and/or victims of violence against Women and Children that is gender sensitive.  
22. Repatriation is an effort to return Women and Child victims of violence from abroad to the debarkation point/entry point, or from the receiving area to the area of origin.  
23. Social reintegration is an effort to reunite victims with family, substitute family, or community that can provide protection and fulfillment of needs for victims.  
24. Safe house is a temporary shelter that aims to provide protection and a sense of security to victims of violence according to established standards.  
25. Shelter house is a forum for guidance and welfare services for street Children that carries out social assistance/guidance activities, maintenance, care, education, and health, skills guidance in order to ensure that Children do not carry out activities on the streets so that they can grow and develop properly.  
26. Spiritual guidance is an effort to restore the psyche through strengthening the religion adhered to by the victim.  
27. Gender responsive is consistent and systematic attention to the differences between Women and men in society accompanied by efforts to eliminate structural and cultural barriers in achieving gender equality.  
28. Gender Sensitivity or Gender Awareness is the ability to identify gaps in power relations between Women and men in the family and in the community, the impact of gender-based division of labor on Women and men, and the experiences, problems, needs, interests and aspirations of Women and men are different.  
29. Public service provider is every state organizing institution, corporation, independent institution formed based on Law for public service activities, and other legal entities formed solely for public service activities.  
30. Community Organization is an organization established and formed by the community voluntarily based on common aspirations, will, needs, interests, activities and goals to participate in development for the achievement of the goals of the Unitary State of the Republic of Indonesia based on Pancasila.  
31. Parents are biological father and/or mother, or step father and/or mother, or adoptive father and/or mother.  
32. Family is the smallest unit in society consisting of husband and wife or husband, wife and their Child, or father and his Child, or mother and - 7 - her Child, or blood relatives in a straight line upward or downward up to the third degree.  
33. Integrated Service Center for Women's Empowerment and Child, hereinafter abbreviated as P2TP2A is an integrated service center in efforts to empower Women in various fields of development, as well as protection of Women and Children from various types of discrimination and acts of violence, including human trafficking, which is formed by the government or community-based.  
34. Human Immunodeficiency Virus hereinafter abbreviated as HIV is a virus that attacks the immune system and if not treated can reduce the human body's resistance until the condition of Acquired Immuno Deficiency Syndrome (AIDS) occurs.

## Bagian Kedua

Principles, Objectives and Scope

#### Pasal 2

1. The implementation of Protection of Women is based on the principles of:a. the best interests of Women;  
b. gender justice and gender equality;  
c. local wisdom; andd. legal certainty.  
2. The implementation of Protection of Children is based on the principles of:  a. non-discrimination;  
b. the best interests of the Child;  
c. the right to life, survival, and development;  
d. respect for the views of the Child; and  e. legal certainty.

#### Pasal 3

The regulation of Protection of Women and Children aims to:  a. serve as a guideline for the Regency Government in planning, policies and strategies for the Protection of Women and Children;  
b. protect Women and Children from actions, decisions and policies that violate the rights of Women and Children;  
c. prevent violations of the Rights of Women and Children;  
d. increase empowerment of Women and Children;  
e. enhance the role and value of local wisdom in the Protection of Women and Children; andf. enhance the role of Regional Apparatus and non - 8 - Government entities in the Regency in efforts to Protect Women and Children.

#### Pasal 4

The scope of regulation of Protection of Women and Children includes:
a. prevention;  
b. implementation of Protection of the Rights of Women and Children;  
c. handling;  
d. empowerment; ande. guidance and supervision.

# BAB II

## RIGHTS OF WOMEN AND RIGHTS OF CHILDREN

## Bagian Kesatu

Rights of Women

#### Pasal 5

Every Woman has the right to:
a. obtain protection from acts of discrimination;  
b. obtain protection from economic exploitation and sexual exploitation;  
c. obtain protection from physical, psychological, sexual violence, and neglect;  
d. obtain decent work in accordance with her capabilities;  
e. obtain work leave while receiving wages/salary in accordance with laws and regulations;  
f. obtain education and teaching in all types, levels and pathways of education;  
g. obtain special protection in the performance of work or profession against matters that may threaten safety and/or health in connection with the reproductive function of Women;  
h. the right to choose, be elected, be appointed in work, positions, and professions in accordance with the requirements and laws and regulations; andi. ownership and management of joint property in marriage in accordance with the provisions of Laws and Regulations.

## Bagian Kedua

Rights of Children

#### Article 6

1. Every Child has the right to:a. every child has the right to protection by parents, family, community, and the state;  
b. the rights of the child are human rights and for their benefit the rights of the child are recognized and protected by law even from the womb;  
c. every child from the womb has the right to live, survive, and improve their standard of living;  
d. every child from birth has the right to a name and citizenship status;  
e. every child with physical and/or mental disabilities has the right to obtain care, education, training, and special assistance at state expense, to ensure their life in accordance with human dignity, enhance self-confidence, and the ability to participate in community, national, and state life;  
f. every child has the right to worship according to their religion, think, express themselves in accordance with their intellectual level and age under the guidance of parents and/or guardians;  
g. every child has the right to know who their parents are, to be raised and cared for by their own parents;  
h. in the event that the child's parents are unable to raise and care for their child properly, then the child may be cared for or adopted as a child by another person in accordance with the provisions of laws and regulations;  
i. every child has the right to be raised, cared for, nurtured, educated, directed, and guided in their life by their parents or guardian until adulthood in accordance with the provisions of laws and regulations;  
j. every child has the right to obtain adoptive parents or a guardian based on a court decision if both parents have died or due to a valid reason cannot carry out their obligations as parents;  
k. adoptive parents or guardians as referred to in letter j must carry out the obligations as actual parents;  

10 -
l. every child has the right to obtain legal protection from all forms of physical or mental violence, neglect, maltreatment, and sexual abuse while in the care of parents or guardians, or other parties responsible for the care of the child;  
m. in the event that parents, guardians, or caregivers of a child commit any form of physical or mental abuse, neglect, maltreatment, and sexual abuse including rape, and or murder of a child who should be protected, then aggravated punishment must be imposed;  
n. every child has the right not to be separated from their parents against the child's own will, except if there are valid legal reasons and rules showing that the separation is in the best interest of the child;  
o. in the circumstances as referred to in letter n, the child's right to continue to meet directly and maintain personal contact regularly with their parents is still guaranteed by law;  
p. every child has the right to obtain education and teaching in the context of developing their personality in accordance with their interests, talents, and level of intelligence;  
q. every child has the right to seek, receive, and provide information in accordance with their intellectual level and age for their self-development as long as it is in accordance with the values of decency and propriety;  
r. every child has the right to rest, associate with peers, play, recreate, and create in accordance with their interests, talents, and level of intelligence for their self-development;  
s. every child has the right to obtain health services and social security appropriately, in accordance with their physical and spiritual mental needs;  
t. every child has the right not to be involved in war events, armed conflicts, social unrest, and other events containing elements of violence;  
u. every child has the right to obtain protection from economic exploitation activities and any work that endangers them, so that it can disrupt their education, physical health, morals, social life, and spiritual mental well-being;  

11 -
v. every child has the right to obtain protection from sexual exploitation and abuse activities, kidnapping, child trafficking, as well as from various forms of narcotics, psychotropic, and other addictive substance abuse;  
w. every child has the right not to be subjected to persecution, torture, or imposition of inhuman punishment;  
x. every child has the right not to be deprived of their freedom unlawfully;  
y. arrest, detention, or imprisonment of a child may only be carried out in accordance with applicable law and can only be implemented as a last resort;  
z. every child who is deprived of their freedom has the right to be treated humanely and with attention to the needs of personal development in accordance with their age and must be separated from adults, except in their best interest;  

aa. every child who is deprived of their freedom has the right to obtain legal assistance or other assistance effectively at every stage of applicable legal remedies;  

bb. every child who is deprived of their freedom has the right to defend themselves and obtain justice before an objective and impartial Juvenile Court in a closed hearing to the public.  

2. Every child who is a victim or perpetrator of sexual violence or who is in conflict with the law has the right to confidentiality.  
3. Every Child with Disabilities in addition to obtaining the rights as referred to in paragraph (1) is also entitled to obtain:a. special education/special needs education;  
b. rehabilitation;  
c. social assistance; andd. maintenance of social welfare standards.  
4. Every Child who has excellence has the right to receive special education.

## OBLIGATIONS AND RESPONSIBILITIES

## Part One

Regency Government - 12 -

#### Article 7

1. The Regency Government in efforts to protect Women is obligated and responsible to:  a. guarantee Women's Rights without discrimination in accordance with the provisions of Laws and Regulations;  
b. establish and implement policies, programs and activities for the protection of Women;  
c. cooperate with the community and/or Community Organizations in the implementation of Women's Protection in accordance with the provisions of Laws and Regulations;  
d. carry out rehabilitation and empowerment of Women who are victims of violence;  
e. provide special protection to Women victims of violence and human trafficking; andf. take preventive action in order to prevent violations of Women's rights.  
2. The responsibility of the Regency Government in efforts to protect Women's Rights as referred to in paragraph (1) is carried out by DPPKBP-PPPA.

1. The Regency Government in efforts to protect Children is obliged and responsible to:  a. respect the fulfillment of Children's rights without distinguishing ethnicity, religion, race, class, gender, ethnicity, culture and language, legal status, birth order, and physical and/or mental condition;  
b. provide facilities, infrastructure, and availability of human resources in the implementation of Child Protection;  
c. guarantee the protection, care, and welfare of Children by taking into account the rights and obligations of Parents, Guardians, or other persons who are legally responsible for the Child;  
d. guarantee Children to convey their right to express opinions in accordance with the age and intelligence level of the Child;  
e. establish and implement policies and programs/activities for the protection of Children's rights;  
f. cooperate with the community and/or Community Organizations in the implementation of Child Protection in accordance with the provisions of Laws and Regulations;  
g. conduct rehabilitation of Children who are victims of acts of violence;

2. in conflict with the law;  
3. from minority and isolated groups;  
4. who are exploited economically and/or sexually;  
5. who are victims of narcotics, alcohol, psychotropic, and other addictive substance abuse;  6. who are victims of pornography;  
7. with HIV/AIDS;  
8. victims of kidnapping, sale, and/or trafficking;  
9. victims of physical and/or psychological violence;  
10. victims of sexual crimes;  
11. victims of terrorism networks;  
12. persons with disabilities;  
13. victims of mistreatment and neglect;  14. with deviant social behavior; and

## Part Two

Community

#### Article 10

1. The community is obliged and responsible in implementing the protection of Women and Children including:a. participation in prevention and providing support for the development of prevention programs for violations of the rights of Women and Children;  
b. providing information and/or reporting violations of the rights of Women and Children to the competent authorities; andc. supervising the law enforcement process for cases of violations of the rights of Women and Children.  (2) The responsibility of the community as referred to in paragraph (1) is in accordance with the provisions of laws and regulations and the values of local wisdom of the local community.

Parents and/or Family

### Paragraph 1

Responsibility for the Protection of Women's Rights

#### Article 11

The responsibility of the family in efforts to protect women includes:
a. protecting women from conflict and domestic violence that degrades the dignity of women;  
b. providing equal opportunities for women to develop themselves in the fields of:
1. education;  
2. social;  
3. economy; and
4. politics and culture.  c. preventing violations of women's rights in the household.

### Paragraph 2

Responsibility for the Protection of Children's Rights

#### Article 12

1. Parents in efforts to protect Children are responsible for:a. caring for, maintaining, educating, and protecting Children;  

15 -
b. developing Children according to their abilities, talents, and interests;  
c. providing formal and non-formal education;

# CHAPTER IV

## PREVENTION

## Part One

Regency Government

#### Article 13

1. Efforts made by the Regency Government in order to prevent violations of the rights of Women include:a. conducting socialization regarding the prevention of violence against Women by optimizing the role of customary, cultural, religious institutions and community organizations;  
b. providing guidelines on handling acts of violence against Women;  
c. involving community participation in recovery and reintegration;  
d. developing an integrated database and information system on acts of violence against Women;  
e. developing family empowerment programs;  
f. strengthening the function of community organizations engaged in Women's empowerment; and  g. providing public facilities that are comfortable and safe for Women.  
2. Efforts made by the Regency Government in order to prevent violations of Children's rights include:a. preventing acts of violence against Children by optimizing the role of customary, cultural, religious institutions and community organizations;  
b. developing child-friendly schools;  
c. encouraging increased child participation through the establishment of Children's Forums;  
d. providing guidelines on handling acts of violence against Children;  

16 -
e. involving community participation in recovery and reintegration services for child victims of violence;  
f. developing an integrated database and information system on acts of violence against Children;  
g. strengthening the function of community organizations engaged in Child protection; andh. encouraging the establishment of a Child-Friendly Regency.

## Part Two

Community

#### Article 14

1. Efforts made by the community in order to prevent violations of the rights of Women include:a. monitoring various conditions that occur in their environment that may cause violations of the rights of Women;  
b. strengthening the role of community, religious organizations and the family environment; andc. preventing unregistered marriage/siri marriage.  
2. Efforts made in order to prevent violations of the rights of Children include:  a. monitoring various conditions that occur in their environment that cause violations of the rights of Children;  
b. strengthening the role of community, religious organizations and the family environment; andc. preventing marriage at the age of Children.

## Part Three

Parents and/or Family

#### Article 15

1. Efforts made by the family in order to prevent violations of the rights of Women include:a. protecting women from various violations of rights in the household; andb. encouraging the formation of a harmonious, educative and democratic family;  
2. Efforts made by parents in order to prevent violations of the rights of Children include:a. providing good examples to children in accordance with religious teachings and customs;  
b. supervising children in their activities inside - 17 - or outside the home;  
c. providing opportunities for children to engage in dialogue and express opinions democratically; and  d. protecting children from various acts of violations of Children's rights in the household.

# CHAPTER V

## IMPLEMENTATION OF PROTECTION

## FOR WOMEN AND CHILDREN

## Part One

Implementation of Protection for Women

### Paragraph 1

General

#### Article 16

The Regency Government implements Protection for Women by involving:
a. family;  
b. community leaders;  
c. religious leaders;  
d. teachers and/or academics;  
e. community; andf. community organizations in the field of protection for Women.

### Paragraph 2

Marriage

#### Article 17

During the marriage bond or after divorce, a Woman has the same rights as the man who is her husband/former husband over all matters related to the consequences of marriage and/or divorce.

### Paragraph 3

Health

#### Article 18

1. The Regency Government provides adequate health and maternity facilities for pregnant and nursing mothers.  
2. Public service providers must provide special facilities for nursing mothers.  

18 -

### Paragraph 4

Employment

#### Article 19

1. Employers who employ Women must maintain decency, safety and security while in the workplace.  
2. Employers must place female workers in places that are safe for Women's reproductive health.  
3. Employers must fulfill the rights of female workers to receive wages in accordance with the provisions of laws and regulations.  
4. Employers provide special facilities for nursing mothers in their companies.

#### Article 20

1. Female workers/laborers aged at most 18 (eighteen) years are prohibited from being employed between 23.00 WIB until 07.00 WIB.  
2. Employers are prohibited from employing pregnant female workers/laborers who according to a doctor's statement are dangerous to the health and safety of their pregnancy or themselves during working hours from 23.00 WIB until 07.00 WIB.  
3. Employers who employ female workers/laborers between 23.00 WIB until 07.00 WIB, must:a. provide nutritious food and drinks; and  b. maintain decency and security while in the workplace.  
4. Employers must provide shuttle transportation for female workers/laborers who depart and return from work between 23.00 WIB until 05.00 WIB.

### Paragraph 5

Education

#### Article 21

Women are guaranteed the right to obtain education at every level of formal and non-formal education.  

19 -

### Paragraph 6

Social

#### Article 22

1. The Regency Government implements empowerment of Women who experience socio-economic vulnerability through productive economic business activities.  
2. Community Organizations may conduct empowerment for Women who experience socio-economic vulnerability as referred to in paragraph (1) in accordance with the provisions of Laws and Regulations.

## Part Two

Implementation of Protection of Children's Rights

### Paragraph 1

Health

#### Article 23

1. The Regency Government's efforts to provide protection for Children in the health sector include:  a. free health services for Children from poor families;  
b. implementation of optimal health for Children to obtain good health status from the womb;  
c. ensuring that babies receive exclusive breastfeeding from their mothers properly and naturally;  
d. ensuring Children receive adequate nutrition;  
e. ensuring newborn Children undergo screening; andf. ensuring the provision of monitoring of growth and development of toddlers and pre-school age children.  
2. The Regency Government, family, and parents protect children from attempts to transplant their body organs to other parties.

### Paragraph 2

Education

#### Article 24

Children in formal and non-formal educational institution environments must be protected from acts of violence.  

20 -

#### Article 25

Every Child aged 7 (seven) to 18 (eighteen) years who has not completed education, exceeding 3 (three) years beyond the formal education age limit may pursue education through non-formal education units:
a. Package A equivalency program equivalent to Elementary School and Madrasah Ibtidaiyah;  
b. Package B equivalency program equivalent to Junior High School and Madrasah Tsanawiyah; andc. Package C equivalency program equivalent to Senior High School, Madrasah Aliyah and Vocational High School.

#### Article 26

1. Children with special needs and/or who have potential intelligence and/or special talents are given equal opportunities and facilities to obtain regular education or inclusive education.  
2. Inclusive education as referred to in paragraph (1) is implemented by the Regency Government and the community.

#### Article 27

Every formal education provider is prohibited from expelling Children without guarantees for the continuity of the Child's education.

#### Article 28

1. The Regency Government provides scholarships and/or educational assistance for:a. children from poor families;  
b. neglected children; andc. children residing in remote areas.  (2) The implementation of scholarship programs and/or educational assistance as referred to in paragraph (1) is supported by community participation and community organizations.  
3. Further provisions regarding the provision of scholarships and or educational assistance as referred to in paragraph (1) are regulated by Regent Regulation.

#### Article 29

Children in conflict with the law, Children victims of violence and sexual abuse and Children victims of HIV/AIDS transmission are protected in their rights to obtain education.  

21 -

#### Article 30

The Regency Government and the community implement Early Childhood Education (PAUD).

### Paragraph 3

Employment

#### Article 31

Employers are prohibited from employing Children.

#### Article 32

1. The provisions as referred to in Article 31 may be exempted for Children aged between 13 (thirteen) years to 15 (fifteen) years, to perform light work as long as it does not interfere with physical, mental, and social development and health.  
2. Employers who employ Children in light work as referred to in paragraph (1) must meet requirements including:a. written permission from parents or guardians;  
b. work agreement between the employer and parents or guardians;  
c. maximum working time of 3 (three) hours;  
d. conducted during the day and does not interfere with school time;  
e. occupational safety and health;  
f. clear employment relationship; andg. receive wages in accordance with the provisions of laws and regulations.  
3. The provisions as referred to in paragraph (2) letter a, letter b, letter f, and letter g are exempted for Children who work in family businesses.

#### Article 33

In the event that a company employs Children as referred to in Article 32, then the Children's workplace must be separated from the workplace of adult workers.

#### Article 34

1. Children may perform work in a workplace that is part of an educational curriculum or training approved by the authorized official.  
2. Children as referred to in paragraph (1) are at least 14 (fourteen) years old.  

22 -

#### Article 35

1. Everyone is prohibited from employing and involving Children in the worst forms of work.  
2. The worst forms of work as referred to in paragraph (1) include:a. all work in the form of slavery or the like;  
b. all work that exploits, provides, or offers Children for:  1. prostitution;  
2. pornography production;  
3. pornographic acts;  
4. gambling;  
5. production and trade of alcoholic beverages;  6. narcotics;  
7. psychotropics; and
8. other addictive substances; and/orc. all work that endangers the health, safety, or morals of Children.

### Paragraph 4

Social

#### Article 36

1. The Regency Government is obligated to provide care for neglected Children.
2. Care as referred to in paragraph (1) may be carried out by Community Organizations in the social field.
3. In the event that a Child is neglected due to a reason that the parents neglect their obligations, then the Family or Regency Government or Community Organization as referred to in paragraph (2) may submit an application to the court to determine the Child as a neglected Child.

#### Article 37

1. The Regency Government organizes skills training for street Children and neglected Children.

#### Article 38

1. The Regency Government provides shelters to foster street Children and social institutions for neglected Children.
2. Shelters as referred to in paragraph (1) may be organized in cooperation with Community Organizations.

#### Article 39

The implementation of protection for Women and Children is organized by Regional Apparatus whose duties and functions are related to the protection of Women and Children.

# CHAPTER VI

## HANDLING

## Part One

Handling of Women and Children Victims of Violence

#### Article 40

The implementation of handling Women and Children victims of violence includes:
a. handling complaints of victims of violence;
b. health services;
c. social rehabilitation;
d. law enforcement and legal assistance; and
e. repatriation and social reintegration.

#### Article 41

1. The implementation of handling Women and Children victims of violence as referred to in Article 40 is carried out through integrated services.
2. Integrated services for Women and Children victims of violence as referred to in paragraph (1) use the principles of:
a. gender responsive;
b. non-discrimination;
c. equal and respectful relationships;
d. maintaining confidentiality;
e. providing a sense of security and comfort;
f. respecting individual differences;
g. non-judgmental;
h. respecting the choices and decisions of victims;
i. sensitive to the background and condition of victims and the use of appropriate language understood - 24 - by victims;
j. fast and simple;
k. empathy;
l. fulfillment of the rights of Women and Children; and
m. free of charge.

#### Article 42

Complaint handling services for victims of violence against Women and Children are carried out by:
a. hospitals;
b. community health centers;
c. P2TP2A;
d. Women and Children Service Units (UPPA);
e. Trauma Center Shelter Houses (RPTC);
f. Children's Social Shelter Houses (RPSA);
g. Women Crisis Centre (WCC)
h. Advisory Board for Marriage Guidance and Preservation (BP4);
i. Police;
j. Prosecutor's Office;
k. Courts;
l. Legal Aid Institutions (LBH);
m. Child Protection Institutions (LPA);
n. Social Welfare Protection Institutions; and
o. Shelters.

#### Article 43

1. Complaints of acts of violence against Women and Children as referred to in Article 42 may be made by reporters consisting of:
a. Victims;
b. Family;
c. Community; and/or
d. community organizations in the field of protection of Women and Children.
2. Reporters as referred to in paragraph (1) may make complaints:
a. directly;
b. by telephone; and
c. by letter.
3. In addition to handling complaints submitted by reporters, officers at service institutions as referred to in Article 42 are authorized to handle victims of violence against Women and Children obtained through other information.
4. Further provisions regarding the procedures for complaint handling services as referred to in paragraph (1) and paragraph (2) are regulated by Regional Regulation.

## Part Two

Health Services for Women and Children Victims of Violence

#### Article 44

1. Health services for Women and Children victims of violence include aspects of:
a. Promotive;
b. Preventive;
c. Curative;
d. rehabilitative.
2. Health services for Women and Children victims of violence are carried out:
a. Comprehensively;
b. accessible 24 (twenty-four) hours;
c. with quality; and
d. in accordance with health service standards.
(3) Special health services for Children victims of violence must prioritize the best interests of the Child.
4. Women and Children victims of violence are entitled to health services, including:
a. medical services;
b. medicolegal services; and
c. psychosocial counseling.
5. Further provisions regarding the procedures for implementing health services as referred to in paragraph (4) are regulated by Regional Regulation.

## Part Three

Social Rehabilitation Services for Women and Children Victims of Violence

#### Article 45

1. Social rehabilitation services for Women and Children victims of violence are aimed at restoring and developing the abilities of victims who experience social dysfunction to be able to carry out their social functions in society properly.
2. Social rehabilitation services as referred to in paragraph (1) include:
a. counseling services;
b. provision of safe houses;
c. spiritual guidance.
3. The implementation of services as referred to in paragraph (2) is organized by:
a. Regional Apparatus that has duties and functions in the social field;

26 -
b. DPPKBPP-PA;
c. P2TP2A;
d. Women Crisis Centre (WCC);
e. community organizations in the social field; and/or
f. religious institutions.

#### Article 46

1. Counseling services as referred to in Article 45 paragraph (2) letter a aim to:
a. help victims recognize their problems and find effective ways to overcome them themselves;
b. empower victims to determine their future;
c. provide moral support for victims in facing the process they are undergoing; and
d. make victims feel accepted in the community environment.
2. Counseling services are carried out by gender-responsive social rehabilitation officers, consisting of:
a. counselors;
b. psychologists; and
c. psychiatrists.
3. Counseling services are carried out in a place that guarantees a sense of security, comfort and confidentiality of information from victims.
4. Counseling service stages include:
a. initial counseling;
b. follow-up counseling;
c. case conference; and
d. home visit.

#### Article 47

1. The Regency Government may provide safe houses for Women and Children victims of violence.
(2) Safe houses as referred to in paragraph (1) aim to protect Women and Children victims of violence from intimidation by perpetrators or other parties.
3. The location of safe houses as referred to in paragraph (1) must be kept confidential.
4. Criteria for Women and Children victims of violence who require safe houses include:
a. their lives are threatened;
b. receiving rejection from family or community;
c. requiring intensive services but their residence is relatively far away; and/or - 27 -
d. neglected if not placed in a safe house.
5. The implementation of safe house services is carried out by social rehabilitation officers who have gender sensitivity or gender awareness, consisting of:
a. social workers/volunteers;
b. security officers; and
c. community leaders.
6. Further provisions regarding the implementation of safe house services are regulated by Regional Regulation.

#### Article 48

1. Every Woman and Child victim of violence is provided with spiritual guidance aimed at psychological recovery.
2. Spiritual guidance as referred to in paragraph (1) is implemented based on the principles of: a. not imposing any religion or belief; and b. leaving the final decision in the hands of the victim (self determination).
3. Spiritual guidance as referred to in paragraph (2) is conducted by spiritual guidance officers.
4. Spiritual guidance officers as referred to in paragraph (3) consist of: a. consultants of the Marriage Advisory, Development and Preservation Agency (BP4);
b. Officers of the Office of Religious Affairs (KUA);
c. Ustad or Ulama;
d. Pastor;
e. Minister; and f. other religious leaders who have gender sensitivity or gender awareness.

## Part Four

Law Enforcement and Legal Aid Services

#### Article 49

1. Law enforcement and legal aid services constitute actions of law enforcement officials provided to Women and Child victims of violence and/or witnesses.
2. Law enforcement and legal aid services as referred to in paragraph (1) are conducted by prioritizing the best interests of the victim.

28 -

#### Article 50

1. Law enforcement and legal aid services as referred to in Article 49 are aimed at providing assistance and protection to Women and Child victims of violence and witnesses with a gender-responsive approach so that victims obtain justice and legal certainty.
2. Types of legal aid services as referred to in paragraph (1) include: a. legal aid in the reporting process and preparation of BAP;
b. legal aid in the prosecution process at the prosecutor's office;
c. legal aid in the examination process at court;
d. legal aid in the court hearing decision process until there is a final legal decision; and e. legal aid in the mediation process.
3. The Regency Government provides legal aid to Child and Woman victims of violence free of charge.
4. In providing legal aid as referred to in paragraph (3) the Regency Government may cooperate with: a. Legal Aid Institute (LBH);
b. Women Crisis Center (WCC);
c. Advocates; and / or d. other legal assistants who are gender sensitive.

## Part Five

Repatriation and Social Reintegration Services

#### Article 51

1. Repatriation of victims of violence is aimed at returning Women and Child victims of violence to their area of origin.
2. Repatriation of victims of violence of Women and Children as referred to in paragraph (1) is accompanied by social reintegration efforts.
3. The Regency Government implements repatriation and social reintegration of Women and Child victims of violence in accordance with the provisions of Laws and Regulations.

# CHAPTER VII

## WOMEN'S EMPOWERMENT

#### Article 52

1. The Regency Government guarantees the implementation of empowerment for Women.
2. The implementation of Women's empowerment as referred to in paragraph (1) is supported by community participation and Community Organizations.

#### Article 53

1. The Regency Government provides skills training to increase Women's capacity. (2) In providing training as referred to in paragraph (1) the Regency Government may cooperate with training institutions organized by community organizations.
(3) The Regency Government expands access and opportunities for Women in obtaining skills and employment.

#### Article 54

1. The Regency Government facilitates: a. provision of business capital assistance for Women with weak economies;
b. marketing of products resulting from Women's businesses;
c. organization of training for community capacity development through economic empowerment of pre-prosperous families;
d. expansion of access, information and provision of knowledge to Women regarding marketing efforts for products produced; and e. provision of comprehensive entrepreneurship training to Women.
2. The Regency Government in facilitating activities as referred to in paragraph (1) cooperates with the community and/or community organizations.

# CHAPTER VIII

## CHILD PARTICIPATION

#### Article 55

1. Every Child has the right to participate in expressing opinions, ideas, and thoughts related to life based on awareness and understanding, in accordance with age and level of thinking maturity.

30 - (2) Child participation as referred to in paragraph (1) may be conducted through Child Forums starting from the village level, sub-district within the regency.

3. Child Forums as referred to in paragraph (2) represent Children, both geographic domicile representation, socio-cultural groups and Children's educational backgrounds.
4. The establishment of Child Forums as referred to in paragraph (2) is facilitated by the Regency Government.
(5) The establishment of Child Forums as referred to in paragraph (4) is stipulated by Regent's Decree.

#### Article 56

1. In every formulation of policies related to Children, the Regency Government considers and accommodates Children's Opinions submitted through Child Forums.
2. The Regency Government includes representatives of Child Forums in the Regency Development Planning Deliberation.

# CHAPTER IX

## CHILD-FRIENDLY REGENCY

#### Article 57

b. facilitation;

d. supervision;
e. consultation; and f. education and training.

# CHAPTER X

## GUIDANCE AND SUPERVISION

#### Article 58

1. The Regent conducts guidance and supervision of the implementation of Women and Child Protection.
(2) Guidance as referred to in paragraph (1) may be conducted in the form of: a. training for government officials and law enforcement officers on prevention and handling of violence;

31 -
b. training for religious leaders, community leaders, customary leaders, educators, journalists and media managers in order to prevent violence against Women and Children;
c. training for health workers who provide health services to Women and Child victims of violence at community health centers, integrated service units, and general hospitals;

3. The Regent in conducting guidance and supervision as referred to in paragraph (1) may delegate to the Head of DPPKBPP-PA.

#### Article 59

#### Article 60

1. The Regent conducts supervision and evaluation of the implementation of Women and Child Protection periodically 1 (one) time every 3 (three) months.
2. The results of the evaluation as referred to in paragraph (1) constitute evaluation material for Women and Child protection program policies in the Regency.
