b. that women, as a national asset who play a role in the process of continuing and creating quality generations, need to be guaranteed the fulfillment of their rights and protection from acts of violence and discrimination in order to build society, the nation, and the state;  
c. that based on Article 12 paragraph (2) letter b of Law Number 23 of 2014 concerning Regional Government, it is essentially stated that women’s empowerment is a mandatory governmental affair that must be administered by regional governments;

CHAPTER I  
GENERAL PROVISIONS  
Article 1  
In this Regional Regulation, what is meant by:  
1. Region means Semarang Regency.  
2. Regional Government means the administration of governmental affairs by the Regional Government and the Regional House of Representatives according to the principles of autonomy and co-administration tasks with the principle of the broadest possible autonomy within the system and principles of the Unitary State of the Republic of Indonesia as referred to in the 1945 Constitution of the Republic of Indonesia.  
3. Regional Government means the Regent and regional apparatus as elements administering Regional Government.  
4. The Regional House of Representatives of Semarang Regency, hereinafter abbreviated as DPRD, is the regional people’s representative institution as an element administering regional government.  
5. Regent means the Regent of Semarang.  
6. Regional Apparatus Work Unit in charge, hereinafter referred to as the relevant SKPD, is a Regional Apparatus Work Unit that has main duties and functions in accordance with the related field.  
7. Human Rights are a set of rights inherent in the nature and existence of human beings as creatures of God Almighty and are His gift that must be respected, upheld, and protected by the state, law, Government, and every person for the honor and protection of human dignity and worth.  
8. Women’s Empowerment is every effort to improve physical, mental, spiritual, social abilities, knowledge, and skills so that women are ready to be utilized according to their respective abilities.  
9. Women’s Protection is all efforts aimed at protecting women and providing a sense of security in fulfilling their rights by providing consistent and systematic attention aimed at achieving gender equality.  
10. Discrimination Against Women is any distinction, exclusion, or restriction made on the basis of sex that has the effect or purpose of reducing or eliminating the recognition, enjoyment, or exercise of human rights and fundamental freedoms in the political, economic, socio-cultural, civil, or any other field by women, irrespective of their marital status, on the basis of equality between men and women.  
11. Gender is a concept that refers to differences in the roles and responsibilities of men and women that occur as a result of, and can change due to, the social and cultural conditions of society.  
12. Gender Equality is the same condition for men and women to obtain opportunities and their rights as human beings so as to be able to play roles and participate in political, economic, socio-cultural, defense and security activities, and equality in enjoying the results of development.  
13. Community means individuals, families, groups and social organizations and/or community organizations.  
14. Family is the smallest unit in society consisting of husband and wife, husband-wife and their children, or father and his children, or mother and her children.  
15. Workforce means every person who is able to do work to produce goods and/or services either to meet their own needs or for society.  
16. Worker/Laborer means every person who works by receiving wages or remuneration in other forms.  

CHAPTER II  
SCOPE  
Article 2  
The scope of this Regional Regulation includes:  
a. principles and objectives;  
b. women’s rights;  
c. obligations and responsibilities of the regional government;  
d. women’s empowerment;  
e. women’s protection;  
f. implementation of women’s protection;  
g. strategies for women’s empowerment and protection;  
h. mechanisms for administering women’s empowerment and protection;  
i. financing;  
j. guidance and supervision;  
k. transitional provisions; and  
l. closing provisions.  

CHAPTER III  
PRINCIPLES AND OBJECTIVES  
Article 3  
Women’s Empowerment and Protection are implemented based on the following principles:  
a. respect for human rights;  
b. gender justice and equality;  
c. non-discrimination; and  
d. the best interests of women.  

Article 4  
The objectives of this Regional Regulation are:  
a. to empower women both individually and in groups and the wider community so that they are able to live more independently; and  
b. to prevent and address the risks of violence and women’s vulnerability so that their survival can be fulfilled in accordance with the principles of equality and justice.  

CHAPTER IV  
WOMEN’S RIGHTS  
Article 5  
(1) Every woman has the right to:  
a. live and sustain life and improve her standard of living;  
b. fulfill her basic needs to grow and develop properly, to have a family and continue offspring;  
c. develop her personality, obtain education, and improve her quality of life so as to become a person who is faithful, pious, responsible, of noble character, happy, and prosperous in accordance with human rights;  
d. obtain justice, a sense of security, and freedom to express opinions without discrimination;  
e. be involved in every stage of the development process;  
f. be free from slavery or servitude and threats;  
g. obtain protection of her person, family, honor, dignity, and property rights;  
h. obtain welfare and a decent life;  
i. participate in politics;  
j. perform legal acts; and  
k. freely choose a spouse in marriage.  
(2) Every wife during the marital bond has the same rights and responsibilities as her husband in all matters relating to her marital life, relations with her children, and ownership and management of joint property in accordance with applicable laws and regulations.  
(3) After the dissolution of marriage, a woman has rights and responsibilities with her former husband in all matters relating to the child by paying attention to the best interests of the child.  

CHAPTER V  
OBLIGATIONS AND RESPONSIBILITIES OF THE REGIONAL GOVERNMENT  
Article 6  
(1) The Regional Government is obliged and responsible for women’s empowerment and protection.  
(2) Efforts for women’s empowerment and protection as referred to in paragraph (1) include:  
a. stipulation of policies, programs, and activities;  
b. stipulation of implementation guidelines;  
c. provision of services; and  
d. coordination of policies, programs, and activities.  
(3) Efforts for women’s empowerment and protection as referred to in paragraph (2) are carried out in an integrated manner in accordance with applicable laws and regulations.  

CHAPTER VI  
WOMEN’S EMPOWERMENT  
Article 7  
Women’s empowerment is directed to obtain opportunities and rights as human beings so as to be able to play roles and participate in the fields of:  
a. economy;  
b. socio-culture;  
c. politics; and  
d. law.  

Article 8  
Women’s empowerment in the economic field as referred to in Article 7 letter a is carried out through:  
a. provision of skills and job training;  
b. facilitation of the formation of productive economic business groups;  
c. facilitation of strengthening and development of productive economic business groups;  
d. facilitation and capital assistance; and  
e. facilitation of marketing network development.  

Article 9  
Women’s empowerment in the socio-cultural field as referred to in Article 7 letter b is carried out through:  
a. increasing knowledge, attitudes, and skills to encourage fulfillment of education in stages according to potential to improve social status;  
b. increasing awareness, willingness, and ability to overcome health problems through quality promotive, preventive, curative, and rehabilitative efforts, especially in the field of reproductive health;  
c. increasing awareness and knowledge about independent, healthy, and prosperous family planning including access to consultation services and marriage registration; and  
d. facilitation and efforts to preserve customs and develop science, technology, arts, and culture for women’s advancement.  

Article 10  
(1) Women’s empowerment in the political field as referred to in Article 7 letter c includes:  
a. involving women in decision-making at various levels;  
b. providing opportunities for women to hold public office;  
c. participation in general elections; and  
d. self-development through organizations to associate, assemble, and express opinions.  
(2) Women’s empowerment in the political field as referred to in paragraph (1) is carried out in accordance with applicable laws and regulations.  

Article 11  
(1) Women’s empowerment in the legal field as referred to in Article 7 letter d includes:  
a. increasing awareness and knowledge in the legal field through communication, information, and education services;  
b. facilitating access and legal consultation services.  
(2) Women’s empowerment in the legal field as referred to in paragraph (1) is carried out in accordance with applicable laws and regulations.  

CHAPTER VII  
WOMEN’S PROTECTION  
Article 12  
(1) Women’s protection includes:  
a. social protection;  
b. economic protection;  
c. legal protection.  
(2) Social protection as referred to in paragraph (1) letter a is to ensure women’s survival in the form of social protection to guarantee the fulfillment of decent living needs.  
(3) Economic protection as referred to in paragraph (1) letter b is to prevent and address the risk of vulnerability due to shocks and social vulnerability of an individual, family, group and/or community for women’s survival.  
(4) Legal protection as referred to in paragraph (1) letter c is implemented in providing protection for women who are witnesses and victims of acts of violence and discrimination.  

CHAPTER VIII  
IMPLEMENTATION OF WOMEN’S PROTECTION  
Part One  
General  
Article 13  
The implementation of women’s protection is provided to:  
a. women workers/laborers;  
b. elderly women;  
c. women with disabilities;  
d. homeless women;  
e. women domestic workers;  
f. women heads of household;  
g. women former inmates of correctional institutions;  
h. women disaster victims;  
i. women commercial sex workers; and  
j. women victims of violence.  

Part Two  
Women Workers/Laborers  
Article 14  
(1) The implementation of protection for women workers/laborers as referred to in Article 13 letter a is to provide protection from acts of economic exploitation and cruel, inhuman treatment and to seek proper appreciation for work.  
(2) Women workers/laborers as referred to in paragraph (1) receive protection for:  
a. recognition of rights, wages, and decent working conditions;  
b. health and social security;  
c. access to information and legal consultation services;  
d. opportunities to obtain knowledge and skills to improve status; and  
e. witnesses and victims.  

Part Three  
Elderly Women  
Article 15  
(1) The implementation of protection for elderly women as referred to in Article 13 letter b is in order to extend life expectancy so that they can carry out their social functions and play an active role reasonably in their social life.  
(2) Elderly women as referred to in paragraph (1) receive protection rights for:  
a. basic health services to maintain and improve health status including gerontological/geriatric services;  
b. use of facilities and accessibility of public facilities and infrastructure; and  
c. government administrative services.  

Part Four  
Women with Disabilities  
Article 16  
(1) The implementation of protection for women with disabilities as referred to in Article 13 letter c is in order to provide protection and prevention against forms of discrimination including being free from cruel, inhuman treatment, degrading human dignity, and free from exploitation.  
(2) Women with disabilities as referred to in paragraph (1) receive protection rights for:  
a. guarantees of continuity and personal self-development;  
b. participation in education, health, employment, politics, sports, arts and culture as well as the use of information and communication technology;  
c. accessibility of information, legal aid, mobility, social services; and  
d. early intervention efforts including treatment and rehabilitation to improve their function and capacity.  

Part Five  
Homeless Women  
Article 17  
(1) The implementation of protection for homeless women as referred to in Article 13 letter d is in order to provide protection and prevention against forms of violence caused by the absence of shelter or permanent residence.  
(2) Homeless women as referred to in paragraph (1) receive protection rights for:  
a. decent housing;  
b. health and social security;  
c. population administration registration;  
d. opportunities to obtain skills to improve economic status; and  
e. access to information and legal consultation services.  

Part Six  
Women Domestic Workers  
Article 18  
(1) The implementation of protection for women domestic workers as referred to in Article 13 letter e is in order to provide protection and prevention against forms of discrimination, cruel, inhuman treatment, degrading human dignity, and exploitation in employment relations.  
(2) Women domestic workers as referred to in paragraph (1) receive protection for:  
a. recognition of rights, wages, and decent working conditions;  
b. health and social security;  
c. access to information and legal consultation services; and  
d. opportunities to obtain knowledge and skills to improve economic status.  

Part Seven  
Women Heads of Household  
Article 19  
(1) The implementation of protection for women heads of household as referred to in Article 13 letter f is in order to provide protection and prevention against forms of discrimination, negative stigma, and economic empowerment for women heads of household.  
(2) Women heads of household as referred to in paragraph (1) receive protection rights for:  
a. discriminatory actions and efforts to weaken women’s function as the family’s economic support;  
b. strengthening capabilities and recognition as head of household in their environment; and  
c. accessibility to economic and financial institutions.  

Part Eight  
Women Former Inmates of  
Correctional Institutions  
Article 20  
(1) The implementation of protection for women former inmates of Correctional Institutions as referred to in Article 13 letter g is in order to provide protection from obstacles to adjusting to life in society, so that they can live normally.  
(2) Women former inmates of Correctional Institutions as referred to in paragraph (1) receive protection for:  
a. rehabilitation and social reintegration; and  
b. opportunities to obtain knowledge and skills to improve economic status.  

Part Nine  
Women Disaster Victims  
Article 21  
(1) The implementation of protection for women disaster victims as referred to in Article 13 letter h is in order to provide protection during disasters and post-disaster.  
(2) Women disaster victims as referred to in paragraph (1) receive protection for:  
a. access to decent housing;  
b. health services;  
c. access to information services and legal consultation; and  
d. post-disaster recovery.  

Part Ten  
Women Commercial Sex Workers  
Article 22  
(1) The implementation of protection for women commercial sex workers as referred to in Article 13 letter i is in order to provide health protection and improve life skills as capital to improve social status.  
(2) Protection for women commercial sex workers as referred to in paragraph (1) includes:  
a. providing life skills capabilities;  
b. facilitating reproductive health services; and  
c. preventing trafficking in persons.  

Part Eleven  
Women Victims of Violence  
Article 23  
(1) The implementation of protection for women victims of violence as referred to in Article 13 letter j is in order to provide legal protection and/or health recovery.  
(2) Protection for women victims of violence as referred to in paragraph (1) includes:  
a. legal aid;  
b. assistance after the occurrence of acts of violence;  
c. rehabilitation and social reintegration; and  
d. health services.  

CHAPTER IX  
STRATEGIES FOR WOMEN’S EMPOWERMENT AND  
PROTECTION  
Article 24  
(1) Women’s empowerment and protection are implemented by integrating gender mainstreaming and gender-disaggregated data in an integrated manner based on applicable laws and regulations.  
(2) In women’s empowerment and protection, a Working Group and Integrated Services are formed, stipulated by a Regent’s Decree.  
(3) Women’s empowerment and protection are coordinated by the Agency handling Women’s Empowerment.  

CHAPTER X  
MECHANISM FOR ADMINISTERING WOMEN’S EMPOWERMENT  
AND PROTECTION  
Article 25  
The mechanism for administering women’s empowerment and protection is carried out through the stages of:  
a. formulation of policies for planning programs and activities;  
b. budgeting of programs and activities;  
c. implementation of programs and activities; and  
d. monitoring, evaluation, and reporting.  

Article 26  
(1) The formulation of policies for planning programs and activities as referred to in Article 25 letter a is carried out through:  
a. mapping, collecting, processing, and presenting data;  
b. coordination and synchronization of the preparation of program and activity planning with related SKPD; and  
c. determination of programs and activities in planning documents.  
(2) The formulation of policies for planning programs and activities is further regulated in minimum service standards and standard operating procedures.  
(3) The formulation of policies for planning programs and activities for administering women’s empowerment and protection is coordinated by the SKPD in charge of regional development planning.  
(4) Budgeting of programs and activities as referred to in Article 25 letter b is carried out through the general budget policy set forth in the APBD.  
(5) Implementation of programs and activities as referred to in Article 25 letter c is carried out through:  
a. implementation of programs and activities contained in SKPD budget documents; and  
b. coordination and synchronization of the implementation of programs and activities with related SKPD.  
(6) Implementation of monitoring, evaluation, and reporting as referred to in Article 25 letter d is carried out through:  
a. conducting periodic monitoring and evaluation of the implementation of programs and activities that have been carried out; and  
b. preparing periodic reports.  

CHAPTER XI  
FINANCING  
Article 27  
All costs for implementing policies for planning programs and activities related to Women’s Empowerment and Protection are sourced from:  
a. the State Revenue and Expenditure Budget;  
b. the Regional Revenue and Expenditure Budget; and  
c. other lawful and non-binding sources.  

CHAPTER XII  
GUIDANCE AND SUPERVISION  
Article 28  
(1) The Regent or the SKPD in charge of women’s empowerment conducts guidance and supervision over women’s empowerment and protection.  
(2) The forms of guidance as referred to in paragraph (1) are as follows:  
a. providing outreach to the community and the business sector regarding women’s empowerment and protection policies;  
b. providing books, leaflets, brochures regarding women’s empowerment and protection, and other related issues and disseminating them to the community;  
c. facilitating capacity building of institutions administering women’s empowerment and protection;  
d. facilitating forums for women’s participation and women’s organizations;  
e. facilitating rehabilitation and reintegration services for women disaster victims and women victims of violence;  
f. coordinating the participation of the business sector and other institutions, including international assistance for women’s empowerment and protection; and  
g. giving awards to the community and the business sector, both individually and institutionally, that have undertaken efforts for women’s empowerment and protection.  
(3) The form of supervision as referred to in paragraph (1) may be in the form of monitoring and evaluation activities of women’s empowerment and protection.  

CHAPTER XIII  
TRANSITIONAL PROVISIONS  
Article 29  
At the time this Regional Regulation comes into force, all provisions of regional laws and regulations related to the Administration of Women’s Empowerment and Protection that have existed so far remain in effect insofar as they do not conflict with the provisions in this Regional Regulation.  

CHAPTER XIV  
CLOSING PROVISIONS  
Article 30  
This Regional Regulation comes into force on the date of its promulgation.

I. GENERAL  
The 1945 Constitution of the Republic of Indonesia mandates the authority of regional governments to regulate and manage their own governmental affairs directed at accelerating the realization of community welfare through improving services, empowerment, and community participation. The authority of the Regional Government is to make regional policies to provide services, increase participation, initiatives, and community empowerment aimed at improving the welfare of the people.  

The implementation of regional autonomy must always be oriented toward improving community welfare while always paying attention to the interests and aspirations that grow in society. Therefore, governmental affairs related to basic services such as basic education, health, fulfillment of minimum living needs, and basic environmental infrastructure are mandatory to be fulfilled.  

Human rights are basic rights that by nature are inherent in human beings, universal and enduring, and therefore must be protected, respected, defended, and must not be ignored, reduced, or taken away by anyone. In the context of enforcing Human Rights (HAM) to guarantee justice for every individual, efforts are made to prevent all forms of unequal treatment or treatment deemed detrimental to others, whether between individuals, between groups of individuals, between individuals, and between the government and society, including the process of marginalization of women which causes women to be vulnerable to acts of violence, discrimination, and exploitation in both domestic and public spheres.  

Women have the same and equal rights as men; however, in certain discriminatory conditions, the government is obliged to carry out affirmative action so that women’s rights and women in vulnerable groups need to receive protection so that they do not experience violence and can live decently in accordance with the principles of humanity, equality, and justice.  

Women’s empowerment and women’s protection are implemented in order to realize gender equality and justice. To carry out these affairs, efforts are needed to increase the capacity of women’s resources and strengthen gender mainstreaming institutions, through gender mainstreaming strategies and gender-disaggregated data.  

The Government of Semarang Regency, as a party that has the authority to regulate and protect the residents of Semarang Regency, deems it necessary to continuously carry out and formulate regulations to provide guarantees for women’s empowerment and women’s protection in Semarang Regency.  

This Regional Regulation is expected to be able to encourage efforts to improve women’s physical and mental conditions in fulfilling their rights and living needs as part of human rights in various fields of development, increase the commitment and effectiveness of the regional government in implementing policies, programs and activities, and encourage the institutional capacity of government and society that improves women’s quality of life.

Homeless women are women who do not have a permanent place to live and for various reasons must live under bridge overpasses, public parks, roadsides, or various other public facilities to sleep and carry out daily life.  

Article 18  
Women domestic workers are persons who work for one or several persons in a household to perform household work by receiving wages.  

Article 19  
Women heads of household are women who, for various reasons, are left by their husbands, and ultimately act or contribute and function to carry out their roles and responsibilities as head of household.  

Article 20  
Women former inmates of social institutions are women who have served a sentence in a correctional institution.  

Article 21  
Women disaster victims are women who suffer or die as a result of natural and social disasters.  
Post-disaster recovery includes psychological services for victims, health services, housing facilities, facilitation of public facilities and infrastructure, and restoration of economic access as well as population data services and legal aid.  

Article 22  
Women commercial sex workers are women who work as sex workers by their own will and/or are prostituted. Improving life skills for commercial sex workers is intended to provide additional knowledge through training and skills in accordance with their talents and abilities so that they can live more independently and obtain decent work to improve their standard of living and change their social status so that they do not become commercial sex workers because such work is not legalized.  
Protection of commercial sex workers in this Regional Regulation is not intended to legalize the existence of commercial sex workers, but to provide protection to the intended commercial sex workers in various sectors.

Article 24  
Paragraph (1)  
Gender Mainstreaming, hereinafter abbreviated as PUG, is a strategy built to integrate gender as an integral dimension of planning, budgeting, implementation, monitoring, and evaluation of development policies, programs and activities. PUG is a strategy that can reduce gender gaps to achieve gender equality by using a gender perspective in the development process; women’s empowerment is an affirmative strategy to achieve gender equality aimed at increasing the fulfillment of women’s rights to protection from acts of violence and discrimination.
