REGENT OF PADANG PARIAMAN  
WEST SUMATRA PROVINCE  
REGIONAL REGULATION OF PADANG PARIAMAN REGENCY  
NUMBER 6 OF 2017  
CONCERNING  
WOMEN’S EMPOWERMENT AND CHILD PROTECTION  
BY THE GRACE OF GOD ALMIGHTY  
THE REGENT OF PADANG PARIAMAN,  

Considering: a. that in order to guarantee Women’s Empowerment and Child Protection in accordance with human dignity and worth and to obtain Protection from Violence, discrimination and other violations, legal certainty is necessary;  
b. that to provide legal certainty in the implementation of Women’s Empowerment and Child Protection and to carry out the mandatory affairs of the Regional Government, regulation in the form of a Regional Regulation is necessary;  
c. that based on the considerations as referred to in letter a and letter b, it is necessary to stipulate a Regional Regulation concerning Women’s Empowerment and Child Protection;  

Bearing in mind: 1. Article 18 paragraph (6) of the 1945 Constitution of the Republic of Indonesia;  
2. Law Number 12 of 1956 concerning the Establishment of Autonomous Regency Regions within the Province of Central Sumatra (State Gazette of the Republic of Indonesia Year 1956 Number 25));  
3. Law Number 39 of 1999 concerning Human Rights (State Gazette of the Republic of Indonesia Year 1999 Number 165, Supplement to State Gazette Number 3886);  
4. Law Number 23 of 2002 concerning Child Protection (State Gazette of the Republic of Indonesia Year 2002 Number 109, Supplement to State Gazette of the Republic of Indonesia Number 4235) as amended by Law Number 35 of 2014 concerning Amendment to Law Number 23 of 2002 concerning Child Protection (State Gazette of the Republic of Indonesia Year 2014 Number 397, Supplement to State Gazette of the Republic of Indonesia Number 5606);  
5. Law.......  
5. Law Number 23 of 2014 concerning Regional Government (State Gazette of the Republic of Indonesia Year 2014 Number 244, Supplement to State Gazette of the Republic of Indonesia Number 5587) as amended several times, most recently by Law Number 9 of 2015 ((State Gazette of the Republic of Indonesia Year 2015 Number 58, Supplement to State Gazette of the Republic of Indonesia Number 5679);  
6. Regulation of the State Minister for Women’s Empowerment and Child Protection Number 1 of 2010 concerning Minimum Service Standards in the Field of Integrated Services for Women and Children Victims of Violence;  
7. Regulation of the State Minister for Women’s Empowerment and Child Protection Number 11 of 2011 concerning the Policy for the Development of Child-Friendly Regencies;

THE REGIONAL PEOPLE’S REPRESENTATIVE COUNCIL OF PADANG PARIAMAN REGENCY and

DECIDES:  
To stipulate: REGIONAL REGULATION CONCERNING WOMEN’S EMPOWERMENT AND CHILD PROTECTION.  

CHAPTER I  
GENERAL PROVISIONS  

Article 1  
In this Regional Regulation, what is meant by:  
1. Region is Padang Pariaman Regency.  
2. Regional Government is the Government of Padang Pariaman Regency.  
3. Regent is the Regent of Padang Pariaman Regency.  
4. Regional Apparatus Work Unit, hereinafter abbreviated as SKPD, is a Regional Apparatus Work Unit within the Government of Padang Pariaman Regency.  
5. Women’s Empowerment is efforts of Protection, strengthening of human rights, improvement of quality of life, and increased participation of women in development.  
6. Child Protection is all activities to guarantee and protect Children and their rights so that they can live, grow, develop, and participate optimally in accordance with human dignity and worth, and obtain Protection from Violence and discrimination.  
7. Child is a person who is not yet 18 (eighteen) years old, including a child still in the womb and unmarried.  
8. Prevention is direct efforts carried out by the Regional Government, the community and parents and/or family to prevent violations of women’s and children’s rights.  
9. Violence is any act resulting in misery or suffering, whether physical, sexual, psychological and/or household neglect, including threats to commit acts, coercion, or unlawful deprivation of a person’s liberty.  
10. Children’s Forum is a forum for children’s participation formed with the aim of facilitating children’s involvement in decision-making processes and enjoying changes related to their lives, either directly or indirectly, carried out with the consent and willingness of all children based on awareness and understanding, in accordance with age and level of maturity of thinking.  
11. Child-Friendly Regency, hereinafter abbreviated as KLA, is a Regency that has a child-rights-based development system through the integration of commitments and resources of government, society and the business sector that is comprehensively and sustainably planned in policies, programs and activities to ensure the fulfillment of children’s rights.  
12. Gender Mainstreaming is a strategy built to integrate gender as an integral dimension of planning, formulation, implementation, monitoring and evaluation of national development policy programs.  
13. Community is individuals, families, groups and social organizations and/or community organizations.  
14. Women Victims of Violence are women who experience acts that result in misery or suffering physically, sexually or psychologically, including threats of certain acts, coercion or arbitrary deprivation of liberty, whether occurring in the public sphere or in private life.  
15. Child Victims of Violence are children who experience acts that result in misery or suffering physically, mentally, sexually, psychologically or neglect that threatens life, body and degrades human dignity.  
16. Trafficking in persons is the act of recruiting, transporting, harboring, sending, transferring, or receiving a person by means of threat of Violence, use of Violence, abduction, confinement, forgery, fraud, abuse of power or a position of vulnerability, debt bondage or giving payments or benefits, thereby obtaining the consent of a person having control over another person, whether carried out within a country or across countries, for the purpose of exploitation or resulting in a person being exploited.  
17. Complaint handling is a series of actions carried out by integrated service providers to follow up reports of acts of Violence against women and children, submitted by victims, families or the community.  
18. Social rehabilitation is a service aimed at restoring and developing the abilities of a person experiencing social dysfunction so that they can carry out their social function normally.  
19. Law enforcement is actions by officials authorized by the state to implement laws and regulations.  
20. Legal aid is legal services provided by legal assistants and advocates to carry out assistance processes for witnesses and/or victims of Violence against women and children that are gender-sensitive.  
21. Repatriation is efforts to return women and child victims of Violence from abroad to the debarkation point/entry point, or from the receiving Region to the Region of origin.  
22. Social reintegration is efforts to reunite victims with family, substitute family, or the community that can provide Protection and fulfillment of needs for victims.  
23. Safe house is a temporary shelter intended to provide Protection and a sense of safety to victims of Violence.  
24. Shelter house is a forum for guidance and welfare services for street children that carries out social assistance/guidance activities, maintenance, care, education, and health, skills guidance to ensure that children do not carry out activities on the streets so that they can grow and develop normally.  
25. Spiritual Guidance is an effort to restore mental well-being through strengthening the religion adhered to by the victim.  
26. Gender Mainstreaming is a strategy built to integrate gender as an integral dimension of planning, formulation, implementation, monitoring, and evaluation of development policies, programs, and activities in the Region.  
27. Gender-responsive is consistent and systematic attention to differences between women and men in society accompanied by efforts to eliminate structural and cultural barriers in achieving gender equality.  
28. Gender Sensitivity or Gender Awareness is the ability to identify gaps in power relations between women and men within the family and within the community, the impact of gender-based division of labor on women and men, and that the experiences, problems, needs, interests and aspirations of women and men are different.  
29. Public service providers are any state-organizing institution, corporation, independent institution formed based on Law for public service activities, and other legal entities formed solely for public service activities.  
30. Community Organization is an organization established and formed by the community voluntarily based on shared aspirations, will, needs, interests, activities and objectives to participate in development for the achievement of the goals of the Unitary State of the Republic of Indonesia based on Pancasila.  
31. Parents are biological father and/or mother, or stepfather and/or stepmother, or adoptive father and/or adoptive mother.  
32. Family is the smallest unit in society consisting of husband and wife or husband, wife and their children, or father and his children, or mother and her children, or blood relatives in a direct line upward or downward up to the third degree.  
33. Customs are enduring and hereditary codes of conduct from one generation to another as a legacy, thus strongly integrated with patterns of community behavior.  

Article 2  
The implementation of Women’s Empowerment and Child Protection is based on the principles of:  
a. the best interests of women and children;  
b. gender justice and gender equality;  
c. local wisdom;  
d. law enforcement and legal certainty.  
e. non-discrimination;  
f. the right to life, survival, and development;  
g. respect for the views of the child; and  
h. law enforcement and legal certainty.  

Article 3  
This Regional Regulation aims to:  
a. guarantee the fulfillment of women’s and children’s rights in the Region  
b. serve as a guideline for the Region in planning, policies and strategies for Women’s Empowerment and Child Protection;  
c. protect and prevent women and children from actions, decisions and policies that violate women’s and children’s rights;  
d. enhance the role and value of local wisdom as well as the role of customary institutions in Women’s Empowerment and Child Protection;  

Article 4  
The scope of regulation of Women’s Empowerment and Child Protection includes:  
a. improving women’s quality of life;  
b. protection of women;  
c. gender and child data system;  
d. fulfillment of children’s rights;  
e. child protection;  
f. community participation; and  
g. guidance and supervision.  

CHAPTER II  
IMPROVING WOMEN’S QUALITY OF LIFE  

Part One  
General  

Article 5  
(1) In order to improve women’s quality of life, the Regional Government is responsible for:  
a. respecting and guaranteeing women’s rights without discrimination in accordance with human dignity and worth;  
b. implementing Gender Mainstreaming in the Region;  
c. establishing a Gender Mainstreaming working group in the Region;  
d. enhancing Women’s Empowerment in roles, quality and capacity in the fields of:  
1. politics;  
2. law;  
3. social; and  
4. economy.  
e. strengthening and developing institutions providing Women’s Empowerment services in the Region;  
(2) The responsibility of the Regional Government in efforts to improve women’s quality of life as referred to in paragraph (1) is carried out by the SKPD in accordance with their duties and functions.  

Part Two  
Implementation of Gender Mainstreaming in the Region  

Article 6  
(1) The Regent is responsible for the implementation of Gender Mainstreaming in the Region.  
(2) The responsibility of the Regent as referred to in paragraph (1) may be delegated to the Deputy Regent.  

Article 7  
In implementing Gender Mainstreaming in the Region as referred to in Article 5 paragraph (1) letter b, the Regent designates the SKPD in charge of Women’s Empowerment as the coordinator for the implementation of Gender Mainstreaming.  

Part Three  
Establishment of the Gender Mainstreaming Working Group in the Region  

Article 8  
(1) In efforts to accelerate the institutionalization of Gender Mainstreaming in all Regional SKPD, a Gender Mainstreaming working group is formed in the Region.  
(2) Members of the Gender Mainstreaming working group are all heads/leaders of SKPD.  
(3) The Regent appoints the Head of Bappeda as the chair of the Regional Gender Mainstreaming working group and the head of the SKPD in charge of Women’s Empowerment as the head of the secretariat of the Regional Gender Mainstreaming working group.  
(4) The establishment of the Regional Gender Mainstreaming working group is stipulated by a Regent’s decree.  

Article 9  
The Regional Gender Mainstreaming working group as referred to in Article 14 has the following duties:  
a. promote and facilitate Gender Mainstreaming to each SKPD;  
b. conduct socialization and advocacy of Gender Mainstreaming to sub-district heads, and wali nagari;  
c. prepare an annual work program;  
d. encourage the realization of gender-responsive planning and budgeting;  
e. prepare the annual work plan of the Gender Mainstreaming working group;  
f. be accountable to the Regent through the Deputy Regent;  
g. formulate policy recommendations to the Regent;  
h. compile the Regional Gender profile;  
i. monitor the implementation of Gender Mainstreaming in each agency;  
j. establish a technical team to conduct analysis of the Regional budget;  
k. prepare the Regional action plan for Regional Gender Mainstreaming; and  
l. encourage the selection and appointment of focal points in each SKPD.  

Article 10  
(1) The Technical Team as referred to in Article 9 letter j consists of officials who understand gender-responsive budget analysis.  
(2) The Regional action plan for Regional Gender Mainstreaming as referred to in Article 9 letter k contains:  
a. Gender Mainstreaming in laws and regulations in the Region;  
b. Gender Mainstreaming in the development cycle in the Region;  
c. strengthening the institutional framework for Gender Mainstreaming in the Region; and  
d. strengthening community participation in the Region.  

Article 11  
(1) The Gender Mainstreaming Focal Point in each SKPD consists of officials and/or staff responsible for planning and/or program tasks.  
(2) The Gender Mainstreaming Focal Point as referred to in paragraph (1) has the following duties:  
a. promote Gender Mainstreaming in the work unit;  
b. facilitate the preparation of SKPD work plans and budgeting that are gender-responsive;  
c. conduct training, socialization, and advocacy of Gender Mainstreaming to all officials and staff within the SKPD;  
d. report the implementation of Gender Mainstreaming to the SKPD leadership;  
e. encourage gender analysis of policies, programs, and activities in the work unit; and  
f. facilitate the preparation of gender data in each SKPD;  
(3) The Gender Mainstreaming Focal Point as referred to in paragraph (1) is selected and appointed by the SKPD leadership.  

Part Four  
Enhancing Women’s Roles, Quality, and Capacity  
Political Sector  

Article 12  
Efforts to enhance women’s roles, quality and capacity in the political sector as referred to in Article 5 paragraph (1) letter d number 1 include:  
a. conducting socialization to increase women’s political participation in general elections;  
b. implementing programs that encourage women’s involvement in political processes and public office;  
c. facilitating women’s self-development in organizing, associating and assembling;  
d. involving women in decision-making concerning women’s interests;  

Part Five  
Enhancing Women’s Roles, Quality, and Capacity  
Legal Sector  

Article 13  
Efforts to enhance women’s roles, quality and capacity in the legal sector as referred to in Article 5 paragraph (1) letter d number 2 include:  
a. conducting socialization to increase women’s legal knowledge and awareness;  
b. mapping and reviewing gender-biased policies;  
c. involving women’s organizations in the process of drafting and discussing Regional legal products related to women’s participation;  

Part Six  
Enhancing Women’s Roles, Quality, and Capacity  
Social Sector  

Article 14  
Efforts to enhance women’s roles, quality and capacity in the social sector as referred to in Article 5 paragraph (1) letter d number 3 include:  
a. increasing knowledge and skills to encourage fulfillment of education in accordance with their potential;  
b. improving and fulfilling the quality of women’s health;  
c. increasing awareness and knowledge about independent, healthy and prosperous family planning, including access to consultation services and marriage registration;  
d. increasing women’s awareness of the impact of early marriage;  
e. facilitating and pursuing the preservation of customs and the development of science, technology, arts and culture for women’s advancement;  
f. increasing women’s awareness and knowledge regarding independent, healthy and prosperous family planning;  
g. implementing programs to reduce maternal mortality;  
h. encouraging the establishment of women’s organizations in the Region;  

Part Seven  
Enhancing Women’s Roles, Quality, and Capacity  
Economic Sector  

Article 15  
Efforts to enhance women’s roles, quality and capacity in the economic sector as referred to in Article 5 paragraph (1) letter d number 4 include:  
a. implementing programs, activities, and training to improve women’s skills and expertise in order to realize improved women’s welfare;  
b. facilitating strengthening of business capital for women with weak economic conditions;  
c. marketing products of women’s business results with weak economic conditions;  
d. training to develop women’s capabilities through empowerment of pre-prosperous family economies;  
e. comprehensive entrepreneurship training for women.  

CHAPTER III  
PROTECTION OF WOMEN  

Part One  
General  

Article 16  
(1) The Regional Government in efforts to protect women is responsible for:  
a. establishing and implementing policies, programs and activities for the protection of women;  
b. undertaking efforts to prevent acts of Violence against women, including the criminal act of trafficking in persons;  
c. increasing the effectiveness of services for women victims of Violence;  
d. increasing protection efforts for women workers;  
e. strengthening and developing institutions providing women’s protection services;  
f. cooperating with the Government, other Regional Governments, the private sector, the business sector and/or NGOs in efforts to prevent acts of Violence against women;  
g. involving the active role of families, ninik mamak, bundo kanduang, traditional leaders, religious leaders, the community, and institutions in the field of women’s protection;  
(2) The responsibility of the Regional Government in efforts to protect women as referred to in paragraph (1) is carried out by the SKPD in accordance with their duties and functions and may coordinate with relevant agencies.  

Part Two  
Prevention of Acts of Violence against Women  

Article 17  
In order to prevent acts of Violence against women, the Regional Government:  
a. conducts socialization regarding prevention of Violence against women by optimizing the role of customary, cultural, religious institutions and community organizations;  
b. builds networks and cooperation with law enforcement officials, government officials, universities and various non-governmental organizations that work and/or care about women and children victims of Violence;  
c. provides public facilities that are comfortable and safe for women;  

Part Three  
Effectiveness of Services for Women Victims of Acts of Violence  

Paragraph 1  
General  

Article 18  
(1) Protection of women victims of acts of Violence is carried out through integrated services.  
(2) The provision of integrated services for women victims of acts of Violence as referred to in paragraph (1) uses the principles of:  
a. gender-responsive;  
b. non-discrimination;  
c. equal and respectful relations;  
d. maintaining confidentiality;  
e. providing a sense of safety and comfort;  
f. respecting individual differences;  
g. non-judgmental;  
h. respecting the victim’s choices and decisions;  
i. sensitive to the victim’s background and condition and the use of language appropriate and understood by the victim;  
j. fast and simple;  
k. empathy; and  
l. fulfillment of women’s rights.  

Article 19  
Forms of integrated services for women victims of acts of Violence include:  
a. complaint handling;  
b. health rehabilitation;  
c. social rehabilitation;  
d. law enforcement and legal aid; and  
e. repatriation and social reintegration.  

Paragraph 2  
Complaint Handling  

Article 20  
Integrated complaint handling services as referred to in Article 19 letter a are carried out by:  
a. hospitals;  
b. community health centers (puskesmas);  
c. women and children service units;  
d. trauma center protection houses;  
e. women crisis centres;  
f. advisory body for guidance and preservation of marriage;  
g. police;  
h. prosecutor’s office;  
i. courts;  
j. ministry of religious affairs; and/or  
k. legal aid institutions;  

Article 21  
(1) Complaints of acts of Violence against women may be made by reporters consisting of:  
a. the victim,  
b. family,  
c. the community; and/or  
d. community organizations in the field of protection of women and children, such as: P2TP2A, Safe House, etc.)  
(2) Reporters as referred to in paragraph (1) may submit complaints:  
a. directly;  
b. by telephone; and/or  
c. by letter.  
(3) In addition to handling complaints submitted by reporters, officers at service institutions as referred to in Article 20 are also authorized to handle women victims of acts of Violence obtained through other information.  
(4) Further provisions regarding procedures for complaint handling services as referred to in paragraph (1) and paragraph (2) are regulated by a Regent Regulation.  

Paragraph 3  
Health Rehabilitation  

Article 22  
(1) Health rehabilitation as referred to in Article 19 letter b is an effort covering aspects of:  
a. promotive;  
b. preventive;  
c. curative; and  
d. rehabilitative.  
(2) Health rehabilitation as referred to in paragraph (1) is carried out comprehensively, can be accessed 24 (twenty-four) hours, is of quality and is carried out in accordance with health service standards.  
(3) Women victims of acts of Violence have the right to free-of-charge health services, including:  
a. medical services;  
b. medicolegal services;  
c. psychosocial counseling; and  
d. medical examination report (visum).  
(4) Further provisions regarding procedures for implementing health services as referred to in paragraph (3) are regulated by a Regent Regulation.  

Paragraph 4  
Social Rehabilitation  

Article 23  
(1) Social rehabilitation as referred to in Article 19 letter c is intended to restore and develop the abilities of women victims of acts of Violence who experience social dysfunction so that they can carry out their social function in society normally.  
(2) Social rehabilitation as referred to in paragraph (1) includes services of:  
a. counseling;  
b. provision of a safe house; and  
c. spiritual guidance.  
(3) The provision of services as referred to in paragraph (2) is organized by:  
a. SKPD that has duties and functions in the social sector;  
b. SKPD in charge of Women’s Empowerment, and may cooperate with integrated service centers, women crisis centres or existing community organizations in the social sector; or  
c. the ministry of religious affairs.  

Article 24  
(1) Counseling services as referred to in Article 23 paragraph (2) letter a aim to:  
a. help women victims of acts of Violence recognize their problems and find effective ways to overcome them themselves;  
b. empower women victims of acts of Violence to determine their future;  
c. provide moral support to women victims of acts of Violence in facing the process they undergo; and  
d. make women victims of acts of Violence feel accepted in the community environment.  
(2) Counseling services are carried out by gender-responsive social rehabilitation officers, consisting of:  
a. counselors;  
b. psychologists; and  
c. psychiatrists.  
(3) Counseling services are carried out in a place that guarantees safety, comfort and confidentiality of information from women victims of acts of Violence.  
(4) Stages of counseling services include:  
a. initial counseling;  
b. follow-up counseling;  
c. case discussion; and  
d. site visits.  

Article 25  
(1) The Regional Government may provide a safe house for women victims of acts of Violence.  
(2) The service of providing a safe house as referred to in Article 23 paragraph (2) letter b aims to protect women victims of acts of Violence from intimidation by perpetrators or other parties.  
(3) The location of the safe house as referred to in paragraph (1) must be kept confidential.  
(4) Criteria for women victims of acts of Violence who require a safe house include:  
a. their life is threatened;  
b. they are rejected by family or the community;  
c. they require intensive services but their residence is relatively far; and/or  
d. they would be neglected if not placed in a safe house.  
(5) The provision of safe house services is carried out by social rehabilitation officers who have gender sensitivity or gender awareness, consisting of:  
a. social workers/volunteers;  
b. security officers; and/or  
c. community leaders.  
(6) Further provisions regarding the provision of safe house services are regulated by a Regent Regulation.  

Article 26  
(1) Spiritual guidance as referred to in Article 23 paragraph (2) letter c aims for psychological recovery.  
(2) Spiritual guidance as referred to in paragraph (1) is carried out based on the principles of:  
a. not forcing any religion or belief; and  
b. leaving the final decision in the hands of the victim (self determination).  
(3) Spiritual guidance is carried out by spiritual guidance officers.  
(4) Spiritual guidance officers as referred to in paragraph (3) consist of consultants of the Advisory, Guidance and Preservation of Marriage Body (BP4), officers of the Office of Religious Affairs, Ustad/Ulama, priests, pastors and other religious leaders who have gender sensitivity or gender awareness.  

Paragraph 5  
Law Enforcement and Legal Aid  

Article 27  
(1) Law enforcement and legal aid services as referred to in Article 19 letter d are actions by law enforcement officials to implement laws and regulations in order to handle cases of acts of Violence against women, especially to impose sanctions on perpetrators and provide Protection for witnesses and/or victims.  
(2) Law enforcement and legal aid services as referred to in paragraph (1) are carried out by prioritizing the best interests of the victim.  

Article 28  
(1) Law enforcement and legal aid services are intended to provide assistance and Protection for women victims of acts of Violence and witnesses with a gender-responsive approach so that victims obtain justice and legal certainty.  
(2) Types of legal aid services include:  
a. legal aid in the reporting process and preparation of examination reports;  
b. legal aid in the prosecution process at the prosecutor’s office;  
c. legal aid in the examination process in court;  
d. legal aid in the court hearing decision process until a final and binding legal decision; and  
e. legal aid in the mediation process.  
(3) The Regional Government provides legal aid to women victims of acts of Violence based on the provisions of laws and regulations.  
(4) In providing legal aid as referred to in paragraph (3), the Regional Government may cooperate with legal aid institutions, women crisis centers, advocates and/or other gender-sensitive legal assistants.  

Paragraph 6  
Repatriation and Social Reintegration  

Article 29  
(1) Repatriation is intended to return women victims of acts of Violence to their Region of origin.  
(2) Repatriation of women victims of acts of Violence is accompanied by efforts of social reintegration.  

Article 30  
(1) Social reintegration is carried out in order to reunite women victims of acts of Violence with family, substitute family, or the community that can provide Protection and fulfillment of needs for victims.  
(2) Social reintegration is intended so that:  
a. women victims of acts of Violence can resume their social life; and  
b. women victims of acts of Violence can have a decent life.  
(3) Social reintegration as referred to in paragraph (1) is carried out after the victim receives social rehabilitation services.  

Article 31  
The Regional Government carries out repatriation and social reintegration of women victims of acts of Violence in accordance with laws and regulations.  

Part Four  
Employment Protection  

Article 32  
(1) Employers who employ women are obliged to maintain decency, safety and security while at the workplace.  
(2) Employers are obliged to place female workers in a place that is safe for women’s reproductive health.  
(3) Employers are obliged to fulfill the rights of female workers to receive wages in accordance with the provisions of laws and regulations.  
(4) Employers are obliged to provide special breastfeeding facilities in their companies.  

Article 33  
(1) Female workers/laborers under 18 (eighteen) years of age are prohibited from being employed between 23:00 WIB and 07:00 WIB.  
(2) Employers are prohibited from employing pregnant female workers/laborers who, according to a doctor’s statement, would endanger the health and safety of their pregnancy.  
(3) Employers who employ female workers/laborers between 23:00 WIB and 07:00 WIB are obliged to:  
a. provide nutritious food and drinks; and  
b. maintain decency and security while at the workplace.  
(4) Employers are obliged to provide shuttle transportation for female workers/laborers who depart for and return from work between 23:00 WIB and 05:00 WIB.  

CHAPTER V  
IMPROVING FAMILY QUALITY  

Article 34  
(1) In order to improve family quality, the Regional Government is responsible for:  
a. undertaking efforts to improve family quality to realize gender equality and children’s rights;  
b. providing services for families in realizing gender equality and children’s rights.  
c. strengthening and developing institutions providing family quality improvement services in the Region; and  
(2) The responsibility of the Regional Government in efforts to improve family quality as referred to in paragraph (1) is carried out by the SKPD in accordance with their duties and functions.  

Article 35  
The Regional Government undertakes efforts to improve family quality as referred to in Article 34 paragraph (1) letter a including:  
a. guidance;  
b. counseling;  
c. supervision;  
d. facilitation of the implementation of family development;  
e. improving the quality of health of pregnant and breastfeeding mothers;  

Article 36  
Guidance as referred to in Article 35 letter a includes:  
a. growth and development of toddlers;  
b. protection of adolescent reproductive health;  
c. maturation of marriage age;  
d. birth regulation; and  
e. population management.  

Article 37  
Counseling as referred to in Article 35 letter b includes:  
a. conducting outreach on improving women’s quality of life;  
b. conducting premarital counseling;  

Article 38.  
Supervision as referred to in Article 35 letter c includes:  
a. conducting evaluation of data on toddler growth and development;  
b. conducting evaluation of post-marriage data;  

Article 39  
Provision of services for families as referred to in Article 34 paragraph (1) letter b includes:  
a. communication, information and education;  
b. contraceptive devices and medicines;  
c. recording and reporting of family planning services;  
d. adequate health service facilities and infrastructure;  
e. breastfeeding corner rooms in every public facility.  

Article 40  
Communication, information and education as referred to in Article 39 letter a are communication activities to increase knowledge and improve attitudes and behavior of families and the community in population and family planning programs.  

Article 41  
(1) In addition to the Regional Government, the private sector (including limited liability companies that provide public services) is obliged to provide breastfeeding corner rooms.  
(2) Breastfeeding corner rooms as referred to in paragraph (1) are rooms that are proper and adequate.  
(3) Private parties that do not provide breastfeeding corner rooms as referred to in paragraph (2) are subject to administrative sanctions in the form of:  
a. written warning;  
b. restriction of business activities;  
c. freezing of business activities;  
d. administrative fines; and/or  
e. revocation of permits.  

CHAPTER VI  
FULFILLMENT OF CHILDREN’S RIGHTS  

Part One  
General  

Article 42  
(1) The Regional Government in efforts to fulfill children’s rights is responsible for:  
a. establishing and implementing policies and programs/activities for fulfilling children’s rights;  
b. respecting and guaranteeing the human rights of every child without distinguishing ethnicity, religion, race, group, sex, ethnic origin, culture and language, the child’s legal status, birth order, and physical and/or mental condition;  
c. strengthening and developing institutions providing family quality improvement services in realizing children’s rights;  
d. guaranteeing the Protection, care, and welfare of children by paying attention to the rights and obligations of parents, guardians, or other persons legally responsible for the child;  
e. providing facilities and infrastructure in the implementation of fulfilling children’s rights;  
f. guaranteeing the child’s right to express opinions in accordance with the child’s age and level of intelligence;  
g. conducting supervision and guidance in the implementation of fulfilling children’s rights;  
h. cooperating with the private sector and/or NGOs in the implementation of Child Protection;  
(2) The responsibility of the Regional Government in efforts of Child Protection as referred to in paragraph (1) is carried out by the SKPD in accordance with their duties and functions.  

Part Two  
Birth Registration  

Article 43  
(1) The Regional Government is obliged to organize birth registration of children by issuing birth certificates.  
(2) Birth certificates as referred to in paragraph (1) are provided free of charge.  

Part Three  
Education  

Article 44  
The Regional Government guarantees the implementation of the 9 (nine) year compulsory education program for children aged 7 (seven) to 15 (fifteen) years.  

Article 45  
(1) Every education unit provider is obliged to provide opportunities for children to obtain education services without discrimination.  
(2) Every education unit provider is prohibited from expelling children from educational institutions unless there is a guarantee of the continuity of the child’s education.  
(3) In the event that a child victim of acts of Violence drops out of school, the education unit provider is obliged to coordinate with relevant agencies.  

Article 46  
(1) Every child has the right to obtain education and instruction in order to develop their personality and level of intelligence in accordance with their interests and talents.  
(2) Every child has the right to obtain Protection in education units from sexual crimes and Violence committed by educators, education personnel, fellow students, and/or other parties.  

Article 47  
(1) Children with disabilities and children with special needs are given equal opportunities to obtain formal, non-formal and informal education services, including inclusive education programs.  
(2) The Regional Government provides educators for inclusive education in each education unit.  

Part Four  
Health and Social  

Article 48  
(1) The Regional Government is obliged to provide comprehensive health services for children, so that every child obtains an optimal degree of health since in the womb.  
(2) Comprehensive health services as referred to in paragraph (1) include:  
a. promotive, preventive, curative and rehabilitative efforts for basic health services;  
b. exemption from cost burdens for malnourished children, children with disabilities, children with special needs, children infected with HIV/AIDS, child laborers, children victims of abuse of narcotics, alcohol, psychotropics and other addictive substances, children who are victims of Violence, and children victims of trafficking in persons; and  
(3) The Regional Government, the community, families and parents are obliged to endeavor so that children born are protected from diseases that threaten survival and/or cause disability.  

Article 49  
(1) Promotive efforts for basic health services as referred to in Article 46 paragraph (2) letter a are intended to improve the degree of health more optimally.  
(2) Promotive efforts as referred to in paragraph (1) are carried out by providing socialization or counseling to the community regarding healthy lifestyles which include:  
a. the importance of exclusive breastfeeding for infants;  
b. the importance of immunization for toddlers;  
c. clean living habits;  
d. the importance of nutritious food;  
e. the dangers of drugs and smoking; and  
(3) In addition to promotive efforts as referred to in paragraph (2), the Regional Government may carry out other activities in the implementation of health.  

Article 50  
(1) Preventive efforts for basic health services as referred to in Article 46 paragraph (2) letter a are intended to prevent the occurrence of disease or health problems.  
(2) Efforts as referred to in paragraph (1) are carried out by:  
a. requiring mothers to provide exclusive breastfeeding to infants up to the age of 6 (six) months;  
b. providing free immunizations and vitamins to infants and toddlers;  
c. providing nutritious food to toddlers from underprivileged families;  
d. optimizing the role of posyandu for health services for mothers and toddlers;  
e. conducting periodic health checks; and  
f. providing sanitation facilities and clean water in every school.  

Article 51  
(1) Curative efforts for basic health services as referred to in Article 46 paragraph (2) letter a are intended to prevent disease from becoming more severe through treatment.  
(2) Treatment as referred to in paragraph (1) is carried out free of charge for children from underprivileged families.  

Article 52  
(1) Rehabilitative efforts for basic health services as referred to in Article 46 paragraph (2) letter a are intended to maintain and restore conditions after illness.  
(2) Rehabilitative efforts as referred to in paragraph (1) are carried out through periodic health checks.  

Article 53  
(1) The Regional Government is obliged to provide Social Welfare for:  
a. children in conflict with the law;  
b. children victims of Violence who are economically and sexually exploited, neglected children, children victims of abduction and children victims of trafficking in persons;  
c. children who do not have parents and are cared for by parties or families who are unable;  
d. children victims of natural disasters or social disasters; and  
e. children with disabilities;  
(2) Social welfare as referred to in paragraph (1) may be in the form of providing services:  
a. health;  
b. education;  
c. legal aid;  
d. assistance;  
e. social, mental and spiritual guidance;  
f. social rehabilitation; and/or  
g. reintegration.  

Part Five  
Employment  

Article 54  
The Regional Government conducts periodic supervision of every person/entity that employs children in efforts to protect child workers.  

Article 55  
Every person/entity that employs children must pay attention to the requirements:  
a. at least 15 (fifteen) years of age;  
b. obtain written consent from the parents/guardian of the child worker;  
c. have a written employment agreement between the employer and the parents/guardian of the child worker and obtain approval from the authorized agency;  
d. not employ children at night;  
e. working time no more than 3 (three) hours in a day;  

Article 56  
In the event that children are employed together with adult workers, the children’s workplace must be separated from the adult workers’ workplace.  

Article 57  
(1) Every person/entity is prohibited from employing and involving children in the worst forms of work.  
(2) The worst forms of work referred to in paragraph (1) include:  
a. all work in the form of slavery or similar practices;  
b. all work that uses, provides, or offers children for prostitution, pornography production, pornographic performances, or gambling;  
c. all work that uses, provides, or involves children for the production and trafficking of alcoholic beverages, narcotics, psychotropics, and other addictive substances; and/or  
d. all work that endangers the health, safety, or morals of children.  

Part Six  
Facilitation of the Child Justice System  

Article 58  
(1) Facilitation of the child justice system includes criminal and civil justice.  
(2) Facilitation of the criminal justice system as referred to in paragraph (1) includes:  
a. provision of child companions or volunteers during examination by investigators;  
b. provision of legal aid services and psychologists;  
c. involving members/investigators of the local police in efforts to prevent criminal acts committed by children as well as children as victims;  
d. realizing a restorative justice process for children in conflict with the law; and  
e. rehabilitation and social reintegration services;  
(3) Fulfillment of children’s rights in the civil justice system as referred to in paragraph (1) includes the provision and delivery of consultation services and legal aid during the judicial process.  
(4) In facilitating the child justice system as referred to in paragraph (1) and paragraph (2), the authorized SKPD is the SKPD in charge of protection of women and children  

Part Seven  
Children’s Forum  

Article 59  
(1) In organizing the fulfillment of children’s rights to express opinions and participate, the Regional Government facilitates the establishment of a Children’s Forum.  
(2) The Children’s Forum as referred to in paragraph (1) is a representation of children in the Region, both representation of children’s geographic domicile, components of children’s socio-cultural groups and children’s educational backgrounds.  
(3) In every formulation of policies related to children, the Regional Government is obliged to pay attention to and accommodate children’s opinions conveyed through the Children’s participation forum.  
(4) Further provisions regarding the establishment of the Children’s Forum as referred to in paragraph (1) to paragraph (3) are stipulated by a Regent’s Decree.  

Part Eight  
Child-Friendly Regency  

Article 60  
(1) In order to fulfill children’s rights in an integrated, systematic and sustainable manner, the Regional Government establishes a policy for the Development of KLA.  
(2) The KLA Development Policy contains:  
a. the KLA concept concerning objectives, strategies and the roles of related parties;  
b. children’s rights; and  
c. the KLA development approach.  

Article 61  
(1) The KLA development policy is directed at fulfilling children’s rights which are divided into 5 (five) groups, including:  
a. civil rights and freedoms;  
b. family environment and alternative care;  
c. basic health and welfare;  
d. education, use of leisure time, and cultural activities; and  
e. special protection.  
(2) Further provisions regarding the KLA development policy as referred to in paragraph (1) are regulated by a Regent Regulation.  

Article 62  
(1) In order to ensure the effectiveness of implementing KLA policy in the Region, a KLA Task Force is formed.  
(2) The establishment and composition of membership of the KLA Task Force are stipulated by a Regent’s Decree.  
(3) The KLA Task Force is domiciled at the Office of Women’s Empowerment and Family Planning.  
(4) The KLA Task Force as referred to in paragraph (1) has the main duties:  
a. coordinate the implementation of KLA policy and development;  
b. conduct socialization, advocacy and communication, information and education of KLA policy;  
c. collect basic data for KLA development;  
d. conduct needs analysis based on basic data;  
e. disseminate basic data;  
f. determine program focus and priorities in realizing KLA, adjusted to regional potential;  
g. prepare a 5 (five) year Regional action plan for Child-Friendly City and work mechanisms; and  
h. conduct monitoring, evaluation and reporting at least 1 (one) time per year.  

Article 63  
In carrying out its duties, the KLA Task Force may coordinate and cooperate with PPT institutions and other institutions that provide Child Protection services.  

CHAPTER VII  
CHILD PROTECTION  

Article 64  
The Regional Government, in order to provide protection for children:  
a. implements and supports national policies in the implementation of Child Protection in the Region;  
b. guarantees children to exercise their right to express opinions in accordance with the child’s age and level of intelligence;  
c. conducts prevention of Violence against children by optimizing the role of customary institutions;  
d. conducts socialization, seminars and counseling regarding Protection and children’s rights;  
e. develops child-friendly school models;  
f. provides public facilities that are child-friendly and safe for children  
g. encourages community concern about the importance of protection for children;  

Article 65  
Service efforts carried out by the Regional Government for child victims of acts of Violence as referred to in Article 42 to Article 64 apply mutatis mutandis to service efforts for child victims of acts of Violence.  

CHAPTER VIII  
PARTICIPATION OF THE COMMUNITY, AND PARENTS AND/OR FAMILY  

Part One  
Community  

Article 66  
(1) Community participation in efforts to protect women and children:  
a. participate in prevention and provide support for the development of programs to prevent violations of women’s and children’s rights;  
b. provide information and/or report violations of women’s and children’s rights to the authorized parties; and  
c. oversee the law enforcement process for cases of violations of women’s and children’s rights.  
(2) Ninik mamak, alim ulama and cadiak pandai as well as bundo kanduang are responsible for protecting and preventing violations of women’s and children’s rights.  
(3) Community participation as referred to in paragraph (1) and paragraph (2) is based on laws and regulations and the values of local wisdom of the local community.  

Article 67  
(1) In order to prevent violations of women’s rights, the community:  
a. monitors various conditions in its environment that can cause violations of women’s rights;  
b. increases concern for violations of women’s rights;  
c. strengthens the role of community organizations, religious organizations and the family environment;  
d. supervises the implementation of Women’s Empowerment and Child Protection organized by the Regional Government through mechanisms for conveying aspirations to the Regent or the Regional People’s Representative Council;  
(2) In order to prevent violations of children’s rights, the community:  
a. monitors various conditions in its environment that cause violations of children’s rights;  
b. increases concern for violations of children’s rights;  
c. strengthens the role of community organizations, religious organizations and the family environment; and  
prevents marriage at an early age.  

Part Two  
Parents and/or Family  

Article 68  
The family in efforts to protect women’s rights plays a role to:  
a. protect women from conflict and domestic Violence that degrades women’s dignity;  
b. provide equal opportunities for women to develop themselves in education, social, economic, political and cultural fields; and  
c. prevent violations of women’s rights in the household.  

Article 69  
(1) Parents in efforts to protect children’s rights play a role to:  
a. nurture, care for, educate, and protect children;  
b. develop children in accordance with their abilities, talents, and interests;  
c. provide formal and non-formal education;  
d. provide character education and instill moral values in children;  
e. guide children to behave in accordance with religion, ethics and morals as well as customs; and  
f. prevent marriage at an early age.  
(2) In the event that parents do not exist, or their whereabouts are unknown, or for some reason cannot carry out their obligations and responsibilities, then the obligations and responsibilities as referred to in paragraph (1) may transfer to the family.  

CHAPTER IX  
GUIDANCE AND SUPERVISION  

Article 70  
(1) The Regional Government is obliged to provide guidance and supervision of the implementation of protection for women and children victims of Violence.  
(2) Guidance as referred to in paragraph (1) includes:  
a. guidelines and fulfillment standards;  
b. technical guidance and training;  
c. provision of facilities;  
d. monitoring; and  
e. evaluation.  
(3) Guidance as referred to in paragraph (2) is in order to realize the objectives of protection for women and children victims of Violence in accordance with minimum service standards.  

Article 71  
(1) Supervision of the implementation of Child Protection as regulated in this Regional Regulation is carried out by the Regional Government Supervisory Institution of Padang Pariaman Regency and P2TP2A;  
(2) In carrying out supervision, P2TP2A is authorized to recommend cases to law enforcement officials, government agencies and other community institutions as regulated in the Law  
(3) Recommendations submitted by P2TP2A must be followed up by law enforcement officials, government agencies, community institutions and other related individuals.  

CHAPTER X  
FUNDING  

Article 72  
Funding for the implementation of programs and activities of Women’s Empowerment and Child Protection is sourced from:  
a. the regional revenue and expenditure budget; and  
b. other lawful sources in accordance with the provisions of laws and regulations.  

CHAPTER XI  
CLOSING PROVISIONS  

Article 73  
Implementing regulations of this Regional Regulation shall be stipulated no later than 6 (six) months from the date this Regional Regulation is promulgated.  

Article 74  
This Regional Regulation comes into force on the date of promulgation; so that everyone may know it, the promulgation of this Regional Regulation is ordered by placing it in the Regional Gazette of Padang Pariaman Regency.
