REGENT OF NORTH CENTRAL TIMOR  
EAST NUSA TENGGARA PROVINCE  
REGIONAL REGULATION OF NORTH CENTRAL TIMOR REGENCY NUMBER 3 OF 2020  
CONCERNING  
THE IMPLEMENTATION OF WOMEN’S EMPOWERMENT  
BY THE GRACE OF GOD ALMIGHTY  
THE REGENT OF NORTH CENTRAL TIMOR,  

Considering: a. that every person has the same dignity and worth to live in society, as a nation and as a state and is entitled to protection of human rights and fundamental freedoms without discrimination as an effort to realize gender equality and gender justice in national and regional development;  
b. that in order to realize gender equality and gender justice in development in North Central Timor Regency, it is necessary to increase the effectiveness and optimize the implementation of the sub-affair of women’s empowerment as an inseparable part of the functional activities of governmental and non-governmental institutions in order to improve the status, role, and quality of women in development in North Central Timor Regency;  
c. that to ensure legal certainty and to fill a legal vacuum in order to realize gender equality and gender justice in development in North Central Timor Regency, it is necessary to regulate it in a Regional Regulation concerning the implementation of women’s empowerment;  
d. that based on the considerations as referred to in letter a, letter b, and letter c, it is necessary to stipulate a Regional Regulation concerning the Implementation of Women’s Empowerment;  

Bearing in mind: 1. Article 18 paragraph (6) of the 1945 Constitution of the Republic of Indonesia;  
2. Law Number 69 of 1958 concerning the Establishment of Level II Regions within the Territory of Level I Regions of Bali, West Nusa Tenggara and East Nusa Tenggara (State Gazette of the Republic of Indonesia Year 1958 Number 122, Supplement to the State Gazette of the Republic of Indonesia Number 1655);  
3. Law Number 23 of 2014 concerning Regional Government (State Gazette of the Republic of Indonesia Year 2014 Number 244, Supplement to the State Gazette of the Republic of Indonesia Number 5587); as amended several times, most recently by Law Number 9 of 2015 concerning the Second Amendment to Law Number 23 of 2014 concerning Regional Government (State Gazette of the Republic of Indonesia Year 2015 Number 58, Supplement to the State Gazette of the Republic of Indonesia Number 5679);  

With Joint Approval of:  
THE REGIONAL PEOPLE’S REPRESENTATIVE COUNCIL OF NORTH CENTRAL TIMOR REGENCY and  
THE REGENT OF NORTH CENTRAL TIMOR  

DECIDES:  
To stipulate: REGIONAL REGULATION CONCERNING THE IMPLEMENTATION OF WOMEN’S EMPOWERMENT.  

CHAPTER I  
GENERAL PROVISIONS  
Article 1  
In this Regional Regulation, what is meant by:  
1. Region is North Central Timor Regency.  
2. Regional Government is the Government of North Central Timor Regency.  
3. Regent is the Regent of North Central Timor.  
4. Regional People’s Representative Council, hereinafter abbreviated as DPRD, is the Regional People’s Representative Council of North Central Timor Regency.  
5. Regional Secretary is the Regional Secretary of North Central Timor Regency.  
6. Office (Dinas) is the Regional Apparatus that administers governmental affairs in the field of women’s empowerment and child protection.  
7. Head of Office is the Head of the Regional Apparatus that administers governmental affairs in the field of women’s empowerment.  
8. Regional Apparatus, hereinafter abbreviated as PD, is the Regional Apparatus in North Central Timor Regency.  
9. Head of Regional Apparatus is the Head of a Regional Apparatus in North Central Timor Regency.  
10. Agency (Badan) is the Regional Apparatus that administers governmental affairs in the field of development planning and regional research.  
11. Head of Agency is the Regional Apparatus that administers governmental affairs in the field of planning.  
12. Women’s Empowerment is every effort to improve physical, mental-spiritual, social abilities, knowledge, and skills so that women are ready to be utilized according to their respective abilities.  
13. Gender Mainstreaming, hereinafter abbreviated as PUG, is a strategy built to integrate gender as an integral dimension of the planning, implementation, monitoring, and evaluation of regional development policies and programs.  
14. Gender is a concept that refers to the roles and responsibilities of men and women that occur as a result of societal construction and can change due to the social and cultural conditions of society.  
15. Gender Equality is the same condition for men and women to obtain opportunities and their rights as human beings so as to be able to play a role and participate in the development process.  
16. Gender Justice is a process to be fair to men and women.  
17. Gender Mainstreaming Working Group, hereinafter referred to as Pokja PUG, is a consultation forum for implementers and drivers of gender mainstreaming from various agencies/institutions in the region.  
18. Gender Mainstreaming Focal Point, hereinafter referred to as Focal Point PUG, is a PD apparatus that has the ability to carry out gender mainstreaming in their respective work units.  
19. Regional Medium-Term Development Plan, hereinafter abbreviated as RPJMD, is an elaboration of the Regent’s vision, mission, and programs, the preparation of which is guided by the Regional Long-Term Development Plan, containing regional development strategies, general regional policies, regional programs, and a macroeconomic framework that includes an overall description of the economy including the direction of fiscal policy in a work plan in the form of an indicative regulatory framework and funding framework.  
20. Gender Analysis is a systematically built process to identify and understand the division of labor or roles of men and women, access to and control over development resources, participation in development, and the benefits they enjoy.  
21. Gender-Responsive Budget is a performance-based budget allocation for certain programs or activities whose input, output, and outcome indicators can ensure the fair fulfillment of the aspirations and needs of women and men.  
22. Communication, Information and Education, hereinafter abbreviated as KIE, is the delivery of messages in the form of information, ideas, or facts related to gender mainstreaming that need to be known by the public directly or indirectly through communication channels to message recipients to obtain effects, as a process of behavior change toward gender responsiveness.  
23. Village/Urban Village Development Planning Deliberation, hereinafter referred to as Musrembangkel, is an annual village/urban village development planning deliberation forum involving development actors in the village/urban village.  
24. Subdistrict Development Planning Deliberation, hereinafter referred to as Musrembangcam, is a deliberation forum among development actors to discuss and agree on steps for handling priority activity programs listed in the proposed list of village development activity planning that is integrated with the regional development priorities of North Central Timor Regency in the subdistrict area, coordinated by the Regional Development Planning Agency and implemented by the Subdistrict Head.  
25. Development planning deliberation for the preparation of the Regional Medium-Term Development Plan, hereinafter referred to as Musrembang RPJMD, is a deliberation forum among stakeholders to discuss, sharpen, and agree on the draft Regional Medium-Term Development Plan.  
26. Strategic Plan, hereinafter referred to as Renstra, is a five-year planning document containing the vision, mission, goals, strategies, policies, programs, and development activities in accordance with the duties and functions of the organization as an elaboration of the RPJMD.  
27. Work Plan, hereinafter referred to as Renja, is a one-year planning document containing policies, programs, and development activities either planned directly by the Regional Apparatus or pursued by encouraging community participation as an elaboration of the Renstra.  
28. Regional Government Work Plan, hereinafter abbreviated as RKPD, is a one-year planning document containing policies, programs, and development activities either planned directly by the Regional Government or pursued by encouraging community participation as an elaboration of the Renstra.  
29. Regional Action Plan, hereinafter abbreviated as RAD, is a planning document for activities prepared by a technical team to be implemented in a certain period/time.  
30. Gender Analysis Pathway, hereinafter abbreviated as GAP, is a gender analysis tool that can be used to assist planners in carrying out gender mainstreaming in the planning of development policies/programs/activities with the aim of identifying gender gaps and gender issues while simultaneously preparing development policy/program/activity plans aimed at reducing or eliminating such gender gaps.  
31. Technical Team is a team formed by the Pokja PUG with the task of conducting gender-perspective budget analysis and preparing the RAD PUG.  

CHAPTER II  
SCOPE  
Article 2  
The scope of the implementation of Women’s Empowerment includes:  
a. women’s quality of life;  
b. women’s protection;  
c. family quality; and  
d. gender data system.  

CHAPTER III  
WOMEN’S QUALITY OF LIFE  
Part One  
General  
Article 3  
Women’s Quality of Life includes:  
a. institutionalization of PUG;  
b. Women’s Empowerment in the political, legal, socio-cultural, and economic fields in community organizations; and  
c. strengthening and development of institutions providing women’s empowerment services.  

Part Two  
Institutionalization of PUG  
Paragraph 1  
General  
Article 4  
Institutionalization of PUG includes:  
a. designation of the PUG coordinator;  
b. Pokja PUG;  
c. Focal Point PUG;  
d. budgeting;  
e. gender-based development planning;  
f. monitoring and evaluation;  
g. reporting; and  
h. supervision.  

Paragraph 2  
PUG Coordinator  
Article 5  
(1) The Regent has the authority to designate the Head of PD that administers governmental affairs in the field of Women’s Empowerment as the coordinator for the implementation of PUG.  
(2) The designation as referred to in paragraph (1) shall be by Regent’s Decree.  

Paragraph 3  
Pokja PUG  
Article 6  
(1) The Regent has the authority to establish the Pokja PUG.  
(2) The establishment of the Pokja PUG as referred to in paragraph (1) shall be stipulated by Regent’s Decree.  
(3) The Regent’s Decree as referred to in paragraph (2) shall be valid for 5 (five) years.  
(4) The management of the Pokja PUG consists of:  
a. chairperson by the head of the agency;  
b. secretariat by the head of the office;  
c. members include:  
1. heads of PD;  
2. technical team consisting of:  
a) apparatus who have attended training or education in gender-perspective budget analysis, at most 5 (five) persons.  
b) representatives from higher education institutions, at most 5 (five) persons;  
c) press representatives, at most 5 (five) persons; and  
d) representatives of non-governmental organizations whose orientation scope is in the field of gender, at most 5 (five) persons.  

Article 7  
The duties of the Pokja PUG include:  
a. promoting and facilitating dialogue among work units within the scope of the regional government;  
b. developing work networks in accordance with the main duties, functions, and authority given by leaders in an effort to realize gender equality and gender justice;  
c. assigning the technical team to prepare the RAD PUG in order to realize gender equality and gender justice adjusted to the RPJMD periodization;  
d. elaborating and following up on implementation policies and programs to be gender responsive, implicitly and explicitly contained in the RPJMD;  
e. coordinating the establishment of Focal Point PUG in each PD or work unit;  
f. promoting PUG in each PD;  
g. strengthening capacity for Focal Point PUG in preparing gender-responsive budgets by the technical team;  
h. overseeing the preparation of gender-responsive planning and budgeting in each PD;  
i. including gender indicators in the guidelines for preparing the APBD;  
j. ensuring gender-responsive budgeting becomes a consideration in the discussion and stipulation of the APBD together with the DPRD;  
k. holding coordination meetings every 6 (six) months to ensure the implementation of PUG in the Region;  
l. preparing and submitting reports on the implementation of PUG to the Regent every 6 (six) months; and  
m. holding a coordination meeting prior to the preparation of the RKPD.  

Paragraph 4  
Focal Point PUG  
Article 8  
(1) The Regional Secretary has the authority to establish a Focal Point PUG within the Regional Secretariat.  
(2) The establishment of the Focal Point PUG as referred to in paragraph (1) shall be stipulated by the Regional Secretary’s Decree.  
(3) The management of the Focal Point PUG as referred to in paragraph (1) consists of:  
a. person in charge by the regional secretary;  
b. coordinator by an echelon IIB official in charge of governmental affairs in the legal field;  
c. deputy coordinator by echelon IIIA officials of each division in the regional secretariat except the echelon IIB official as referred to in letter b; and  
d. members by representatives of officials and/or staff from all divisions in the regional secretariat.  

Article 9  
(1) The Head of PD must establish a PD Focal Point PUG.  
(2) The establishment of the Focal Point PUG as referred to in paragraph (1) shall be by Decree of the Head of PD.  
(3) The management of the Focal Point PUG as referred to in paragraph (1) consists of:  
a. person in charge by the head of PD;  
b. coordinator by an echelon IIIA official in charge of planning affairs in the PD;  
c. deputy coordinator by an echelon IIIB official who has competence in PUG; and  
d. members by representatives of officials or staff from the divisions and secretariat in the PD.  

Article 10  
(1) The Hospital Director has the authority to establish a Hospital Focal Point PUG.  
(2) The establishment of the Focal Point PUG as referred to in paragraph (1) shall be stipulated by the Hospital Director’s Decree.  
(3) The management of the Focal Point PUG as referred to in paragraph (1) consists of:  
a. person in charge by the hospital director;  
b. coordinator by an echelon IIIB official in charge of hospital planning;  
c. deputy coordinator by an echelon IVA official who has competence in PUG; and  
d. members by representatives of officials or staff in the fields or sections within the hospital.  

Article 11  
(1) The Head of the Community Health Center has the authority to establish a Puskesmas Focal Point PUG.  
(2) The establishment of the Focal Point PUG as referred to in paragraph (1) shall be stipulated by Decree of the Head of the Community Health Center.  
(3) The management of the Puskesmas Focal Point PUG consists of:  
a. person in charge by the Head of the Puskesmas;  
b. coordinator by an echelon IVB official in charge of Puskesmas planning; and  
c. members consisting of Puskesmas staff.  

Article 12  
The duties of the Focal Point PUG include:  
a. assisting work unit policy makers within the scope of the duties, main tasks, and functions of their institutions to take fully planned steps if there is a gender gap;  
b. encouraging and assisting work units to evaluate and improve mandates, policies, programs, projects, activities, and budgets to be more gender responsive;  
c. facilitating the implementation of gender sensitivity training, gender analysis training, and developing gender work networks with other agencies, institutions, organizations, and work units, both governmental and non-governmental;  
d. striving for the implementation of gender analysis as one stage in every development process starting from planning, implementation, monitoring, and evaluation stages;  
e. elaborating and following up on development implementation policies and programs to be gender responsive as implied in the PD Renstra, PD Renja, and RKPD;  
f. participating in various activities organized by the Pokja PUG and/or the national PUG working group; and  
g. preparing periodic activity reports and reporting them to the Regent through the chairperson of the Pokja PUG.  

Paragraph 5  
Gender-Based Regional Development Planning  
Article 13  
(1) The Regional Government is responsible for preparing planning for development policies, programs, and activities with a gender perspective.  
(2) The responsibility as referred to in paragraph (1) is coordinated by the PD that administers governmental affairs in the field of development planning, regional research, and development.  
(3) The planning as referred to in paragraph (1) includes the stages of:  
a. Musrenbangdus;  
b. Musrembangdes/kel;  
c. Musrembangcam;  
d. regional-level Musrenbang;  
e. preparation of the RPJMD;  
f. preparation of PD Renstra;  
g. preparation of PD Renja; and  
h. RKPD;  
i. preparation of the RAPBD;  
(4) The planning as referred to in paragraph (1) is carried out through Gender Analysis using the Gender Analysis Pathway method or other analysis methods.  
(5) The implementation of Gender Analysis for the RPJMD and PD Renstra may cooperate with higher education institutions or other parties that have capability in their fields.  

Article 14  
(1) The Agency is responsible for preparing guidelines for gender-based development planning.  
(2) The guidelines as referred to in paragraph (1) must be used in facilitating:  
a. Musrenbangdus;  
b. Musrembangdes/kel;  
c. Musrembangcam;  
d. regional-level Musrenbang;  
e. preparation of the RPJMD;  
f. preparation of PD Renstra; and  
g. preparation of PD Renja,  
that are gender responsive.  
(3) The preparation of the guidelines as referred to in paragraph (1) may cooperate with the Pokja PUG Technical Team.  

Article 15  
(1) The Gender Analysis Pathway method as referred to in Article 13 paragraph (4) is used as a way to make planning in each PD.  
(2) The method as referred to in paragraph (1) includes:  
a. Stage I: analysis of gender-responsive policy;  
b. Stage II: formulation of gender-responsive policy;  
c. Stage III: gender-responsive action plan;  
d. Stage IV: implementation of the activities that have been prepared; and  
e. Stage V: monitoring and evaluation of each stage and step taken.  

Paragraph 6  
Budgeting  
Article 16  
(1) The Regional Government must allocate a budget for the implementation of PUG in the Region.  
(2) The implementation budget as referred to in paragraph (1) is sourced from:  
a. APBD; and  
b. assistance from other non-binding parties, as long as it is in accordance with the provisions of applicable laws and regulations.  
(3) The budget as referred to in paragraphs (1) and (2) is used to finance the activities of the Pokja PUG and PD Focal Point PUG in integrating Gender at the level of planning, preparation, implementation, monitoring, and evaluation of policies and development in the Region.  

Paragraph 7  
Monitoring and Evaluation  
Article 17  
(1) The Regent has the authority to conduct monitoring and evaluation of the implementation of PUG.  
(2) The Regent may delegate the implementation of monitoring and evaluation to the Pokja PUG.  
(3) Monitoring and evaluation are carried out in each PD and/or other institutions that implement PUG efforts.  
(4) Monitoring and evaluation are carried out at least every 6 (six) months.  

Article 18  
Monitoring of the implementation of PUG is carried out through:  
a. monitoring the preparation of gender-responsive development planning documents both at the PD level and at the regional level;  
b. monitoring the RAD PUG; and  
c. verification of the RAD PUG implementation report.  

Article 19  
Evaluation of the implementation of PUG is carried out through:  
a. evaluation of achievements and challenges in implementing the RAD PUG;  
b. preparation of the next period RAD PUG;  
c. evaluation of achievements and challenges in implementing PUG every 6 (six) months; and  
d. preparation of the PUG implementation plan for the next 6 (six) months.  

Paragraph 8  
PUG Reporting  
Article 20  
(1) The Pokja PUG is responsible for preparing a PUG Implementation Report every 6 (six) months and a five-year RAD PUG Implementation Report.  
(2) The reporting framework as referred to in paragraph (1) is guided by the reporting framework as referred to in laws and regulations concerning the General Guidelines for the Implementation of PUG in the Region.  
(3) The Pokja PUG report as referred to in paragraph (1) is reported to the Regent.  
(4) The Regent is responsible for examining and declaring approval of the Pokja PUG report as referred to in paragraph (1).  
(5) The Regent is responsible for sending the Pokja PUG report as referred to in paragraph (1) to the Minister administering domestic governmental affairs.  

Paragraph 9  
Supervision  
Article 21  
(1) The Regent is responsible for supervising the implementation of PUG.  
(2) The supervision as referred to in paragraph (1) is in the form of:  
a. gender-responsive planning and budgeting carried out by the regional government budget team;  
b. coordination implementation between the Pokja PUG and gender focal points; and  
c. progress in achieving the RAD PUG each year.  
(3) The responsibility as referred to in paragraph (1) technically operationally may be carried out by the Chairperson of the Pokja PUG and the Head of the Pokja PUG Secretariat.  
(4) The implementation of the responsibility as referred to in paragraph (3) is reported to the Regent.  

Part Three  
Women’s Empowerment in the Political, Legal, Socio-Cultural and Economic Fields in Community Organizations  
Paragraph 1  
General  
Article 22  
Efforts for Women’s Empowerment in the political, legal, socio-cultural and economic fields in community organizations include the stages of:  
a. planning;  
b. implementation; and  
c. reporting and evaluation.  

Paragraph 2  
Planning  
Article 23  
(1) The Agency is responsible for facilitating the planning of Women’s Empowerment in the political, legal, socio-cultural and economic fields in community organizations.  
(2) The planning as referred to in paragraph (1) is carried out in an integrated manner in development planning deliberations based on the gender analysis pathway instrument.  

Paragraph 3  
Implementation  
Article 24  
The implementation of Women’s Empowerment in community organizations is carried out by PD in accordance with the main duties and functions borne, in accordance with regional financial capacity.  

Article 25  
(1) The PD that administers governmental affairs in the field of national unity and politics is responsible for facilitating women’s empowerment in the political field.  
(2) The empowerment as referred to in paragraph (1) includes:  
a. conducting education and training for women in order to improve competence, especially in the political field; and  
b. encouraging women’s representation in every management structure of community organizations.  

Article 26  
(1) The PD that administers governmental affairs in the legal field is responsible for facilitating Women’s Empowerment in the legal field.  
(2) The implementation of Women’s Empowerment in the legal field includes:  
a. increasing awareness and knowledge in the legal field through communication, information, and education services; and  
b. facilitating access to and services for legal consultation.  

Article 27  
(1) The PD that administers governmental affairs in the social field is responsible for facilitating Women’s Empowerment in the socio-cultural field.  
(2) Women’s Empowerment in the socio-cultural field through:  
a. increasing knowledge, attitudes, and skills to encourage fulfillment of tiered education according to potential to improve social status;  
b. increasing awareness, willingness, and ability to overcome health problems through quality promotive, preventive, curative, and rehabilitative efforts, especially in the field of reproductive health;  
c. increasing awareness and knowledge about independent, healthy, and prosperous family planning including access to consultation services and marriage registration; and  
d. facilitation and efforts to preserve customs and develop science, technology, arts, and culture for the advancement of women.  

Article 28  
(1) The PD that administers governmental affairs in the field of cooperatives, small and medium enterprises, and industry and trade is responsible for facilitating Women’s Empowerment in the economic field.  
(2) The implementation of Women’s Empowerment in the economic field is carried out through:  
a. provision of skills and job training;  
b. facilitation of the formation of productive economic business groups;  
c. facilitation of strengthening and development of productive economic business groups;  
d. facilitation and capital assistance; and  
e. facilitation of marketing network development.  

Paragraph 4  
Reporting and Evaluation  
Article 29  
(1) Each PD is responsible for making a report on the implementation of Women’s Empowerment activities that have been carried out to the Regent through the Office (Dinas).  
(2) The Office (Dinas), after receiving the report submitted by the PD, conducts an evaluation of the implementation of the activities.  
(3) The results of the evaluation of the implementation of the activities as referred to in paragraph (2) are reported to the Regent.  
(4) The evaluation results as referred to in paragraph (3) are used for budgeting planning for the following year.  

CHAPTER IV  
WOMEN’S PROTECTION  
Part One  
General  
Article 30  
Women’s Protection includes:  
a. strengthening and development of institutions providing women’s protection services;  
b. prevention of violence against women; and  
c. strengthening and development of institutions providing protection services for women victims of violence;  

Part Two  
Strengthening and Development of Institutions Providing Women’s Protection Services  
Paragraph 1  
Regional Technical Implementation Unit  
for Women and Child Protection  
Article 31  
(1) The PD that administers governmental affairs in the field of institutions and organization is responsible for facilitating the establishment of a regional technical implementation unit for women and child protection.  
(2) The establishment of the UPTD P2TP2A as referred to in paragraph (1) is regulated by a Regent Regulation.  
(3) The establishment of the UPTD P2TP2A as referred to in paragraph (2) shall be no later than 2 (two) years after this Regional Regulation is stipulated.  

Paragraph 2  
Regional Action Plan  
Article 32  
(1) The preparation of the RAD as referred to in Article 7 letter c is carried out through the mechanism of:  
a. determination of the drafting team;  
b. holding meetings to form working groups based on indicators;  
c. each Pokja identifies objectives, targets, and indicators;  
d. determining priority programs and activities related to achieving each target; and  
e. determining the budget allocation for each program/activity.  
(2) The RAD at least contains:  
a. introduction;  
b. policy direction and achievement strategy;  
c. monitoring and evaluation;  
d. closing; and  
e. attachments containing the RAD implementation matrix.  
(3) The preparation of the RAD is carried out by taking into account:  
a. RPJMN;  
b. RPJMD; and  
c. other related documents.  
(4) Provisions regarding the implementation of the RAD are further regulated by a Regent Regulation.  

Paragraph 3  
Monitoring and Evaluation  
Article 33  
(1) The Regent conducts monitoring of the implementation of policies, programs, and activities for women’s protection.  
(2) The monitoring as referred to in paragraph (1) is intended to determine developments and obstacles in the implementation of policies, programs, and activities for women’s protection in the Region.  
(3) The monitoring as referred to in paragraph (2) is carried out periodically on PD through the Office (Dinas).  
(4) Monitoring is carried out from planning through implementation of policies, programs, and activities for women’s protection for the current year.  

Article 34  
(1) Evaluation of the implementation of policies, programs, and activities for Women’s Protection is carried out at the end of each fiscal year.  
(2) The results of the evaluation of the implementation of policies, programs, and activities for women’s protection are used as input material for the preparation of policies, programs, and activities for Women’s Protection for the following year.  
(3) The evaluation as referred to in paragraph (1) is carried out in accordance with the provisions of laws and regulations.  

Part Three  
Prevention of Violence Against Women  
Article 35  
(1) The Office (Dinas) is responsible for carrying out efforts to prevent violence against women.  
(2) The prevention as referred to in paragraph (1) is carried out in the form of:  
a. conducting counseling and outreach regarding matters related to violence against women;  
b. conducting outreach on women’s rights;  
c. seeking to improve education for women;  
d. providing counseling to immediately report to local authorities if violence against women occurs;  
e. empowering social community organizations and women’s organizations to increase women’s knowledge and insight; and  
f. forming work networks in the context of efforts to prevent violence against women.  

Part Four  
Strengthening and Development of Institutions Providing Protection Services for Women Victims of Violence  
Article 36  
(1) The Office (Dinas) may provide assistance to Service Institutions in the region in the form of:  
a. strengthening Service Institutions; and  
b. development of Service Institutions.  
(2) Strengthening of service institutions as referred to in paragraph (1) letter a is carried out through:  
a. increasing the competence of members of service institutions; and/or  
b. facilitating periodic paralegal training.  
(3) Development of Service Institutions as referred to in paragraph (1) letter b is carried out through:  
a. providing additional facilities and infrastructure;  
b. providing an information-technology-based service approach; and/or  
c. recruiting village cadres as assistant paralegals.  

CHAPTER V  
FAMILY QUALITY  
Part One  
General  
Article 37  
Family Quality includes:  
a. improving family quality in realizing gender equality; and  
b. strengthening and development of institutions providing services to improve family quality in realizing gender equality.  

Part Two  
Improving Family Quality in Realizing Gender Equality  
Article 38  
(1) The Office (Dinas) is responsible for facilitating activities to improve family quality in the Region.  
(2) Improving family quality as referred to in paragraph (1) is carried out through:  
a. facilitating the establishment of service provider institutions;  
b. in a coordinated manner with PD and/or related institutions that have the main duties and functions to carry out activities:  
1. improving entrepreneurial ability for women; or  
2. developing job training for women.  

Part Three  
Strengthening and Development of Institutions Providing Services to Improve Family Quality in Realizing Gender Equality  
Article 39  
(1) Facilitation of the establishment of service provider institutions as referred to in Article 36 paragraph (2) letter a is carried out by establishing a family learning center.  
(2) The stages of establishing a family learning center include:  
a. preparing regulations;  
b. preparing human resources;  
c. preparing facilities and infrastructure; and  
d. establishing the family learning center.  
(3) The family learning center functions:  
a. as a service institution in prevention and counseling;  
b. as a referral liaison place as a solution for child and family problems;  
c. strengthening family capacity in nurturing and protecting children;  
d. as a place for family learning through education for parents; and  
e. as a one-stop holistic integrative family service based on children’s rights.  

CHAPTER VI  
GENDER DATA SYSTEM  
Part One  
General  
Article 40  
The Gender Data System includes:  
a. data collection;  
b. data processing; and  
c. analysis and presentation of gender data.  

Part Two  
Data Collection  
Article 41  
(1) PD that carries out the duties of the PUG focal point is responsible for reporting Gender data and PUG activities carried out to the Regent through the Office (Dinas).  
(2) Reporting of Gender data and PUG activities as referred to in paragraph (1) is carried out every 6 (six) months.  

Part Three  
Data Processing  
Article 42  
(1) The Office (Dinas), after receiving reports of Gender data and PUG activities from each Gender focal point in the region, carries out data processing.  
(2) Data processing as referred to in paragraph (1) is carried out to sort Gender data and PUG activities with good grouping and easy to analyze.  

Part Four  
Analysis and Presentation of Gender Data  
Article 43  
(1) The Office (Dinas) conducts analysis of the processed data.  
(2) The analysis as referred to in paragraph (1) is carried out comprehensively based on adequate mapping so that it is easy to understand.  
(3) Adequate analysis as referred to in paragraph (2) is presented as regional Gender data manually and/or electronically.  

CHAPTER VII  
SANCTIONS PROVISIONS  
Article 44

EXPLANATION OF  
REGIONAL REGULATION OF NORTH CENTRAL TIMOR REGENCY NUMBER 3 OF 2020  
CONCERNING  
THE IMPLEMENTATION OF WOMEN’S EMPOWERMENT  

I. GENERAL  
The enactment of Law Number 23 of 2014 concerning Regional Government as amended several times, most recently by Law Number 9 of 2015 concerning the Second Amendment to Law Number 23 of 2014 concerning Regional Government, and Law Number 33 of 2004 concerning Fiscal Balance between the Central Government and Regional Governments constitutes an entry point for Women’s Empowerment in regional development. Women’s empowerment is an appropriate development strategy to realize gender equality and gender justice through gender-sensitive and gender-responsive development policies and programs in the future, particularly in North Central Timor Regency.  
In North Central Timor Regency there are several conditions that need to be intervened through the establishment of a Regional Regulation on Women’s Empowerment. This occurs because there is much discrimination against women in developing themselves and opportunities to fulfill their rights. Women are only considered as second-class. Their task is to take care of the household and they do not need to work. This must be opposed; this stigma becomes a problem that must be resolved with empowerment programs.  
The above paradigm is reflected in data on the presence of women working in Government; the data show that women are not in strategic positions to participate in decision-making or policies, especially in governmental affairs, through civil servant (ASN) data by sex available in the field.  
Based on this thinking, it is time for North Central Timor Regency to establish a Regional Regulation on Women’s Empowerment in North Central Timor Regency. This Regional Regulation will regulate the implementation of gender mainstreaming clearly, firmly, balanced, and comprehensively to realize gender equality and gender justice, while also providing understanding of the importance of women’s empowerment as a gender analysis strategy in the planning, preparation, implementation, monitoring, and evaluation of development policies and programs.
