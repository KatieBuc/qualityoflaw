NUMBER 2 OF 2022

THE REGENT OF BIREUEN,  
Considering: a. that women, as noble and dignified beings, need to have their capacities empowered and are entitled to protection in accordance with Islamic Shari’ah;  
b. that based on Article 231 of Law Number 11 of 2006 concerning the Government of Aceh, it is stated that the Regency Government is obliged to advance and protect the rights of women and children and to undertake dignified empowerment efforts;  
c. that based on the considerations as referred to in letter a and letter b, it is necessary to establish a Qanun concerning Women’s Protection;  
Bearing in mind: 1. Article 18 paragraph (6) of the 1945 Constitution of the Republic of Indonesia;  
2. Law Number 39 of 1999 concerning Human Rights (State Gazette of the Republic of Indonesia Year 1999 Number 165, Supplement to the State Gazette of the Republic of Indonesia Number 3886);

4. Law Number 23 of 2004 concerning the Elimination of Domestic Violence (State Gazette of the Republic of Indonesia Year 2004 Number 95, Supplement to the State Gazette of the Republic of Indonesia Number 4419);

To stipulate: QANUN CONCERNING WOMEN’S PROTECTION.

CHAPTER I  
GENERAL PROVISIONS  
Article 1  
In this Qanun, what is meant by:  
1. Region means Bireuen Regency.  
2. Regency means Bireuen Regency which is part of the Province of Aceh led by a Regent.  
3. Regency Administration means the administration of Bireuen Regency as the conduct of affairs carried out by the regency government and the Regional House of Representatives of Bireuen Regency in accordance with their respective functions and authorities.  
4. Regency Government means the elements administering the Regency Government consisting of the Regent and regency apparatus.  
5. Regent means the Head of the Government of Bireuen Regency elected through a democratic process conducted based on the principles of direct, general, free, confidential, honest, and fair elections in accordance with laws and regulations.  
6. The Regional House of Representatives of the Regency, hereinafter abbreviated as DPRK, means an element of administering the government of Bireuen Regency whose members are elected through general elections in accordance with laws and regulations.  
7. Woman means a person (human) who has reached the age of 15 years who menstruates, becomes pregnant, gives birth, and breastfeeds.  
8. Women’s Protection means all efforts aimed at protecting women and providing a sense of security in the fulfillment of their rights by giving consistent and systematic attention aimed at achieving fair and equal relations with men.  
9. Victim means a woman who experiences threats of violence and/or violence within and/or outside the household and/or becomes an object of trafficking/exploitation, and/or discrimination in all forms and aspects.  
10. Threat of Violence means any act of non-physical abuse using or not using means unlawfully that can endanger the body, life, liberty, and human rights of women.  
11. Violence means any act against a person, especially a woman, resulting in misery or suffering physically, psychologically, sexually and/or household neglect, including threats to commit acts, coercion, or unlawful deprivation of liberty, whether within or outside the household sphere.  
12. Discrimination Against Women means any distinction, exclusion, or restriction made on the basis of sex that has the effect or purpose of reducing or eliminating the recognition, enjoyment, or exercise of human rights and fundamental freedoms in the political, economic, socio-cultural, civil, or any other field by women, irrespective of their marital status, on the basis of equality between men and women.

13. Women’s Rights means all rights possessed by every woman as part of human rights.  
14. Special Protection means protection provided to women and children in emergency situations, women and children in conflict with the law, women and children from minority and isolated groups, women and children economically and/or sexually exploited, women and children who are trafficked, women and children who are victims of kidnapping, sale, trafficking, women and children victims of violence both physical and/or mental.  
15. Community means individuals, families, groups and social organizations and/or community organizations.  
16. Family means the smallest unit in society consisting of husband and wife, or husband-wife and their child(ren), or father and his child(ren), or mother and her child(ren), or blood relatives in a direct line upward or downward up to the third degree.  
17. Labor means any person capable of performing work to produce goods and/or services either to meet their own needs or for society.  
18. Worker/Laborer means any person who works by receiving wages or remuneration in another form.  
19. Person with Disabilities means any person who experiences long-term physical, intellectual, mental, and/or sensory limitations which, in interacting with the environment, may encounter obstacles and difficulties to participate fully and effectively with other citizens on the basis of equal rights.  
20. Service means actions carried out as soon as possible to a victim when seeing, hearing, and knowing that violence against the victim will occur, is occurring, or has occurred.  
21. Rehabilitation means a series of actions carried out by the competent authorities in their field to restore the physical, mental, and psychosocial condition of women to their previous condition.  

CHAPTER II  
PRINCIPLES AND OBJECTIVES  
Article 2  
Women’s protection is implemented based on the principles of:  
a. respect for the values of Islamic Shari’ah and Acehnese customs that are responsive to women;  
b. legal certainty;  
c. public interest;  
d. respect for human rights;  
e. justice and gender equality;  
f. non-discrimination;  
g. professionalism, accountability, and transparency; and  
h. protection and fulfillment of victims’ rights.

Article 3  
Women’s protection aims to:  
a. strengthen regional policy and budget support in order to optimize the implementation of women’s protection;  
b. affirm the commitment of policy makers at the regency, subdistrict, and gampong levels in the framework of increasing efforts and guarantees of protection for women;  
c. improve coordination, integration, synchronization, and synergy among SKPK and all stakeholders, including religious organizations, professional organizations, academics, the private sector, and the wider community, in efforts to improve the quality of women’s protection;  
d. build public knowledge and awareness to support efforts to protect women;  
e. increase the institutional and human resource capacity of the UPTD PPA;  
f. provide guarantees for women to be able to fulfill their rights as human beings in all aspects of life;  
g. provide a sense of security for women in all aspects of life; and  
h. reduce the rate of violence against women.  

CHAPTER III  
SCOPE  
Article 4  
The scope of regulation in this Qanun includes:  
a. principles and objectives;  
b. women’s rights;  
c. women’s protection;  
d. obligations and responsibilities of the Regency Government;  
e. support of the gampong government;  
f. strategies and mechanisms for implementing women’s protection;  
g. community participation; and  
h. awards.  

CHAPTER IV  
WOMEN’S RIGHTS  
Part One  
General  
Article 5  
(1) Every woman has the right to:  
a. live and sustain her life, and improve her level of welfare in her life;  
b. practice religious teachings in accordance with Islamic Shari’ah;  
c. obtain justice, a sense of security, and freedom to express opinions without discrimination;  
d. obtain protection of her personal self, family, honor, dignity, and property rights;  
e. fulfill her basic needs to grow and develop properly, to have a family and continue offspring;  

f. receive fair and equal treatment in carrying out her roles and functions both in family and community life and to participate fully in every process of making, determining, and implementing development policies;  
g. obtain education and instruction at all types, levels, and pathways of education, without any obstacles and pressure whatsoever in order to improve her skills and quality of life;  
h. obtain information and knowledge for women about health, especially reproductive health, in full; and  
i. obtain special protection in the performance of her work or profession against matters that threaten her safety and/or health in relation to reproductive functions.  
(2) Every woman in her capacity as a wife, while in the bond of marriage, has the same rights and responsibilities as her husband in all matters relating to her marital life, her relationship with her children, and the ownership and management of joint property in accordance with applicable laws and regulations.  
(3) In addition to the rights as referred to in paragraph (2), a woman as a wife has the right to receive material and spiritual maintenance from her husband.  
(4) After the dissolution of marriage, a woman has rights and responsibilities from her former husband in all matters relating to the child, taking into account the best interests of the child.  

Part Two  
Rights of Women with Disabilities  
Article 6  
Every woman with disabilities has the right to obtain:  
a. education in all units, pathways, types, and levels of education;  
b. decent work and livelihood in accordance with the type and degree of disability, education, and ability;  
c. equal treatment to play a role in development and enjoy its results;  
d. accessibility in order to achieve independence;  
e. rehabilitation, social assistance, and maintenance of the level of social welfare;  
f. equal rights to develop talents, abilities, and social life; and  
g. other rights as regulated in laws and regulations.  

Part Three  
Rights of Women Victims of Violence  
Article 7  
Every woman victim of violence has the right to obtain:  
a. respect for her dignity and worth as a human being;  
b. recovery of health and psychological condition from the suffering experienced by the victim;  
c. determine her own decisions in handling the violence case she experienced;  
d. obtain information;  
e. obtain guarantees of confidentiality;  
f. social rehabilitation;  
g. complaint handling;  
h. obtain facilitation in the judicial process;  
i. assistance; and  
j. obtain empowerment after her case has been handled.  

Part Four  
Special Treatment  
Article 8  
(1) Every woman who is in a condition of disability, elderly, and/or pregnant, has the right to obtain facilities and special treatment related to access to public services.  
(2) Every elderly woman, person with physical disability and/or mental disability, has the right to obtain care, education, training, and special assistance at the expense of the Regency Government to guarantee a decent life in accordance with human dignity.  
(3) Every woman entangled with narcotics either as a perpetrator or victim has the right to receive fair treatment both in the law enforcement process and the rehabilitation (recovery) process.  

CHAPTER V  
WOMEN’S PROTECTION  
Part One  
Implementation of Women’s Protection  
Article 9  
The implementation of women’s protection is provided to:  
a. women as housewives;  
b. women heads of households;  
c. women domestic workers;  
d. women workers/laborers;  
e. women with disabilities;  
f. homeless women;  
g. women former correctional inmates;  
h. women victims of violence;  
i. women victims of conflict; and  
j. elderly women.  

Paragraph 1  
Women as Housewives  
Article 10  
The implementation of protection for women as housewives as referred to in Article 9 letter a, is in the following matters:  
a. material and spiritual maintenance;  
b. the head of the family provides special protection to the housewife during pregnancy; and  
c. a housewife who has given birth must receive protection from the head of the family to reduce the burden as a housewife.  

Article 11  
A housewife who works outside the home must receive protection from the head of the family in the form of granting permission and being able to provide a domestic worker.  

Paragraph 2  
Women Heads of Households  
Article 12  
(1) Women heads of households as referred to in Article 9 letter b, are widows left by the death of their husbands and as a result of divorce, and must receive protection.  
(2) The implementation of protection for women heads of households as referred to in paragraph (1) is in order to provide protection and prevention against forms of discrimination, negative stigma, and economic empowerment for women heads of households.  
(3) Women as heads of households as referred to in paragraph (2) obtain the right to protection against:  
a. discriminatory actions and efforts to weaken the function of women as the economic support of the family;  
b. strengthening of capability and recognition as head of household in her environment; and  
c. accessibility to economic and financial institutions.  
(4) The implementation of protection for women as heads of households whose husbands have died as referred to in paragraph (1) includes:  
a. women as heads of households obtain 1/8 (one eighth) of the total remaining assets of their husbands if they have children; and  
b. women as heads of households obtain 1/4 (one quarter) of the total remaining assets of their husbands if they do not have children.  
(5) The implementation of protection for women as heads of households due to divorce as referred to in paragraph (1) during the iddah period, the former husband is responsible for providing housing and maintenance.  

Paragraph 3  
Women Domestic Workers  
Article 13  
(1) The implementation of protection for women domestic workers as referred to in Article 9 letter c, is in order to provide protection and prevention against forms of discrimination, cruel treatment, inhuman treatment, degrading human dignity, and exploitation in employment relations.  
(2) Women domestic workers as referred to in paragraph (1) must receive protection from the employer in the form of:  
a. recognition of rights, wages, and decent working conditions;  
b. health and social security; and  
c. guarantees of not committing discrimination, inhuman treatment, degrading human dignity, and exploitation.  

Paragraph 4  
Women Workers/Laborers  
Article 14  
(1) The implementation of protection for women workers/laborers as referred to in Article 9 letter d, is to provide protection from acts of economic exploitation and cruel, inhuman treatment and to seek proper appreciation for work.  
(2) Women workers/laborers as referred to in paragraph (1) must receive protection from employers in the form of:  
a. recognition of rights, wages, and decent working conditions;  
b. health and social security; and  
c. guarantees of not committing discrimination, inhuman treatment, degrading human dignity, and exploitation.  

Paragraph 5  
Women with Disabilities  
Article 15  
(1) The implementation of protection for women with disabilities as referred to in Article 9 letter e, is in order to provide protection and prevention against forms of discrimination, including being free from cruel treatment, inhuman treatment, degrading human dignity, and free from exploitation.  
(2) Women with disabilities as referred to in paragraph (1) obtain the right to protection for:  
a. guarantees of continuity and personal self-development;  
b. accessibility of information, legal aid, mobility, social services; and  
c. early handling efforts including treatment and rehabilitation to improve their function and capacity.  

Paragraph 6  
Homeless Women  
Article 16  
(1) The implementation of protection for homeless women as referred to in Article 9 letter f, is in order to provide protection and prevention against forms of violence caused by the absence of shelter or a permanent place of residence.  
(2) Homeless women as referred to in paragraph (1) obtain the right to protection for:  
a. decent housing;  
b. health and social security;  
c. population administration registration;  
d. opportunities to obtain education and skills to improve economic status; and  
e. access to information and legal consultation services.  

Paragraph 7  
Women Former Correctional Inmates  
Article 17  
(1) The implementation of protection for women former correctional inmates as referred to in Article 9 letter g, is in order to provide protection from obstacles to adjusting in community life, so that they can live normally.  
(2) Women former correctional inmates as referred to in paragraph (1) obtain protection for:  
a. rehabilitation and social reintegration; and  
b. opportunities to obtain knowledge and skills to improve economic status.  

Paragraph 8  
Women Victims of Violence  
Article 18  
(1) The implementation of protection for women victims of violence as referred to in Article 9 letter h, is in order to provide legal protection and/or health recovery.  
(2) Protection for women victims of violence as referred to in paragraph (1) includes:  
a. legal aid;  
b. post-violence assistance;  
c. rehabilitation and social reintegration; and  
d. health services.  

Article 19  
(1) Every victim as referred to in Article 18 has the right to obtain protection, information, integrated services, continuous handling up to the rehabilitation stage, and confidential handling from individuals, groups, or institutions, both the Regency Government and the community.  
(2) In the event of violence, every victim has the right to obtain health services and assistance, both psychological and legal, and to obtain guarantees of her rights related to her status as a woman.  

Paragraph 9  
Women Victims of Conflict  
Article 20  
The implementation of protection for women victims of conflict as referred to in Article 9 letter i, is through:  
a. providing a sense of security;  
b. fulfilling the basic needs of women victims of conflict;  
c. eliminating trauma; and  
d. providing the services needed by victims.  

Paragraph 10  
Elderly Women  
Article 21  
(1) The implementation of protection for elderly women as referred to in Article 9 letter j, is in order to carry out their social function and play an active role reasonably in their social life.  
(2) Protection for elderly women as referred to in paragraph (1) prioritizes protection from their children and heirs.  
(3) If elderly women do not receive services from their children and heirs and/or do not have children and other heirs as referred to in paragraph (2), then the protection of elderly women becomes the responsibility of the Regency Government.  
(4) Elderly women as referred to in paragraph (1) obtain the right to protection for:  
a. basic health services to maintain and improve health status including gerontological/geriatric services;  
b. use of facilities and accessibility of public infrastructure and facilities; and  
c. government administrative services.  

CHAPTER VII  
OBLIGATIONS AND RESPONSIBILITIES  
OF THE REGENCY GOVERNMENT  
Part One  
General  
Article 22  
(1) The Regency Government is obliged and responsible for women’s protection.  
(2) Women’s protection efforts as referred to in paragraph (1) include:  
a. stipulation of policies, programs, and activities;  
b. stipulation of implementation guidelines;  
c. provision of services; and  
d. coordination of policies, programs, and activities.  
(3) Women’s protection as referred to in paragraph (2) is carried out in an integrated manner in accordance with laws and regulations.  

Article 23  
(1) In implementing policies, programs, and activities for women’s protection and empowerment for women victims, the Regency Government may cooperate with the community/community institutions.  
(2) Cooperation as referred to in paragraph (1) is carried out in accordance with the provisions of laws and regulations.  

Article 24  
In implementing policies, programs, and activities for women’s protection, the Regent undertakes efforts to:  
a. coordinate the implementation of policies, programs, and activities for women’s protection among regency apparatus;  
b. cooperate with regencies/cities within one province, and inter-regency/city cooperation in other provinces in implementing policies, programs, and activities for women’s protection in accordance with the provisions of laws and regulations;  
d. strengthen institutional capacity for implementing policies, programs, and activities for women’s protection;  
e. facilitate women’s protection services;  
f. provide women’s protection services;  
g. implement affirmative action for women’s protection; and  
h. develop a data collection system for women’s protection.  

Article 25  
(1) The Regency Government is obliged and responsible to implement efforts to prevent violence against women in the form of:  
a. collecting data and information about victims of violence as well as laws and regulations;  
b. conducting education on anti-violence values against women;  
c. conducting dissemination of laws and regulations related to the implementation of protection for victims of violence;  
d. fostering support from religious leaders and customary leaders to build public awareness of the importance of protecting women;  
e. conducting monitoring and evaluation of the implementation of protection for victims of violence; and  
f. preparing reports on the implementation of protection for women victims of violence.  
(2) To anticipate acts of violence against women, the Regency Government is obliged to provide and organize services for victims in the form of:  
a. establishing institutions and facilitating the implementation of integrated services for victims by involving elements of the community; and  
b. encouraging public concern about the importance of protecting victims.  
(3) To prevent acts of violence against women, the Regency Government undertakes the following efforts:  
a. disseminating regulations and policies related to violence against women to gampong apparatus, customary leaders, and religious leaders;  
b. providing counseling to immediately report to local authorities if violence against women occurs;  
c. establishing a work network in efforts to prevent violence; and  
d. other efforts deemed strategic to prevent violence against women.  

Article 26  
(1) The regency government is obliged to provide social assistance for women experiencing social problems and disaster victims.  
(2) The regency government must provide the right to social security for poor women and women with limited ability.  

Part Two  
Women’s Protection Institution  
Article 27  
(1) The Regency Government establishes a women’s protection institution.  
(2) The women’s protection institution as referred to in paragraph (1) also handles protection for children.  
(3) The women’s protection institution as referred to in paragraph (2) is implemented by establishing the UPTD PPA.  
(4) The Regency Government must guarantee the availability of facilities and infrastructure, human resources, and an adequate budget to realize the operation of the UPTD PPA.  
(5) Further provisions regarding the UPTD PPA are regulated by a Regent Regulation.  

CHAPTER VIII  
SUPPORT OF THE GAMPONG GOVERNMENT  
Article 28  
(1) The Gampong Government supports efforts to protect women by:  
a. issuing gampong-level regulations on women’s protection;  
b. disseminating regulations related to women’s empowerment and protection to the community;  
c. encouraging the realization of a gampong environment that is friendly to women;  
d. building community awareness, including customary leaders and religious leaders at the gampong level, to care about the issue of violence against women;  
e. allocating a budget to support women’s empowerment and protection programs; and  
f. building a system for handling violence against women at the gampong level.  
(2) Efforts as referred to in paragraph (1) letter e above can be developed in accordance with the gampong’s financial capacity, problems, and women’s needs.  

CHAPTER IX  
STRATEGIES AND MECHANISMS FOR WOMEN’S PROTECTION  
Part One  
General  
Article 29  
(1) The Regent integrates policies, programs, and activities for women’s protection into regency development planning.  
(2) The integration of policies, programs, and activities for women’s protection must be set forth in planning and budgeting documents including the Regional Long-Term Development Plan (RPJPD), Regional Medium-Term Development Plan (RPJMD), Regency Apparatus Work Unit Strategic Plan (Renstra-SKPK), Regency Government Work Plan (RKPK), and Regency Apparatus Work Unit Work Plan and Budget (RKA-SKPK).  
(3) The Regency Apparatus Work Unit whose duties and functions handle women’s empowerment affairs facilitates the integration of policies, programs, and activities for women’s protection into the preparation of the planning documents as referred to in paragraph (2).  
(4) In carrying out the duties in paragraph (3), the work unit that handles women’s empowerment in the region coordinates with the Regional Development Planning Agency (BAPPEDA).  

Part Two  
Strategy  
Article 30  
(1) Women’s protection is implemented by integrating Gender Mainstreaming (PUG) and an integrated violence database, sex-disaggregated data, and gender data based on applicable laws and regulations.  
(2) The Regency Apparatus Work Unit that manages women’s empowerment and protection coordinates with other Regional Apparatus organizations in order to realize the integrated implementation of women’s protection.  
(3) Coordination as referred to in paragraph (2) is carried out in the framework of strengthening synergy in policy formulation, support for programs and activities, and monitoring and evaluation.  

Part Three  
Mechanism  
Article 31  
The mechanism for implementing women’s protection is carried out through the stages of:  
a. formulation of policies for planning programs and activities;  
b. budgeting for programs and activities;  
c. implementation of programs and activities; and  
d. monitoring, evaluation, and reporting.  

Article 32  
(1) The formulation of policies for planning programs and activities as referred to in Article 31 letter a is carried out through:  
a. mapping, collecting, processing, and presenting disaggregated data;  
b. coordination and synchronization of the preparation of program and activity planning with related SKPK; and  
c. determination of programs and activities in planning documents.  
(2) The formulation of policies for planning programs and activities is further regulated in service standards and standard operating procedures.  
(3) Budgeting for programs and activities as referred to in Article 31 letter b is carried out through general budget policies set forth in the APBK.  
(4) The implementation of programs and activities as referred to in Article 31 letter c is carried out through:  
a. implementation of programs and activities set forth in SKPK budget documents; and  
b. coordination and synchronization of the implementation of programs and activities with related SKPK.  
(5) The implementation of monitoring, evaluation, and reporting as referred to in Article 31 letter d is carried out through:  
a. periodic monitoring and evaluation of the implementation of programs and activities that have been carried out; and  
b. preparing periodic reports annually submitted to the Regent.  

CHAPTER VIII  
COMMUNITY PARTICIPATION  
Article 33  
(1) The community may participate in women’s protection.  
(2) Community participation as referred to in paragraph (1) may be carried out by individuals and/or organizations.  
(3) Participation as referred to in paragraph (1) and paragraph (2) may be carried out by:  
a. providing input and suggestions to the Regency Government related to policies, programs, and activities for women’s protection;  
b. monitoring Regency Government policies related to women’s protection;  
c. initiating or proposing new policies that need to be established by the Regency Government in efforts to optimize women’s protection;  
d. assisting gampong apparatus in implementing women’s protection efforts as regulated in this Qanun;  
e. conducting outreach on the elimination of violence against women;  
f. providing information and/or reporting violence against women to law enforcement institutions or the competent authorities; and  
g. monitoring the performance of law enforcement officers in handling cases of violence against women.  

Article 34  
To ensure the best services for women victims of violence, the community, whether individually, in groups, or institutions, may coordinate with the UPTD PPA.  

Article 35  
(1) The Regency Government encourages the business sector to participate in protecting women from acts of violence.  
(2) Such participation is carried out both internally and externally.  
(3) Internal participation as referred to in paragraph (2) may include:  
a. outreach to employees within the company environment regarding the dangers of violence against women;  
b. building an internal women’s system to handle issues of violence against women; and  
c. providing social assistance as a form of corporate social responsibility.  
(4) External participation as referred to in paragraph (2) may include:  
a. providing special opportunities for women victims of violence to work in the company;  
b. providing special education and training for women victims of violence;  
c. educational assistance for women victims of violence who have been removed from education; and  
d. fostering and increasing the economic independence of women victims of violence.  

CHAPTER IX  
AWARDS  
Article 36  
(1) The Regency Government grants awards to State Apparatus deemed to have commitment and to have succeeded in carrying out women’s protection duties.  
(2) The Regency Government grants awards to Community Organizations, the Private Sector, and Individuals deemed to have innovated and succeeded in supporting women’s protection efforts.  
(3) Awards are also given to Gampong Governments deemed to have commitment and to have succeeded in supporting women’s protection efforts.  
(4) Further arrangements regarding the granting of awards as referred to in paragraph (1) and paragraph (2) are regulated by a Regent Regulation.  

CHAPTER X  
FINANCING  
Article 37  
All costs for implementing policies for planning programs and activities related to women’s protection are sourced from:  
a. the Regency Revenue and Expenditure Budget; and  
b. other lawful and non-binding sources.  

CHAPTER XI  
GUIDANCE, MONITORING, AND EVALUATION  
Article 38  
(1) The Regent through the Regional Apparatus in charge of women’s empowerment affairs conducts guidance and supervision over women’s protection.  
(2) The forms of guidance as referred to in paragraph (1) are as follows:  
a. providing outreach to the community and the business sector regarding women’s protection policies;  
b. providing books, leaflets, brochures regarding women’s protection and other related issues and disseminating them to the community;  
c. facilitating capacity building of institutions implementing women’s protection;  
d. facilitating forums for women’s participation, women’s organizations, and organizations of persons with disabilities;  
e. facilitating rehabilitation and reintegration services for women victims of disasters and victims of violence;  
f. coordinating the participation of the business sector and other institutions, including international assistance for women’s protection; and  
g. granting awards to the community and the business sector, both individually and institutionally, that have undertaken women’s protection efforts.  

Article 39  
(1) To ensure synergy, continuity, and effectiveness of integrated steps in implementing policies, programs, and activities for women’s protection, the Regency Government conducts monitoring.  
(2) Monitoring as referred to in paragraph (1) is intended to determine progress and obstacles in implementing policies, programs, and activities for women’s protection.  
(3) Monitoring is carried out periodically through coordination and direct monitoring of regional apparatus that implement policies, programs, and activities for women’s protection.  
(4) Monitoring is carried out from planning through implementation of policies, programs, and activities.  

Article 40  
(1) Evaluation of the implementation of policies, programs, and activities for women’s protection is carried out at the end of each fiscal year.  
(2) The results of the evaluation of the implementation of policies, programs, and activities for women’s protection are used as input material for the preparation of policies, programs, and activities for women’s protection for the following year.  
(3) The evaluation as referred to in paragraph (1) is carried out in accordance with the provisions of laws and regulations.

EXPLANATION  
OF  
BIREUEN REGENCY QANUN  
NUMBER 3 OF 2022  
CONCERNING  
WOMEN’S PROTECTION  
I. GENERAL  
1. That Islamic Shari’ah gives a high and noble position to women. Women have the same position as men as servants of Allah and dignified beings. In the Qur’an and the Hadith of the Prophet Muhammad (peace be upon him) there are never found teachings and activities that lead to discrimination and exploitation of women. In fact, one of the missions of the Prophet Muhammad (peace be upon him) in this world was to elevate the dignity and worth of women from a very low and despicable condition.  
2. That Law Number 44 of 1999 concerning the Implementation of Aceh’s Special Status and Law Number 11 of 2006 concerning the Government of Aceh have provided a foundation for women’s empowerment and protection, which are important activities because the condition of women so far is quite concerning in terms of education, economy, health, labor, law, and even threats of violence, exploitation, discrimination, pornography and pornographic acts often target women.  
3. The obligation of the Bireuen Regency Government feels it necessary to have a Bireuen Regency Qanun concerning Women’s Empowerment and Protection, it is hoped that every woman can be empowered and obtain protection without discrimination, and also that the Integrated Service Center for Women’s and Children’s Empowerment (P2TP2A) that already exists in Bireuen Regency has a juridical basis as one of the organizers of women’s empowerment and protection in Bireuen Regency.
