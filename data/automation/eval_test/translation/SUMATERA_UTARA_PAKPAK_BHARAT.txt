CHAPTER I
GENERAL PROVISIONS
Article 1
In this Regional Regulation, what is meant by:
1. Region is Pakpak Bharat Regency.
2. Regional Government is the Regional Head as an element of regional government administration who leads the implementation of governmental affairs that are the authority of the autonomous region.
3. Regent is the Regent of Pakpak Bharat.
4. Regional House of Representatives hereinafter abbreviated as DPRD is a regional people's representative institution positioned as an element of Regional Government administration.
5. Social Service is the Social Service of Pakpak Bharat Regency.
6. Discrimination is any distinction, harassment or exclusion, whether direct or indirect, based on human differentiation on the grounds of religion, tribe, race, ethnic group, social status, economic status, gender, language, political beliefs resulting from the reduction, deviation or elimination of recognition, implementation or use of human rights and basic freedoms in life both individually and collectively in the political, economic, legal, social, cultural and other aspects of life.
7. Human trafficking is the act of recruitment, transportation, harboring, sending, transfer, or receipt of a person by threat of violence, use of violence, abduction, confinement, forgery, fraud and abuse of power or position of vulnerability, debt bondage or giving payment or benefits, so as to obtain consent from the person holding control over that other person, whether conducted within the country or between countries, for the purpose of exploitation or resulting in a person being exploited.
8. Child is a person who has not reached 18 years of age including those in the womb.
9. Violence is any act unlawfully with or without using means against physical and psychological that causes danger to life, body or results in the deprivation of a person's freedom.
10. Violence against women is any action that results in misery or suffering of women physically, sexually or psychologically, including threats of certain actions, coercion or deprivation of freedom arbitrarily, whether occurring in the public sphere or in private life.

11. Violence against children is any act against a child that results in misery or suffering physically, mentally, sexually, psychologically, including neglect and maltreatment that threatens bodily integrity and degrades the dignity of the child committed by parties who should be responsible for the child or those who have power over the child, who should be trusted, such as parents, close family, teachers and companions.
12. Victim is a woman and child who experiences misery and/or suffering either directly or indirectly as a result of violence.
13. Health Rehabilitation is an effort to restore the victim's condition including physical, psychological, and sexual health so that the victim can perform their role again properly and naturally both in the family and in society.
14. Social Rehabilitation is a service aimed at restoring and developing the ability of a person experiencing social dysfunction so that they can perform their social functions properly.

23. Family is the smallest unit in society consisting of husband and wife, or husband-wife and their children, or father and children and mother and children.
24. Household is family members and relatives such as grandchildren, nieces/nephews, older siblings, younger siblings, grandfathers, grandmothers, husbands, wives, cousins and so forth and non-relatives such as domestic workers, drivers and so forth who live and reside in one house.

CHAPTER II
PRINCIPLES AND OBJECTIVES
Article 2
Protection of women and children victims of violence is conducted based on the principles of:
a. respect for human rights;
b. gender equality and justice;
c. non-discrimination;
d. protection of victims' rights;
e. legal certainty.

Article 3
(1) The objectives of providing protection for women and children victims of violence are to:
a. prevent acts of violence against women and children including human trafficking;
b. eliminate all forms of violence and exploitation against women and children;
c. protect and provide a sense of security for women and children; d. provide services to women and children victims of acts of violence, reporters and witnesses;
e. facilitate and mediate domestic disputes to realize the integrity of a harmonious and prosperous household.
(2) Violence as referred to in paragraph (1) may take the form of:
a. physical violence;
b. psychological violence;
c. sexual violence;
d. economic neglect;
e. restriction of movement.

CHAPTER III
RIGHTS OF VICTIMS
Article 4
Every woman and child victim of violence has the right to obtain:
a. respect for dignity and worth as a human being;
b. protection from family, community, Regional Government and/or other parties either temporarily or based on the determination of a protection order from the court;
c. health services according to medical needs;
d. special handling related to the confidentiality of victims;
e. assistance by social workers and legal aid at every level of the examination process in accordance with the provisions of laws and regulations;
f. spiritual guidance services;
g. to determine their own decisions.

CHAPTER IV
OBLIGATIONS AND RESPONSIBILITIES
Article 5
Obligations and responsibilities in providing protection to women and children victims of violence are the shared responsibility of:
a. regional government;
b. community;
c. family;
d. parents.

Article 6
(1) The Regional Government has the obligation and responsibility in providing protection to women and children victims of violence.
(2) The obligations and responsibilities of the regional government as referred to in Article 5 letter a include:
a. implementing policies for the protection of women and children victims of violence based on laws and regulations;
b. establishing policies, programs, and activities for the protection of women and children victims of violence;
c. providing support for facilities and infrastructure for the implementation of protection of women and children victims of violence;
d. facilitating assistance, legal aid and legal services according to the needs of victims;
e. allocating budget for the implementation of protection of women and children from victims of violence in accordance with regional financial capacity; and;
f. fostering and supervising the implementation of protection of women and children victims of violence.
(3) In order to carry out the obligations and responsibilities as referred to in paragraph (1), the Regent establishes programs and action activities for the protection of women and children in one Regional Action Plan as a basis for OPD in implementing the protection of women and children from acts of violence.
(4) The Regional Action Plan as referred to in paragraph (3), constitutes part of the Regional Medium-Term Development Plan (RPJMD).

Article 7
(1) The obligations and responsibilities of the community as referred to in Article 5 letter b, shall be implemented in the form of community participation. (2) The form of community participation as referred to in paragraph (1), includes:
a. preventing the occurrence of acts of violence against women and children;
b. providing information and/or reporting acts of violence against women and children to law enforcement or the authorized party;
c. participating in the handling of victims of acts of violence;
d. providing protection to victims; and
e. providing emergency assistance.

Article 8
The obligations of family and/or parents as referred to in Article 5 letters c and d, who legally have full responsibility to prevent all forms of violence and protect women and children as family members.

CHAPTER V
PREVENTION OF ACTS OF VIOLENCE
Article 9
(1) To prevent the occurrence of acts of violence against women and children, the regional government shall conduct empowerment and awareness-raising to families, parents, and the community by providing information, guidance and/or counseling.
(2) In addition to empowerment and awareness-raising as referred to in paragraph (1), the regional government shall undertake efforts as follows:
a. improvement of the quantity and quality of education both formal and non-formal and informal;
b. opening of accessibility to obtain education, training, funding, income enhancement and social services;
c. opening of employment opportunities for women;
d. building community participation and concern towards prevention and protection of women and children from acts of violence;
e. building and providing a complete and easily accessible information system;
f. building networks and cooperation with law enforcement apparatus, government apparatus, universities and various non-governmental organizations that work and/or care about women and children; and
g. opening complaint posts for the protection of women and children from acts of violence.

Article 10
(1) Prevention of acts of violence against women and children as referred to in Article 9, shall be implemented by OPD whose duties and functions are in the field of:
a. health;
b. education;
c. social affairs;
d. women's empowerment and child protection;
e. peace and order.
(2) Prevention of acts of violence by OPD as referred to in paragraph (1), shall be implemented in an integrated and continuous manner based on the regional action plan.

I. GENERAL

The national objectives of the Unitary State of the Republic of Indonesia, as stipulated in the Preamble of the 1945 Constitution in the fourth paragraph are stated to include: (1) protecting the entire Indonesian nation and all of Indonesia's homeland, (2) advancing public welfare, (3) developing the intellectual life of the nation and (4) participating in implementing world order based on (a) independence, (b) lasting peace, and (c) social justice.

The Republic of Indonesia is a State of Law (rechtstaat) and not a state of power (machstaat), so that all actions of its citizens, including those in the form of violence, committed by both men and women, are also regulated by law.

The rights of women and children are recognized as Human Rights (HAM), which must be upheld and respected by all parties, however in reality there are still many women and children who become victims of violence. The commitment of the Central Government to protect children and women is reflected in the legislation beneath it.

Various acts of violence both against women and children within the household and outside the household continue to increase from time to time. This condition, if without serious handling will destroy the life of households, society, nation and state. Therefore, the government of Pakpak Bharat Regency is obligated to provide protection to women and children from acts of violence which is an implementation of various laws and regulations, including Law Number 39 of 1999 concerning Human Rights, Law Number 23 of 2002 concerning Child Protection, Law Number 23 of 2004 concerning the Elimination of Domestic Violence, Law Number 21 of 2007 concerning the Eradication of the Criminal Act of Human Trafficking.

Protection of women and children from acts of violence is the obligation of Regional Government as regulated in Law Number 23 of 2014 concerning Regional Government which states that: "in carrying out regional autonomy has the obligation to protect the community, maintain unity, oneness and national harmony as well as the integrity of the Unitary State of the Republic of Indonesia."

Based on that, the Government of Pakpak Bharat Regency has taken steps as an effort to provide protection to women and children from acts of violence, however it has not been able to provide maximum and adequate protection against acts of violence that occur in the social life of the community especially to women and children jdih.pakpakbharatkab.go.id - 13 - due to the still low understanding of the community regarding the rights of women and children, handling has not been well coordinated, its implementation has not been sustainable and so forth. Therefore, a regulation in the form of a Regional Regulation concerning the protection of women and children from acts of violence is needed to answer while providing legal certainty in the protection of women and children from acts of violence that occur.

With the enactment of this Regional Regulation concerning the Protection of Women and Children from Acts of Violence, in Pakpak Bharat Regency in particular, then handling, rehabilitation and reintegration can be carried out properly and more smoothly in the process of prevention and handling of violence against women and children which in its implementation the Regional Government cooperates with government agencies, other regional governments, the community and also the participation of the business world in preventing violence against women and children from the beloved Pakpak Bharat Regency.

Article 2
Letter a The principle of humanity becomes the foundation of the concept of protection of women and children from acts of violence, constitutes respect for human rights and the dignity of every citizen and resident of Indonesia proportionally (the second principle of Pancasila) Letter b The principle of gender equality and justice, that gender justice is a process to be fair to men and women. Gender equality is the equality of conditions for men and women to obtain opportunities and their rights as human beings so they are able to play a role and participate in political, economic, social cultural, governmental activities and equality in enjoying the results of development.
Letter c The principle of non-discrimination, that in providing protection to women and children from acts of violence there shall be no differentiation on the basis of age, sex, race, ethnicity, tribe, religion and between groups.
Letter d The principle of protection of victims' rights, that all actions concerning women and children carried out by families, communities and Regional Government to fulfill the rights of women and children victims of acts of violence as regulated in laws and regulations.
Letter e The principle of legal certainty is the best interest for women and children, constitutes a principle specific to women and children themselves such as for example, how the interests of child victims of violence to undergo sanctions imposed jdih.pakpakbharatkab.go.id - 14 - upon them, or how children will face perpetrators in trial, must be stated in accordance with the interests of child victims of violence.
Likewise for women victims of violence who wish to be given freedom in accordance with their interests, women who wish to forget the perpetrator who incidentally is a person they once loved, and so on. So we as a society or as law enforcement officials need to give opportunities to children and women to express their interests concerning everything related to the perpetrator as well as how to overcome the impact of violence that befalls them.

Article 3
Paragraph (1) Sufficiently clear Paragraph (2) Point a What is meant by "physical violence" is an act that results in pain, falling ill, serious injury such as slapping, hitting, spitting on, pulling hair/tugging hair, kicking, burning with hot objects, hitting/injuring with weapons and or resulting in bodily disability of a person, miscarriage, fainting and or causing death.
Point b What is meant by "psychological violence" is an act that results in fear, loss of self-confidence, loss of ability to act, feeling of helplessness and or severe psychological suffering in a person. Psychological violence can be in the form of emotional abuse, hurtful comments that even demean self-worth, isolating the wife from the outside world, threatening/intimidating as a means of imposing one's will.
Point c What is meant by "sexual violence" is violence against sexuality which includes isolating (distancing) the wife from her spiritual needs, forcing sexual relations, forcing one's own sexual preferences or not paying attention to the wife's satisfaction.
Sexual violence can also be in the form of forced sexual relations committed against a person residing within the scope of the household, forced sexual relations against someone within the scope of one's household with another person for commercial purposes and or certain purposes.
Point d What is meant by "Economic Neglect" is neglecting the economic needs of the wife and children or family or relatives such as not providing for the wife or spending the wife's money, not giving or withholding the wages of domestic workers or drivers including acts of limiting or prohibiting family members from working in a proper manner.
Point e What is meant by "Restriction of movement" is limiting the movement of the wife or children to develop their potential whether for economic jdih.pakpakbharatkab.go.id - 15 - or social purposes such as prohibiting gathering or socializing with the community as well as limiting the space of relatives such as preventing domestic workers and drivers from communicating or having contact with their families.
