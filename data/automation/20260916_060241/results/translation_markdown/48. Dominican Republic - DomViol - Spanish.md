PRELIMINARY PROVISIONS

###### Art. 1

An infraction that the laws punish with police penalties is a contravention. An infraction that the laws punish with correctional penalties is a misdemeanor. An infraction that the laws punish with an afflictive or infamous penalty is a felony.

###### Art. 2

Any attempt at a felony may be considered as the felony itself, when it manifests itself with a beginning of execution, or when the guilty party, despite having done everything in his power to consummate it, does not achieve his purpose due to causes independent of his will; these circumstances being subject to the appreciation of the judges.

###### Art. 3

Attempts at misdemeanors are not deemed misdemeanors, except in cases where a special provision of the law so determines.

###### Art. 4

Contraventions, misdemeanors and felonies that are committed may not be punished except by virtue of a provision of law promulgated prior to their commission.

###### Art. 5

The provisions of this code are not applicable to military contraventions, misdemeanors or felonies.

## BOOK ONE

ON PENALTIES IN CRIMINAL AND CORRECTIONAL MATTERS AND THEIR EFFECTS

###### Art. 6

Penalties in criminal matters are afflictive and infamous; or infamous only.

###### Art. 7.- (Modified by Law 46-99 of May 20, 1999)

The afflictive and infamous penalties are: 1st. Major imprisonment; 2nd. Detention and 3rd. Minor imprisonment.

###### Art. 8.- Civic degradation is an infamous penalty

###### Art. 9

Penalties in correctional matters are: 1st., banishment; 2nd., confinement; 3rd., temporary imprisonment; 4th., interdiction for a determined time of certain civic, civil or family rights; 5th., the fine*.

###### Art. 10

The penalties pronounced by law for felonies, misdemeanors and contraventions shall always be imposed, without prejudice to restitutions and damages that may result in favor of the aggrieved parties.

###### Art. 11

Penalties common to criminal and correctional matters are: the subjection of the convicted person to the surveillance of the high police, the fine** and the special confiscation of the corpus delicti, when it is the property of the convicted person, that of things produced by the offense, and finally, that of those which served for its commission or which were destined for that purpose.

#### CAPÍTULO II

DE LAS PENAS EN MATERIA CORRECCIONAL

###### Art. 37.- (Modified implicitly by article 8 of the Constitution of the Republic)

Every person condemned to banishment shall be taken, by order of the Government, outside the territory of the Republic. The duration of banishment may not exceed three years nor be less than one.

###### Art. 38.- (Modified implicitly by article 8 of the Constitution of the Republic)

If before the expiration of the penalty the banished person enters Dominican territory, he shall be condemned, upon verification of his identity, to imprisonment for a time at least equal to that which remained for the expiration of the banishment, without the sentence imposed in this case being able to be pronounced for a longer time.

###### Art. 39.- (Modified by Law 4381 of February 7, 1956

G.O. 7945 and modified implicitly by article 8 of the Constitution of the Republic). Every person condemned to confinement shall be taken to the provincial or district capital city or to the municipality of the territory of the Republic indicated by the sentence of conviction. The duration of this penalty shall be six months at least, and two years at most. In the event that the confined person leaves the place of his confinement, he shall be condemned to correctional imprisonment, for a time equal to that which remained for the expiration of the confinement.

###### Art. 40

Every person condemned to correctional imprisonment shall be detained in a house of correction. He shall be assigned, according to his choice, to one of the workshops established in the house. The duration of this penalty shall be six days at least, and two years at most; except in cases of recidivism or others in which the law provides

otherwise. The computation of time for the duration of penalties is twenty-four hours for each day of arrest, and thirty days for each month.

###### Art. 41

A part of the product of the work of those detained for correctional offenses shall be allocated to the common expenses of the house, another to provide them with some advantages or relief during their detention, if they deserve it, reserving the third part to form a fund for them, which shall be delivered to them upon their release from prison. In compliance with these provisions, what is prescribed by the regulations that the Executive Power issues on the matter shall be observed.

###### Art. 42

The courts that hear cases in correctional matters may, in certain cases, deprive the convicted person of part or all of the exercise of his civic, civil and family rights as follows: 1o., that of voting and election; 2o., that of eligibility; 3o., that of being a juror or appointed to exercise other public functions, or for positions in the administration; 4o., that of bearing arms; 5o., that of voting or suffrage in family deliberations; 6o., that of being a guardian or curator of persons other than his own children, and with the consent of the family; 7o., that of being an expert or serving as a witness in public acts; 8o., that of giving testimony in court, unless it is received as simple information.

###### Art. 43

The courts shall not pronounce the interdiction referred to in the preceding article, except when the law expressly authorizes or orders it.

#### CAPÍTULO III

DE LAS PENAS Y DE LAS OTRAS CONDENACIONES QUE PUEDEN PRONUNCIARSE POR CRÍMENES Y DELITOS

###### Art. 44.- (Modified by the Constitution of 1966)

Subjection to the surveillance of the high police gives the Government the right to determine certain places to which the convicted person may not present himself, except after having served his sentence.

At least fifteen days before the convicted person obtains his freedom, he must indicate the place where he is going to fix his residence; and if he does not do so, the Government shall fix one for him. The individual condemned to the surveillance of the high police may not leave the residence he has chosen or that has been indicated to him before six months, without the authorization of the Secretary of State for Interior and Police. However, the Provincial Governors may grant this authorization: 1o., in the case of simple change of residence, within the limits of their province; and 2o., in cases of urgency, but on a provisional basis only. After six months have elapsed, or earlier in the case of having obtained the competent authorization, the convicted person may move to any place where he is not prohibited from doing so, notifying the Governor or local authority eight days in advance. The stay of six months, referred to in the article, is mandatory for the convicted person, in each of the places he successively chooses, during the time he is subject to the surveillance of the high police, unless he obtains special authorization granted, in accordance with the preceding provisions, by the Secretary of State for Interior and Police, or by the Provincial Governors. The convicted person who returns to his residence shall obtain a route order that regulates the itinerary he must follow, from which he may not deviate; nor may he exceed the time assigned to him for permanence in the transit places. He shall be obliged to present himself within twenty-four hours of his arrival, before the authority of the place where he is going to reside.

###### Art. 45

In case of violation of the provisions prescribed in the preceding article, the individual subject to the surveillance of the high police shall be condemned by the correctional courts to imprisonment, which may not exceed two years.

###### Art. 46.- (Modified by Laws 224 of June 26, 1984 and 46-99 of May 20, 1999)

In no case may the duration of the penalty under the surveillance of the high police exceed five years. Those condemned to major imprisonment,

to detention and to minor imprisonment, shall remain by operation of law, after they have served their sentence and for five years, under the surveillance of the high police. Nevertheless, the conviction judgment may reduce this term, and even declare that the convicted person shall not be subject to the surveillance of the high police. Every person condemned to the maximum penalty of major imprisonment, who obtains commutation or remission of his sentence, shall remain, by operation of law, subject to the surveillance of the high police, for five years, unless otherwise resolved by the pardon decree.

###### Art. 47

Surveillance under the high police may be pardoned or reduced by pardon; and may be suspended by governmental provision.

###### Art. 48

The prescription of the penalty does not relieve the convicted person from surveillance under the high police, to which he is subject. In the case of prescription of the greater afflictive penalty, the convicted person shall be, by operation of law, under the surveillance of the high police, for five years; and it shall not produce its effects, except from the day on which the prescription is fulfilled.

###### Art. 49

Individuals who have been convicted for crimes or offenses against the internal or external security of the State, must remain subject to the surveillance of the high police.

###### Art. 50

Outside of the cases determined by the preceding articles, the convicted persons shall not remain subject to the surveillance of the high police, except in the case that it is established by a particular provision of the law.

###### Art. 51

When restitutions are warranted, the guilty party may also be condemned in favor of the aggrieved party, if the latter so requests, to the indemnification of the damages that the former has caused him, these damages to be assessed by the court, when the law has not determined them. In no case may the courts, not even with the consent of the aggrieved party, allocate the indemnifications to pious works or any others whatsoever.

###### Art. 52

The execution of the condemnations to the fine*, to the restitutions, to the damages and losses, and to the costs may be pursued by way of corporal constraint.

###### Art. 53

When the fines** and the costs are pronounced in favor of the treasury, if after the expiration of the penalty, whether afflictive or infamous, or correctional, the convicted person proves by legal means his insolvency, the court shall order his release.

###### Art. 54

When the property of the convicted person is not sufficient to cover the condemnations to which he is simultaneously subjected for payment of restitutions, damages and losses and the fine***, the first condemnations shall always be satisfied, with preference over the last.

###### Art. 55

All individuals convicted for the same crime or for the same offense, are jointly and severally liable for the fines****, restitutions, damages and losses and costs that are pronounced.

###### Art. 59

Accomplices to a crime or to an offense shall be imposed the penalty immediately inferior to that which corresponds to the authors of this crime or offense; except in the cases in which the law provides otherwise.

###### Art. 60

The following shall be punished as accomplices to an action qualified as a crime or offense: those who by gifts, promises, threats, abuse of power or authority, machinations or culpable plots, provoked that action or gave instructions to commit it: those who, knowingly, provided arms or instruments, or facilitated the means that served to execute the action. Those who, knowingly, helped or assisted the author or authors of the action, in those acts that prepared or facilitated its realization, or in those that consummated it; without prejudice to the penalties that are specifically established in the present code, against the authors of plots or provocations threatening the internal or external security of the State, even in the case in which the crime that the conspirators or provocateurs intended to execute has not been committed.

###### Art. 61

Those who, knowing the criminal conduct of the malefactors who engage in raids or violence against the security of the State, the public peace, persons or property, habitually provide them with lodging, hiding place or meeting place, shall be punished as their accomplices.

###### Art. 62

Those who knowingly have concealed in their totality or in part, things robbed, stolen, removed or acquired by means of crime or offense, shall also be considered as accomplices, and punished as such.

###### Art. 63

(Modified by Laws 64 of November 19, 1924 G.O. 3596; 224 of June 26, 1984 and 46-99 of May 20, 1999). In no case may the penalty of major imprisonment be pronounced, when proceeding against the concealers, except after they have been convinced of having had knowledge, at the moment of the concealment, of the circumstances to which the law applies the penalty of thirty years of major imprisonment or that of major imprisonment: otherwise, they shall be imposed the penalty of detention.

###### Art. 64

When at the moment of committing the action the accused was in a state of insanity, or when he was compelled to it by a force to which he could not resist, there is no crime or offense.

###### Art. 65

The crimes and offenses that are committed, cannot be excused, nor can the penalties that the law imposes on them be mitigated, except in the cases and circumstances in which the same law declares the excuse admissible, or authorizes the imposition of a lesser penalty.

SECTION 1: CRIMES AND OFFENSES AGAINST THE EXTERNAL SECURITY OF THE STATE

###### Art. 75

(Modified by Laws 64 of November 19, 1924 G.O. 3596; 224 of June 26, 1984 and 46-99 of May 20, 1999). Every Dominican who takes up arms against the Republic shall be punished with the penalty of thirty years of major imprisonment.

###### Art. 76

(Modified by Laws 64 of November 19, 1924 G.O. 3596; 1384 of March 13, 1947 G.O. 6605;
224 of June 26, 1984 and 46-99 of May 20, 1999).
Any person who, from the territory of the Republic, enters or attempts to enter into agreement with foreign States or with their agents, or with any foreign institution or private foreign persons, to attempt to have a war undertaken against the Republic or against the Government that represents it, or to have them harmed in any way, or who, contrary to the provisions of the Government, intervenes in any manner in the life of the State or in that of any institution thereof, or who provides assistance for said purposes, shall be punished with the penalty of thirty years of major imprisonment. The aforementioned sanction applies to every Dominican who carries out the mentioned acts, even if this is done from foreign territory.

###### Art. 77

(Modified by Laws 64 of November 19, 1924 G.O. 3596; 224 of June 26, 1984 and 46-99 of May 20, 1999). Likewise shall be punished with the penalty of thirty years of major imprisonment anyone who has entered into agreement with the enemies of the State, or who by means of plots and concert with them, procures the means to facilitate their entry into the territory of the Republic and its dependencies, or the surrender of cities, fortresses, squares, posts, ports, warehouses, arsenals, ships or vessels belonging to the Republic. The same penalty shall be imposed on those who provide the enemies with assistance of men, soldiers, provisions, arms or supplies of food and ammunition, or who favor the progress of their arms in the possessions of the Republic, or against the Dominican forces on land and sea, or who employ enticement, or attempt to corrupt the officers, soldiers, sailors or others attached to the army, causing them to fail in the fidelity owed to the Government or to the Nation, or who in any other manner attack national independence.

###### Art. 78

However, if the result of correspondence with subjects of an enemy power is to provide the enemies with instructions prejudicial to the military or political situation of the Republic or its allies, even though that correspondence did not have as its object any of the crimes enumerated in the preceding article, those who maintained it shall be punished with detention; without prejudice to more severe penalties, in the event that those instructions were the consequence of a concert of measures constituting the crime of espionage.

###### Art. 79

The penalties pronounced by articles 76 and 77 shall be imposed on those who direct their machinations, plots or maneuvers to the prejudice of the Republic, or of the allies who, in agreement with it, act against the common enemy.

###### Art. 80

The penalties expressed in article 76 shall be imposed on any public official, agent of the Government or any other person who, charged or instructed, by reason of their position, with the secret of a negotiation or expedition, communicated it to the agents of some foreign Power, or to those of the enemy.

###### Art. 81.- (Modified by Laws 224 of June 26, 1984 and 46-99 of May 20, 1999)

Any public official, agent or delegate of the Government who, charged by reason of their office, with the custody of plans of fortifications, arsenals, ports, coves, inlets or roadsteads, delivered one or many of those to the enemy, or to the agents of the enemy shall be punished with the penalty of thirty years of major imprisonment. If the plans have been delivered to the agents of a friendly, allied or neutral Power, the penalty shall be detention.

###### Art. 82

Any person who, by corruption, fraud or violence, manages to steal said plans, and delivers them to the enemy, or to the agents of a foreign Power, shall be punished as the official or agent mentioned in the preceding article, and according to the distinctions established therein. If said plans were in the hands of the person who delivered them, without unlawful means being employed to obtain them, the penalty in the first case of article 81, shall be detention; and in the second case of the same article, correctional imprisonment of one to two years shall be imposed on the guilty party.

###### Art. 83.- (Modified by Laws 224 of June 26, 1984 and 46-99 of May 20, 1999)

Whoever knowingly concealed or caused to be concealed enemy soldiers or spies sent on reconnaissance, shall be sentenced to the penalty of thirty years of major imprisonment.

###### Art. 84.- (Modified implicitly by article 8 of the Constitution of the Republic)

Whoever, by hostile acts disapproved by the Government, exposed the Republic to a declaration of war, shall be punished with the penalty of banishment; and if war was the consequence of those acts, the penalty of detention shall be applied.

###### Art. 85.- (Modified implicitly by article 8 of the Constitution of the Republic)

Whoever, with acts not approved, nor

authorized by the Government, exposed Dominicans to suffer reprisals, in their persons or in their property, shall be punished with the penalty of banishment.

###### PARAGRAPH I: ATTACKS AND PLOTS AGAINST THE HEAD OF STATE

###### Art. 86

Any offense committed publicly toward the person of the Head of State shall be punished with imprisonment of six months to two years, and a fine of fifty to five hundred pesos.

###### Art. 87.- (Modified by Laws 224 of June 26, 1984 and 46-99 of May 20, 1999)

The attack whose object is to change the form of government established by the Constitution, or to incite citizens to arm themselves against the legally constituted authority, shall be punished with the penalty of minor imprisonment.

###### Art. 88.- The execution or the attempt shall alone constitute the attack

###### Art. 89

(Modified by Laws 224 of June 26, 1984; 46-99 of May 20, 1999 and modified implicitly by article 8 of the Constitution of the Republic). The plot that has as its object the crime mentioned in article 87, shall be punished with minor imprisonment, if the acts were committed or begun to be committed to prepare its execution. If no act has been committed or begun to be committed to prepare its execution the penalty shall be banishment.

###### Art. 90

There is a conspiracy from the moment when two or more persons agree among themselves on the resolution to act. If there has been a proposal made, and not accepted, to form a conspiracy to consummate the crime mentioned in article 87, the one who made the proposal shall be punished with correctional imprisonment.

###### PÁRRAFO II

CRIMES TENDING TO DISTURB THE STATE WITH CIVIL WAR, WITH THE ILLEGAL USE OF ARMED FORCE, PILLAGE AND PUBLIC DEVASTATION

###### Art. 91.- (Modified by Laws 5007 of June 28, 1911

G.O. 2209; 4381 of February 17, 1956 G.O. 7945; 224 of June 26, 1984 and 46-99 of May 20, 1999). The attempt that has as its object to provoke civil war, inciting citizens or inhabitants to arm themselves against one another, with the purpose of bringing devastation, pillage or slaughter to one or several municipalities, shall be punished with the penalty of twenty years of major imprisonment. The conspiracy formed to achieve one of the crimes provided for in this article, and the proposal to form it, shall be punished with the penalties designated in article 89, according to the distinctions established therein.

###### Art. 92

(Modified by Laws 5007 of June 28, 1911 G.O. 2209; 224 of June 26, 1984 and 46-99 of May 20, 1999). Those who, without order or authorization from legitimate power, have raised armies, enlisted or recruited soldiers, or who without the same order or authorization have supplied them with arms or supplies, or have procured them for them, shall be punished with the penalty of twenty years of major imprisonment.

###### Art. 93

(Modified by Laws 5007 of June 28, 1911 G.O. 2209; 224 of June 26, 1984 and 46-99 of May 20, 1999). Those who, without right or legitimate motive, have taken command of an army corps, a troop, a fleet, a squadron, a warship, a fortified place, a port, a post or a city, or who against the order of the Government have retained any military command, shall be punished with the penalty of twenty years of major imprisonment.

###### Art. 94

The penalty of detention shall be imposed on anyone who, having at his disposal the public force, has requisitioned or ordered, caused to be requisitioned or ordered its action or use, against the legally established recruitment.

###### Art. 95.- (Modified by Laws 896 of April 25, 1935

G.O. 4789; 224 of June 26, 1984 and 46-99 of May 20, 1999). Any individual who has set fire to or destroyed, or attempted to set fire to or destroy, in whole or in part, by means of a mine, bomb or any other explosive mechanism, buildings, warehouses, shipyards, arsenals, ships, docks, vehicles of all kinds, or other properties belonging to the State, shall be punished with the penalty of thirty years of major imprisonment.

###### Art. 96

(Modified by Laws 5007 of June 28, 1911 G.O. 2209; 224 of June 26, 1984 and 46-99 of May 20, 1999). Whoever, in order to invade the domains, properties or public revenues, the places, cities, fortresses, posts, warehouses, arsenals, ports, ships or vessels of the State; whoever, in order to pillage or distribute public or national properties, or those of a generality of citizens; and finally, whoever, in order to attack or resist the public force acting against the perpetrators of those crimes, has placed himself at the head of armed bands or gangs, or has exercised in them any command or function whatsoever, shall be punished with the penalty of twenty years of major imprisonment. The same penalties shall be applied to those who have directed the association, raised or caused to be raised, organized or caused to be organized the bands or gangs, or who knowingly and voluntarily have facilitated or supplied them with arms, ammunition or instruments for the crime, or have sent them convoys of provisions or in any other way have been in agreement with the directors or gang leaders.

###### Art. 97

(Modified by Laws 5007 of June 28, 1911 G.O. 2209; 224 of June 26, 1984 and 46-99 of May 20, 1999). In the event that one or many of the crimes mentioned in articles 87 and 91 have been executed, or that there has only been an attempt at execution by a gang, the penalty of twenty years of major imprisonment shall be applied, without distinction of degrees, to all individuals who have belonged to the band or gang, or who have been

apprehended at the place of the seditious assembly. The same penalty shall be imposed, even if not apprehended at the places, on anyone who has directed the sedition, or has exercised in the gang any employment or command whatsoever.

###### Art. 98.- (Modified by Laws 224 of June 26, 1984 and 46-99 of May 20, 1999)

Except in the case where the seditious assembly has had as its object or result one or many of the crimes enumerated in articles 87 and 91, the individuals who have formed part of the gangs mentioned, without exercising in them any command or employment, shall be punished with the penalty of minor imprisonment, provided they have been arrested at the point of the seditious assembly.

###### Art. 99

Those who, knowing the object and tendencies of said gangs, have provided or facilitated lodging, hiding place or meeting place for them, without having been forced to do so, shall be sentenced to the penalty of detention.

###### Art. 100

No penalty shall be pronounced for the offense of sedition against those who, having formed part of those gangs without exercising in them any employment or function, have withdrawn at the first notice of the civil or military authority, or who do so even afterwards, provided they have been arrested without arms, outside the places of the seditious assembly and without offering resistance. They shall not be punished except for the particular crimes they have personally committed, being able, however, to remain for a time that shall not be less than one year nor exceed five, subject to the surveillance of the high police.

###### Art. 101

The word arms includes all machines, instruments or utensils that are cutting, piercing or blunt.

###### Art. 102

Razors, pocket knives, scissors or simple reeds shall not be considered arms, except when they have been used to kill, wound or strike.

###### Art. 103

Any individual who, whether by speeches, shouts or threats uttered in public places or assemblies, whether by means of writings, printed matter, drawings, engravings, paintings or emblems, sold or distributed, put up for sale or displayed in public places or assemblies; whether by posters and placards posted to public view, has incited the author or authors of any action qualified as a crime or offense to commit it, shall be considered an accomplice and shall be punished as such.

###### Art. 104

Anyone who, by one of the means enumerated in the preceding article, has incited to commit one or many crimes, without this incitement having been followed by any effect, shall be punished with correctional imprisonment, which may not be less than three months nor more than two years, and with a fine* that may not be less than ten pesos nor exceed one thousand.

###### Art. 105

Anyone who, by one of the same means, has incited to commit one or many offenses, without said incitement having been followed by any effect, shall be sentenced to correctional imprisonment and a fine** of ten to five hundred pesos; or to one of the two penalties only, according to the circumstances; except in cases where the law pronounces a lesser penalty against the author of the offense himself, which shall then be applied to the inciter.

###### Art. 106

Incitement, by one of the aforementioned means, to disobedience to the laws, shall be punished with the penalties determined in the preceding article.

#### CHAPTER II: CRIMES AND OFFENSES

AGAINST THE CONSTITUTION
SECTION 1ST:
ON CRIMES AND OFFENSES RELATING TO THE EXERCISE OF POLITICAL RIGHTS

###### Art. 109

Tumultuous assemblies that, using violence or threats, have as their object to prevent one or more citizens from exercising their political rights, shall be punished with correctional imprisonment of six months to two years, which shall be imposed on each of the individuals who formed part of them. They shall also be disqualified for at least one year, and five at most, from being an elector or elected to any public office of popular appointment.

###### Art. 110

(Modified by Law 4381 of February 7, 1956 G.O. 7945 and implicitly modified by article 8 of the Constitution of the Republic). If the offense were the consequence of a concerted plan, and whose execution was to be carried out throughout the Republic, or in one or more of its provinces, districts or municipalities, the penalty shall be that of banishment.

###### Art. 111

Citizens who, entrusted in electoral acts with the counting of ballots, are caught falsifying registration ballots or diverting them from the ballot box, or adding to it ballots different from those deposited by the voters, or inscribing on those of voters who cannot write, names different from those they had indicated to them, shall be punished with civic degradation.

###### Art. 112

Other persons who make themselves guilty of the offenses stated in the preceding article shall be sentenced to imprisonment of six months to two years, and to the interdiction of the right to elect and be elected, for at least one year, and five at most.

###### Art. 113

Any citizen who, in elections, has bought or sold a vote, whatever its price, shall suffer the penalty of disqualification for public positions and offices, from one to five years, and a fine* of ten to one hundred pesos. The buyer of the vote and his accomplice shall be sentenced to a fine** that each shall pay for himself, and whose amount shall be raised to double the value of the things received or offered. If this value cannot be determined, the fine*** shall be from ten to one hundred pesos.

SECTION 3: COALITION OF OFFICIALS

###### Art. 123

Public officials or employees, corporations or depositaries of a part of the public authority who concert or agree among themselves on the execution of measures and provisions contrary to the laws, or who with the same purpose carry on correspondence or send deputations to each other, shall be punished with imprisonment of two to six months, and absolute disqualification from one to five years, for public positions and offices.

###### Art. 124

(Implicitly amended by article 8 of the Constitution of the Republic; Amended by Laws 224 of June 26, 1984 and 46-99 of May 20, 1999). If the concert of measures entered into by the officials and employees referred to in the preceding article has the purpose of opposing the execution of the laws or the orders of the Government, the penalty of banishment shall be imposed on the guilty parties. If the concert has been effected between the civil authorities and the military corps and their chiefs, those who are found to be authors or instigators shall be punished with minor imprisonment, and the other guilty parties shall be punished with the penalty of banishment.

###### Art. 125

(Amended by Laws 5007 of June 28, 1911 G.O. 2209; 224 of June 26, 1984 and 46-99 of May 20, 1999). If from the concert results an attack against the internal security of the State, the penalty of twenty years of major imprisonment shall be imposed on the guilty parties.

###### Art. 126.- (Implicitly amended by article 8 of the Constitution of the Republic)

Public officials who have deliberately resolved to submit resignations, with the purpose of preventing or suspending the administration of justice, or the fulfillment of any service, shall be punished as guilty of malfeasance, and punished with the penalty of confinement.  

SECTION 4: USURPATION OF AUTHORITY
BY OFFICIALS OF THE ADMINISTRATIVE OR JUDICIAL ORDER

###### Art. 127

Shall be considered guilty of malfeasance, and shall be punished with civic degradation: judges, prosecutors or their substitutes, and police officers who have interfered with the exercise of Legislative Power, issuing regulations that contain legislative provisions or suspending the execution of one or many laws or deliberating as to whether the laws shall be executed or promulgated.

###### Art. 128

Shall be punished with the same penalty, judges, prosecutors or their substitutes, and police officers who exceed their powers, interfering in matters that correspond to the administrative authorities, whether they regulate in those matters, or whether they prohibit the execution of orders emanating from the Government.

###### Art. 129

In addition to the penalties indicated in the articles of this section, the guilty parties may be sentenced to the damages and losses they have caused.

###### Art. 130.- (Amended by the 1966 Constitution). Provincial Governors, City Councils, Syndics and

other administrators, shall be punished with civic degradation, when they interfere in the exercise of Legislative Power, taking provisions or issuing general rulings, whose tendencies are to intimate orders or prohibitions to the courts.

###### Art. 131.- (Amended by Law 4427 of April 5, 1956 G.O. 7971)

The administrative employees indicated in the preceding article who usurp judicial powers shall incur the same penalty, interfering in the knowledge of private rights and interests within the jurisdiction of the courts, and who after the claim of the parties or one of them nevertheless decide the matter; or who in any way require, instruct or make recommendations to the judicial authorities to conform their actions, decisions or rulings, to the particular interest or criterion of those employees.

###### PARAGRAPH I: OF THE COUNTERFEITING OF CURRENCY

###### Art. 132.- (Amended by Laws 224 of June 26, 1984 and 46-99 of May 20, 1999)

Whoever counterfeits or alters gold and silver coins that have legal circulation in the Republic, or who issues, introduces or expends said false or altered coins, shall be sentenced to the maximum penalty of major imprisonment.

###### Art. 133.- (Amended by Laws 224 of June 26, 1984 and Law 46-99 of May 20, 1999)

Shall be punished with the penalty of three to ten years of major imprisonment, whoever counterfeits or alters copper or nickel coins, that are in legal circulation in the Republic, or who introduces, issues or expends them.

###### Art. 134.- (Amended by Law 428 of November 22, 1972 G.O. 9288)

The penalties of the preceding article shall be imposed on whoever in the Republic counterfeits or alters metallic coins, bank notes or foreign securities, or who introduces, issues or expends them. The sentence shall always order the confiscation of the coins, notes or securities.

###### Art. 135

Any person who has colored coins that have legal tender in the Republic, or foreign coins, with intent or purpose to deceive as to the material of the metal; or who has issued or introduced them into the territory of the Republic, shall be punished with imprisonment of six months to two years. The participation indicated in the preceding articles of this section shall not include those persons who, having received false coins as good, have returned them to circulation.

Those guilty of the crimes mentioned in articles 132 and 133 shall be exempt from criminal liability, provided that before the perpetration of the crime, or before the investigations and proceedings begin, they give notice thereof to the constituted authority, or reveal to it the names of the perpetrators. They shall enjoy the same exemption after the proceedings have begun, if they facilitate the capture of the

###### PÁRRAFO II

FALSIFICATION OF THE SEALS, STAMPS, STAMPED PAPER, MARKS AND PUNCHES OF THE STATE, OF BANK NOTES, AND OF PUBLIC CREDIT DOCUMENTS.

###### Art. 139.- (Modified by Laws 224 of June 26, 1984 and 46-99 of May 20, 1999)

Whoever falsifies the seals of the State, or makes use of the falsified seal, whoever falsifies public credit documents issued by the public treasury with its seals, or bank notes authorized by law, or who makes use of those falsified documents or bank notes, or who introduces or distributes them in the territory of the Republic, shall be sentenced to major imprisonment.

###### Art. 140.- (Modified by Laws 224 of June 26, 1984 and 46-99 of May 20, 1999)

Whoever falsifies the punches intended for the assay of gold or silver materials, or who makes use of falsified papers, public credits, stamps, stamped paper or punches, shall be sentenced to three to ten years of major imprisonment.

###### Art. 141.- (Modified by Laws 224 of June 26, 1984 and 46-99 of May 20, 1999)

Whoever, by improper and reprehensible means, obtains the true seals, marks or punches intended for one of the uses expressed in the preceding article, and makes uses and applications of them prejudicial to the interests of the State, shall be sentenced to minor imprisonment.

###### Art. 145.- (Modified by Laws 224 of June 26, 1984 and 46-99 of May 20, 1999)

The public employee or official who, in the exercise of his functions, commits forgery, counterfeiting or feigning handwriting, signature or rubric, altering the nature of acts, writings or signatures, supposing in an act the intervention or presence of persons who have not

taken part in it, inserting writings in the registers or other public acts after their preparation or closure, shall be sentenced to the penalty of major imprisonment.

###### Art. 146.- (Modified by Laws 224 of June 26, 1984 and 46-99 of May 20, 1999)

They shall be likewise punished with the penalty of major imprisonment: any official or public officer who, in the exercise of his ministry, has willfully and fraudulently misrepresented the substance of acts or their circumstances; drafting agreements different from those which the parties had dictated or formulated; recording in the acts, as true, false facts, or as acknowledged and approved by the parties, those which had not really been so; altering the true dates, giving an authentic copy of a supposed document, or manifesting in it something contrary or different from what the true original contains.

###### Art. 147.- (Modified by Laws 224 of June 26, 1984 and 46-99 of May 20, 1999)

Any other person who commits forgery in authentic or public writing, or in commercial and banking writings, shall be punished with the penalty of three to ten years of major imprisonment, whether by imitating or altering the writings or signatures, or by stipulating or inserting agreements, provisions, obligations or discharges after the closure of those, or by adding or altering clauses, declarations or facts that should be received or recorded in said acts.

###### Art. 148.- (Modified by Laws 224 of June 26, 1984 and 46-99 of May 20, 1999)

In all cases of this paragraph, whoever has made use of the false acts shall be punished with the penalty of minor imprisonment.

###### PÁRRAFO V: FALSEDAD EN LOS PASAPORTES, ÓR-DENES DE RUTA Y CERTIFICACIONES

###### Art. 153

(Modified by Laws 282 of March 28, 1968; 224 of June 26, 1984 and 46-99 of May 20, 1999). The penalty of three to ten years of major imprisonment shall be imposed on whoever makes a false passport, whoever falsifies an originally true passport, and whoever makes use of a false or falsified passport.

###### Art. 154

Whoever has himself registered in a passport under a false name, or who as a witness has attended with the purpose of having the passport issued under a false name, shall be punished with correctional imprisonment, from three months to one year. The same penalty shall apply to any individual who makes use of any passport issued under a name other than his own.  
Innkeepers, hoteliers or tavern keepers who, knowingly, register in their records under false or assumed names, persons who lodge in their establishments, shall be punished with imprisonment of six days to one month.

###### Art. 155

A public official who, knowingly, issues a passport under a false name, shall be punished with imprisonment of six months to two years.

###### Art. 156

Whoever commits forgery in a travel order, or forges one that was originally genuine, and whoever makes use of that false or forged order, shall be punished according to the following distinctions: if the travel order had no other purpose than to deceive the vigilance of the public authority, the penalty shall be six months to two years of imprisonment; if the public treasury has paid the bearer of the false order a travel allowance that was not owed to him, or whose value exceeded that to which he could be entitled, the penalty of confinement shall be imposed, provided that the sum collected does not exceed one hundred pesos, the penalty being raised to one to two years of imprisonment if the sum unduly received amounts to more than one hundred pesos.

###### Art. 157

The penalties pronounced by the preceding article shall be applied according to the distinctions established therein, to any person who under a false name has had a travel order issued by the public authority, or who has made use of a travel sheet delivered under a name other than his own.

###### Art. 158.- (Modified by Laws 224 of June 26, 1984 and 46-99 of May 20, 1999)

If the authority that issued the order had knowledge, at the time of issuing it, of the false name, the penalty shall be, in the first case of article 156, that of confinement; in the second case of the same article, imprisonment of one to two years shall be imposed; and if found in the last case, he shall be punished with lesser imprisonment. In the first two cases he may, in addition, be deprived of the rights mentioned in article 42 of this code, for at least one year and at most five, counting from the day he has completed his sentence.

###### Art. 159

Anyone who, for the purpose of exempting himself or any other person from a public service, takes the name of a physician, surgeon, or any other health officer and issues a certificate of illness or habitual ailment, shall be punished with correctional imprisonment of six months to two years.

###### Art. 160.- (Implicitly modified by article 8 of the Constitution of the Republic)

Physicians, surgeons or health officers who, to favor someone, give false certification of illnesses or ailments that exempt him from public service, shall be punished with imprisonment of six months to two years. If they have acted impelled by gifts or promises, the penalty of banishment shall be imposed. In both cases he may, in addition, be deprived of the rights mentioned in article 42 of this code, for at least one year and at most five, counting from the day he has completed his sentence. The corruptors shall, in the second case, be punished with the same penalties.

###### Art. 161

The penalty of three months to one year of imprisonment shall be imposed on anyone who takes the name of a public official or officer, and issues certification of life and conduct, of insolvency or other circumstances that attract the benevolence of the Government or of private individuals, regarding the person designated therein, or that facilitate employment, credit or assistance for him. The same penalty shall be imposed on whoever forges a certification of the kind mentioned in this article, with the purpose of appropriating it to a person other than the one to whom it was originally issued, even though the certification was originally genuine. Also guilty of the same penalty shall be whoever has made use of the false or forged certification. If this certification is made under the name of a private individual, the forgery and use shall be punished with the penalty of fifteen days to six months of imprisonment.

###### Art. 162

False certifications other than those expressed, and from which result harm to third parties or to the public treasury, shall be punished as appropriate, in accordance with the provisions of paragraphs 3 and 4 of this section.

SECCIÓN 4TA.: RESISTANCE, DISOBEDIENCE, CONTEMPT AND OTHER OFFENSES COMMITTED AGAINST PUBLIC AUTHORITY.

###### PÁRRAFO I: REBELLION

###### Art. 209

Acts of rebellion are classified, according to the circumstances that accompany them, as a crime or offense of rebellion. There is rebellion in the assault, resistance, violence or acts of force exercised against public employees and officials, their agents, delegates, or representatives, whatever their rank and the class to which they belong, when they act in the exercise of their functions, and whatever the public function they exercise.

###### Art. 210.- (Modified by Laws 224 of June 26, 1984 and 46-99 of May 20, 1999)

The assault or resistance carried out by more than twenty armed persons shall result in the imposition on the guilty parties of the penalty of minor imprisonment, reducing it to correctional imprisonment if it was executed without weapons.

###### Art. 211

Rebellion committed by a number of three to twenty persons shall be punished with imprisonment of six months to two years, reducing the penalty to three months to one year of imprisonment if the guilty parties were not armed.

###### Art. 212

Rebellion committed by one or two armed persons shall be punished with imprisonment of six months to two years, and with the same penalty of six days to six months if they executed it without arms.

###### Art. 213

In case of banding together or tumultuous assembly, the penalty indicated in article 100 of this code shall be imposed on the rebels who do not exercise functions or employment in the band, provided that they have withdrawn at the first summons of the public authority, or that they withdrew afterwards, and that they have not been arrested at the place of the rebellion, but outside of it, without new resistance and without arms.

###### Art. 214

Any gathering of individuals that has as its object the commission of a crime or an offense is deemed an armed gathering if two or more among them are carriers of visible weapons.

###### Art. 215

Persons who are found provided with concealed weapons, and who have formed part of a mob or gathering that is not deemed armed, shall be individually punished as if they had formed part of an armed mob or gathering.

###### Art. 216

Those who on the occasion of a rebellion, or while it lasts, become guilty of common crimes and offenses, shall be punished with the penalties that the code assigns to each of those crimes or offenses, provided that they are more serious than those assigned for rebellion.

###### Art. 217

Any person who by speeches, posters, libels, writings or by any other means of publicity has provoked rebellion shall be considered guilty of rebellion and punished as such. If the rebellion does not take place, the provoker shall be punished with imprisonment of six days to one year.

###### Art. 218

Whenever the law imposes on the offense of rebellion only the penalty of correctional imprisonment, the guilty parties, in those cases, may be sentenced accessorily to a fine* of ten to one hundred pesos.

###### Art. 219

Gatherings that are formed with or without arms by workers or day laborers of manufactures or workshops, mines or agricultural establishments; those formed by individuals admitted to hospices, or by prisoners, prosecuted persons, accused or convicted persons, shall be considered and classified in the same category as gatherings of rebels, when their object is to force or threaten the administrative authority, police officers or agents or the public force.

###### Art. 220

Prosecuted, accused or convicted persons for common offenses who become guilty of rebellion shall suffer the penalty imposed on them for this offense after having served the sentence that motivated their imprisonment; or if they are discharged from the accusation, they shall suffer it after the acquittal sentence becomes irrevocable.

###### Art. 221

The leaders, provokers and instigators of a rebellion may be sentenced accessorily to subjection to the surveillance of the high police, from one to five years, which shall be counted from the day they complete their sentence.

###### PÁRRAFO III: DENEGACIÓN DE SERVICIOS LEGAL-MENTE DEBIDOS

###### Art. 234

Those in charge and depositaries of the public force who, legally required by civil authority, refuse to provide the assistance of the force they have under their command, shall be punished with imprisonment of one to three months.

###### Art. 235

They shall also be sentenced to the indemnifications that may be decreed, in accordance with article 10 of the present code.

###### Art. 236

Witnesses who, to exempt themselves from the duties that rest upon them, allege a cause whose falsity is known, shall be sentenced to correctional imprisonment of six days to two months; without prejudice to the fine* to which they become liable for their non-appearance.

###### PARAGRAPH IV: ESCAPE OF PRISONERS AND CONCEALMENT OF CRIMINALS

###### Art. 237

Those in charge of the custody of prisoners, bailiffs, superior or subordinate chiefs of the police or public force, to whom is entrusted the escort for the conveyance, transfer or custody of prisoners; those to whom is entrusted the surveillance of posts, jails or prisons, shall be condemned, in case of escape of the prisoners entrusted to their care, according to the distinctions established by the following articles.

###### Art. 238

If the escaped prisoner were accused of police offenses, or those that only merit simply infamous penalties, or if he were a prisoner of war, those in charge of his conveyance or custody, who were only guilty of negligence, shall be punished with correctional imprisonment of six days to two months. If there has been connivance between the escapee and his custodian, the penalty shall be six months to two years of imprisonment. Those who, being in charge of the custody or conveyance of the prisoner, shall have procured or facilitated his escape, shall be subject to the penalty of six days to three months of imprisonment.

###### Art. 239.- (Modified by Laws 224 of June 26, 1984 and 46-99 of May 20, 1999)

If the escaped prisoners, or any of them, were under the weight of a conviction to temporary afflictive penalty, or accused of a crime that merits that penalty, those in charge of their custody or conveyance shall be punished with imprisonment of two to six months, if the escape were a consequence of their carelessness; and in case of connivance, they shall be imposed the penalty of minor reclusion. ###### Art. 240

If the escapees or any of them was condemned to thirty years of major reclusion or to major reclusion, or if he was accused of crimes that merited said penalties, their guardians or conveyors shall be punished, in case of carelessness, with imprisonment from one to two years, and in that of connivance, they shall be punished with detention. ###### Art. 248

Those who conceal or cause to be concealed those guilty of crimes whose penalty is afflictive, shall suffer correctional imprisonment of three months to two years, if at the time of concealment they had knowledge of the crime committed. ###### PARAGRAPH V

Guardians of objects sealed by order of the Government, or judicial mandate, shall be punished with correctional imprisonment of six days to six months, when through their carelessness said seals are broken or breached.

###### Art. 251

Whoever intentionally breaks or attempts to break the seals affixed on papers or effects of the kind enumerated in the preceding article, or whoever has participated in the breaking of the seals or in the attempt of said breaking, shall be punished with imprisonment of one to two years. If it is the guardian himself who has broken the seals or committed the attempt to break them, he shall be sentenced to two years of imprisonment. In both cases, the guilty party shall be sentenced to a fine* of ten to one hundred pesos. He may also be deprived of the rights mentioned in article 42 of this code, for at least one year and at most five, counting from the day on which he has served his sentence; and may also be placed under the surveillance of the high police, for the same number of years.

###### Art. 252

However, if the guilty party is the guardian of the seals, the penalty shall be six months to two years of imprisonment.

###### Art. 253

Thefts and misappropriations committed by breaking seals shall be considered as thefts committed with breaking and entering.

SECCIÓN 6TA.:
CRIMES COMMITTED BY MEANS OF WRITINGS, IMAGES OR ENGRAVINGS DISTRIBUTED WITHOUT THE NAME OF THE AUTHOR, PRINTER OR ENGRAVER

###### Art. 283

Any publication or distribution of works, writings, notices, bulletins, announcements, newspapers, periodicals or other printed materials, in which the indication of the true name, profession and residence of the author and printer is not found, shall give rise, by this fact alone, to the punishment with imprisonment of six days to six months of any person who, knowingly, has contributed to said publication or distribution.

###### Art. 284

The penalty established in the preceding article shall be reduced to simple police penalties: 1o. with respect to criers, sellers, distributors or posters who denounce the person from whom they received the work or the printed writing; 2o. with respect to any person among them who has denounced the printer; 3o. with respect to the printer who has denounced the author.

###### Art. 285

If in the writing one or more persons are provoked or incited to commit crimes or offenses, those in charge of its

sale, distribution, announcement or posting on corners or public places, shall be punished with the same penalties imposed on the author, unless they reveal who he is; in which case they shall only incur the penalty of six days to three months of correctional imprisonment. Liability as an accomplice shall only be required of those who have concealed the names of the persons from whom they received the printed writing. The same penalties shall be imposed on the printer if he is known.

###### Art. 286

In all the cases previously stated, the confiscation of the seized copies shall be ordered.

###### Art. 287

The exhibition or distribution of songs, pamphlets, figures or images contrary to morality and good customs, shall be punished with a fine* of sixteen to one hundred pesos, and correctional imprisonment of one month to one year; the plates and printed or engraved copies of the songs and other objects of the crime shall be confiscated.

###### Art. 288

The imprisonment and fine** imposed by the preceding article shall be reduced to simple police penalties with respect to persons who sell, cry out or distribute the copies, if they discover the one who delivered the object of the crime to them. The same reduction shall be made with respect to those who make known the printer or engraver, who denounces the author or the person who commissioned the printing or engraving.

###### Art. 289

In all cases provided for in this section, the maximum penalty established for the crime of which he has been guilty shall be imposed on the author, when he is known.

###### Art. 290

The preceding provisions do not in any way alter, modify or repeal those which in the body of this code or other laws, punish provocations and complicity resulting from acts other than those provided for in this section.

###### PARAGRAPH I: HOMICIDE, MURDER, PARRICIDE, INFANTICIDE AND POISONING

###### Art. 295.- Whoever voluntarily kills another, becomes guilty of homicide

###### Art. 296

Homicide committed with premeditation or ambush is classified as murder.

###### Art. 297

Premeditation consists of the design formed before the action, to attack the person of a determined individual, or that of the one who is or may be found, even when that design depends on some circumstance or condition.

###### Art. 298

Ambush consists of waiting, for more or less time, in one or several places, for any individual, with the purpose of killing him, or of exercising acts of violence against him.

###### Art. 299

Whoever kills his legitimate, natural or adoptive father or mother, or his legitimate ascendants, becomes guilty of parricide.

###### Art. 300.- Whoever kills a newborn child, becomes guilty of infanticide

###### Art. 301

The attack against the life of a person, committed by means of substances that can produce death with

more or less promptness, is classified as poisoning, whatever the manner of administering or employing those substances, and whatever their consequences.

###### Art. 302

(Modified by Laws 64 of November 19, 1924 G.O. 3596; 224 of June 26, 1984 and 46-99 of May 20, 1999). Those guilty of murder, parricide, infanticide and poisoning shall be punished with the penalty of thirty years of major imprisonment.

###### Art. 303.- (Modified by Law 24-97 dated January 28, 1997 G.O. 9945)

The application of substances or methods tending to annul the personality or will of persons or to diminish their physical or mental capacity also constitutes torture or act of barbarity, even when they do not cause physical pain or psychic suffering.

###### Art. 303-1

(Added by Law 24-97 of January 28, 1997 G.O. 9945 and modified by Law 46-99 of May 20, 1999). The act of subjecting a person to torture or acts of barbarity is punished with major imprisonment of ten to fifteen years.

###### PARAGRAPH II: THREATS

###### Art. 305

(Modified by Laws 64 of November 19, 1924 G.O. 3596; 224 of June 26, 1984 and 46-99 of May 20, 1999). The threat that, by anonymous or signed writing, is made to murder, poison or attack in any manner whatsoever, against an individual, shall be punished with detention, when the penalty indicated for the consummated offense is that of thirty years of major imprisonment, or major imprisonment, provided that said threat is accompanied by the circumstance of having demanded the deposit or delivery of some sum in a determined place, or the fulfillment of any condition whatsoever. In this case, as in the previous articles, the guilty party shall be subjected to high police surveillance.

###### Art. 308

###### Art. 330.- (Amended by Law 24-97 of January 28, 1997 G.O. 9945). Any sexual action committed with violence, constraint, threat, surprise, or deceit constitutes a sexual assault.

###### Art. 331

(Amended by Laws 24-97 of January 28, 1997 G.O. 9945 and 46-99 of May 20, 1999). Any act of sexual penetration, of whatever nature, committed against a person by means of violence, constraint, threat or surprise constitutes rape.

The same penalty shall be imposed on any person who engages in non-consensual sexual activity in a relationship, in any of the following cases: a) Through the use of force, violence, intimidation or threat; b) If their capacity to resist has been nullified without their consent by any means; c) When due to illness or mental incapacity, temporary or permanent, the victim is unable to understand the nature of the act at the time of its commission; d) When their partner is compelled or induced with physical or psychological violence to participate or become involved in an unwanted sexual relationship with third parties.

SECTION 5TH: ILLEGAL DETENTION AND CONFINEMENT

###### Art. 341.- (Modified by Laws 224 of June 26, 1984 and 46-99 of May 20, 1999)

Those guilty of illegal confinement and detention, and as such, subject to the penalty of minor imprisonment, are: 1o. those who without order of constituted authority and outside the cases in which the law permits the arrest of the accused, arrest, detain or confine one or more persons; 2o. those who provide the place for the detention or confinement to take place; 3o. those who in any way help to carry out the detention or confinement.

###### PARAGRAPH I: FALSE TESTIMONY

###### Art. 361.- (Modified by Executive Order 202 of August 28, 1918, G.O. 2939-A)

1.	Perjury is the affirmation of a false fact, under oath or promise to tell the truth; whether when testifying before any Court, Judge, official or other person competent to receive the oath or promise; whether in any document signed by the person making the declaration, in any civil or criminal proceeding, in any case where the law requires or admits the oath or promise;  
2.	Accomplices to perjury are those who by threats, promises, persuasion, inducement, supplication or gifts have obtained that another person commit perjury;  
3.	Perjury is committed even in the case that the oath or promise is irregular due to defects of form;  
4.	(Modified by Laws 224 of June 26, 1984 and 46-99 of May 20, 1999). Perjury shall be punished with the penalties and according to the following distinctions:
a)	When as a consequence of perjury an accused person has been sentenced to thirty years of major imprisonment, and the sentence has been executed, the maximum of major imprisonment shall be imposed on the author of the perjury.  
b)	Outside the case provided in the preceding paragraph, whenever as a consequence of perjury the accused has suffered totally or partially a criminal or correctional penalty, the same penalty shall be imposed on the author of the perjury.  
c)	When the accused sentenced as a consequence of perjury has not suffered totally or partially the penalty imposed, six months of correctional imprisonment or a fine* of not less than one hundred pesos (RD$100.00) nor more than one thousand pesos (RD$1,000.00) or both penalties at once shall be applied to the author of the perjury.  
d)	Any other case that is not among those provided in the preceding paragraphs shall be punished with a fine** of fifty pesos (RD$50.00) to ten thousand pesos (RD$10,000.00); or correctional imprisonment of one month to two years, or both penalties at once.  
e)	The accomplice or accomplices to perjury shall be imposed the same penalty as the author of the perjury.  
5.	Article 463 of the Penal Code is not applicable to cases of perjury, neither with respect to the authors nor to the accomplices;  
6.	Any law or part of a law that is contrary to the provisions of this order is repealed.

###### Art. 390

Inhabited houses are considered to be buildings, dwellings, huts, hovels, even movable ones, which, even if not currently inhabited, are intended for habitation. Also considered inhabited places are the dependencies, such as courtyards, corrals, granaries, stables and other buildings that are enclosed within them, whatever the use to which they are destined, and even when they have a particular enclosure within the general fence or circuit.

###### Art. 391

Also considered as dependencies of an inhabited house are the corrals, pigsties and pigpens, intended to enclose large or small livestock, whatever the material of which they are constructed, when they depend on huts or other shelter places for the guardians of said livestock.

###### Art. 392

Enclosed land is considered to be land surrounded by ditches, stakes, brambles, boarding, palisades, living or dead hedges, or walls, whatever the nature of the materials used in their construction, and also whatever their height and depth, and their state of deterioration or age, and even if there is no door that closes with a key or otherwise, or even if the door is a screen or is habitually open.

###### Art. 393

Breaking is defined as the forcing, breaking, deterioration or demolition of walls, roofs, floors, mezzanines, doors, windows, bolts, padlocks or other utensils or instruments that serve to close or prevent passage. Also classified as breaking is that of any other type of enclosure, whatever it may be.

###### Art. 394.- Breaking may be exterior or interior

###### Art. 395

Exterior breaking is that which an individual uses to penetrate houses, courtyards, enclosures or their dependencies, or dwellings or other inhabited places; and interior breaking is that which, after the culprit penetrates the places mentioned in the preceding paragraph, is done to interior doors, windows or hedges, as well as that which has the purpose of opening cabinets and other closed furniture.

###### Art. 396

Included in the categories of interior breaking is: the simple theft of boxes, small boxes, bundles arranged with packaging, and other closed furniture containing effects, whatever they may be, and even if the breaking does not occur in the place where the theft was committed.

###### Art. 397

Climbing is defined as: entry into houses, courtyards, gardens, corrals and other enclosed buildings, effected over walls, doors or roofs, or by overcoming any other fence. One who enters through underground passages, which have not been established to serve as an entrance, is assimilated to the person guilty of theft by climbing.

###### Art. 398

False keys are and are deemed to be: hooks, picklock, skeleton keys and any others; and other instruments that the culprit uses to open the bolts, padlocks or locks of doors, windows, cabinets and other closed furniture, when those are not the ones that the owner, guest or tenant used for that purpose.

GENERAL PROVISIONS

###### Art. 463.- (Modified by Laws 224 of June 26, 1984 and 46-99 of May 20, 1999)

When there are mitigating circumstances in favor of the accused, the courts shall modify the penalties according to the following scale:
1o. (Implicitly modified by Article 8 of the Constitution of the Republic). When the law pronounces the penalty of thirty years of major imprisonment, the maximum penalty of major imprisonment shall be imposed. However, if it concerns crimes against the internal or external security of the State, the criminal court by its sentence of conviction shall place the convicts at the disposal of the Government, so that they may be banished or expelled from the territory;  
2o.  When the penalty of the law is the maximum of major imprisonment, a penalty of three to ten years of said imprisonment shall be imposed, and even minor imprisonment, if there are more than two mitigating circumstances in favor of the convict;  
3o. (Modified by Law 5901 of May 14, 1962). When the Law imposes on the offense major imprisonment that is not the maximum, the courts may reduce the penalty to minor imprisonment, or correctional imprisonment the duration of which may not be less than one year, unless the law permits a reduction of imprisonment to a shorter time;  
4o. (Implicitly modified by Article 8 of the Constitution of the Republic). When the penalty is minor imprisonment, detention, banishment or civic degradation, the courts shall impose

correctional imprisonment, without the minimum duration of the penalty being able to fall below two months;  
5o. When the code pronounces the maximum of an afflictive penalty, and there are mitigating circumstances in favor of the convict, the courts shall apply the minimum of the penalty, and may even impose the lower one to the degree they deem appropriate;  
6o. When the code simultaneously pronounces the penalties of imprisonment and fine, the correctional courts, in the event that mitigating circumstances exist, are authorized to reduce the time of imprisonment to less than six days, and the fine* to less than five pesos, even in the case of recidivism. They may also impose one or the other of the penalties referred to in this paragraph, and even substitute the penalty of imprisonment with that of a fine, without in any case being able to impose penalties lower than those of simple police.

FOURTH BOOK: POLICE CONTRAVENTIONS AND THEIR PENALTIES

#### CHAPTER I

ON PENALTIES

###### Art. 464

The penalties in police matters are: arrest, fine* and confiscation of certain seized objects.

###### Art. 465

Arrest for police contraventions is from one to five days, according to the cases and distinctions that shall be established below. Days of arrest consist of twenty-four hours.

###### Art. 466

Fines for police contraventions shall be imposed from one to five pesos inclusive, according to the cases and distinctions established below.

###### Art. 467

The proceeds of fines shall be deposited in the communal treasury of the place where the contravention was committed.

###### Art. 468

When the property of the convicted person is not sufficient to cover all the convictions that are pronounced, payment of restitutions and compensation owed to the aggrieved party shall have preference over the fine.

###### Art. 469

Payment of restitutions shall be demanded even by way of enforcement, and the convicted person shall remain in prison until full payment.

###### Art. 470

Police courts shall also pronounce, in cases determined by law, the confiscation of useful things and instruments intended for, produced by or taken in contravention.

Dated January 5, 2007, promulgated by the executive power on 01/24/2007 and published in O.G. no. 10409, which establishes that fines or pecuniary sanctions for different infractions, whether crimes or offenses, whose amount is less than one-third of the minimum wage of the public sector, shall be raised to said amount, and also raises contraventions to the amount between one-fifth and one-third of said wage

THE NATIONAL CONGRESS
In the Name of the Republic

WHEREAS FIRST: That the amount of fines contained in our Penal Code and special laws in force respond to economic and social realities different from the current one;

WHEREAS SECOND: That the operational costs involved in collecting such fines are so high that the effectiveness of these penalties has become irrelevant and out of proportion;

WHEREAS THIRD: That the Executive Power, by means of Decree No. 528-05, dated the twentieth (20th) day of September of the year two thousand five (2005), declared of national interest the installation of the New Penitentiary Model in the Dominican Republic and consequently the establishment of a new management and administration system in the Correction and Rehabilitation Centers, as well as control and security in charge of the new Penitentiary Treatment Guards (VTP), which would allow a greater possibility of reintegration into society and constitutes an effective policy in crime prevention;

WHEREAS FOURTH: That the New Penitentiary Model imposes on the Attorney General's Office commitments that entail expenditure of funds exceeding the allocated appropriations;

WHEREAS FIFTH: That in order to guarantee the rights inherent to human beings confined in the modern Correction and Rehabilitation Centers into which Dominican prisons must be transformed, a greater concentration of both economic and human resources is necessary for their strengthening and development;

WHEREAS SIXTH: That what concerns the improvement of the prison system benefits Dominican society;

WHEREAS SEVENTH: That the penalty of a fine has a deterrent character and constitutes a fiscal measure that aspires to provide the State with resources for the combat and prevention of crime.
