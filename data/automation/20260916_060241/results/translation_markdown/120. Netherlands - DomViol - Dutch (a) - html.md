Original title and preamble
Act of 9 October 2008, containing rules aimed at imposing a temporary
restraining order on persons who pose a serious threat of domestic violence
(Temporary restraining order act)
We Beatrix, by the grace of God, Queen of the Netherlands, Princess of Orange-Nassau,
etc. etc. etc.  
All who shall see or hear these read, greetings! make known:
Whereas We have taken into consideration that it is desirable to introduce a measure
by which a person who poses a serious and immediate danger to the safety of one or more persons with whom he shares a household can temporarily be imposed a
restraining order in order to safeguard the safety of these persons and to create a period in which measures can be taken to eliminate the threat
of domestic violence;  
Such is it that We, having heard the Council of State, and in consultation with the States General,
have approved and understood, as We approve and understand by this:

## Article 1

In this act the following is understood by:  
a.  
police officer:
the police officer referred to in

## article 2, under a, of the Police Act 2012

, and the police officer referred to in article 2, under c, of that act, insofar as
he is charged with the execution of the police task;  
b.  
restraining order:
decision containing an order to immediately leave a certain dwelling
and a prohibition to enter, stay at or be present in that dwelling
and a prohibition to make contact with those who live with the person to whom the decision
is addressed in the same dwelling or who stay there other than incidentally;  
c.  
evicted person:
the person to whom a restraining order has been imposed.

## Article 2

1
The mayor may impose a restraining order on a person if facts or circumstances
show that his presence in the dwelling poses a serious and immediate danger
to the safety of one or more persons who live with him in the dwelling or who stay there
other than incidentally or if on the basis of facts or circumstances
a serious suspicion of this danger exists. The prohibition applies for a period of
ten days, subject to extension in accordance with

## article 9

. By or pursuant to general administrative order further rules are laid down regarding
the nature of the facts and circumstances that may give rise to imposing a restraining order
.  
2
A restraining order may only be imposed on an adult person.  
3
If the mayor intends to impose the restraining order due to child abuse
or a serious suspicion thereof, he contacts the advice and reporting centre
for domestic violence and child abuse referred to in

## article 4.1.1 of the Social Support Act 2015

in order to consult about the intention to impose a restraining order.  
4
The restraining order contains in any case:  
a.  
a description of the place and the duration for which it applies;  
b.  
the facts and circumstances that have given rise to imposing the
restraining order, and
c.  
the names of the persons with respect to whom the prohibition to make contact applies.  
5
By ministerial regulation further rules may be given with respect to the
restraining order.  
6
The evicted person indicates where or in what manner he can be reached. If the
evicted person cannot communicate this immediately, he communicates this within 24 hours after
the restraining order has been imposed to the mayor.  
7
If the situation is so urgent that the restraining order cannot be put in writing beforehand
, the restraining order may be announced orally. The mayor
ensures subsequent prompt writing and notification. If the place of residence
of the evicted person is not known, notification may take place by depositing
the restraining order at the municipal secretariat.  
8
The mayor communicates without delay the content of the restraining order and the consequences of non-compliance
thereof for the evicted person to the person with whom the evicted person shares a
household. The mayor also communicates the content of the restraining order to
the institution designated by the mayor for advice or assistance, and if
the restraining order is imposed due to child abuse or a serious suspicion thereof
to the foundation referred to in the third paragraph.  
9
The mayor may in any case withdraw the restraining order if the evicted person
has accepted an offer of assistance and this has been confirmed by the institution for advice or
assistance designated pursuant to the eighth paragraph, and this acceptance
also means that the evicted person will not obstruct assistance to one or more persons who
live with the evicted person in the dwelling or who stay there other than incidentally
and will cooperate if this is requested of him by the institution
for advice or assistance.

## Article 4

1
If the danger referred to in

## artikel 2, eerste lid

, occurs, the mayor or a police officer is authorised, with a view to imposing the house ban, to enter the dwelling without the consent of the resident, insofar as this is reasonably necessary for the performance of his duties.  
2
The police officer demands from the person removed from the house the house key or house keys and is authorised, if necessary after having searched the person removed from the house for this purpose on his clothing, to take these from the person removed from the house against issue of a receipt of acknowledgement.  
3
For the purpose of monitoring compliance with the house ban, police officers are authorised to enter any place where the person removed from the house may not be present on the basis of the house ban, with the exception of a dwelling without the consent of the resident.

1
If a request for interim relief as referred to in

## artikel 8:81 van de Algemene wet bestuursrecht

, is made against the house ban:  
a.  
in derogation from

## artikel 8:83, eerste lid, van die wet

, the person removed from the house is heard by the preliminary relief judge within three days;  
b.  
the preliminary relief judge gives judgment in derogation from

## artikel 8:84, eerste lid, van die wet

immediately after hearing the parties unless there are special circumstances, in which case the preliminary relief judge gives judgment within 24 hours after the hearing of the case.  
2
The court also takes into account in the assessment of the house ban the facts and circumstances that occurred after the imposition of the house ban.  
3
In derogation from
artikelen 8:41, eerste lid
, and
8:82, eerste lid, van de Algemene wet bestuursrecht

The court gives minors who belong to the household of the person removed from the house and who have reached the age of twelve years the opportunity to make their opinion known to him, unless the expeditious handling of the case precludes this.  
He may give minors who have not yet reached the age of twelve years the opportunity to make their opinion known to him in a manner to be determined by him.

## Artikel 8

1
The clerk sends a copy of the judgment or of the minutes of the oral judgment of the preliminary relief judge without delay, and a copy of the judgment or of the minutes of the oral judgment of the court within two weeks after the date of the judgment free of charge to:  
a.  
the spouse, registered partner, other cohabitant or other adult person with whom the person removed from the house shares the household;  
b.  
the police of the municipality where the house ban was imposed;  
c.  
if minors are involved: the advice and reporting centre for domestic violence and child abuse, referred to in

## artikel 4.1.1 van de Wet maatschappelijke ondersteuning 2015

.  
2
The clerk informs the person removed from the house, the lawyer of the person removed from the house and the mayor orally of the judgment without delay. The mayor informs the persons referred to in the first paragraph, under a, insofar as they were not present at the hearing at which the judgment was given, and the police orally of the judgment without delay.
