LAW AGAINST FEMICIDE AND OTHER FORMS OF VIOLENCE AGAINST WOMEN

DECREE NUMBER 22-2008

THE CONGRESS OF THE REPUBLIC OF GUATEMALA

WHEREAS:

The Political Constitution of the Republic establishes that the State of Guatemala is organized to protect persons and the family, its supreme purpose is the realization of the common good, in addition to protecting human life from its conception, as well as the integrity and security of persons.  

WHEREAS:
Guatemala approved through Decree Law Number 49-82 the Convention on the Elimination of All Forms of Discrimination Against Women, and through Decree Number 69-94 the Inter-American Convention to Prevent, Punish and Eradicate Violence against Women, and as a State Party it obligated itself to adopt all appropriate measures to modify or repeal laws, regulations, customs and practices that constitute discrimination against women, and to issue all those laws that are necessary for its purpose.  

WHEREAS:
Guatemalan women have the right to recognition, enjoyment, exercise and protection of all human rights and freedoms enshrined in the Political Constitution of the Republic and international instruments on human rights, and that the problem of violence and discrimination against women, girls and adolescents that has prevailed in the country has been aggravated by murder and impunity, due to the unequal power relations existing between men and women, in the social, economic, legal, political, cultural and family spheres, for which reason a law of prevention and penalization is necessary.  

THEREFORE:
In exercise of the powers conferred upon it by literal a) of article 171 of the Political Constitution of the Republic of Guatemala,

DECREES:

The following:

LAW AGAINST FEMICIDE AND OTHER FORMS OF VIOLENCE AGAINST WOMEN

## CHAPTER I GENERAL PART

GENERAL PROVISIONS

### Article 1

Object and purpose of the law. The purpose of this law is to guarantee the life, liberty, integrity, dignity, protection and equality of all women before the law, and of the law, particularly when by reason of gender, in relations of power or trust, in the public or private sphere the aggressor commits against them discriminatory practices, physical, psychological, economic violence or contempt for their rights.  

The purpose is to promote and implement provisions aimed at the eradication of physical, psychological, sexual, economic violence or any type of coercion against women, guaranteeing them a life free of violence, as stipulated in the Political Constitution of the Republic and international instruments on women's human rights ratified by Guatemala.

### Article 2

Applicability. This law shall be applied when a woman's right to a life free of violence in its different manifestations is violated, both in the public and private spheres.

## CHAPTER II DEFINITIONS

### Article 3

Definitions. For the purposes of this law, the following shall be understood as:

a. Access to information: It is the right of women victims of violence to receive full information and adequate counseling for their personal situation, through the services, agencies or offices that may be made available by competent institutions, both public and private. Such information shall include the measures contemplated in this law, relating to their protection and safety, and the rights and assistance provided therein, as well as that referring to the place where care, emergency, support and comprehensive recovery services are provided.

b. Private sphere: It comprises domestic, family or trust interpersonal relationships within which acts of violence against women are committed, when the aggressor is the spouse, ex-spouse, cohabitant or ex-cohabitant, with whom the victim has procreated or not, the aggressor is the boyfriend or ex-boyfriend, or a relative of the victim.

c. Also included in this sphere are relationships between the spouse, ex-spouse, cohabitant or ex-cohabitant, boyfriend or ex-boyfriend of a woman with her daughters.

d. Public sphere: It comprises interpersonal relationships that take place in the community and that include the social, labor, educational, religious sphere or any other type of relationship that is not included in the private sphere.

e. Comprehensive assistance: Women victims of violence, their daughters and sons, have the right to social services of care, emergency, support, shelter, as well as recovery. Multidisciplinary care shall especially involve:

1. Medical and psychological care.

2. Social support.

3. Legal follow-up of claims for women's rights.

4. Support for training and labor insertion.

5. Assistance of an interpreter.

e. Femicide: Violent death of a woman, occurring in the context of unequal power relations between men and women, in the exercise of gender power against women.

f. Misogyny: Hatred, contempt or underestimation of women for the sole fact of being women.

g. Power relations: Manifestations of control or domination that lead to the submission of women and discrimination against them.

h. Reparation to the victim: Reparation shall be understood as the set of measures tending to bring the victim's situation closer to the state in which she would have been had the criminal act not occurred.

i. Reparation must be characterized by its comprehensiveness and includes, in addition to compensation of an economic nature, all those measures tending to provide the victim with medical, psychological, moral and social reparation.

j. Victim: It is a woman of any age who is inflicted with any type of violence.

k. Violence against women: Any action or omission based on belonging to the female sex that results in immediate or subsequent harm, physical, sexual, economic or psychological suffering for women, as well as threats of such acts, coercion or arbitrary deprivation of liberty, whether it occurs in the public sphere or in the private sphere.

l. Economic violence: Actions or omissions that affect the use, enjoyment, availability or accessibility of a woman to material goods that belong to her by right, by marital bond or de facto union, by capacity or by inheritance, causing deterioration, damage, transformation, subtraction, destruction, retention or loss of objects or material goods of her own or of the family group, as well as the retention of work instruments, personal documents, goods, valuables, rights or economic resources.

m. Physical violence: Acts of aggression in which direct bodily force is used or by means of any object, weapon or substance with which harm, physical suffering, injuries or illness is caused to a woman.

n. Psychological or emotional violence: Actions that can produce psychological or emotional harm or suffering to a woman, her daughters or her sons, as well as actions, threats or violence against daughters, sons or other family members up to the fourth degree of consanguinity and second degree of affinity of the victim, in both cases with the purpose of intimidating her, undermining her self-esteem or controlling her, who subjected to that emotional climate may suffer a progressive psychological weakening with depressive conditions.

o. Sexual violence: Actions of physical or psychological violence whose purpose is to violate the freedom and sexual indemnity of women, including sexual humiliation, forced prostitution and the denial of the right to use family planning methods, both natural and artificial, or to adopt protective measures against sexually transmitted diseases.

## CHAPTER III

PREVENTIVE MEASURES

### Article 4

Interinstitutional coordination. The State of Guatemala, through the governing body for policies related to the prevention and eradication of violence against women, shall be responsible for interinstitutional coordination, the promotion and monitoring of awareness campaigns and the generation of spaces for discussion for the agreement and promotion of public policies for the prevention of violence against women and femicide, which are considered of national urgency and of social interest, in congruence with the international commitments signed and ratified on the matter.

## CHAPTER IV OFFENSES AND PENALTIES

### Article 5

Public action. The offenses defined in this law are subject to public action.

### Article 6

Femicide. The offense of femicide is committed by whoever, within the framework of unequal power relations between men and women, causes the death of a woman, because of her condition as a woman, making use of any of the following circumstances:

a. Having unsuccessfully attempted to establish or reestablish a relationship of partnership or intimacy with the victim.

b. Maintaining at the time the act is perpetrated, or having maintained with the victim family, marital, cohabitation, intimacy or courtship relations, friendship, companionship or employment relationship.

c. As a result of the repeated manifestation of violence against the victim.

d. As a result of group rituals using or not using weapons of any type.

e. In contempt of the victim's body to satisfy sexual instincts, or committing acts of genital mutilation or any other type of mutilation.

f. Due to misogyny.

g. When the act is committed in the presence of the victim's daughters or sons.

h. With the concurrence of any of the qualifying circumstances contemplated in article 132 of the Penal Code.

The person responsible for this offense shall be punished with imprisonment of twenty-five to fifty years, and may not be granted a reduction of the sentence for any reason. Persons prosecuted for the commission of this offense may not enjoy any substitute measure.

### Article 7

Violence against women. The offense of violence against women is committed by whoever, in the public or private sphere, exercises physical, sexual or psychological violence, making use of the following circumstances:

a. Having attempted, repeatedly or continuously, unsuccessfully, to establish or reestablish a relationship of partnership or intimacy with the victim.

b. Maintaining at the time the act is perpetrated, or having maintained with the victim family, marital, cohabitation, intimacy or courtship relations, friendship, companionship or employment, educational or religious relationship.

c. As a result of group rituals using or not using weapons of any type.

d. In contempt of the victim's body to satisfy sexual instincts, or committing acts of genital mutilation.

e. Due to misogyny.

The person responsible for the offense of physical or sexual violence against women shall be punished with imprisonment of five to twelve years, according to the gravity of the offense, without prejudice to the facts constituting other offenses stipulated in ordinary laws.

The person responsible for the offense of psychological violence against women shall be punished with imprisonment of five to eight years, according to the gravity of the offense, without prejudice to the facts constituting other offenses stipulated in ordinary laws.

### Article 8

Economic violence. Commits the crime of economic violence against women whoever, within the public or private sphere, engages in conduct comprised in any of the following circumstances:

Diminishes, limits or restricts the free disposal of her property or patrimonial or labor rights.

Compels the woman to sign documents that affect, limit, restrict her patrimony or put it at risk; or that exempt him from economic, criminal, civil or any other nature of responsibility.

Destroys or conceals documents proving ownership or personal identification, or property, personal objects, work instruments that are indispensable for her to execute her habitual activities.

Subjects the woman's will through economic abuse by not covering her basic needs and those of her daughters and sons.

Exercises psychological, sexual or physical violence against the woman, with the purpose of controlling income or the flow of monetary resources that enter the home.

The person responsible for this crime shall be punished with imprisonment of five to eight years, without prejudice to the facts constituting other crimes stipulated in ordinary laws.

### Article 9

Prohibition of justification grounds. In crimes typified against women, customs or cultural or religious traditions may not be invoked as grounds for justification or exculpation to perpetrate, inflict, consent to, promote, instigate or tolerate violence against women.

With the sole denunciation of the act of violence in the private sphere, the jurisdictional body that hears it must issue the security measures referred to in article 7 of the Law to Prevent, Punish and Eradicate Intrafamily Violence, being applicable to the woman who is a victim of the crimes established in this law, even when the aggressor is not her relative.

### Article 10

Aggravating circumstances. The circumstances that aggravate violence against women must be analyzed according to the following:

a)	In relation to the personal circumstances of the person who aggresses.

b)	In relation to the personal circumstances of the victim.

c)	In relation to the power relations existing between the victim and the person who aggresses.

d)	In relation to the context of the violent act and the damage produced to the victim.

e)	In relation to the means and mechanisms used to perpetrate the act and the damage produced.

## CHAPTER V REPARATIONS

### Article 11

Compensation to the victim. Reparation to the victim shall be proportional to the damage caused and the degree of culpability of the author of the crime. In no case shall it imply unjust enrichment of the victim.

Compensation may be decreed by the justice bodies that hear the specific case.

When the victim has died, the right to reparation extends to her successors, according to what is established in the Civil Code and Criminal Procedure Code.

### Artículo 12

State Responsibility. In compliance with the provisions established in the Political Constitution of the Republic of Guatemala and in the international conventions and treaties on human rights accepted and ratified by the State of Guatemala, the State shall be jointly and severally liable for the action or omission incurred by public officials who obstruct, delay or deny compliance with the sanctions provided for in this law, and may exercise against them the right of recourse if found liable, without prejudice to administrative or civil liabilities.

## CHAPTER VI STATE OBLIGATIONS

### Artículo 13

Rights of the victim. It is the obligation of the State to guarantee to women who are victims of any form of violence, the following rights:

a)	Access to information.  
b)	Comprehensive assistance.  

Officials who without justified cause deny or delay the delivery of information or comprehensive assistance to the detriment of the process or of the victim, shall be subject to labor and administrative measures and sanctions, without prejudice to civil or criminal liabilities as the case may be.

### Artículo 14

Strengthening of agencies responsible for criminal investigation. For the fulfillment of the provisions contained in this law, the Public Prosecutor's Office shall create the Prosecutor's Office for Crimes Against the Life and Physical Integrity of Women, specialized in the investigation of crimes created by this law, with the budgetary, physical, material, scientific and human resources that enable it to fulfill its purposes.

### Artículo 15

Creation of specialized jurisdictional bodies. The Supreme Court of Justice shall implement specialized jurisdictional bodies that shall have jurisdiction over the crimes established in this law, organizing their operation on a twenty-four (24) hour basis, without prejudice to the jurisdiction attributed to criminal courts.

### Artículo 16

Comprehensive Support Centers for Women Survivors of Violence. It is the obligation of the State to guarantee access, relevance, quality and financial, human and material resources for the operation of Comprehensive Support Centers for Women Survivors of Violence. It shall be the National Coordinator for the Prevention of Intrafamily Violence and Against Women - CONAPREVI - who shall promote their creation and provide accompaniment, advice and monitoring to specialized women's organizations that administer them.

### Artículo 17

Institutional strengthening. The National Coordinator for the Prevention of Intrafamily Violence and Against Women - CONAPREVI - is the coordinating, advisory and promoting entity for public policies aimed at reducing intrafamily violence and violence against women.  

It is the responsibility of the State to strengthen and institutionalize the already created instances for addressing the social problem of violence against women, to ensure their sustainability, among them: CONAPREVI, the Office of the Defender of Indigenous Women - DEMI -, the Presidential Secretariat for Women - SEPREM -, as well as the free legal assistance service to victims provided by the Institute of Public Criminal Defense. Likewise, the strengthening of other non-governmental organizations in the same sense shall be guaranteed.

### Artículo 18

Training for State officials. Within the framework of the execution of the National Plan for the Prevention and Eradication of Domestic Violence and Violence Against Women - PLANOVI -, the National Coordinator for the Prevention of Domestic Violence and Against Women - CONAPREVI - and other non-governmental organizations are responsible for advising, monitoring and following up on training and capacity-building processes on violence against women and with ethnic-cultural relevance directed at public officials, with special emphasis on justice operators.

### Artículo 19

Legal assistance to the victim. The State has the obligation to provide free legal assistance to the victim or their family members, and must provide them with the services of a public defender attorney, to guarantee the effective exercise of their rights.

### Artículo 20

National information system on violence against women. The National Institute of Statistics -INE- is obligated to generate, with the information that must be sent to it by the Judicial Branch, Public Prosecutor's Office, Attorney General's Office, Human Rights Ombudsman's Office, National Civil Police, Public Criminal Defense Institute, Popular Legal Clinics and any other institution that has knowledge of the crimes contemplated in this law, indicators and statistical information, and must create a National Information System on Violence Against Women. The referred entities must implement adequate mechanisms, according to their internal regulations, for the fulfillment of this obligation.

### Artículo 21

Budgetary allocations: The Ministry of Public Finance must allocate resources within the State Revenue and Expenditure Budget for the fulfillment of this law, in relation to the following aspects:

a)	Creation of the Prosecutor's Office for Crimes Against the Life and Physical Integrity of Women.  

b)	Strengthening of the National Institute of Forensic Sciences -INACIF-.  

c)	Creation of specialized jurisdictional bodies for hearing crimes against the life and physical integrity of women.  

d)	Strengthening and adequate functioning of the National Coordinator for the Prevention of Domestic Violence and Against Women -CONAPREVI-.  

e)	Implementation of the National Plan for the Prevention and Eradication of Domestic Violence and Violence Against Women -PLANOVI-.  

f)	Strengthening of the protection service for procedural subjects and persons linked to the administration of criminal justice.  

g)	Strengthening of the Public Criminal Defense Institute for the provision of free legal assistance services.

## CHAPTER VII FINAL AND TRANSITIONAL PROVISIONS

### Article 22

Transitional. While the Supreme Court of Justice implements the specialized jurisdictional bodies referred to in Article 15 of this law, the provisions established in the Criminal Procedure Code, Decree Number 51-92 of the Congress of the Republic and its amendments, and the Law of the Judicial Branch shall be observed. While the specialized courts are established, those determined by the Supreme Court of Justice shall have jurisdiction to hear cases under this law.

The specialized jurisdictional bodies referred to in Article 15 of this law must be established progressively within twelve months following the entry into force of this law, throughout the Republic.

### Article 23

Transitional. While the Public Prosecutor's Office has not implemented the Prosecutor's Office for Crimes Against the Life and Physical Integrity of Women provided for in Article 14 of this law, the Attorney General and Head of the Public Prosecutor's Office must determine, in accordance with the internal regulations of the Public Prosecutor's Office, which prosecutor's offices should hear such cases.

The prosecutor's office referred to in Article 14 of this law must be established within twelve months following the entry into force of this law.

### Article 24

Article 2 of Decree Number 70-96, Law for the Protection of Procedural Subjects and Persons Linked to the Administration of Criminal Justice, is amended to read as follows:

### Article 2

Purpose. The protection service has as its essential objective to provide protection to officials and employees of the Judicial Branch, civil security forces, and the Public Prosecutor's Office, as well as to witnesses, experts, consultants, adhesive complainants, women victims of violence, their daughters and sons, as well as other persons who are exposed to risks due to their involvement in criminal proceedings. It shall also provide coverage to journalists who need it because they are at risk due to the performance of their informative function.

### Article 25

Supplementary application. The provisions of Decree Number 17-73 Criminal Code; Decree Number 51-92, Criminal Procedure Code; Decree Number 2-89, Law of the Judicial Branch; Decree Number 97-96, Law on the Dignification and Comprehensive Promotion of Women; Decree Number 42-2001, Social Development Law; Decree-Law 106, Civil Code; Decree-Law 107, Civil and Commercial Procedure Code, as well as the modifications and amendments to all the aforementioned laws, are supplementarily applicable to this law.

### Article 26

Sources of interpretation. The provisions established in the Political Constitution of the Republic of Guatemala and in international conventions and treaties on human rights, accepted and ratified by the State of Guatemala, constitute sources of interpretation of this law. In particular, the following shall be sources of interpretation of this law:

The Convention on the Elimination of All Forms of Discrimination Against Women.

The Inter-American Convention to Prevent, Punish and Eradicate Violence Against Women.

### Article 27

Repeal. All legal or regulatory provisions that oppose or contravene the norms contained in this law are repealed.

### Artículo 28

Effective Date. This Decree shall enter into force eight days after its publication in the Official Gazette.

Second paragraph. Establishment of the Academy. The Academy of Mayan Languages of Guatemala shall be established within one year from the date this Decree is sanctioned by the Executive Branch and published in all the media of the country in accordance with the Official Gazette that publishes officially all that is ordered and everything that is convenient for this purpose. The Academy shall have the following functions:

To promote the knowledge of all the languages and cultures of the country.

To promote the will and spirit of the peoples of America for their preservation, development and growth of the languages and cultures of the country.

Third paragraph. Repeal. All provisions of laws that contradict or oppose what is established in this Decree are hereby repealed.

Fourth paragraph. Date of entry into force. This Decree shall enter into force eight days after its publication in the Official Gazette.
