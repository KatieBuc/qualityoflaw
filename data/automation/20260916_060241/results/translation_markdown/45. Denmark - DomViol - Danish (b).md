Act on restraining orders, exclusion zones and expulsion

WE MARGRETHE THE SECOND, by the Grace of God Queen of Denmark, make known: The Folketing has passed and We by Our consent have confirmed the following act:

## Chapter 1

Restraining orders and exclusion zones

### § 1

By restraining order, a person may be prohibited from approaching another by personal, oral or written contact, including by electronic communication, or otherwise contacting or following the other.

### § 2. A restraining order may be issued if

1)	there is reasonable suspicion that a person
a)	has violated another's peace by pursuing or harassing the other by contact etc. as stated in

### § 1 or

b)	has committed against the other a criminal offence that may be equated with such a violation of peace, and
2)	there are specific grounds to assume that the person concerned will continue to violate the other as stated in no. 1.  
Stk. 2. A restraining order may also be issued if
1)	there is reasonable suspicion that a person has committed a violation of the Criminal Code's provisions on homicide, robbery, deprivation of liberty, violence, arson, rape or other sexual offence or attempt at one of the mentioned offences, and
2)	the injured party or their next of kin, considering the seriousness of the offence, cannot be expected to tolerate contact etc. with the person concerned as stated in § 1.

### § 3

By exclusion zone, a person may be prohibited from staying or moving in a specifically defined area near another person's residence or place of work, education or stay or other area where that person frequently moves.

### § 4

An exclusion zone may be issued if the conditions for a restraining order under § 2 are met, when
1)	the suspicion concerns repeatedly repeated violation under § 2, subsection 1, no. 1, intentional violation of a restraining order under § 1 or an offence covered by § 2, subsection 2, or § 8, no. 1, and
2)	a restraining order under § 1 cannot be considered sufficient to protect the other person.

### § 5

A restraining order is issued for a specific period of up to 5 years. An exclusion zone is issued for a specific period of up to 1 year.

### § 6

A restraining order or exclusion zone may be extended to cover a member of the injured party's household if it is found necessary for the purpose of the restraining order or exclusion zone.

## Chapter 2

Expulsion

### § 7

By expulsion, a person over 18 years of age may be prohibited from staying in their home.

### § 8. A person may be expelled when

1)	there is reasonable suspicion that the person concerned has committed against a member of their household a violation of the Criminal Code's §§ 210, 213 or 266 or a violation covered by the Criminal Code's chapters 24-26 and which under the law may result in imprisonment for 1 year and 6 months, or that the person concerned has behaved in a manner that otherwise constitutes a threat of violence against a member of the household, and

2)	according to the information available, there are specific grounds to assume that the person concerned by remaining in the home will commit an offence mentioned in no. 1.

### § 9

The police may, in connection with the processing of a case concerning expulsion, detain a person who may reasonably be assumed to have behaved in a manner that constitutes a threat of violence against a member of the person's household.  
Stk. 2. For the detention, the rules on arrest in the Administration of Justice Act's §§ 755-759 and

### § 760

, subsection 1, otherwise apply. The detention may not be extended beyond 24 hours.

### § 10. Expulsion takes place for a specific period of up to 4 weeks

### § 11

At the latest simultaneously with a decision on expulsion, the police must notify the municipality of the case.  
Stk. 2. When notifying the municipality, information and documents in the case must also be sent, including electronically stored data, that are necessary for the social authorities' processing of the case.

## Chapter 3

Common provisions

### § 12

Restraining orders, exclusion zones and expulsion must not be disproportionate to the disruption thereby caused to the person's circumstances, the consideration for the person or persons whom the measure is to protect, and the nature of the conduct displayed by the person concerned.

### § 13

Restraining orders, exclusion zones and expulsion do not cover contact, stay or movement which for special reasons must be considered justified.

### § 14

Restraining orders, exclusion zones and expulsion may be decided when a person whom the measure is to protect requests it, or when general considerations require it.

### § 15

In cases concerning restraining orders, exclusion zones and expulsion, the police advise the parties on the rules in this act when there is reason to do so.  
Stk. 2. Decisions on restraining orders, exclusion zones and expulsion are made by the chief of police.  
Stk. 3. The Director of Public Prosecutions processes complaints concerning restraining orders, exclusion zones and expulsion. The Director of Public Prosecutions' decision cannot be appealed.

### § 16

Decisions on restraining orders, exclusion zones and expulsion must be served. When serving exclusion zones and expulsion, the person concerned must be advised of the right to require the decision brought before the court.

## Chapter 4

Judicial review

### § 17

The person who is given an exclusion zone or expelled may, within 14 days after the decision has been served on the person concerned, require that the police bring the decision before the district court.  
Stk. 2. The person who requires a decision on an exclusion zone or expulsion brought before the court (the complainant) must provide the police with an address and any telephone number to which summons to a possible court hearing may be communicated. Service of summons to a court hearing is not necessary.  
Stk. 3. The case must be brought before the district court as soon as possible and at the latest within 1 week after receipt of the request at the place where information in the case can most appropriately be assumed to be obtained. In cases concerning expulsion, however, bringing must take place within 24 hours.  
Stk. 4. A request for bringing before the court does not have suspensive effect.

### § 18

A lawyer is appointed for the person who requires a decision on expulsion brought before the court. A lawyer may be appointed for the person who requires a decision on an exclusion zone brought before the court if the court, according to the nature of the case, the nature of the intervention, the complainant's person and the circumstances otherwise, finds it desirable. The appointed lawyer has the same powers as a defence counsel in a criminal case. Concerning

fees and reimbursement for expenses to an appointed lawyer, the same rules apply as for appointed defence counsel, cf. the Administration of Justice Act's § 741.  
Stk. 2. A lawyer may be appointed for an injured party who is to give testimony in court. The rules in the Administration of Justice Act's §§ 741 c and 741 d apply correspondingly. Concerning fees and reimbursement for expenses to the appointed lawyer, the same rules apply as in cases where legal aid has been granted, cf. the Administration of Justice Act's chapter 31.

### § 19. The case is dealt with in writing or orally at the court's discretion

Stk. 2. If the case is dealt with orally, a court hearing must be held as soon as possible. If the request for bringing the case before the court is made upon service of a first-instance decision on expulsion, a court hearing must be held within 5 days.  
Stk. 3. The complainant has the right to be present at any court hearing and to make a statement, unless the court finds that this for special reasons is futile or harmful to the person concerned. The case may be dealt with even if the person concerned fails to appear. If a lawyer has been appointed pursuant to § 18, stk. 1, however, the case may only be dealt with if the appointed lawyer has appeared.  
Stk. 4. The court makes its decision on the basis of the case documents and any statement in court from the complainant. In special circumstances, the court may allow further presentation of evidence.  
Stk. 5. The court's decision is made by order. If the decision approves the intervention, the order shall state the specific circumstances of the case on which it is based that the conditions are met.  
Stk. 6. If the complainant is ordered to pay legal costs in a criminal case concerning matters that have given rise to the restraining order or expulsion, the judgment in the criminal case shall also order the person concerned to pay the costs of the case concerning the restraining order or expulsion. However, the court may exceptionally decide that these costs shall be paid in whole or in part by the Treasury if special circumstances warrant it.

### § 20. Otherwise, the Administration of Justice Act's rules on criminal procedure apply

Stk. 2. With regard to claims for compensation, § 1018 h of the Administration of Justice Act applies. With regard to detention pursuant to § 9, however, § 1018 a of the Administration of Justice Act applies when expulsion is not carried out or approved.

## Kapitel 5

Penal provisions

### § 21

Any person who intentionally violates a restraining order pursuant to § 1, a restraining order pursuant to § 3 or an expulsion pursuant to § 7 shall be liable to a fine or imprisonment for up to 2 years.  
Stk. 2. When determining the penalty pursuant to stk. 1, it shall be considered an aggravating circumstance that the conduct has formed part of systematic and persistent persecution or harassment.  
Stk. 3. Violation of stk. 1 is only prosecuted at the request of the aggrieved party, unless public interest requires it.

## Kapitel 7

Entry into force provisions, etc.

### § 25

The Act enters into force on 1 March 2012.  
Stk. 2. Act no. 449 of 9 June 2004 on expulsion and authority to issue restraining orders, etc. is repealed.

### § 26. The Act does not apply to the Faroe Islands

Stk. 2. The Act does not apply to Greenland, but with the exception of §§ 22 and 23 may be brought into force for Greenland by Royal Decree with such amendments as Greenlandic conditions warrant.
