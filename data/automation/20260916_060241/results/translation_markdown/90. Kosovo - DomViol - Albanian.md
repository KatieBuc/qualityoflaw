## CHAPTER I GENERAL PROVISIONS

### Article 1 Purpose

This law aims to prevent and address violence against women, domestic violence and gender-based violence, to protect and contribute to the elimination of all forms of discrimination against women, the promotion of gender equality and the empowerment of women as well as to build the necessary mechanisms for providing support to victims of all forms of violence regulated by this law.

### Article 2 Scope

1.	The provisions of this law are binding on all public and private institutions, individuals, civil society and other responsible professionals, to provide assistance, support, protection and preservation of the dignity of victims of all forms of violence, according to the provisions of this Law, other legislation in force in the Republic of Kosovo, as well as conventions and key international instruments directly applicable in the domestic legal system.

2.	This law applies in the civil and administrative justice system, and where applicable, as an additional measure in the criminal justice system to supplement protection from the forms of violence covered by this law.

1.2.	"Violence against women" - is understood as a violation of human rights and a form of discrimination against women and shall mean all acts of gender-based violence that result in or are likely to result in physical, sexual, psychological or economic harm or suffering to women, including threats of such acts, coercion or arbitrary deprivation of liberty, whether in public or private life;  

1.3.	"Domestic violence" - means all acts of physical, sexual, psychological and economic violence that occur within the family relationship, or household unit, regardless of blood ties or family relationship, or by a former intimate partner or current intimate partner, regardless of the fact whether the perpetrator of violence shares or has shared the same residence with the victim with the aim of violating the dignity and maintaining under continuous control and dependent position, the persons against whom violence has been exercised;  

1.4.	"Gender-based violence against women" - means violence that is directed against a woman because she is a woman or that affects women disproportionately. Gender-based violence against women includes the causes and result of the unequal power relationship between women and men as a result of a social problem and not an individual one;  

1.5.	"Political harassment" - political harassment is one or more acts of pressure, persecution, harassment or threats, committed by a person or group of people, directly or through third parties, against women candidates, elected, appointed or in exercise of political-public function or against their families, with the aim of curtailing, suspending, preventing or limiting the essential functions in their position, to incite or force them to perform, against their will, an action or not to act, in the performance of duties or in the exercise of their rights;  

1.6.	"Political Violence" - political violence means actions, conduct and/or physical, psychological, sexual aggressions committed by a person or group of people, directly or through third parties, against women candidates, elected, appointed or in exercise of political-public function; or against her family, to curtail, suspend, prevent or limit the exercise of her duty or to incite or force her to perform, against her will, an action or not to act, in the performance of duties or in the exercise of their rights;  

1.7.	"Gender" - means the socially constructed roles, behaviors, activities and attributes that a given society considers appropriate for women and men;  

1.8.	"Psychological violence" - includes continuous control, verbal abuse, isolation, humiliation or emotional abuse through insults, offenses, blackmail, intimidation, stalking and continuous surveillance, concealment of information, misinformation and threatening behavior, degrading treatment, threats or similar forms of control or psychological pressure, placing a person under reasonable fear of the use of violence against him or persons in family relationship, threat to damage property or keeping under continuous emotional distress;

## CHAPTER II FUNDAMENTAL PRINCIPLES

### Article 4 Non-discrimination

1.	Domestic violence, violence against women and gender-based violence is a violation of human rights and freedoms, and constitutes discrimination that limits the enjoyment of rights and freedoms on an equal basis with men.  

2.	The implementation of the provisions of this law by the parties, especially measures for the protection of victims, shall be ensured without discrimination on the basis of gender, race, color, religion, political opinion or other opinion, national or social origin, belonging to a national minority, property, birth, sexual orientation, gender identity, age, health condition, disability, marital status, migrant or refugee status or other status.  

3.	The measures, activities and services of this law shall be undertaken to implement the principle of equality, to eliminate stereotypes related to gender roles, prevention and protection from all forms of discrimination against women, victims of gender-based violence and victims of domestic violence.

### Article 5

Priority treatment

1.	State institutions, legal persons performing public authorizations determined by law, officials, officers and other authorized persons performing work on behalf of the state, have the obligation to treat with priority all measures and activities to prevent any effect of gender-based violence against women and domestic violence.  

2.	The subjects from paragraph 1 of this article are obliged to undertake with priority all appropriate legislative, administrative, judicial and other measures, to prevent, protect, investigate, punish and ensure fair compensation for victims including also the priority division of joint property for victims of domestic violence.  

3.	Official persons in institutions that carry out activities in the field of social protection, internal affairs, health, child protection and education, as well as competent courts in civil, administrative and criminal proceedings for domestic violence, gender-based violence and violence against women are obliged to act urgently, with increased priority towards the interests and needs of the victim.  

4.	Priority treatment also implies increased care in undertaking necessary measures for risk assessment, by all relevant authorities, with the aim of preventing the repetition of violence.

### Article 6

Prohibition of victimization

Any action taken or omission that intentionally or unintentionally aims to punish or place in a less favorable position a person who has taken actions to be protected from domestic violence, gender-based violence and, respectively if the person has reported violence, has initiated a protection procedure, or has testified during the procedure or otherwise, has participated in a protection procedure according to the provisions of this law and international conventions, applicable in the domestic legal system, is prohibited.

### Article 7

Appropriateness of actions

1.	Public institutions which bear obligations under this law to undertake measures, activities and services for victims of gender-based violence against women and domestic violence must adapt them to the specific needs of vulnerable victims.  

2.	Measures, activities and services for prevention and protection of women from gender-based violence and from domestic violence, are provided in a manner that enables the empowerment of the potential and capacities of victims for independent organization of life in the future.  

3.	The victim and/or legal representative has the right to participate in the assessment of the situation and needs, to be informed about the measures, activities and services for protection and to participate in the selection of services and measures for assistance, support and protection.  

4.	Without the consent of the victim, respectively their legal representative, measures, activities and services for protection cannot be undertaken, except when there is a direct risk to their life and body, including also child victims of domestic violence.

### Article 8

Gender mainstreaming

Public institutions are obliged to include the gender perspective as a criterion which is integrated in all phases of the policy-making process, situation assessment, implementation and evaluation of policies arising from this law.

### Article 28

Protection orders and protective measures

1. Protection orders are also issued for persons who are not in a family relationship, as well as for the protection of victims of sexual violence and sexual harassment and other categories protected by this law.

2.1. Regular protection order is an order issued by the Court in regular procedure. 2.2. This order is valid until expiration, interruption, or issuance of a new protection order by the court;

2.3. Emergency protection order is an order issued by the Court in expedited procedure to provide immediate protection for the protected party;

2.4. Temporary emergency protection order is an order issued by the Police outside the working hours of the Court. This order is valid until the first working day of the

### Article 29

Protective measure of prohibition of exercising violence

The measure of prohibition of harassment may be issued to prohibit the responsible party from directly, through third persons or any other means of communication harassing, disturbing, manipulating, offending, threatening, stalking or monitoring the protected party and persons in family relationship with them.

3.2.	The responsible party poses a direct and immediate threat to the safety, health and well-being of the protected party or of a person with whom the protected party has a family relationship, who must be protected by means of a protection order and,

3.3.	The issuance of an emergency temporary protection order is necessary to ensure the health or well-being of the protected party or of a person with whom the protected party has a family relationship.  

4.	A request for an emergency temporary protection order may also be submitted to the Kosovo Police by NGOs that have reliable information about the violence committed and that are familiar with the victim.

1.3.	The explanation that after the expiration of the emergency temporary protection period, the protected party may submit a request for an emergency protection order, which if granted, would be subject to holding a hearing or submitting a request for a protection order

### Article 54

Violation of protection orders

1.	Violation in whole or in part of the protection order, emergency protection order or emergency temporary protection order constitutes a criminal offense sanctioned pursuant to the Criminal Code of the Republic of Kosovo.  

2.	Continuous repetition of violation in whole or in part of the protection order, emergency protection order or emergency temporary protection order is considered an aggravating circumstance for the perpetrator of the offense.  

3.	Violation of the protection order, emergency protection order or emergency temporary protection order is prosecuted ex officio.  

4.	The Kosovo Police regularly monitors and enforces protection orders issued by the competent authority, depending on the degree of danger in accordance with the ongoing assessment of the risk of repetition of violence.  

5.	The Kosovo Police executes protection orders issued by the competent authority.

### Article 55

Prohibition of application of alternative procedures

The application of alternative procedures in criminal and administrative proceedings for the resolution of cases of domestic violence, against women and gender-based violence is prohibited, but not limited to mediation and reconciliation.

### Article 56

Orders issued by other countries

1.	A protection order issued by another state is registered and enters into force by the courts of Kosovo and is enforced by the respective institutions as if it were issued in accordance with this law.  

2.	The court performs the registration of the protection order issued by another state based on the following documents:

2.1.	A certified copy of the protection order, and/or;  

2.2.	A statement signed by an official of the court of the other state from which the order was issued, through which it is verified that the issued order is still in force on the day of certification.  

3.	The court retains the right to keep a certified copy of the protection order for registration purposes.  

4.	The court is obliged to send a copy of the protection order to the police and all other relevant institutions within 24 hours.

### Article 57

Recognition of protection orders issued by other states

1.	Courts may decide not to recognize a protection order issued by another country in cases where:

1.1.	At the moment of submission of the request to the court of Kosovo, the protection order was not in full force in the state where it was issued, or

1.2.	At the moment of submission of the request to the courts of Kosovo, the term of the protection order has expired;

1.3.	The protective measures included in the protection order issued by the other state are not provided for in the legislation in force of Kosovo.

### Article 58

Limitation of issuing protection orders against persons incited to commit violence

1.	With the exception of the existence of special circumstances which according to the court make the issuance of a protection order necessary, protection orders are not issued against children under the age of 14, elderly persons in conditions of mental health that limits the possibility of reasonable judgment and persons with limited capacity, who have been incited to commit violence.

2.	In case of issuing protection orders against the categories specified in paragraph 1 of this article, the Court takes into consideration their well-being and decides on:

2.1.	Appointment of legal aid to represent them during court proceedings;

2.2.	Appointment of guardianship and appropriate care from service providers according to the needs of the responsible party, belonging to the groups mentioned in paragraph 1 of this article.

### Article 59

Relations of the victim and the protected party with employers

1.	Victims of domestic violence and protected parties have the right to receive 15 calendar days of paid leave, to address health, legal problems, change of residence, for themselves or for persons dependent on him/her.

2.	Victims of domestic violence have the right to request up to one year unpaid leave to address health problems that are a direct consequence of the violence experienced.

3.	Employers have the right to request from their employees, victims of domestic violence, documentary evidence for the days spent addressing health, legal problems, change of residence, etc.

4.	Victims are guaranteed retention of the workplace without pay for up to one year, only if the employed victim of domestic violence has been employed in the workplace for no less than 12 months.

### Article 60

Protective Measures for Asylum Seekers, Immigrants and Foreign Workers Victims of domestic violence and against women

1.	The provisions of this law apply without prejudice and in the same manner to asylum seekers, immigrants and foreign workers, and members of their family who are located in the territory of the Republic of Kosovo.

2.	In case the protected party has obtained a residence permit or temporary residence or the request for granting asylum status in the territory of the Republic of Kosovo through the application of the responsible party, the competent authorities examine the application for residence permit of the protected party, separately from the application or case of the responsible party.

### Article 61

Obligation for collection and registration of data

1.	The relevant Ministry in the field of justice creates and maintains the electronic information management system - registration of cases of domestic violence, and violence against women which is supervised and maintained by the Office of the National Coordinator for Protection from Domestic Violence and Violence against Women within the Ministry responsible for the field of justice.

2.	The National Coordinator for Protection from Domestic Violence and the Coordinating Group decides on the creation, modification, completion and expansion of the integrated data system.

3.	All institutions that provide services in cases of domestic violence have the obligation to register data within 24 hours in the database for registration of cases of domestic violence in the Republic of Kosovo.

4.	The relevant Ministry for the field of justice issues an administrative instruction which determines the manner of maintenance, security and use of the system and the issuance of public reports while ensuring confidentiality for the victim.

## CHAPTER V

TRANSITIONAL AND FINAL PROVISIONS

### Article 62

Implementation

1.	Six (6) months after the entry into force of this law, the Government of the Republic of Kosovo issues the sub-legal acts that derive from this law.

2.	For cases for which court proceedings have commenced before the entry into force of this law, the provisions of Law No. 03/L-182 on Protection from Domestic Violence shall apply.

3.	For all procedural matters that are not regulated by this Law, the provisions of the Law on Contentious Procedure apply and are applied accordingly.

### Article 63

Repeal

With the entry into force of this law, Law No. 03/L-182 on Protection from Domestic Violence is repealed.

### Article 64

Entry into force

This law enters into force fifteen (15) days after publication in the Official Gazette of the Republic of Kosovo.
