### Article 2. For the purposes of this Law, the following terms are defined as follows

1. Aggressor. Whoever performs any action or omission described in the definition of violence, to the detriment of persons who are protected by this Law.
2. Cohabit. To maintain a consensual relationship similar to that of spouses.
3. Abuse. Offenses in fact and word, serious or intolerable, that diminish the obligations of affection and respect that should govern relations between persons.
4. Protection measure. Mandate issued in writing by the competent authority, in which measures are dictated so that an aggressor refrains from incurring or performing certain acts or conduct constituting domestic violence.
5. Couple relationship. It is the relationship between spouses, ex-spouses, persons who cohabit or have cohabited, who maintain or have maintained an intimate consensual relationship and those who have procreated a son or daughter between them.
6. Survivor victim. Person who suffers or has suffered physical, verbal, socio-emotional, sexual or patrimonial abuse.
7. Violence. Any action, omission or negligent treatment committed by a person that harms the physical, psychological, sexual, patrimonial integrity or the freedom of persons who are subjects of this Law.
8. Domestic violence. Pattern of conduct in which physical force or sexual or psychological violence, intimidation or persecution is used against a person by their spouse, ex-spouse, family members or relatives with whom they cohabit or have cohabited, live or have lived under the same roof or maintain or have maintained a legally recognized relationship, or with whom they maintain a consensual relationship, or with a person with whom they have procreated a son or daughter at minimum, to cause physical harm to their person or to the person of another to cause emotional harm.
9. Physical violence. Use of force or coercion, by the aggressor, against the survivor victim to make them do something they do not wish or stop doing something they wish above their rights.
10. Patrimonial violence. Willful action or omission that implies damages, losses, transformation, theft, destruction, retention or misappropriation of objects, work instruments, personal documents, goods or other economic resources intended to satisfy the basic needs of the persons included within article 3 of this Law. The unjustified limitation to access and management of common goods shall also be considered as patrimonial violence.
11. Sexual violence. Action that obliges a person, through the use of force, intimidation, coercion, blackmail, bribery, manipulation, threat, use of substances or drugs or any other mechanism that affects their will, to participate in sexual interactions that by themselves do not necessarily constitute crimes against decency and sexual freedom. Likewise, it is considered sexual violence that the aggressor obliges the aggrieved person to perform some of these acts with third parties or to witness them.
12. Psychological violence. Any action or omission that a person performs against another, intended to coerce, degrade or control the actions, behaviors, beliefs, feelings or decisions of the persons to whom this Law is applicable. It is manifested through intimidation, manipulation, direct or indirect threat, permanent surveillance, harassment, harassment or contempt for personal value, destruction of objects appreciated by the person, deprivation of access to food, humiliation, isolation or any other conduct that implies harm to psychological health, self-determination or personal development.

### Article 3. The measures and precepts enshrined in this Law are applicable to

1. Marriages.
2. Common-law unions.
3. Couple relationships that have not completed five years, whose intention of permanence can be proven.
4. Kinship by consanguinity, affinity or adoption.
5. Minor sons and daughters not in common who live or do not live within the family.
6. Persons who have procreated a son or daughter between them.
They shall also apply to the situations indicated in the previous numerals, even
when they have ended at the time of the aggression.

Article 4. Without prejudice to the initiation or continuation of the respective civil, criminal, family or administrative proceeding, the authority, when it becomes aware of the act, is immediately empowered, according to its competence, to apply, in favor of persons who are surviving victims of domestic violence, the following protection measures:
1.	Order the provisional arrest of the aggressor or the female aggressor, for a term not exceeding twenty-four hours.
2.	Order the alleged aggressor, or the alleged female aggressor to vacate the dwelling that he or she shares with the surviving victim, regardless of who is the owner of the dwelling.
3.	Proceed to search with the purpose of rescuing or immediately assisting the alleged surviving victim of the act of violence, in accordance with constitutional and legal guarantees.
4.	Authorize the surviving victim, if he or she so requests, to provisionally reside in a different domicile from the common one to protect him or her from future aggressions, respecting the confidentiality of the domicile.
5.	Prohibit the introduction or keeping of weapons in the common domicile, as well as seize them in order to guarantee that they are not used to intimidate, threaten or cause harm.
6.	Prohibit the alleged aggressor or the alleged female aggressor from approaching the common domicile or that where the surviving victim is located, in addition to the place of work, study or other place habitually frequented by him or her.
7.	Reintegrate to the common domicile the assaulted person who has had to leave it, if he or she so requests and, consequently, must immediately apply the measure established in numeral 2 of this article.

9.	Suspend the regulation of visits to the alleged aggressor or the alleged female aggressor, taking into account the gravity of the acts of violence and/or the direct or indirect harm or danger to which the minors are subjected.
10.	Issue notes to the migration and boarding authorities, in which the impediment to leave the country is ordered for the minor children of the parties.
11.	Prepare the inventory of movable property of the household nucleus, to secure the common patrimony.

### Article 215

A. The person who physically, sexually, patrimonially or psychologically assaults another or harasses them, shall be sanctioned with imprisonment of 1 to 3 years or with a curative security measure, consisting of a multidisciplinary therapeutic treatment program with specialized care, approved by the trial court.  
Psychological assault must be verified by the forensic psychiatrist or by a forensic psychologist.  
For the purposes of this Chapter, the norms contemplated in the types described are applicable to:
1.	Marriages.  
2.	Common-law unions.  
3.	Couple relationships that have not reached five years, whose intention of permanence can be proven.  
4.	Kinship by consanguinity, affinity or adoption.  
5.	Minor sons or daughters not in common who do or do not live within the family.  
6.	Persons who have procreated a son or daughter with each other.  
They shall also apply to the situations indicated in the preceding numerals, even when they have ended at the time of the assault.  
6

### Article 215

B. If the conduct described in the preceding article produces the permanent weakening of a sense or of an organ, or a visible mark to the naked eye and permanent on the face; or if inflicted on a woman in a state of pregnancy it hastens childbirth, the sanction shall be 2 to 4 years of imprisonment.  
If the conduct described in the preceding article produces incurable bodily or psychic harm, the loss of a sense, of an organ or extremity; impotence or loss of capacity to procreate; permanent alteration of vision, deformation of the face or body for life, or permanent incapacity for work, the sanction shall be 3 to 5 years of imprisonment.

### Article 215

C. In cases of first-time female or male aggressors, the trial judge may sanction with a curative security measure, consisting of a multidisciplinary therapeutic treatment program, in accordance with Article 115 of the Criminal Code, duly supervised by the Corrections Department of the Ministry of Government and Justice, or with community service supervised by the competent authority within the corregimiento in which they reside.  
In case of non-compliance with the curative security measure or supervised community service, the judge must substitute it with the corresponding prison sentence.

### Article 215

D. The person who maltreats a child or adolescent under 18 years of age, shall be sanctioned with imprisonment of 2 to 6 years or with a curative security measure or both.  
The following conducts typify the maltreatment of minors:
1.	Causing, permitting or causing to be caused physical, mental or emotional harm to them, including physical injuries caused by corporal punishment.  
2.	Using them or inducing them to be used, for profit, in begging, pornography or in propaganda or advertising not appropriate for their age.  
3.	Employing them in prohibited work or work contrary to morality, or that endangers their life or health.  
4.	Imposing negligent treatment and mistreatment on them that may affect their physical and mental health.

### Article 215

E. The male or female official or private individual who has knowledge of the execution of any of the acts typified in this Title, and does not bring it to the attention of the authorities, shall be sanctioned with 50 to 150 days - fine. In case the commission of the crime is not proven, the male or female official or private individual shall be exempt from any legal responsibility by reason of the complaint.

### Article 224

### Article 17

For the crimes described in Articles 215 A and 215 D of the Criminal Code, the trial judge may, at the time of imposing the prison sanction on the male or female aggressor, order that its fulfillment be carried out during weekends, with the purpose of preserving their source of income.

### Article 18. Article 1984 A of the Judicial Code shall read as follows

### Article 1984

A. In cases of domestic violence, withdrawal by the affected person is permitted when they are of legal age, provided that the following conditions are met:
1.	That the accused is not a repeat offender of this crime or other intentional crimes contemplated in Panamanian criminal law.  
2.	That the accused presents a certificate of good conduct and evaluation by two (2) psychiatrists or mental health physicians, appointed by the Public Prosecutor's Office.  
3.	That the accused submits to treatment by a multidisciplinary mental health team, when the judge of the case deems it necessary, under the judge's supervision.  
When it concerns patrimonial violence, even if the affected person is a minor, withdrawal shall be accepted when the damage caused has been compensated.

### Article 19

Before subjecting a child or adolescent to the practice of any proceeding, the authority shall order that a psychological and/or psychiatric evaluation be conducted by a professional in the service of the Public Prosecutor's Office, in order to guarantee that the practice of the proceeding will not cause psycho-emotional disturbance.

### Article 20

The court of the case shall take the necessary measures so that the surviving victim of any of the crimes contemplated in this Law receives treatment that allows their physical and psychological recovery as well as their social reintegration, which must be paid for by the aggressor.

### Article 21

The Public Prosecutor's Office may, ex officio, at the request of the surviving victim or their legal representative, order that the victim receive therapeutic treatment for the duration of the investigation.

## Chapter V

Public Policies

### Article 22

The Ministry of Youth, Women, Children and Family shall have responsibility for monitoring, coordinating, promoting and evaluating progress in the implementation of this Law. Consequently, it shall submit annual reports to the Executive Branch and to the Commission on Women's Affairs, Children's Rights, Youth and Family of the Legislative Assembly.

### Article 23

The Ministry of Government and Justice, together with the Ministry of Youth, Women, Children and Family, shall coordinate, promote, develop and supervise dissemination programs, through social media, aimed at preventing and eradicating violence; in addition, it shall promote and encourage programs for the dissemination of this Law. Likewise, it shall formulate and execute training programs for National Police personnel in order to guarantee their effective and timely intervention in cases of violence described in this Law.

### Article 24

The Ministry of Health shall strengthen and train personnel at health centers, regional and national hospitals, in the prevention and care of cases of violence established in this Law.

### Article 25

All health centers, emergency rooms, medical or hospital centers, clinics and offices, public or private, must attend to cases of violence regulated by this Law.  
Those who work in these institutions may not deny medical or hospital care to surviving victims of violence, without prejudice to the fact that they may subsequently be referred to other centers for continued care, provided that their transfer does not entail risks to their health or integrity.

### Article 26

The Executive Branch, through the Ministry of Health and with the advice of the Public Prosecutor's Office, shall regulate and design forms to record the aggressions caused by the different types of violence indicated by this Law.

### Article 27

Health personnel and administrative personnel working in the various health institutions of the country must document, by means of forms distributed by the Ministry of Health, the medical history, clinical findings, diagnosis and provisional incapacity of the patient who declares having been a surviving victim of domestic violence or child or adolescent abuse.  
The aforementioned form, duly sealed and signed, shall be sent to the Institute of Legal Medicine within forty-eight hours following the patient's care, so that the Institute may evaluate the medical report contained therein and proceed with the corresponding procedure for the summaries to be carried out for that purpose. In the case of children and adolescents, it shall be referred to the corresponding Childhood and Adolescence Court.  
9

The certificate issued by the qualified physician who attends the surviving victim must indicate the total psychic and physical incapacity that corresponds to them.

### Article 28

The Ministry of Education must incorporate content aimed at promoting values based on the principles of tolerance, respect for differences and diversity, as well as gender equality and equity in the plans and study programs of all levels. In addition, it shall promote programs aimed at peaceful conflict resolution, in order to prevent domestic violence and child and adolescent abuse.

### Article 29

The Ministry of Government and Justice and the municipalities of the Republic shall train night judges and corregidores concerning the correct application of this Law, with the objective that each of these officials, during the exercise of their office, have the due and necessary sensitivity, as well as the precise knowledge for the treatment of issues of domestic violence and child and adolescent abuse.

### Article 30

In all cases, the authority shall keep a record of acts of domestic violence and child and adolescent abuse, in which the particulars of those involved and the description of the events that occurred shall be recorded, which shall be sent to the National Commission on Criminal Statistics (CONADEC) of the Ministry of Government and Justice, with the objective of creating a database of persons involved in the acts regulated by this Law.  
The surviving victim shall have the right to be given, at no cost, an authenticated copy of the aforementioned record.

### Article 31

Civil society shall intervene actively in the dissemination, training, coordination and execution of this Law, together with the various levels of the State responsible for developing public policies on this matter.

### Article 32

Private entities or independent professionals, as well as non-governmental organizations that provide care to surviving victims of violence or to abused children and adolescents who qualify as subjects of free legal sponsorship, may deduct from their income tax returns the cost of this care.  
For this purpose, the corresponding account must be duly documented and approved by the judge of the case.  
In the case of the private sector, accounts submitted as costs of care to these surviving victims shall have executory merit.
