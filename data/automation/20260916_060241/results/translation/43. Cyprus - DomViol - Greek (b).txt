The Law on the Prevention and Combating of Violence against Women and Domestic Violence and on Related Matters of 2021 is issued by publication in the Official Gazette of the Republic of Cyprus in accordance with Article 52 of the Constitution.

Number 115(I) of 2021

LAW PROVIDING FOR THE PREVENTION AND COMBATING OF VIOLENCE AGAINST WOMEN AND DOMESTIC VIOLENCE, AS WELL AS FOR RELATED MATTERS

Preamble.	WHEREAS all forms of violence against women, including domestic violence, constitute a serious violation of human rights,

AND WHEREAS the Republic of Cyprus has ratified the Council of Europe Convention on Preventing and Combating Violence against Women and Domestic Violence,
by Law No. 14(III) of 2017,

AND WHEREAS according to the Convention, the contracting states are encouraged to take measures to protect all victims of violence against women and to upgrade their legislative
framework to address violence against women and to eliminate gender discrimination,

AND WHEREAS violence against women constitutes a violation of human rights and a form of discrimination against women, and includes all acts of gender-based violence, which result or are likely to result in physical, sexual, psychological or economic harm or suffering to women, as well as threats to commit such acts, coercion
or arbitrary deprivation of liberty, whether in public or in private life,

AND WHEREAS the implementation of de jure and de facto equality between women and men is a key element for the prevention and combating of violence against women,

AND WHEREAS, as recognised by the United Nations Committee on the Elimination of Discrimination against Women in its General Recommendation No. 28, of 16 December 2019, concerning the core obligations of contracting parties, in the context of Article 2 of the Convention on the Elimination of All Forms of Discrimination against Women, which was ratified by Law No. 78 of 1985, discrimination on the basis of sex constitutes a violation of human rights and an obstacle to the enjoyment of human rights and
fundamental freedoms,

AND WHEREAS violence against women constitutes a manifestation of the historically unequal power relations between women and men, which have led to domination and discrimination against
women by men and to the impediment of the full advancement of women,

AND WHEREAS the nature of violence against women as gender-based violence is one of the social mechanisms through which women are placed in a subordinate position compared to men,

AND WHEREAS women and young girls are often exposed to aggravated forms of violence, such as domestic violence, sexual violence, sexual harassment, psychological violence, economic violence, rape, stalking, dissemination of pornographic or sexual material, forced marriage, crimes committed in the name of so-called "honour" and female genital mutilation, which constitute serious violations of the human rights of women and girls,
as well as a major obstacle to the achievement of equality between women and men,

AND WHEREAS there are continuing violations of human rights during armed conflicts, which affect civilians, especially women in the form of widespread or systematic rape and sexual violence and the possibility of increased gender-based violence,
both during and after conflicts,

AND WHEREAS women and girls are exposed to a greater risk of gender-based violence than men and boys,

AND WHEREAS domestic violence affects women disproportionately in relation to
men, without disregarding that men may also be victims of domestic violence,

AND WHEREAS children, when they are witnesses of violence in the family, are also victims of domestic violence,

AND WHEREAS, the adoption or taking of special measures which are deemed necessary for the prevention and protection of women from gender-based violence are not considered discriminatory treatment, in accordance with the provisions of the Council of Europe Convention on
Preventing and Combating Violence against Women and Domestic Violence,

AND WHEREAS for the purposes of better implementation of the provisions of the Council of Europe Convention on Preventing and Combating Violence against Women and Domestic Violence (Ratification) Law, and the United Nations Convention
on the Elimination of All Forms of Discrimination against Women (Ratification) Law, the adoption of special legislative measures is deemed necessary,

AND WHEREAS the Council of Europe Convention on Preventing and Combating Violence against Women and Domestic Violence applies in conjunction with the Laws set out in the Second Schedule of the Council of Europe Convention on Preventing and Combating Violence against Women and Domestic
Violence (Ratification) Law,

AND WHEREAS related fundamental principles of human rights which the Republic is obliged to implement arise from the International Covenant on Civil and Political Rights and the International Covenant on Economic, Social and Cultural Rights of the United Nations, which were ratified by Law No. 14 of 1969, the Convention on the Elimination of All Forms of Discrimination against Women (CEDAW) of the United Nations, and its Optional Protocol which was ratified by Law No. 1 (III) of 2002, as well as General Recommendation No. 19 of the CEDAW Committee on Violence against Women, the Beijing Declaration and the Platform for Action adopted in Beijing on 15 September 1995 by the Fourth United Nations World Conference on Women (Action for Equality, Development and Peace), the United Nations Convention on the Rights of the Child, which was ratified by Law No. 243 of 1990 and its Optional Protocols which were ratified by Laws No. 6 (III) of 2006 and 13 (III) of 2017 and the United Nations Convention on the
Rights of Persons with Disabilities, which was ratified by Law No. 8 (III) of 2011,

For all these reasons, the House of Representatives votes as follows:

Short title.	1. This Law shall be cited as the Law on the Prevention and Combating of Violence against Women and Domestic Violence and on Related Matters of 2021.

Interpretation. 2. For the purposes of this Law, unless the context otherwise requires-

act, as well as coercion or arbitrary deprivation of liberty, whether occurring in public or in private life;

Purpose of this Law. 3. The purpose of this Law is the definition of offences of violence against women and domestic violence, their prevention, suppression and combating, the protection of women from all forms of violence and the elimination of violence against women and domestic violence, the promotion of substantive equality between women and men, the design of a comprehensive framework, policies and measures for the protection and support of all victims and the provision of support and assistance to organisations and involved services towards effective cooperation, so as to adopt an integrated approach to the
elimination of violence against women and domestic violence. Principles upon which
the application of the provisions of this
Law is based. 4.-(1) The provisions of this Law and the measures for the protection and promotion of the rights of victims of violence against women and domestic violence are applied by every involved service and non-governmental organisation without any discrimination on grounds of sex, gender, race, colour, language, religion, political or other opinion, national or social origin, association with a national minority, property, birth, sexual orientation,
gender identity, age, state of health, disability, marital status, migratory or refugee status or other status. (2) Child victims are treated by every involved service and non-governmental organisation in accordance with the principles and provisions of the United Nations Convention on the Rights of the Child and other international standards concerning the treatment of child victims of criminal offences, safeguarding the interests of women who have not completed the eighteenth (18th) year of their age and taking into account their age and degree of psychological and physical development or maturity, as well as
their vulnerable situation. PART II
CRIMINAL OFFENCES AND JURISDICTION OF COURTS

Offences
of violence against women. 5. The following offences are deemed to be offences of violence against women:

Schedule. (a)	Any of the offences referred to in the Schedule;

(b)	the offence of psychological violence provided for in article 6;

(c)	the offence of sexual harassment provided for in article 7;

(d)	the offence of economic violence provided for in article 8;

(e)	the offence of dissemination of material of pornographic or sexual content provided for in article 9;

(f)	the offence of solicitation of a child provided for in article 10;

(g)	any offence falling within the definition of the term "domestic violence". Exercise of
Psychological violence. 6. A person who, by his or her conduct expressed through coercion, pressure, derogatory comments, insult or threats, seriously impairs the psychological integrity of a woman or causes her real fear, is guilty of an offence and, upon conviction, is liable to imprisonment not exceeding five (5) years or to a fine not exceeding
ten thousand euros (€10,000) or to both such penalties. Sexual harassment. 7. A person who engages in unwanted conduct of a sexual nature towards a woman which is expressed in words or acts and has the purpose or effect of violating the woman's dignity, particularly when, through such conduct, that person creates an intimidating, hostile, degrading, humiliating or offensive climate towards that woman, is guilty of an offence and, upon conviction, is liable to imprisonment not exceeding five (5) years or to a fine not exceeding ten thousand euros
(€10,000) or to both such penalties. Economic violence. 8. A spouse or partner of a woman who is economically dependent on him, who deprives her of the necessary economic means for living including maintenance, medical care, clothing and shelter, with the purpose of causing her physical and/or psychological harm and/or with the purpose of coercing her to commit any act or omission and/or recklessly, not caring whether he will cause her physical or psychological harm, is guilty of an offence and, upon conviction, is liable to imprisonment not exceeding five (5) years or to a fine not exceeding ten thousand euros (€10,000) or to both
such penalties. Dissemination of material of pornographic or sexual content. 9.-(1) A person who sends, distributes, circulates, publishes, disseminates, reproduces or transmits by any electronic, digital, printed or other means of any nature, material with pornographic or sexual content relating to a woman, without her consent, under conditions of reasonable expectation of privacy, with the purpose of intimidating and/or humiliating and/or harassing her and/or causing her emotional disturbance and/or economic or other loss or harm and/or to obtain unlawful economic benefit, is guilty of a felony and, upon conviction, is liable to imprisonment not exceeding fourteen (14)
years. (2) A person who blackmails or threatens a woman that without her consent he or she will send, distribute, publish, disseminate, circulate, reproduce or transmit by any electronic, digital, printed or other means of any nature, material with pornographic or sexual content depicting her, is guilty of a felony and, upon
conviction, is liable to imprisonment not exceeding fourteen (14) years. (3) A person who attempts to commit the offences provided for in subsections (1) and (2) is guilty of an offence and, upon conviction, is liable to imprisonment not exceeding five (5) years. Solicitation of a child to commit an offence. 10. A person who solicits a child to commit an offence referred to in paragraphs (a), (b), (c), (d), (e) or (g) of article 5, is guilty of an offence and, upon conviction, is liable to the same penalty of imprisonment or fine that those offences carry for the perpetrator. Aggravating circumstances. 11. The Court, when exercising its powers to measure and impose a sentence for an offence of violence against women, shall take into account as aggravating, provided they do not already form
part of the constituent elements of the offence, the following circumstances:

(a)	the offence was committed against a person by a former or current spouse or partner or member of his or her family or by a person cohabiting or having cohabited with the victim or by
a person who has abused or exploited a position of authority, trust or influence;

(b)	the offence or related offences were committed concurrently;

(c)	the offence was committed against a person who is in a vulnerable position due to mental or physical disability or state of dependence or against a woman who was pregnant at the time of commission of the offence or who was under other special
circumstances;

(d)	the offence was committed against or in the presence of a child, that is within his or her visual or auditory field;

(e)	the offence was committed by two (2) or more persons acting jointly;

(f)	the offence was preceded by the exercise of extreme violence, coercion or threat or was accompanied by such violence, coercion or threat;

(g)	the offence was committed with the use of or under the threat of a weapon or other dangerous object;

(h)	the offence resulted in causing serious harm to the victim;

(i)	the convicted person had previously been convicted of an offence of the same nature;

(j)	the offence was committed by a public official in the performance of his or her duties:

45(I) of 2021. Liability of legal persons. 12.-(1) A legal person is liable for the commission of an offence of violence against women when it is committed by a person acting, either individually or as a member of an organ of that legal person, who holds in it a position based on-

(a)	authority to represent the legal person, or

(b)	authority to take decisions on behalf of the legal person, or

(c)	authority to exercise control within the legal person. (2) Without prejudice to the provisions of subsection (1), a legal person may be held liable for the commission of an offence of violence against women, in a case where the lack of supervision or lack of control by a person specified in subsection (1) has made
possible the commission of that offence by a person acting under its jurisdiction. (3) The liability of a legal person provided for in subsections (1) and (2) does not preclude the criminal prosecution of a natural person who acts as a perpetrator, instigator or accomplice
in an offence of violence against women. (4) A legal person convicted of committing an offence of violence against women is liable to a fine not exceeding three hundred thousand euros (€300,000):

Provided that, in addition to criminal liability for committing an offence of violence against women, the legal person bears civil liability. Additional penalties or
sanctions against a legal or natural
person. 13.-(1) Notwithstanding the provisions of any other Law and irrespective of the imposition of any other penalty or sanction for the commission of an offence of violence against women, the Court may, at any stage of the proceedings or upon imposing a sentence on a convicted person, order as an additional penalty or sanction-
(a)	exclusion from public benefits or subsidies,

(b)	dissolution of the legal person,

(c)	temporary or permanent closure of the premises or facilities used for the commission of the offence,

(d)	confiscation and forfeiture of any object or means used for the commission of an offence of violence against women. (2) Failure to comply with an order of the Court issued pursuant to the provisions of subsection (1) constitutes a criminal offence punishable by imprisonment not

Jurisdiction of courts. 15.-(1) Subject to the provisions of article 5 of the Criminal Code, the courts of the Republic have jurisdiction to try offences of violence against women, provided they were committed-

(a)	on board a ship flying the flag of the Republic,

(b)	on board an aircraft registered in accordance with the legislation of the Republic. (2) An offence committed on board a ship or aircraft, as provided in subsection (1), shall be tried by a competent court of the Nicosia district. (3) Subject to the provisions of article 5 of the Criminal Code and notwithstanding the provisions of article 6 thereof, the courts of the Republic have jurisdiction to try offences of violence against women, provided they are committed with the aid of an information system accessible from the territory of the Republic, irrespective of whether the information system is located
on the territory of the Republic or not. Exclusion of defence. 16. It shall not constitute a defence or mitigating factor or justification in relation to an offence of violence against women for the accused to plead that the victim has violated cultural, religious, social or traditional rules or customs of appropriate conduct or on grounds of sexual orientation or gender identity or that the offence was committed for reasons of
honour. Reporting by professionals. 17. In a case where a person, in the course of exercising his or her profession or professional duties, whether in the public or private sector, has sufficient reason to believe that an offence of violence against women has been committed or that further acts of violence against a woman are expected to be committed and makes a report to the prosecuting authorities, the rules of confidentiality provided for in any Law in force shall not apply in relation to the report, except in the case where the information was obtained by a lawyer in the context of providing legal advice and/or representation and/or defence of a person in relation to criminal or civil proceedings. PART III
RIGHTS AND PROTECTION OF VICTIMS IN THE CONTEXT OF CRIMINAL PROCEEDINGS - RIGHT OF VICTIMS TO COMPENSATION

Treatment of victims. 18. Subject to the provisions of the Establishment of Minimum Standards on the Rights, Support and Protection of Victims of Crime Law and the Protection of Witnesses Law, to the extent that they apply in relation to victims of offences of violence against women-

(a)	the involved services and involved non-governmental organisations, according to their competences and within their framework, treat victims with respect for their dignity, rights and legitimate interests, in the context of criminal proceedings and ensure that victims receive special treatment that responds in the best way to their best interests, individualised
situation and age and degree of maturity,

(b)	the Social Welfare Services ensure that assistance, support and protection are provided to a victim, as soon as those services or any other involved service ascertain that there are reasonable grounds to believe that an offence of violence against women may have been committed against the victim, irrespective of the victim's willingness to cooperate in the
criminal proceedings, and

(c)	the Social Welfare Services ensure that, where the age of a victim of an offence of violence against women is not ascertained and there are reasons indicating that he or she is a child, that person is presumed to be a child, so as to have immediate access to assistance, support and protection, in accordance with the provisions of Part III and Part IV. Individual risk assessment for the determination of special protection needs. 21.-(1) The Police shall conduct a timely individual assessment of the victim, with the aim of-

(a) assessing the risk of lethality, the seriousness of the situation and the risk of repeated violence,

(b) managing the risk and determining the special protection needs of the victim, and

(c) making a decision as to whether and to what extent the victim may benefit from special measures during the criminal proceedings, as provided for in articles 22 and 23 of the Law on the Establishment of Minimum Standards concerning the Rights, Support and Protection of Victims of Crime, due to the particular risk of suffering secondary and repeated victimisation, intimidation and retaliation. (2) Depending on the result of the assessment referred to in subsection (1), the Police shall cooperate, where required, with the Social Welfare Services, the Mental Health Services and the Medical Services for further assessment of the victim's needs, in accordance with the provisions of this Law and article 11 of the Law on the Establishment of Minimum Standards concerning the Rights, Support and Protection of Victims of Crime. (3) In the assessment referred to in subsection (1), the following elements shall be taken into account, as a minimum:

(a) The personal characteristics of the victim;

(b) the type and/or nature of the offence;

(c) the circumstances of the offence; and

(d) the fact that the perpetrator has in their possession firearms or non-firearms or has access to them. (4) In the context of the individual assessment, the prosecuting authorities, in cooperation with the Social Welfare Services, the Mental Health Services and the Medical Services, shall pay particular attention to victims who have suffered significant harm due to the seriousness of the offence, to victims of an offence due to prejudice or discrimination which may be related to their personal characteristics, and to victims who are particularly vulnerable due to their relationship with the perpetrator or their dependence on them, in particular victims of terrorism, organised crime, trafficking in human beings, gender-based violence, violence in the context of an intimate relationship, sexual violence or exploitation or hate crime and to victims with disabilities. (5) For the purposes of this Law, where the victim is a child, it shall be presumed that they have special protection needs and shall be subject to an individual assessment, in accordance with the provisions of subsection (1), to determine whether and to what extent they will benefit from the special measures provided for in articles 22 and 23 of the Law on the Establishment of Minimum Standards concerning the Rights, Support and Protection of Victims of Crime. (6) The extent of the individual assessment may be adapted, depending on the seriousness of the offence and the degree of apparent harm suffered by the victim. (7) The individual assessment shall be carried out with the close participation of the victim and during its conduct their wishes shall be taken into account, including the wish not to receive the special measures provided for in articles 22 and 23 of the Law on the Establishment of Minimum Standards concerning the Rights, Support and Protection of Victims of Crime. (8) In a case where the circumstances which form the basis of an individual assessment change significantly, the services involved shall take all appropriate measures, so that the individual assessment is updated throughout the criminal proceedings. Interim order for removal of the victim. 30.-(1) The Court may, following an ex parte application by the victim, the Police, the Attorney General of the Republic, the Social Welfare Services or, where the victim is a child, a guardian appointed under the provisions of the Relations between Parents and Children Law, issue an interim order for the removal of the victim, until a criminal case is registered and heard against a suspect or accused for an offence of violence against women. (6) The Body has the capacity to contract.

Qualifications, resignation and removal of the Chairperson and members of the Body.	40.-(1)(a) No person shall be appointed as Chairperson or member of the Body unless he is a person of recognised standing and integrity.
(b) The Chairperson and the members of the Body are appointed on the basis of their training and/or experience in matters of violence against women and domestic violence.

(2) The Council of Ministers may, by its Decision, remove the Chairperson or a member of the Body for-

(a)	his conviction for an offence involving lack of integrity or moral turpitude;

(b)	the imposition on him of a sentence of imprisonment following his conviction for a criminal offence, in violation of the provisions of this Law or another Law;

(c)	his declaration of bankruptcy;

(d)	illness, disability or physical incapacity, which renders him unable to adequately perform his powers, authorities or duties for the remainder of his term;

(e)	abuse of his position, in such a way that the continuation of his term would be detrimental to the public interest.

(3) The Chairperson and the members of the Body may at any time submit their resignation in writing to the Council of Ministers.

(4) Any vacancy arising for any reason in the Body shall be filled by the appointment of a new Chairperson or new member, as the case may be, for the remainder of the term of the Body.

Operation, procedures and structures of the Coordinating Body.	42.-(1) The Chairperson represents the Body, convenes the meetings and presides over them.
(2) The Body meets regularly every two (2) months.

(3) The Body may meet on an extraordinary basis, whenever this is deemed necessary by its Chairperson or requested by the Minister, for serious, extraordinary and urgent reasons.

(4) The validity of any act or work of the Body is not affected by any vacancies in it, provided that the number of its members amounts to at least five (5) members.

(5) The decisions of the Body are taken by simple majority and the Chairperson has a second or casting vote.

(6) In the absence of the Chairperson, the members of the Body elect a member to perform the duties of Chairperson during the meeting.

(7) The Body may be organised into working groups with a more specialised subject matter, for its more effective operation, which may meet on a regular or extraordinary basis.

(8) Working groups, which are established under the provisions of subsection (7), determine the involved service which will coordinate and convene them.

(9) The working groups inform the Body of the work, decisions and practical measures they take concerning the implementation of the provisions of this Law at its regular meetings.

(10) The Body prepares internal regulations for the better conduct of its work, which are submitted to the Minister for approval and their forwarding to the Council of Ministers.

(11) The Body may have its own scientific or other necessary staff, which is placed in it from the governmental sector or recruited by it through the purchase of services, and may further have an Office and equipment, which may be provided to it by the Ministry of Justice and Public Order.

Budget of the Body.	43. The budget of the Body is covered by a relevant appropriation, which is specifically provided for in the budget of the Ministry of Justice and Public Order.

The Body ensures the collection, coordination, processing, analysis, study, and publication of statistical data, at regular intervals, in relation to violence against women and domestic violence in the Republic, as well as the creation of a common database, through the grouping of information concerning incidents of violence against women and domestic violence, which come inter alia from all the involved services and non-governmental organisations:

Supportive and therapeutic measures and assessment of perpetrator.	45.-(1) The Mental Health Services, in cooperation with other involved services, ensure the availability of diagnostic assessment, appropriate therapeutic interventions and effective programmes, with the aim of preventing and minimising the risks of repetition of acts of violence.

(2) The following persons may have access to the programmes or intervention measures referred to in subsection (1):

(a)	A person who is being prosecuted criminally for an offence of violence against a woman, under conditions that are neither detrimental nor contrary to the right of defence or the requirements of a fair and impartial trial and in particular the principle of the presumption of innocence; and

(b)	a person who has been convicted of an offence of violence against a woman, following an order of the Court, in accordance with the provisions of section 46.

(3) The Mental Health Services, in cooperation with the Social Welfare Services, the Police and with other involved services, where applicable and with the assistance of experts with training and experience in the specialties of forensic psychiatry and/or forensic psychology, at any stage of the procedure have the responsibility of assessing the personality of the persons referred to in subsection (2) and the possible risk of repetition of an offence of violence against a woman, with the aim of determining appropriate programmes or intervention measures and assisting the Court.

(4) The Police, in cooperation with other involved services, where applicable, takes the necessary measures, so that the persons referred to in subsection (2), to whom programmes or intervention measures have been proposed, in accordance with subsection (1), are-

(a)	fully informed of the reasons for the proposal or the order of the Court,

(b)	consent to participate in the programmes or measures.

Orders in addition to sentences.	46. Notwithstanding the provisions of any other Law and the imposition of any other penalty or sanction for the commission of an offence of violence against a woman, the Court may, when imposing a sentence on a convicted person and with his consent, order-

(a)	his submission to diagnostic examination or treatment by a psychiatrist and/or clinical psychologist of the Mental Health Services,

(b)	his attendance of the programmes provided for in section 44, so as to become capable of adopting behaviour, with the aim of preventing the exercise of violence and changing the violent forms of behaviour manifested by him, and/or

(c)	his submission to therapeutic treatment of self-control by experts or under other conditions that the Court deems necessary, so as to avoid the repetition of offences of violence against women.
