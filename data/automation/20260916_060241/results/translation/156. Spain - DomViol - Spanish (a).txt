JUAN CARLOS I

KING OF SPAIN
To all who see and understand this. Know: That the General Courts have approved and I hereby sanction the following organic law. PREAMBLE

I
Gender-based violence is not a problem that affects the private sphere. On the contrary, it manifests itself as the most brutal symbol of the inequality existing in our society. It is violence directed at women by the very fact of being women, because they are considered by their aggressors to lack the minimum rights of freedom, respect and decision-making capacity. Our Constitution incorporates in its article 15 the right of all to life and physical and moral integrity, without in any case being subjected to torture or to inhuman or degrading punishment or treatment. Furthermore, our Constitution continues, these rights bind all public authorities and their exercise may only be regulated by law. The United Nations Organisation at the Fourth World Conference in 1995 already recognised that violence against women is an obstacle to achieving the objectives of equality, development and peace and violates and impairs the enjoyment of human rights and fundamental freedoms. It also defines it broadly as a manifestation of historically unequal power relations between women and men. There is even a technical definition of battered woman syndrome which consists of "the assaults suffered by women as a consequence of the sociocultural conditioning that acts on the male and female gender, placing her in a position of subordination to men and manifested in the three basic spheres of personal relationships: abuse within partner relationships, sexual assault in social life and harassment in the workplace". In Spanish reality, assaults on women have a particular incidence, with greater awareness today than in previous eras about this, thanks, to a large extent, to the effort made by women's organisations in their fight against all forms of gender-based violence. It is no longer an "invisible crime", but produces collective rejection and evident social alarm. II
Public authorities cannot be indifferent to gender-based violence, which constitutes one of the most flagrant attacks on fundamental rights such as freedom, equality, life, security and non-discrimination proclaimed in our Constitution. Those same public authorities have, in accordance with the provisions of article 9.2 of the Constitution, the obligation to adopt positive action measures to make these rights real and effective, removing the obstacles that prevent or hinder their fulfilment. In recent years there have been legislative advances in Spanish law on combating gender-based violence, such as Organic Law 11/2003, of 29 September, on Specific Measures on Public Safety, Domestic Violence and Social Integration of Foreigners; Organic Law 15/2003, of 25 November, amending Organic Law 10/1995, of 23 November, on the Criminal Code, or Law 27/2003, of 31 July, regulating the Protection Order for Victims of Domestic Violence; in addition to the laws approved by various Autonomous Communities, within their sphere of competence. All of them have affected different civil, criminal, social or educational spheres through their respective regulations. The Law seeks to respond to the recommendations of international bodies in the sense of providing a comprehensive response to violence against women. In this regard, reference may be made to the Convention on the Elimination of All Forms of Discrimination against Women of 1979; the United Nations Declaration on the Elimination of Violence against Women, proclaimed in December 1993 by the General Assembly; the Resolutions of the last International Conference on Women held in Beijing in September 1995; Resolution WHA49.25 of the World Health Assembly declaring violence as a priority public health problem proclaimed in 1996 by the WHO; the report of the European Parliament of July 1997; the Resolution of the United Nations Commission on Human Rights of 1997; and the Declaration of 1999 as the European Year of Combating Gender-Based Violence, among others. Very recently, Decision No 803/2004/EC of the European Parliament, approving a Community action programme (2004-2008) to prevent and combat violence against children, young people and women and to protect victims and groups at risk (Daphne II programme), has established the position and strategy of the representatives of the citizens of the Union in this regard. The scope of the Law covers both preventive, educational, social, welfare and post-victim care aspects, as well as civil regulations that affect the family or cohabitation sphere where assaults mainly occur, as well as the principle of subsidiarity in Public Administrations. Likewise, the punitive response that all manifestations of violence regulated by this Law must receive is decisively addressed. Gender-based violence is approached by the Law in a comprehensive and multidisciplinary manner, starting with the process of socialisation and education. The achievement of equality and respect for human dignity and the freedom of individuals must be a priority objective at all levels of socialisation. The Law establishes awareness-raising and intervention measures in the educational sphere. It reinforces, with specific reference to the advertising sphere, an image that respects the equality and dignity of women. It supports victims through the recognition of rights such as information, free legal aid and other social protection and economic support rights. It therefore provides a comprehensive legal response that covers both procedural rules, creating new instances, and substantive criminal and civil rules, including the appropriate training of health, police and legal operators responsible for obtaining evidence and applying the law. Awareness-raising and intervention measures are also established in the health sphere to optimise early detection and physical and psychological care for victims, in coordination with other support measures. Situations of violence against women also affect minors who are within their family environment, direct or indirect victims of this violence. The Law also contemplates their protection not only for the protection of the rights of minors, but to effectively guarantee the protection measures adopted with respect to women. III
The Law is structured in a preliminary title, five titles, twenty additional provisions, two transitional provisions, one repealing provision and seven final provisions. The preliminary title contains the general provisions of the Law which refer to its object and guiding principles. Title I determines the awareness-raising, prevention and detection and intervention measures in different spheres. In the educational sphere, the obligations of the system for the transmission of values of respect for the dignity of women and equality between men and women are specified. The fundamental objective of education is to provide comprehensive training that allows them to shape their own identity, as well as

to build a conception of reality that integrates both knowledge and ethical assessment of it. In Secondary Education, education on equality between men and women and against gender-based violence is incorporated as curricular content, incorporating in all School Councils a new member who promotes educational measures in favour of equality and against violence against women. In the field of advertising, it must respect the dignity of women and their right to a non-stereotyped, non-discriminatory image, whether displayed in public or private media. On the other hand, the action for cessation or rectification of advertising is modified, legitimising institutions and associations working in favour of equality between men and women to exercise it. In the health sphere, actions for early detection and care support for victims are contemplated, as well as the application of health protocols in the face of assaults arising from the violence covered by this Law, which will be sent to the corresponding Courts in order to expedite judicial proceedings. Likewise, a Commission is created within the Interterritorial Council of the National Health System, responsible for technically supporting, coordinating and evaluating the health measures established in the Law. In Title II, relating to the rights of women victims of violence, in its Chapter I, the right of access to information and comprehensive social assistance is guaranteed, through permanent, urgent care services with specialised benefits and professional multidisciplinarity. In order to contribute to the implementation of these services, a Fund will be allocated to which the Autonomous Communities may have access, in accordance with the objective criteria determined in the respective Sectoral Conference. Likewise, the right to free legal aid is recognised, in order to guarantee those victims with insufficient resources to litigate legal representation in all proceedings and procedures, related to gender-based violence, in which they are a party, with the same legal representation providing their assistance in all proceedings. The measure is extended to those harmed in the event of the victim's death. Protection measures are also established in the social sphere, amending Royal Legislative Decree 1/1995, of 24 March, approving the consolidated text of the Workers' Statute Law, to justify absences from work by victims of gender-based violence, enable their geographical mobility, suspension with job reservation and termination of the contract. Similarly, support measures are provided for female civil servants who suffer forms of violence combated by this Law, amending the corresponding provisions of Law 30/1984, of 2 August, on Measures for the Reform of the Civil Service. Economic support measures are also regulated, amending Royal Legislative Decree 1/1994, of 20 June, approving the consolidated text of the General Social Security Law, so that victims of gender-based violence generate entitlement to the legal situation of unemployment when they terminate or voluntarily suspend their employment contract. To guarantee victims of gender-based violence who lack economic resources social assistance in those cases where it is estimated that the victim, due to their age, lack of general or specialised training and social circumstances, will not substantially improve their employability, their incorporation into the specific action programme created for this purpose for their professional integration is provided for. These aids, which will be modulated in relation to the age and family responsibilities of the victim, have as their fundamental objective to provide them with minimum subsistence resources that allow them to become independent from the aggressor; such aids will be compatible with those provided for in Law 35/1995, of 11 December, on Aid and Assistance to Victims of Violent Crimes and Crimes Against Sexual Freedom. In Title III, concerning Institutional Protection, two administrative bodies are created. Firstly, the Special Government Delegation against Violence against Women, in the Ministry of Labour and Social Affairs, which will be responsible, among other functions, for proposing the Government's policy in relation to violence against women and coordinating and promoting all actions carried out in this matter, which must necessarily include all those actions that make effective the guarantee of women's rights. The State Observatory on Violence against Women is also created, as a collegiate body in the Ministry of Labour and Social Affairs, and which will have as its main functions to serve as a centre for analysis of the situation and evolution of violence against women, as well as to advise and collaborate with the Delegate in the preparation of proposals and measures to eradicate this type of violence. In its Title IV the Law introduces provisions of a criminal nature, by which it is intended to include, within the aggravated types of injuries, a specific one that increases the criminal sanction when the injury is caused against someone who is or has been the author's wife, or a woman who is or has been linked to him by a similar relationship of affection, even without cohabitation. Minor coercion and minor threats of any kind committed against the women mentioned above will also be punished as a crime. For citizens, for women's groups and specifically for those who suffer this type of assault, the Law wants to give a firm and forceful response and show firmness by embodying them in specific criminal types. Title V establishes the so-called Judicial Protection to guarantee adequate and effective treatment of the legal, family and social situation of victims of gender-based violence in intra-family relationships. From the judicial point of view we are faced with a complex phenomenon in which it is necessary to intervene from different legal perspectives, which must range from procedural and substantive rules to provisions relating to victim care, intervention that is only possible through specific legislation. A Law for the prevention and eradication of violence against women must be a Law that includes procedural measures that allow agile and summary procedures, such as that established in Law 27/2003, of 31 July, but, in addition, that combines, in the civil and criminal spheres, protection measures for women and their sons and daughters, and precautionary measures to be executed urgently. The current civil, criminal, advertising, social and administrative regulations have many deficiencies, mainly due to the fact that until now this issue has not been given a comprehensive and multidisciplinary response. From the criminal point of view the response can never be a new grievance for women.Regarding the legal measures adopted to guarantee adequate and effective treatment of the legal, family and social situation of victims of violence against women in intra-family relationships, the following have been adopted: in accordance with Spanish legal tradition, a formula of specialization within the criminal jurisdiction has been chosen for the Investigating Judges, creating the Courts on Violence against Women and excluding the possibility of creating a new jurisdictional order or the assumption of criminal jurisdiction by Civil Judges. These Courts shall have jurisdiction over the investigation and, where appropriate, the judgment of criminal cases involving violence against women, as well as related civil cases, so that both at first instance are subject to procedural treatment before the same court. This ensures the guaranteeing mediation of due criminal process in the intervention of the fundamental rights of the alleged aggressor, without in any way reducing the legal possibilities that this Law provides for the greater, more immediate and effective protection of the victim, as well as the resources to prevent repetitions of the aggression or escalation of violence. With respect to the express regulation of the protection measures that may be adopted by the Court on Violence against Women, express inclusion has been chosen, since they are not included as precautionary measures in the Criminal Procedure Act, which only regulates the prohibition of residence and the prohibition of going to a certain place for offences covered in article 57 of the Criminal Code (

Article 544

bis of the Criminal Procedure Act, introduced by Organic Law 14/1999, of 9 June, amending the Criminal Code of 1995, on the protection of victims of ill-treatment and the Criminal Procedure Act). Furthermore, the option is taken for the temporal delimitation of these measures (when they are precautionary measures) until the end of the proceedings. However, the possibility is added that any of these protection measures may be used as a security measure, from the beginning or during the execution of the sentence, thereby increasing the list of Article 105 of the Criminal Code (amended by Organic Law 15/2003, of 25 November, amending Organic Law 10/1995, of 23 November, on the Criminal

Code), and enabling the Judge to guarantee the protection of victims beyond the end of the proceedings.
Provisions are contemplated that affect the functions of the Public Prosecutor's Office, through the creation of the Prosecutor against Violence on Women, responsible for the supervision and coordination of the Public Prosecutor's Office in this regard, as well as through the creation of an equivalent Section in each Prosecutor's Office of the High Courts of Justice and the Provincial Courts to which Prosecutors with specialisation in the matter will be assigned. The Prosecutors shall intervene in criminal proceedings for acts constituting offences or misdemeanours whose jurisdiction is attributed to the Courts on Violence against Women, in addition to intervening in civil proceedings for nullity, separation or divorce, or concerning the custody of minor children in which ill-treatment of the spouse or children is alleged.
In its additional provisions the Law carries out a profound reform of the legal system to adapt existing rules to the framework introduced by this text. In order to harmonise previous rules and offer a coordinated context among legal texts, part of the comprehensive reform has been carried out through the amendment of existing rules. In this regard, the additional provisions develop the measures provided for in the articles, but integrating them directly into educational, advertising, labour, Social Security and Public Service legislation; likewise, said provisions affect, in particular, the recognition of pensions and the allocation of the Fund provided for in this Law to promote comprehensive social assistance to victims of gender-based violence.
In matters of transitional regime, the application of this Law is extended to proceedings in progress at the time of its entry into force, although respecting the judicial jurisdiction of the respective bodies.
Finally, this Law includes in its final provisions the necessary authorisations for the regulatory development of its precepts.

PRELIMINARY TITLE

Article 1. Object of the Law

1.	The object of this Law is to act against violence which, as a manifestation of discrimination, the situation of inequality and the power relations of men over women, is exercised over them by those who are or have been their spouses or by those who are or have been linked to them by similar relationships of affection, even without cohabitation.
2.	This law establishes comprehensive protection measures whose purpose is to prevent, punish and eradicate this violence and provide assistance to women, their minor children and minors subject to their guardianship, or custody, victims of this violence.
3.	Gender-based violence referred to in this Law comprises every act of physical and psychological violence, including assaults on sexual freedom, threats, coercion or arbitrary deprivation of liberty.
4.	Gender-based violence referred to in this Law also comprises violence which with the objective of causing harm or damage to women is exercised over their relatives or close persons who are minors by the persons indicated in the first paragraph.

Article 2. Guiding principles

Through this Law a comprehensive set of measures is articulated aimed at achieving the following purposes:
a)	To strengthen measures for public awareness and prevention, providing public authorities with effective instruments in the educational, social services, health, advertising and media spheres.
b)	To enshrine rights of women victims of gender-based violence, enforceable before Public Administrations, and thus ensure rapid, transparent and effective access to the services established for that purpose.

c)	To reinforce until the achievement of the minimum standards required by the objectives of the law the social services of information, care, emergency, support and comprehensive recovery, as well as to establish a system for the most effective coordination of services already existing at municipal and regional level.
d)	To guarantee rights in the labour and civil service sphere that reconcile the requirements of the employment relationship and public employment with the circumstances of those workers or civil servants who suffer gender-based violence.
e)	To guarantee economic rights for women victims of gender-based violence, in order to facilitate their social integration.
f)	To establish a comprehensive system of institutional protection in which the General State Administration, through the Special Delegation of the Government against Violence on Women, in collaboration with the State Observatory on Violence against Women, promotes the creation of public policies aimed at offering protection to the victims of the violence contemplated in this Law.
g)	To strengthen the existing criminal and procedural framework to ensure comprehensive protection, from the judicial instances, to victims of gender-based violence.
h)	To coordinate the resources and instruments of all kinds of the different public authorities to ensure the prevention of acts of gender-based violence and, where appropriate, the appropriate punishment of those guilty of them.
i)	To promote the collaboration and participation of entities, associations and organisations that act against gender-based violence from civil society.
j)	To encourage the specialisation of professional groups involved in the process of information, care and protection of victims.
k)	To guarantee the principle of mainstreaming of measures, so that in their application the specific needs and demands of all women victims of gender-based violence are taken into account.

TITLE I

Measures for awareness-raising, prevention and detection

Article 3. Awareness plans

1.	Under the responsibility of the State Government and immediately upon the entry into force of this law, with the corresponding budgetary allocation, a permanent State Plan for Awareness and Prevention of Gender Violence shall be launched, which shall include at least the following elements:
a)	That it introduces into the social scenario new scales of values based on respect for fundamental rights and freedoms and equality between men and women, as well as on the exercise of tolerance and freedom within the democratic principles of coexistence, all from the perspective of gender relations.
b)	Directed at both men and women, from a community and intercultural work approach, including the field of information technologies and the digital sphere.
c)	That it contemplates a broad programme of complementary training and retraining of professionals who intervene in these situations.
d)	Controlled by a Commission with broad participation, which shall be created within a maximum period of one month, in which the presence of victims and their environment, institutions, professionals and persons of recognised social prestige related to the treatment of these issues must be ensured.
The Government Delegation against Gender Violence, having heard the Commission referred to in the preceding paragraph, shall prepare the annual evaluation Report of the State Plan for Awareness and Prevention of Gender Violence and shall submit it to the Cortes Generales.
2.	Public authorities, within the framework of their competences, shall also promote specific information and awareness campaigns in order to prevent gender violence.

3.	Information and awareness campaigns against this form of violence shall be carried out in such a way as to guarantee access to them for persons with disabilities.

CHAPTER I

In the educational field

Article 4. Principles and values of the education system

1.	The Spanish education system shall include among its aims training in respect for fundamental rights and freedoms and equality between men and women, as well as in the exercise of tolerance and freedom within the democratic principles of coexistence.
Likewise, the Spanish education system shall include, within its quality principles, the elimination of obstacles that hinder full equality between men and women and training for conflict prevention and for their peaceful resolution.
2.	Early Childhood Education shall contribute to developing in children learning in the peaceful resolution of conflicts.
3.	Primary Education shall contribute to developing in pupils their capacity to acquire skills in the peaceful resolution of conflicts and to understand and respect equality between the sexes.
4.	Compulsory Secondary Education shall contribute to developing in pupils the capacity to relate to others in a peaceful manner and to know, value and respect equal opportunities for men and women.
5.	Baccalaureate and Vocational Training shall contribute to developing in pupils the capacity to consolidate their personal, social and moral maturity, enabling them to act in a responsible and autonomous manner and to critically analyse and assess gender inequalities and promote real and effective equality between men and women.
6.	Education for adults shall include among its objectives the development of activities in the peaceful resolution of conflicts and the promotion of respect for the dignity of persons and equality between men and women.
7.	Universities shall include and promote in all academic fields training, teaching and research in gender equality and non-discrimination in a transversal manner.

Article 5. Immediate schooling in cases of gender violence

The competent Administrations must provide for the immediate schooling of children who are affected by a change of residence arising from acts of gender violence.

Article 6. Promotion of equality

In order to guarantee effective equality between men and women, educational Administrations shall ensure that all educational materials eliminate sexist or discriminatory stereotypes and that they promote the equal value of men and women.

Article 7. Initial and continuing training of teachers

Educational Administrations shall adopt the necessary measures to ensure that initial and continuing teacher training plans include specific training on equality, in order to ensure that they acquire the knowledge and techniques necessary to enable them to:
a)	Educate in respect for fundamental rights and freedoms and equality between men and women and in the exercise of tolerance and freedom within the democratic principles of coexistence.
b)	Educate in conflict prevention and in their peaceful resolution, in all areas of personal, family and social life.

c)	Early detection of violence in the family sphere, especially against women and sons and daughters.
d)	The promotion of attitudes aimed at the exercise of equal rights and obligations by women and men, both in the public and private spheres, and co-responsibility between them in the domestic sphere.

Article 8. Participation in School Councils

The necessary measures shall be adopted to ensure that School Councils promote the adoption of educational measures that foster real and effective equality between men and women. For the same purpose, in the State School Council the representation of the Women's Institute and of organisations that defend the interests of women, with presence throughout the national territory, shall be ensured.

Article 9. Action of the educational inspectorate

Educational inspection services shall ensure compliance with and application of the principles and values set out in this chapter in the education system aimed at promoting real equality between women and men.

CHAPTER II

In the field of advertising and the media

Article 10. Unlawful advertising

In accordance with the provisions of Law 34/1988, of 11 November, General Advertising Law, advertising that uses the image of women in a degrading or discriminatory manner shall be considered unlawful.

Article 11

The public Entity responsible for ensuring that audiovisual media comply with their obligations shall adopt the appropriate measures to ensure treatment of women in accordance with constitutional principles and values, without prejudice to possible actions by other entities.

Article 12. Holders of the action for cessation and rectification

The Special Government Delegation against Violence against Women, the Women's Institute or equivalent body of each Autonomous Community, the Public Prosecutor's Office and Associations whose sole objective is the defence of women's interests shall be entitled to bring before the Courts the action for cessation of unlawful advertising for using the image of women in a degrading manner, under the terms of Law 34/1988, of 11 November, General Advertising Law.

Article 13. Media

1. Public Administrations shall ensure strict compliance with legislation regarding the protection and safeguarding of fundamental rights, with special attention to the eradication of conduct favouring situations of inequality of women in all social media, in accordance with current legislation.
2. The public Administration shall promote self-regulation agreements that, having effective preventive control and extrajudicial dispute resolution mechanisms, contribute to compliance with advertising legislation.

Article 14

The media shall promote the protection and safeguarding of equality between men and women, avoiding all discrimination between them.
The dissemination of information relating to violence against women shall guarantee, with the corresponding informative objectivity, the defence of human rights, freedom and

dignity of women victims of violence and their children. In particular, special care shall be taken in the graphic treatment of information.

CHAPTER III

In the health field

Article 15. Awareness-raising and training

1. Health Administrations, within the Interterritorial Council of the National Health System, shall promote and drive actions by health professionals for the early detection of gender-based violence and shall propose the measures they deem necessary in order to optimise the contribution of the health sector in the fight against this type of violence.
2. In particular, awareness-raising and continuous training programmes for health personnel shall be developed in order to improve and promote early diagnosis, assistance and rehabilitation of women in situations of gender-based violence referred to in this Law.
3. The competent educational Administrations shall ensure that in the curricular areas of degree and diploma courses, and in the specialisation programmes of socio-health professions, content aimed at training for prevention, early detection, intervention and support for victims of this form of violence is incorporated.
4. In the relevant National Health Plans, a section on prevention and comprehensive intervention in gender-based violence shall be included.

Article 16. Interterritorial Council of the National Health System

Within the Interterritorial Council of the National Health System, a Commission against Gender-based Violence shall be established, within one year from the entry into force of this Law, which shall provide technical support and guide the planning of the health measures contemplated in this chapter, evaluate and propose those necessary for the implementation of the health protocol and any other measures deemed necessary for the health sector to contribute to the eradication of this form of violence.
The Commission against Gender-based Violence of the Interterritorial Council of the National Health System shall be composed of representatives of all the Autonomous Communities with competence in the matter.
The Commission shall issue an annual report which shall be sent to the State Observatory on Violence against Women and to the Plenary of the Interterritorial Council.

TITLE II

Rights of women victims of gender-based violence

CHAPTER I

Right to information, comprehensive social assistance and free legal assistance

Article 17. Guarantee of the rights of victims

1. All women victims of gender-based violence have guaranteed the rights recognised in this law, without there being any discrimination in access to them.
2. Information, comprehensive social assistance and legal assistance to victims of gender-based violence, under the terms regulated in this chapter, contribute to making their constitutional rights to physical and moral integrity, to freedom and security and to equality and non-discrimination on grounds of sex real and effective.
3. Information and guidance services, immediate psychosocial assistance, by telephone and online, 24-hour legal advice, reception services and comprehensive social assistance,

consisting of legal, psychological and social guidance for victims of violence against women shall have the character of essential services.
In the event that any situation arises that hinders access to or provision of such services, the competent public administrations shall adopt the necessary measures to guarantee their normal operation and their adaptation, if necessary, to the specific needs of victims arising from the situation of such exceptional situation.
Likewise, the normal operation and provision of the system for monitoring by telematic means of compliance with precautionary measures and penalties prohibiting approach in matters of gender-based violence shall be guaranteed.

Article 18. Right to information

1. Women victims of gender-based violence have the right to receive full information and advice appropriate to their personal situation, without there being any discrimination in access to them, through the services, bodies or offices that public administrations may provide.
Such information shall include the measures contemplated in this law relating to their protection and safety, and the rights and aid provided for therein, as well as that relating to the place of provision of care, emergency, support and comprehensive recovery services.
2. It shall be guaranteed, through the necessary means, that women with disabilities who are victims of gender-based violence have comprehensive access to information about their rights and about existing resources. This information must be offered in a format accessible and comprehensible to persons with disabilities, such as sign language or other modalities or communication options, including alternative and augmentative systems.
3. Likewise, the necessary means shall be articulated so that women victims of gender-based violence who, due to their personal and social circumstances, may have greater difficulty in comprehensive access to information, have guaranteed the effective exercise of this right. The information must be accessible to women who do not know Spanish or, where applicable, the other official language of their territory of residence.

Article 19. Right to comprehensive care

1. Women victims of gender-based violence have the right to social services for care, emergency assistance, support and shelter, and comprehensive recovery. The organisation of these services by the autonomous communities and Local Corporations shall respond to the principles of permanent care, urgent action, specialisation of benefits and professional multidisciplinarity.
2. Multidisciplinary care shall involve in particular:
a) Information to victims.
b) Psychological care.
c) Social support.
d) Follow-up of claims for women's rights.
e) Educational support to the family unit.
f) Preventive training in the values of equality aimed at their personal development and the acquisition of skills in the non-violent resolution of conflicts.
g) Support for training and labour market integration.
3. The services shall adopt organisational formulas which, through the specialisation of their staff and their characteristics of convergence and integration of actions, guarantee the effectiveness of the indicated principles, ensuring, in all cases, their availability, accessibility and quality. In all cases, an equitable territorial distribution of services shall be sought and their accessibility to women in rural areas and other remote areas shall be guaranteed.
4. These services shall act in a coordinated manner and in collaboration with the Security Forces, the Courts for Violence against Women, the health services and the institutions responsible for providing legal assistance to victims in the corresponding geographical area.

These services may request from the judicial body the urgent measures they consider necessary.
5. Minors who are under the parental authority or custody of the assaulted person, or who live in family contexts in which acts of gender-based violence are committed, shall also have the right to comprehensive social assistance through these social services. For these purposes, the social services must have specifically trained staff to care for minors, in order to effectively prevent and avoid situations that may cause psychological and physical harm to minors living in family environments where gender-based violence exists. In particular, they must have child psychology professionals for the care of minor daughters and sons who are victims of gender-based violence, including vicarious violence.
6. The instruments and procedures for cooperation between the General State Administration and the Administration of the autonomous communities in the matters regulated in this article shall include commitments to contribute, by the General State Administration, financial resources specifically referred to the provision of services.
7. The equality bodies shall guide and assess the programmes and actions carried out and shall issue recommendations for their improvement.

Article 19

bis. Right to health care.
1. The Public Health System shall guarantee women victims of gender-based violence, as well as their sons and daughters, the right to health care, with special psychological and psychiatric care, and to monitoring of the evolution of their state of health until their full recovery, with regard to the symptoms or psychological and physical sequelae arising from the situation of violence suffered. Likewise, the health services must have child psychologists for the care of minor sons and daughters who are victims of vicarious violence.
2. These services shall be provided, guaranteeing the privacy and intimacy of women and respect, in all cases, for the decisions they make in relation to their health care.
3. Likewise, specific measures shall be established for the detection, intervention and assistance in situations of violence against women with disabilities, women with mental health problems, addictions or other problems or other cases of addictions derived from or added to the violence.

Article 20. Legal assistance

1. Victims of gender-based violence have the right to receive free legal advice immediately prior to filing a complaint, and to free defence and representation by a lawyer and solicitor in all proceedings and administrative procedures that have a direct or indirect cause in the violence suffered. In these cases, a single legal counsel must assume the defence of the victim, provided that this adequately guarantees their right of defence. This right shall also assist the successors in title in the event of the victim's death, provided they were not participants in the facts. In all cases, free and specialised legal defence shall be guaranteed immediately to all victims of gender-based violence who request it.
2. In all cases, when it comes to guaranteeing the defence and legal assistance to victims of gender-based violence, proceedings shall be conducted in accordance with the provisions of Law 1/1996, of 10 January, on Free Legal Assistance.
3. Bar Associations, when they require specialisation courses for the exercise of the duty roster, shall ensure specific training that contributes to the professional exercise of an effective defence in matters of gender-based violence.
4. Likewise, Bar Associations shall adopt the necessary measures for the urgent appointment of a duty lawyer in proceedings for gender-based violence and to ensure their immediate presence and assistance to victims.

5. Solicitors' Associations shall adopt the necessary measures for the urgent appointment of a solicitor in proceedings for gender-based violence when the victim wishes to appear as a private prosecutor.
6. The lawyer appointed for the victim shall also have legal authorisation for the procedural representation of the victim until the appointment of the solicitor, as long as the victim has not appeared as a prosecutor in accordance with the provisions of the following paragraph. Until then, the lawyer shall fulfil the duty of indicating an address for the purposes of notifications and transmission of documents.
7. Victims of gender-based violence may appear as a private prosecutor at any time during the proceedings, although this shall not allow the reversal or repetition of actions already carried out before their appearance, nor may it result in a reduction of the accused's right of defence.

CHAPTER II

Labour rights and Social Security benefits

Article 21. Labour and Social Security rights

1. The female worker victim of gender-based violence shall have the right, under the terms provided in the Workers' Statute, to the reduction or reorganisation of her working time, to geographical mobility, to change of workplace, to the adaptation of her job position and to the support she may require by reason of her disability for her reincorporation, to the suspension of the employment relationship with reservation of job position and to the termination of the employment contract.
2. Under the terms provided in the General Social Security Act and in Organic Law 4/2000, of 11 January, on the rights and freedoms of foreigners in Spain and their social integration, the suspension and termination of the employment contract provided for in the preceding paragraph shall give rise to a legal situation of unemployment. The period of suspension shall be considered as a period of actual contribution for the purposes of Social Security and unemployment benefits.
3. Companies that formalise interim contracts to replace female workers victims of gender-based violence who have suspended their employment contract or exercised their right to geographical mobility or to change of workplace, shall be entitled to a 100 per cent reduction in employer Social Security contributions for common contingencies, during the entire period of suspension of the replaced worker or for six months in cases of geographical mobility or change of workplace. When reincorporation takes place, it shall be carried out under the same conditions existing at the time of suspension of the employment contract, guaranteeing the reasonable adjustments that may be required by reason of disability.
4. Absences or lack of punctuality at work motivated by the physical or psychological situation arising from gender-based violence shall be considered justified and shall be remunerated, when so determined by the social care services or health services, as appropriate, without prejudice to such absences being communicated by the worker to the company as soon as possible.
5. Self-employed female workers victims of gender-based violence who cease their activity to make effective their protection or their right to comprehensive social assistance, shall be considered in a situation of temporary cessation of activity under the terms provided in the consolidated text of the General Social Security Act, approved by Royal Legislative Decree 8/2015, of 30 October, and their obligation to contribute shall be suspended for a period of six months, which shall be considered as actual contribution for the purposes of Social Security benefits. Likewise, their situation shall be considered as assimilated to registration.
For the purposes of the provisions of the preceding paragraph, a contribution base equivalent to the average of the bases contributed during the six months prior to the suspension of the obligation to contribute shall be taken.

Article 22. Specific employment programme

1. Within the framework of the annual employment plans referred to in Article 11 of the consolidated text of the Employment Act, approved by Royal Legislative Decree 3/2015, of 23 October, a specific action programme shall be developed for victims of gender-based violence registered as jobseekers.
This programme shall include measures to favour the start of a new self-employment activity.

Article 23. Accreditation of situations of gender-based violence

Situations of gender-based violence that give rise to the recognition of the rights regulated in this law shall be accredited by means of a conviction judgment for any of the manifestations of violence against women provided for in this law, a protection order or any other judicial decision that agrees a precautionary measure in favour of the victim, or by the report of the Public Prosecutor's Office indicating the existence of evidence that the applicant is a victim of gender-based violence. Situations of violence against women may also be accredited by means of a report from the social services, specialised services, or reception services of the competent Public Administration intended for victims of gender-based violence, or by any other title, provided that this is provided for in the regulatory provisions of a sectoral nature that regulate access to each of the rights and resources.
In the case of minor victims, accreditation may also be carried out by official health documents communicating to the Public Prosecutor's Office or to the judicial body.
The Government and the autonomous communities, within the framework of the Sectoral Conference on Equality, shall design, by common agreement, the basic procedures that allow the implementation of systems for accrediting situations of gender-based violence.

CHAPTER III

Rights of female public servants

Article 24. Scope of rights

The female public servant victim of gender-based violence shall have the right to the reduction or reorganisation of her working time, to geographical mobility of workplace and to leave of absence under the terms determined in her specific legislation.

Article 25. Justification of absences

Total or partial absences from work motivated by the physical or psychological situation arising from gender-based violence suffered by a female public servant shall be considered justified under the terms determined in her specific legislation.

Article 26

Accreditation of situations of gender-based violence exercised against female public servants.
The accreditation of the circumstances that give rise to the recognition of the rights to geographical mobility of workplace, leave of absence, and reduction or reorganisation of working time, shall be carried out under the terms established in Article 23.

CHAPTER IV

Economic rights

Article 27. Social assistance

1.	When victims of gender-based violence lack income exceeding, on a monthly basis, 75 percent of the minimum interprofessional wage, excluding the proportional part of two extraordinary payments, they shall receive a one-time payment assistance, provided that it is presumed that due to their age, lack of general or specialized training and social circumstances, the victim will have special difficulties in obtaining employment and

due to such circumstance will not participate in the employment programmes established for their professional integration.
2.	The amount of this assistance shall be equivalent to six months of unemployment benefit. When the victim of violence exercised against women has officially recognized disability equal to or greater than 33 percent, the amount shall be equivalent to twelve months of unemployment benefit.
3.	These assistance payments, financed from the General State Budget, shall be granted by the Administrations competent in social services matters. In the processing of the granting procedure, a report from the Public Employment Service must be incorporated referring to the predictability that due to the circumstances referred to in paragraph 1 of this article, the application of the employment programme will not have a substantial impact on improving the victim's employability.
The concurrence of circumstances of violence shall be accredited in accordance with the provisions of Article 23 of this Law.
4.	In the event that the victim has family responsibilities, its amount may reach that of a period equivalent to 18 months of benefit, or 24 months if the victim or any of the family members living with her has officially recognized disability equal to or greater than 33 percent, in the terms established by the implementing provisions of this Law.
5.	These assistance payments shall be compatible with any of those provided for in Law 35/1995, of 11 December, on Assistance and Aid to Victims of Violent Crimes and against Sexual Freedom, as well as with any other economic assistance of an autonomous or local nature granted for the situation of gender-based violence.

Article 28. Access to housing and public residences for the elderly

Women victims of gender-based violence shall be considered priority groups in access to protected housing and public residences for the elderly, in the terms determined by applicable legislation.

CHAPTER V

Right to reparation

Article 28

bis. Scope and guarantee of the right.
Victims of gender-based violence have the right to reparation, which includes economic compensation for damages and harm derived from the violence, the necessary measures for their complete physical, psychological and social recovery, symbolic reparation actions and guarantees of non-repetition.

Article 28

ter. Measures to guarantee the right to reparation.
1.	Victims of gender-based violence have the right to reparation, which includes the compensation referred to in the following paragraph, the necessary measures for their complete physical, psychological and social recovery, symbolic reparation actions and guarantees of non-repetition.
2.	Public administrations shall ensure that victims have effective access to the compensation corresponding to damages and harm, which must guarantee the economically assessable satisfaction of, at least, the following concepts:
a)	Physical and psychological harm, including moral harm and harm to dignity.
b)	Loss of opportunities, including opportunities for education, employment and social benefits.
c)	Material damages and loss of income, including loss of profits.
d)	Social harm, understood as harm to life project.
e)	Therapeutic, social and sexual and reproductive health treatment.
3.	Compensation shall be satisfied by the person or persons civilly or criminally liable, in accordance with current regulations.

4.	Public administrations shall guarantee the complete physical, psychological and social recovery of victims through the network of comprehensive care resources provided for in Title II. Likewise, with the objective of guaranteeing symbolic recovery, they shall promote the restoration of their dignity and reputation, the overcoming of any situation of stigmatization and the right of deletion applied to Internet search engines and public media outlets.
Likewise, public administrations may establish complementary assistance intended for victims who, due to the specificity or severity of the sequelae derived from the violence, do not find an adequate or sufficient response in the network of care and recovery resources. In particular, such victims may receive additional assistance to finance appropriate health treatments, including female genital reconstruction treatments, if necessary.
5.	With the objective of fulfilling guarantees of non-repetition, public administrations, within the framework of their respective competences, shall promote the necessary measures so that victims have effective protection against reprisals or threats.
6.	Public administrations shall promote, through tributes and public dissemination actions, collective commitment against violence against women and respect for victims.

TITLE III

Institutional Protection

Article 29. The Government Delegation against Gender-based Violence

1.	The Government Delegation against Gender-based Violence, attached to the Ministry of Equality or to the department with competences in the matter, shall formulate public policies in relation to gender-based violence to be developed by the Government and shall prepare the Macro-survey on Violence against Women. Likewise, it shall coordinate and promote all actions carried out in said matter, working in collaboration and coordination with the administrations with competence in the matter.
2.	The head of the Government Delegation against Gender-based Violence shall be entitled before judicial bodies to intervene in defense of the rights and interests protected in this law in collaboration and coordination with the administrations with competences in the matter.
3.	The rank and specific functions of the head of the Government Delegation against Gender-based Violence shall be determined by regulation.

Article 30. State Observatory on Violence against Women

1. The State Observatory on Violence against Women shall be established as a collegiate body attached to the Ministry of Labour and Social Affairs, which shall be responsible for advice, evaluation, institutional collaboration, preparation of reports and studies, and proposals for action on gender-based violence. These reports, studies and proposals shall give special consideration to the situation of women at greater risk of suffering gender-based violence or with greater difficulties in accessing services. In any case, the data contained in such reports, studies and proposals shall be recorded disaggregated by sex.
2. The State Observatory on Violence against Women shall submit to the Government and the Autonomous Communities, on an annual basis, a report on the evolution of violence against women in the terms referred to in Article 1 of this Law, specifying the criminal offences that have been applied, and the effectiveness of the measures agreed for the protection of victims. The report shall also highlight the needs for legal reform in order to ensure that the application of the protection measures adopted can ensure the maximum level of protection for women.
3. Its functions, its operating regime and its composition shall be determined by regulation, which shall guarantee, in any case, the participation of the Autonomous Communities, local entities, social partners, consumer and user associations, and women's organisations with presence throughout the territory of the State as well as the most representative business and trade union organisations.

Article 31. Security Forces and Corps

1. The Government shall establish, in the State Security Forces and Corps, specialised units in the prevention of gender-based violence and in monitoring the execution of judicial measures adopted.
2. The Government, in order to make the protection of victims more effective, shall promote the necessary actions so that Local Police, within the framework of their collaboration with the State Security Forces and Corps, cooperate in ensuring compliance with the measures agreed by the judicial bodies when these are any of those provided for in this Law or in Article 544 bis of the Criminal Procedure Act or in Article 57 of the Criminal Code.
3. The action of the Security Forces and Corps shall take into account the Protocol of Action of the Security Forces and Corps and of Coordination with the Judicial Bodies for the protection against domestic and gender-based violence.
4. The provisions of this article shall apply in the Autonomous Communities that have police forces that perform the functions of protection of persons and property and the maintenance of order and public safety within the autonomous territory, in the terms provided for in their Statutes, in Organic Law 2/1986, of 13 March, on Security Forces and Corps, and in their police laws, all with the aim of making the protection of victims more effective.

Article 32. Collaboration plans

1. Public authorities shall prepare collaboration plans that guarantee the coordination of their actions in the prevention, assistance and prosecution of acts of gender-based violence, which must involve the health administrations, the Administration of Justice, the Security Forces and Corps and the social services and equality bodies.
2. In development of such plans, action protocols shall be articulated that determine the procedures that ensure a global and comprehensive action of the different administrations and services involved, and that guarantee the evidentiary activity in the proceedings that follow.
3. Administrations with health competences shall promote the application, permanent updating and dissemination of protocols containing uniform guidelines for health action, both in the public and private spheres, and in particular, of the Protocol approved by the Interterritorial Council of the National Health System.
Such protocols shall promote prevention activities, early detection and continued intervention with women subjected to gender-based violence or at risk of suffering it.
The protocols, in addition to referring to the procedures to be followed, shall make express reference to relations with the Administration of Justice, in those cases where there is confirmation or well-founded suspicion of physical or psychological harm caused by these aggressions or abuses.
4. In the actions provided for in this article, special consideration shall be given to the situation of women who, due to their personal and social circumstances, may be at greater risk of suffering gender-based violence or have greater difficulties in accessing the services provided for in this law, such as those belonging to minorities, immigrants, those in situations of social exclusion, women with disabilities, older women or those living in rural areas.

Article 37. Protection against ill-treatment

CHAPTER I

On the Courts for Violence against Women

Article 43. Territorial organisation

An article 87 bis is added to Organic Law 6/1985, of 1 July, on the Judiciary, with the following wording:
'1. In each judicial district there shall be one or more Courts for Violence against Women, with their seat in the capital thereof and jurisdiction throughout its territorial scope. They shall take their designation from the municipality of their seat.

3. Article 44. Jurisdiction

An article 87 ter is added to Organic Law 6/1985, of 1 July, on the Judiciary, with the following wording:
'1. The Courts for Violence against Women shall have jurisdiction, in criminal matters, in accordance in all cases with the procedures and appeals provided for in the Criminal Procedure Act, over the following cases:
a) The preliminary proceedings for criminal liability for offences contained in the titles of the Criminal Code relating to homicide, abortion, injuries, injuries to the foetus, offences against freedom, offences against moral integrity, against freedom and sexual indemnity or any other offence committed with violence or intimidation, provided that they have been committed against a person who is or has been his wife, or a woman who is or has been linked to the perpetrator by an analogous relationship of affection, even without cohabitation, as well as those committed against descendants, whether his own or those of the wife or cohabitant, or against minors or incapacitated persons who live with him or who are subject to the parental authority, guardianship, curatorship, fostering or de facto care of the wife or cohabitant, when an act of gender-based violence has also occurred.

c) The adoption of the corresponding protection orders for victims, without prejudice to the powers attributed to the Duty Judge.
d) The jurisdiction and judgment of minor offences contained in titles I and II of book III of the Criminal Code, when the victim is any of the persons designated as such in subparagraph a) of this paragraph.
2. The Courts for Violence against Women may have jurisdiction in civil matters, in all cases in accordance with the procedures and appeals provided for in the Civil Procedure Act, over the following matters:

c) Those concerning parent-child relationships.
d) Those having as their object the adoption or modification of measures of family significance.
e) Those concerning exclusively the care and custody of minor children or maintenance claimed by one parent against the other in the name of minor children.
f) Those concerning the need for consent in adoption.
g) Those having as their object opposition to administrative decisions on the protection of minors.
3. The Courts for Violence against Women shall have exclusive jurisdiction in civil matters when the following requirements are met simultaneously:
a) That it is a civil proceeding having as its object any of the matters indicated in number 2 of this article.
b) That one of the parties to the civil proceeding is a victim of acts of gender-based violence, in the terms referred to in paragraph 1 a) of this article.

CHAPTER II

Civil procedural rules

Article 57

Loss of subject-matter jurisdiction when acts of violence against women occur.
A new article 49 bis is added to Law 1/2000, of 7 January, on Civil Procedure, the wording of which is as follows:
«Article 49 bis.	Loss of jurisdiction when acts of violence against women occur.
1.	When a Judge, who is hearing a civil proceeding at first instance, becomes aware of the commission of an act of violence as defined in article 1 of the Organic Law on Integrated Protection Measures against Gender Violence, which has given rise to the initiation of criminal proceedings or to a protection order, after verifying the concurrence of the requirements provided for in paragraph 3 of article 87 ter of the Organic Law of the Judiciary, must decline jurisdiction, remitting the case file in the state in which it is found to the competent Court on Violence against Women, unless the oral trial phase has commenced.
2.	When a Judge who is hearing a civil proceeding becomes aware of the possible commission of an act of gender violence, which has not given rise to the initiation of criminal proceedings, nor to the issuing of a protection order, after verifying that the requirements of paragraph 3 of article 87 ter of the Organic Law of the Judiciary are met, must immediately summon the parties to an appearance with the Public Prosecutor's Office to be held within the following 24 hours so that the latter may take cognisance of all relevant data concerning the events that have occurred. Following this, the Prosecutor must, immediately, decide whether to proceed, within the following 24 hours, to report the acts of gender violence or to request a protection order before the competent Court on Violence against Women. In the event that a complaint is filed or a protection order is requested, the Prosecutor must deliver a copy of the complaint or request to the Court, which shall continue to hear the matter until it is, where appropriate, required to decline jurisdiction by the competent Court on Violence against Women.
3.	When a Court on Violence against Women that is hearing a criminal case for gender violence becomes aware of the existence of civil proceedings, and verifies the concurrence of the requirements of paragraph 3 of article 87 ter of the Organic Law of the Judiciary, it shall require the Civil Court to decline jurisdiction, which must immediately order its declination and the remission of the case file to the requiring body.
For the purposes of the preceding paragraph, the requirement to decline jurisdiction shall be accompanied by testimony of the initiation of preliminary proceedings or of misdemeanour proceedings, of the order admitting the criminal complaint, or of the protection order adopted.
4.	In the cases provided for in paragraphs 1 and 2 of this article, the Civil Court shall remit the case file to the Court on Violence against Women without the provisions of article 48.3 of the Law on Civil Procedure being applicable, the parties being required from that moment to appear before said body.
In these cases the remaining rules of this section shall not apply, nor shall a plea of lack of jurisdiction be admitted, parties wishing to assert the jurisdiction of the Court on Violence against Women being required to present testimony of any of the decisions issued by said Court referred to in the final paragraph of the preceding number.

5.	The Courts on Violence against Women shall exercise their jurisdiction in civil matters exclusively and to the exclusion of others, and in all cases in accordance with the procedures and remedies provided for in the Law on Civil Procedure.»

CHAPTER III

Criminal procedural rules

Article 58. Jurisdiction in criminal matters

Article 14 of the Criminal Procedure Act is amended, and shall read as follows:
"Outside the cases that expressly and limitedly attribute jurisdiction to specific Judges and Courts under the Constitution and the laws, the following shall have jurisdiction:
1. For the hearing and judgment of proceedings for minor offences, the Investigating Judge, unless jurisdiction corresponds to the Judge for Violence against Women in accordance with paragraph five of this article. However, the Justice of the Peace of the place where they were committed shall hear proceedings for minor offences classified in articles 626, 630, 632 and 633 of the Criminal Code. Justices of the Peace shall also hear proceedings for minor offences classified in article 620.1.º and 2.º of the Criminal Code, except when the victim is one of the persons referred to in article 173.2 of the same Code.
2. For the investigation of cases, the Investigating Judge of the district in which the offence was committed, or the Judge for Violence against Women, or the Central Investigating Judge with respect to the offences determined by law.
3. For the hearing and judgment of cases for offences to which the law assigns a custodial sentence of duration not exceeding five years or a fine of any amount, or any other sentences of a different nature, whether single, joint or alternative, provided that their duration does not exceed ten years, as well as for minor offences, whether or not incidental, attributable to the perpetrators of these offences or to other persons, when the commission of the minor offence or its proof is related to them, the Criminal Judge of the district where the offence was committed, or the Criminal Judge corresponding to the district of the Court for Violence against Women as appropriate, or the Central Criminal Judge within its own scope, without prejudice to the jurisdiction of the Duty Investigating Judge of the place where the offence was committed to issue a judgment by consent, or of the competent Judge for Violence against Women as appropriate, under the terms established in article 801.
Notwithstanding, in cases within the jurisdiction of the Criminal Judge, if the offence is one of those attributed to the Jury Court, the hearing and judgment shall correspond to the latter.
4. For the hearing and judgment of cases in all other instances, the Provincial Court of the district where the offence was committed, or the Provincial Court corresponding to the district of the Court for Violence against Women as appropriate, or the Criminal Chamber of the National Court.
Notwithstanding, in cases within the jurisdiction of the Provincial Court, if the offence is one of those attributed to the Jury Court, the hearing and judgment shall correspond to the latter.
5. The Courts for Violence against Women shall have jurisdiction in the following matters, in all cases in accordance with the procedures and appeals provided for in this Act:
a) For the investigation of proceedings to demand criminal liability for offences contained in the titles of the Criminal Code relating to homicide, abortion, injuries, injuries to the foetus, offences against freedom, offences against moral integrity, against freedom and sexual indemnity or any other offence committed with violence or intimidation, provided that they have been committed against a person who is or has been his wife, or a woman who is or has been linked to the perpetrator by an analogous relationship

of affection, even without cohabitation, as well as those committed against descendants, whether his own or those of his wife or cohabitant, or against minors or incapacitated persons who live with him or who are subject to the authority, guardianship, curatorship, fostering or de facto care of his wife or cohabitant, when an act of gender-based violence has also occurred.
b) For the investigation of proceedings to demand criminal liability for any offence against family rights and duties, when the victim is one of the persons identified as such in the preceding paragraph.
c) For the adoption of the corresponding protection orders for victims, without prejudice to the powers attributed to the Duty Judge.
d) For the hearing and judgment of minor offences contained in titles I and II of book III of the Criminal Code, when the victim is one of the persons identified as such in paragraph a) of this section."

Article 59. Territorial jurisdiction

A new article 15 bis is added to the Criminal Procedure Act, which shall read as follows:
"In the case of any of the offences or minor offences whose investigation or hearing corresponds to the Judge for Violence against Women, territorial jurisdiction shall be determined by the place of the victim's domicile, without prejudice to the adoption of the protection order, or urgent measures under article 13 of this Act that may be adopted by the Judge of the place where the events were committed."

Article 60. Jurisdiction by connection

A new article 17 bis is added to the Criminal Procedure Act, which shall read as follows:
"The jurisdiction of the Courts for Violence against Women shall extend to the investigation and hearing of connected offences and minor offences provided that the connection originates in any of the circumstances provided for in paragraphs 3.º and 4.º of article 17 of this Act."

CHAPTER IV

Judicial measures for the protection and security of victims

Article 61. General provisions

1. The protection and security measures provided for in this chapter shall be compatible with any of the precautionary and security measures that may be adopted in civil and criminal proceedings.
2. In all proceedings related to gender-based violence, the competent Judge must rule in all cases, ex officio or at the request of the victims, the children, persons living with them or subject to their care or custody, the Public Prosecutor's Office or the Administration on which the victim assistance or reception services depend, on the appropriateness of adopting the precautionary and security measures contemplated in this chapter, especially those set out in articles 64, 65 and 66, determining their duration and their regime of compliance and, if appropriate, the complementary measures to them that may be necessary.

Article 62. On the protection order

Upon receipt of an application for the adoption of a protection order, the Judge for Violence against Women and, as appropriate, the Duty Judge, shall act in accordance with the provisions of article 544 ter of the Criminal Procedure Act.

Article 63. On data protection and limitations on publicity

1. In proceedings and procedures related to gender-based violence, the privacy of victims shall be protected; in particular, their personal data, those of their descendants and those of any other person who is under their care or custody.
2. The competent Judges may order, ex officio or at the request of a party, that hearings be held in camera and that proceedings be confidential.

Article 64

On measures of leaving the home, restraining order or suspension of communications.
1. The Judge may order the mandatory departure of the accused of gender-based violence from the home in which he had been living or where the family unit has its residence, as well as the prohibition to return to it.
2. The Judge, on an exceptional basis, may authorize the protected person to arrange, with an agency or public company where one exists and which includes among its activities the rental of housing, the exchange of the attributed use of the family home of which they are co-owners, for the use of another home, for the time and under the conditions to be determined.
3. The Judge may prohibit the accused from approaching the protected person, which prevents him from approaching her in any place where she may be, as well as approaching her home, her place of work or any other place frequented by her.
The use of instruments with appropriate technology may be agreed upon to verify immediately any breach thereof.
The Judge shall establish a minimum distance between the accused and the protected person which may not be reduced, under warning of incurring criminal liability.
4. The restraining measure may be agreed upon regardless of whether the affected person, or those whom it is intended to protect, had previously left the place.
5. The Judge may prohibit the accused from all kinds of communication with the person or persons indicated, under warning of incurring criminal liability.
6. The measures referred to in the preceding paragraphs may be agreed upon cumulatively or separately.

Article 65

On measures of suspension of parental authority or custody of minors.
The Judge may suspend for the accused of gender-based violence the exercise of parental authority, guardianship and custody, foster care, tutorship, curatorship or de facto guardianship, with respect to minors who depend on him.
If suspension is not agreed upon, the Judge must in any case rule on the manner in which parental authority and, where applicable, guardianship and custody, foster care, tutorship, curatorship or de facto guardianship of the minors shall be exercised. Likewise, he shall adopt the necessary measures to guarantee the safety, integrity and recovery of the minors and of the woman, and shall carry out periodic monitoring of their progress.

Article 66

On the measure of suspension of the visitation, stay, relationship or communication regime with minors.
The Judge shall order the suspension of the visitation, stay, relationship or communication regime of the accused of gender-based violence with respect to the minors who depend on him. If, in the best interest of the minor, suspension is not agreed upon, the Judge must in any case rule on the manner in which the stay, relationship or communication regime of the accused of gender-based violence with respect to the minors who depend on him shall be exercised. Likewise, he shall adopt the necessary measures to guarantee the safety, integrity and recovery of the minors and of the woman, through specialized care services, and shall carry out periodic monitoring of their progress, in coordination with said services.

Article 67

On the measure of suspension of the right to possession, carrying and use of weapons.
The Judge may agree, with respect to those accused of crimes related to the violence referred to in this Law, the suspension of the right to possession, carrying and use of

weapons, with the obligation to deposit them under the terms established by current regulations.

Article 68. Guarantees for the adoption of measures

The rights-restrictive measures contained in this chapter must be adopted by means of a reasoned order in which their proportionality and necessity are assessed, and, in all cases, with the intervention of the Public Prosecutor's Office and respect for the principles of adversarial proceedings, hearing and defense.

Article 69. Maintenance of protection and security measures

The measures of this chapter may be maintained after the final judgment and during the processing of any applicable appeals. In this case, the maintenance of such measures must be recorded in the judgment.

CHAPTER V

On the Prosecutor against Violence against Women

Article 70. Functions of the Prosecutor against Violence against Women

An article 18 quater is added to Law 50/1981, of 30 December, regulating the Organic Statute of the Public Prosecutor's Office, with the following wording:
«1. The State Attorney General shall appoint, after hearing the Fiscal Council, as delegate, a Prosecutor against Violence against Women, with the rank of Chamber Prosecutor, who shall exercise the following functions:
a) Carry out the proceedings referred to in article 5 of the Organic Statute of the Public Prosecutor's Office, and intervene directly in those criminal proceedings of special significance appreciated by the State Attorney General, concerning crimes for acts of gender-based violence included in article 87 ter.1 of the Organic Law of the Judiciary.
b) Intervene, by delegation of the State Attorney General, in civil proceedings included in article 87 ter.2 of the Organic Law of the Judiciary.
c) Supervise and coordinate the actions of the Sections against Violence against Women, and request reports from them, informing the Chief Prosecutor of the Prosecutor's Offices in which they are integrated.
d) Coordinate the action criteria of the various Prosecutor's Offices in matters of gender-based violence, for which purpose he may propose to the State Attorney General the issuance of the corresponding instructions.
e) Prepare biannually, and present to the State Attorney General, for transmission to the Board of Chamber Prosecutors of the Supreme Court, and to the Fiscal Council, a report on the proceedings followed and actions carried out by the Public Prosecutor's Office in matters of gender-based violence.
2. For its adequate performance, the professionals and experts necessary to assist him on a permanent or occasional basis shall be assigned to him.»

Five. A new paragraph 5 is added to article 30 of Law 30/1984, of 2 August, on Measures for the Reform of the Civil Service with the following content:
"5. In cases where female civil servants who are victims of gender-based violence have to be absent from their job for this reason, these absences, total or partial, shall be considered justified for the time and under the conditions determined by the social care or health services, as appropriate. Female civil servants who are victims of violence against women, in order to make effective their protection or their right to comprehensive social assistance, shall have the right to the reduction of working hours with proportional reduction of remuneration, or to the reorganization of working time, through the adaptation of the schedule, the application of flexible hours or other forms of organization of working time that are applicable, under the terms established by the competent Public Administration in each case for these cases."

Tenth additional provision. Amendment of the Organic Law of the Judiciary. One. Paragraph two of article 26 of Organic Law 6/1985, of 1 July, of the Judiciary, is amended and is worded as follows:
"Article 26. Courts of First Instance and Preliminary Proceedings, Commercial Courts, Courts on Violence against Women, Criminal Courts, Administrative Courts, Labour Courts, Juvenile Courts and Prison Supervision Courts."
Two. The heading of chapter V of title IV of book I of Organic Law 6/1985, of 1 July, of the Judiciary, is amended and is worded as follows:
"Chapter V. On the Courts of First Instance and Preliminary Proceedings, Commercial Courts, Criminal Courts, Courts on Violence against Women, Administrative Courts, Labour Courts, Prison Supervision Courts and Juvenile Courts."
Three. Paragraph 1 of article 87 of Organic Law 6/1985, of 1 July, of the Judiciary, is amended and is worded as follows:
"Article 87. 1. The Courts of Preliminary Proceedings shall have jurisdiction, in the criminal order:
a) Over the preliminary proceedings of cases for crimes whose trial corresponds to the Provincial Courts and the Criminal Courts, except for those cases that are the competence of the Courts on Violence against Women. b) They are also responsible for issuing judgments in conformity with the prosecution in the cases established by Law.c) The hearing and determination of trials for misdemeanours, except those that are the competence of the Justices of the Peace, or of the Courts of Violence against Women.
d) Habeas corpus proceedings.
e) Appeals established by law against resolutions issued by the Justices of the Peace of the district and questions of jurisdiction between them.
f) The adoption of protection orders for victims of violence against women when performing on-call duties, provided that it cannot be adopted by the Court of Violence against Women.

Three bis. A new paragraph is added to section 2 of article 89 bis of the Organic Law of the Judiciary, with the following content:

"In order to facilitate the hearing of cases investigated by the Courts of Violence against Women, and taking into account the number of existing cases, one or more Courts in each province must be specialized, in accordance with the provisions of article 98 of this Law."

Four. Section 1 of article 210 of Organic Law 6/1985, of 1 July, of the Judiciary, is amended to read as follows:

"1. The Judges of First Instance and Investigation, Commercial, Criminal, Violence against Women, Administrative, Minors and Social Courts shall substitute for each other in localities where there are several of the same jurisdictional order, in the manner agreed by the Governing Chamber of the High Court of Justice, at the proposal of the Board of Judges."

Five. A new paragraph is added to section 3 of article 211 of Organic Law 6/1985, of 1 July, of the Judiciary, which reads as follows:

"The Judges of Violence against Women shall be substituted by the Investigating Judges or First Instance and Investigation Judges, according to the order established by the Governing Chamber of the respective High Court of Justice."

Eleventh additional provision. Evaluation of the application of the Law.

The Government, in collaboration with the Autonomous Communities, three years after the entry into force of this Organic Law, shall prepare and submit to the Congress of Deputies a report evaluating the effects of its application in the fight against gender-based violence.

Twelfth additional provision. Amendments to the Criminal Procedure Law.

A fourth additional provision is added to the Criminal Procedure Law with the following content:

"1. The references made to the Investigating Judge and the First Instance Judge in sections 1 and 7 of article 544 ter of this Law, in the wording given by Law 27/2003, of 31 July, regulating the Protection Order for Victims of Domestic Violence, shall be understood as made, where applicable, to the Judge of Violence against Women.

2. The references made to the Duty Judge in title III of book IV, and in articles 962 to 971 of this Law, shall be understood as made, where applicable, to the Judge of Violence against Women."

Thirteenth additional provision. Funding of the Fund.

In order to contribute to the implementation of the services established in article 19 of this Law, and to guarantee inter-territorial equity in their implementation, during the two years following the entry into force of this Law, a Fund shall be established to which the Autonomous Communities may have access, in accordance with the objective criteria to be determined in the respective Sectoral Conference. Notwithstanding this, the Autonomous Community of the Basque Country and the Foral Community of Navarre shall be governed, in these financial aspects, by their special regimes of Economic Agreement and Convention.

The Autonomous Communities, in the exercise of their powers, during the year following the approval of this Law, shall carry out a diagnosis jointly with the Local Administrations, on the impact of gender-based violence in their Community, as well as an assessment of needs, resources and services necessary to implement article 19 of this Law.

The allocation of the Fund shall be made in accordance with the provisions of the respective General State Budget Laws.

Fourteenth additional provision. Report on financing.

Without prejudice to the financial responsibility of the Autonomous Communities, in accordance with the provisions of Law 21/2001, of 27 December, regulating the fiscal and administrative measures of the new financing system for the Autonomous Communities of common regime and Cities with Statute of Autonomy, and in accordance with the principle of institutional loyalty in the terms of article 2.1.e) of Organic Law 8/1980, of 22 September, on Financing of the Autonomous Communities, the competent Ministries, at the proposal of the corresponding inter-territorial bodies, shall prepare reports on the economic repercussions of the application of this Law. These reports shall be submitted

to the Ministry of Economy and Finance, which shall forward them to the Fiscal and Financial Policy Council.

Fifteenth additional provision. Agreements on housing matters.

Through agreements with the competent Administrations, the Government may promote specific processes for the allocation of protected housing to victims of gender-based violence.

Sixteenth additional provision. Coordination of Public Employment Services.

In the development of Law 56/2003, of 16 December, on Employment, the necessary coordination of the Public Employment Services shall be taken into account, to facilitate access to the labour market for victims of gender-based violence when, due to the exercise of the right to geographical mobility, they are obliged to relocate their domicile and this implies a change of Autonomous Community.

Seventeenth additional provision. School enrolment.

The educational authorities shall adopt the necessary measures to guarantee the immediate school enrolment of children in the event of a change of residence motivated by violence against women.

Eighteenth additional provision. Structure of the Courts of Violence against Women.

An annex XIII is added to Law 38/1988, of 28 December, on Judicial Demarcation and Structure, the text of which is included as an annex to this Organic Law.

Nineteenth additional provision. Maintenance payment guarantee fund.

Within the framework of protection against economic violence in the terms provided for in this law, the State shall guarantee the payment of recognised and unpaid maintenance in favour of minor children in judicially approved agreements or in judicial resolutions, through specific legislation that shall specify the coverage system in such cases and which, in any event, shall take into account the circumstances of victims of gender-based violence.

To strengthen support measures for victims of economic violence, the Government shall modify the current regulation of the Maintenance Payment Guarantee Fund in order to improve its accessibility, effectiveness and financial allocation, through the amendment of Royal Decree 1618/2007, of 7 December, on the organisation and operation of the Maintenance Payment Guarantee Fund.

Twentieth additional provision. Change of surnames.

Article 58 of the Civil Registry Law, of 8 June 1957, reads as follows:

"2. When exceptional circumstances exist, and despite the absence of the requirements indicated in that article, the change may be granted by Royal Decree at the proposal of the Ministry of Justice, with the hearing of the Council of State. In the event that the applicant for authorisation to change their surnames is a victim of gender-based violence and in any other case where the urgency of the situation so requires, the change may be granted by Order of the Ministry of Justice, in the terms established by the Regulations."

Twenty-first additional provision. Macro-survey on violence against women.

The Government Delegation against Gender-based Violence shall conduct and publish the results of the Macro-survey on violence against women provided for in article 29 of this law with a minimum three-yearly frequency.

First transitional provision. Application of measures.

Civil or criminal proceedings related to gender-based violence that are in progress at the entry into force of this Law shall continue to be the competence of the bodies that have been hearing them until their conclusion by final judgment.

Second transitional provision. Transitional law.

In proceedings concerning facts contemplated in this Law that are in progress at its entry into force, the Courts or Tribunals hearing them may adopt the measures provided for in chapter IV of title V.

Sole derogatory provision.

All provisions, of equal or lower rank, that are contrary to the provisions of this Law are hereby repealed.

First final provision. Regulatory references.

All references and mentions contained in criminal procedural laws to Investigating Judges must also be understood as referring to Judges of Violence against Women in matters within their competence.

Second final provision. Competence authorisation.

This Law is enacted pursuant to the provisions of article 149.1, 1st, 5th, 6th, 7th, 8th, 17th, 18th and 30th of the Spanish Constitution.

Third final provision. Nature of this Law.

This Law has the character of an Organic Law, with the exception of the following provisions: title I, title II, title III, articles 42, 43, 44, 45, 46, 47, 70, 71, 72, as well as the first, second, sixth, seventh, eighth, ninth, eleventh, thirteenth, fifteenth, sixteenth, seventeenth, eighteenth, nineteenth and twentieth additional provisions, the second transitional provision and the fourth, fifth and sixth final provisions.

Fourth final provision. Regulatory authorisation.

1. The Government is authorised to issue, within six months from the publication of this Law in the "Official State Gazette", the provisions necessary for its application. Through the Ministry of Justice, the necessary measures shall be adopted within the aforementioned period for the implementation of the Courts of Violence against Women, as well as for the adaptation of the structure of the Public Prosecutor's Office to the provisions of this Law.

2. Within six months from the entry into force of this Organic Law, the General Council of the Judiciary shall issue the necessary regulations for the organisation of hearings, adaptation of on-call services to the existence of the new Courts of Violence against Women, and coordination of the Judicial Police with the aforementioned Courts.

Fifth final provision. Regulatory amendments.

The Government, within six months from the approval of this Law, shall proceed to amend article 116.4 of Royal Decree 190/1996, of 9 February, approving the Penitentiary Regulations, establishing the obligation for the Penitentiary Administration to carry out the specific treatment programmes for inmates referred to in this Law.

Within the same period, Royal Decree 738/1997, of 23 May, approving the Regulations on aid to victims of violent crimes and crimes against sexual freedom, and Royal Decree 996/2003, of 25 July, approving the Regulations on free legal assistance, shall be amended.

Within the period mentioned in the previous paragraph, the State and the Autonomous Communities, within the scope of their respective powers, shall adapt their regulations to the provisions contained in this Law.

Sixth final provision. Amendment of Law 1/1996, of 10 January, on Free Legal Assistance.

Section 5 of article 3 of Law 1/1996, of 10 January, on Free Assistance, is amended to read as follows:

"5. Nor shall it be necessary for victims of gender-based violence to prove in advance that they lack resources when they request specialised free legal defence, which shall be provided to them immediately, without prejudice to the fact that if the right thereto is not subsequently recognised, they must pay the lawyer the fees accrued for their intervention."

Seventh final provision. Entry into force.

This Organic Law shall enter into force thirty days after its publication in the "Official State Gazette", except for the provisions of titles IV and V, which shall do so after six months.

Therefore,

I command all Spaniards, individuals and authorities, to observe and enforce this organic law.

Madrid, 28 December 2004.
