Law on Protection from Domestic Violence

Law on Protection from Domestic Violence
The Law was published in the "Official Gazette of Montenegro", No. 46/2010.
See: Art. 152 of the Law - 40/2011-1.

I.	BASIC PROVISIONS
Subject of the law

Article 1

Protection from domestic violence (hereinafter: protection) is exercised in accordance with this law.
Domestic violence

Article 2

Domestic violence (hereinafter: violence), within the meaning of this law, is an act or omission by a family member that endangers the physical, psychological, sexual or economic integrity, mental health and peace of another family member, regardless of the place where it was committed.
Family members

Article 3

Family members, within the meaning of this law, are considered to be:
1)	spouses or former spouses, their children and the children of each of them;
2)	common-law spouses or former common-law spouses, regardless of the duration of the common-law union, their children and the children of each of them;
3)	blood relatives and relatives from full adoption in direct line without limitation, and in collateral line up to and including the fourth degree;
4)	relatives from incomplete adoption;
5)	relatives by marriage up to and including the second degree in marital or common-law union;
6)	persons living in the same family household, regardless of kinship;
7)	persons who have a common child or the child has been conceived.
Right to assistance and protection

Article 4

A victim of violence (hereinafter: victim) has the right to psychosocial and legal assistance and social and medical protection, in accordance with the law.
Protection of the victim is also ensured by imposing protective measures.
Special assistance and protection is enjoyed by a victim who is a child, an elderly person, a person with a disability and a person who is not capable of taking care of themselves, in accordance with the law.
Institutions dealing with protection

Article 5

The administrative body competent for police affairs (hereinafter: police), the authority for misdemeanours, the State Prosecutor's Office, the social work centre or other institution for social and child protection, health institution, as well as other bodies and institutions dealing with protection are obliged to provide, within their powers, complete and coordinated protection that is necessary for the protection of the victim depending on the degree of their endangerment.
A non-governmental organization, other legal entity and natural person may provide protection in accordance with the law.
The bodies and institutions from paragraph 1 of this Article are obliged to give priority to resolving cases of violence in accordance with the law and to ensure mutual notification and assistance in order to prevent and detect violence, eliminate causes and provide assistance to the victim in establishing conditions for a safe life.
Urgency of procedure

Article 6

In procedures relating to protection, the bodies and institutions from Article 5 paragraph 1 of this law are obliged to act urgently, taking into account that the interest and welfare of the victim, and especially if the victim is a child,

an elderly person, a person with a disability and a person who is not capable of taking care of themselves, are a priority in those procedures.
Use of gender-sensitive language

Article 7

Terms used in this law for natural persons in the masculine gender include the same terms in the feminine gender.
II.	PROTECTION OF THE VICTIM
1.	Forms and methods of providing assistance and protection
Forms of violence

Article 8

Endangering the physical, psychological, sexual or economic integrity, mental health and peace of another family member is considered to exist especially if a family member:
1)	uses physical force, regardless of whether bodily injury to another family member has occurred;
2)	threatens with an attack or causes danger that may cause a feeling of personal insecurity or psychological pain to another family member;
3)	verbally attacks, curses, calls by a derogatory name and otherwise insults another family member;
4)	restricts another family member's freedom to communicate with third parties;
5)	exhausts by work, denies sleep and other types of rest, threatens with eviction from the apartment and taking away children;
6)	sexually harasses another family member;
7)	stalks and otherwise grossly harasses another family member;
8)	damages or destroys common property or the property of another family member or attempts to do so;
9)	denies basic means of subsistence to another family member;
10)	by rude behaviour endangers the family peace of a family member with whom they do not live in a family community.
Endangering the physical, psychological, sexual or economic integrity, mental health and peace of another family member is also considered to exist if a family member does not take sufficient care of:
1)	nutrition, hygiene, clothing, medical protection or regular school attendance or does not prevent the child from harmful association, vagrancy, begging or theft or otherwise to a greater extent neglects the upbringing and education of the child;
2)	nutrition, hygiene, clothing or medical protection of another family member whom they are obliged to care for, and who needs special assistance due to illness, disability, old age or other personal characteristics due to which they are not capable of taking care of themselves.
A more serious form of domestic violence is considered to be the concealment of a family member with special needs.

Duty to report violence

Article 9

State bodies, other bodies, health, educational and other institutions are obliged to report to the police committed violence of which they become aware in the performance of duties within their competence or activity.
A report on committed violence must be submitted to the police by the responsible person in the body or institution from paragraph 1 of this Article, as well as by a health and social worker, teacher, educator and other person who becomes aware of committed violence in the performance of their duties.
The authority for misdemeanours and the police are obliged to notify the social work centre of reported violence.
Emergency intervention

Article 10

After becoming aware of violence, the police shall without delay undertake actions and measures for the purpose of protecting the victim, in accordance with this law and laws governing the work and powers of the police, misdemeanour procedure, criminal procedure and witness protection.
The social work centre, or other institution for social and child protection, health institution, as well as other bodies and institutions dealing with protection are obliged to, without delay, provide protection and assistance to the victim in accordance with their competences.
The bodies and institutions from paragraph 2 of this Article are obliged to take care of all the needs of the victim and enable them access to all forms of assistance and protection.

Plan for assistance to the victim

Article 11

The social work centre forms an expert team consisting of representatives of that institution, bodies and services of local administration, police, non-governmental organizations and experts dealing with family issues, in order to determine the plan for assistance to the victim and coordinate activities in the process of assisting the victim, in accordance with their needs and choice.
The plan for assistance to the victim specifically contains measures that need to be undertaken in accordance with the law governing social and child protection. If the victim is a child, the plan for assistance to the victim also contains measures for the protection of the child in accordance with the law governing family relations.
For the purpose of implementing activities from paragraph 1 of this Article, the expert team may also be formed by another body, institution or organization dealing with protection.

Social protection

Article 12

Social protection of the victim includes material and non-material assistance, accommodation and social work services, in accordance with the law governing social and child protection.
Social work centres are obliged to establish precise records of children and persons with special needs in their territory.
For the purpose of protection from violence against these persons, the centres are obliged to form special teams that will, at least once a month, visit families in which children and persons with special needs are accommodated or live, monitor the treatment of these persons and, based on the established situation, prepare a written report.
Social work centres are obliged to, at least once every six months, submit a written report to the ministry competent for social welfare affairs on the degree of realisation of social protection of children and persons with special needs.

Legal assistance

Article 13

The victim has the right to free legal assistance, in accordance with a special law.

Security of the victim

Article 14

A police officer is obliged to accompany the victim to the apartment or other residential space when taking personal belongings that are necessary for their daily life, unless the victim expressly objects to this.
The action from paragraph 1 of this article may also be carried out to secure temporary accommodation and care for the victim.
Confidentiality of proceedings

Article 15

The bodies, institutions, organizations and other legal and natural persons from Article 5 of this law are obliged to maintain the confidentiality of information and ensure the protection of personal data, in accordance with the law.
Information based on which the victim or a family member of the victim can be identified must not be publicly disclosed about the victim or the family member who committed the violence (hereinafter: perpetrator of violence), unless the adult victim has expressly consented to this.

Confidential person

Article 16

The victim may choose a person who will be present at all procedures and actions related to protection (hereinafter: confidential person).
The confidential person may be a family member, a person from a body, institution, non-governmental organization and other legal entity, or another person in whom the victim has confidence.
The confidential person cannot be the perpetrator of violence.
The victim may choose a confidential person before or during the procedure and undertaking of actions related to protection.
The competent bodies are obliged to enable the presence of the confidential person in all procedures and actions in which the victim is involved, and which are related to family relations.

Multidisciplinary team and principle of cooperation

Article 17

For the purpose of organizing, monitoring and improving coordinated and efficient protection, the body or institution from Article 5 paragraph 1 of this law may form a team of experts from the fields of social and child protection, health protection, justice, police protection, protection of human rights and freedoms, as well as representatives of non-governmental organizations dealing with protection.

Protection strategy

Article 18

Protection is implemented in accordance with the strategy for protection from domestic violence (hereinafter: Strategy), which contains:
1)	assessment of the situation and identification of key problems in social and other protection;
2)	objectives and measures for improving social and other protection, particularly in relation to: raising the level of citizens' awareness about the problem of violence and forming attitudes about the unacceptability of violence; development of programs for violence prevention; support to the family in violence prevention; further development of the normative framework in the field of protection; strengthening cooperation of bodies, institutions, organizations and other legal and natural persons dealing with protection; acquisition of new knowledge and skills of all those dealing with protection; improvement of the system for collecting and analyzing data and reporting on cases of violence.
Activities for implementing the objectives and measures from paragraph 1 item 2 of this article are determined by the action plan for implementing the Strategy.
The Strategy and the action plan for implementing the Strategy are adopted by the Government of Montenegro.
2.	Protective measures
Purpose of protective measures

Article 19

Protective measures are imposed for the purpose of preventing and suppressing violence, eliminating the consequences of committed violence and undertaking effective measures for re-education of the perpetrator of violence and eliminating circumstances that favor or encourage the commission of new violence.

Types of protective measures

Article 20

One or more protective measures may be imposed on the perpetrator of violence:
1)	removal from the apartment or other residential space (hereinafter: removal from the apartment);
2)	prohibition of approach;
3)	prohibition of harassment and stalking;
4)	mandatory treatment for addiction;
5)	mandatory psychosocial treatment.
The bodies and institutions from Article 5 paragraph 1 of this law are obliged to provide the perpetrator of violence with appropriate information about their rights.

Removal from the apartment

Article 21

Removal from the apartment may be imposed on the perpetrator of violence who lives with the victim in an apartment or other residential space, regardless of the ownership and other rights of the perpetrator of violence and the victim to the apartment or other residential space, if there is a danger that they will commit violence again.
The perpetrator of violence to whom removal from the apartment has been imposed is obliged to immediately leave the apartment or other residential space.
Removal from the apartment is imposed for a duration that cannot be shorter than thirty days nor longer than six months.
Prohibition of approach

Article 22

Prohibition of approach to the victim may be imposed on the perpetrator of violence if there is a danger that they will commit violence again or if the presence of the perpetrator of violence near the victim would create a high degree of mental suffering in the victim which, for a shorter or longer time, prevents the victim's normal mental activities.
The misdemeanor authority is obliged to determine in the decision on prohibition of approach the place or area in which the perpetrator of violence must not approach the victim.
Prohibition of approach is imposed for a duration that cannot be shorter than thirty days nor longer than one year.
Prohibition of harassment and stalking

Article 23

Prohibition of harassment and stalking of the victim may be imposed on the perpetrator of violence if there is a danger that they will commit violence again.
The protective measure from paragraph 1 of this article is imposed for a duration that cannot be shorter than thirty days nor longer than one year.

Mandatory treatment for addiction

Article 24

Mandatory treatment for addiction may be imposed on the perpetrator of violence who commits violence as a result of addiction to alcohol, narcotic drugs or psychotropic substances, and there is a danger that they will commit violence again due to that addiction.
The protective measure from paragraph 1 of this article lasts as long as there is a need for treatment, but no longer than one year.
Mandatory psychosocial treatment

Article 25

Mandatory psychosocial treatment may be imposed on the perpetrator of violence for the purpose of eliminating the causes of violent behavior and re-education, that is, with the aim of reducing and eliminating the danger that they will commit violence again.
The protective measure from paragraph 1 of this article lasts until the cessation of the reasons for which it was imposed, but no longer than six months.
The detailed manner of determining and implementing the protective measure from paragraph 1 of this article is prescribed by the ministry responsible for social welfare affairs.
3.	Procedure for determining protective measures
Imposition of protective measure

Article 26

A protective measure may be imposed together with a penalty or as an independent sanction.
The misdemeanor authority may impose one or more protective measures on the perpetrator of violence when the conditions for their imposition prescribed by this law exist.
The misdemeanor authority may decide to extend the duration of the protective measures from Articles 21 to 25 of this law if the reasons for which they were determined have not ceased, but no longer than two years.

Applicants

Article 27

A request for determining a protective measure (hereinafter: request) may be submitted by the victim or their representative, the social work center, that is, another institution of social and child protection, the police or the state prosecutor.
The misdemeanor authority may also determine a protective measure ex officio.

Order

Article 28

A police officer may, in order to eliminate danger to the physical integrity of the victim, order the perpetrator of violence to be removed or prohibited from returning to an apartment or other residential premises, which may not last longer than three days.
A written order on removal or prohibition from returning to an apartment or other residential premises must be delivered by the police officer to the perpetrator of violence and the victim immediately, and at the latest within two hours, in the presence of an adult person who may be another police officer, and who is not a family member.
The written order from paragraph 2 of this article must contain: the day and hour of removal or prohibition from returning to an apartment or other residential premises; the boundaries of the area in which the perpetrator of violence may not move, stay or approach the victim and the address of residence of the perpetrator of violence for the duration of the removal or prohibition from returning.
When leaving the apartment or other residential premises, the perpetrator of violence has the right to take his personal belongings necessary for daily life and the obligation to hand over to the police officer the keys to the apartment or other residential premises.
The police officer attaches the written order from paragraph 2 of this article to the official report on the incident and immediately, and at the latest within 12 hours, notifies the misdemeanor authority and the social welfare centre.
The misdemeanor authority shall, within the time limit from paragraph 1 of this article, decide whether to impose a protective measure.
The detailed content and appearance of the form of the written order from paragraph 2 of this article and the manner of carrying out actions in the application of the provisions of this article shall be prescribed by the ministry responsible for internal affairs.

Imposition of a protective measure before and during proceedings

Article 29

If it assesses that it is necessary to protect the victim without delay, the misdemeanor authority may impose a protective measure before initiating and during proceedings, at the latest within 48 hours from the moment of receiving the request.
The misdemeanor authority may request from the social welfare centre or other social and child protection institution to provide assistance in obtaining necessary evidence and to present its opinion on the appropriateness of the requested protective measure.
If the request was submitted before initiating proceedings, and the applicant of that request does not submit a request to initiate proceedings within five days, the misdemeanor authority shall revoke the imposed protective measure.
The misdemeanor authority is obliged to warn the applicant of the consequences of failing to submit the request from paragraph 3 of this article.
Appeal

Article 30

An appeal against the decision on determining a protective measure may be filed within three days from the day of delivery of the decision.
The appeal is decided by the second instance authority within three days from the day of receiving the appeal. The appeal does not delay the execution of the decision on determining a protective measure.

Extension, termination and replacement of a protective measure imposed before and during proceedings

Article 31

A protective measure imposed before and during proceedings may last until the reasons for which the measure was determined cease, and at the longest until the completion of proceedings.
Before the completion of proceedings, the misdemeanor authority may replace a protective measure imposed before and during proceedings with another protective measure.

Obligation to comply with a protective measure

Article 32

The perpetrator of violence is obliged to act in accordance with the imposed protective measure.
A person who, within the scope of his work, learns that the perpetrator of violence is not acting in accordance with the imposed protective measure is obliged to notify the misdemeanor authority, the social welfare centre, or other social and child protection institution, the police or the state prosecutor.

Execution of the decision

Article 33

The decision on the imposed protective measure must be delivered by the misdemeanor authority immediately to the authority or institution competent for carrying out the execution, and at the latest within three days from the day of issuing the decision.
The decision on the imposed protective measure from Articles 21, 22 and 23 of this law is delivered for execution to the police.
The decision on the imposed protective measure from Articles 24 and 25 of this law is delivered to the authority, or institution that executes the protective measure in accordance with the law governing the treatment and rehabilitation of persons dependent on psychoactive substances (alcoholics and drug addicts) and persons with other behavioural disorders.
The detailed manner of executing the decision on the imposed protective measure from Articles 21, 22 and 23 of this law shall be prescribed by the ministry responsible for internal affairs, and the detailed manner of executing the decision on the imposed protective measure from Articles 24 and 25 of this law shall be prescribed by the ministry responsible for health affairs.

Delivery of the decision and records

Article 34

The decision on the imposed protective measure must be delivered by the misdemeanor authority to the social welfare centre in whose territory the victim and the perpetrator of violence have residence or domicile.
Records on reported cases of violence, victims, perpetrators of violence, imposed protective measures, as well as on other protection and assistance measures are kept by the authorities and institutions from Article 5 paragraph 1 of this law, in accordance with their competences and they deliver them to the ministry responsible for the protection of human and minority rights.
Corresponding application of the law

Article 35

The provisions of laws governing the work and powers of the police, misdemeanor proceedings, criminal proceedings, criminal sanctions and their application and execution apply to the procedure for determining and implementing protective measures, unless otherwise regulated by this law.

Misdemeanor liability of a family member

Article 36

A fine of 150 euros or imprisonment of at least ten days shall be imposed for a misdemeanor on a family member if he:
1)	uses physical force, regardless of whether bodily injury to another family member has occurred;
2)	threatens with an attack or causes danger that may cause a feeling of personal insecurity or mental pain to another family member;
3)	verbally attacks, swears, calls by a derogatory name and otherwise insults another family member;
4)	restricts another family member's freedom to communicate with third parties;
5)	exhausts by work, denies sleep and other rest, threatens with eviction from the apartment and taking away children;
6)	sexually harasses another family member;
7)	stalks and otherwise grossly harasses another family member;
8)	damages or destroys common property or property of another family member or attempts to do so;
9)	denies basic means of subsistence to another family member;
10)	by insolent behaviour endangers the family peace of a family member with whom he does not live in a family community (

Article 8 paragraph 1)

A fine of at least 250 euros or imprisonment of at least twenty days shall be imposed for a misdemeanor on an adult family member if he commits violence from paragraph 1 of this article in the presence of a child.
A fine of at least 500 euros or imprisonment of at least thirty days shall be imposed for a misdemeanor on a family member who commits violence from paragraph 1 of this article if the victim is a child.
A fine of at least 1,000 euros or imprisonment of at least 60 days shall be imposed for a misdemeanor on a family member who conceals a family member with special needs (

Article 8 paragraph 3)

Article 37

A fine of 250 euros or imprisonment of at least ten days shall be imposed for a misdemeanor on a family member if he does not take sufficient care:

Liability for failure to comply with an order

Article 38

A perpetrator of violence who fails to comply with a police officer's order to remove themselves or prohibition to return to an apartment or other residential premises shall be punished for a misdemeanour by a fine of at least 800 euros or imprisonment of at least forty days (

Article 28

paragraphs 1 and 2).

Misdemeanour liability of another person

Article 39

A fine of 100 euros to 500 euros shall be imposed for a misdemeanour on:
1)	a responsible person in a state body, other body, health, educational or other institution, a health or social worker, teacher, educator or other person who fails to report to the police violence committed of which they become aware in the performance of their duties (

Article 9 paragraph 2);

2)	a person who in the scope of their work becomes aware that a perpetrator of violence is not acting in accordance with an imposed protective measure and fails to notify the misdemeanour authority, social welfare centre or other social and child protection institution, the police or the state prosecutor thereof (

Subject matter of the law

Article 1

Protection from domestic violence (hereinafter: protection) shall be exercised in accordance with this law.
Domestic violence

Article 2

Domestic violence (hereinafter: violence), within the meaning of this law, is an act or omission by a family member which endangers the physical, psychological, sexual or economic integrity, mental health and peace of another family member, regardless of the place where it was committed.
Family members

Article 3

Family members, within the meaning of this law, shall be considered:
1)	spouses or former spouses, their children and the children of each of them;
2)	extramarital partners or former extramarital partners, regardless of the duration of the extramarital union, their children and the children of each of them;

4)	relatives from incomplete adoption;
5)	relatives by marriage up to and including the second degree in a marital or extramarital union;
6)	persons living in the same family household, regardless of kinship;

Article 4

Protection of the victim shall also be ensured by imposing protective measures.
Special assistance and protection shall be enjoyed by a victim who is a child, an elderly person, a person with a disability or a person who is not capable of taking care of themselves, in accordance with the law.
Institutions dealing with protection

Article 5

The administrative body responsible for police affairs (hereinafter: police), the misdemeanour authority, the State Prosecutor's Office, the social welfare centre or other social and child protection institution, health institution, as well as other bodies and institutions dealing with protection shall be obliged to provide, within their powers, complete and coordinated protection that is necessary for the protection of the victim depending on the degree of their endangerment.
A non-governmental organisation, other legal entity or natural person may provide protection in accordance with the law.
The bodies and institutions referred to in paragraph 1 of this Article shall be obliged to give priority to resolving cases of violence in accordance with the law and to ensure mutual notification and assistance in order to prevent and detect violence, eliminate causes and provide assistance to the victim in establishing conditions for a safe life.
Urgency of procedure

Article 6

In procedures relating to protection, the bodies and institutions referred to in Article 5 paragraph 1 of this law shall be obliged to act urgently, taking into account that the interest and welfare of the victim, and especially if the victim is a child, an elderly person, a person with a disability or a person who is not capable of taking care of themselves, is a priority in those procedures.

Use of gender-sensitive language

Article 7

Terms used in this law for natural persons in the masculine gender include the same terms in the feminine gender.

Protection shall be realised in accordance with the strategy for protection from domestic violence (hereinafter: Strategy), which contains:
1)	assessment of the situation and identification of key problems in social and other protection;
2)	objectives and measures for improving social and other protection, and in particular in relation to: raising the level of citizens' awareness of the problem of violence and forming attitudes on the unacceptability of violence; development of programmes for violence prevention; support to the family in violence prevention; further development of the normative framework in the field of protection; strengthening cooperation of bodies, institutions, organisations and other legal and natural persons dealing with protection; acquisition of new knowledge and skills of all those dealing with protection; improvement of the system for collection and analysis of data and reporting on cases of violence.
Activities for implementing the objectives and measures from paragraph 1 item 2 of this article shall be determined by an action plan for implementing the Strategy.
The Strategy and the action plan for implementing the Strategy shall be adopted by the Government of Montenegro.
2.	Protective measures
Purpose of protective measures

Article 19

Protective measures shall be imposed for the purpose of preventing and suppressing violence, eliminating the consequences of committed violence and undertaking efficient measures for re-education of the perpetrator of violence and eliminating circumstances that favour or encourage the commission of new violence.

Types of protective measures

Article 20

One or more protective measures may be imposed on the perpetrator of violence:
1)	removal from the apartment or other residential space (hereinafter: removal from the apartment);
2)	prohibition of approach;
3)	prohibition of harassment and stalking;
4)	mandatory treatment for addiction;
5)	mandatory psychosocial treatment.
Bodies and institutions from Article 5 paragraph 1 of this law are obliged to provide the perpetrator of violence with appropriate information about his rights.

Removal from the apartment

Article 21

Removal from the apartment may be imposed on the perpetrator of violence who lives with the victim in an apartment or other residential space, regardless of the ownership and other rights of the perpetrator of violence and the victim to the apartment or other residential space, if there is a danger that he will commit violence again.
The perpetrator of violence to whom removal from the apartment has been imposed is obliged to immediately leave the apartment or other residential space.
Removal from the apartment shall be imposed for a duration that cannot be shorter than thirty days nor longer than six months.
Prohibition of approach

Article 22

Prohibition of approach to the victim may be imposed on the perpetrator of violence if there is a danger that he will commit violence again or if the presence of the perpetrator of violence in the vicinity of the victim would create in the victim a high degree of mental suffering which, for a shorter or longer time, prevents the normal mental activities of the victim.
The misdemeanour body is obliged to determine in the decision on prohibition of approach the place or area in which the perpetrator of violence must not approach the victim.
Prohibition of approach shall be imposed for a duration that cannot be shorter than thirty days nor longer than one year.
Prohibition of harassment and stalking

Article 23

Prohibition of harassment and stalking of the victim may be imposed on the perpetrator of violence if there is a danger that he will commit violence again.
The protective measure from paragraph 1 of this article shall be imposed for a duration that cannot be shorter than thirty days nor longer than one year.

Mandatory treatment for addiction

Article 24

Mandatory treatment for addiction may be imposed on the perpetrator of violence who commits violence as a result of addiction to alcohol, narcotic drugs or psychotropic substances, and there is a danger that he will commit violence again due to that addiction.
The protective measure from paragraph 1 of this article shall last as long as there is a need for treatment, but not longer than one year.
Mandatory psychosocial treatment

Article 25

Mandatory psychosocial treatment may be imposed on the perpetrator of violence in order to eliminate the causes of violent behavior and re-education, that is, with the aim of reducing and eliminating the danger of committing violence again.
The protective measure from paragraph 1 of this article lasts until the cessation of the reason for which it was imposed, but no longer than six months.
The detailed manner of determining and implementing the protective measure from paragraph 1 of this article shall be prescribed by the ministry responsible for social welfare affairs.
3. Procedure for determining protective measures
Imposition of protective measure

Article 26

A protective measure may be imposed together with a penalty or as an independent sanction.
The misdemeanor authority may impose one or more protective measures on the perpetrator of violence when the conditions for their imposition prescribed by this law exist.
The misdemeanor authority may decide to extend the duration of the protective measures from Articles 21 to 25 of this law if the reasons for which they were determined have not ceased, but no longer than two years.

Applicants

Article 27

A request for determining a protective measure (hereinafter: request) may be submitted by the victim or their representative, the social welfare center, that is, another social and child protection institution, the police or the state prosecutor.
The misdemeanor authority may determine a protective measure ex officio.

Order

Article 28

A police officer may, in order to eliminate danger to the physical integrity of the victim, order the perpetrator of violence to be removed or prohibited from returning to an apartment or other residential space, which may not last longer than three days.
A written order on removal or prohibition from returning to an apartment or other residential space must be delivered by the police officer to the perpetrator of violence and the victim immediately, and at the latest within two hours, in the presence of an adult person who may be another police officer, and who is not a family member.
The written order from paragraph 2 of this article must contain: the day and hour of removal or prohibition from returning to an apartment or other residential space; the boundaries of the area in which the perpetrator of violence may not move, stay or approach the victim and the address of residence of the perpetrator of violence for the duration of the removal or prohibition from returning.
When leaving the apartment or other residential space, the perpetrator of violence has the right to take their personal belongings necessary for daily life and the obligation to hand over the keys to the apartment or other residential space to the police officer.
The police officer shall attach the written order from paragraph 2 of this article to the official report on the incident and shall immediately, and at the latest within 12 hours, notify the misdemeanor authority and the social welfare center thereof.
The misdemeanor authority shall, within the time limit from paragraph 1 of this article, decide whether to impose a protective measure.
The detailed content and appearance of the written order form from paragraph 2 of this article and the manner of implementing actions in applying the provisions of this article shall be prescribed by the ministry responsible for internal affairs.

Imposition of protective measure before and during proceedings

Article 29

If it assesses that it is necessary to protect the victim without delay, the misdemeanor authority may impose a protective measure before initiating and during proceedings, at the latest within 48 hours from the time of receipt of the request.
The misdemeanor authority may request from the social welfare center or another social and child protection institution to provide assistance in obtaining necessary evidence and to present its opinion on the appropriateness of the requested protective measure.
If the request was submitted before initiating proceedings, and the applicant of that request does not submit a request for initiating proceedings within five days, the misdemeanor authority shall revoke the imposed protective measure.
The misdemeanor authority is obliged to warn the applicant of the consequences of failure to submit the request from paragraph 3 of this article.
Appeal

Article 30

An appeal against the decision on determining a protective measure may be filed within three days from the day of delivery of the decision.
The second instance authority decides on the appeal within three days from the day of receipt of the appeal. The appeal does not delay the execution of the decision on determining a protective measure.

Extension, cessation and replacement of protective measure imposed before and during proceedings

Article 31

A protective measure imposed before and during proceedings may last until the reasons for which the measure was determined cease, and at the longest until the completion of proceedings.
Before the completion of proceedings, the misdemeanor authority may replace a protective measure imposed before and during proceedings with another protective measure.

Obligation to comply with protective measure

Article 32

The perpetrator of violence is obliged to act in accordance with the imposed protective measure.
A person who, in the scope of their work, learns that the perpetrator of violence is not acting in accordance with the imposed protective measure is obliged to notify the misdemeanor authority, the social welfare center, that is, another social and child protection institution, the police or the state prosecutor thereof.

Execution of decision

Article 33

The misdemeanor authority is obliged to deliver the decision on the imposed protective measure immediately to the authority or institution competent for implementing execution, and at the latest within three days from the day of issuing the decision.
The decision on the imposed protective measure from Articles 21, 22 and 23 of this law shall be delivered to the police for execution.
The decision on the imposed protective measure from Articles 24 and 25 of this law shall be delivered to the authority, that is, the institution that executes the protective measure in accordance with the law governing the treatment and rehabilitation of persons dependent on psychoactive substances (alcoholics and drug addicts) and persons with other behavioral disorders.
The detailed manner of executing the decision on the imposed protective measure from Articles 21, 22 and 23 of this law shall be prescribed by the ministry responsible for internal affairs, and the detailed manner of executing the decision on the imposed protective measure from Articles 24 and 25 of this law shall be prescribed by the ministry responsible for health affairs.

Delivery of decision and records

Article 34

The misdemeanor authority is obliged to deliver the decision on the imposed protective measure to the social welfare center in whose territory the victim and the perpetrator of violence have residence or domicile.
Records on reported cases of violence, victims, perpetrators of violence, imposed protective measures, as well as on other protection and assistance measures shall be kept by the authorities and institutions from Article 5 paragraph 1 of this law, in accordance with their competences, and shall be submitted to the ministry responsible for the protection of human and minority rights.
Corresponding application of law

Article 35

The provisions of laws governing the work and powers of the police, misdemeanor procedure, criminal procedure, criminal sanctions and their application and execution shall apply to the procedure for determining and implementing protective measures, unless otherwise regulated by this law.
III. PENAL PROVISIONS
Misdemeanor liability of family member

Article 36

A fine of at least three times the amount of the minimum wage in Montenegro or imprisonment of at least ten days shall be imposed for a misdemeanor on a family member, if they:
1) use physical force, regardless of whether bodily injury to another family member has occurred;
2) threaten with attack or cause danger that may produce a feeling of personal insecurity or mental pain to another family member;
3) verbally attack, curse, call by a derogatory name and otherwise insult another family member;
4) restrict another family member's freedom to communicate with third parties;
5) exhaust through work, deprive of sleep and other rest, threaten with eviction from the apartment and taking away children;
6) sexually harass another family member;
7) stalk and otherwise grossly harass another family member;
8) damage or destroy common property or the property of another family member or attempt to do so;
9) deny basic means of subsistence to another family member;
10) by insolent behavior endanger the family peace of a family member with whom they do not live in a family community (

Article 8 paragraph 1)

A fine of at least five times the amount of the minimum wage in Montenegro or imprisonment of at least twenty days shall be imposed for a misdemeanor on an adult family member if, in the presence of a child, he commits violence referred to in paragraph 1 of this Article.
A fine of at least ten times the amount of the minimum wage in Montenegro or imprisonment of at least thirty days shall be imposed for a misdemeanor on a family member who commits violence referred to in paragraph 1 of this Article if the victim is a child.
A fine of at least twenty times the amount of the minimum wage in Montenegro or imprisonment of at least 60 days shall be imposed for a misdemeanor on a family member who conceals a family member with special needs (

Article 8 paragraph 3)

Misdemeanor liability for neglect

Article 37

A fine of at least five times the amount of the minimum wage in Montenegro or imprisonment of at least ten days shall be imposed for a misdemeanor on a family member if he does not take sufficient care:
1)	of nutrition, hygiene, clothing, medical protection or regular school attendance or does not prevent the child from harmful association, vagrancy, begging or theft or otherwise neglects to a greater extent the upbringing and education of the child (

Article 8 paragraph 2 point 1);

2)	of nutrition, hygiene, clothing or medical protection of another family member whom he is obliged to care for, and who needs special assistance due to illness, disability, old age or other personal characteristics due to which he is not able to take care of himself (

Article 8 paragraph 2 point 2)

Liability for failure to comply with an order

Article 38

A fine of at least fifteen times the amount of the minimum wage in Montenegro or imprisonment of at least forty days shall be imposed for a misdemeanor on a perpetrator of violence if he does not comply with the order of a police officer on removal or prohibition of return to an apartment or other residential premises (

Article 28

paragraphs 1 and 2).
Misdemeanor liability of another person

Article 39

A fine of two to ten times the amount of the minimum wage in Montenegro shall be imposed for a misdemeanor on:
1)	a responsible person in a state body, another body, health, educational and other institution, health and social worker, teacher, educator and other person if he does not report to the police committed violence of which he becomes aware in the performance of his duties (

Article 9 paragraph 2);

2)	a person who, within the scope of his work, learns that the perpetrator of violence does not act in accordance with the imposed protective measure and does not notify the misdemeanor authority, social welfare center or other social and child protection institution, police or state prosecutor thereof (

Article 32 paragraph 2)
