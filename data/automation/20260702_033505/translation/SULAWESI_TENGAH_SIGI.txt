CHAPTER I
GENERAL PROVISIONS
Article 1

In this Regional Regulation what is meant by:
1. Region is Sigi Regency.
2. Regional Government is the Regent as an element of the Regional Government administration who leads the implementation of government affairs that are the authority of the autonomous Region.
3. Regent is the Regent of Sigi.
4. Regional People's Representative Council hereinafter abbreviated as DPRD, is the regional people's representative institution of Sigi Regency as an element of regional government administration.
5. Regional Work Unit hereinafter abbreviated as SKPD, is the Regional Work Unit of Sigi Regency.
6. Child is a person who is not yet 18 years old, including a child in the womb.
7. Woman is an adult human being of female sex and a person who is recognized by law as a woman.
8. Prevention is an activity carried out to prevent the occurrence of acts of violence against women and children.
9. Protection is all service actions to guarantee and protect the rights of victims of acts of violence organized by the Integrated Service Center.
10. Violence is any act that results in or causes suffering and distress both physical, sexual, psychological including neglect, threats of certain actions, coercion or arbitrary deprivation of freedom, whether occurring in public or in private life.
11. Physical violence is any act that causes pain, injury, wounds or disability to a person's body, miscarriage or unconsciousness and/or causes death.
12. Psychological violence is an act that causes fear, loss of self-confidence, loss of ability to act, distrust or severe psychological suffering to a person.
13. Sexual violence is any act in the form of sexual harassment, forced sexual intercourse, whether unnatural or unwanted with another person for commercial purposes and/or certain purposes.
14. Victim is a woman and child who experiences acts of violence.
15. Victim of acts of violence is an individual or group of people who experience suffering whether physical, mental or emotional, economic loss, or experience neglect, reduction or deprivation of their basic rights, as a result of violations of Human Rights (HAM), or criminal acts as well as acts of violence committed either by state apparatus or by the state or Regional Government apparatus or by individuals.
16. Service is immediate activities and actions carried out by professional personnel in accordance with their respective professions in the form of counseling, therapy and advocacy for the strengthening and recovery of victims of violence.
17. Assistance is activities and actions carried out by assistants during the service process.
18. Assistant is a person or representative from an institution who has the expertise to provide assistance.
19. Repatriation is an effort to return victims of acts of violence to the family, substitute family, or community that can provide protection and fulfill their needs.
20. Rehabilitation is the recovery of victims from psychosocial disorders and the restoration of normal social functioning, both in the family and in society.
21. Social Reintegration is an effort to reunite victims with family, community, institutions, or other social environments that can provide protection.
22. Institution is an agency/office/body/office within the scope of regional government and/or non-governmental organizations (NGOs) that provide assistance.
23. Integrated Service Center hereinafter abbreviated as PPT, is an institution providing services to victims of violence, based in hospitals, managed jointly in the form of medical services (including medico-legal), psychosocial and legal services.
24. Integrated Service Center for Women's Empowerment and Child Protection hereinafter abbreviated as P2TP2A, is an integrated service unit established by the Regional Government to provide services in the context of empowerment of women and children victims of acts of violence.
25. Regional Action Plan is a foundation and guideline for related agencies, vertical agencies, and the community, in the context of implementing prevention and protection activities for women and children victims of acts of violence.
26. Safe House is temporary housing, provided to give protection to victims in accordance with predetermined standards.
27. Community is individuals, families, groups, social organizations and/or community organizations.

CHAPTER II
PRINCIPLES AND OBJECTIVES
Article 2

The implementation of protection of women and children victims of violence is carried out based on the principles of:
a. humanity, including respect for the rights of women and children, protection and security for victims, best, affordable and fast service, sensitivity and empathy, maintaining victim confidentiality;
b. justice, including equality, legal certainty, presumption of innocence, equal rights before the law, best interests of victims, not blaming victims;
c. transparency, accountability, and participatory;
d. tolerance and non-discrimination in differences of race, between groups, gender, religion, ethnicity, age, economic background and social status.

Article 3

(1) The purpose of implementing protection of women and children victims of violence is to:
a. prevent the occurrence of all forms of violence against women and children;
b. provide initial handling of victims;
c. provide health services for victims both physically and psychologically;
d. provide guarantees of protection, security, and legal certainty to women and children victims of violence, witnesses, and reporters;
e. seek victim recovery and rehabilitation;
f. empower victims and reintegration;
g. conduct coordination and evaluation of handling violence against women and children.
(2) Violence as referred to in paragraph (1) may be in the form of:
a. physical violence;
b. psychological violence;
c. sexual violence;
d. economic neglect; and
e. restriction of public access.

CHAPTER III
RIGHTS OF WOMEN AND CHILDREN VICTIMS OF VIOLENCE
Article 4

Women and children victims of acts of violence receive the following rights:
a. the right to have their dignity respected as human beings;
b. the right to fast and affordable complaint handling and services.
c. the right to determine their own decisions;
d. the right to obtain information;
e. the right to confidentiality;
f. the right to compensation;
g. the right of victims and their families to obtain facilities in the judicial process;
h. the right to assistance;
i. the right to health and psychological recovery from the suffering experienced by victims; and
j. the right to rehabilitation and social reintegration.

Article 5

Child victims of acts of violence in addition to receiving the rights as referred to in Article 4, also receive special rights, as follows:
a. the right to obtain respect and services for survival, growth and development;
b. the right to basic services;
c. the right to be free from negative stigma; and
d. the right to obtain freedom to determine choices in deciding their own care after parental divorce.

CHAPTER IV
OBLIGATIONS AND RESPONSIBILITIES
Article 6

Obligations and responsibilities in providing protection to women and children from acts of violence are the joint responsibility of:
a. Regional Government;
b. community.

Article 7

(1) The obligations and responsibilities of the Regional Government as referred to in Article 6 letter a, include:
a. establishing policies, programs, and activities for the protection and recovery of women and children from acts of violence;
b. implementing protection and recovery policies for women and children from acts of violence established by the government;
c. conducting cooperation in the implementation of protection and recovery of women and children from acts of violence;
d. providing support for facilities and infrastructure for the implementation of protection and recovery of women and children from acts of violence;
e. allocating budget for the implementation of protection of women and children from acts of violence according to the financial capacity of the Region; and
f. fostering, supervising, evaluating the implementation of protection and recovery of women and children from acts of violence.
(2) In order to carry out the obligations and responsibilities as referred to in paragraph (1) the Regent establishes a regional action plan as a basis for SKPD and UKPD.
(3) The regional action plan as referred to in paragraph (2) is prepared by involving non-governmental organizations and parties concerned and working on assistance for women and children, with a term of duty for 3 (three) years.
(4) Implementation and technical instructions for the regional action plan as referred to in paragraph (2) are regulated by Regent Regulation.

Article 8

The obligations and responsibilities of the community as referred to in Article 6 letter b, include prevention, handling and recovery actions for women and children victims of acts of violence.

CHAPTER V
COMMUNITY PARTICIPATION
Article 9

(1) In implementing protection for women and children victims of violence, the community can:
a. form family partners at the village/sub-district level by the community;
b. form women and child protection units within community organizations;
c. conduct socialization of women's and children's rights independently;
d. provide first aid to victims; and
e. report to the competent authority if violence against victims occurs in their environment.
(2) Community participation as referred to in paragraph (1) is carried out by individuals, social community institutions, non-governmental organizations, educational institutions, religious institutions, the private sector, and mass media.

CHAPTER VI
PREVENTION OF ACTS OF VIOLENCE
Article 10

(1) To prevent acts of violence against women and children, the Regional Government conducts empowerment and awareness to families, parents, and the community by providing information, counseling and/or guidance.
(2) In addition to empowerment and awareness as referred to in paragraph (1), the Regional Government makes efforts as follows:
a. increase the quantity and quality of education both formal, non-formal and informal;
b. increase accessibility to obtain education, training, funding, income enhancement, and social services;
c. open employment opportunities for women;
d. build community participation and concern for the prevention of protection of women and children from acts of violence;
e. build and provide a complete and easily accessible information system;
f. build networks and cooperation with law enforcement apparatus, government apparatus, universities and various non-governmental organizations that work and/or care about women and children; and
g. open complaint posts for the protection of women and children from acts of violence based on sub-districts to villages.

Article 11

Prevention actions against violence against women and children implemented by the community can be in the form of:
a. actively participating in programs and activities initiated by the Government, Regional Government, or other parties as long as they do not conflict with the principles of protection of women and children established in this Regional Regulation;
b. activities initiated by the community as long as they do not conflict with the principles of protection of women and children established in this Regional Regulation.

Article 12

(1) Prevention of acts of violence against women and children as referred to in Article 9, is implemented by SKPD and UPTD whose duties and functions are in the fields of:
a. social affairs;
b. health;
c. education;
d. employment;
e. women's empowerment and child protection;
f. mental and spiritual; and
g. peace and order.
(2) Prevention of acts of violence by SKPD and UPTD as referred to in paragraph (1), is implemented in an integrated and sustainable manner based on the technical guidelines of the Regional Action Plan.

CHAPTER VII
SERVICES FOR VICTIMS OF ACTS OF VIOLENCE
Article 13

(1) Forms of services provided to women and children victims of acts of violence, are as follows:
a. complaint services;
b. health services;
c. legal aid services;
d. repatriation services;
e. psychological services; and
f. rehabilitation and social reintegration services.
(2) Forms of services as referred to in paragraph (1), are implemented in accordance with Minimum Service Standards (SPM) established by the Government and Regional Government and implemented by SKPD and UPTD whose duties and functions are in the fields of:
a. social affairs;
b. health;
c. women's empowerment and child protection; and
d. mental and spiritual.
(3) In carrying out duties and functions as referred to in paragraph (1), the Regional Government cooperates with Government agencies, other Regional/city Governments, Community Organizations, Non-Governmental Organizations, Academics, Journalists and other stakeholders who are concerned and agree with the ideals of realizing a peaceful world without violence against women and children.

Article 14

(1) In order to maximize the role of the Regional Government in implementing the service function for women and children victims of acts of violence, a safe house is established.
(2) Further provisions regarding safe house services as referred to in paragraph (1) are regulated by Regent Regulation.

CHAPTER VIII
THE ROLE OF CUSTOMARY INSTITUTIONS IN THE PROTECTION OF WOMEN AND CHILDREN VICTIMS OF ACTS OF VIOLENCE
Article 15

(1) Customary institutions that provide services for resolving cases of violence against women and children victims of acts of violence can carry out their functions in accordance with applicable customary provisions as long as they do not conflict with the principles and objectives established in this Regional Regulation.
(2) Certain cases that have consequences for criminal acts based on positive law related to the service function of customary institutions as referred to in paragraph (1) are transferred to the competent authority.
(3) Decisions and sanctions regarding cases of violence against women and children other than the provisions regulated in paragraph (2) resulting from customary courts can be implemented in accordance with local customary provisions.

CHAPTER IX
INSTITUTIONAL
Article 16

(1) In order to maximize protection services for women and children from acts of violence, the Regional Government through P2TP2A establishes an institutional system and work procedures that ensure integration, cooperation and sustainability in its programs and activities involving relevant parties in the Region.
(2) Further provisions regarding the organization and work procedures of P2TP2A as referred to in paragraph (1), are established by Regent Regulation.

CHAPTER X
COOPERATION AND PARTNERSHIP
Part One
Cooperation
Article 17

(1) In order to achieve the goal of protecting women and children from acts of violence, the Regional Government cooperates with:
a. government;
b. provincial government;
c. other Regional Governments; and
d. non-governmental institutions.
(2) Cooperation as referred to in paragraph (1) includes:
a. exchange of data and information;
b. rehabilitation of victims of acts of violence;
c. provision of evidence and witnesses; and
d. referral mechanism for case follow-up in accordance with applicable laws and regulations.
(3) Cooperation as referred to in paragraphs (1) and (2) is set forth in the form of a joint agreement.

Part Two
Partnership
Article 18

(1) The Regional Government establishes partnerships with business entities in the protection of women and children from victims of violence.
(2) Partnerships as regulated in paragraph (1) are carried out through:
a. notification of employment opportunity information for women victims of violence;
b. education and training for women victims of violence;
c. educational assistance for women and children victims of violence who have dropped out of their education;
d. fostering and increasing economic independence of women victims of violence;
e. business facilities and access to capital.
(3) Partnerships as referred to in paragraphs (1) and (2) are set forth in a joint agreement.

CHAPTER XI
GUIDANCE AND SUPERVISION
Article 19

(1) The Regional Government is obliged to conduct guidance and supervision of the implementation of protection of women and children from violence.
(2) Guidance as referred to in paragraph (1), includes:
a. guidelines and fulfillment standards;
b. technical guidance;
c. education and training;
d. provision of facilities;
e. monitoring; and
f. evaluation.
(3) In order to realize the goal of protecting women and children from acts of violence, guidance as referred to in paragraph (2) is implemented in accordance with minimum service standards.

Article 20

(1) The Regional Government is obliged to supervise the implementation of protection of women and children from acts of violence.
(2) Supervision as referred to in paragraph (1), is carried out professionally based on the principles of transparency, accountability and participation.

Article 21

The community can supervise the implementation of protection of women and children from victims of violence, organized by the Regional Government directly or indirectly.

CHAPTER XII
FINANCING
Article 22

Financing for the implementation of protection of women and children from victims of violence is sourced from:
a. Regional Revenue and Expenditure Budget (APBD);
b. other legitimate sources in accordance with the provisions of laws and regulations.

Article 23

(1) The Regional Government can provide financial assistance to community organizations, social organizations and non-governmental organizations that implement protection of women and children from discrimination and acts of violence.
(2) Financial assistance as referred to in paragraph (1), is in accordance with the financial capacity of the Region, and is implemented in accordance with the provisions of laws and regulations in the field of Regional finance.

CHAPTER XIII
CLOSING PROVISIONS
Article 24

Regulations as implementing rules of this Regional Regulation are established no later than 1 (one) year from the promulgation of this Regional Regulation.

Article 25

This Regional Regulation comes into force on the date of promulgation.

In order that everyone may know of it, orders the promulgation of this Regional Regulation by placing it in the Regional Gazette of Sigi Regency.

I. GENERAL

Various acts of violence against women and children that occur within the household and outside the household will destroy the life of families, communities, government, nation, and state. Therefore, the Regional Government of Sigi Regency is obliged to provide protection for women and children victims of violence which is an implementation of several laws and regulations, including Law Number 7 Year 1984 concerning Ratification of the Convention on the Elimination of All Forms of Discrimination Against Women, Law Number 39 Year 1999 concerning Human Rights, Law Number 23 Year 2002 concerning Child Protection, Law Number 23 Year 2004 concerning the Elimination of Domestic Violence, and Law Number 21 Year 2007 concerning the Eradication of the Crime of Trafficking in Persons.

Protection of Women and Children victims of violence is also an obligation as regulated in Law Number 23 Year 2014 concerning Regional Government. On that basis, the Government of Sigi Regency has made various efforts to provide Protection to Women and Children Victims of Violence, but this is still far from expectations, because there is no continuous work synergy from concerned parties, including the still very minimal public understanding caused by various things, including the still strong patriarchal culture and entrenched poverty, handling by parties that have not been well coordinated. Therefore, the Regional Regulation on the Protection of Women and Children Victims of Violence was created to provide legal certainty and protection for victims.

The Regional Regulation on the Protection of Women and Children Victims of Violence, gives responsibility to the Regional Government starting from efforts to prevent various forms of violence to handling victims of violence. In its implementation, the Regional Government cooperates with the Government and Provincial Government, other Regional/city Governments, Community Organizations, Non-Governmental Organizations, Academics, Journalists and other stakeholders who are concerned and agree with the ideals of realizing a peaceful world without violence against women and children.

What is meant by the principle of humanity is an existential state of humans as living beings with reason who are endowed with natural rights that are innate which must be recognized, appreciated, and respected by everyone, including specific aspects inherent in women according to their gender, which in the process of implementing protection of women and children victims of acts of violence must be respected and fulfilled by the state, including among others the human rights of women and children, protection and security as victims of acts of violence, obtaining the best, affordable and fast service, sensitivity and empathy, and maintaining their confidentiality as victims of acts of violence.

Letter b
What is meant by the principle of justice, is a mental state of every human being who naturally/innately wants to obtain a sense of justice as part of society and/or fulfilled sense of justice for everyone in their position as citizens. In relation to the protection of women and children victims of acts of violence whose implementation is regulated through this regional regulation, must fulfill the principle of justice at least including gender equality, legal certainty, presumption of innocence, equal rights before the law, not blaming victims, best interests of victims. The meaning of best interests for victims, is that in the process of handling cases experienced by victims, all parties involved in it, whether government, community, legislative, executive, judicial bodies must place the interests of victims as the most important in all situations.

Letter e
Mediation is conducted for cases of domestic violence that fall into the category of complaint offenses as regulated in Law Number 23 Year 2004 concerning the Elimination of Domestic Violence.

Paragraph (2)
Letter a
What is meant by "physical violence" is an act that causes pain, illness, or serious injury such as slapping, hitting, spitting, pulling hair/grabbing, kicking, burning with hot objects, hitting/injuring with weapons, and/or causing disability to a person's body, miscarriage, unconsciousness and/or causing death.

Letter b
What is meant by "psychological violence" is an act that causes fear, loss of self-confidence, loss of ability to act, feeling of helplessness, and/or severe psychological suffering to a person. Psychological violence can be in the form of emotional abuse such as insults, hurtful comments or degrading self-esteem, isolating the wife from the outside world, threatening or intimidating as a means of imposing one's will.

Letter c
What is meant by "sexual violence" is any act that violates a person's human dignity based on gender discrimination that targets a person's body and sexuality, which results in or can result in physical, psychological, economic, sexual, political and/or social harm or suffering to the victim. This formulation is the essence of the findings of 15 forms of sexual violence, including: 1) Rape; An attack in the form of forced sexual intercourse using the penis towards the vagina, anus or mouth of the victim. Can also use fingers or other objects. The attack is carried out with violence, threats of violence, detention, psychological pressure, abuse of power, or by taking advantage of a coercive environment. Molestation is another term for rape known in the Indonesian legal system. This term is used when rape is committed outside of forced penetration of the penis into the vagina and when sexual intercourse occurs with a person who is not yet able to give full consent, for example to a child or someone under 18 years old.

Sexual violence also includes isolating (distancing) the wife from her spiritual needs, forcing sexual intercourse, imposing one's own sexual preferences or not paying attention to the wife's satisfaction. Sexual violence can also be in the form of forced sexual intercourse committed against a person residing within the household, forced sexual intercourse against one person within the household with another person for commercial purposes and/or certain purposes.

Letter d
What is meant by "economic neglect" is neglecting the economic needs of the wife or children or family members or relatives such as not providing for the wife, even spending the wife's money, not giving or withholding the wages of domestic workers or drivers and including acts of restricting and/or prohibiting family members from working properly.

Letter e
What is meant by "restriction of movement" is restricting the movement of the wife or children in developing their potential both for economic and social purposes such as prohibiting gathering and socializing in the community and restricting the movement of relatives such as preventing domestic workers or drivers from communicating or having relations with their families.

Letter d

Letter h
What is meant by the right to assistance includes among others psychologists, psychiatrists, health experts, clergy, advocates, and family members. Assistants are individuals who work as volunteers to provide protection and support to women and children victims of violence during the judicial process, these assistants can come from family members, friends or independent organizations that pay attention to witnesses and victims or advocates.

Letter i
What is meant by the victim's right to rehabilitation, includes: access to medical services for physical and psychological recovery, legal assistance to restore civil rights

Article 5
Letter a
Sufficiently clear

Letter b
The basic rights referred to include the right to education and access to parents during the handling process

Letter c
What is meant by negative stigma, is negative labeling (naming, symbolizing) by the public or society on the victim which can result in the victim's alienation in their own community, including other mental pressures.

Letter d
What is meant by freedom to determine choice after parental divorce is the legal consequence of the dissolution of marriage due to divorce. Based on the provisions in Article 41 of Law Number 1 Year 1974 concerning Marriage ("Marriage Law") it is stated that the consequences of the dissolution of a marriage due to divorce are:

a. Both mother or father remain obliged to maintain and educate their children, solely based on the interests of the child, if there is a dispute regarding custody of the children, the Court gives its decision.
b. The father is responsible for all maintenance and education costs required by the child, if the father in reality cannot fulfill these obligations the court can determine that the mother shares these costs.
c. The court can require the former husband to provide living expenses and/or determine an obligation for the former wife.

The only rule that clearly and firmly provides guidance for judges in deciding the granting of custody of the child is contained in Article 105 of the Compilation of Islamic Law (KHI) which states: "In the event of divorce:
a) custody of a child who is not yet mumayyiz or not yet 12 years old is the mother's right.
b) custody of a child who is already mumayyiz is handed over to the child to choose between the father or mother as the holder of custody rights.
c) maintenance costs are borne by the father."

Article 19
Paragraph (1)
What is meant by guidance for the implementation of prevention and protection of women and children victims of violence is an effort made by the Regional Government and the community to realize the achievement of the goals of prevention and protection of women and children victims of violence

What is meant by supervision of the implementation of prevention and protection of women and children victims of violence is a process of activities aimed at ensuring that efforts made by the Regional Government and the community run efficiently and effectively in accordance with plans and provisions of laws and regulations.
