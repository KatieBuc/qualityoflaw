CHAPTER I
GENERAL PROVISIONS

Article 1
In this Regional Regulation what is meant by:
1. Region is Indramayu Regency.
2. Regional Government is the Regent together with Regional Apparatus as elements of regional government administration.
3. Regent is the Regent of Indramayu.
4. Regional People's Representative Council hereinafter abbreviated as DPRD is the Regional People's Representative Council of Indramayu Regency as an element of Regional Government administration.
5. Agency is a Regional Apparatus Work Unit authorized in the field of Prevention, Protection and Recovery of Women and Children as Victims of Violence in Indramayu Regency.
6. Woman is an adult human being of female gender and a person recognized by law as a woman.
7. Child is a person who has not reached 18 years of age, including a child in the womb and marriage cannot change the status of a child.
8. Prevention is activities carried out to prevent the occurrence of acts of violence against women and children.
9. Recovery is rehabilitating victims until they are physically and/or psychologically healthy and accepted back into society.
10. Protection is activities and/or actions in order to protect women and children from violence and exploitation.
11. Violence is any act that results in or causes misery and suffering both physical, sexual, psychological including threats of certain actions, coercion or arbitrary deprivation of freedom, whether occurring in public or in private life.
12. Physical violence is any act that causes pain, injury, wounds or disability to a person's body, miscarriage or fainting and/or causes death.
13. Psychological violence is an act that causes fear, loss of self-confidence, loss of ability to act, distrust or severe psychological suffering to a person.
14. Sexual violence is any act in the form of sexual harassment, forced sexual intercourse, whether unnatural or unwanted with another person for commercial purposes and/or certain purposes.
15. Victim is a woman and child who experiences acts of violence, whether physical, psychological or sexual violence.
16. Service is immediate activities and actions carried out by professional personnel in accordance with their respective professions in the form of counseling, therapy and advocacy for strengthening and recovery of victims of violence.
17. Accompaniment is activities and actions carried out by companions during the service process.
18. Companion is a person or representative from an institution who has expertise in providing accompaniment.
19. Institution is an agency/body and/or non-governmental organization (NGO) that provides accompaniment.
20. Integrated Service Center hereinafter abbreviated as PPT, is an institution providing services to victims of violence based in hospitals, managed jointly in the form of medical services (including medico-legal), psychosocial and legal services.
21. Safe house (Shelter) is temporary housing, provided to give protection to victims in accordance with predetermined standards.
22. Community is individuals, families, groups, social organizations and/or community organizations.
23. P2TP2A is one form of service facility for women and children in efforts to fulfill information and needs in the fields of education, health, economy, law, protection and handling of violence and trafficking against women and children.

CHAPTER II
PRINCIPLES AND OBJECTIVES

Article 2
The implementation of Prevention, Protection and Recovery of Women and Children as Victims of Violence is carried out based on the principles of:
a. Respect for the rights of victims;
b. Justice and gender equality;
c. Non-discrimination; and
d. Best interests of victims.

Article 3
The purpose of implementing prevention, protection and recovery of women and children as victims of violence is to provide prevention and protection covering aspects of:
a. Prevention;
b. Service and accompaniment;
c. Recovery; and
d. Empowerment.

CHAPTER III
RIGHTS OF VICTIMS

Article 4
Every victim has the right to receive:
a. Protection;
b. Information;
c. Minimum service according to Minimum Service Standards (SPM);
d. Continuous handling up to the rehabilitation stage;
e. Confidential handling;
f. Psychological and legal accompaniment; and
g. Guarantee of rights related to status as a family member and community member.

CHAPTER IV
OBLIGATIONS AND AUTHORITY
OF REGIONAL GOVERNMENT

Article 5
(1) The Regional Government is obliged to implement efforts to prevent the occurrence of violence against women and children in the form of:
a. collecting data and information about women and children victims of violence;
b. socializing laws and regulations related to the implementation of prevention, protection and recovery of women and children as victims of violence and exploitation;
c. socializing understanding and anti-violence values toward Women and Children through IEC (communication, information and education) both through community, schools and universities;
d. monitoring and evaluating the implementation of prevention, protection and recovery of women and children as victims of violence and exploitation.
(2) The Regional Government in anticipating the occurrence of acts of violence against Women and Children, has the authority to provide and organize services for victims in the form of:
a. providing and facilitating the formation of integrated service institutions for victims of violence by involving institutions and community elements;
b. encouraging community awareness of the importance of protection for victims of violence;
c. empowering the community for prevention of acts of violence.
(3) The obligations and authority of the Regional Government are implemented by the Agency

CHAPTER V
PREVENTION AND PROTECTION
Part One
Institutional

Article 6
(1) In order to implement prevention, protection and recovery of women and children victims of violence in an integrated, harmonious, and sustainable manner, the Regent establishes P2TP2A at the Regency level.
(2) Further provisions regarding the establishment of P2TP2A management, P2TP2A membership, and P2TP2A working procedures as referred to in paragraph (1) are regulated by the Regent.
(3) Regency P2TP2A can receive case referrals from Hospitals, Community Health Centers, NGOs, Safe Houses (Shelters) and the Community.

Part Two
Forms and Mechanisms of Prevention

Article 7
Forms of prevention of acts of violence against women and children carried out by P2TP2A can be implemented through:
a. socialization activities of laws and regulations and/or regional regulations to the community related to the protection of the rights of women and children;
b. training of P2TP2A members related to the implementation of their duties in preventing acts of violence against women and children.

Article 8
(1) Regency P2TP2A in carrying out its duties and functions can coordinate with the Religion and Social Welfare Division of the Regional Secretariat of Indramayu Regency, sub-districts and competent parties in making efforts to prevent the occurrence of acts of violence against women and children.
(2) The mechanism for implementing prevention duties as referred to in paragraph (1) is further regulated by Regent Regulation.

Article 9
Forms of services for victims organized by P2TP2A include:
a. medical services, in the form of treatment and recovery of physical injuries aimed at restoring the physical condition of victims carried out by medical personnel and paramedics;
b. medico-legal services are forms of medical services for the purposes of proof in the legal field;
c. Psychological services are services provided in order to restore the traumatic condition of victims, including provision of safe houses to protect victims from various threats and intimidation for victims and provide social support so that victims have self-confidence, strength and independence, in resolving their problems;
d. Legal services to assist victims in undergoing legal processes;
e. Economic independence services in the form of skills training and providing economic access so that victims can be independent.

Article 10
(1) In carrying out the task of serving victims of violence against women and children, Regency P2TP2A coordinates with Sub-district Community Health Centers.
(2) The mechanism for implementing duties as referred to in paragraph (1) is regulated in Standard Operating Procedures (SOP), further regulated by Regent Regulation.

Part Three
Accompaniment Mechanism

Article 11
(1) Accompaniment is carried out by persons and institutions cooperating with P2TP2A.
(2) The accompaniment mechanism as referred to in paragraph (1) is set forth in Standard Operating Procedures (SOP).
(3) Provisions regarding Standard Operating Procedures (SOP) for accompaniment mechanisms as referred to in paragraph (2), are further regulated by Regent Regulation.

Part Four
Principles of Prevention, Protection
and Recovery

Article 12
The implementation of prevention, protection and recovery of victims of violence is carried out with:
a. Fast, safe, empathetic;
b. Guarantee of confidentiality;
c. Easily accessible; and
d. No fees charged.

CHAPTER VI
RECOVERY
Part One
Minimum Service Standards

Article 13
(1) In order to implement prevention, protection and recovery of women and children victims of violence in an integrated, harmonious, and sustainable manner, the Regional Government establishes Minimum Service Standards (SPM).
(2) Further provisions regarding Minimum Service Standards as referred to in paragraph (1), are further regulated by Regent Regulation.
(3) The Regional Government is obliged to apply Minimum Service Standards in Hospitals, Community Health Centers, NGOs, Safe Houses (Shelters) designated by the Regent.

Part Two
Forms and Mechanisms of Recovery

Article 14
Forms of recovery for women and children victims carried out by P2TP2A can be implemented through:
a. rehabilitation and accompaniment activities are carried out for a maximum of one month;
b. P2TP2A must provide competent resources such as psychiatrists and psychologists;
c. P2TP2A must provide physical and psychological health tests for victims of violence;

CHAPTER VII
GUIDANCE, CONTROL AND
SUPERVISION

Article 15
(1) The Regent has the authority to conduct guidance, control and supervision of all activities related to the implementation of prevention, protection and recovery of women and children victims of violence and exploitation in the region.
(2) The authority as referred to in paragraph (1) is technically and operationally implemented by the Agency.

Article 16
Guidance, control and supervision as referred to in Chapter VII are directed to:
a. increase the level of community awareness so as to be able to prevent, protect and/or restore the physical and or psychological condition of women and children victims of violence;
b. fulfill the community's need for information and protection services for women and children victims of violence;
c. provide convenience in order to support the improvement of efforts to implement prevention, protection, recovery of women and children victims of violence;
d. improve the quality of health for women and children who experience violence.

CHAPTER VIII
COMMUNITY PARTICIPATION

Article 17
(1) The community can participate in efforts to prevent, protect and recover victims of violence against women and children.
(2) Community participation as referred to in paragraph (1) can be carried out by individuals or organizations.
(3) Community participation as referred to in paragraph (2) can be carried out in the form of:
a. preventing the occurrence of acts of violence against women and children;
b. submitting reports to the authorities if acts of violence against women and children occur;
c. providing assistance to victims of acts of violence against women and children.

CHAPTER IX
REPORTING

Article 18
(1) P2TP2A is obliged to report the implementation of prevention, protection and recovery of women and children victims of violence to the Regent.
(2) Reporting as referred to in paragraph (1) includes:
a. Administration;
b. Finance;
c. Services;
d. Performance.
(3) Submission of written reports as referred to in paragraph (2) is carried out at least once every 3 (three) months.

CHAPTER X
FUNDING SOURCES

Article 19
(1) Expenditure for programs and activities of Implementation of Prevention, Protection and Recovery of Women and Children as Victims of Violence is sourced from the Regional Budget of Indramayu Regency.
(2) In addition to being sourced from the Regional Budget, expenditure for programs and activities as referred to in paragraph (1) can be sourced from the State Budget, Provincial Regional Budget and other legal and non-binding sources.
(3) To support the operational expenditure of P2TP2A, a budget can be provided sourced from the Regional Revenue and Expenditure Budget (APBD).

CHAPTER XI
CRIMINAL SANCTIONS

Article 20
(1) Any person who obstructs, disturbs and/or hinders the implementation of programs for prevention, protection and recovery of women and children victims of violence, is threatened with imprisonment of a maximum of 3 (three) months or a fine of a maximum of Rp. 50,000,000.00 (Fifty Million Rupiah).
(2) In addition to criminal acts as referred to in paragraph (1) whether in the form of criminal violations and/or actions that result in losses to the Regional Government, individuals or other parties are threatened with criminal law in accordance with applicable laws and regulations.

CHAPTER XII
INVESTIGATION

Article 21
(1) Certain Civil Servant Officials within the Regional Government are given special authority as Investigators to conduct investigations of criminal acts, as referred to in the Criminal Procedure Law.
(2) Investigators as referred to in paragraph (1) are certain civil servant officials within the Regional Government appointed by authorized officials in accordance with the provisions of laws and regulations.
(3) The authority of Investigators as referred to in paragraph (1) is:
a. receive, search for, collect, and examine information or reports concerning criminal acts so that such information or reports become more complete and clear;
b. examine, search for, and collect information about individuals or Bodies regarding the truth of acts committed in connection with criminal acts;
c. request information and evidence from individuals or Bodies in connection with criminal acts;
d. examine books, records, and other documents concerning criminal acts;
e. conduct searches to obtain evidence of bookkeeping, recording, and other documents, and conduct seizures of such evidence;
f. request the assistance of experts in the context of carrying out the task of investigating criminal acts;
g. order to stop and/or prohibit someone from leaving a room or place when an examination is in progress and examine the identity of persons, objects, and/or documents carried;
h. photograph someone related to criminal acts;
i. summon people to be heard and examined as suspects or witnesses;
j. terminate investigations; and/or
k. take other actions necessary for the smooth investigation of criminal acts in accordance with the provisions of laws and regulations.
(4) Investigators as referred to in paragraph (1) notify the commencement of investigations and submit the results of their investigations to the Public Prosecutor through the Investigator official of the Police of the Republic of Indonesia, in accordance with the provisions regulated in the Criminal Procedure Law.

CHAPTER XIII
CLOSING PROVISIONS

Article 22
This Regional Regulation comes into force on the date of promulgation.

I. GENERAL

The State has an obligation to provide a sense of security to its citizens from threats and actions that can disturb and damage security, psychology, physical, sexual, and economic. This is philosophically stated in the preamble of the 1945 Constitution, that the purpose of establishing the Unitary State of the Republic of Indonesia is to protect the entire nation and all of Indonesia's territory.

Because the Indonesian government has signed the Universal Declaration of Human Rights (UDHR 1948) and ratified CEDAW (Law Number 7 Year 1984 concerning the elimination of all forms of Discrimination against Women), it must fulfill these provisions. The enforcement and fulfillment of Human Rights is the responsibility of the State.

Through Law Number 23 Year 2002 concerning Child Protection, the government recognizes the existence of children's rights. Human Rights inherent in children, among others, include basic rights as human beings, namely the Right to life, Right to Growth and Development, Right to Protection and Right to Participation.

To fulfill the right to life, children need food in sufficient quantities, healthy and nutritious, and optimal health access. Protection from violence that threatens their safety and health must be provided from an early age. Protection is provided to prevent physical, mental and sexual violence and exploitation. To fulfill the right to growth and development, children need space to play, exercise, education appropriate to their physical and psychological development. In fulfilling children's rights, every government administrator, community and parents must understand and care about children's rights. The existence of legal instruments and law enforcement officials who defend children's interests is needed for this protection effort. Legislation that is principled to defend the best interests of children (The best Interest of Child) is needed to realize protection that is legal in nature.

Furthermore, it is not uncommon to find cases of women and children who experience problems of physical, psychological, sexual and social violence. For example, women and children victims of persecution, neglect, eviction and abuse by parents or others. For women and children who experience these problems, the government must provide Safe Houses (Children protection homes) or integrated service centers for women and children (P2TP2A) for women and children victims of violence. Safe Houses (shelters) are safe places and are Friendly (friendly) for women and children.

The Integrated Service Center is equipped with companions from educators, doctors, psychologists, religious leaders/clergy and networks with lawyers for women and children as well as RPK Units at the Police.

The fate of women and children victims of violence must be given attention by the government. The large number of cases of violence against women and children are often caused by factors that develop in society, such as low economic levels, education, environments in the industrial sector. Therefore victims of violence like this need to receive protection in accordance with the principles of truth, justice, legal certainty, equality and upholding Human Rights.

From the above framework, the regional government is responsible for taking actions both legally, politically, economically and socially to prevent, suppress, reduce, and eliminate all forms of violence against women. As one of these efforts is the realization of a legal framework in the implementation of protection for women and children victims of violence.

II. ARTICLE BY ARTICLE

Article 1
Sufficiently clear

Article 2
- What is meant by "Respect for the rights of victims" is a series of actions to respect, appreciate and guarantee the fulfillment of victims' rights.
- What is meant by "Justice and gender equality" is a process to be fair to men and women and equality of conditions for men and women to obtain opportunities and their rights as human beings, so as to be able to play a role and participate in political, economic, social, cultural, national defense and security activities and equality in enjoying the results of such development.
- What is meant by "Non-Discrimination" is attitudes and treatment toward victims without making distinctions based on age, gender, race, ethnicity, religion and between groups.
- What is meant by "Best interests of victims" is that all actions concerning victims carried out by regional government, community, legislative bodies and judicial bodies, must be the primary consideration.

Article 3
Letter a
The "prevention" aspect is a protection strategy effort through:
a. Primary prevention, all people, families, communities and the state in efforts to increase knowledge, understanding and ensure that violence against women and children does not occur, including policy socialization, adequate services, supportive workplace policies, and life skill training for women and children.
What is meant by life skill training includes conflict resolution without violence, stress management skills, resource management, effective decision making, effective interpersonal communication, guidance on psychosocial development of women and children.
b. Secondary prevention, aimed at community groups at high risk in efforts to improve skills, including training and victim services to ensure that violence against women and children does not occur in the next generation. Activities carried out here include home visits for parents who have just had children to conduct self-assessment whether they are at risk of committing violence against children in the future.
c. Tertiary prevention, intended to improve parenting abilities that ensure that violence against women and children is not repeated, what is done here is integrated services for women and children who are victims of violence, through counseling, stress management training.

Letter b
The "service and accompaniment" aspect is immediate activities and actions carried out by professional personnel and accompaniment in accordance with their respective professions in the form of counseling, therapy and advocacy for strengthening and recovery of victims of violence.

Letter c
The "recovery" aspect is an effort to restore and recover the physical and psychological condition of victims which then reunites them with the victim's family and community.

Letter d
The "empowerment" aspect is a process of increasing the role of the community to participate in the implementation of protection for women and children victims of violence which aims to obtain access and participation in the protection process for women and children broadly.

Article 4
Letter a
What is meant by "receiving protection" is receiving protection from individuals, groups and institutions both government and non-government.

Letter b
What is meant by "receiving information" is access and information about the existence of complaint centers, PPT, and all matters related to the fulfillment of their rights and involvement in the decision-making process related to accompaniment and case developments.

Letter c
What is meant by "minimum service" is services that include medical, extensive medicolegal, psychosocial and legal services.

Letter d
What is meant by "continuous handling up to the rehabilitation stage" is handling that does not stop at physical and psychological healing, but until the victim can resume life in society including restoration of their good name.

Letter e
What is meant by "confidential handling" is an effort to guarantee certainty for victims that their identity, medical treatment and legal handling will not be disseminated.

Letter f
What is meant by "receiving psychological accompaniment" is assistance provided by psychologists to victims suffering from trauma/other psychological problems to restore the victim's psychological condition. While legal accompaniment is assistance provided by persons and/or legal aid institutions to persons at every level of examination and during the legal process.

Letter g
What is meant by "guarantee of rights related to victim status" is an effort to provide certainty and protection for victims as family members and community.
