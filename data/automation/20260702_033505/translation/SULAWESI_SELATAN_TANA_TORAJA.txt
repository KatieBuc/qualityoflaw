REGENT OF TANA TORAJA
SOUTH SULAWESI PROVINCE
REGIONAL REGULATION OF TANA TORAJA REGENCY
NUMBER 4 YEAR 2017
CONCERNING
PROTECTION OF WOMEN AND CHILDREN
BY THE GRACE OF ALMIGHTY GOD
THE REGENT OF TANA TORAJA,

Considering: a. that the Republic of Indonesia is a State that upholds the dignity and worth of every human being, guarantees the rights and security of women and children and freedom from all forms of violence, maltreatment, exploitation, trafficking and neglect;
b. that women as mothers of the nation and children as the buds and future generation of the nation are creatures of Almighty God and therefore must be protected and their honor, dignity and self-worth maintained in a proper and proportional manner, both legally, economically, politically, socially and culturally, without distinguishing ethnicity, religion, race and class;
c. that based on the considerations as referred to in letters a and b, it is necessary to establish a Regional Regulation on the Protection of Women and Children;

Bearing in Mind: 1. Article 18 paragraph (6) of the 1945 Constitution of the Republic of Indonesia;
2. Law Number 29 of 1959 concerning the Establishment of Level II Regions in Sulawesi (State Gazette of the Republic of Indonesia Year 1959 Number 74, Supplement to the State Gazette of the Republic of Indonesia Number 1822);
3. Law Number 39 of 1999 concerning Human Rights (State Gazette of the Republic of Indonesia Year 1999 Number 165, Supplement to the State Gazette of the Republic of Indonesia Number 3886);
4. Law Number 23 of 2002 concerning Child Protection (State Gazette of the Republic of Indonesia Year 2002 Number 109, Supplement to the State Gazette of the Republic of Indonesia Number 4235) as amended by Law Number 35 of 2014 concerning Amendment to Law Number 23 of 2002 concerning Child Protection (State Gazette of the Republic of Indonesia Year 2014 Number 297, Supplement to the State Gazette of the Republic of Indonesia Number 5606);
5. Law Number 13 of 2003 concerning Manpower (State Gazette of the Republic of Indonesia Year 2003 Number 39, Supplement to the State Gazette of the Republic of Indonesia Number 4279);
6. Law Number 23 of 2004 concerning the Elimination of Domestic Violence (State Gazette of the Republic of Indonesia Year 2004 Number 95, Supplement to the State Gazette of the Republic of Indonesia Number 4419);
7. Law Number 12 of 2011 concerning the Formulation of Legislation (State Gazette of the Republic of Indonesia Year 2011 Number 82, Supplement to the State Gazette of the Republic of Indonesia Number 5234);
8. Law Number 23 of 2014 concerning Regional Government (State Gazette of the Republic of Indonesia Year 2014 Number 244, Supplement to the State Gazette of the Republic of Indonesia Number 5587) as amended several times, most recently by Law Number 9 of 2015 concerning the Second Amendment to Law Number 23 of 2014 concerning Regional Government (State Gazette of the Republic of Indonesia Year 2015 Number 58, Supplement to the State Gazette of the Republic of Indonesia Number 5679);
9. Law Number 8 of 2016 concerning Persons with Disabilities (State Gazette of the Republic of Indonesia Year 2016 Number 69, Supplement to the State Gazette of the Republic of Indonesia Number 5871);

To Establish: REGIONAL REGULATION CONCERNING PROTECTION OF WOMEN AND CHILDREN.

CHAPTER I
GENERAL PROVISIONS

Article 1
In this Regional Regulation, what is meant by:
1. Region is Tana Toraja Regency.
2. Regent is the Regent of Tana Toraja.
3. Regional Government is the administration of governmental affairs by the regional government and the regional house of representatives according to the principles of autonomy and assistance tasks with the principle of the broadest possible autonomy within the system and principles of the Unitary State of the Republic of Indonesia as referred to in the 1945 Constitution of the Republic of Indonesia.
4. Regional Government is the regional head as an element of the Regional Government administration who leads the implementation of governmental affairs that are the authority of the autonomous region.
5. Regional House of Representatives hereinafter abbreviated as DPRD is a regional people's representative institution that is positioned as an element of Regional Government administration.
6. Sub-district is a part of the regency area led by a sub-district head.
7. Lembang is another term for village which is part of the regency area led by the Head of Lembang;
8. Lembang Government is the term for village government in Tana Toraja Regency.
9. Community is individuals, families, groups, and social organizations and/or community organizations.
10. Family is the smallest unit in society consisting of husband, wife and children, or father and children or mother and children or blood relatives in a straight line upward or downward or sideways to the third degree.
11. Parents are biological father and/or mother or stepfather and/or stepmother, or adoptive father and/or mother.
12. Guardian is a person or body that in reality exercises parental authority as a parent over a child.
13. Woman is a human being who can menstruate, become pregnant, give birth and breastfeed.
14. Child is someone who is not yet 18 (eighteen) years old, including babies still in the womb.
15. Neglected child is a child whose needs are not properly fulfilled, whether physical, mental, spiritual, or social.
16. Protection is all efforts aimed at providing a sense of security to victims carried out by family, advocates, social institutions, police, prosecutors, courts, or other parties either temporarily or based on court determination.
17. Protection of women is all efforts made to guarantee the fulfillment of women's rights as a form of respect for human rights.
18. Women's empowerment is every effort to improve physical, mental, spiritual, social capacity, knowledge, and skills so that women are ready to be utilized according to their respective abilities.
19. Child protection is all activities to guarantee and protect children and their rights so they can live, grow, develop and participate optimally in accordance with human dignity and receive protection from violence and discrimination.
20. Child with Disabilities is a Child who has physical, mental, intellectual, or sensory limitations for a long period of time who in interacting with the environment and community attitudes may encounter obstacles that make it difficult to participate fully and effectively based on equal rights.
21. Child with Excellence is a Child who has extraordinary intelligence or has special potential and/or talents not limited to intellectual abilities, but also in other fields.
22. Adopted Child is a Child whose rights are transferred from the environment of the authority of the Family of Parents, legal Guardian, or other person responsible for the care, education, and raising of the Child into the environment of the adoptive Parents' Family based on a court decision or determination.
23. Foster Child is a Child who is cared for by a person or institution to be given guidance, maintenance, care, education, and health because the Parents or one of the Parents is unable to guarantee the child's proper growth and development.
24. Custody is the power of Parents to care for, educate, maintain, foster, protect, and develop the Child in accordance with the religion professed and in accordance with abilities, talents, and interests.
25. Child's Rights are part of human rights that must be guaranteed, protected, and fulfilled by Parents, Family, community, state, government, and regional government.
26. Companion is a social worker who has professional competence in their field.
27. Violence is any unlawful act that results in suffering or hardship physically, psychologically, sexually, and/or neglect, including threats to commit acts, coercion, or deprivation of a person's freedom.
28. Violence against women is any act based on gender differences that results or may result in suffering or hardship for women physically, sexually or psychologically, including threats of certain actions, coercion or arbitrary deprivation of freedom, whether occurring in the public sphere or in private life.
29. Violence against children is any act against a child that results in suffering or hardship physically, mentally, sexually, psychologically, including neglect and maltreatment that threatens bodily integrity and degrades the dignity of the child.
30. Integrated Service Center hereinafter abbreviated as PPT is a cooperative network that organizes integrated social service functions.
31. Integrated Service Center for the Protection of Women and Children hereinafter abbreviated as P2TP2A is a cooperative network that organizes integrated service functions for the protection of women and children.
32. Community Social Institution is a Non-Governmental Organization (NGO) and/or other community organizations.
33. Family Welfare Consultation Institution hereinafter abbreviated as LK3 is an integrated social service unit that handles family psychosocial problems to realize family resilience.
34. Regional Child Protection Commission hereinafter abbreviated as KPAD is an integrated social service unit that implements child protection services to realize the fulfillment of children's rights.
35. Gender mainstreaming is a strategy built to integrate gender into an integral dimension of planning, formulation, implementation, monitoring, and evaluation of national development policy programs.
36. Discrimination is any restriction, harassment, or exclusion that is directly or indirectly based on human differentiation on the basis of religion, ethnicity, race, ethnic group, group, class, social status, economic status, gender, language, political beliefs, which results in reduction, deviation or elimination of recognition, implementation or use of human rights and fundamental freedoms in life both individually and collectively in the political, economic, legal, social, cultural fields and other aspects of life.
37. Safe House (Shelter) is a protective house for women and children including families and witnesses of violence victims.

CHAPTER II
SCOPE

Article 2
The Scope of this Regional Regulation consists of:
a. implementation of women's protection; and
b. implementation of child protection;
c. obligations and responsibilities;
d. institutions;
e. child-friendly regency;
f. special development planning deliberation for women and children.

CHAPTER III
PRINCIPLES AND OBJECTIVES

Article 3
The implementation of women's protection is based on Pancasila and the 1945 Constitution of the Republic of Indonesia including:
a. respect for human rights;
b. justice and gender equality;
c. non-discrimination;
d. victim protection;
e. the best interests of women;
f. respect for women's opinions;
g. legal certainty; and
h. women's empowerment.

Article 4
The implementation of child protection is based on Pancasila and the 1945 Constitution of the Republic of Indonesia as well as the basic principles of the convention on children's rights including:
a. non-discrimination;
b. the best interests of the child;
c. the right to life, survival, and development; and
d. respect for the child's opinion.

Article 5
The objectives of implementing women's protection are:
a. to guarantee the fulfillment of women's rights;
b. to protect and provide security for women;
c. to maintain household integrity to realize a harmonious and prosperous family;
d. to guarantee the fulfillment of women's rights;
e. to provide services to women;
f. to provide protection to reporters and witnesses; and
g. to empower women victims of violence.

Article 6
The objectives of implementing child protection are:
a. to guarantee the fulfillment of children's rights;
b. to protect and provide security for children;
c. to maintain household integrity to realize a harmonious and prosperous family;
d. to guarantee the fulfillment of children's rights;
e. to realize quality, noble and prosperous children;
f. to provide services to child victims of violence; and
g. to provide protection to reporters and witnesses.

CHAPTER IV
IMPLEMENTATION OF WOMEN'S PROTECTION

Part One
Women's Rights

Article 7
Women's rights are as follows:
a. to live, maintain life and improve their standard of living;
b. to live peacefully, safely, peacefully, happily, prosperously physically and spiritually;
c. to enjoy a good and healthy living environment;
d. to live in a family and continue offspring in a legal marriage bond based on statutory provisions;
e. to obtain work according to ability, conditions and fair and decent wages;
f. especially for women with disabilities, the right to obtain facilities and special treatment in public services;
g. to play an active role in the political and governmental fields according to their potential and abilities;
h. to obtain information and legal services;
i. to obtain services to increase their income; and
j. to obtain other rights in accordance with human dignity and based on statutory provisions.

Part Two
Women's Empowerment

Article 8
(1) In order to improve the position, role, and quality of women, as well as efforts to realize gender equality and justice in family, community, national, and state life, women must be empowered.
(2) Women's empowerment as referred to in paragraph (1) is directed to obtain opportunities and their rights as human beings, so they are able to play a role and participate in activities:
a. economic;
b. socio-cultural;
c. political; and
d. legal.

Article 9
The implementation of women's empowerment in the economic field as referred to in Article 8 paragraph (2) letter a, is carried out through:
a. provision of skills and job training;
b. facilitation of the formation of productive economic business groups;
c. facilitation of strengthening and development of productive economic business groups;
d. facilitation of capital assistance; and
e. facilitation of marketing network development.

Article 10
Women's empowerment in the socio-cultural field as referred to in Article 8 paragraph (2) letter b, is carried out through:
a. increasing knowledge, attitudes and skills to encourage the fulfillment of tiered education according to potential to improve social status;
b. increasing awareness, willingness and ability to overcome health problems through quality promotional, preventive, curative, and rehabilitative efforts, especially in the field of reproductive health;
c. increasing awareness and knowledge about independent, healthy and prosperous family planning including access to consultation services and marriage registration; and
d. facilitation and efforts to preserve customs and development of science, technology, arts and culture for women's advancement.

Article 11
(1) The implementation of women's empowerment in the political field as referred to in Article 8 paragraph (2) letter c, includes:
a. involvement of women in decision-making at various levels;
b. providing opportunities for women to hold public positions;
c. participation in general elections; and
d. self-development through organizations to associate, assemble and express opinions.
(2) The implementation of women's empowerment in the political field as referred to in paragraph (1) is carried out in accordance with statutory provisions.

Article 12
(1) The implementation of women's empowerment in the legal field as referred to in Article 8 paragraph (2) letter d, includes:
a. increasing awareness and knowledge in the legal field through communication, information and education services; and
b. facilitation of access and legal consultation services.
(2) The implementation of women's empowerment in the legal field as referred to in paragraph (1) is carried out in accordance with statutory provisions.

Article 13
(1) The Regional Government is obliged and responsible for women's empowerment efforts.
(2) Women's empowerment efforts as referred to in paragraph (1) are carried out in an integrated manner in accordance with statutory provisions.

Article 14
(1) To implement women's empowerment as referred to in Article 13 paragraph (1), the Regional Government organizes gender mainstreaming including:
a. establishing the implementation of gender mainstreaming;
b. coordinating, facilitating, and mediating gender mainstreaming;
c. facilitating institutional strengthening and development of gender mainstreaming mechanisms in government institutions, women's study centers, research and development institutions, non-governmental institutions;
d. implementing gender mainstreaming related to the fields of development, education, health, economy, law, human rights and politics, environment and socio-culture;
e. improving the quality of women's lives related to the fields of development, education, health, economy, law, human rights and politics, environment and socio-culture;
f. integrating efforts to improve the quality of women's lives related to the fields of development, education, health, economy, law, human rights and politics, environment and socio-culture;
g. coordinating the implementation of improving the quality of women's lives related to the fields of development, education, health, economy, law, human rights and politics, environment and socio-culture;
h. organizing the protection of women, especially protection against victims of violence, female workers, elderly women, and women with disabilities, in conflict areas and disaster-affected areas;
i. facilitating the integration of women's protection especially against victims of violence, female workers, elderly women and women with disabilities, in conflict areas and disaster-affected areas;
j. coordinating the protection of women especially against victims of violence, female workers, elderly women and women with disabilities;
k. facilitating the strengthening and development of networks of community institutions or organizations and the business world for the implementation of gender mainstreaming;
l. developing a gender-based information system; and
m. providing and organizing integrated or non-integrated services for women victims of violence, female workers, elderly women, and women with disabilities.
(2) Forms of women's empowerment and gender mainstreaming mechanisms as referred to in paragraph (1), are implemented in accordance with statutory regulations.

Part Three
Prevention of Violence and Participation in Prevention

Paragraph 1
Prevention of Violence

Article 15
(1) Efforts to prevent violence against women are carried out in an integrated manner by the Regional Government coordinated by the agency that has the task and function of coordination in the field of women's empowerment and child protection.
(2) Prevention efforts as referred to in paragraph (1), are implemented through:
a. forming a network in efforts to prevent violence;
b. conducting coordination, integration, synchronization of violence prevention based on partnership work patterns between Regional Government institutions and the community;
c. building an integrated security system in areas prone to cases of violence against women;
d. building complaint and service units regarding violence against women at the sub-district, kelurahan and/or Lembang levels; and
e. campaigns for awareness, socialization and education to the community about the protection and empowerment of women.

Paragraph 2
Participation in Prevention

Article 16
Participation in prevention efforts as referred to in Article 15, the Regional Government must involve:
a. families;
b. community; and
c. women's advocacy institutions.

Part Four
Protection of Women

Article 17
(1) Protection of women includes:
a. protection of the personal security of victims or witnesses;
b. physical and psychological threats;
c. confidentiality of victim and witness identity;
d. provision of testimony during the legal process regulated through protection mechanisms.
(2) Protection for women victims also includes the provision of safe houses as temporary shelter to obtain a sense of security during the judicial process and/or outside the judicial process.
(3) The implementation of protection and safe houses is carried out by the Regional Government.
(4) Further provisions on the mechanism for protecting women as referred to in paragraph (2), and the operation of safe houses as referred to in paragraph (3), will be regulated in a Regent Regulation.

CHAPTER V
IMPLEMENTATION OF CHILD PROTECTION

Part One
Rights and Obligations of Children

Article 18
Children's rights are as follows:
a. the right to survival, growth, development and participation in a proper manner in accordance with human dignity and to receive protection from violence and discrimination;
b. the right to a name as self-identity and citizenship status;
c. the right to worship according to their religion, think, and express themselves according to their level of intelligence and age, under parental guidance;
d. the right to know their parents, be raised, and cared for by their own parents;
e. the right to obtain health services and social security according to physical, mental, spiritual, and social needs;
f. the right to obtain education and teaching in the context of developing their personality and level of intelligence according to their interests and talents;
g. especially for children with disabilities, also have the right to obtain special education, while children who have excellence also have the right to receive special education;
h. the right to express and be heard their opinions, receive, seek, and provide information according to their level of intelligence and age for their self-development in accordance with moral values and propriety;
i. the right to rest and use leisure time, socialize with peers, play, recreate, and create according to their interests, talents, and level of intelligence for self-development;
j. children with disabilities have the right to obtain rehabilitation, social assistance and maintenance of social welfare standards;
k. the right to be cared for by their own parents, unless there are legal reasons and/or rules showing that the separation is in the best interests of the child and is a last consideration;
l. the right to meet directly and have personal contact regularly with both parents;
m. the right to receive care, maintenance, education and protection for the growth and development process from both parents according to abilities, talents, and interests;
n. the right to obtain living expenses from both parents;
o. the right to obtain protection from abuse in political activities, involvement in armed conflicts, involvement in social unrest, involvement in events containing elements of violence and involvement in warfare.

Article 19
(1) Every child has the right to obtain protection from being the target of persecution, torture, or imposition of inhuman punishment.
(2) Every child has the right to obtain freedom in accordance with the law.
(3) Every child has the right to be protected from arrest, detention, and/or criminal imprisonment of children can only be done if in accordance with applicable law and can only be done as a last resort.

Article 20
A child who is detained for a legal case has the right to:
a. receive humane treatment and placement separated from adults;
b. obtain legal assistance or other assistance effectively at every stage of applicable legal efforts; and
c. defend themselves and obtain justice before an objective and impartial juvenile court in a closed session for the public.

Article 21
Every child who becomes a perpetrator or victim of sexual violence or who is in conflict with the law has the right to confidentiality.

Article 22
In the event that for some reason the parents cannot guarantee the child's growth and development, resulting in the child becoming neglected, then the child has the right to be cared for or adopted as a foster child or adopted child by another person or institution in accordance with statutory provisions.

Article 23
Every child is obliged to:
a. respect parents, guardians, and teachers;
b. love family, community, and care for friends;
c. love the homeland, nation and state;
d. perform worship according to the teachings of their religion; and
e. implement noble ethics and morals.

Part Two
Prevention

Article 24
(1) Efforts to prevent violence against children are carried out in an integrated manner by the Regional Government coordinated by the agency that has the task and function of coordination in the field of women's empowerment and child protection.
(2) Prevention efforts as referred to in paragraph (1) are implemented through:
a. forming a network in efforts to prevent violence;
b. conducting coordination, integration, synchronization of violence prevention based on partnership work patterns between regional government institutions and the community;
c. building an integrated security system in areas prone to cases of violence against children;
d. including local content material about children's rights and the elimination of violence against children as teaching material in teaching and learning activities in early childhood education institutions up to the level of Senior High School;
e. building complaint and service units regarding violence against children at the sub-district, kelurahan and/or lembang levels, as well as schools;
f. campaigns for awareness, socialization and education to the community about child protection.

Part Three
Child Protection

Article 25
(1) Every child receives protection from acts of violence, exploitation, trafficking, discrimination, maltreatment and neglect.
(2) Protection as referred to in paragraph (1) is described in various fields of life, including:
a. in the field of health;
b. in the field of religion;
c. in the field of education;
d. in the social field; and
e. special protection.

Article 26
(1) Protection in the health field as referred to in Article 25 paragraph (2) letter a, is carried out by the government through the provision of health facilities and seeking comprehensive health services for children, so that every child obtains optimal health status from the womb.
(2) Comprehensive health services as referred to in paragraph (1) include promotional, preventive, curative, and rehabilitative efforts, both for basic health services and referrals.
(3) Comprehensive health service efforts as referred to in paragraph (2) are implemented by the Regional Government free of charge for families who cannot afford it.

Article 27
(1) Parents and families provide protection to maintain children's health starting from examining the child's growth and development from the womb.
(2) In the event that parents and families are unable to carry out protection as referred to in paragraph (1), then the Regional Government is obliged to fulfill it.
(3) The Regional Government provides free assistance and services for people who cannot afford it as referred to in paragraph (2), and its implementation is carried out in accordance with statutory provisions.

Article 28
(1) The Regional Government provides protection to children from organ transplant efforts for the benefit of other parties.
(2) The Regional Government, parents and families protect children from acts of:
a. taking organs and/or body tissues of children without regard to the child's health;
b. buying and selling organs and/or body tissues of children; and
c. health research that uses children as research objects without parental permission.

Article 29
(1) Every child receives protection in the religious field as referred to in Article 25 paragraph (2) letter b, to worship according to their religion.
(2) Protection of children in embracing their religion as referred to in paragraph (1) includes fostering, guiding, and practicing good religious teachings for children.

Article 30
(1) Every child receives protection in the field of education as referred to in Article 25 paragraph (2) letter c, to obtain education.
(2) The Regional Government, parents, and families must provide the widest possible opportunity for children to obtain education from an early age.
(3) The Regional Government provides adequate facilities and infrastructure to support the teaching and learning process for children.
(4) Non-governmental institutions may provide teaching and learning facilities and infrastructure for children, in accordance with statutory provisions.

Article 31
Education as referred to in Article 30 paragraph (1), is intended and directed to:
a. development of personality abilities, mental, talents, and physical of children optimally;
b. development of respect for parents and others;
c. development of understanding of cultural and customary values in the place where children grow and develop;
d. preparation of children for responsible life; and
e. development of respect and love for the nation and state.

Article 32
(1) The Regional Government provides protection for children with disabilities, to get opportunities in obtaining special education.
(2) Children who have excellence are given the opportunity to receive special education.

Article 33
The Regional Government provides education costs and/or free assistance or special services for children from families who cannot afford it, neglected children, and children in remote areas.

Article 34
(1) Protection in the field of education as referred to in Article 30 paragraph (1), applies both outside the school environment and within the school environment.
(2) Protection outside the school environment is to protect and keep children from acts of violence, exploitation, trafficking and discrimination.
(3) Protection in the school environment is protection from acts of violence that can be committed either by school administrators, by teachers, or by their own friends.

Article 35
Special services as referred to in Article 33, in addition to assistance in the form of money, school equipment, can also be in the form of providing school shuttle buses whose procurement is in accordance with regional financial capacity.

Article 36
(1) Child protection in the Social field as referred to in Article 25 paragraph (2) letter d, includes the maintenance and care of neglected children.
(2) Maintenance and care as referred to in paragraph (1), includes:
a. fulfillment of basic needs consisting of clothing, food, shelter, education, health, security guarantees, freedom to worship according to their religion and equal treatment with other children to achieve optimal social integration; and
b. fulfillment of special needs for children with disabilities.
(3) Maintenance of neglected children as referred to in paragraph (1), can also be carried out by individuals and non-governmental institutions or community institutions.
(4) Government institutions, and/or individuals, and/or community institutions in providing services as referred to in paragraph (2), can cooperate with various related service units.
(5) Supervision of the implementation of maintenance and care as referred to in paragraph (1), is carried out by regional apparatus organizations (OPD) that administer affairs in the social field in accordance with statutory provisions.

Article 37
Special protection as referred to in Article 25 paragraph (2) letter e, is given specifically to children namely:
a. children in emergency situations;
b. children in conflict with the law;
c. children from minority and isolated groups;
d. children exploited economically and/or sexually;
e. child victims of trafficking;
f. children who are victims of abuse of narcotics, alcohol, psychotropics, and other addictive substances (drugs);
g. child victims of kidnapping;
h. child victims of violence both physical and mental; and
i. children with disabilities

Article 38
(1) Children in emergency situations as referred to in Article 37 letter a, consist of:
a. children who become refugees;
b. child victims of riots;
c. child victims of natural disasters; and
d. children in armed conflict situations.
(2) Special protection for children in emergency situations as referred to in paragraph (1), is implemented in accordance with statutory provisions.

Article 39
(1) The regional government and community provide special protection for children in conflict with the law as referred to in Article 34 letter b, which includes children in conflict with the law and child victims of criminal acts.
(2) Special protection for children in conflict with the law as referred to in paragraph (1) is implemented through:
a. treatment of children in a humane manner in accordance with dignity and respect for children's rights;
b. provision of special companion officers for children;
c. provision of special facilities and infrastructure;
d. imposition of appropriate sanctions for the best interests of the child;
e. continuous monitoring and recording of the development of children in conflict with the law;
f. provision of guarantees to maintain relationships with parents or family; and
g. protection from identity reporting through mass media and to avoid labeling.
(3) Special protection for children who become victims of criminal acts as referred to in paragraph (1) is implemented through:
a. child rehabilitation efforts;
b. protection efforts from identity reporting through mass media and to avoid labeling;
c. provision of safety guarantees for victim witnesses;
d. provision of accessibility to obtain information regarding case developments.

Article 40
Special protection for children from minority and isolated groups as referred to in Article 37 letter c, is carried out through the provision of facilities and infrastructure to be able to enjoy their own culture, recognize and practice their own religious teachings, and use their own language.

Article 41
(1) The Regional Government provides special protection for children who are exploited economically and/or sexually as referred to in Article 37 letter d and child victims of violence both physical and mental as referred to in Article 37 letter h.
(2) Special protection for exploited children and child victims of violence as referred to in paragraph (1) is carried out through:
a. socialization of statutory regulations related to the protection of children exploited economically and/or sexually;
b. monitoring, reporting, and imposing sanctions; and
c. involvement of various organizations, Regional Government, companies, labor unions, social institutions and the community.

Article 42
Special protection for children involved in and victims of abuse of narcotics, alcohol, psychotropics, and other addictive substances (drugs) as referred to in Article 37 letter f, is carried out through supervision, prevention, treatment, and rehabilitation efforts by the Regional Government and community.

Article 43
The Regional Government provides special protection for child victims of trafficking as referred to in Article 37 letter e and child victims of kidnapping as referred to in Article 37 letter g which is carried out through efforts:
a. supervision;
b. protection;
c. prevention;
d. treatment; and
e. rehabilitation.

Article 44
The Regional Government provides special protection for children with disabilities as referred to in Article 37 letter i, through efforts:
a. treatment of children in a humane manner in accordance with dignity, and respect for children's rights;
b. fulfillment of special needs; and
c. obtaining equal treatment with other children to achieve optimal social integration.

CHAPTER VI
OBLIGATIONS AND RESPONSIBILITIES

Part One
General

Article 45
Obligations and responsibilities in providing protection to women and children from acts of violence and discrimination are the joint responsibility of:
a. Regional Government;
b. Lembang Government;
c. Community; and
d. Parents and Family.

Part Two
Regional Government

Article 46
(1) The obligations and responsibilities of the Regional Government as referred to in Article 45 letter a, include:
a. establishing policies, programs, and activities for the protection of women and children from acts of violence, exploitation, trafficking and discrimination;
b. implementing policies for the protection of women and children from acts of violence established by the government;
c. cooperating with other parties in implementing the protection of women and children from acts of violence, exploitation, trafficking and discrimination;
d. providing support for facilities and infrastructure for implementing the protection of women and children from acts of violence;
e. allocating budgets for implementing the protection of women and children from acts of violence according to regional financial capacity;
f. fostering and supervising the implementation of protection of women and children from acts of violence, exploitation, trafficking and discrimination;
g. providing recovery and social reintegration services;
h. encouraging and increasing community participation; and
i. providing human resources in implementing the protection of women and children victims of acts of violence, exploitation, trafficking and discrimination.
(2) In the event that a child becomes neglected as referred to in Article 22, then the Regional Government is responsible and obliged to take over and provide services to neglected children as referred to in Article 36 paragraph (2) letter a.
(3) Community institutions, and/or individuals may submit applications to the court to become guardians and/or foster parents for neglected children.

Article 47
(1) In order to carry out obligations and responsibilities as referred to in Article 46 paragraph (1), the Regent establishes programs and activities for the protection of women and children in the regional action plan as a basis for regional apparatus in implementing the protection of women and children from acts of violence, exploitation, trafficking and discrimination.
(2) The regional action plan as referred to in paragraph (1), is part of the Regional Medium-Term Development Plan (RPJMD).
(3) Further provisions regarding the Regional Action Plan as referred to in paragraph (2) are regulated by Regent Regulation.

Part Three
Lembang Government

Article 48
The obligations and responsibilities of the Lembang Government as referred to in Article 45 letter b, include:
a. implementing policies for the protection of women and children from acts of violence, exploitation, trafficking and discrimination established by the Regional Government;
b. cooperating with other parties in implementing the protection of women and children from acts of violence, exploitation, trafficking and discrimination;
c. providing support for facilities and infrastructure for implementing the protection of women and children from acts of violence, exploitation, trafficking and discrimination;
d. allocating budgets for implementing the protection of women and children from acts of violence, exploitation, trafficking and discrimination according to lembang financial capacity;
e. fostering and supervising the implementation of protection of women and children from acts of violence, exploitation, trafficking and discrimination; and
f. encouraging and increasing community participation.

Part Four
Community

Article 49
(1) The obligations and responsibilities of the community as referred to in Article 45 letter c, are carried out by participating in the protection of women and children victims of violence, exploitation, trafficking and discrimination, both individually and in groups.
(2) The role of the community as referred to in paragraph (1) is carried out by individuals, women and child protection institutions, social welfare institutions, community organizations, educational institutions, mass media, and the business world.
(3) The role of the community in implementing the protection of women and children as referred to in paragraph (1) is carried out by:
a. providing information through socialization and education regarding the protection of women and children from acts of violence, exploitation, trafficking and discrimination;
b. providing input in the formulation of policies related to the protection of women and children from acts of violence, exploitation, trafficking and discrimination;
c. reporting to the authorities if there are violations of acts of violence, exploitation, trafficking and discrimination against women and children;
d. playing an active role in the rehabilitation and social reintegration process for women and children victims of acts of violence, exploitation, trafficking and discrimination;
e. conducting monitoring and supervision of the Protection of Women and Children from acts of violence, exploitation, trafficking and discrimination;
f. providing facilities and infrastructure in order to support the protection of women and children from acts of violence, exploitation, trafficking and discrimination;
g. playing an active role in eliminating negative labeling of women and children victims of acts of violence, exploitation, trafficking and discrimination; and
h. providing space for women and children to be able to participate and express opinions.

Part Five
Parents and Family

Article 50
(1) The obligations and responsibilities of parents and families as referred to in Article 45 letter d, are to protect and prevent all forms of violence, exploitation, trafficking and discrimination both against women and against children.
(2) The obligations and responsibilities of parents towards children as referred to in paragraph (1), are to:
a. care for, maintain, educate, and protect children;
b. develop children according to abilities, talents, and interests;
c. prevent marriage at child age; and
d. provide character education and instill moral values in children.
(3) Guardians and/or foster parents who have obtained a determination as referred to in Article 46 paragraph (3), must be able to fulfill the provisions as referred to in Article 36 paragraph (2) letter a.
(4) Guardians and/or foster parents appointed as referred to in paragraph (3) must have the same religion as that professed by the Child.
(5) Guardians and/or foster parents as referred to in paragraph (3) are responsible for the child and are obliged to manage the property of the child concerned for the best interests of the child.
(6) Guardians and/or foster parents as referred to in paragraph (3), may represent the child to perform legal acts, both inside and outside the court for the best interests of the child.

CHAPTER VII
INSTITUTIONS

Article 51
(1) In the context of protecting women and children, the Regional Government may establish PPT.
(2) The establishment and development of PPT is adjusted to priority needs, regional financial capacity and the capacity of existing institutions and personnel in the region.
(3) PPT as referred to in paragraph (1), may take the form of:
a. P2TP2A;
b. LK3;
c. KPAD; and
d. other institutions according to needs and in accordance with statutory provisions.

Article 52
(1) P2TP2A as referred to in Article 51 paragraph (3) letter a, is positioned at the regional level and sub-district level.
(2) P2TP2A is under the coordination of OPD or other units that handle women's empowerment and child protection.
(3) P2TP2A as referred to in paragraph (1) does not have the authority to formulate policies for women's empowerment and child protection.
(4) P2TP2A is one form of integrated service unit, which functions as:
a. information center for women and children;
b. service center for women and children victims of violence; and
c. empowerment center for women and children.

Article 53
(1) The establishment of P2TP2A is determined by Regent Decree for the regional level and sub-district head decree for the sub-district level.
(2) P2TP2A membership may come from elements of the Regional Government, professional organizations, academics, community leaders and other elements as needed.
(3) Further provisions regarding P2TP2A are regulated in a Regent Regulation.

Article 54
(1) In order to handle psychosocial problems of victims, restore the psychosocial condition of victims and strengthen family resilience, LK3 may be established in the region.
(2) The procedure for establishing, main tasks and functions as well as the service mechanism of LK3 are carried out in accordance with statutory provisions.

Article 55
(1) In order to provide protection and fulfillment of children's rights, KPAD may be established in the region.
(2) The procedure for establishing, main tasks and functions as well as the service mechanism of KPAD as referred to in paragraph (1), are carried out in accordance with statutory provisions.

CHAPTER VIII
CHILD-FRIENDLY REGENCY

Article 56
(1) To guarantee the fulfillment of children's rights so they can live, grow, develop, and participate optimally in accordance with human dignity and receive protection from violence and discrimination, real efforts are needed in an integrated manner between the regional government, community and business world through the development of KLA.
(2) Further provisions regarding the development of KLA as referred to in paragraph (1), are regulated by regional regulation.

CHAPTER IX
SPECIAL DEVELOPMENT PLANNING DELIBERATION FOR WOMEN AND CHILDREN

Article 57
(1) Special development planning deliberation for women and children is a form of annual meeting or gathering held specifically for women and children to explore various ideas and input for the fulfillment of women's and children's rights in regional development program activities.
(2) Special development planning deliberation for women and children is carried out before the implementation of development planning deliberation.
(3) Further provisions regarding the implementation mechanism of special development planning deliberation for women and children as referred to in paragraph (1), are regulated by Regent Regulation.

CHAPTER X
FINANCING

Article 58
The costs of implementing the protection of women and children are sourced from:
a. Regional Revenue and Expenditure Budget;
b. Lembang Revenue and Expenditure Budget; and
c. Other legal and non-binding sources in accordance with statutory provisions.

CHAPTER XI
PROHIBITIONS

Article 59
(1) Every business operator whose activities can interfere with the growth and development of children is prohibited from receiving child visitors.
(2) Business operations as referred to in paragraph (1), include:
a. night clubs including discotheques, bars and pubs;
b. adult karaoke;
c. massage parlors;
d. steam bath/sauna parlors; and
e. other similar business fields.

Article 60
Every operator of hotels, motels, inns, tourism guesthouse businesses and similar business activities is prohibited from renting rooms to children without being accompanied by parents or adult family members or supervising teachers/persons in charge in the context of carrying out school activities or other activities.

Article 61
(1) Every operator of internet services, play stations and types of children's games based on information and communication technology is required to manage and design child-friendly places.
(2) Every business operator as referred to in paragraph (1) is obliged to:
a. arrange rooms/booths with adequate lighting and not closed;
b. not accept children wearing uniforms and/or during school hours;
c. locally block sites containing pornographic and pornographic elements; and
d. limit visiting hours and supervise the use of existing facilities.

CHAPTER XII
ADMINISTRATIVE SANCTIONS

Article 62
(1) Every person or business entity that violates the provisions as referred to in Article 59, Article 60 and Article 61, is subject to administrative sanctions in the form of:
a. written warning; and
b. license revocation.
(2) Written warning as referred to in paragraph (1) letter a, is given 3 (three) times with a grace period between one warning and another of at most 1 (one) month.
(3) If after the third warning is not heeded, then license revocation will be carried out.

CHAPTER XIII
CRIMINAL SANCTIONS

Article 63
Every person or institution that commits acts that result in suffering or hardship physically, psychologically, sexually, and/or neglect, coercion, or deprivation of freedom against women and children so that violations of this regional regulation occur, will be subject to criminal sanctions in accordance with statutory provisions.

CHAPTER XIV
CLOSING PROVISIONS

Article 64
Regent Regulations as implementing regulations of this Regional Regulation must be established at the latest 1 (one) year from the promulgation of this Regional Regulation.

Article 65
This regional regulation comes into force on the date of promulgation.
In order that everyone may know it, orders the promulgation of this Regional Regulation by placing it in the Regional Gazette of Tana Toraja Regency.

EXPLANATION
OF
REGIONAL REGULATION OF TANA TORAJA REGENCY
NUMBER 4 YEAR 2017
CONCERNING
PROTECTION OF WOMEN AND CHILDREN

GENERAL
Women and children are God's creatures who have dignity and human rights that need to be respected and valued.
Women are pillars of the nation and children are the buds of the next generation of the nation's ideals. That children, who will later bear great responsibility as the next generation of the nation, need to get the widest possible opportunity to grow and develop optimally, both physically, mentally, spiritually and socially, so that children have noble character.
Women and children have a strategic role in determining the success of development programs in all aspects of state life. However, due to the still low understanding of the dignity of women and children, they are vulnerable to becoming victims of violence and discrimination both within the family and in their environment.
Various acts of violence against women and children continue to increase from time to time. If there is no awareness to give serious attention and handling, it will destroy household life, society, nation and state.
Considering this, it is time for a regulation in the form of a regional regulation on the Protection of Women and Children that is able to answer while providing legal certainty for the Protection of Women and Children from acts of violence and discrimination.
As a concrete step in efforts to provide Protection for Women and Children in Tana Toraja Regency, through the initiative of the DPRD, a Regional Regulation on the Protection of Women and Children was drafted as an implementation of various statutory regulations, including Law Number 39 of 1999 concerning Human Rights, Law Number 23 of 2002 concerning Child Protection, Law Number 23 of 2004 concerning the Elimination of Domestic Violence, Law Number 35 of 2014 concerning Amendment to Law Number 23 of 2002 concerning Child Protection, Law Number 8 of 2016 concerning Persons with Disabilities and Regulation of the Minister of Women's Empowerment and Child Protection Number 6 of 2015 concerning the System for Women's Empowerment and Child Protection.
This Regional Regulation is intended to give responsibility to the Regional Government from preventing acts of violence to handling victims of violence.
In this regional regulation, it is regulated about the rights of Women and Children, Women's Empowerment, Prevention, Protection of Women and Children. It also regulates special handling of neglected children, responsibilities of regional government, responsibilities of Lembang government, up to Community, Parents and Family Participation in prevention, protection and handling of Women and Children.
In this regional regulation, it is also regulated about financing sourced from APBD, APBL and other non-binding sources, which is expected to guarantee the implementation of protection of Women and Children from acts of violence, exploitation, trafficking and discrimination.
