CHAPTER I
GENERAL PROVISIONS
Article 1
In this Regional Regulation, what is meant by:
1. Regent is the Regent of Sanggau.
2. Region is Sanggau Regency.
3. Regional Government is the Regent as an element of the Regional Government organizer who leads the implementation of government affairs that are the authority of the Region.
4. Regional Apparatus is an auxiliary element of the Regent and the Regional House of Representatives in organizing government affairs that are the authority of the Region.
5. Violence is any act that results in or may result in misery or suffering whether physical, sexual, economic, social and psychological to the Victim.
6. Violence Against Women is any action that results in or may result in misery or suffering to women physically, sexually, economically, socially, psychologically
including threats of certain actions, coercion or deprivation of liberty, whether occurring in public or private life.
7. Victim is a woman who experiences misery and/or suffering either directly or indirectly as a result of Violence and life vulnerability.
8. Protection of Women is all activities aimed at providing a sense of security carried out by the Regional Government, community, and/or other parties who know or hear that Violence Against Women will, is, or has occurred.
9. Service/Handling is action taken as soon as possible to the Victim when seeing, hearing and/or knowing that Violence against the Victim will, is, or has occurred.
10. Assistance is a person or representative from an institution who has expertise in assisting Victims to conduct counseling, therapy and/or advocacy.
11. Integrated Service Center, hereinafter abbreviated as PPT is an institution or functional work unit that organizes integrated services for Victims of Violence.
12. Safe House is a residence for Victims during the Assistance process for the safety and comfort of Victims from threats and danger.
13. Complaint Handling is a series of actions taken by integrated service providers to follow up on reports of acts of Violence Against Women submitted by Victims, Families and the community.
14. Health Services are efforts that include promotive, preventive, curative and rehabilitative aspects.
15. Social Rehabilitation is services aimed at restoring and developing the ability of a person experiencing social dysfunction to be able to carry out social functions properly.
16. Legal Aid is legal services provided by legal assistants and advocacy to conduct the process of Assisting witnesses and/or Victims of Violence Against Women that is gender sensitive.
17. Law Enforcement is action by officials authorized by the state to implement statutory regulations.

18. Repatriation is an effort to return female Victims of Violence from abroad to the debarkation/entry point, or from the receiving area to the area of origin.
19. Social Reintegration is an effort to reunite Victims with Family, substitute family, or community that can provide protection and fulfillment for Victims.
20. Family is the smallest unit in society consisting of husband and wife, or husband-wife and their children, or father and his children, or mother and her children, or blood relatives in a straight line upward or downward.
CHAPTER II
SCOPE OF PROTECTION
Article 2
The scope of Protection of Women from acts of Violence includes:
a. prevention;
b. Service/Handling; and
c. empowerment.
Part One
Prevention
Article 3
(1) Efforts to prevent Violence Against Women are carried out in an integrated manner by the Regional Government coordinated by the Regional Apparatus in charge of women's empowerment.
(2) Prevention efforts as referred to in paragraph (1) are implemented by:
a. forming work networks;
b. conducting coordination, integration, and synchronization of Violence prevention based on partnership patterns;
c. forming a Violence prevention system;
d. socializing statutory regulations related to Protection of Women Victims of Violence; and
e. providing information regarding Protection of Women from acts of Violence through communication, information and education (KIE) media.

Article 4
In addition to prevention efforts carried out by the Regional Government as referred to in Article 3, prevention efforts are also carried out by Families and/or closest relatives, communities, educational institutions and customary institutions.
Part Two
Service/Handling
Article 5
(1) Implementation of Service/Handling for Victims is carried out in an integrated manner by PPT.
(2) PPT as referred to in paragraph (1) may receive and send case referrals from and/or to other service units in a networked manner.
Article 6
Forms of Service/Handling for Victims include:
a. Complaint Handling;
b. health/medical rehabilitation services;
c. Social Rehabilitation services;
d. Law Enforcement and Legal Aid services; and
e. Repatriation and Social Reintegration services.
Article 7
Implementation of Service/Handling for Victims is carried out with the principles of:
a. fast and appropriate;
b. safe and comfortable;
c. sense of empathy;
d. non-discrimination;
e. easily accessible;
f. not charged; and
g. guaranteed confidentiality.
Part Three
Empowerment
Article 8
Forms of empowerment for female Victims of Violence include:
a. forming communities of female Victims of Violence;
b. job training;

c. productive economic enterprises and joint business groups; and
d. capital assistance.
Article 9
Job training as referred to in Article 8 letter b includes:
a. skills training;
b. field work practice; and
c. apprenticeship.
Article 10
Productive economic enterprises and joint business groups as referred to in Article 8 letter c include:
a. entrepreneurial skills training; and
b. Assistance in business implementation.
Article 11
Capital assistance as referred to in Article 8 letter d includes:
a. facilities and infrastructure assistance; and
b. working/business capital assistance facilities.
CHAPTER III
VIOLENCE
Article 12
Violence Against Women consists of:
a. physical violence;
b. psychological violence;
c. sexual violence;
d. neglect;
e. exploitation; and/or
f. other violence.
Article 13
Physical violence as referred to in Article 12 letter a includes acts that result in pain, wounds, injury or disability to the body, miscarriage, fainting, and/or death.
Article 14
Psychological violence as referred to in Article 12 letter b includes acts that result in fear, loss of self-confidence, loss of ability to act, feelings of helplessness, and/or severe psychological suffering.
Article 15
Sexual violence as referred to in Article 12 letter c includes:
a. acts in the form of sexual harassment;
b. forced sexual relations in an unnatural or unwanted manner; and/or
c. forced sexual relations with others for commercial purposes and/or certain purposes.
Article 16
Neglect as referred to in Article 12 letter d includes:
a. acts that neglect women within the scope of their household; and/or
b. acts that result in economic dependence.
Article 17
Exploitation as referred to in Article 12 letter e includes:
a. actions that exploit women economically or sexually with the intention of benefiting oneself or others;
b. actions with or without the consent of the Victim which include but are not limited to prostitution, forced labor or services, slavery or similar practices, oppression, extortion, utilization of physical, sexual, reproductive organs, or unlawfully transferring or transplanting organs and/or body tissues or utilizing the labor or ability of a person by another party to obtain material or immaterial benefits; and/or
c. all forms of utilization of sexual organs or other body organs of the Victim to obtain benefits, including but not limited to all prostitution or indecency activities.
Article 18
Other violence as referred to in Article 12 letter f includes:
a. any unlawful act in the form of speech, writing, images, symbols, or body movements, whether with or without using means that cause fear or restrict a person's fundamental freedom; and/or

b. a situation where a person/Victim is ordered to do something in such a way that the person does something contrary to their own will.
CHAPTER IV
RIGHTS OF FEMALE VICTIMS OF VIOLENCE
Article 19
Every female Victim of Violence has the right to:
a. have her dignity and honor respected as a human being;
b. obtain physical and psychological health recovery from the suffering experienced;
c. determine her own decisions;
d. obtain information;
e. have her identity kept confidential;
f. obtain compensation;
g. obtain Social Rehabilitation;
h. obtain Complaint Handling;
i. obtain facilitation in the judicial process; and
j. obtain Assistance.
CHAPTER V
GOVERNMENT RESPONSIBILITY AND INSTITUTIONS
Part One
Regional Government
Article 20
(1) The Regional Government is responsible for implementing efforts to Protect Women from acts of Violence in the form of:
a. forming PPT;
b. policy formulation;
c. planning and implementing programs and activities;
d. providing facilities and infrastructure support; and
e. supervising service delivery to Victims with minimum service standards.
(2) In carrying out the responsibilities as referred to in paragraph (1), the Regional Government prepares a Regional Action Plan for Protection of Women.
(3) Further provisions regarding the Regional Action Plan as referred to in paragraph (2) are regulated by Regent Regulation.

Article 21
The responsibilities of the Regional Government as referred to in Article 20 paragraph (1) are coordinated by the Regional Apparatus in charge of women's empowerment.
Part Two
Community and Family
Article 22
The Community and Family have obligations and responsibilities to:
a. prevent the occurrence of Violence Against Women;
b. report if Violence occurs;
c. protect Victims; and
d. provide emergency assistance.
Part Three
Coordination of Victim Protection
Article 23
(1) Efforts to provide and organize Service/Handling for Victims are carried out in a networked manner.
(2) The network as referred to in paragraph (1) aims to:
a. coordinate and synchronize PPT service handling;
b. develop networks and referral systems; and
c. collect, compile and present Violence reports.
(3) The network as referred to in paragraph (2) carries out its duties and functions in accordance with the forms of service as referred to in Article 6.
CHAPTER VI
PPT
Article 24
(1) PPT may be formed by the Regional Government, vertical agencies in the Region, and/or community institutions.
(2) PPT as referred to in paragraph (1) consists of elements of the Regional Police, Regional Apparatus in charge of Health, Education, Social Affairs, Community Empowerment, Women's Empowerment and Child Protection as well as Community Social Institutions, which function to provide protection to women from acts of Violence
carried out in a networked manner.

(3) PPT as referred to in paragraph (1) is tasked with providing services and temporary protection in the form of a Safe House (shelter) for Victims.
(4) In the event that PPT does not have a Safe House as referred to in paragraph (3), then the Victim is referred to a PPT that has a Safe House.
(5) Provisions regarding the formation, composition of the management team and organizational structure of PPT formed by the Regional Government are stipulated by Regent Decree.
CHAPTER VII
INFORMATION AND REPORTING SYSTEM
Article 25
The Regional Apparatus in charge of women's empowerment implements an information system and submits reports on the implementation of Protection of Women to the Regent in accordance with statutory provisions.
CHAPTER VIII
FUNDING
Article 26
Funding for Protection of Women activities carried out by the Regional Government is sourced from the Regional Revenue and Expenditure Budget and/or other legitimate sources in accordance with statutory provisions.
CHAPTER IX
COMMUNITY PARTICIPATION
Article 27
(1) The community participates in the implementation of Protection of Women.
(2) Community participation as referred to in paragraph (1) is in the form of:
a. forming family partners at the sub-district/village level by the community;
b. forming women's protection units in community organizations;
c. conducting independent socialization of women's rights;

d. conducting prevention efforts if indications of Violence Against Women are found in the community;
e. providing first aid to Victims; and
f. reporting to the competent authorities if Violence Against Women occurs in their environment.
(3) Community participation as referred to in paragraph (1) may be carried out by individuals, social community institutions, non-governmental organizations, educational institutions, religious institutions, private sector, and/or mass media.
CHAPTER X
GUIDANCE AND SUPERVISION
Article 28
(1) The Regional Government conducts guidance and supervision of the implementation of Protection of Women.
(2) Guidance and supervision as referred to in paragraph (1) include:
a. coordination;
b. guidance;
c. education and training; and
d. monitoring and evaluation.
(3) Coordination as referred to in paragraph (2) letter a covers planning and implementation.
(4) Guidance as referred to in paragraph (2) letter b covers planning, implementation, procedures, funding, quality, control, and supervision.
(5) Education and training as referred to in paragraph (2) letter c are conducted in accordance with statutory provisions.
(6) Monitoring and evaluation as referred to in paragraph (2) letter d are conducted based on statutory regulations periodically every 6 (six) months.
CHAPTER XI
CLOSING PROVISIONS
Article 29
This Regional Regulation comes into force on the date of promulgation.

In order that everyone may know of it, orders the promulgation of this Regional Regulation by placing it in the Regional Gazette of Sanggau Regency.

I. GENERAL
Acts of Violence Against Women constitute violations of human rights so that their dignity and honor need to be protected and their right to life guaranteed in accordance with their nature and destiny without discrimination.
In order to prevent and overcome Violence Against Women in Sanggau Regency so as to avoid Violence, threats of violence, torture or treatment that degrades human dignity and honor, it is necessary to form a Regional Regulation concerning Protection of Women.
Protection of Women is all efforts aimed at protecting women and providing a sense of security in the fulfillment of their rights by providing consistent and systematic attention.
Existing statutory regulations or those related to Protection of Women Victims of Violence have not yet regulated efforts to Protect Women in the Region, so institutional support and regulations at the Regional level are needed that can guarantee its implementation.
This Regional Regulation regulates efforts to Protect Women specifically in the form of prevention, Service/Handling, and empowerment of women in Sanggau Regency.

Number 14
What is meant by efforts from the promotive aspect is the effort to promote/socialize about acts of Violence to the community, with the aim of increasing community vigilance and sensitivity to the danger of Violence that threatens women.
What is meant by efforts from the preventive aspect is efforts that prioritize prevention aspects targeting communities vulnerable to becoming/being Victims of Violence and receiving its impact. Preventive efforts can be carried out through community empowerment in the form of training activities and others.

What is meant by efforts from the curative aspect is efforts carried out in the form of handling for Victims of Violence who need handling according to their needs, namely health rehabilitation handling if the Victim suffers the impact of Violence physically or psychologically, provision of Legal Aid and Law Enforcement
for Victims who need Legal Aid and Law Enforcement, and so on.
What is meant by the rehabilitative aspect is efforts carried out as recovery efforts for Victims of Violence both physically, psychologically and psychosocially.
