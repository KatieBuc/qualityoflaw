MAYOR OF SOLOK
PROVINCE OF WEST SUMATRA
REGIONAL REGULATION OF SOLOK CITY
NUMBER 2 YEAR 2020
CONCERNING
IMPLEMENTATION OF PROTECTION FOR WOMEN AND CHILDREN
BY THE GRACE OF ALMIGHTY GOD
MAYOR OF SOLOK,

Considering: a. that every person including women and children has the right to obtain protection from violence, exploitation, and discriminatory treatment in order to provide security and comfort;

b. that the implementation of protection for women and children from acts of violence, discrimination, exploitation, and human rights violations in Solok City has not been optimal so that the role of Regional Government and society is required;

c. that to fill the legal vacuum related to the implementation of protection for women and children from acts of violence, discrimination, exploitation and human rights violations, it is necessary to establish a regional regulation;

d. that based on the considerations as referred to in letter a, letter b, and letter c, it is necessary to establish a Regional Regulation concerning the Implementation of Protection for Women and Children;

In View of: 1. Article 18 paragraph (6) of the 1945 Constitution of the Republic of Indonesia;

2. Law Number 8 Year 1956 concerning the Establishment of Small City Autonomous Regions Within the Central Sumatra Provincial Region (State Gazette of the Republic of Indonesia Year 1956 Number 19);

3. Law Number 7 Year 1984 concerning Ratification of the Convention on the Elimination of All Forms of Discrimination Against Women (State Gazette of the Republic of Indonesia Year 1984 Number 29, Supplement to State Gazette of the Republic of Indonesia Number 3277);

4. Law Number 23 Year 2002 concerning Child Protection (State Gazette of the Republic of Indonesia Year 2002 Number 109, Supplement to State Gazette of the Republic of Indonesia Number 4235) as amended several times most recently by Law Number 17 Year 2016 concerning Stipulation of Government Regulation in Lieu of Law Number 1 Year 2016 concerning Second Amendment to Law Number 23 Year 2002 concerning Child Protection into Law (State Gazette of the Republic of Indonesia Year 2016 Number 99, Supplement to State Gazette of the Republic of Indonesia Number 5882);

5. Law Number 23 Year 2014 concerning Regional Government (State Gazette of the Republic of Indonesia Year 2014 Number 244, Supplement to State Gazette of the Republic of Indonesia Number 5587) as amended several times most recently by Law Number 9 Year 2015 concerning Second Amendment to Law Number 23 Year 2014 concerning Regional Government (State Gazette of the Republic of Indonesia Year 2015 Number 58, Supplement to State Gazette of the Republic of Indonesia Number 5679);

6. Government Regulation Number 12 Year 2017 concerning Guidance and Supervision of Regional Government Administration (State Gazette of the Republic of Indonesia Year 2017 Number 73, Supplement to State Gazette of the Republic of Indonesia Number 6041);

7. Regulation of the Minister of Home Affairs Number 8 Year 1970 concerning Implementation of Solok Municipality Government and Payakumbuh Municipality;

8. Regulation of the Minister of Women's Empowerment and Child Protection Number 4 Year 2018 concerning Guidelines for Establishment of Regional Technical Implementation Units for Women and Children (State News of the Republic of Indonesia Year 2018 Number 532);

9. Regional Regulation of West Sumatra Province Number 5 Year 2013 concerning Protection of Women and Children (Regional Gazette of West Sumatra Province Year 2013 Number 51 Supplement to Regional Gazette of West Sumatra Province Number 88);

With Joint Approval of
THE REGIONAL PEOPLE'S REPRESENTATIVE COUNCIL OF SOLOK CITY
and
MAYOR OF SOLOK

DECIDES:

To Establish: REGIONAL REGULATION CONCERNING IMPLEMENTATION OF PROTECTION FOR WOMEN AND CHILDREN.

CHAPTER I
GENERAL PROVISIONS

Article 1
In this Regional Regulation what is meant by:
1. Region is Solok City.
2. Regional Government is the Mayor as an element of Regional Government administration who leads the implementation of government affairs that are the authority of the autonomous region.
3. Mayor is the Mayor of Solok.
4. Regional Apparatus is an assistant element to the Mayor and DPRD in administering government affairs that are the authority of the Region.
5. Protection of Women is all efforts to guarantee and protect the rights of women so that they can live healthily, develop themselves and participate optimally in accordance with human dignity and receive protection from neglect, violence, and discrimination whether in the form of preventive, repressive, or postremedial protection.
6. Child is a person who has not reached 18 (eighteen) years of age, including a child still in the womb.
7. Child Rights are part of human rights that must be guaranteed, protected and fulfilled by parents, family, society, government and the state.
8. Child Protection is all activities to guarantee and protect children and their rights so that they can live, grow, develop, and participate optimally in accordance with human dignity, and receive protection from violence and discrimination.
9. Neglected Child is a child whose needs are not properly fulfilled, whether physical, mental, spiritual, or social.
10. Street Child is a child whose life is irregular by spending most of their time outside the home to earn a living on the streets or in public places.
11. Regional Children's Forum hereinafter referred to as Forda is a forum for children's participation formed with the aim of facilitating children's involvement in decision-making processes and enjoying changes concerning their lives either directly or indirectly, which is carried out with the approval and will of all children based on awareness and understanding, in accordance with age and level of thinking maturity.
12. Regional Technical Implementation Unit for Protection of Women and Children hereinafter abbreviated as UPTD PPA is a regional technical implementation unit formed by the Regional Government in providing services for women and children who experience violence, discrimination, special protection, and other problems.
13. Service is an action that must be immediately taken for victims when seeing, hearing and knowing that violence against victims will, is, or has occurred.
14. Child Protection is all activities to guarantee and protect children and their rights so that they can live, grow, develop and participate, optimally in accordance with human dignity, and receive protection from violence and discrimination.
15. Assistance is all actions taken in the form of complaint services, health, advocacy and legal aid, social rehabilitation including spiritual guidance, counseling, psychological therapy and economic empowerment, repatriation and social rehabilitation for strengthening and advocacy as well as recovery of victims of violence;
16. Violence is any act unlawfully with or without means against physical and psychological that causes danger to life, body or results in the deprivation of a person's freedom.
17. Violence Against Women is any action based on gender differences that results or may result in suffering or hardship for women, physically, sexually or psychologically, including threats of certain actions, coercion or arbitrary deprivation of freedom, whether occurring in the public sphere or in private life.
18. Complaint Handling is a series of actions taken by integrated service providers to follow up on reports of acts of violence against women and children submitted by victims, families and the community.
19. Social Rehabilitation is a service aimed at restoring and developing the ability of a person experiencing social dysfunction to be able to carry out their social functions properly.
20. Legal Aid is legal services provided by legal assistants and advocates to carry out the process of assisting witnesses and/or victims of violence against women and children.
21. Social Rehabilitation is an effort to reunite victims with their families, substitute families, or communities that can provide protection and fulfill the needs of victims.
22. Spiritual Guidance is an effort to restore the soul through strengthening the religion adhered to by the victim.

Article 2
The scope of this Regional Regulation includes:
a. rights of women and children;
b. protection of women;
c. fulfillment of children's rights;
d. special protection for children;
e. handling of women and children victims of violence;
f. establishment of UPTD PPA;
g. guidance and supervision;
h. community participation; and
i. financing.

CHAPTER II
RIGHTS OF WOMEN AND CHILDREN

Part One
Rights of Women

Article 3
Every woman has the right to:
a. obtain protection from discriminatory actions;
b. obtain protection from economic and sexual exploitation;
c. obtain protection from physical, psychological, sexual violence, and neglect;
d. obtain work leave while receiving salary or wages in accordance with laws and regulations;
e. obtain decent work according to their abilities;
f. obtain education and teaching in all types, levels and paths of education;
g. ownership and management of joint property in marriage in accordance with laws and regulations;
h. elect and be elected in general elections in accordance with laws and regulations;
i. obtain special protection in carrying out work or profession against things that can threaten safety and/or health related to women's reproductive functions; and
j. obtain health services in accordance with standards.

Part Two
Rights of Children

Article 4
Every child has the right to:
a. play, create, participate, grow and develop;
b. freely assemble, associate, live with parents, relate to parents if separated and survival;
c. obtain name, identity, citizenship, education, child-appropriate information;
d. obtain health standards according to decent living standards;
e. obtain protection and parental love;
f. obtain protection from exploitation as child labor, minority groups/isolated indigenous groups and from sights or situations that by their nature are not yet appropriate for children to see; and
g. obtain special protection in emergency/urgent situations, displacement/displaced persons/evicted persons, if experiencing legal conflicts and armed conflicts or social conflicts.

CHAPTER III
PROTECTION OF WOMEN

Part One
General

Article 5
(1) The Regional Government is responsible for implementing protection of women by involving the active role of parents, families, ninik mamak, cadiak pandai, alim ulama, bundo kanduang, traditional leaders, religious leaders, society, and institutions in the field of women's protection.
(2) The responsibility of the Regional Government in implementing protection of women as referred to in paragraph (1) is carried out by regional apparatus in accordance with their duties and functions.

Part Two
Regional Government

Paragraph 1
Protection

Article 6
The Regional Government in efforts to protect women is responsible for:
a. providing protection from discriminatory actions in accordance with laws and regulations;
b. providing protection from economic exploitation and social exploitation;
c. providing protection from physical, psychological, sexual violence and neglect;
d. providing equal opportunities with men to obtain work according to their abilities;
e. providing work leave while still receiving salary or wages in accordance with laws and regulations;
f. providing adequate health and maternity facilities for pregnant and nursing mothers;
g. providing comprehensive free health services;
h. guaranteeing women's rights to elect and be elected in general elections in accordance with laws and regulations;
i. providing special protection in carrying out work or profession against things that can threaten safety and/or health related to women's reproductive functions;
j. providing special protection to women victims of violence and human trafficking;
k. taking preventive actions in order to prevent violations against women; and
l. making regulations to regulate employers who employ women to comply with labor laws and regulations.

Paragraph 2
Prevention

Article 7
The Regional Government in order to prevent violence against women is responsible for:
a. conducting socialization regarding prevention of violence against women to regional apparatus, community leaders, and community organizations;
b. optimizing the role of traditional, cultural, religious institutions and community organizations in preventing acts of violence;
c. providing guidelines on handling acts of violence against women;
d. involving community participation in recovery and reintegration of women victims of violence;
e. developing an integrated database and information system on acts of violence against women; and
f. developing family resilience programs.

Part Three
Society

Paragraph 1
Protection

Article 8
(1) Society which in this case also includes ninik mamak, alim ulama, cadiak pandai, bundo kanduang in the context of protection of women is responsible for:
a. participating and providing support for the development of women's protection programs;
b. providing information and/or reporting violations of women's rights to authorized institutions or parties; and
c. supervising the legal protection process for cases of women's rights violations.
(2) The responsibility as referred to in paragraph (1) is based on laws and regulations and local wisdom values of the local community.

Paragraph 2
Prevention

Article 9
Society in order to prevent violence against women is responsible for:
a. monitoring various conditions occurring in their environment that can lead to violence against women;
b. strengthening the role of community, religious organizations and family environment; and
c. preventing unregistered marriages.

Part Four
Parents and/or Family

Paragraph 1
Protection

Article 10
Parents and/or family in order to prevent violence against women are responsible for:
a. protecting women from conflicts and domestic violence that demean women's dignity;
b. providing equal opportunities for women to develop themselves in the fields of education, social, economic, political and cultural; and
c. preventing violations of women's rights in the household.

Paragraph 2
Prevention

Article 11
Parents and/or family in preventing violations of women's rights are responsible for:
a. protecting women from various rights violations in the household; and
b. encouraging the formation of harmonious, educative and democratic families.

CHAPTER IV
FULFILLMENT OF CHILDREN'S RIGHTS

Part One
General

Article 12
(1) The Regional Government in the context of fulfillment of children's rights is responsible for:
a. establishing and implementing policies, programs, and activities for fulfillment of children's rights;
b. providing facilities and infrastructure in implementing fulfillment of children's rights;
c. conducting guidance and supervision in the context of implementing fulfillment of children's rights;
d. respecting and guaranteeing the human rights of every child without distinguishing ethnicity, religion, race, class, gender, ethnicity, culture and language, legal status of children, birth order of children, and physical and/or mental conditions; and
e. cooperating with the private sector and/or non-governmental organizations in implementing fulfillment of children's rights.
(2) The Regional Government in the context of fulfillment of children's rights may cooperate with society, business world, and mass media.
(3) The responsibility of the Regional Government in efforts to fulfill children's rights as referred to in paragraph (1) is carried out by regional apparatus in accordance with their duties and functions.

Article 13
The Regional Government in efforts to guarantee, protect and fulfill children's rights is responsible for establishing and implementing policies for protection and fulfillment of children's rights covering the fields of birth registration, health, education, employment and social affairs.

Article 14
Society in efforts to fulfill children's rights is responsible for:
a. providing space to gather and organize as well as listening to children to express their opinions;
b. supervising children in accessing various information and providing healthy and safe information;
c. providing information through socialization and education regarding children's rights and laws and regulations concerning children; and
d. conducting supervision and guidance in the context of implementing fulfillment of children's rights through child facilitators.

Article 15
Business world in efforts to fulfill Children's Rights, is responsible for:
a. implementing child-perspective policies;
b. products intended for children must be safe for children; and
c. contributing to the fulfillment of children's rights through corporate social responsibility.

Article 16
Media is responsible in fulfilling children's rights to disseminate information and educational materials that are beneficial by taking into account the best interests of children from social, cultural, educational, religious and health aspects.

Part Two
Birth Registration

Article 17
(1) The Regional Government is obliged to conduct birth registration of children with issuance of birth certificates.
(2) Birth certificates as referred to in paragraph (1) are conducted free of charge.

Part Three
Health

Article 18
Policies for protection and fulfillment of children's rights in the health field are as follows:
a. providing free health services for children from poor families;
b. conducting comprehensive health efforts for children;
c. ensuring that babies receive exclusive breast milk from their mothers;
d. ensuring that children receive nutrition according to standards;
e. protecting children from organ transplant efforts for other parties; and
f. ensuring provision of growth and development monitoring for toddlers and pre-school age children.

Article 19
(1) The Regional Government is obliged to conduct comprehensive basic health services for children in the context of fulfilling children's rights in the health field so that children obtain optimal health status from the womb.
(2) Comprehensive basic health services as referred to in paragraph (1) include:
a. promotive efforts;
b. preventive efforts;
c. curative efforts; and
d. rehabilitative efforts.
(3) Comprehensive basic health services as referred to in paragraph (2) are conducted free of charge for children with malnutrition, children with disabilities, children with special needs, children infected with human immunodeficiency virus/acquired immunodeficiency syndrome, child laborers, child victims of narcotics abuse, alcohol, psychotropics and other addictive substances, children who are victims of violence, and/or child victims of human trafficking.

Article 20
Promotive efforts for basic health services as referred to in Article 19 paragraph (2) letter a are carried out through socialization or counseling to the community regarding healthy lifestyles including:
a. the importance of exclusive breast milk for babies;
b. the importance of immunization for babies and toddlers; and
c. the importance of healthy lifestyles and balanced nutrition;

Article 21
Preventive efforts for basic health services as referred to in Article 19 paragraph (2) letter b are implemented in order to prevent diseases or health problems by:
a. requiring mothers to provide exclusive breast milk to babies up to 6 (six) months of age;
b. providing free immunization and vitamins to babies and toddlers;
c. providing nutritious food to babies and toddlers from underprivileged families;
d. providing growth and development monitoring for toddlers and pre-school age children;
e. conducting comprehensive health efforts for children;
f. optimizing the role of integrated service posts in serving maternal and child health; and
g. providing sanitation facilities and clean water in every school.

Article 22
Curative efforts for basic health services as referred to in Article 19 paragraph (2) letter c are carried out in the context of treatment for disease healing, reduction of suffering due to disease, disease control, or restoration of disability so that children's health quality is maintained optimally.

Article 23
Rehabilitative efforts for basic health services as referred to in Article 19 paragraph (2) letter d are aimed at restoring conditions after illness which is carried out through regular health examinations.

Part Four
Education

Article 24
(1) Every organizer of education units is obliged to provide opportunities for children to obtain education without discrimination.
(2) Children with disabilities and children with intelligence potential and/or special talents are given equal opportunities to obtain formal, non-formal, and informal education services.
(3) The Regional Government provides educators for children with disabilities and intelligence potential and/or special talents in every education unit.

Article 25
Every child in the education unit environment is protected from acts of violence.

Article 26
Children in conflict with the law, children who experience pregnancy outside of marriage, child victims of human immunodeficiency virus/acquired immunodeficiency syndrome transmission, and child victims of narcotics, alcohol, psychotropics and other addictive substances abuse are protected in their rights to obtain education.

Article 27
(1) Every organizer of education units is prohibited from expelling children from educational institutions without guarantees for the continuity of children's education.
(2) Every organizer of formal education violating the provisions as referred to in paragraph (1) may be subject to administrative sanctions in the form of:
a. verbal warning;
b. written warning;
c. temporary suspension from activities;
d. termination of cooperation;
e. revocation of operational license; and
f. withdrawal of facilities.
(3) Provisions regarding procedures for applying administrative sanctions as referred to in paragraph (2) are regulated by Mayor Regulation.

Part Five
Employment

Article 28
(1) Employers are prohibited from employing children except for children aged 13 (thirteen) to 15 (fifteen) years who may perform light work as long as it does not interfere with health, physical, mental, social development and school time.
(2) Employers who employ children in light work as referred to in paragraph (1) must meet the requirements:
a. written permission from parents/guardians;
b. work agreement between employer and parents/guardians;
c. maximum working time of 3 (three) hours per day;
d. conducted during the day and does not interfere with school time;
e. occupational safety and health;
f. clear employment relationship;
g. receiving wages in accordance with applicable laws and regulations; and
h. children may perform work for talent and interest development.
(3) The provisions as referred to in paragraph (2) letter a, letter b, letter f and letter g are excluded for children working in family businesses.
(4) In the case that companies employ children as referred to in paragraph (1) and paragraph (2), children's workplaces must be separated from adult workers' workplaces.
(5) Children may perform work in workplaces that are part of the education or training curriculum validated by authorized officials.
(6) Children as referred to in paragraph (5) are at least 14 (fourteen) years old.
(7) Work as referred to in paragraph (6) may be carried out with the conditions:
a. given clear instructions on how to carry out work as well as guidance and supervision in carrying out work; and
b. given occupational safety and health protection.

Article 29
(1) Every person or entity is prohibited from employing and involving children in the worst forms of work.
(2) The worst forms of work as referred to in paragraph (1) are:
a. all work in the form of slavery or the like;
b. all work that exploits, provides or offers children for prostitution, pornography production, pornographic performances or gambling;
c. all work that exploits, provides, or involves children for production and trade of alcoholic beverages, narcotics, psychotropics and other addictive substances; and/or
d. all work that endangers children's health, safety, or morals.

Part Six
Social

Article 30
(1) The Regional Government conducts maintenance of neglected children.
(2) Maintenance as referred to in paragraph (1) may be carried out by Regional Apparatus implementing authority affairs in the social field.

Article 31
(1) In the case that a child is neglected for some reason their parents neglect their obligations, then the Regional Government, community organizations as referred to in Article 30 paragraph (2) may submit an application to the court to determine the child as a neglected child.
(2) The court determination as referred to in paragraph (1) simultaneously determines the place of shelter and maintenance.

Article 32
The Regional Government guarantees protection, maintenance, and welfare of children by taking into account the rights and obligations of parents, guardians, or other persons legally responsible for children.

Article 33
The Regional Government provides child service information centers as interactive communication facilities.

Article 34
(1) The Regional Government conducts skills training for street children and neglected children originating from the Region.
(3) Skills training as referred to in paragraph (1) is conducted by Regional Apparatus implementing authority affairs in the social field and may cooperate with community organizations.

Article 35
(1) The Regional Government provides shelters to foster street children and social institutions for neglected children.
(2) Shelters as referred to in paragraph (1) may be conducted by Regional Apparatus whose duties and functions are in the social and employment fields and may cooperate with community organizations.

Part Seven
Regional Children's Forum

Article 36
(1) The Regional Government facilitates the formation of Forda in implementing fulfillment of children's rights to express opinions and participate.
(2) Forda as referred to in paragraph (1) represents children, both representation of children's geographical domicile, components of children's socio-cultural groups and children's educational backgrounds.
(3) In every formulation of policies related to children, the Regional Government must consider and accommodate children's opinions submitted through Forda.
(4) Further provisions regarding the formation of Forda as referred to in paragraph (1), paragraph (2) and paragraph (3) are stipulated by Mayor Decree.

CHAPTER V
SPECIAL PROTECTION FOR CHILDREN

Part One
General

Article 37
The Regional Government is responsible for implementing special protection for children by involving the active role of society, ninik mamak, cadiak pandai, alim ulama, bundo kanduang and parents/family.

Part Two
Regional Government

Article 38
(1) The Regional Government in efforts for special protection of children is responsible for:
a. providing facilities and infrastructure in implementing special protection for children;
b. providing access to public services and social services for children with disabilities;
c. establishing and implementing policies and programs/activities for special protection of children;
d. cooperating with society and/or community organizations in implementing special protection for children in accordance with laws and regulations;
e. conducting rehabilitation and assistance for children who are victims of violence;
f. providing special protection to children in emergency situations, children in conflict with the law, child victims of violence and human trafficking, children from minority and isolated groups, children exploited economically and/or sexually, children who are victims of narcotics, alcohol, psychotropics abuse, and HIV/AIDS;
g. providing inclusive education for children with special needs; and
h. taking preventive actions in order to prevent violations of children's rights.
(2) The responsibility of the Regional Government in efforts for special protection of children as referred to in paragraph (1) is carried out by regional apparatus implementing authority affairs in the social field.

Part Three
Society

Article 39
(1) Society, ninik mamak, alim ulama, cadiak pandai, bundo kanduang in the context of special protection for children are responsible for:
a. providing support for the development of special protection programs for children;
b. playing an active role in responding to symptoms occurring in society that have the potential to violate children's rights;
c. conducting monitoring and supervision of the implementation of special protection for children; and
d. providing information and/or reporting if violence against children occurs to integrated service centers for women's empowerment and children or authorized parties.
(2) The responsibility of society, ninik mamak, alim ulama, cadiak pandai, bundo kanduang as referred to in paragraph (1) is based on laws and regulations and local wisdom values of the local community.

Part Three
Parents and Family

Article 40
(1) The responsibility of parents and family for special protection of children is as follows:
a. providing formal and non-formal education according to their talents and interests;
b. caring for, maintaining, educating and protecting children from acts of violence, exploitation, pornography, emergency situations and the possibility of children in conflict with the law; and
c. facilitating children with special needs and children in conflict with the law as well as children who are victims of violence, exploitation, pornography and emergency situations.
(2) In the case that parents do not exist, or their whereabouts are unknown, or for some reason cannot carry out their responsibilities, then the responsibility as referred to in paragraph (1) is transferred to guardians.

CHAPTER VI
HANDLING OF WOMEN AND CHILDREN VICTIMS OF VIOLENCE

Part One
General

Article 41
(1) Implementation of handling women and children victims of violence is carried out through integrated services.
(2) Integrated services for women and children victims of violence use the principles of:
a. gender responsive;
b. non-discrimination;
c. equal and respectful relationships;
d. maintaining confidentiality;
e. providing a sense of security and comfort;
f. respecting individual differences;
g. non-judgmental;
h. respecting victims' choices and decisions;
i. sensitive to victims' backgrounds and conditions and use of appropriate language understood by victims;
j. fast and simple;
k. empathy; and
l. fulfillment of women's and children's rights.

Part Two
Forms of Handling

Paragraph 1
General

Article 42
Implementation of handling women and children victims of violence includes services for:
a. complaint handling;
b. health services;
c. social rehabilitation;
d. law enforcement and legal aid; and/or
e. repatriation and social rehabilitation.

Paragraph 2
Complaint Handling

Article 43
(1) Complaint handling services for victims of violence against women and children as referred to in Article 42 letter a are carried out by:
a. integrated service center for women's empowerment and children;
b. hospitals;
c. community health centers;
d. child social protection houses/shelters;
e. marriage counseling, guidance and preservation advisory board;
f. state prosecutor's office;
g. district court;
h. child welfare institutions;
i. social welfare coordination institutions;
j. elderly welfare institutions;
k. family welfare consultation institutions; and
l. domestic violence response groups.
(2) Complaints of acts of violence against women and children may be made by reporters consisting of victims, families, society and/or community organizations in the field of protection of women and children.
(3) Reporters as referred to in paragraph (1) may make complaints:
a. directly;
b. by telephone; and/or
c. by letter.
(4) In addition to handling complaints submitted by reporters, officers at service institutions as referred to in paragraph (1), are also authorized to handle victims of violence against women and children obtained through other information.

Paragraph 3
Health Services

Article 44
(1) Health services for women and children victims of violence as referred to in Article 42 letter b are efforts that include curative and rehabilitative aspects.
(2) Health services for women and children victims of violence are conducted comprehensively, accessible 24 (twenty-four) hours, quality and conducted in accordance with health service standards.
(3) Health services specifically for child victims of violence, must prioritize the best interests of children.
(4) Women and children victims of violence have the right to health services, including:
a. medical services; and
b. psychosocial counseling.

Paragraph 4
Social Rehabilitation

Article 45
Social rehabilitation services for women and children victims of violence as referred to in Article 42 letter c are aimed at restoring and developing the abilities of victims experiencing social dysfunction to be able to carry out their social functions in society properly.

Article 46
Social rehabilitation services as referred to in Article 45 are as follows:
a. counseling services;
b. provision of safe houses; and
c. spiritual guidance.

Article 47
(1) Counseling services as referred to in Article 46 letter a aim to:
a. help women and children victims of violence recognize their problems and find effective ways to overcome them themselves;
b. provide moral support to women and children victims of violence in facing the process they are going through;
c. help alleviate, protect and restore the physical, psychological, social and spiritual conditions of victims of violence so that they are able to carry out their social functions normally again; and
d. make victims feel accepted in the community environment.
(2) Counseling services as referred to in paragraph (1) are carried out by gender-responsive social rehabilitation officers, consisting of:
a. counselors;
b. psychologists; and
c. psychiatrists.
(3) Counseling services as referred to in paragraph (1) are conducted in places that guarantee a sense of security, comfort, and confidentiality of information from victims.
(4) Stages of counseling services as referred to in paragraph (1) include:
a. initial counseling;
b. follow-up counseling;
c. case conference; and
d. home visit.
(5) Counseling organizers are conducted by:
a. regional apparatus having duties and functions in the social field;
b. regional apparatus in charge of women's empowerment, and may cooperate with integrated service centers for women's empowerment and children or existing social community organizations; and/or
c. religious institutions.

Article 48
(1) Provision of safe houses as referred to in Article 46 letter b may be provided by the Regional Government.
(2) Safe houses as referred to in paragraph (1) aim to protect women and children victims of violence from intimidation by perpetrators or other parties.
(3) The location of safe houses as referred to in paragraph (1) must be kept confidential.

Article 49
Criteria for women and children victims of violence who need safe houses include:
a. their lives are threatened;
b. receiving rejection from family or society;
c. requiring intensive services but their residence is relatively far; and/or
d. neglected if not placed in safe houses.

Article 50
Implementation of safe house services is carried out by social rehabilitation officers who have gender sensitivity or gender awareness consisting of:
a. social workers or volunteers;
b. security officers; and/or
c. community leaders.

Article 51
(1) Spiritual guidance as referred to in Article 46 letter c is aimed at psychological recovery for women and children victims of violence.
(2) Spiritual guidance as referred to in paragraph (1) is implemented based on the principles of:
a. not imposing any religion or belief; and
b. leaving the final decision in the hands of victims.
(3) Spiritual guidance is carried out by spiritual guidance officers, consisting of advisory board consultants, marriage guidance and preservation boards, religious affairs office officers, ustads or ulamas and/or other religious leaders.

Paragraph 5
Law Enforcement and Legal Aid

Article 52
(1) Law enforcement and legal aid services are actions by law enforcement officials to implement laws and regulations in handling cases of violence against women and children, especially to provide sanctions to perpetrators and provide protection for witnesses and/or victims.
(2) Law enforcement and legal aid services as referred to in paragraph (1) are carried out by prioritizing the best interests of victims.
(3) Law enforcement and legal aid services are aimed at providing assistance and protection to women and children victims of violence and witnesses with a gender-responsive approach so that victims obtain justice and certainty of their legal rights.
(4) Types of legal aid services include:
a. legal aid in the reporting process and preparation of examination reports;
b. legal aid in the prosecution process at the prosecutor's office;
c. legal aid in the examination process at court;
d. legal aid in the court trial process until there is a final and binding decision; and
e. legal aid in the mediation process.

Article 53
(1) The Regional Government provides legal aid to women and children victims of violence free of charge.
(2) The Regional Government in providing legal aid may cooperate with legal aid institutions, advocates and/or other legal assistants.

Part Seven
Repatriation and Social Rehabilitation

Article 54
(1) Repatriation of victims of violence is aimed at returning women and children victims of violence to their area of origin.
(2) Repatriation of victims of violence against women and children is accompanied by social rehabilitation efforts.
(3) The Regional Government implements repatriation and social rehabilitation of women and children victims of violence in accordance with laws and regulations.

CHAPTER VII
ESTABLISHMENT OF UPTD PPA

Article 55
(1) The Regional Government in the context of administering women's empowerment and child protection affairs may establish UPTD PPA.
(2) UPTD PPA functions to provide services to women and children experiencing criminal and civil problems.
(3) The scope of UPTD PPA services includes complaint services, consultation, mediation, outreach, health, social rehabilitation, spiritual guidance, assistance, law enforcement and legal aid, and repatriation and social rehabilitation services.
(4) In carrying out its functions as referred to in paragraph (2) UPTD PPA is obliged to:
a. provide services and handling as quickly as possible to women and children experiencing problems;
b. provide convenience, comfort, safety and free of charge to women and children experiencing problems;
c. maintain confidentiality of women and children experiencing problems; and
d. guarantee justice and legal certainty for women and children experiencing problems.
(5) Further provisions regarding the organization and work procedures of UPTD PPA as referred to in paragraph (1) are regulated by Mayor Regulation.

CHAPTER VIII
GUIDANCE AND SUPERVISION

Article 56
(1) The Regional Government conducts guidance on the implementation of protection for women and children.
(2) Guidance as referred to in paragraph (1) may be carried out in the form of:
a. conducting socialization of programs and activities for protection of women and children; and
b. providing training for government officials, law enforcers, religious leaders, community leaders, traditional leaders, educators, journalists, and media managers regarding prevention and handling of violence against women and children.

Article 57
(1) The Regional Government conducts supervision of the implementation of protection for women and children.
(2) Supervision as referred to in paragraph (1) is carried out 1 (one) time in 1 (one) year by Regional Apparatus whose duties and functions are in the supervision field.
(3) The results of supervision as referred to in paragraph (1) are evaluation materials for policies on protection of women and children in the Region.

CHAPTER IX
COMMUNITY PARTICIPATION

Article 58
(1) Society may participate in the implementation of protection for women and children.
(2) Society as referred to in paragraph (1) among others consists of parents, families, ninik mamak, cadiak pandai, alim ulama, bundo kanduang, traditional leaders, religious leaders, mass media, and business world.
(3) Community participation is carried out through ways of:
a. optimizing the function of community organizations in the Region;
b. conducting socialization and education of laws and regulations related to protection of women and children;
c. providing input in the process of formulating laws and regulations in the Region;
d. conducting socialization on prevention of child marriage;
e. conducting recovery and reintegration of women victims of violence;
f. providing information and/or reporting the occurrence of violence, discrimination, exploitation, and human rights violations against women and children to authorized parties;
g. supervising the process of handling cases of violence, discrimination, exploitation, and human rights violations against women and children;
h. providing space to gather and organize for children to express themselves, create and express their opinions;
i. supervising children in accessing various information;
j. providing safe products for children;
k. contributing to the fulfillment of children's rights through corporate social responsibility; and/or
l. disseminating information and educational materials that are beneficial for women and children.

CHAPTER X
FINANCING

Article 59
Financing for the implementation of policies, programs and activities for protection of women and child protection is sourced from:
a. Regional Revenue and Expenditure Budget; and
b. other legitimate sources of income in accordance with the provisions of laws and regulations.

CHAPTER X
CLOSING PROVISIONS

Article 60
Implementing regulations of this Regional Regulation are stipulated at the latest 1 (one) year from the promulgation of this Regional Regulation.

Article 61
This Regional Regulation comes into force on the date of promulgation.

I. GENERAL

The 1945 Constitution of the Republic of Indonesia mandates Regional Governments to regulate and manage their own regions for the realization of community welfare through improved services, empowerment and community participation. One of the authorities of regional governments is to make regional policies with the aim of improving the welfare of the people in their regions.

In addition, the 1945 Constitution has also mandated in Article 28D paragraph (1) that every person has the right to recognition, guarantees, protection and fair legal certainty as well as equal treatment before the law. Article 28B Paragraph 2 also states that every Child has the right to survival, growth and development and has the right to protection from violence and discrimination. This shows that girls should be protected by the state, especially against violence because their rights are already regulated in the constitution.

Therefore, it is necessary to make efforts to prevent and eradicate all forms of discrimination and violence against women and children, especially for the Solok City region. Solok City is one of the parties most authorized in empowering women and protecting children by conducting and formulating regulations to provide guarantees for the implementation of protection for women and children.

One form of such regulation is the Regional Regulation of Solok City. The regulation is expected to be able to encourage efforts to improve the physical and mental conditions of women and children victims of violence.
