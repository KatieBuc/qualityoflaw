REGIONAL REGULATION OF OGAN KOMERING ULU SELATAN REGENCY NUMBER 7 OF 2020
CONCERNING
PROTECTION OF WOMEN AND CHILDREN
BY THE GRACE OF ALMIGHTY GOD
THE REGENT OF OGAN KOMERING ULU SELATAN,

Considering: a. that the government has an obligation to provide protection, advancement, enforcement and fulfillment of Human Rights of every citizen including the Rights of Women and Children;

b. that Women and Children are vulnerable groups who tend to experience violence and discrimination and therefore need to receive optimal protection;

c. that the fulfillment of the constitutional rights of Women and Children as well as improving the quality of life of Women and Children, is one of the mandatory affairs that is the duty, authority and responsibility of the Regency Government;

d. that based on the considerations as referred to in letter a, letter b and letter c, it is necessary to establish a Regional Regulation on the Protection of Women and Children;

In View of: 1. Article 18 paragraph (6) of the 1945 Constitution of the Republic of Indonesia;

CHAPTER I
GENERAL PROVISIONS

Part One
Definitions, Meanings and Terms

Article 1

In this Regional Regulation what is meant by:

1. Regency is Ogan Komering Ulu Selatan Regency.

2. Regency Government is the Government of Ogan Komering Ulu Selatan Regency.

3. Regent is the Regent of Ogan Komering Ulu Selatan.

4. Regional Apparatus hereinafter abbreviated as PD is the Regional Apparatus within the Government of Ogan Komering Ulu Selatan Regency.

- 4 -

5. Office of Population Control, Family Planning, Women's Empowerment and Child Protection hereinafter abbreviated as DPPKBPP-PA is the Regional Apparatus that administers the affairs of Women's Empowerment and Child Protection in Ogan Komering Ulu Selatan.

6. Protection of Women is all efforts to guarantee and protect the rights of Women so that they can live healthily and participate optimally in accordance with human dignity and receive protection from neglect, violence and discrimination, both in the form of preventive, repressive and postremedial protection (assistance and treatment).

7. Child Protection is all activities to guarantee and protect Children and their rights so that they can live, grow, develop, and participate, optimally in accordance with human dignity, and receive protection from violence and discrimination.

8. Child is someone who is not yet 18 (eighteen) years old, including a Child who is still in the womb.

9. Child with Disability is a Child who has physical, mental, intellectual, or sensory limitations for a long period of time who in interacting with the environment and community attitudes may encounter obstacles that make it difficult to participate fully and effectively based on equal rights.

10. Child with Excellence is a Child who has extraordinary intelligence or has special potential and/or talent not limited to intellectual ability, but also in other fields.

11. Prevention is a direct effort carried out by the Regency Government, community and parents and/or family to prevent violations of the rights of Women and the Rights of Children.

12. Children's Forum is a forum for Child participation formed with the aim of facilitating the involvement of Children in the decision-making process and enjoying changes relating to their lives both directly and indirectly, which is carried out with the approval and will of all Children based on awareness and understanding, in accordance with age and level of thinking maturity.

13. Child-Friendly Regency hereinafter abbreviated as KLA is a Regency that has a child rights-based development system through

- 5 -

the integration of government commitment and resources, community and business world that is planned comprehensively and sustainably in policies, programs and activities to ensure the fulfillment of Children's rights.

14. Neglected Child is a Child whose needs are not met properly, whether physical, mental, spiritual, or social.

15. Women victims of violence are Women who experience actions that result in misery or suffering physically, sexually or psychologically, including threats of certain actions, coercion or arbitrary deprivation of freedom, whether occurring in the public sphere or in private life.

16. Child victims of violence are Children who experience actions that result in misery or suffering physically, mentally, sexually, psychologically or neglect that threatens life, body and degrades human dignity.

17. Trafficking in persons is the act of recruitment, transportation, harboring, sending, transfer, or receipt of a person by threat of violence, use of violence, kidnapping, confinement, forgery, fraud, abuse of power or vulnerable position, debt bondage or giving payment or benefits, so as to obtain consent from the person who holds control over that other person, whether carried out within the Country or between Countries, for the purpose of exploitation or resulting in the person being exploited.

18. Complaint handling is a series of actions carried out by integrated service providers to follow up on reports of acts of violence against Women and Children, submitted by victims, families or the community.

19. Social rehabilitation is a service aimed at restoring and developing the ability of a person experiencing social dysfunction to be able to carry out social functions properly.

20. Law enforcement is the action of officers authorized by the state to implement laws and regulations.

21. Legal aid is legal services provided by legal assistants and advocates to carry out the process of assisting witnesses and/or victims of violence against Women and Children that is gender sensitive.

22. Repatriation is an effort to return Women and Child victims of violence from abroad to the debarkation/entry point, or from the receiving area to the area of origin.

- 6 -

23. Social reintegration is an effort to reunite victims with family, substitute family, or community that can provide protection and fulfillment of needs for victims.

24. Safe house is a temporary shelter that aims to provide protection and a sense of security to victims of violence according to established standards.

25. Shelter is a forum for guidance and welfare services for street children that carries out activities of social assistance/guidance, maintenance, care, education, and health, skills guidance in order to ensure that Children do not carry out activities on the streets so that they can grow and develop normally.

26. Spiritual guidance is an effort to restore the soul through strengthening the religion adhered to by the victim.

27. Gender responsive is consistent and systematic attention to the differences between Women and men in society accompanied by efforts to eliminate structural and cultural barriers in achieving gender equality.

28. Gender Sensitivity or Gender Awareness is the ability to recognize the gap in power relations between Women and men in the family and in the community, the impact of gender-based division of labor on Women and men, and the experiences, problems, needs, interests and aspirations of Women and men are different.

29. Public service provider is every state organizing institution, corporation, independent institution formed based on Law for public service activities, and other legal entities formed solely for public service activities.

30. Community Organization is an organization established and formed by the community voluntarily based on common aspirations, will, needs, interests, activities and goals to participate in development for the achievement of the goals of the Unitary State of the Republic of Indonesia based on Pancasila.

31. Parents are biological father and/or mother, or stepfather and/or stepmother, or adoptive father and/or mother.

32. Family is the smallest unit in society consisting of husband and wife or husband, wife and their Child, or father and Child, or mother and

- 7 -

Child, or blood family in a straight line up or down to the third degree.

33. Integrated Service Center for Women's Empowerment and Children, hereinafter abbreviated as P2TP2A is an integrated service center in efforts to empower Women in various fields of development, as well as protection of Women and Children from various types of discrimination and acts of violence, including trafficking in persons, established by the government or community-based.

34. Human Immunodeficiency Virus hereinafter abbreviated as HIV is a virus that attacks the immune system and if not treated can reduce human immunity to the point of Acquired Immuno Deficiency Syndrome (AIDS).

Part Two
Principles, Objectives and Scope

Article 2

(1) The implementation of Protection of Women is based on the principles of:

a. the best interests of Women;

b. gender justice and gender equality;

c. local wisdom; and

d. legal certainty.

(2) The implementation of Child Protection is based on the principles of:

a. non-discrimination;

b. the best interests of the Child;

c. the right to life, survival, and development;

d. respect for the Child's opinion; and

e. legal certainty.

Article 3

The regulation of Protection of Women and Children aims to:

a. serve as a guideline for the Regency Government in planning, policies and strategies for Protection of Women and Children;

b. protect Women and Children from actions, decisions and policies that violate the rights of Women and Children;

c. prevent violations of the Rights of Women and Children;

d. increase empowerment of Women and Children;

e. increase the role and value of local wisdom in the Protection of Women and Children; and

f. increase the role of Regional Apparatus and non-

- 8 -

Government in the Regency in efforts to Protect Women and Children.

Article 4

The scope of regulation of Protection of Women and Children includes:

a. prevention;

b. implementation of Protection of the Rights of Women and Children;

c. handling;

d. empowerment; and

e. guidance and supervision.

CHAPTER II
RIGHTS OF WOMEN AND RIGHTS OF CHILDREN

Part One
Rights of Women

Article 5

Every Woman has the right to:

a. obtain protection from acts of discrimination;

b. obtain protection from economic exploitation and sexual exploitation;

c. obtain protection from physical, psychological, sexual violence, and neglect;

d. obtain decent work according to her ability;

e. obtain work leave while receiving wages/salary in accordance with laws and regulations;

f. obtain education and teaching in all types, levels and paths of education;

g. obtain special protection in carrying out work or profession against things that can threaten safety and/or health related to Women's reproductive function;

h. have the right to choose, be elected, be appointed in work, position, and profession in accordance with the requirements and laws and regulations; and

i. ownership and management of joint assets in marriage in accordance with the provisions of Laws and Regulations.

- 9 -

Part Two
Rights of Children

Article 6

(1) Every Child has the right to:

a. every child has the right to protection by parents, family, community, and the state;

b. children's rights are human rights and for their interests children's rights are recognized and protected by law even from the womb;

c. every child from the womb, has the right to live, maintain life, and improve their standard of living;

d. every child from birth, has the right to a name and citizenship status;

e. every child who is physically and/or mentally disabled has the right to receive care, education, training, and special assistance at state expense, to ensure their life in accordance with human dignity, increase self-confidence, and the ability to participate in community, national, and state life;

f. every child has the right to worship according to their religion, think, express themselves according to their level of intellectuality and age under the guidance of parents and/or guardians;

g. every child has the right to know who their parents are, be raised, and cared for by their own parents;

h. in the event that the child's parents are unable to raise and care for their child properly, then the child may be cared for or adopted as a child by another person in accordance with the provisions of laws and regulations;

i. every child has the right to be raised, cared for, nurtured, educated, directed, and guided in their life by parents or guardians until adulthood in accordance with the provisions of laws and regulations;

j. every child has the right to obtain adoptive parents or a guardian based on a court decision if both parents have died or for a valid reason cannot carry out their obligations as parents;

k. adoptive parents or guardians as referred to in letter j must carry out obligations as actual parents;

- 10 -

l. every child has the right to obtain legal protection from all forms of physical or mental violence, neglect, bad treatment, and sexual abuse while in the care of parents or guardians, or other parties responsible for the care of the child;

m. in the event that parents, guardians, or child caregivers commit all forms of physical or mental persecution, neglect, bad treatment, and sexual abuse including rape, and/or murder of children who should be protected, then they must be subject to aggravated punishment;

n. every child has the right not to be separated from their parents against the child's own will, except if there are reasons and valid legal rules that show that the separation is in the best interests of the child;

o. in the circumstances as referred to in letter n, the child's right to continue to meet directly and have personal relations regularly with their parents is still guaranteed by Law;

p. every child has the right to obtain education and teaching in the context of developing their personality according to interests, talents, and level of intelligence;

q. every child has the right to seek, receive, and provide information according to their level of intellectuality and age for their own development as long as it is in accordance with the values of decency and propriety;

r. every child has the right to rest, associate with children of the same age, play, recreate, and create according to interests, talents, and level of intelligence for their own development;

s. every child has the right to obtain health services and social security appropriately, according to their physical and spiritual mental needs;

t. every child has the right not to be involved in war events, armed conflicts, social unrest, and other events containing elements of violence;

u. every child has the right to obtain protection from economic exploitation activities and any work that endangers themselves, so that it can interfere with education, physical health, morals, social life, and spiritual mentality;

- 11 -

v. every child has the right to obtain protection from sexual exploitation and abuse activities, kidnapping, child trafficking, as well as from various forms of abuse of narcotics, psychotropics, and other addictive substances;

w. every child has the right not to be targeted for persecution, torture, or imposition of inhuman punishment;

x. every child has the right not to be deprived of freedom unlawfully;

y. arrest, detention, or imprisonment of children may only be carried out in accordance with applicable law and can only be implemented as a last resort;

z. every child who is deprived of freedom has the right to be treated humanely and with attention to the needs of personal development according to their age and must be separated from adults, except in their interests;

aa. every child who is deprived of freedom has the right to obtain legal assistance or other assistance effectively at every stage of applicable legal efforts;

bb. every child who is deprived of freedom has the right to defend themselves and obtain justice before an objective and impartial Juvenile Court in a closed session for the public.

(2) Every child who becomes a victim or perpetrator of sexual violence or who is in conflict with the law has the right to be kept confidential.

(3) Every Child with Disability in addition to obtaining the rights as referred to in paragraph (1) is also entitled to obtain:

a. special education/special education;

b. rehabilitation;

c. social assistance; and

d. maintenance of social welfare standards.

(4) Every Child who has excellence has the right to receive special education.

CHAPTER III
OBLIGATIONS AND RESPONSIBILITIES

Part One
Regency Government

- 12 -

Article 7

(1) The Regency Government in efforts to protect Women is obliged and responsible to:

a. guarantee the Rights of Women without discrimination in accordance with the provisions of Laws and Regulations;

b. establish and implement policies, programs and activities for the protection of Women;

c. cooperate with the community and/or Community Organizations in the implementation of Protection of Women in accordance with the provisions of Laws and Regulations;

d. carry out rehabilitation and empowerment of Women who are victims of acts of violence;

e. provide special protection to Women victims of acts of violence and trafficking in persons; and

f. take preventive actions in order to prevent violations of women's rights.

(2) The responsibility of the Regency Government in efforts to protect the Rights of Women as referred to in paragraph (1) is carried out by DPPKBP-PPPA.

Article 8

(1) The Regency Government in efforts to protect Children is obliged and responsible to:

a. respect the fulfillment of Children's rights without distinguishing ethnicity, religion, race, class, gender, ethnicity, culture and language, legal status, birth order, and physical and/or mental condition;

b. provide support for facilities, infrastructure, and availability of human resources in the implementation of Child Protection;

c. guarantee the protection, maintenance, and welfare of Children taking into account the rights and obligations of Parents, Guardians, or other persons who are legally responsible for the Child;

d. guarantee Children to convey their right to express opinions according to the age and level of intelligence of the Child;

e. establish and implement policies and programs/activities for the protection of Children's rights;

f. cooperate with the community and/or Community Organizations in the implementation of Child protection in accordance with the provisions of Laws and Regulations;

g. carry out rehabilitation of Children who are victims of acts of violence;

- 13 -

h. provide special protection to Children:

1. in emergency situations;

2. in conflict with the law;

3. from minority and isolated groups;

4. who are exploited economically and/or sexually;

5. who are victims of abuse of narcotics, alcohol, psychotropics, and other addictive substances;

6. who are victims of pornography;

7. with HIV/AIDS;

8. victims of kidnapping, sale, and/or trafficking;

9. victims of physical and/or psychological violence;

10. victims of sexual crimes;

11. victims of terrorism networks;

12. with disabilities;

13. victims of mistreatment and neglect;

14. with deviant social behavior; and

15. who are victims of stigmatization from labeling related to their parents' condition.

i. organize inclusive education for Children with special needs;

j. take preventive actions in order to prevent violations against Children; and

k. implement and support National policies in the implementation of Child Protection in the Regency carried out through efforts to realize a Child-Friendly Regency, Child-Friendly Sub-district and Child-Friendly Village.

(2) The responsibility of the Regency Government in efforts to protect Children as referred to in paragraph (1) is carried out by DPPKBP-PPPA.

Article 9

(1) The Regency Government in order to carry out its responsibilities, establishes programs and activities for the protection of Women and Children with a Regional Action Plan as the basis for Regional Apparatus in implementing the protection of Women and Children.

(2) Further provisions regarding the Regional Action Plan as referred to in paragraph (1) are regulated by Regent Regulation.

- 14 -

Part Two
Community

Article 10

(1) The community is obliged and responsible in implementing the protection of Women and Children including:

a. participation in prevention and providing support for the development of prevention programs for violations of the rights of Women and Children;

b. providing information and/or reporting violations of the rights of Women and Children to the authorities; and

c. supervising the law enforcement process against cases of violations of the rights of Women and Children.

(2) Community responsibility as referred to in paragraph (1) is in accordance with the provisions of laws and regulations and the values of local wisdom of the local community.

Part Three
Parents and/or Family

Paragraph 1
Responsibility for Protection of Women's Rights

Article 11

Family responsibility in efforts to protect women includes:

a. protecting women from conflict and domestic violence that degrades the dignity of women;

b. providing equal opportunities for women to develop themselves in the fields of:

1. education;

2. social;

3. economic; and

4. politics and culture.

c. preventing violations of women's rights in the household.

Paragraph 2
Responsibility for Protection of Children's Rights

Article 12

(1) Parents in efforts to protect Children are responsible for:

a. caring for, maintaining, educating, and protecting Children;

- 15 -

b. developing Children according to their abilities, talents, and interests;

c. providing formal and non-formal education;

d. guiding Children to behave in accordance with religion, ethics and morals as well as customs; and

e. preventing marriage at Child age.

(2) In the event that parents do not exist, or their whereabouts are unknown, or for some reason, cannot carry out their obligations and responsibilities, then the obligations and responsibilities as referred to in paragraph (1) may transfer to the family.

CHAPTER IV
PREVENTION

Part One
Regency Government

Article 13

(1) Efforts made by the Regency Government in order to prevent violations of women's rights include:

a. organizing socialization regarding prevention of violence against Women by optimizing the role of customary, cultural, religious institutions and community organizations;

b. providing guidelines on handling acts of violence against Women;

c. involving community participation in recovery and reintegration;

d. developing databases and integrated information systems on acts of violence against Women;

e. developing family empowerment programs;

f. strengthening the function of community organizations engaged in Women's empowerment; and

g. providing comfortable and safe public facilities for Women.

(2) Efforts made by the Regency Government in order to prevent violations of Children's rights include:

a. preventing acts of violence against Children by optimizing the role of customary, cultural, religious institutions and community organizations;

b. developing child-friendly schools;

c. encouraging increased child participation through the formation of Children's Forums;

d. providing guidelines on handling acts of violence against Children;

- 16 -

e. involving community participation in recovery and reintegration services for child victims of violence;

f. developing databases and integrated information systems on acts of violence against Children;

g. strengthening the function of community organizations engaged in Child protection; and

h. encouraging the formation of Child-Friendly Regencies.

Part Two
Community

Article 14

(1) Efforts made by the community in order to prevent violations of women's rights include:

a. monitoring various conditions occurring in their environment that can cause violations of women's rights;

b. strengthening the role of community organizations, religious organizations and family environment; and

c. preventing unregistered marriage/siri marriage.

(2) Efforts made in order to prevent violations of Children's rights include:

a. monitoring various conditions occurring in their environment that cause violations of Children's rights;

b. strengthening the role of community organizations, religious organizations and family environment; and

c. preventing marriage at Child age.

Part Three
Parents and/or Family

Article 15

(1) Efforts made by the family in order to prevent violations of women's rights include:

a. protection of women from various violations of rights in the household; and

b. encouraging the formation of harmonious, educative and democratic families;

(2) Efforts made by parents in order to prevent violations of Children's rights include:

a. providing good examples to children according to religious teachings and customs;

b. supervising children in activities inside

- 17 -

or outside the home;

c. providing opportunities for children to dialogue and express opinions democratically; and

d. protecting children from various acts of violation of Children's rights in the household.

CHAPTER V
IMPLEMENTATION OF PROTECTION
OF WOMEN AND CHILDREN

Part One
Implementation of Protection of Women

Paragraph 1
General

Article 16

The Regency Government implements Protection of Women by involving:

a. family;

b. community leaders;

c. religious leaders;

d. teachers and/or academics;

e. community; and

f. community organizations in the field of protection of Women.

Paragraph 2
Marriage

Article 17

During marriage or after divorce, a Woman has the same rights as the man who is her husband/ex-husband over all matters relating to the consequences of marriage and/or divorce.

Paragraph 3
Health

Article 18

(1) The Regency Government provides adequate health and maternity facilities for pregnant and nursing mothers.

(2) Public service providers must provide special facilities for nursing mothers.

- 18 -

Paragraph 4
Employment

Article 19

(1) Employers who employ Women must maintain decency, safety and security while at work.

(2) Employers must place female workers in places that are safe for Women's reproductive health.

(3) Employers must fulfill the rights of female workers to receive wages in accordance with the provisions of laws and regulations.

(4) Employers provide special facilities for nursing mothers at their companies.

Article 20

(1) Female workers/laborers who are at most 18 (eighteen) years old are prohibited from being employed between 23.00 WIB to 07.00 WIB.

(2) Employers are prohibited from employing pregnant female workers/laborers who according to a doctor's statement are dangerous to the health and safety of their pregnancy or themselves during working hours from 23.00 WIB to 07.00 WIB.

(3) Employers who employ female workers/laborers between 23.00 WIB to 07.00 WIB, must:

a. provide nutritious food and drinks; and

b. maintain decency and security while at work.

(4) Employers must provide shuttle transportation for female workers/laborers who leave and return from work between 23.00 WIB to 05.00 WIB.

Paragraph 5
Education

Article 21

Women are guaranteed the right to obtain education at every level of formal and non-formal education.

- 19 -

Paragraph 6
Social

Article 22

(1) The Regency Government organizes empowerment of Women experiencing socio-economic vulnerability through productive economic business activities.

(2) Community Organizations may carry out empowerment for Women experiencing socio-economic vulnerability as referred to in paragraph (1) in accordance with the provisions of Laws and Regulations.

Part Two
Implementation of Protection of Children's Rights

Paragraph 1
Health

Article 23

(1) The Regency Government's efforts to provide Child protection in the health sector include:

a. free health services for Children from poor families;

b. organizing optimal health for Children to obtain good health status from the womb;

c. ensuring that babies receive exclusive breastfeeding from their mothers properly and naturally;

d. ensuring Children receive adequate nutrition;

e. ensuring newborn Children undergo screening; and

f. ensuring the provision of growth and development monitoring for toddlers and pre-school age children.

(2) The Regency Government, family, and parents protect children from attempts to transplant their body organs to other parties.

Paragraph 2
Education

Article 24

Children in formal and non-formal educational institution environments must be protected from acts of violence.

- 20 -

Article 25

Every Child aged 7 (seven) to 18 (eighteen) years who has not completed education, exceeding 3 (three) years beyond the formal education age limit may pursue education through non-formal education units:

a. Package A equivalency program equivalent to Elementary School and Madrasah Ibtidaiyah;

b. Package B equivalency program equivalent to Junior High School and Madrasah Tsanawiyah; and

c. Package C equivalency program equivalent to Senior High School, Madrasah Aliyah and Vocational High School.

Article 26

(1) Children with special needs and/or who have potential intelligence and/or special talents are given equal opportunities and facilities to obtain regular education or inclusive education.

(2) Inclusive education as referred to in paragraph (1) is organized by the Regency Government and the community.

Article 27

Every organizer of formal education is prohibited from expelling Children without guaranteeing the continuity of the Child's education.

Article 28

(1) The Regency Government provides scholarships and/or educational assistance for:

a. children from poor families;

b. neglected children; and

c. children living in remote areas.

(2) The implementation of scholarship programs and/or educational assistance as referred to in paragraph (1) is supported by community participation and community organizations.

(3) Further provisions regarding the provision of scholarships and or educational assistance as referred to in paragraph (1) are regulated by Regent Regulation.

Article 29

Children in conflict with the law, Child victims of violence and sexual abuse and Child victims of HIV/AIDS transmission are protected in their rights to obtain education.

- 21 -

Article 30

The Regency Government and the community organize Early Childhood Education (PAUD).

Paragraph 3
Employment

Article 31

Employers are prohibited from employing Children.

Article 32

(1) The provisions as referred to in Article 31 may be exempted for Children aged between 13 (thirteen) years to 15 (fifteen) years, to perform light work as long as it does not interfere with their physical, mental, and social development and health.

(2) Employers who employ Children in light work as referred to in paragraph (1) must meet the requirements including:

a. written permission from parents or guardians;

b. work agreement between employer and parents or guardian;

c. maximum working time of 3 (three) hours;

d. carried out during the day and does not interfere with school time;

e. occupational safety and health;

f. clear employment relationship; and

g. receive wages in accordance with the provisions of laws and regulations.

(3) The provisions as referred to in paragraph (2) letters a, b, f, and g are exempted for Children working in family businesses.

Article 33

In the event that a company employs Children as referred to in Article 32, then the Children's workplace must be separated from the workplace of adult workers.

Article 34

(1) Children may perform work at a workplace that is part of an educational or training curriculum validated by the authorized official.

(2) Children as referred to in paragraph (1) are at least 14 (fourteen) years old.

- 22 -

Article 35

(1) Everyone is prohibited from employing and involving Children in the worst forms of work.

(2) The worst forms of work as referred to in paragraph (1) include:

a. all work in the form of slavery or the like;

b. all work that utilizes, provides, or offers Children for:

1. prostitution;

2. pornography production;

3. pornographic acts;

4. gambling;

5. production and trade of alcoholic beverages;

6. narcotics;

7. psychotropics; and

8. other addictive substances; and/or

c. all work that endangers the health, safety, or morals of Children.

Paragraph 4
Social

Article 36

(1) The Regency Government is obliged to organize care for neglected Children.

(2) Care as referred to in paragraph (1) may be carried out by Community Organizations in the social field.

(3) In the event that a Child is neglected because for some reason the parents neglect their obligations, then the Family or Regency Government or Community Organization as referred to in paragraph (2) may submit an application to the court to determine the Child as a neglected Child.

(4) The court determination as referred to in paragraph (3) simultaneously determines the shelter and care for the neglected Child concerned.

Article 37

(1) The Regency Government organizes skills training for street Children and neglected Children.

(2) Skills training as referred to in paragraph (1) is organized by Regional Apparatus whose duties and functions are in the social and employment fields and may cooperate with Community Organizations.

- 23 -

Article 38

(1) The Regency Government provides shelters to guide street Children and social institutions for neglected Children.

(2) Shelters as referred to in paragraph (1) may be organized in cooperation with Community Organizations.

(3) Further provisions regarding shelters as referred to in paragraph (1) are regulated by Regent Regulation.

Article 39

The implementation of protection of Women and Children is carried out by Regional Apparatus whose duties and functions are related to the protection of Women and Children.

CHAPTER VI
HANDLING

Part One
Handling of Women and Child Victims of Violence

Article 40

The implementation of handling Women and Child victims of violence includes:

a. handling complaints from victims of violence;

b. health services;

c. social rehabilitation;

d. law enforcement and legal assistance; and

e. repatriation and social reintegration.

Article 41

(1) The implementation of handling Women and Child victims of violence as referred to in Article 40 is carried out through integrated services.

(2) Integrated services for Women and Child victims of violence as referred to in paragraph (1) use the principles of:

a. gender responsive;

b. non-discrimination;

c. equal and respectful relationships;

d. maintaining confidentiality;

e. providing a sense of security and comfort;

f. respecting individual differences;

g. non-judgmental;

h. respecting the choices and decisions of victims;

i. sensitive to the background and condition of victims and use of appropriate language understood

- 24 -

by victims;

j. fast and simple;

k. empathy;

l. fulfillment of the rights of Women and Children; and

m. free of charge.

Article 42

Services for handling complaints from victims of violence against Women and Children are carried out by:

a. hospitals;

b. community health centers;

c. P2TP2A;

d. Women and Children Service Unit (UPPA);

e. Trauma Center Protection House (RPTC);

f. Child Social Protection House (RPSA);

g. Women Crisis Centre (WCC)

h. Advisory Board for Marriage Guidance and Preservation (BP4);

i. Police;

j. Prosecutor's Office;

k. Court;

l. Legal Aid Institute (LBH);

m. Child Protection Institute (LPA);

n. Social Welfare Protection Institute; and

o. Shelter.

Article 43

(1) Complaints of acts of violence against Women and Children as referred to in Article 42 may be made by reporters consisting of:

a. Victims;

b. Family;

c. Community; and/or

d. community organizations in the field of protection of Women and Children.

(2) Reporters as referred to in paragraph (1) may make complaints:

a. directly;

b. by telephone; and

c. by letter.

(3) In addition to handling complaints submitted by reporters, officers at service institutions as referred to in Article 42 are authorized to handle victims of violence against Women and Children obtained through other information.

(4) Further provisions regarding the procedures for complaint handling services as referred to in paragraphs (1) and (2) are regulated by Regional Regulation.

- 25 -

Part Two
Health Services for Women and Child Victims of Violence

Article 44

(1) Health services for Women and Child victims of violence include aspects of:

a. Promotive;

b. Preventive;

c. Curative;

d. rehabilitative.

(2) Health services for Women and Child victims of violence are carried out:

a. Comprehensively;

b. accessible 24 (twenty-four) hours;

c. quality; and

d. in accordance with health service standards.

(3) Special health services for Child victims of violence, must prioritize the best interests of the Child.

(4) women and Child victims of violence are entitled to health services, including:

a. medical services;

b. medicolegal services; and

c. psychosocial counseling.

(5) Further provisions regarding the procedures for implementing health services as referred to in paragraph (4) are regulated by Regional Regulation.

Part Three
Social Rehabilitation Services for Women and Child Victims of Violence

Article 45

(1) Social rehabilitation services for Women and Child victims of violence are aimed at restoring and developing the ability of victims experiencing social dysfunction to be able to carry out social functions in society properly.

(2) Social rehabilitation services as referred to in paragraph (1) include:

a. counseling services;

b. provision of safe houses;

c. spiritual guidance.

(3) The implementation of services as referred to in paragraph (2) is organized by:

a. Regional Apparatus that has duties and functions in the social field;

- 26 -

b. DPPKBPP-PA;

c. P2TP2A;

d. Women Crisis Centre (WCC);

e. community organizations in the social field; and/or

f. religious institutions.

Article 46

(1) Counseling services as referred to in Article 45 paragraph (2) letter a aim to:

a. help victims recognize their problems and find effective ways to overcome them themselves;

b. empower victims to determine their future;

c. provide moral support for victims in facing the process they are going through; and

d. make victims feel accepted in the community environment.

(2) Counseling services are carried out by gender-responsive social rehabilitation officers, consisting of:

a. counselors;

b. psychologists; and

c. psychiatrists.

(3) Counseling services are carried out in places that guarantee a sense of security, comfort and confidentiality of information from victims.

(4) Stages of counseling services include:

a. initial counseling;

b. follow-up counseling;

c. case conference; and

d. home visit.

Article 47

(1) The Regency Government may provide Safe houses for Women and Child victims of violence.

(2) Safe houses as referred to in paragraph (1) aim to protect Women and Child victims of violence from intimidation by perpetrators or other parties.

(3) The location of Safe houses as referred to in paragraph (1) must be kept confidential.

(4) Criteria for Women and Child victims of violence who need Safe houses include:

a. their lives are threatened;

b. receiving rejection from family or community;

c. requiring intensive services but their residence is relatively far; and/or

- 27 -

d. neglected if not placed in a safe house.

(5) The implementation of Safe house services is carried out by social rehabilitation officers who have gender sensitivity or gender awareness, consisting of:

a. social workers/volunteers;

b. security officers; and

c. community leaders.

(6) Further provisions regarding the implementation of Safe house services are regulated by Regional Regulation.

Article 48

(1) Every Woman and Child victim of violence is given spiritual guidance aimed at psychological recovery.

(2) Spiritual guidance as referred to in paragraph (1) is carried out based on the principles of:

a. not imposing any religion or belief; and

b. leaving the final decision in the hands of the victim (self determination).

(3) Spiritual guidance as referred to in paragraph (2) is carried out by spiritual guidance officers.

(4) Spiritual guidance officers as referred to in paragraph (3) consist of:

a. consultants of the Advisory Board for Marriage Guidance and Preservation (BP4);

b. Officers of the Office of Religious Affairs (KUA);

c. Ustad or Ulama;

d. Priests;

e. Pastors; and

f. other religious leaders who have gender sensitivity or gender awareness.

Part Four
Law Enforcement and Legal Assistance Services

Article 49

(1) Law enforcement and legal assistance services are actions of law enforcement officers provided to Women and Child victims of violence and/or witnesses.

(2) Law enforcement and legal assistance services as referred to in paragraph (1) are carried out by prioritizing the best interests of victims.

- 28 -

Article 50

(1) Law enforcement and legal assistance services as referred to in Article 49 are aimed at providing assistance and protection to Women and Child victims of violence and witnesses with a gender-responsive approach so that victims obtain justice and certainty of legal rights.

(2) Types of legal assistance services as referred to in paragraph (1) include:

a. legal assistance in the reporting process and making of BAP;

b. legal assistance in the prosecution process at the prosecutor's office;

c. legal assistance in the examination process in court;

d. legal assistance in the court hearing decision process until there is a final legal decision; and

e. legal assistance in the mediation process.

(3) The Regency Government provides legal assistance to Children and Women victims of violence free of charge.

(4) In providing legal assistance as referred to in paragraph (3) the Regency Government may cooperate with:

a. Legal Aid Institute (LBH);

b. Women Crisis Center (WCC);

c. Advocates; and/or

d. other legal assistants who are gender sensitive.

Part Five
Repatriation and Social Reintegration Services

Article 51

(1) Repatriation of victims of violence is aimed at returning Women and Child victims of violence to their area of origin.

(2) Repatriation of victims of violence against Women and Children as referred to in paragraph (1) is accompanied by social reintegration efforts.

(3) The Regency Government implements repatriation and social reintegration of Women and Child victims of violence in accordance with the provisions of Laws and Regulations.

- 29 -

CHAPTER VII
WOMEN'S EMPOWERMENT

Article 52

(1) The Regency Government guarantees the implementation of empowerment for Women.

(2) The implementation of Women's empowerment as referred to in paragraph (1) is supported by community participation and Community Organizations.

Article 53

(1) The Regency Government provides skills training to increase Women's capacity.

(2) In providing training as referred to in paragraph (1) the Regency Government may cooperate with training institutions organized by community organizations.

(3) The Regency Government expands access and opportunities for Women to obtain skills and employment.

Article 54

(1) The Regency Government facilitates:

a. provision of business capital assistance for Women with weak economies;

b. marketing of Women's business products;

c. organizing training for community capacity development through economic empowerment of pre-prosperous families;

d. expanding access, information and providing knowledge to Women regarding efforts to market products produced; and

e. providing comprehensive entrepreneurship training to Women.

(2) The Regency Government in facilitating activities as referred to in paragraph (1) cooperates with the community and/or community organizations.

CHAPTER VIII
CHILD PARTICIPATION

Article 55

(1) Every Child has the right to participate in expressing opinions, ideas, and thoughts related to life based on awareness and understanding, according to age and level of thinking maturity.

- 30 -

(2) Child participation as referred to in paragraph (1) may be carried out through Children's Forums starting from the village, sub-district to regency level.

(3) Children's Forums as referred to in paragraph (2) represent Children, both geographical domicile representation, socio-cultural groups and Children's educational backgrounds.

(4) The formation of Children's Forums as referred to in paragraph (2) is facilitated by the Regency Government.

(5) The formation of Children's Forums as referred to in paragraph (4) is stipulated by Regent Decree.

Article 56

(1) In every formulation of policies related to Children, the Regency Government pays attention to and accommodates the Opinions of Children submitted through Children's Forums.

(2) The Regency Government includes representatives of Children's Forums in the Regency Development Planning Deliberation.

CHAPTER IX
CHILD-FRIENDLY REGENCY

Article 57

(1) The Regency Government provides guidance to Sub-district Governments in the development of Child-Friendly Regencies.

(2) Guidance as referred to in paragraph (1) includes:

a. coordination;

b. facilitation;

c. guidance;

d. supervision;

e. consultation; and

f. education and training.

(3) Provisions regarding Child-Friendly Regencies are further regulated by Regional Regulation.

CHAPTER X
GUIDANCE AND SUPERVISION

Article 58

(1) The Regent conducts guidance and supervision of the implementation of Protection of Women and Children.

(2) Guidance as referred to in paragraph (1) may be carried out in the form of:

a. training for government officials and law enforcers on prevention and handling of violence;

- 31 -

b. training for religious leaders, community leaders, customary leaders, educators, journalists and media managers in order to prevent violence against Women and Children;

c. training for health workers who provide health services to Women and Child victims of violence at community health centers, integrated service units, and general hospitals;

d. training for social rehabilitation officers and spiritual guidance officers in order to protect Women and Child victims of violence; and

e. increasing the professionalism of officers, managers of violence case data against Women and Children at community health centers, integrated service units and hospitals.

(3) The Regent in conducting guidance and supervision as referred to in paragraph (1) may delegate to the Head of DPPKBPP-PA.

Article 59

(1) The Head of DPPKBPP-PA must submit a report on the implementation of Women and Child protection programs to the Regent.

(2) The report as referred to in paragraph (1) is submitted periodically once every 3 (three) months.

Article 60

(1) The Regent conducts supervision and evaluation of the implementation of Protection of Women and Children periodically once every 3 (three) months.

(2) The evaluation results as referred to in paragraph (1) are material for evaluation of Women and Child protection program policies in the Regency.

CHAPTER XI
FINANCING

Article 61

Financing required for the implementation of Child-Friendly Regency activities is charged to:

a. Regency Regional Revenue and Expenditure Budget;

b. State Revenue and Expenditure Budget; and

c. other legal and non-binding sources.

CLOSING PROVISIONS
