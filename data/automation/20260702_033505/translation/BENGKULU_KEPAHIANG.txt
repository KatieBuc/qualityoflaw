CHAPTER I
GENERAL PROVISIONS

Article 1

In this Regional Regulation, what is meant by:
1. Region is the Region of Kepahiang Regency.
2. Regent is the Regent of Kepahiang.
3. Regional Government is the Regent as an element of the Regional Government administrator who leads the implementation of government affairs that are autonomous authority.
4. Regional People's Representative Council hereinafter abbreviated as DPRD is a regional people's representative institution that is positioned as an element of the Regional Government administrator.
5. Child is a person who has not reached 18 years of age, including a child still in the womb.
6. Woman is a person of female gender.
7. Violence is any act that results in or may result in misery or suffering whether physical, sexual, economic, social, and psychological to the victim.
8. Violence against women and children is any action that results in or may result in misery or suffering of women physically, sexually, economically, socially, psychologically, including threats of certain actions, coercion or deprivation of freedom, whether occurring in public or private life.
9. Victim is a woman and child who experiences acts of violence occurring in the territory of Kepahiang Regency.
10. Protection of women and children is a series of fulfillment of the rights of women and children victims of violence starting from prevention, handling, recovery and empowerment.
11. Service is an action taken as soon as possible to the victim when seeing, hearing and knowing that violence against the victim will, is, or has occurred.
12. Companion is a person or representative from an institution who has expertise in accompanying victims to conduct counseling, therapy and advocacy for strengthening and recovery of violence victims.
13. Integrated Service Center for Women's Empowerment and Child Protection hereinafter abbreviated as P2TP2A is a cooperative network that organizes the function of integrated services for the protection of women and children victims of violence.
14. Safe House is a residence for victims during the process of assistance and recovery for the safety and comfort of victims from threats and actions of perpetrators.
15. Standard Operating Procedures hereinafter abbreviated as SOP are standard steps that must be taken in protecting victims starting from complaints, identification, health and psychological recovery, legal services up to repatriation and reintegration of victims into society.
16. Family is the smallest unit in society consisting of husband and wife, or husband-wife and their children, or father and children, or mother and children, or blood relatives in direct lineage upward or downward up to the third degree.
17. Household is husband, wife, and children, and people who have family relationships due to blood relations, marriage, breastfeeding, care, and representation, and/or domestic workers and reside in that household.
18. Regional Revenue and Expenditure Budget hereinafter abbreviated as APBD is the APBD of Kepahiang Regency.

CHAPTER II
PRINCIPLES AND OBJECTIVES

Article 2

The principles of protection of children and women who are victims of violence are:
a. respect and fulfillment of victims' rights as human rights;
b. justice and equality;
c. non-discrimination;
d. rights-based handling; and
e. the best interests of the victim.

Article 3

The objectives of protection of women and children are:
a. to prevent acts of violence against women and children;
b. to eliminate all forms of violence and exploitation against women and children;
c. to protect and provide security for women and children;
d. to provide services to women and children both as victims of acts of violence, as well as reporters and witnesses;
e. to guarantee fair and dignified handling and resolution of acts of violence against women and children; and
f. to carry out recovery and empowerment of women and children victims of acts of violence.

Article 4

The scope of protection of women and children includes prevention, service, recovery and empowerment efforts.

CHAPTER II
RIGHTS OF WOMEN AND CHILDREN VICTIMS OF VIOLENCE

Article 5

Every woman and child victim of violence has the following rights:
a. the right to have their dignity respected as human beings;
b. the right to health and psychological recovery from the suffering experienced by the victim;
c. the right to determine their own decisions;
d. the right to obtain information;
e. the right to confidentiality of their identity;
f. the right to complaint handling;
g. the right to assistance and legal aid;
h. the right to obtain facilities in the judicial process; and
i. the right to compensation;
j. the right to social recovery;

Article 6

Child victims of violence in addition to obtaining the rights as referred to in Article 5, also obtain special rights, as follows:
a. the right to respect for survival, growth and development;
b. the right to basic services;
c. the right to equal protection;
d. the right to be free from various stigmas; and
e. the right to obtain freedom.

CHAPTER III
ACTS OF VIOLENCE AGAINST WOMEN AND CHILDREN

Article 7

Forms of violence include:
a. physical violence;
b. psychological violence;
c. sexual violence;
d. neglect;
e. exploitation; and/or
f. other violence.

Article 8

Physical violence as referred to in Article 7 letter (a) is caused by acts or physical actions that result in pain, injury, wounds or disability to a person's body, miscarriage, fainting and/or causing death.

Article 9

Psychological violence as referred to in Article 7 letter (b) is caused by acts that result in fear, loss of self-confidence, loss of ability to act, feelings of helplessness and/or severe psychological suffering to a person.

Article 10

Sexual violence as referred to in Article 7 letter (c) is caused by:
a. acts in the form of sexual harassment;
b. forced sexual intercourse;
c. forced unnatural sexual intercourse; and/or
d. forced sexual intercourse with others for commercial purposes and/or certain purposes.

Article 11

Neglect as referred to in Article 7 letter d is caused by:
a. acts that result in the unfulfillment of children's needs properly, whether physical, mental, spiritual or social, committed by parents, guardians, or any other party responsible for their care;
b. acts of deliberately neglecting to maintain, care for, or take care of children as they should, committed by parents, guardians, or any other party responsible for their care;
c. acts of neglecting people within their household scope, even though according to the law applicable to them or due to agreement or contract they are obliged to provide life, care, or maintenance to that person; and
d. acts that result in economic dependence by limiting and/or prohibiting proper work inside or outside the home so that the victim is under the control of that person.

Article 12

Exploitation as referred to in Article 7 letter (e) is caused by:
a. acts that exploit economically or sexually with the intention of benefiting oneself or others;
b. acts with or without the victim's consent that include but are not limited to prostitution, forced labor or services, slavery or similar practices, oppression, extortion, utilization of physical, sexual, reproductive organs, or unlawfully transferring or transplanting organs and/or body tissues or utilizing the labor or ability of a person by another party to obtain material or immaterial benefits; and/or
c. all forms of utilization of sexual organs or other body organs of the victim to obtain benefits, including but not limited to all prostitution or indecency activities

Article 13

Other violence as referred to in Article 7 letter (f) is caused by:
a. threats of violence include any unlawful act in the form of speech, writing, images, symbols, or body movements, whether with or without using means that cause fear or restrict a person's fundamental freedom; and
b. coercion, includes a situation where a person/victim is ordered or forced to do something in such a way that the person does something contrary to their own will.

CHAPTER III
PROTECTION OF WOMEN AND CHILDREN VICTIMS OF ACTS OF VIOLENCE
Part One
Prevention

Article 14

(1) Prevention efforts against violence against women and children are carried out in an integrated manner by the Regional Government coordinated by the SKPD that has the main tasks and functions of coordination in the field of women's empowerment, family planning and child protection.
(2) Prevention efforts as referred to in paragraph (1) are implemented by:
a. preparing Action Programs and activities for Women's and Children's Empowerment in the Strategic Plan;
b. forming a work network in violence prevention efforts;
c. coordinating, integrating, and synchronizing prevention of violence against women and children.
d. establishing a violence prevention system;
e. socializing legislation related to the rights and protection of women and children against acts of violence;
f. providing critical education about the rights of women and children to the community.

Part Two
Services

Article 16

The provision of services to victims is implemented with the principles of:
a. fast;
b. safe and comfortable;
c. sense of empathy;
d. non-discrimination;
e. easily accessible;
f. not charged; and
g. guaranteed confidentiality.

Article 17

Forms of services to victims include:
a. complaint, consultation, and counseling services;
b. assistance services;
c. health services;
d. social recovery services;
e. legal services; and
f. repatriation and reintegration services into society.

Article 18

Complaint, consultation, and counseling services as referred to in Article 17 letter a include:
a. initial identification or recording of victims; and
b. informed consent.

Article 19

Assistance services as referred to in Article 17 letter b include:
a. accompanying victims during the examination and health recovery process;
b. accompanying victims during the medicolegal process;
c. accompanying victims during the examination process at the Police, Prosecutor's Office and court;
d. monitoring the interests and rights of victims in the examination process at the Police, Prosecutor's Office and Court;
e. maintaining the privacy and confidentiality of victims from all parties who are not interested, including coverage by the mass media;
f. coordinating with other companions; and
g. providing continuous handling up to the recovery stage.

Article 20

Health services as referred to in Article 17 letter c include:
a. first aid to victims;
b. care and recovery of physical injuries aimed at restoring the physical condition of victims carried out by medical and paramedical personnel; and
c. referral to health services.

Article 21

Social recovery services as referred to in Article 17 letter d are services provided by companions in order to restore the traumatic condition of victims, including the provision of safe houses to protect victims from various threats and intimidation for victims and provide social support so that victims have self-confidence, strength, and independence in solving their problems, by:
a. providing spiritual guidance to victims; and
b. psychological recovery of victims.

Article 22

Legal services as referred to in Article 17 letter e to assist victims in undergoing the judicial process by:
a. providing legal consultation that includes information regarding victims' rights and the judicial process;
b. accompanying victims at the investigation, prosecution, and examination levels in court hearings and helping victims to fully describe the violence they experienced;
c. coordinating with fellow law enforcers, volunteer companions, and social workers so that the judicial process proceeds as it should.

Article 23

(1) Repatriation and reintegration services into society as referred to in Article 17 letter f aim to return victims to their families and social environment.
(2) Repatriation and reintegration services into society as referred to in paragraph (1) are carried out by the Regional Government in coordination with relevant government and non-government agencies and institutions.

Part Three
Empowerment

Paragraph 1
Empowerment of Women Victims of Violence

Article 24

Forms of empowerment of women victims of violence include:
a. job training;
b. productive economic enterprises and joint business groups; and
c. capital assistance.

Article 25

Job training as referred to in Article 24 letter a includes:
a. skills training;
b. field work practice; and
c. apprenticeship.

Article 26

Productive economic enterprises and joint business groups as referred to in Article 24 letter b include:
a. entrepreneurial skills training;
b. facilitation of joint business group formation; and
c. business implementation assistance.

Article 27

Capital assistance as referred to in Article 24 letter c includes:
a. work facilities and infrastructure assistance; and
b. facilitation of working capital assistance

CHAPTER IV
OBLIGATIONS AND RESPONSIBILITIES

Article 28

Obligations and responsibilities in the implementation of protection of women and children from acts of violence are the shared responsibility of:
a. Regional Government;
b. Village Government and Village/Kelurahan Customary Institution Kutei
c. community;
d. family;
e. parents; and
f. related institutions both directly and indirectly.

Part One
Regional Government

Article 29

(1) The obligations and responsibilities of the regional government as referred to in Article 28 letter a, include:
a. implementing protection policies for women and children from acts of violence established by the government;
b. establishing policies, programs, and activities for the protection of women and children from acts of violence;
c. forming the P2TP2A institution;
d. cooperating in the implementation of protection of women and children from acts of violence;
e. providing facilities and infrastructure support for the implementation of protection of women and children from acts of violence;
f. allocating a budget for the implementation of protection of women and children from acts of violence according to regional financial capacity;
g. fostering and supervising the implementation of protection of women and children from acts of violence;
h. cooperating in handling acts of violence against women and children with other Regions or related institutions and parties; and
i. evaluating programs and activities for the protection of women and children against acts of violence.
(2) The SKPD that has the main tasks and functions in the field of women's empowerment, family planning and child protection coordinates, synchronizes and evaluates the protection of women and children against acts of violence by involving relevant parties at least once every 6 (six) months.
(3) In order to carry out the obligations and responsibilities as referred to in paragraph (1), the Regional Government establishes programs and activities for the protection of women and children in 1 (one) Regional Action Plan as a basis for relevant SKPDs in implementing the protection of women and children from acts of violence.
(4) The Regional Action Plan as referred to in paragraph (3), is part of the Regional Medium-Term Development Plan (RPJMD).
(5) Further provisions regarding the Regional Action Plan as referred to in paragraph (4), are regulated by Regent Regulation.

Article 30

The Regional Government is obliged and responsible to:
a. supervise the provision of services to victims, with minimum service standards; and
b. provide funds for victim protection through the APBD and/or other legitimate regional financial sources.

Part Two
Village Government and Village/Kelurahan Customary Institution Kutei

Article 31

Village Government in the protection of women and children against acts of violence has the role of:
a. actively participating in preventing the occurrence of physical violence, psychological violence, sexual violence, neglect, exploitation, and/or other violence against women and children;
b. protecting victims;
c. securing perpetrators caught red-handed and evidence before handing them over to the authorities;
d. based on the victim's request, the Village Government hands over the perpetrator to the Indonesian National Police, if there are criminal elements; and
e. providing emergency assistance.

Article 32

Village Kutei and/or Kelurahan Kutei have obligations and responsibilities in the following matters:
a. actively participating in preventing the occurrence of physical violence, psychological violence, sexual violence, neglect, exploitation, and/or other violence against women and children based on customary law provisions;
b. resolving acts of violence against women and children if there are violations of customary law norms;
c. participating in supervising the implementation of protection of women and children carried out by the government and other institutions.

Part Three
Community and Family

Article 33

The community and family have obligations and responsibilities in the following matters:
a. preventing the occurrence of physical violence, psychological violence, sexual violence, neglect, exploitation, and/or other violence against women and children;
b. reporting if violence occurs;
c. protecting victims;
d. providing security for perpetrators caught red-handed; and
e. providing emergency assistance.
f. participating in supervising the implementation of protection of women and children carried out by the government and other institutions.

Article 35

(1) In the event of being caught red-handed as referred to in Article 33 letter d, the community may secure the perpetrator before handing them over to the authorities.
(2) The obligations of the family and/or parents as referred to in Article 28 letters b and e, who legally have full responsibility to prevent all forms of violence and protect women and children as family members;
(3) The role of the community as referred to in Article 34 paragraph (1) is carried out by individuals, child protection institutions, social community institutions, non-governmental organizations, educational institutions, religious institutions, business entities and mass media.

CHAPTER V
P2TP2A

Article 38

(1) The Regional Government in order to provide services and protection to women and children victims of acts of violence establishes P2TP2A.
(2) P2TP2A as referred to in paragraph (1) is a functional work unit that has the task, principal, and function of providing integrated protection to women and children from acts of violence.

Article 39

(1) The organizational structure of P2TP2A established by the Regional Government as referred to in Article 19 paragraph (1) consists of:
a. general chairperson or other equivalent designation;
b. secretary;
c. treasurer; and
d. executive chairperson overseeing fields.
(2) The fields as referred to in paragraph (1) letter d, at least include:
a. complaint service field;
b. health service field;
c. social recovery service field;
d. repatriation and reintegration field into society; and
e. advocacy and legal aid service field.
(3) The organizational structure of P2TP2A as referred to in paragraph (1) is established by the Regent.

Article 40

The complaint service field as referred to in Article 39 paragraph (2) letter a has the task of:
a. conducting interviews and observations of the victim's condition;
b. making recommendations for follow-up services;
c. coordinating and referring follow-up services with relevant parties; and
d. administering the complaint process.

Article 41

The health service field as referred to in Article 39 paragraph (2) letter b has the task of:
a. conducting examinations, treatment, and follow-up care for victims;
b. coordinating the implementation of health recovery and medicolegal;
c. conducting medicolegal examinations including collecting evidence from victims and making visum et repertum;
d. conducting supporting examinations and laboratory tests on evidence;
e. consulting with specialist doctors or making referrals; and
f. making case reports.

Article 42

The social recovery service field as referred to in Article 39 paragraph (2) letter c has the task of:
a. providing assistance during the case handling process;
b. conducting counseling; and
c. facilitating obtaining a certificate of the victim's psychological condition.

Article 43

The repatriation and reintegration field into society as referred to in Article 39 paragraph (2) letter d has the task of:
a. coordinating with relevant agencies for victim repatriation;
b. making progress reports on the repatriation and social recovery assistance process; and
c. monitoring for at least three months after the victim is returned to their family.

Article 44

The legal aid service field as referred to in Article 39 paragraph (2) letter e has the task of:
a. accompanying and defending every legal service process; and
b. making progress reports on legal handling.

Article 45

Further provisions regarding the development, mechanisms and networked working relationships of P2TP2A with relevant parties in handling violence against women and children are established by Regent Regulation.

CHAPTER VII
SAFE HOUSE

Article 46

(1) Safe houses must be provided by the Social Service to handle cases of violence against women and children that pose a high risk to the victim's safety.
(2) The management of Safe Houses as referred to in paragraph (1) refers to applicable laws and regulations.
(3) The financing of Safe House management as referred to in paragraph (2) is charged to the APBD.

CHAPTER VIII
INFORMATION/REPORTING SYSTEM

Article 47

(1) The SKPD whose main tasks and functions are in the field of women's empowerment, family planning and child protection implements an information/reporting system for the protection of women and children victims of acts of violence which is reported to the Regent.
(2) The information/reporting system as referred to in paragraph (1) is organized in accordance with the provisions of laws and regulations.

CHAPTER IX
GUIDANCE AND SUPERVISION

Article 48

(1) The Regional Government conducts guidance and supervision of the implementation of minimum service standards of P2TP2A.
(2) Guidance and Supervision as referred to in paragraph (1) include:
a. coordination;
b. guidance;
c. education and training; and
d. monitoring and evaluation.
(3) Coordination as referred to in paragraph (2) letter a covers aspects related to planning and implementation.
(4) Guidance as referred to in paragraph (2) letter b covers aspects related to planning, implementation, procedures, funding, quality, control and supervision.
(5) Education and training as referred to in paragraph (2) letter c are conducted in accordance with laws and regulations.
(6) Monitoring and evaluation as referred to in paragraph (2) letter d are conducted periodically.

CHAPTER X
FUNDING

Article 49

(1) Funding for victim protection activities carried out by the Regional Government is charged to the APBD and/or other legitimate sources in accordance with the provisions of laws and regulations.
(2) Every SKPD related to programs and activities for the protection of women and children against acts of violence must allocate its budget.

CHAPTER XI
COMMUNITY PARTICIPATION

Article 50

(1) The community participates in the implementation of protection for victims.
(2) Community participation as referred to in paragraph (1) is in the form of:
a. forming family partners at the Kelurahan/Village level;
b. forming women and child protection units within community organizations;
c. independently socializing the rights of women and children;
d. providing first aid to victims; and
e. reporting to the competent authority if violence against victims occurs in their environment.
(3) Community participation as referred to in paragraph (1) is carried out by individuals, social community institutions, non-governmental organizations, educational institutions, religious institutions, and mass media.

CHAPTER XII
CLOSING PROVISIONS

Article 51

This Regional Regulation comes into force on the date of promulgation.

In order that everyone may know, orders the promulgation of this Regional Regulation by placing it in the Regional Gazette.

I. GENERAL

Acts of violence against women and children constitute violations of human rights so that their dignity and honor need to be protected and their right to life guaranteed in accordance with their nature and destiny without discrimination.

Sociologically, acts of violence against women and children have increasingly increased both quantitatively and qualitatively. This is in line with technological developments that can be imitated by perpetrators of acts of violence at any time so that the quality is also increasing. Culturally, the Kepahiang community works and lives on plantations so that attention and responsibility for their children is neglected. In order to prevent and overcome violence against women and children in Kepahiang Regency so that they are protected from violence, threats of violence, torture or treatment that degrades human dignity and degree, protection of women and children victims of violence needs to be carried out in the form of regional regulations.

Juridically, legislation regulating the protection of women and children victims of violence has not yet regulated protection efforts in the Region so that institutional and regulatory support is needed that can guarantee its implementation.

This Regional Regulation regulates protection efforts for victims, especially in terms of prevention, service and empowerment of women and children victims of violence in Kepahiang Regency.
