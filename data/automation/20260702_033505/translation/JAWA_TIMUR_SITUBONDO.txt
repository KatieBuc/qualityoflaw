VICTIMS OF VIOLENCE

WITH THE GRACE OF ALMIGHTY GOD
THE REGENT OF SITUBONDO,

Considering a. that until now the number of violence against women and children in Situbondo Regency is still high, while services and protection have not been carried out optimally;

b. that in order for protection efforts for women and children to obtain optimal results, there needs to be concrete action from the regional government and it is necessary to increase broad community participation;

c. that to realize the provision of protection for women and children and to provide legal certainty in the implementation of protection for women and children in Situbondo Regency, the implementation of protection for women and children needs to be regulated in a Regional Regulation;

d. that based on the considerations as referred to in letter a, letter b, and letter c, it is necessary to establish a Regional Regulation concerning the Implementation of Protection for Women and Children.

CHAPTER I
GENERAL PROVISIONS

Article 1

1. Regional Government is the Government of Situbondo Regency.

2. Regent is the Regent of Situbondo.

3. Child is a person who has not yet reached 18 years of age, including those in the womb.

4. Violence is any act unlawfully with or without means against physical and psychological that causes danger to life, body or results in the deprivation of a person's freedom.

5. Violence against women is any action that results in or may result in suffering or hardship for women physically, sexually or psychologically, including threats of certain actions, coercion or arbitrary deprivation of freedom, whether occurring in the public sphere or in private life.

6. Violence against children is any act against a child that results in suffering or hardship physically, mentally, sexually, psychologically, including neglect and ill-treatment that threatens bodily integrity and degrades the dignity of the child committed by parties who should be responsible for the child or those who have power over the child, who should be trustworthy, such as parents, close family, teachers and companions.

7. Victim is a woman and child who experiences suffering and/or hardship either directly or indirectly as a result of violence.

8. Service is an action that must be immediately taken for victims when seeing, hearing and knowing that violence against victims will, is, or has occurred.

9. Companion is a person or representative from an institution who has the expertise to provide assistance to victims to conduct counseling, therapy and advocacy for strengthening and recovery of victims of violence.

10. Integrated Service Center (PPT) for Victims of Violence Against Women and Children of Situbondo Regency, hereinafter referred to as PPT of Situbondo Regency, is an institution providing services to victims of violence, based in a hospital, managed jointly in the form of medical services (including medico-legal), psychosocial and legal services.

11. Safe house (shelter) is a temporary residence used to provide protection to victims in accordance with determined operational standards.

12. Community is individuals, families, groups, social organizations and/or community organizations.

13. Woman is a person of female gender.

14. Family is the smallest unit in society consisting of husband and wife, or husband, wife and their children, or father and his children, and mother and her children.

15. Household is family members and relatives such as grandchildren, nephews/nieces, older siblings, younger siblings, grandfathers, grandmothers, husbands, wives, cousins and so on and non-relatives such as domestic workers, drivers and so on who live and reside in one house.

16. Agency for Community and Women's Empowerment hereinafter abbreviated as BPMP is the Agency for Community and Women's Empowerment of Situbondo Regency.

CHAPTER II
PRINCIPLES AND OBJECTIVES

Article 2

The implementation of protection for women and children victims of violence is carried out based on the principles of:
a. respect for human rights;
b. gender equality and justice;
c. protection of victims' rights;
d. non-discrimination; and
e. best interests of victims.

Article 3

(1) The purpose of implementing protection for women and children victims of violence is to provide protection and services to victims of gender-based violence and the best interests of women and children that occur at home and/or in public places.

(2) Violence as referred to in paragraph (1) may take the form of:
a. physical violence;
b. psychological violence;
c. sexual violence;
d. economic neglect; and
e. restriction of movement.

CHAPTER III
RIGHTS OF VICTIMS

Article 4

Every victim of violence has the right to receive:
a. protection and assistance;
b. legal aid;
c. health services according to medical needs;
d. psychosocial services;
e. information; and
f. integrated services.

Article 5

(1) In the event that victims of violence as referred to in Article 4 require ongoing treatment, they have the right to stay in a safe house or alternative residence.

(2) Ongoing treatment as referred to in paragraph (1) is treatment at the rehabilitation stage carried out by individuals, groups or government and non-government institutions.

Article 6

(1) The safe house as referred to in Article 5 paragraph (1) provides a place and/or temporary safe shelter for victims of violence until their rights are restored.

(2) The implementation and/or management of the safe house as referred to in paragraph (1) is carried out by PPT.

(3) For the safety and at the request of victims of violence, the manager or operator of the safe house as referred to in paragraph (2) may place victims of violence in a certain confidential location.

CHAPTER IV
IMPLEMENTATION OF PROTECTION

Part One
Institutional

Article 7

(1) The implementation of protection for victims of violence is carried out in an integrated manner through the PPT of Situbondo Regency.

(2) Provisions concerning PPT of Situbondo Regency as referred to in paragraph (1) are regulated by Regent Regulation.

Part Two
Forms and Mechanisms of Service

Article 8

(1) Forms of service to victims of violence organized by PPT include:
a. medical services in the form of care and recovery of physical conditions carried out by medical and paramedical personnel;
b. medicolegal services which are a form of medical service for evidentiary purposes in the legal field;
c. legal aid to assist victims of violence in undergoing judicial processes;
d. first psychosocial assistance to victims conducted to identify the occurrence of violence and trauma experienced by victims;
e. assistance to victims of violence carried out by competent persons or institutions who have expertise to conduct counseling, therapy and advocacy for strengthening and recovery of victims of violence and have cooperated with PPT; and
f. economic independence services in the form of services for skills training and providing economic access so that victims can be independent.

(2) The service mechanism as referred to in paragraph (1) is organized according to Standard Operating Procedures (SOP) regulated by Regent Regulation.

Part Three
Service Principles

Article 9

(1) The provision of services to victims is carried out free of charge, quickly, safely, with empathy, non-discriminatory, easily accessible and with a guarantee of confidentiality.

(2) The organizer or manager of PPT or Safe House is prohibited from charging any fees to victims of violence.

CHAPTER V
RESPONSIBILITY

Article 10

The implementation of protection for victims of violence is the joint responsibility of:
a. regency regional government;
b. community;
c. family; and
d. parents.

Article 11

(1) The Regional Government is responsible for implementing efforts to prevent violence and protect victims of violence.

(2) Prevention of violence as referred to in paragraph (1) is in the form of:
a. collecting data and information about women and children and related legislation;
b. conducting education on anti-violence values; and
c. socializing legislation related to the implementation of prevention and protection of victims of violence.

(3) Protection of victims of violence as referred to in paragraph (1) is in the form of:
a. establishing and facilitating the implementation of integrated service institutions for victims by involving relevant agencies/institutions and community elements;
b. facilitating assistance, legal aid and legal services according to the needs of victims;
c. providing housing both safe houses and alternative residences along with handling, service, and psychosocial mechanisms;
d. conducting ongoing treatment up to the rehabilitation and social reintegration stage;
e. monitoring and evaluating the implementation of protection for victims of violence; and
f. encouraging community awareness of the importance of protection for victims of violence.

(4) In order to carry out the responsibilities as referred to in paragraph (1), the Regional Government establishes programs and action activities for protection of women and children in one Regional Action Plan for Protection of Women and Children Victims of Violence.

(5) The Regional Action Plan for Protection of Women and Children Victims of Violence as referred to in paragraph (2), is established for a period of 3 (three) years and is determined by Regent Decree.

Article 12

The responsibility of the community as referred to in Article 10 letter b is implemented in the form of:
a. preventing acts of violence against women and children; and
b. providing information and/or reporting acts of violence against women and children to law enforcement or authorized parties.

Article 13

Family and/or parents as referred to in Article 10 letter c and letter d have full responsibility to prevent all forms of violence and protect women and children as family members.

CHAPTER VI
SUPERVISION

Article 14

(1) Supervision of the implementation of protection for victims of violence is carried out by BPMP.

(2) In conducting supervision as referred to in paragraph (1), BPMP has the authority to:
a. place victims of violence in safe houses;
b. summon and present the families of victims of violence to be heard for their statements; and
c. order PPT to provide protection to someone who is a victim of violence.

(3) BPMP must report regularly to the Regent regarding the implementation of protection for women and children victims of violence.

CHAPTER VII
FINANCING

Article 15

(1) Financing for the implementation of protection for women and children victims of violence is charged to the Regional Budget of Situbondo Regency and other lawful sources.

(2) Budget management for the implementation of protection for victims of violence is carried out with the principles of fairness, efficiency, transparency and accountability.

CHAPTER VIII
CRIMINAL PROVISIONS

Article 16

Any person who deliberately allows violence against women and children to occur and/or does not report the occurrence of violence against women and children, shall be subject to sanctions as regulated in applicable legislation.

Article 17

(1) Any person who deliberately violates the provisions as referred to in Article 6 paragraph (3), shall be punished with imprisonment of a maximum of 3 (three) months or a fine of a maximum of Rp. 50,000,000,- (fifty million rupiah).

(2) In the event that the act as referred to in paragraph (1) is committed by an official appointed to implement protection, shall be punished with imprisonment of a maximum of 6 (six) months or a fine of a maximum of Rp. 50,000,000,- (fifty million rupiah).

(3) The organizer or manager of PPT or Safe House who is proven to violate the provisions as referred to in Article 9 paragraph (2), shall be punished with imprisonment of a maximum of 6 (six) months or a fine of a maximum of Rp. 50,000,000,- (fifty million rupiah).

CHAPTER IX
CLOSING PROVISIONS

Article 18

This Regional Regulation comes into force on the date of promulgation.

In order that everyone may know of it, the promulgation of this Regional Regulation is ordered by placing it in the Regional Gazette of Situbondo Regency.

I. GENERAL

The implementation of protection for victims of violence is organized based on the principles of humanity, integration and justice. Therefore, everyone must receive their right to protection for a sense of security and freedom from torture or treatment that degrades human dignity which is contrary to what is aspired to in the Preamble of the 1945 Constitution of the Republic of Indonesia, namely the creation of a just and civilized humanity. To achieve integrated and comprehensive Protection Services for Women and Children Victims of Violence, an integrated form of service from various related institutions is required including medical services, medicolegal, psychosocial, legal aid, assistance, economic independence (training). These services are carried out evenly across all levels of society so that every citizen has the right to obtain equal opportunities to obtain security and legal protection.

In principle, the strategic target of implementing protection is to maintain the sustainability of protection through integrated, comprehensive service efforts and safe and comfortable safe houses that are conducive for victims of violence. These strategic targets are pursued through interrelated stages, both planning, protection, services and financing, supervision and sanctions.

There are four important phenomena that need to be observed in relation to the Implementation of Protection for Women and Children Victims of Violence. First, is violence against women and children from year to year the number increases with the mode of violence tending to be more sadistic (perpetrators who slit throats, pour hot water, throw into wells, to breaking some limbs and so on). Second, the many cases of violence against Domestic Workers (PRT) dominated by women that have occurred recently require attention from all parties, especially the existence of legal protection, regulations and policies that protect them. Third, the lack of facilities and infrastructure for safe and comfortable safe houses that are conducive for victims of violence. These three phenomena indicate the increasing complexity of violent behavior whose victims are dominated by women and children both who become domestic workers and others. Fourth, children are a trust and gift from Almighty God, in them is inherent dignity and worth as a complete human being. Children are the next generation of the nation's struggle ideals who have a strategic role, and have special characteristics and traits that are expected to ensure the continuity of the nation and state's existence in the future. Children need to get the widest possible opportunity to grow and develop optimally, both physically, mentally, cognitively, spiritually and socially.

Because of its nature, children's growth and development must be carried out in an environment that protects from all dangers in the form of optimal care.

Therefore, a legal umbrella in the form of a Regional Regulation is needed that is able to accommodate all issues related to the implementation of protection for women and children victims of violence, able to provide integrated services holistically and comprehensively, and firmly give a mandate to institutions to coordinate policies and supervision, and a mandate to institutions to provide services. In addition, Regional Regulations can also open the involvement of non-governmental institutions and the community to play a broad role.

The implementation of protection for women and children victims of violence in Situbondo Regency is carried out based on Situbondo Regent Regulation Number 6 Year 2006 concerning Implementation of Protection for Victims of Acts of Violence Against Women and Children and Situbondo Regent Decree No. 188/237/P.001.2/2007 concerning Integrated Service Center (PPT) for Victims of Violence Against Women and Children in Situbondo Regency. As the phenomena described above, Regent Regulation Number 6 Year 2006 has not been able to become a comprehensive legal umbrella in providing protection for women and children victims of violence. With this Regional Regulation, it is expected to anticipate and overcome the conditions of violence that occur in Situbondo Regency, so that it can provide protection and services to victims of violence in an integrated and comprehensive manner.

II. ARTICLE BY ARTICLE

Article 1
Sufficiently clear.

Article 2
Letter a
What is meant by "the principle of respect for human rights" is that the implementation of protection for women and children victims of violence is to ensure the fulfillment of the rights of victims of violence as a manifestation of respect for human rights.

Letter b
What is meant by "the principle of gender equality and justice" is a process of equality and conditions for men and women to obtain opportunities and their rights as human beings, so that they are able to play a role and participate in political, economic, social, cultural, defense and national security activities and equality in enjoying the results of such development.

Letter c
What is meant by "the principle of protection of victims' rights" is protection of personal self, family, honor, dignity, and property under their control, a sense of security and protection from threats of fear, freedom from torture or treatment that degrades human dignity.

Letter d
What is meant by "non-discriminatory principle" is attitudes and treatment towards victims without making distinctions based on age, gender, race, ethnicity, religion and between groups.

Letter e
What is meant by "the principle of the best interests of victims" is that all actions concerning victims carried out by the government, community, legislative bodies and judicial bodies, the best interests of victims must be the primary consideration.

Article 3
Paragraph (1)
What is meant by gender-based violence is action based on gender relations that place women subordinate to men.

Paragraph (2)
Letter a
What is meant by "physical violence" is an act that results in pain, illness, or serious injury such as slapping, hitting, spitting, pulling hair/yanking, kicking, burning with hot objects, hitting/injuring with weapons, and/or causing disability to a person's body, miscarriage, fainting and/or causing death.

Letter b
What is meant by "psychological violence" is an act that results in fear, loss of self-confidence, loss of ability to act, feelings of helplessness, and/or severe psychological suffering in a person. Psychological violence can be in the form of emotional abuse such as insults, comments that are hurtful or demeaning to self-esteem, isolating the wife from the outside world, threatening or frightening as a means of imposing one's will.

Letter c
What is meant by "sexual violence" is violence against sexuality which includes isolation (distancing) of the wife from her inner needs, forcing sexual relations, imposing one's own sexual preferences or not paying attention to the wife's satisfaction. Sexual violence can also take the form of forced sexual relations carried out against people living within the household, forced sexual relations against one person in their household with another person for commercial purposes and/or certain purposes.

Letter d
What is meant by "economic neglect" is neglecting the economic needs of the wife or children or family members and relatives such as not providing for the wife, even spending the wife's money, not giving or withholding the wages of domestic workers and drivers and includes acts of limiting and/or prohibiting family members from working properly.

Letter e
What is meant by "restriction of movement" is limiting the movement of the wife or children in developing their potential both for economic and social purposes such as prohibiting gathering and socializing in the community and limiting the movement of relatives such as preventing domestic workers or drivers from communicating or relating with their families.
