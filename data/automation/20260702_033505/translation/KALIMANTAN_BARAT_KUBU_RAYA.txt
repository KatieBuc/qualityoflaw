REGENT OF KUBU RAYA

REGIONAL REGULATION OF KUBU RAYA REGENCY NUMBER 9 YEAR 2016
CONCERNING
PROTECTION OF WOMEN FROM ACTS OF VIOLENCE WITH THE BLESSING OF ALMIGHTY GOD REGENT OF KUBU RAYA,

Considering Recalling
a. that violence against women constitutes a violation of human rights so that women need to be protected in their dignity and honor and their right to life guaranteed in accordance with their nature and destiny;

b. that to address violence against women in Kubu Raya Regency, prevention efforts and optimization of addressing violence against women are required through policies to guarantee legal protection for women;

c. that to provide legal protection for women, the regulation of protection of women from acts of violence needs to be regulated in a Regional Regulation;

d. that based on the considerations as referred to in letters a, b and c, it is necessary to establish a Regional Regulation concerning Protection of Women From Acts of Violence;

1. Article 18 paragraph (6) of the 1945 Constitution of the Republic of Indonesia;

2. Law Number 35 Year 2007 concerning the Establishment of Kubu Raya Regency in West Kalimantan Province (State Gazette of the Republic of Indonesia Year 2007 Number 101, Supplement to the State Gazette of the Republic of Indonesia Number 4751);

To Establish A REGIONAL REGULATION CONCERNING PROTECTION OF WOMEN FROM ACTS OF VIOLENCE.

CHAPTER I
GENERAL PROVISIONS

Article 1

In this Regional Regulation, what is meant by:
1. Region is Kubu Raya Regency.
2. Regional Government is the Regent as an element of Regional Government Administration who leads the Implementation of Government Affairs which are the Authority of the Autonomous Region.
3. Regent is the Regent of Kubu Raya.
4. Regional Work Unit hereinafter abbreviated as SKPD is an organization or institution in the regional government that is responsible to the Regent.
5. Women are human beings of female gender starting from infants, adolescents, adults up to the elderly.
6. Women with Disabilities are women who have self-limitations, which can be physical, cognitive, mental, sensory, emotional, developmental or some combination of these.
7. Elderly Women are women who have reached 60 years of age and above.
8. Violence is any act that results in or can result in misery or suffering whether physical, sexual, economic, social, psychological including threats of certain actions, coercion or deprivation of freedom, whether occurring in public or private life.
9. Violence against women is any action that results in or may result in misery or suffering to women physically, sexually, economically, socially, psychologically including certain threats, coercion or deprivation of freedom, whether occurring in public or private life.
10. Victim is a woman who experiences misery and/or suffering either directly or indirectly as a result of violence and life vulnerability.
11. Protection of Women is all activities aimed at providing a sense of security carried out by the regional government, community, and/or other parties who know or hear that violence against victims will occur or has occurred.
12. Service/handling is action taken as soon as possible to victims when seeing, hearing and/or knowing that violence against women will occur, is occurring or has occurred.
13. Assistance is a person or marriage from an institution that has expertise in assisting victims to conduct counseling, therapy and/or advocacy.

14. Integrated Service Center, hereinafter abbreviated as PPT is an institution or functional work unit that organizes integrated services for victims of violence.
15. Safe House is a residence for victims during the assistance process for the safety and comfort of victims from threats and dangers.
16. Minimum Service Standards hereinafter abbreviated as SPM is a performance benchmark for integrated service units in providing 5 (five) types of services for women and child victims of violence, namely (1) Complaint Handling, (2) Health Services, (3) Social Rehabilitation, (4) Law Enforcement and Legal Aid, and (5) Repatriation and Social Reintegration.
17. Complaint Handling is a series of actions taken by integrated service providers to follow up on reports of acts of violence against women submitted by victims, families and the community.
18. Health Services are efforts that include promotive, curative and rehabilitative aspects.
19. Social Rehabilitation is a service aimed at restoring and developing the ability of a person experiencing social dysfunction to be able to carry out social functions properly.
20. Law Enforcement is action by officials authorized by the State to implement statutory regulations.
21. Legal Aid is legal services provided by legal assistants and advocates to conduct the process of assisting witnesses and/or victims of violence against women that is gender sensitive.
22. Repatriation is an effort to return women victims of violence from abroad to the debarkation/entry point, or from the receiving area to the area of origin.
23. Social Reintegration is an effort to reunite victims with family, substitute family or community that can provide protection and fulfillment for victims.
24. Integrated Service Unit hereinafter abbreviated as UPT is a unit that organizes integrated service functions for women victims of violence.
25. Family is the smallest unit in society consisting of husband and wife, or husband-wife and their children, or father and his children, or mother and her children, or blood relatives in one straight line upward or downward.
26. Household is husband, wife and children, people who have family relationships due to blood relations, marriage, breastfeeding, foster care and guardianship and/or domestic workers who reside in that household.

Article 2

Protection of Women from acts of violence is based on:
a. respect and fulfillment of victims' rights;
b. justice and gender equality;
c. non-discrimination; and
d. the best interests of victims.

Article 3

Protection of women from acts of violence aims to:
a. prevent violence against women;

b. eliminate all forms of violence and exploitation against women;
c. protect and provide a sense of security for women;
d. facilitate and mediate household disputes to realize the integrity of a harmonious and prosperous household;
e. provide services and/or handling to victims; and
f. empower women victims of violence and vulnerable groups.

CHAPTER II
SCOPE OF PROTECTION

Article 4

The scope of protection of women from acts of violence includes:
a. prevention;
b. service and/or handling; and
c. empowerment.

CHAPTER III
PREVENTION, SERVICE AND/OR HANDLING
AND EMPOWERMENT

Part One
Prevention

Article 5

(1) Prevention efforts as referred to in Article 4 letter a are carried out in an integrated manner by the Regional Government coordinated by the SKPD in charge of Women's Empowerment.

(2) Prevention efforts as referred to in paragraph (1) are implemented by:
a. forming a network;
b. coordinating, integrating, and synchronizing violence prevention based on partnership patterns;
c. forming a violence prevention system;
d. socializing laws and regulations related to the protection of women victims of violence, including raising awareness among families and communities;
e. providing education for women victims of violence including access to opening employment opportunities; and
f. through complete and easily accessible communication, information and education media.

(3) In addition to prevention efforts as referred to in paragraph (1), prevention efforts are also carried out by families and/or closest relatives, communities, educational institutions and customary institutions.

Part Two
Service and/or Handling

Article 6

(1) Service and/or handling as referred to in Article 4 letter b is carried out in an integrated manner by PPT.

(2) PPT as referred to in paragraph (1) can receive and send case referrals from and/or to other service units in a networked manner.

(3) Further provisions regarding the development, mechanisms, and working relationships of integrated PPT networks as referred to in paragraph (2) are regulated by Regent Regulation.

Article 7

(1) Forms of service and/or handling of victims include:
a. complaint handling including identification;
b. health/medical rehabilitation services;
c. social rehabilitation services;
d. law enforcement and legal aid services;
e. repatriation and social reintegration services; and
f. psychological services.

(2) Forms of service as referred to in paragraph (1) are implemented in accordance with Minimum Service Standards established by statutory regulations implemented by regional apparatus in accordance with their duties and functions.

Article 8

Service and/or handling of victims is carried out with:
a. speed and accuracy;
b. safety and comfort;
c. sense of empathy;
d. non-discrimination;
e. easy accessibility;
f. no charge; and
g. guaranteed confidentiality.

Part Three
Empowerment

Article 9

Empowerment as referred to in Article 4 letter c includes:
a. forming communities of women victims of violence;
b. job training;
c. productive economic enterprises and joint business groups; and
d. capital assistance.

Article 10

(1) Job training as referred to in Article 9 letter b includes:
a. skills training;
b. field work practice; and
c. apprenticeship.

(2) Implementation of job training as referred to in paragraph (1) can be facilitated by the SKPD handling Women's Empowerment and technical agencies in accordance with their main duties and functions.

Article 11

Productive economic enterprises and joint business groups as referred to in Article 9 letter c include:
a. entrepreneurial skills training; and
b. business implementation assistance.

Article 12

(1) Capital assistance as referred to in Article 9 letter d includes:
a. facilities and infrastructure assistance; and
b. working/business capital assistance facilities.

(2) Capital assistance as referred to in paragraph (1) can be obtained through the SKPD budget or assistance from other legitimate sources in accordance with statutory provisions.

CHAPTER III
VIOLENCE

Article 13

Violence against women consists of:
a. physical violence;
b. psychological violence;
c. sexual violence;
d. neglect;
e. exploitation; and/or
f. other violence.

Article 14

Physical violence as referred to in Article 13 letter a includes acts that result in pain, wounds, injuries or disabilities to the body, miscarriage, fainting, and/or death.

Article 15

Psychological violence as referred to in Article 13 letter b includes acts that result in fear, loss of self-confidence, loss of ability to act, feelings of helplessness, and/or severe psychological suffering.

Article 16

Sexual violence as referred to in Article 13 letter c includes:
a. acts in the form of sexual harassment;
b. forced sexual relations in an unnatural or unwanted manner; and/or
c. forced sexual relations with others for commercial purposes and/or certain purposes.

Article 17

Neglect as referred to in Article 13 letter d includes:
a. acts that neglect women within the scope of their household; and/or
b. acts that result in economic dependence.

Article 18

Exploitation as referred to in Article 13 letter e includes:
a. actions that exploit women economically or sexually with the intention of benefiting oneself or others.

b. actions with or without the victim's consent which include but are not limited to prostitution, forced labor or services, slavery or similar practices, oppression, exploitation, utilization of physical, sexual, reproductive organs, or unlawfully transferring or transplanting organs and/or body tissues or utilizing the labor or ability of a person by another party to obtain benefits both material and immaterial.

c. all forms of utilization of sexual organs or other body organs of victims to obtain benefits, including but not limited to all prostitution or indecency activities.

Article 19

Other violence as referred to in Article 13 letter f includes:
a. any unlawful act in the form of speech, writing, images, symbols, or body movements, whether with or without using means that cause fear or restrict a person's fundamental freedom.

b. a situation where a person/victim is ordered to do something in such a way that the person does something contrary to their own will.

CHAPTER V
RIGHTS OF WOMEN VICTIMS OF VIOLENCE

Article 20

Every woman victim of violence has the right to:
a. have her dignity and honor respected as a human being;
b. obtain physical and psychological health recovery from the suffering experienced;
c. determine her own decisions;
d. obtain information;
e. have her identity kept confidential;
f. obtain compensation;
g. obtain social rehabilitation;
h. obtain spiritual guidance;
i. obtain complaint handling;
j. obtain facilitation in the judicial process;
k. obtain assistance; and
l. guarantee of rights related to status as a family member or community member.

CHAPTER VI
GOVERNMENT RESPONSIBILITY AND INSTITUTIONS

Part One
Regional Government

Article 21

(1) The Regional Government is obliged and responsible for implementing victim protection efforts by:
a. forming PPT;
b. formulating policies;
c. preparing plans and implementing programs and activities;
d. providing facilities and infrastructure support; and
e. supervising service delivery to victims with minimum service standards.

(2) In implementing the obligations and responsibilities as referred to in paragraph (1), the Regional Government prepares a Regional Action Plan for Women's Protection.

(3) Further provisions regarding the Regional Action Plan as referred to in paragraph (2) are regulated by Regent Regulation.

Article 22

The obligations and responsibilities of the Regional Government as referred to in Article 21 paragraph (1) are coordinated by the SKPD in charge of women's empowerment issues.

Part Two
Community and Family

Article 23

The community and family have obligations and responsibilities to:
a. prevent violence against women;
b. report if violence occurs;
c. protect victims; and
d. provide emergency assistance.

Part Three
Women's Protection Coordination

Article 24

(1) Service and/or handling for victims is carried out in a networked manner.

(2) The network as referred to in paragraph (1) aims to:
a. coordinate and synchronize PPT service handling;
b. develop networks and referral systems; and
c. collect, compile and present violence reports.

(3) The network as referred to in paragraph (1) carries out its duties and functions in accordance with the forms of service as referred to in Article 7.

CHAPTER VI
INTEGRATED SERVICE CENTER

Article 25

(1) In providing services and temporary protection in the form of a safe house (shelter) for victims, PPT needs to be formed.

(2) PPT can be formed by vertical agencies in the Region, Regional Government, and/or community institutions.

(3) PPT formed by the Regional Government as referred to in paragraph (2) consists of elements of the Regional Police, SKPD in charge of Health, Education, Social Affairs, Community Empowerment, Women's Empowerment and Child Protection as well as Community Social Institutions that have the function of providing protection to women from acts of violence carried out in a networked manner.

(4) In the event that PPT does not have a safe house (shelter) as referred to in paragraph (1), victims are referred to PPT that has a safe house.

(5) PPT as referred to in paragraph (2) consists of several counseling fields including:
a. legal field;
b. health field;
c. spiritual field; and
d. psychology field.

(6) PPT as referred to in paragraph (2) is established by Regent Decree.

CHAPTER VII
INFORMATION SYSTEM AND REPORTING

Article 26

(1) The SKPD in charge of Women's Empowerment implements the information system and submits reports on the implementation of women's protection to the Regent.

(2) Further provisions regarding the information system and reporting as referred to in paragraph (1) are regulated by Regent Regulation.

CHAPTER VIII
FUNDING

Article 27

Funding for women's protection activities carried out by the Regional Government is sourced from the Regional Revenue and Expenditure Budget and/or other legitimate sources in accordance with statutory regulations.

CHAPTER IX
COMMUNITY PARTICIPATION

Article 28

(1) The community participates in the implementation of women's protection.

(2) Community participation as referred to in paragraph (1) is in the form of:
a. forming family partners at the sub-district/village level by the community;
b. forming women's protection units in community organizations;
c. conducting independent socialization of women's rights;
d. conducting prevention efforts if indications of violence against women are found in the community;
e. providing first aid to victims; and
f. reporting to the competent authorities if violence against women occurs in their environment.

(3) Community participation as referred to in paragraph (1) can be carried out by individuals, community social institutions, non-governmental organizations, educational institutions, religious institutions, the private sector, and/or mass media.

CHAPTER X
GUIDANCE AND SUPERVISION

Article 29

(1) The Regional Government conducts guidance and supervision of the implementation of women's protection.

(2) Guidance and supervision as referred to in paragraph (1) include:
a. coordination;
b. guidance;
c. education and training; and
d. monitoring and evaluation.

(3) Coordination as referred to in paragraph (2) letter a covers planning and implementation.

(4) Guidance as referred to in paragraph (2) letter b covers planning, implementation, procedures, financing, quality, control, and supervision.

(5) Education and training as referred to in paragraph (2) letter c are conducted in accordance with statutory provisions.

(6) Monitoring and evaluation as referred to in paragraph (2) letter d are conducted based on statutory regulations periodically every 6 (six) months.

CHAPTER XI
CLOSING PROVISIONS

Article 30

This Regional Regulation comes into force on the date of promulgation.

I. GENERAL.

Acts of violence against women constitute violations of human rights so that it is necessary to protect their dignity and honor and guarantee their right to life in accordance with their nature and destiny without discrimination.

In order to prevent and address violence against women in Kubu Raya Regency so that they are protected from violence, threats of violence, torture or treatment that degrades human dignity, it is necessary to establish a regional regulation on the protection of women.

Protection of women is all efforts aimed at protecting women and providing a sense of security in fulfilling their rights by providing consistent and systematic attention.

Existing laws and regulations or those related to the protection of women victims of violence have not regulated women's protection efforts in the regions, so institutional support and regulations at the regional level are needed to ensure their implementation.

This Regional Regulation regulates women's protection efforts, particularly in the form of prevention, service/handling, and empowerment of women in Kubu Raya Regency.

What is meant by efforts from the Promotive aspect is the effort to promote/socialize about acts of violence to the community, with the aim of increasing community vigilance and sensitivity to the dangers of violence threatening women.

What is meant by efforts from the Preventive aspect is efforts that prioritize prevention aspects targeting communities vulnerable to becoming victims of violence and receiving its impacts. What is meant by efforts from the Curative aspect is efforts carried out in the form of handling victims of violence who need handling according to their needs, namely health rehabilitation handling if victims suffer the impacts of violence physically or psychologically, providing assistance and law enforcement for victims who need legal assistance and enforcement, and so on.

What is meant by the Rehabilitative aspect is efforts made as an effort to recover victims of violence physically, psychologically and psychosocially.

Article 2
Letter a
What is meant by "respect and fulfillment of victims' rights" is the guarantee of fulfillment of victims' basic rights.

Letter b
What is meant by "justice" is a state where every person, both men and women, is treated equally and obtains equal opportunities to obtain access and welfare.

What is meant by "gender equality" is equality of rights, opportunities, benefits and decision-making between women and men including in entering employment opportunities both in the formal and informal sectors.

Letter c
What is meant by "non-discrimination" is attitudes and treatment toward victims without making distinctions based on age, gender, race, ethnicity, religion and between groups.

Letter d
What is meant by "the best interests of victims" is that all actions concerning victims carried out by the government, regional government, community, legislative bodies, and judicial bodies, must prioritize the best interests of victims.
