REGENT OF KEBUMEN
CENTRAL JAVA PROVINCE
REGIONAL REGULATION OF KEBUMEN REGENCY NUMBER 01 YEAR 2025
CONCERNING
IMPLEMENTATION OF PROTECTION AND EMPOWERMENT OF WOMEN BY THE GRACE OF GOD ALMIGHTY
REGENT OF KEBUMEN,

Considering: a. that women are citizens who have human rights that must be protected, respected, maintained, and may not be ignored, reduced, or deprived by anyone, so they need to receive protection guarantees from acts of violence, exploitation, and discrimination and need to be empowered so they can actualize their potential optimally;

b. that in society there are still many women who experience violence, discrimination, and injustice and often face major obstacles in accessing adequate education, health, and social protection services which have negative impacts on their welfare and self-development;

c. that in order to protect and provide means for self-actualization of women in society in Kebumen Regency, regulations are needed regarding the protection and empowerment of women;

d. that based on the considerations as referred to in letter a, letter b, and letter c, it is necessary to establish a Regional Regulation concerning the Implementation of Protection and Empowerment of Women;

Bearing in mind: 1. Article 18 paragraph (6) of the 1945 Constitution of the Republic of Indonesia;

2. Law Number 13 Year 1950 concerning the Formation of Regency Regions within Central Java Province (State Gazette of the Republic of Indonesia Year 1950 Number 42);

3. Law Number 23 Year 2014 concerning Regional Government (State Gazette of the Republic of Indonesia Year 2014 Number 244, Supplement to State Gazette of the Republic of Indonesia Number 5587) as amended several times, most recently by Law Number 6 Year 2023 concerning Stipulation of Government Regulation in Lieu of Law Number 2 Year 2022 concerning Job Creation into Law (State Gazette of the Republic of Indonesia Year 2023 Number 41, Supplement to State Gazette of the Republic of Indonesia Number 6856);

4. Law Number 11 Year 2023 concerning Central Java Province (State Gazette of the Republic of Indonesia Year 2023 Number 58, Supplement to State Gazette of the Republic of Indonesia Number 6867);

With Joint Approval of
REGIONAL HOUSE OF REPRESENTATIVES OF KEBUMEN REGENCY
and
REGENT OF KEBUMEN

DECIDES:

To Establish: REGIONAL REGULATION CONCERNING IMPLEMENTATION OF PROTECTION AND EMPOWERMENT OF WOMEN.

CHAPTER I
GENERAL PROVISIONS

Article 1

In this Regional Regulation, what is meant by:

1. Region is Kebumen Regency.
2. Regent is the Regent of Kebumen.
3. Regional Government is the Regent as an element of the Regional Government administrator who leads the implementation of government affairs that are the authority of the autonomous region.
4. Regional House of Representatives hereinafter abbreviated as DPRD is the Regional House of Representatives of Kebumen Regency.
5. Regional Apparatus is an auxiliary element of the Regent and DPRD in government affairs that are the authority of the region.
6. Implementation is the execution of something done to achieve a goal.
7. Women are persons of female sex from infants, adolescents, adults to the elderly, who have been recognized by law.
8. Protection of Women is all efforts aimed at protecting and providing a sense of security to women and fulfilling their rights through consistent, structured, and systematic attention aimed at achieving gender equality.
9. Empowerment of Women is an effort to obtain access and control over economic, political, social, and cultural resources, so that women can organize themselves and increase self-confidence to be able to play a role and participate actively in solving problems, so as to be able to build capacity and self-concept.
10. Discrimination against Women is any distinction, exclusion or restriction made on the basis of sex, which has the effect or purpose of reducing or eliminating the recognition, enjoyment or use of human rights and fundamental freedoms in the political, economic, social, cultural, civil or any other field by women, regardless of their marital status, on the basis of equality between men and women.
11. Gender Equality is an equal condition and position between men and women to obtain their rights as human beings, in accessing and controlling resources, participating in all development processes and decision-making, and obtaining benefits from development.
12. Violence is any action that results in misery or suffering physically, sexually, mentally, psychologically, including intimidation, forced eviction, threats of certain actions, coercion or deprivation of liberty, neglect and obstructing the ability to enjoy all rights and freedoms.
13. Violence Against Women is any action based on sex differences that results or may result in misery or suffering of women physically, sexually, and psychologically, including threats of certain actions, arbitrary deprivation of liberty, whether occurring in the private or public sphere.
14. Economic Violence is a form of violence in which the perpetrator controls, manipulates, or limits the victim's access to economic resources with the aim of controlling and dominating the victim, including acts of: taking full control of the victim's income, including salary, savings, or other financial assistance received by the victim, limiting or prohibiting the victim from having or using money, so that the victim is completely dependent on the perpetrator for financial needs, prohibiting or obstructing the victim from working or continuing education with the aim of limiting the victim's financial independence, using the victim's assets or property for personal gain or destroying such assets as a form of punishment, and making all financial decisions without involving the victim, so that the victim has no control over household finances.
15. Physical Violence is any act that causes pain, injury, wounds or disability to a person's body, miscarriage, fainting and/or causes death.
16. Psychological Violence is an act that causes fear, loss of self-confidence, loss of ability to act, feelings of helplessness and/or other psychological suffering to a person.
17. Sexual Violence is any act in the form of rape, indecent acts, forced contraception, forced sterilization, sexual torture, sexual exploitation, sexual slavery, electronic-based sexual violence, acts violating decency contrary to the victim's will, forced prostitution, criminal acts of trafficking in persons aimed at sexual exploitation and other forms of sexual violence explicitly stated as criminal acts of sexual violence as regulated in the provisions of laws and regulations.
18. Family is the smallest unit in society consisting of husband, wife, or husband, wife and children, or father and children, or mother and children, or blood relatives in a straight line up or down to the third degree.
19. Household is husband, wife, children, people who have family relationships due to blood relations, marriage, breastfeeding, care, and/or guardianship representation, and/or domestic workers and reside in that household.
20. Victim is a woman who experiences misery and/or suffering either directly or indirectly as a result of violence.
21. Women's Rights are a set of rights inherent in a woman or group of people not to experience discrimination, misery or suffering either directly or indirectly committed by state apparatus or by the state or by regional government apparatus or individuals which are natural rights given by God Almighty.
22. Prevention is all efforts to prevent acts of violence, exploitation and discrimination against women and recurrence of acts of violence, exploitation and discrimination against victims.
23. Service is all efforts and actions to fulfill the rights of women victims of violence, exploitation and discrimination against women which include complaint services, health, social rehabilitation, legal assistance and enforcement, repatriation, social reintegration, and restitution.
24. Victim Recovery is all efforts and actions to strengthen the capacity of women victims of violence, exploitation and discrimination physically, psychologically, legally, socially, politically and economically so as to obtain a dignified and prosperous life.
25. Integrated Service is the implementation of integrated, multi-aspect, cross-functional and cross-sectoral services for victims, victim families, and/or witnesses of criminal acts of violence, exploitation and discrimination.
26. Complaint is a series of reporting actions carried out by victims and/or companions to the Integrated Service Center organizers to follow up on reports of acts of violence against victims of violence.
27. Health Rehabilitation is the recovery of victims of violence from health problems they suffer.
28. Social Rehabilitation is a service aimed at restoring the psychological condition and developing the ability of a person experiencing social dysfunction to be able to carry out their social functions properly.
29. Legal Assistance is legal services provided by companions, law enforcement officers which include providing legal consultation, exercising power, representing, accompanying, defending and taking other legal actions for victims of violence.
30. Repatriation is an effort to return victims of violence from the receiving area/area where violence occurred to the area of origin or family.
31. Social Reintegration is an effort to reunite victims of violence with family, community, institutions or other social environments that can provide protection.
32. Exploitation is an action with or without the victim's consent which includes but is not limited to prostitution, forced labor or services, slavery or practices similar to slavery, oppression, extortion, physical, sexual, reproductive organ utilization, or unlawfully transferring or transplanting organs and/or body tissues or utilizing the labor or ability of a person by another party to obtain material or immaterial benefits.
33. Discrimination as referred to is any restriction, harassment, or exclusion that is directly or indirectly based on human differentiation on the basis of religion, ethnicity, race, ethnic group, group, class, social status, economic status, sex, language, political beliefs, which results in reduction, deviation or elimination of recognition, implementation or use of human rights and basic freedoms in life both individually and collectively in the political, economic, legal, social, cultural fields, and other aspects of life.
34. Regional Technical Implementation Unit for Protection of Women and Children hereinafter abbreviated as UPTD PPA is a technical operational implementation unit in a work unit that organizes government affairs in the field of women's empowerment and child protection, which functions as an organizer of integrated services for women and children who experience violence, discrimination, and other problems.
35. Village/Sub-district Women and Children Protection Group hereinafter abbreviated as KPPAD/KPPAK is a community-based women's and children's protection institution that is domiciled and carries out women's and children's protection work in the village/sub-district area according to their place of residence.

Article 2

The Implementation of Protection and Empowerment of Women is based on:
a. respect for human rights;
b. non-discrimination;
c. gender justice and gender equality, and
d. benefit.

Article 3

(1) The Implementation of Protection and Empowerment of Women is intended to realize protection for women from acts of violence, exploitation and discrimination and increase the capacity and welfare of Women in various aspects of life.

(2) The implementation of protection and empowerment of women aims to:
a. prevent all forms of violence, exploitation, and discrimination against women;
b. provide services and recovery for victims;
c. fulfill the special needs of women; and
d. improve the quality of life of women.

(3) The scope of this Regional Regulation includes:
a. women's rights;
b. women's protection efforts;
c. women's empowerment;
d. obligations and responsibilities of the Regional Government;
e. institutions;
f. coordination and cooperation;
g. participation;
h. monitoring, evaluation, and reporting;
i. awards; and
j. funding.

CHAPTER II
WOMEN'S RIGHTS

Part One
Basic Rights of Women

Article 4

(1) Every person, both women and men, has the right and responsibility to create an environment that supports gender equality and Women's Empowerment in various aspects of life including rights for every mother.

(2) Every woman has the right:
a. to life;
b. to have a family and continue offspring;
c. to develop themselves;
d. to obtain justice;
e. to personal freedom;
f. to a sense of security;
g. to welfare; and
h. to participate in government.

(3) The right to life as referred to in paragraph (2) letter a includes:
a. the right to maintain life and improve their standard of living;
b. the right to obtain a sense of tranquility, security, peace, happiness, physical and spiritual welfare; and
c. the right to a good and healthy environment.

(4) The right to have a family and continue offspring as referred to in paragraph (2) letter b is the right to form a family and continue offspring through a legal marriage according to religion and the provisions of laws and regulations.

(5) The right to develop themselves as referred to in paragraph (2) letter c includes:
a. the right to women's basic needs to grow and develop properly;
b. the right to protection for personal development, to obtain education, educate themselves and improve quality of life to become faithful, pious, responsible, noble, happy, and prosperous human beings in accordance with human rights;
c. the right to develop and benefit from science and technology, arts and culture in accordance with human dignity for their personal welfare, nation, and humanity;
d. the right to communicate and obtain information needed to develop their personality and social environment in accordance with religious, social, and legal norms;
e. the right to seek, obtain, own, store, process, and convey information using all types of available means in accordance with religious, social, and legal norms;
f. the right to fight for their development rights, both personally and collectively, to build society, nation, and state in accordance with religious, social, and legal norms;
g. the right to carry out social work in accordance with religious, social, and legal norms;
h. the right to participate in planning and implementing government policies in accordance with the provisions of laws and regulations;
i. the right to organize and form organizations in accordance with the provisions of laws and regulations; and
j. the right to organize education and teaching in accordance with religious, social, and legal norms.

(6) The right to obtain justice as referred to in paragraph (2) letter d includes:
a. the right to receive equal treatment without discrimination;
b. the right to obtain justice;
c. the right to submit applications, complaints, and lawsuits, both in criminal, civil, and administrative cases; and
d. the right to be tried through a free and impartial judicial process, in accordance with procedural law that guarantees objective examination by honest and fair judges to obtain a just and correct decision.

(7) The right to personal freedom as referred to in paragraph (2) letter e includes:
a. the right not to be enslaved or enslaved, trafficked, and all acts with similar purposes;
b. the right to personal integrity, both spiritual and physical;
c. the right not to be the object of research without their consent; and
d. the right to freely express and convey opinions with due regard to religious values, decency, order, public interest, and national integrity.

(8) The right to a sense of security as referred to in paragraph (2) letter f includes:
a. the right to protection of personal self, family, honor, dignity, and property rights;
b. the right to recognition before the law as a personal human being; and
c. the right to be free from threats to do or not do something that violates religious, social, and legal norms.

(9) The right to welfare as referred to in paragraph (2) letter g includes:
a. the right to have welfare, either alone or together; and
b. the right to work and receive fair wages in accordance with their work that is comparable and commensurate with human dignity in accordance with the provisions of laws and regulations.

(10) The right to participate in government as referred to in paragraph (2) letter h includes:
a. the right to be elected and vote in general elections, regional head elections, and/or other political position elections based on equal rights through direct, general, free, secret, honest and fair voting according to applicable laws and regulations;
b. the right to have a career in government; and
c. the right to convey aspirations directly or through intermediaries in government.

Part Two
Rights of Women with Disabilities

Article 5

(1) Every woman, including persons with disabilities and members of other social inclusion groups, has the right to obtain protection and empowerment in various aspects of life to ensure equality and social justice.

(2) The rights as referred to in paragraph (1) include:
a. accessing inclusive education at all units, paths, types, and levels of education without discrimination, with support for inclusive facilities and infrastructure;
b. obtaining decent work and livelihood according to their capacity, skills, and special needs, as well as a supportive work environment;
c. receiving equal treatment to play an active role in development and enjoy the results of development fairly;
d. obtaining accessibility in various fields of life, including infrastructure, technology, transportation, public services, and information, to increase independence and social participation;
e. receiving equal treatment before the law, including legal protection that is sensitive to the needs of vulnerable groups;
f. obtaining rehabilitation services, social assistance, and other support to improve welfare and social independence; and
g. having equal opportunities in developing talents, abilities, and social life without structural or cultural barriers.

(3) The Government, society, and business world must play an active role in realizing equality for women with disabilities and other social inclusion groups by providing inclusive and sustainable policies, programs, and services.

Part Three
Rights and Forms of Violence, Exploitation, and Discrimination

Article 6

(1) Every Woman victim of Violence, Exploitation, and Discrimination has:
a. the right to handling;
b. the right to protection; and
c. the right to recovery.

(2) The right to handling as referred to in paragraph (1) letter a includes:
a. the right to information on all processes and results of Handling, Protection, and Recovery;
b. the right to obtain handling result documents;
c. the right to legal services;
d. the right to psychological strengthening;
e. the right to health services including examination, treatment, and medical care;
f. the right to services and facilities according to the special needs of victims; and
g. the right to deletion of sexually charged content for cases of sexual violence using electronic media.

(3) The right to protection as referred to in paragraph (1) letter b includes:
a. provision of information regarding rights and facilitation of protection;
b. provision of access to information on protection implementation;
c. protection from threats or violence by perpetrators and other parties and recurrence of violence;
d. protection of identity confidentiality;
e. protection from attitudes and behavior of law enforcement officers that demean victims;
f. protection from job loss, job transfer, education, or political access; and
g. protection of victims and/or reporters from criminal prosecution or civil lawsuits for criminal acts of sexual violence that have been reported;

(4) The right to recovery as referred to in paragraph (1) letter c includes:
a. medical rehabilitation;
b. mental and social rehabilitation;
c. social empowerment;
d. restitution and/or compensation;
e. social reintegration; and
f. obtaining skills training for economic improvement for vulnerable women.

Article 7

(1) Forms of violence as referred to in Article 6 paragraph (1) include:
a. Physical violence;
b. Psychological violence;
c. Sexual violence; and
d. Economic violence.

(2) Forms of exploitation as referred to in Article 6 paragraph (1) include:
a. utilizing women's labor by providing inadequate wages, forcing excessive working hours, or not providing safe and healthy working conditions;
b. utilizing women for sexual purposes, by coercion, deception or pressure, including human trafficking for prostitution or pornography production; and
c. utilizing women unfairly in economic transactions, such as providing loans with very high interest or manipulating markets for personal gain.

(3) Forms of discrimination referred to in Article 6 paragraph (1) include:
a. deprivation of life;
b. persecution;
c. rape;
d. indecent acts;
e. theft with violence; and
f. or deprivation of liberty

CHAPTER III
WOMEN'S PROTECTION EFFORTS

Part One
General

Article 8

The implementation of women's protection includes efforts to:
a. prevent violence, exploitation, and discrimination;
b. serve victims of violence, exploitation and discrimination;
c. fulfill the special needs of women; and
d. improve family quality.

Part Two
Prevention of Violence, Exploitation and Discrimination

Article 9

(1) The Regional Government makes integrated efforts to prevent violence, exploitation and discrimination against women in the Region.

(2) Prevention of violence, exploitation and discrimination against women is intended to create a safe environment for women and to reduce vulnerability to violence, exploitation and discrimination to vulnerable women's groups.

(3) Vulnerable women's groups as referred to in paragraph (2) include:
a. women in disaster and social conflict situations;
b. women with disabilities;
c. women with Human Immunodeficiency Virus/Acquired Immune Deficiency Syndrome (HIV/AIDS);
d. migrant women workers;
e. girls;
f. women heads of households;
g. domestic women workers;
h. home-based women workers;
i. elderly women;
j. informal women workers;
k. women in situations of intolerance; and
l. other vulnerable women's groups.

(4) Prevention efforts as referred to in paragraph (1) are implemented by means including:
a. coordinating, integrating, synchronizing prevention of acts of violence, exploitation and discrimination based on partnership patterns;
b. socializing laws and regulations related to the protection of women's rights.
c. building and providing a complete and easily accessible information system for prevention of violence, exploitation and discrimination;
d. providing easy access to obtain civil rights, education, health, employment, training and funding, welfare improvement and services;
e. developing communication media, information, education, and public campaigns through media;
f. developing educational materials and curricula;
g. developing safe transportation systems and public spaces;
h. building early detection systems, security and integrated complaint services in industrial areas, companies, residential areas, educational environments, Islamic boarding schools and other public spaces;
i. providing consultation services for families, prospective brides, students, santri, youth, university students and vulnerable groups;
j. encouraging behavioral change in parents and society;
k. developing policies related to safe and women-friendly regencies, sub-districts, schools, Islamic boarding schools, and universities integrated with regional spatial planning policies;
l. forming and developing cadres, communities, and groups from among youth, students, santri, university students, journalists, social media, educators, and religious leaders for prevention;
m. conducting education and advocacy to owners, managers and/or users of social media;
n. developing protection systems and special support for vulnerable women's groups;
o. raising awareness among perpetrators;
p. increasing the capacity of Regional Apparatus, government agencies, educational institutions, religious institutions, health service provider institutions, professional institutions, business world, service institutions, Islamic boarding schools, youth organizations, legal aid organizations, media, religious leaders, community leaders and communities; and
q. forming cooperation networks in prevention efforts from acts of violence, exploitation and discrimination with law enforcement officers, government agencies, Regional Apparatus, community institutions, educational institutions, community leaders, religious institutions, health service provider institutions, professional institutions, business world, service institutions, Islamic boarding schools, youth organizations, legal aid organizations, communities, and media.

(5) Prevention efforts as referred to in paragraph (4) may involve Regional Apparatus, agencies, community institutions, educational institutions, community leaders, family learning centers, religious institutions, health service provider institutions, professional institutions, business world, service institutions, Islamic boarding schools, youth organizations, legal aid organizations, communities, and media.

(6) Further provisions regarding efforts to prevent violence, exploitation, and discrimination as referred to in paragraph (4) are regulated in a Regent Regulation.

Article 10

Prevention of violence, exploitation, and discrimination against women is carried out in an integrated and integrated manner coordinated by the Regional Apparatus that organizes government affairs in the field of women's empowerment and child protection.

Part Three
Services for Victims of Violence, Exploitation, and Discrimination

Article 11

(1) Services are implemented to fulfill the rights of women victims carried out with the following principles:
a. respect for human rights;
b. non-discrimination;
c. confidentiality;
d. empathy;
e. inclusion;
f. empowerment;
g. victim-based decisions;
h. honesty;
i. professionalism;
j. integration; and
k. sustainability.

(2) The implementation of services for victims is carried out in an integrated manner by UPTD PPA.

(3) In implementing services as referred to in paragraph (1), UPTD PPA may cooperate with:
a. community health centers, hospitals, and other health service facilities;
b. technical implementation units that handle affairs in the social field;
c. detention centers, correctional institutions, and correctional centers;
d. police;
e. prosecutors;
f. courts;
g. technical implementation units of agencies that organize protection of migrant workers;
h. provincial regional offices of ministries that organize government affairs in the field of religion;
i. regional offices and technical implementation units of ministries that organize government affairs in the field of law and human rights;
j. social welfare organizing institutions;
k. community-based service provider institutions;
l. universities;
m. mass media;
n. religious organizations; and
o. other institutions.

Article 12

(1) Forms of services for victims of violence, exploitation, and discrimination include:
a. receiving reports and outreach to Victims;
b. providing information about Victim Rights;
c. facilitating the provision of health services;
d. facilitating the provision of psychological strengthening services;
e. facilitating the provision of psychosocial services, social rehabilitation, and social reintegration;
f. providing legal services;
g. identifying economic empowerment needs;
h. identifying temporary shelter needs for Victims and Victim Families that need to be immediately fulfilled;
i. facilitating the needs of Victims with Disabilities;
j. coordinating and cooperating on the fulfillment of Victim Rights with other institutions; and
k. monitoring the fulfillment of Victim Rights by law enforcement officers during the judicial process.

(2) Implementation of forms of services for victims of violence, exploitation and discrimination as referred to in paragraph (1) is in accordance with the provisions of laws and regulations.

Part Four
Fulfillment of Special Needs of Women

Article 13

(1) Fulfillment of special needs of women is related to the protection of women from all things that can threaten the safety and/or health of women's reproductive organs.

(2) Fulfillment of special needs as referred to in paragraph (1) is in the form of:
a. health services especially family planning services, pregnancy, childbirth, postpartum, breastfeeding and menstruation;
b. fulfillment of the right to obtain paid leave including maternity leave; and
c. provision of special facilities and infrastructure for women.

Part Five
Improving Family Quality

Article 14

(1) Implementation of improving family quality aims to:
a. integrate policies, programs, and development activities whose targets are aimed at improving family quality;
b. strengthen and develop family quality improvement service provider institutions; and
c. provide standardized family quality improvement services.

(2) Provisions regarding improving family quality are implemented in accordance with the provisions of laws and regulations.

CHAPTER IV
WOMEN'S EMPOWERMENT

Part One
General

Article 15

(1) Women's empowerment is carried out in an effort to improve the quality of life of women.

(2) In order to improve the quality of life of women as referred to in paragraph (1), the Regional Government is responsible for:
a. respecting and guaranteeing women's rights without discrimination in accordance with human dignity;
b. implementing gender mainstreaming in the Region;
c. increasing women's empowerment in roles, quality, and abilities in the fields of:
1. health;
2. education;
3. economy;
4. law;
5. employment;
6. politics;
7. government;
8. social;
9. culture; and
10. environment.

Part Two
Increasing Women's Empowerment in the Health Field

Article 16

(1) The implementation of Women's Empowerment in the health field as referred to in Article 15 paragraph (2) letter c number 1 is carried out by the Regional Apparatus that organizes government affairs in the health field.

(2) Forms of empowerment in the health field as referred to in paragraph (1) are in the form of health services that are healthy, safe, and free from coercion and/or violence, including for persons with disabilities, the elderly, and women with psychological problems.

(3) Health services as referred to in paragraph (2) include:
a. health services according to life cycles starting from women's health services during infancy and childhood, adolescence, adulthood and old age;
b. maternal health services from pre-pregnancy, pregnancy, childbirth and postpartum periods;
c. contraceptive and sexual health services;
d. reproductive health services;
e. health services in family planning;
f. nutrition services;
g. dental and oral health services;
h. vision and hearing health services;
i. mental health services;
j. communicable disease control services and non-communicable disease control services;
k. family health services;
l. school health services;
m. occupational health services;
n. sports health services;
o. environmental health services;
p. dimensional health services;
q. health services in disasters;
r. blood services;
s. organ and/or body tissue transplantation services, cell-based and/or stem cell therapy, and reconstructive and aesthetic plastic surgery;
t. traditional health services;
u. other health services;
v. services and provision of adequate lactation rooms at every Regional Government agency, public facilities and industry; and
w. health services are provided taking into account the special needs of persons with disabilities, the elderly, and women with psychological problems to ensure accessibility and equal quality of service.

(4) Health services as referred to in paragraph (2) are implemented through promotive, preventive, curative, and rehabilitative approaches.

Part Three
Increasing Women's Empowerment in the Education Field

Article 17

(1) The implementation of Women's Empowerment in the education field as referred to in Article 15 paragraph (2) letter c number 2 is carried out by the Regional Apparatus that organizes government affairs in the education field in the form of organizing formal and non-formal education.

(2) Non-formal education as referred to in paragraph (1) aims to improve women's abilities.

Part Four
Increasing Women's Empowerment in the Economic Field

Article 18

The implementation of women's empowerment in the economic field as referred to in Article 15 paragraph (2) letter c number 3 is carried out by the Regional Apparatus that organizes government affairs in the field of micro, small and medium enterprises and related agencies in the form of:
a. facilitation of formation and strengthening of productive economic business groups; and
b. facilitation of marketing network development.

Part Five
Increasing Women's Empowerment in the Legal Field

Article 19

The implementation of Women's Empowerment in the legal field as referred to in Article 15 paragraph (2) letter c number 4 is carried out by the work unit that handles law in the form of:
a. increasing awareness and knowledge in the legal field through communication, information, and education services; and
b. facilitation of access and legal consultation services.

Part Six
Increasing Women's Empowerment in the Employment Field

Article 20

The implementation of Women's Empowerment in the employment field as referred to in Article 15 paragraph (2) letter c number 5 is carried out by the Regional Apparatus that organizes government affairs in the field of labor and related agencies in the form of:
a. providing skills and job training;
b. women have equal opportunities without discrimination to obtain employment; and
c. employers who use women workers provide access and skills training.

Part Seven
Increasing Women's Empowerment in the Political and Government Fields

Article 21

(1) The implementation of Women's Empowerment in the political and government fields as referred to in Article 15 paragraph (2) letter c numbers 6 and 7 is carried out by the Regional Apparatus that organizes government affairs in the field of national unity and politics, Regional Apparatus and related agencies in the form of:
a. developing women's capacity in the political field;
b. self-development through organizations to associate, assemble, express opinions;
c. involving women in public decision-making;
d. providing access for women to hold public positions; and
e. participation in general elections, regional head elections and other political positions in the Region.

(2) The Regional Government and/or political parties are responsible for providing political education for women.

Part Eight
Increasing Women's Empowerment in the Social Field

Article 22

The implementation of Women's Empowerment in the social field as referred to in Article 15 paragraph (2) letter c number 8 is carried out by the Regional Apparatus that organizes government affairs in the social field and related agencies in the form of:
a. awareness raising and capacity building efforts in the social field;
b. providing and developing access for women to participate in the social field;
c. improving women's competence through training and social education for women;
d. involving women in social activities; and
e. developing forums or women's institutions in the social field.

Part Nine
Increasing Women's Empowerment in the Cultural Field

Article 23

The implementation of Women's Empowerment in the cultural field as referred to in Article 15 paragraph (2) letter c number 9 is carried out by the Regional Apparatus that organizes government affairs in the cultural field and related agencies in the form of:
a. awareness raising and capacity building efforts in the cultural field;
b. providing and developing access for women to participate in the cultural field;
c. improving women's competence through training and cultural literacy education for women;
d. involving women in cultural activities; and
e. developing forums or women's institutions in the cultural field.

Part Ten
Increasing Women's Empowerment in the Environmental Field

Article 24

The implementation of Women's Empowerment in the environmental field as referred to in Article 15 paragraph (2) letter c number 10 is carried out by the Regional Apparatus that organizes government affairs in the environmental field and related agencies in the form of:
a. raising awareness and forming environmentally conscious women's behavior;
b. increasing women's capacity and competence through environmental training and education;
c. providing and developing women's access to participate in the environmental field;
d. involving women in environmental activities; and
e. developing forums or women's institutions in the environmental field.

CHAPTER V
OBLIGATIONS AND RESPONSIBILITIES OF THE REGIONAL GOVERNMENT

Article 25

The obligations of the Regional Government in implementing protection and empowerment of women are as follows:
a. guaranteeing the implementation of fulfillment of women's rights and providing a guarantee of security from acts of violence, exploitation and discrimination against women in the Region;
b. establishing policies, programs, activities for implementing protection and empowerment of women and guarantees of victim handling to eliminate all forms of acts of violence, exploitation, and discrimination against women in the Region; and
c. providing guidance and supervision in efforts to implement protection and empowerment of women carried out by the Regional Apparatus that organizes government affairs in the field of women's empowerment.

Article 26

The Regional Government is responsible for:
a. implementing policies, programs, activities for implementing protection and empowerment of women to implement the fulfillment of women's rights;
b. facilitating facilities and infrastructure for implementing protection and empowerment of women; and
c. applying service standards for implementing protection and empowerment of women in accordance with the provisions of laws and regulations.

CHAPTER VI
INSTITUTIONS

Part One
General

Article 27

In order to implement protection and empowerment of women, the Regional Government forms:
a. UPTD PPA;
b. Women and Children Protection Forum;
c. Village/Sub-district Women and Children Protection Group; and
d. Other women's protection and empowerment institutions in accordance with the provisions of laws and regulations.

Part Two
UPTD PPA

Article 28

(1) The Regional Government in an effort to provide and organize handling of protection services for women victims of violence, exploitation and acts of violence forms UPTD PPA.

(2) UPTD PPA as referred to in paragraph (1) is under and responsible to the head of the Regional Apparatus that organizes government affairs in the field of women's empowerment and child protection.

(3) Further provisions regarding the formation, main tasks and functions and management of UPTD PPA as referred to in paragraph (1) are regulated by Regent Regulation.

Part Three
Women and Children Protection Forum

Article 29

(1) The Regional Government coordinates the handling of protection services for women victims of violence, exploitation, and discrimination at the sub-district level by forming a women and children protection forum.

(2) Membership of the women and children protection forum as referred to in paragraph (1) consists of elements of the Sub-district, Sector Police, Military District Command, Community Health Center, Office of Religious Affairs and other elements.

Part Four
Village/Sub-district Women and Children Protection Group

Article 30

(1) The Regional Government in an effort to provide and organize handling of protection services for women victims of violence, exploitation and discrimination forms KPPAD/KPPAK.

(2) KPPAD/KPPAK as referred to in paragraph (1) is under and responsible to the Regional Apparatus that carries out government affairs in the field of women's empowerment and child protection through the Village Head/Sub-district Head with the knowledge of the local Sub-district Head.

(3) KPPAD/KPPAK as referred to in paragraph (2) has the task of:
a. conducting advocacy, mediation, socialization, and facilitation of the need for fulfillment of women's and children's rights, and the right of women's and children's participation in development;
b. collecting data and information related to the protection of women and children;
c. receiving complaints from the community;
d. conducting cooperation and coordination in implementing the fulfillment of women's and children's rights in accordance with applicable laws and regulations; and
e. making referrals in cases that cannot be resolved by KPPAD/KPPAK to UPTD PPA.

(4) Further provisions regarding the formation, main tasks and functions and management of KPPAD/KPPAK as referred to in paragraph (1) are regulated in a Regent Regulation.

CHAPTER VII
COORDINATION AND COOPERATION

Part One
Coordination

Article 31

In order to implement protection and empowerment of women, the Regional Government carries out coordination with the central government, provincial government, other regency/city governments, related agencies, law enforcement institutions, service institutions, educational institutions, religious institutions, community social institutions, Islamic boarding schools, professional organizations, legal aid organizations, youth organizations, broadcasting supervisory institutions, media, communities, professional institutions, business world and other stakeholders.

Part Two
Cooperation

Article 32

(1) In order to implement protection and empowerment of women, the Regional Government may conduct cooperation.

(2) Cooperation as referred to in paragraph (1) is implemented in accordance with the provisions of laws and regulations.

CHAPTER VIII
PARTICIPATION

Article 33

(1) Every person and/or legal entity may participate in the implementation of protection and empowerment of women in the Region.

(2) Participation as referred to in paragraph (1) is carried out by individuals, groups or community social organizations, non-governmental organizations, educational institutions, law enforcement institutions, service institutions, religious institutions, Islamic boarding schools, professional organizations, legal aid organizations, youth organizations, broadcasting supervisory institutions, media, communities, professional institutions, business world and other related stakeholders.

(3) Participation as referred to in paragraph (1) may be in the form of:
a. providing information or reporting to the competent authority if they know of acts of violence, exploitation and discrimination against women;
b. taking preventive actions against violence against women; and
c. conducting skills training.

CHAPTER IX
MONITORING, EVALUATION, AND REPORTING

Part One
Monitoring

Article 34

(1) To ensure synergy, continuity and effectiveness in implementing policies, programs and activities for implementing protection and empowerment of women, the Regional Government conducts monitoring.

(2) Monitoring as referred to in paragraph (1) is intended to determine developments and obstacles in implementing policies, programs and activities for implementing protection and empowerment of women in the Region.

(3) Monitoring as referred to in paragraph (2) is carried out periodically starting from planning to policies, programs and activities for implementing protection and empowerment of women for the current year.

Part Two
Evaluation

Article 35

(1) Evaluation of the implementation of policies, programs and activities for protection and empowerment of women is carried out by the Regional Apparatus that organizes government affairs in the field of women's empowerment periodically.

(2) The results of the evaluation of the implementation of policies, programs and activities for implementing protection and empowerment of women are used as input material for the preparation of policies, programs and activities.

Part Three
Reporting

Article 36

(1) To improve coordination of services for women in the Region, the Regional Apparatus that organizes government affairs in the field of women's empowerment submits reports to the Regent.

(2) Reporting as referred to in paragraph (1) includes the implementation of handling protection and empowerment of women problems in the Region.

CHAPTER X
AWARDS

Article 37

(1) The Regent gives awards to people, educational institutions, religious institutions, health service provider institutions, professional institutions and business world, service institutions, Islamic boarding schools, youth organizations, legal aid organizations, communities, and media that have merit in efforts to implement protection and empowerment of women;

(2) Further provisions regarding procedures for giving awards are regulated in a Regent Regulation.

CHAPTER XI
FUNDING

Article 38

Funding for the implementation of protection and empowerment of women in the Region comes from:
a. Regional Revenue and Expenditure Budget; and/or
b. other legal and non-binding sources in accordance with the provisions of laws and regulations.

CHAPTER XII

Implementing regulations of this Regional Regulation must be established no later than 1 (one) year from the promulgation of this Regional Regulation.

This Regional Regulation comes into force on the date of promulgation.

I. GENERAL

Women are an inseparable part of citizens who have human rights that must be safeguarded and fulfilled without exception. However, various problems such as violence, exploitation, and discrimination are still often experienced by women in Kebumen Regency. In addition, access to education, health, employment services, and participation in development is still limited, which causes gender inequality and impacts the low quality of life of women. This Regional Regulation is prepared as a manifestation of the Regional Government's responsibility in providing guarantees for the protection of women's rights and creating a fair, inclusive system that sides with vulnerable groups. This Regional Regulation aims to prevent acts of violence, exploitation, and discrimination against women, provide services and recovery for victims, fulfill the special needs of women, and improve the welfare and quality of life of women comprehensively and sustainably.

It is hoped that through this Regional Regulation it can become a comprehensive legal basis to strengthen protection efforts and encourage women's empowerment so that they are able to be empowered and independent in all aspects of life.
