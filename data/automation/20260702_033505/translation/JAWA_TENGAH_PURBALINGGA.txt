CHAPTER I
GENERAL PROVISIONS
Article 1

In this Regional Regulation, what is meant by:
1. Region is Purbalingga Regency.
2. Regional Government is the Regional Head as an element of regional government administration who leads the implementation of government affairs that are the authority of autonomous regions.
3. Regent is the Regent of Purbalingga.
4. Sub-district is part of the Regency area led by a Sub-district Head.
5. Sub-district Head is the leader of the Sub-district who is under and responsible to the Regent through the Regional Secretary.
6. Regional Apparatus is the Regional Apparatus that administers affairs in the Field of Women's Empowerment and Child Protection.
7. Child is a person who has not reached the age of 18 (eighteen) years, including children still in the womb.
8. Gender is a concept that refers to the roles and responsibilities of men and women that occur as a result of and can be changed by the social and cultural conditions of society.
9. Protection is all efforts aimed at providing a sense of security and fulfilling the rights of victims carried out by family, integrated services, advocates, social institutions, police, prosecutors, courts, or other parties either temporarily or based on court decisions.
10. Child Protection is all activities to guarantee and protect children and their rights so that they can live, grow, develop, and participate optimally in accordance with human dignity, and receive protection from violence and discrimination.
11. Implementation of Protection is a series of activities aimed at preventing violence, providing protection and recovery services and social reintegration, conducting coordination and cooperation, and increasing community participation carried out by the Integrated Service Center.
12. Gender-Based Violence is any form of restriction, exclusion, differentiation and all forms of treatment carried out on the basis of gender and aimed at reducing, eliminating the recognition, enjoyment or use of human rights, the consequences of which include but are not limited to physical, sexual, psychological and economic violence.
13. Violence Against Children is any form of restriction, differentiation, exclusion and all forms of treatment carried out against children, the consequences of which include but are not limited to physical, sexual, psychological, and economic violence.
14. Victims of Gender-Based Violence are people who because of their gender experience physical, psychological, economic, social, sexual suffering, and other losses resulting from state policies, acts of violence and/or threats of violence within the household and community.
15. Child Victims of Violence are children who receive harsh treatment both physically, psychologically, economically, socially, sexually, and other losses resulting from state policies, acts of violence and/or threats of violence within the household and community.
16. Victim Recovery is all efforts to strengthen victims of gender-based violence and children to be more empowered, both physically, psychologically, socially, economically and sexually.
17. Companion is a Social Worker who has professional competence in their field.
18. Assistance is all actions in the form of counseling, psychological therapy, advocacy, and spiritual guidance, for the purpose of strengthening victims of gender-based violence and children to resolve the problems they face.
19. Social Reintegration is the process of preparing victims and communities that support the reunification of victims into the family environment or family substitutes that can provide protection and fulfillment of victim needs.
20. Safe House is a temporary residence used to provide protection for victims of gender-based violence and children in accordance with determined operational standards.
21. Regional Action Plan is the foundation and guidance for regional apparatus, vertical agencies, and the community in carrying out activities for implementing protection for victims of gender-based violence and children.
22. Integrated Service Center for Protection of Victims of Gender-Based Violence and Children hereinafter abbreviated as PPT PKBGA is a special service place for victims of gender-based violence and children carried out cross-functionally and cross-sectorally as one unit of implementation so that victims receive maximum service in an integrated and comprehensive manner.
23. Sub-district Level Complaint Service Unit hereinafter abbreviated as UPPTK is a special service place for victims of gender-based violence and children located at the sub-district level so that victims receive maximum service in an integrated and comprehensive manner.

CHAPTER II
PRINCIPLES AND OBJECTIVES

Article 2

The Principles of Implementation of Protection for Victims of Gender-Based Violence and Children are:
a. non-discrimination;
b. best interests of victims;
c. justice and gender equality;
d. victim protection;
e. survival and development of children;
f. respect for children's opinions;
g. openness;
h. integration;
i. empowering; and
j. victim confidentiality.

Article 3

The Objectives of Implementation of Protection for Victims of Gender-Based Violence and Children are to:
a. prevent all forms of gender-based violence and violence against children, occurring within the household and/or community;
b. provide protection in the form of complaint services, health services, social rehabilitation services, legal aid and enforcement services, and repatriation and social reintegration services; and
c. seek to increase community participation.

CHAPTER III
FORMS OF VIOLENCE

Article 4

Forms of violence are:
a. physical violence;
b. psychological violence;
c. sexual violence;
d. neglect; and/or
e. other violence.

CHAPTER IV
IMPLEMENTATION OF PROTECTION

Part One
General

Article 5

(1) Implementation of Protection for Victims of Gender-Based Violence and Children is carried out by:
a. PPT PKBGA; and
b. UPPTK.
(2) Principles in implementing protection consist of:
a. fast, safe, and empathetic;
b. guarantee of confidentiality;
c. easily accessible; and
d. no fees charged.

Part Two
PPT PKBGA

Article 6

(1) PPT PKBGA as referred to in Article 5 paragraph (1) letter a is established by the Regent in accordance with Legislation.
(2) Membership of PPT PKBGA as referred to in paragraph (1) consists of:
a. elements of Regional Government;
b. vertical agencies;
c. non-governmental institutions;
d. health service institutions;
e. law enforcement apparatus;
f. professional personnel;
g. volunteer companions;
h. social workers;
i. clergy; and
j. social rehabilitation services.
(3) Membership of the PPT PKBGA Team as referred to in paragraph (2) is determined by Regent's Decree.

Article 7

Implementation of PPT PKBGA is coordinated by the Regional Apparatus.

Article 8

Technical and integrated service mechanisms are regulated by Regent's Regulation.

Article 9

PPT PKBGA as referred to in Article 5 paragraph (1) letter a has the tasks of:
a. seeking prevention;
b. providing complaint services;
c. providing health services;
d. providing social rehabilitation services;
e. providing legal aid and enforcement services;
f. providing repatriation and social reintegration services;
g. conducting coordination and cooperation;
h. seeking to increase community participation; and
i. conducting monitoring and reporting.

Paragraph 1
Prevention Efforts

Article 10

Prevention Efforts as referred to in Article 9 letter a include:
a. organizing communication, information, and education about gender-based violence and children;
b. socializing legislation related to gender-based violence and children.

Paragraph 2
Complaint Services

Article 11

Complaint Services as referred to in Article 9 letter b include:
a. direct complaint services;
b. complaint services by telephone;
c. complaint services by letter;
d. complaint services from referrals;
e. victim outreach services; and
f. complaint services through social media managed by PPT PKBGA and UPPTK.

Paragraph 3
Health Services

Article 12

Health Services as referred to in Article 9 letter c include:
a. medical services;
b. psychiatric medical services; and
c. medicolegal services, including visum et repertum (VER), visum et psikiatrum (VEP), and deoxyribonucleic acid (DNA) identification.

Paragraph 4
Social Rehabilitation Services

Article 13

(1) Social Rehabilitation Services as referred to in Article 9 letter d include:
a. initial counseling services;
b. follow-up counseling services;
c. provision of safe houses; and
d. spiritual guidance services.
(2) Social Rehabilitation is carried out in an integrated manner.

Paragraph 5
Legal Aid and Enforcement Services

Article 14

(1) Legal Aid and Enforcement Services as referred to in Article 9 letter e consist of:
a. legal aid services including litigation legal assistance and non-litigation legal assistance;
b. law enforcement services including law enforcement at the police level, law enforcement at the prosecutor's level, and law enforcement at the court level;
(2) Law enforcement services at the police level as referred to in paragraph (1) letter b include law enforcement in the investigation and/or inquiry process;
(3) Law enforcement services at the prosecutor's level as referred to in paragraph (1) letter b include law enforcement in the pre-prosecution and prosecution process; and
(4) Law enforcement services at the court level as referred to in paragraph (1) letter b include law enforcement at the pre-trial, trial, and sentencing stages.

Paragraph 6
Repatriation and Social Reintegration Services

Article 15

(1) Repatriation Services as referred to in Article 9 letter f include:
a. repatriation of victims of gender-based violence and children;
b. repatriation of victims of trafficking in persons.
(2) Social Reintegration Services as referred to in Article 9 letter f include:
a. reintegration with family;
b. reintegration with substitute family;
c. reintegration with the environment; and
d. educational reintegration.

Paragraph 7
Coordination and Cooperation

Article 16

Coordination and cooperation as referred to in Article 9 letter g include:
a. coordinating and cooperating in handling violence cases with UPPTK;
b. coordinating and cooperating with integrated service centers between regencies/cities; and
c. coordinating and cooperating with service provider institutions for victims of gender-based violence and children.

Paragraph 8
Efforts to Increase Community Participation

Article 17

Increasing Community Participation as referred to in Article 9 letter h is carried out by:
a. fostering community awareness of cases of gender-based violence and children;
b. encouraging the community to actively participate in providing information and reporting gender-based violence and children;
c. fostering local wisdom in handling gender-based violence and children;
d. organizing strengthening of community groups in handling gender-based violence and children; and
e. disseminating information about legislation related to gender-based violence and children.

Paragraph 9
Monitoring and Reporting

Article 18

Monitoring and Reporting as referred to in Article 9 letter i are activities that include monitoring, documenting and reporting cases of gender-based violence and children.

Part Three
UPPTK

Article 19

(1) UPPTK as referred to in Article 5 paragraph (1) letter b, is established by the Sub-district Head in accordance with the provisions of Legislation.
(2) UPPTK membership as referred to in paragraph (1) consists of:
a. sub-district government elements;
b. sub-district police elements;
c. community organization elements;
d. health service institutions;
e. community leaders; and
f. religious leaders.
(3) Membership of the UPPTK Team as referred to in paragraph (2) is determined by Sub-district Head's Decree.

Article 20

UPPTK as referred to in Article 5 paragraph (1) letter b has the tasks of:
a. receiving complaints of cases of gender-based violence and children;
b. providing initial consultation to victims of gender-based violence and children;
c. conducting crisis intervention for victims of gender-based violence and children; and
d. providing referrals for handling cases of gender-based violence and children to PPT PKBGA.

Article 21

In carrying out tasks as referred to in Article 20, UPPTK has the obligation to:
a. coordinate and consult with PPT PKBGA;
b. document and report every case of gender-based violence and children it handles to PPT PKBGA.

Part Four
Rights of Victims of Violence

Article 22

(1) Every victim of gender-based violence and children has the right to:
a. obtain protection for the security of themselves, their families and property, and be free from threats related to testimony that will be, is being, or has been given;
b. participate in the process of selecting and determining the form of protection and security support;
c. be free from ensnaring questions;
d. obtain information regarding case developments and court decisions; and
e. receive complaint services, health services, social rehabilitation services, legal aid and enforcement services, and repatriation and social reintegration services.
(2) Victim rights as referred to in paragraph (1) are implemented in accordance with the provisions of Legislation.

Part Five
Cooperation

Article 23

(1) In implementing protection for victims of gender-based violence and children, the Regional Government may cooperate with the Government, Provincial Government, other Regional Governments and/or other institutions.
(2) Cooperation between the Regional Government and the Provincial Government or the Government includes consultation, coordination, advocacy, referral, monitoring, evaluation, and reporting.
(3) Cooperation between the Regional Government and other Regional Governments includes coordination, advocacy, and referral.
(4) Implementation of cooperation as referred to in paragraph (1) is in accordance with the provisions of Legislation.

CHAPTER V
OBLIGATIONS AND RESPONSIBILITIES

Article 24

Implementation of protection for victims of gender-based violence and children is the joint obligation and responsibility of:
a. regional government;
b. community;
c. family; and
d. parents.

Part One
Regional Government

Article 25

(1) The Regional Government is obligated and responsible to implement victim protection efforts in the form of:
a. preventing all forms of gender-based violence and children;
b. providing protection by providing complaint services, health services, social rehabilitation services, legal aid and enforcement services, repatriation and social reintegration services, seeking to increase community participation, and monitoring and reporting;
c. encouraging and increasing community participation; and
d. cooperating with service providers in prevention, protection, and recovery efforts for victims of violence.
(2) To carry out obligations and responsibilities as referred to in paragraph (1), the Regional Government has the authority to:
a. formulate policies and programs on the elimination of gender-based violence and children;
b. establish integrated service centers;
c. establish complaint service institutions at the sub-district level;
d. facilitate the implementation of integrated service centers and activities of complaint service institutions at the sub-district level;
e. provide facilities and infrastructure;
f. increase the capacity of service provider institutions;
g. conduct coordination and cooperation in implementing protection for victims of gender-based violence and children;
h. encourage and increase community participation; and
i. conduct monitoring and evaluation.
(3) In implementing its obligations, responsibilities, and authorities, the Regent may delegate to the Regional Apparatus.
(4) The Regional Apparatus receives delegation from the Regent as referred to in paragraph (3) in its implementation prepares a Regional Action Plan for victim protection.
(5) The Regional Government in carrying out obligations as referred to in paragraph (1) and paragraph (2) must pay attention to the rights and obligations of parents, guardians, husbands, wives, or other persons who are legally responsible for victims.

Part Two
Community, Family, and Parents

Article 26

Community, family, and parents have the obligation and responsibility to:
a. prevent violence against women and children;
b. report if violence occurs;
c. protect victims; and
d. provide emergency assistance.

CHAPTER VI
COMMUNITY PARTICIPATION

Article 27

(1) The community may participate in helping prevention and handling efforts for victims of gender-based violence and children.
(2) Community participation as referred to in paragraph (1) is in the form of:
a. providing information and/or reporting any violence known;
b. providing protection for victims;
c. providing emergency assistance;
d. providing advocacy to victims and/or the community regarding handling gender-based violence and children;
e. assisting in the process of submitting applications for protection orders; and
f. assisting in the repatriation and social reintegration process.
(3) Community participation as referred to in paragraph (1) may be carried out by individuals, social community institutions, non-governmental organizations, educational institutions, religious institutions, private sector, and mass media.

CHAPTER VII
GUIDANCE AND SUPERVISION

Article 28

(1) The Regent has the authority to conduct guidance and supervision of the implementation of protection for victims of gender-based violence and children.
(2) The authority as referred to in paragraph (1) is carried out by the Regional Apparatus.

CHAPTER VIII
FUNDING

Article 29

All activities related to the implementation of protection for victims of gender-based violence and violence against children are charged to the Regional Revenue and Expenditure Budget and/or from other legitimate and non-binding receipts.

CHAPTER IX
TRANSITIONAL PROVISIONS

Article 30

At the time this Regional Regulation comes into force, all implementing regulations related to protection of victims of gender-based violence and children are declared to remain in force as long as they have not been replaced and do not conflict with the provisions in this Regional Regulation.

CHAPTER X
CLOSING PROVISIONS

Article 31

Implementation Provisions of this Regional Regulation are stipulated no later than 6 (six) months from the promulgation of this Regional Regulation.

Article 32

This Regional Regulation comes into force on the date of promulgation.
In order that everyone may know of it, orders the promulgation of this Regional Regulation by placing it in the Regional Gazette of Purbalingga Regency.

I. GENERAL
Cases of gender-based violence and children have recently increased, both in number and in the forms and modus operandi which are increasingly diverse. Rape, sexual harassment, domestic violence, sexual exploitation, violence against domestic workers, pornography, and neglect can occur in private, community and public spheres.
Various causes of gender-based violence and children include legal instruments that have not been able to provide protection to victims, the role of media that does not support reporting on gender-based violence and children, public services that are not optimal, poverty issues, misinterpretation of religious teachings, all of which cannot be separated from the strong patriarchal culture in society.
Therefore, victims of gender-based violence and children need to receive protection in accordance with the principles of justice, truth, legal certainty, equality, and upholding human rights. This Regional Regulation regulates the Implementation of Protection for Victims of Gender-Based Violence and Children, particularly in terms of prevention and services, protection mechanisms, community participation, guidance, funding, and supervision.

II. ARTICLE BY ARTICLE

Article 1
Sufficiently clear.

Article 2
Letter a
What is meant by the principle of "non-discrimination" is protection for all victims of gender-based violence and children without distinguishing ethnicity, religion, race, class, gender, ethnicity, culture, language, legal status and physical or mental condition.
Letter b
What is meant by the principle of "best interests of victims" is that all actions concerning victims carried out by government, society, legislative bodies, and judicial bodies, the best interests of victims must be the primary consideration.
Letter c
What is meant by the principle of "gender justice" is fair treatment given to women and men.
What is meant by the principle of "gender equality" is a condition and position that describes harmonious, balanced relations between men and women in obtaining opportunities/chances in access, participation, control and benefits in development implementation and enjoying development results in family life, as well as in society, nation and state.
Letter d
What is meant by the principle of "victim protection" is providing a sense of security to victims carried out by family, advocates, social institutions, police, prosecutors, courts, or other parties either temporarily or based on court decisions.
Letter e
What is meant by the principle of "child survival" is as stated in the principles of the Convention on the Rights of the Child, including the right to identity.
What is meant by "child development" is as stated in the principles of the Convention on the Rights of the Child, including the right to create and recreate.
Letter f
What is meant by the principle of "respect for children's opinions" is respect for children's rights to participate and express their opinions in decision-making that can be considered, especially if it concerns matters that affect their lives.
Letter g
What is meant by the principle of "openness" is that in implementing protection for victims of gender-based violence and children it is transparent among service providers.
Letter h
What is meant by the principle of "integration" is that in implementing protection for victims of gender-based violence and children it is carried out by building coordination among service providers, including medical services, legal assistants, psychologists, clergy, social workers, and police.
Letter i
What is meant by the principle of "empowering" is that every effort given must be able to strengthen victims, both physically, psychologically, socially, and economically.
Letter j
What is meant by the principle of "victim confidentiality" is every action taken to ensure victims are in safe conditions from threats or other actions that threaten the life and psychology of victims.

Article 3
Sufficiently clear.

Article 4
Letter a
What is meant by physical violence is any act that results in pain, injury, wounds or disability to a person's body, miscarriage, fainting and/or causing death.
Letter b
Psychological violence is any act that results in fear, loss of self-confidence, loss of ability to act, feelings of helplessness and/or severe psychological suffering to a person.
Letter c
Sexual violence is any act in the form of sexual harassment, forced sexual intercourse, forced sexual intercourse with others for commercial purposes and/or certain purposes.
Letter d
Neglect is:
a. an act that results in the unfulfillment of children's needs properly, whether physical, mental, spiritual or social carried out by parents, guardians, or any other party responsible for their care;
b. an act of deliberately neglecting to maintain, care for, or take care of children as they should be carried out by parents, guardians, or any other party responsible for their care;
c. an act of neglecting people within the household; and/or
d. an act that results in economic dependence by restricting and/or prohibiting proper work inside or outside the home so that the victim is under the control of that person.
Letter e
Other violence is any act caused by:
a. threat of violence, which includes any unlawful act in the form of words, writing, images, symbols, or body movements, whether with or without using means that cause fear or restrict a person's fundamental freedom;
b. coercion, which includes a situation where a person/victim is ordered to do something in such a way that the person does something contrary to their own will.
