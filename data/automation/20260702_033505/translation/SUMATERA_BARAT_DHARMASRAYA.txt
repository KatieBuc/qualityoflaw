REGENT OF DHARMASRAYA
WEST SUMATRA PROVINCE
REGIONAL REGULATION OF DHARMASRAYA REGENCY
NUMBER 16 YEAR 2018
CONCERNING
WOMEN'S EMPOWERMENT AND CHILD PROTECTION
BY THE GRACE OF ALMIGHTY GOD
THE REGENT OF DHARMASRAYA,

Considering: a. that women and children have the right to obtain a sense of security and freedom from all forms of torture or treatment that degrades human dignity;
b. that women and children are vulnerable groups who tend to experience violence, therefore they need to obtain optimal protection;
c. that the fulfillment of constitutional rights of women and children as well as improving the quality of life of women and children is one of the mandatory affairs that is the duty, authority and responsibility of the Regional Government;
d. that based on the considerations as referred to in letter a, letter b and letter c, it is necessary to establish a Regional Regulation on Women's Empowerment and Child Protection;

Recalling: 1. Article 18 paragraph (6) of the 1945 Constitution of the Republic of Indonesia;
2. Law Number 23 Year 2002 concerning Child Protection (State Gazette of the Republic of Indonesia Year 2002 Number 109, Supplement to State Gazette of the Republic of Indonesia Number 4236) as amended several times most recently by Law Number 17 Year 2016 concerning Stipulation of Government Regulation in Lieu of Law Number 1 Year 2016 concerning Second Amendment to Law Number 23 Year 2002 concerning Child Protection into Law (State Gazette of the Republic of Indonesia Year 2016 Number 237, Supplement to State Gazette of the Republic of Indonesia Number 5946);
3. Law Number 38 Year 2003 concerning Establishment of Dharmasraya Regency, South Solok Regency and West Pasaman Regency in West Sumatra Province (State Gazette of the Republic of Indonesia Year 2003 Number 153, Supplement to State Gazette of the Republic of Indonesia Number 4348);
4. Law Number 23 Year 2004 concerning Elimination of Domestic Violence (State Gazette of the Republic of Indonesia Year 2004 Number 95, Supplement to State Gazette of the Republic of Indonesia Number 4419);
5. Law Number 23 Year 2014 concerning Regional Government (State Gazette of the Republic of Indonesia Year 2014), as amended several times, most recently by Law Number 32 Year 2004 concerning Third Amendment to Law Number 23 Year 2014 concerning Regional Government (State Gazette of the Republic of Indonesia Year 2014);
6. Regional Regulation of West Sumatra Province Number 5 Year 2013 concerning Protection of Women and Children (Regional Gazette of West Sumatra Province Year 2013 Number 5, Supplement to Regional Gazette of West Sumatra Province Number 88);

With Mutual Approval
REGIONAL PEOPLE'S REPRESENTATIVE COUNCIL OF DHARMASRAYA REGENCY
and
REGENT OF DHARMASRAYA

HAS DECIDED:

To Establish: REGIONAL REGULATION CONCERNING WOMEN'S EMPOWERMENT AND CHILD PROTECTION

CHAPTER I
GENERAL PROVISIONS
First Section
Definitions

Article 1
In this Regional Regulation what is meant by:
1. Region is Dharmasraya Regency.
2. Regional Government is the Regent and regional apparatus as elements of the Regional Government administration of Dharmasraya Regency.
3. Regent is the Regent of Dharmasraya.
4. Regional Apparatus is the Regional Apparatus within the Government of Dharmasraya Regency.
5. Social Service, Women's Empowerment, Child Protection, Population Control and Family Planning hereinafter referred to as the Service is the Regional Apparatus that handles affairs in the Field of Implementation of Women and Child Protection.
6. Women's Protection is all efforts to guarantee and protect women's rights so they can live healthily and participate optimally in accordance with human dignity and obtain protection from neglect, violence and discrimination, whether in the form of preventive, repressive or post-remedial protection (assistance and treatment).
7. Gender Mainstreaming is a strategy carried out rationally and systematically to achieve and realize gender equality and justice in a number of aspects of human life, through policies and programs that pay attention to the experiences, aspirations, needs and problems of women and men in the planning, implementation, monitoring and evaluation of all policies and programs in various fields of life and development.
8. Gender Mainstreaming Working Group hereinafter referred to as Gender Mainstreaming Pokja is a consultation forum for implementers and mobilizers of gender mainstreaming from various agencies/institutions in the region.
9. Gender Mainstreaming Focal Point is an apparatus in the Regional Apparatus who has the ability to carry out gender mainstreaming in their respective work units.
10. Child is someone who has not yet reached the age of 18 (eighteen) years, including children still in the womb.
11. Child protection is all activities to guarantee and protect children and their rights so they can live, grow, develop, and participate optimally in accordance with human dignity, and receive protection from violence and discrimination.
12. Prevention is a direct effort carried out by the Regional Government, community and parents and/or family to prevent violations of women's and children's rights.
13. Children's Forum is a forum for children's participation formed with the aim of facilitating children's involvement in the decision-making process and enjoying changes concerning their lives either directly or indirectly, which is carried out with the approval and will of all children based on awareness and understanding, in accordance with age and level of thinking maturity.
14. Child-Friendly Regency hereinafter abbreviated as KLA is a Regency that has a child rights-based development system through integration of government, community and business commitments and resources that are planned comprehensively and sustainably in policies, programs and activities to ensure the fulfillment of children's rights.
15. Women victims of violence are women who experience actions that result in misery or suffering physically, sexually or psychologically, including threats of certain actions, coercion or arbitrary deprivation of freedom, whether occurring in the public sphere or in private life.
16. Child victims of violence are children who experience actions that result in misery or suffering physically, mentally, sexually, psychologically or neglect that threatens life, body and degrades human dignity.
17. Human trafficking is the act of recruitment, transportation, harboring, sending, transfer, or receipt of a person by threat of violence, use of violence, abduction, confinement, forgery, fraud, abuse of power or vulnerable position, debt bondage or giving payment or benefits, so as to obtain consent from the person holding control over that person, whether carried out within the country or between countries, for the purpose of exploitation or resulting in exploitation of the person.
18. Complaint handling is a series of actions carried out by integrated service providers to follow up on reports of acts of violence against women and children, submitted by victims, families or the community.
19. Social rehabilitation is a service aimed at restoring and developing the ability of a person experiencing social dysfunction to be able to carry out social functions properly.
20. Law enforcement is action by officials authorized by the state to implement statutory regulations.
21. Legal aid is legal services provided by legal assistants and advocates to carry out the process of assisting witnesses and/or victims of violence against women and children that is gender sensitive.
22. Repatriation is an effort to return women and child victims of violence from abroad to the debarkation point/entry point, or from the receiving area to the area of origin.
23. Social reintegration is an effort to reunite victims with family, substitute family, or community that can provide protection and fulfillment of needs for victims.
24. Safe house is a temporary shelter that aims to provide protection and a sense of security to victims of violence.
25. Spiritual guidance is an effort to restore the soul through strengthening the religion adhered to by the victim.
26. Gender responsive is consistent and systematic attention to differences between women and men in society accompanied by efforts to eliminate structural and cultural barriers in achieving gender equality.
27. Parents are biological father and/or mother, or stepfather and/or stepmother, or adoptive father and/or mother.
28. Family is the smallest unit in society consisting of husband and wife or husband, wife and their children, or father and children, or mother and children, or blood relatives in direct line upward or downward up to the third degree.
29. Customs are enduring and hereditary conduct patterns from one generation to another as heritage, so that they are strongly integrated with community behavior patterns.

Second Section
Principles, Objectives and Scope

Article 2
(1) Implementation of women's protection is based on the principles of:
a. best interests of women;
b. gender justice and gender equality;
c. local wisdom; and
d. legal certainty.
(2) Implementation of child protection is based on the principles of:
a. non-discrimination;
b. best interests of the child;
c. right to life, survival, and development;
d. respect for the child's opinion; and
e. legal certainty.

Article 3
Women's empowerment and child protection aims to:
a. serve as a guideline for regional government in planning, policies and strategies for women's and children's protection;
b. protect women and children from actions, decisions and policies that violate women's and children's rights;
c. prevent violations of women's and children's rights;
d. increase empowerment of women and children;
e. increase the role and value of local wisdom and the role of customs in protecting women and children; and
f. increase the role of government and non-government institutions in the region in efforts to protect women and children.

Article 4
The scope of regulation of women's and children's protection includes:
a. rights and obligations;
b. improving women's quality of life;
c. protection of women and children;
d. improving family quality;
e. gender and child data system;
f. fulfillment of children's rights;
g. child-friendly regency; and
h. guidance and supervision.

CHAPTER II
RIGHTS AND OBLIGATIONS
First Section
Rights of Women and Children
Paragraph 1
Women's Rights

Article 5
Every woman has the right to:
a. obtain protection from discriminatory actions;
b. obtain protection from economic exploitation and sexual exploitation;
c. obtain protection from physical, psychological, sexual violence, and neglect;
d. obtain decent work according to her ability;
e. obtain work leave while receiving wages/salary in accordance with statutory regulations;
f. obtain education and teaching in all types, levels and pathways of education;
g. obtain special protection in carrying out work or profession against matters that can threaten safety and/or health related to women's reproductive function; and
h. ownership and management of joint property in marriage in accordance with statutory regulations.

Paragraph 2
Children's Rights

Article 6
Every child has the right to:
a. live, grow, develop and participate properly in accordance with human dignity;
b. obtain protection from discriminatory actions;
c. obtain protection from economic exploitation and sexual exploitation;
d. obtain protection from physical, psychological, sexual violence, and neglect;
e. a name as self-identity and citizenship status;
f. obtain a birth certificate free of charge;
g. embrace religion and worship according to the teachings of their religion with guidance from parents/guardians;
h. not be forced to change religion by foster parents, adoptive parents and by anyone;
i. be recognized and know their parents, be raised and cared for by their own parents;
j. obtain health services and social security in accordance with physical, mental, spiritual, and social needs;
k. obtain education and teaching in accordance with their interests and talents;
l. obtain care, education, training, improve themselves and the ability to participate in community and state life for children with physical, mental disabilities and special needs;
m. express and have their opinions heard; and
n. rest and utilize leisure time for self-development.

Second Section
Obligations of Regional Government

Article 7
The obligations of the Regional Government in efforts to protect women include:
a. establish and implement policies, programs and activities for protection of women's rights;
b. cooperate with the community and/or Community Organizations in implementing women's protection in accordance with statutory regulations;
c. carry out rehabilitation and empowerment of women who are victims of violence;
d. provide special protection to women victims of violence and human trafficking; and
e. take preventive actions in order to prevent violations of women's rights.

Article 8
The obligations of the Regional Government in efforts to protect children include:
a. guarantee the human rights of every child without discrimination;
b. provide infrastructure and facilities support in implementing child protection;
c. guarantee the child's right to express opinions in accordance with the child's age and level of intelligence;
d. establish and implement policies and programs/activities for protection of children's rights;
e. cooperate with the community and/or Community Organizations in implementing child protection in accordance with statutory regulations;
f. carry out rehabilitation of children who are victims of violence;
g. provide special protection to children in emergency situations, children in conflict with the law, child victims of violence and human trafficking, children from minority and isolated groups, children exploited economically and/or sexually, children who are victims of narcotics, alcohol, psychotropic abuse and children with HIV/AIDS;
h. organize inclusive education for children with special needs; and
i. take preventive actions in order to prevent violations of children's rights.

Third Section
Obligations of the Community

Article 9
(1) The obligations of the community in efforts to protect women and children:
a. participate in prevention and provide support for the development of prevention programs for violations of women's and children's rights;
b. provide information and/or report violations of women's and children's rights to the authorities; and
c. supervise the law enforcement process for cases of violations of women's and children's rights.
(2) Ninik mamak, alim ulama and cadiak pandai as well as bundo kanduang are responsible for protecting and preventing violations of women's and children's rights.
(3) The obligations of the community as referred to in paragraph (1) and paragraph (2) are based on statutory regulations and local wisdom values of the local community.

Fourth Section
Obligations of Family and/or Parents
Paragraph 1
Obligations Regarding Women's Protection

Article 10
The family in efforts to protect women's rights is obliged to:
a. protect women from conflicts and domestic violence that degrades women's dignity;
b. provide equal opportunities for women to develop themselves in the fields of education, social, economic, political and cultural affairs; and
c. prevent violations of women's rights within the household.

Paragraph 2
Obligations Regarding Child Protection

Article 11
(1) Parents in efforts to protect children's rights are obliged to:
a. care for, maintain, educate, and protect children;
b. develop children according to their abilities, talents, and interests;
c. provide formal and non-formal education;
d. guide children to behave in accordance with religion, ethics and morals as well as customs; and
e. prevent marriage at child age.
(2) In the event that parents do not exist, or their whereabouts are unknown, or due to some reason, cannot carry out their obligations and responsibilities, then the obligations and responsibilities as referred to in paragraph (1) may transfer to the family.

CHAPTER III
IMPROVING WOMEN'S QUALITY OF LIFE
First Section
General

Article 12
(1) In order to improve women's quality of life, the Regional Government is responsible for:
a. respecting and guaranteeing women's rights without discrimination in accordance with human dignity;
b. implementing Gender Mainstreaming in the Region;
c. forming a Gender Mainstreaming working group in the Region;
d. increasing Women's Empowerment in roles, quality and capabilities in the fields of:
1. politics;
2. law;
3. social; and
4. economy.
e. strengthening and developing women's empowerment service provider institutions in the region.
(2) The responsibility of the regional government in efforts to improve women's quality of life as referred to in paragraph (1) is carried out by the relevant Regional Apparatus.

Second Section
Implementation of Gender Mainstreaming in the Region

Article 13
(1) The Regent is responsible for the implementation of Gender Mainstreaming in the Region.
(2) The responsibility of the Regent as referred to in paragraph (1) may be delegated to the Vice Regent.

Article 14
In implementing Gender Mainstreaming in the region, the Regent designates the Regional Apparatus in charge of Women's Empowerment as coordinator of Gender Mainstreaming implementation.

Third Section
Formation of Gender Mainstreaming Working Group

Article 15
(1) In an effort to accelerate the institutionalization of Gender Mainstreaming in all Regional Apparatus, a Gender Mainstreaming Working Group is formed.
(2) The Gender Mainstreaming Working Group as referred to in paragraph (1) consists of:
a. chairperson: Head of Bappeda;
b. secretary: Head of Regional Apparatus in charge of Women's Empowerment duties; and
c. members: all heads/leaders of Regional Apparatus.
(3) The formation of the Gender Mainstreaming working group as referred to in paragraph (1) is stipulated by Regent's Decree.

Article 16
The Regional Gender Mainstreaming Working Group as referred to in Article 15 paragraph (1) has the following duties:
a. promote and facilitate Gender Mainstreaming to each Regional Apparatus;
b. carry out socialization and advocacy of Gender Mainstreaming to Sub-district Heads and Wali Nagari;
c. prepare work programs every year;
d. encourage the realization of gender-responsive planning and budgeting;
e. prepare work plans for the Gender Mainstreaming working group every year;
f. be responsible to the Regent through the Vice Regent;
g. formulate Gender Mainstreaming policy recommendations to the Regent;
h. prepare Regional Gender Profile;
i. monitor the implementation of Gender Mainstreaming in each Regional Apparatus;
j. establish a technical team to conduct analysis of gender-responsive regional budgets;
k. prepare a regional action plan on Gender Mainstreaming in the region; and
l. encourage the implementation of selection and determination of focal points in each Regional Apparatus.

Article 17
(1) The technical team as referred to in Article 16 letter j consists of apparatus who understand gender-responsive budget analysis.
(2) The Regional Gender Mainstreaming regional action plan as referred to in Article 16 letter k contains:
a. gender mainstreaming in statutory regulations in the region;
b. gender mainstreaming in the development cycle in the region;
c. strengthening Gender Mainstreaming institutions in the region; and
d. strengthening community participation in the region.

Article 18
(1) The Gender Mainstreaming Focal Point in each Regional Apparatus as referred to in Article 16 letter l consists of officials and/or staff in charge of planning and/or program duties.
(2) The Gender Mainstreaming Focal Point as referred to in paragraph (1) has the duties of:
a. promoting Gender Mainstreaming in the work unit;
b. facilitating the preparation of work plans and budgeting of Regional Apparatus that is gender responsive;
c. implementing training, socialization, advocacy of Gender Mainstreaming to all officials and staff within the Regional Apparatus;
d. reporting the implementation of Gender Mainstreaming to the leadership of the Regional Apparatus;
e. encouraging the implementation of gender analysis of policies, programs, and activities in the work unit; and
f. facilitating the preparation of gender data in each Regional Apparatus.
(3) The Gender Mainstreaming Focal Point as referred to in paragraph (1) is selected and determined by the leadership of the Regional Apparatus.

Fourth Section
Increasing Women's Role, Quality, and Capability in the Political Field

Article 19
Efforts to increase women's role, quality and capability in the political field include:
a. carry out socialization to increase women's political participation in general elections;
b. implement programs that encourage the level of women's involvement in political processes and public positions;
c. facilitate women's self-development in organizing, associating and assembling;
d. involve women in decision-making concerning women's interests;

Fifth Section
Increasing Women's Role, Quality, and Capability in the Legal Field

Article 20
Efforts to increase women's role, quality and capability in the legal field include:
a. conduct socialization to increase women's legal knowledge and awareness;
b. map and review gender-biased policies;
c. involve women's organizations in the process of drafting and discussing Regional legal products related to women's participation;

Sixth Section
Increasing Women's Role, Quality, and Capability in the Social Field

Article 21
Efforts to increase women's role, quality and capability in the social field include:
a. increasing knowledge and skills to encourage fulfillment of education according to potential possessed;
b. increasing and fulfilling quality of health for women;
c. increasing awareness and knowledge about planning independent, healthy and prosperous families including access to consultation services and marriage registration;
d. increasing women's awareness of the impact of early marriage;
e. facilitating and seeking preservation of customs and development of science, technology, arts and culture for women's advancement;
f. increasing women's awareness and knowledge of planning independent, healthy and prosperous families;
g. implementing programs to reduce maternal mortality rates;
h. encouraging the formation of women's organizations in the Region;

Seventh Section
Increasing Women's Role, Quality, and Capability in the Economic Field

Article 22
Efforts to increase women's role, quality and capability in the economic field include:
a. implement programs, activities, training to improve women's skills and expertise in order to realize improved women's welfare;
b. facilitate strengthening business capital for women with weak economies;
c. marketing products from businesses of women with weak economies;
d. training to develop women's capabilities through economic empowerment of pre-prosperous families;
e. comprehensive entrepreneurship training for women.

CHAPTER IV
PROTECTION OF WOMEN AND CHILDREN
First Section
Prevention of Acts of Violence Against Women and Children

Article 23
(1) The responsibility of the Regional Government to prevent acts of violence against women and children includes:
a. organize socialization regarding prevention of violence against women and children by optimizing the role of customary, cultural, religious institutions and community organizations;
b. provide guidelines on handling acts of violence against women and children;
c. involve community participation in recovery and reintegration;
d. develop an integrated database and information system on acts of violence against women and children;
e. develop family empowerment programs;
f. strengthen the function of community organizations working in the field of women's empowerment and child protection; and
g. provide public facilities that are comfortable and safe for women and children.
(2) Prevention of acts of violence against women and children as referred to in paragraph (1) is carried out by Regional Apparatus whose duties and functions are in the fields of:
a. social;
b. health;
c. education;
d. manpower;
e. women's empowerment and child protection;
f. mental and spiritual;
g. informatics and communication; and
h. peace and order.
(3) Prevention as referred to in paragraph (1) is carried out by Regional Apparatus in accordance with their field of duty in an integrated and sustainable manner based on the Regional Action Plan.

Article 24
The responsibility of the community to prevent acts of violence against women and children includes:
a. supervise various conditions occurring in their environment that can cause violations of women's and children's rights;
b. increase awareness of violations of women's and children's rights;
c. strengthen the role of community, religious organizations and family environment; and
d. prevent unregistered marriages and marriage at child age.

Article 25
The responsibility of parents and family to prevent acts of violence against women and children includes:
a. protect women and children from various rights violations within the household;
b. encourage the formation of harmonious, educative and democratic families;
c. provide good examples to children according to religious teachings and customs;
d. supervise children in activities inside or outside the home; and
e. provide opportunities for children to dialogue and express opinions democratically.

Third Section
Effectiveness of Services for Women and Children
Paragraph 1
General

Article 26
(1) In order to implement Women's and Children's Protection in the Region, the Regional Government forms UPTD PPA in accordance with statutory regulations.
(2) UPTD PPA as referred to in paragraph (1) functions to provide services to women and children experiencing criminal and/or civil problems.
(3) The scope of UPTD PPA services as referred to in paragraph (2) includes:
a. complaint services;
b. health rehabilitation;
c. social rehabilitation;
d. assistance;
e. law enforcement and legal aid; and
f. repatriation and social reintegration.
(4) In carrying out its functions, UPTD PPA is obliged to:
a. provide services and handling as quickly as possible to women and children experiencing problems;
b. provide convenience, comfort, safety and free of charge to women and children experiencing problems;
c. maintain confidentiality of women and children experiencing problems;
d. guarantee justice and legal certainty for women and children experiencing problems.
(5) UPTD PPA is responsible to the Regional Apparatus that administers government affairs in the field of Women's Empowerment and Child Protection.

Article 27
(1) The implementation of UPTD PPA services is integrative, carried out under one roof or networked to provide optimal services to women and children experiencing problems.
(2) In the event that the implementation of UPTD PPA services as referred to in paragraph (1) is carried out under one roof, UPTD PPA is responsible for carrying out the entire process in one unit to provide services needed by women and children experiencing problems.
(3) In the event that the implementation of UPTD PPA services as referred to in paragraph (1) is carried out networked, UPTD PPA is responsible for the entire referral process of services needed by women and children experiencing problems.
(4) Every element within the UPTD PPA environment in carrying out its duties must apply the principles of coordination, integration, synchronization, accountability and openness, both within the UPTD PPA environment, and in relations between Regional Apparatus, related agencies, and community elements.

Article 28
Further provisions regarding the organization and working procedures of UPTD PPA as referred to in Article 26 and Article 27 are stipulated by Regent's Regulation in accordance with statutory regulations.

Paragraph 2
Complaint Services

Article 29
Complaint services as referred to in Article 26 paragraph (3) letter a can be coordinated by UPTD PPA with related agencies consisting of:
a. hospitals;
b. community health centers;
c. integrated service centers for women's empowerment and children;
d. trauma center protection houses;
e. women crisis centers;
f. marriage counseling, guidance and preservation advisory board;
g. police;
h. prosecutor's office;
i. courts;
j. ministry of religion; and/or
k. legal aid organizations;

Article 30
(1) Complaints of acts of violence against women and children can be made by reporters consisting of victims, victims' families, the community and/or community organizations in the field of women's and children's protection.
(2) Reporters as referred to in paragraph (1) can make complaints:
a. directly;
b. by telephone; and/or
c. by letter.
(3) Further provisions regarding procedures for complaint handling services as referred to in paragraph (1) and paragraph (2) are regulated by Regent's Regulation.

Paragraph 3
Health Rehabilitation

Article 31
(1) Health rehabilitation as referred to in Article 26 paragraph (3) letter b is an effort that includes aspects of:
a. promotive, increasing so that health status increases;
b. preventive, preventing from contracting disease;
c. curative, curing someone from illness physically and psychologically;
d. rehabilitative, maintaining so that someone who has not fully recovered can return to being fit as before.
(2) Health rehabilitation as referred to in paragraph (1) is carried out comprehensively, can be accessed 24 (twenty-four) hours, is of quality and is carried out in accordance with health service standards.
(3) Women and child victims of violence have the right to health services free of charge, including:
a. medical services;
b. medicolegal services;
c. psychosocial counseling; and
d. Visum et Repertum.
(4) Further provisions regarding procedures for implementing health services as referred to in paragraph (3) are regulated by Regent's Regulation.

Paragraph 4
Social Rehabilitation

Article 32
(1) Social rehabilitation as referred to in Article 26 paragraph (3) letter c is aimed at restoring and developing capabilities of women and children experiencing social dysfunction due to criminal and/or civil problems to be able to carry out their social functions in society properly.
(2) Social rehabilitation as referred to in paragraph (1) includes services for:
a. counseling;
b. provision of safe houses; and
c. spiritual guidance.
(3) In implementing services as referred to in paragraph (2), UPTD PPA can cooperate with women crisis centers and/or social community organizations that already exist.

Article 33
(1) Counseling services as referred to in Article 32 paragraph (2) letter a aim to:
a. help women and child victims of violence recognize their problems and find effective ways to overcome them themselves;
b. empower women and children experiencing criminal and/or civil problems to determine their future;
c. provide moral support to women and children experiencing criminal and/or civil problems in facing the process they are going through; and
d. make women and children experiencing criminal and/or civil problems feel accepted in the community environment.
(2) Counseling services are carried out by gender-responsive social rehabilitation officers, consisting of:
a. counselors;
b. psychologists; and/or
c. psychiatrists.
(3) Counseling services are carried out in places that guarantee a sense of security, comfort and confidentiality of information from women victims of violence.
(4) Stages of counseling services as referred to in paragraph (3) include:
a. initial counseling;
b. follow-up counseling;
c. case discussion; and
d. site visits.

Article 34
(1) Provision of safe houses as referred to in Article 32 paragraph (2) letter b can be provided by the Regional Government for women and children experiencing criminal and/or civil problems.
(2) Safe house provision services as referred to in paragraph (1) aim to protect women and children as victims of criminal and/or civil problems from intimidation by perpetrators or other parties.
(3) The location of safe houses as referred to in paragraph (1) must be kept confidential.
(4) Criteria for women and children as victims of criminal and/or civil problems who need safe houses include:
a. their lives are threatened;
b. receiving rejection from family or community;
c. requiring intensive services but their residence is relatively far; and/or
d. abandoned if not placed in a safe house.

Article 35
(1) Spiritual guidance as referred to in Article 32 paragraph (2) letter c aims for psychological recovery.
(2) Spiritual guidance as referred to in paragraph (1) is carried out based on the principles of:
a. not imposing any religion or belief; and
b. leaving the final decision in the hands of the victim (self-determination).
(3) Spiritual guidance is carried out by spiritual guidance officers.

Paragraph 5
Assistance

Article 36
(1) Assistance services as referred to in Article 26 paragraph (3) letter d are carried out for strengthening and recovery of violence victims.
(2) In providing assistance services as referred to in paragraph (1), P2TP2A can cooperate with persons or institutions that have expertise to conduct counseling, therapy and advocacy.

Paragraph 6
Law Enforcement and Legal Aid

Article 37
(1) Law enforcement and legal aid services as referred to in Article 26 paragraph (3) letter e are actions by law enforcement officials to implement statutory regulations in order to handle criminal and/or civil problems against women and children, especially to provide sanctions to perpetrators and provide protection for witnesses and/or victims.
(2) Law enforcement and legal aid services as referred to in paragraph (1) are carried out by prioritizing the best interests of victims.
(3) Types of legal aid services as referred to in paragraph (1) include:
a. legal aid in the reporting process and preparation of examination reports;
b. legal aid in the prosecution process at the prosecutor's office;
c. legal aid in the examination process in court;
d. legal aid in the court hearing decision process until there is a final legal decision; and
e. legal aid in the mediation process.
(4) The Regional Government provides legal aid to women and children as victims of criminal and/or civil problems based on statutory regulations.
(5) In providing legal aid as referred to in paragraph (3), UPTD PPA can cooperate with legal aid institutions, women crisis centers, advocates and/or other gender-sensitive legal assistants.

Paragraph 7
Repatriation and Social Reintegration

Article 38
(1) Repatriation as referred to in Article 26 paragraph (3) letter f is aimed at returning women and children as victims of criminal and/or civil problems to their place of origin.
(2) Repatriation of women and children as victims of criminal and/or civil problems is accompanied by social reintegration efforts.

Article 39
(1) Social reintegration as referred to in Article 38 paragraph (2) is carried out in order to reunite women and children as victims of criminal and/or civil problems with family, substitute family, or community that can provide protection and fulfillment of needs for victims.
(2) Social reintegration as referred to in paragraph (1) aims so that women and children as victims can continue their social life and can have a decent life.
(3) Social reintegration as referred to in paragraph (1) is carried out after victims receive social rehabilitation services.

Article 40
Repatriation and social reintegration of women and children as victims of criminal and/or civil problems as referred to in Article 38 and Article 39 are carried out by UPTD PPA in accordance with statutory regulations.

CHAPTER V
IMPROVING FAMILY QUALITY

Article 41
(1) In order to improve family quality, the Regional Government is responsible for:
a. making efforts to improve family quality to realize gender equality and children's rights;
b. providing services for families in realizing gender equality and children's rights; and
c. strengthening and developing family quality improvement service provider institutions in the Region.
(2) The responsibility of the Regional Government as referred to in paragraph (1) is carried out by Regional Apparatus in accordance with their duties and functions.

Article 42
Efforts to improve family quality as referred to in Article 41 paragraph (1) letter a include:
a. guidance;
b. counseling; and
c. supervision.

Article 43
Guidance as referred to in Article 42 letter a includes:
a. growth and development of toddlers;
b. protection of adolescent reproductive health;
c. maturation of marriage age;
d. birth regulation; and
e. improving the health quality of pregnant and breastfeeding mothers.

Article 44
Counseling as referred to in Article 42 letter b includes:
a. conducting outreach to improve women's quality of life;
b. conducting pre-marital counseling; and
c. facilitating the implementation of family development;

Article 45
Supervision as referred to in Article 42 letter c includes:
a. evaluating data on toddler growth and development;
b. evaluating post-marriage data.

Article 46
Provision of services for families as referred to in Article 41 paragraph (1) letter b includes:
a. communication, information and education;
b. recording and reporting of family planning services;
c. adequate health service facilities and infrastructure; and
d. lactation rooms in every public facility.

Article 47
Communication, information and education as referred to in Article 46 letter a is a communication activity to increase knowledge and improve the attitudes and behavior of families and communities in population and family planning programs.

Article 48
(1) Agencies and private parties that provide public services are required to provide lactation rooms.
(2) Lactation rooms as referred to in paragraph (1) are proper and adequate rooms.
(3) Agency leaders who do not provide lactation rooms as referred to in paragraph (1) are subject to administrative sanctions in the form of:
a. written reprimand; and/or
b. employment sanctions based on statutory regulations.
(4) Private parties who do not provide lactation rooms as referred to in paragraph (2) are subject to administrative sanctions in the form of:
a. written warning;
b. limitation of business activities;
c. freezing of business activities;
d. administrative fines; and/or
e. license revocation.

CHAPTER VI
GENDER AND CHILD DATA SYSTEM
First Section
General

Article 49
(1) In order to prepare planning, budgeting, monitoring and evaluation, as well as making development policy decisions that are gender responsive and child-friendly, the regional government organizes a gender and child data system.
(2) To organize a gender and child data system, accurate and accountable disaggregated data is required.

Second Section
Implementation of Gender and Child Data System

Article 50
(1) In implementing the gender and child data system in the region as referred to in Article 49, the Regent forms a working group for gender and child disaggregated data.
(2) The formation of the gender and child disaggregated data working group as referred to in paragraph (1) is stipulated by Regent's Decree.

Article 51
The gender and child disaggregated data working group as referred to in Article 50 has the duties of:
a. preparing work plans for the gender and child disaggregated data working group using measurable indicators and targets for certain periods to be used for monitoring and evaluation of gender and child disaggregated data working group activities;
b. increasing cross-sectoral cooperation in order to strengthen data produced related to data procurement and utilization;
c. carrying out coordination and synchronization with related agencies in order to prepare gender and child data for the Regional level; and
d. collecting, processing, compiling and improving data quality and presenting data in regional apparatus organizations and subsequently making it secondary data on Gender and Child data on an ongoing basis.

CHAPTER VII
FULFILLMENT OF CHILDREN'S RIGHTS
First Section
General

Article 52
(1) The Regional Government in efforts to fulfill children's rights is responsible for:
a. establishing and implementing policies and programs/activities for fulfillment of children's rights;
b. respecting and guaranteeing the human rights of every child without distinguishing ethnicity, religion, race, class, gender, ethnicity, culture and language, legal status of children, birth order of children, and physical and/or mental conditions;
c. strengthening and developing family quality improvement service provider institutions in realizing children's rights;
d. guaranteeing protection, maintenance, and welfare of children by taking into account the rights and obligations of parents, guardians, or other persons legally responsible for children;
e. providing facilities and infrastructure in implementing fulfillment of children's rights;
f. guaranteeing children's rights to express opinions in accordance with the child's age and level of intelligence;
g. conducting supervision and guidance in order to implement fulfillment of children's rights; and
h. cooperating with private parties and/or NGOs in implementing child protection;
(2) The responsibility as referred to in paragraph (1) is carried out by Regional Apparatus in accordance with their duties and functions.

Second Section
Fulfillment of Children's Rights in the Field of Birth Registration and Child Identity

Article 53
(1) The Regional Government is obliged to organize child birth registration with issuance of birth certificates.
(2) Birth certificates as referred to in paragraph (1) are organized free of charge.

Article 54
(1) To encourage increased data collection, protection and public services and to realize the best for children, every child under the age of 17 years is required to have a child identity card.
(2) Child identity cards are issued by the Regional Apparatus in charge of population and civil registration.

Third Section
Fulfillment of Children's Rights in the Field of Education

Article 55
The Regional Government guarantees the implementation of compulsory education programs for children in accordance with statutory regulations.

Article 56
(1) Every education unit organizer is required to provide opportunities for children to obtain education services without discrimination.
(2) Every education unit organizer is prohibited from expelling children from educational institutions unless there is a guarantee for the continuity of children's education.
(3) In the event that child victims of violence experience dropping out of school, then child victims of violence are provided formal or non-formal education, in this case carried out by education unit organizers and coordinated with the Women's and Children's Empowerment Unit.

Article 57
(1) Every child has the right to obtain education and teaching in order to develop their personality and level of intelligence according to their interests and talents.
(2) Every child has the right to receive protection in education units from sexual crimes and violence committed by educators, education personnel, fellow students, and/or other parties.

Article 58
(1) Children with disabilities and children with special needs are given equal opportunities to obtain formal, non-formal and informal education services, including inclusive education programs.
(2) The regional government provides teaching staff for inclusive education in every education unit.

Fourth Section
Fulfillment of Children's Rights in the Field of Health and Social

Article 59
(1) The Regional Government is required to organize comprehensive health services for children, so that every child obtains optimal health status from the womb.
(2) Comprehensive health services as referred to in paragraph (1) include:
a. promotive, preventive, curative and rehabilitative efforts for basic health services;
b. exemption from cost burdens for malnourished children, children with disabilities, children with special needs, children infected with HIV/AIDS, child workers, child victims of narcotics, alcohol, psychotropic and other addictive substance abuse, child victims of violence, and child victims of human trafficking;
(3) The Regional Government, community, family and parents are required to ensure that children born are protected from diseases that threaten survival and/or cause disability.

Article 60
(1) Promotive efforts for basic health services as referred to in Article 59 paragraph (2) letter a are aimed at increasing health status more optimally.
(2) Promotive efforts as referred to in paragraph (1) are carried out by providing socialization or outreach to the community regarding healthy lifestyles including:
a. the importance of exclusive breast milk for babies;
b. the importance of immunization for toddlers;
c. clean living patterns;
d. the importance of nutritious food; and
e. dangers of drugs and smoking.
(3) In addition to promotive efforts as referred to in paragraph (2), the Regional Government can carry out other activities in order to implement health services.

Article 61
(1) Preventive efforts for basic health services as referred to in Article 59 paragraph (2) letter a are aimed at preventing disease or health problems.
(2) Efforts as referred to in paragraph (1) are carried out by:
a. requiring mothers to provide exclusive breastfeeding to babies up to 6 (six) months of age;
b. providing free immunization and vitamins to babies and toddlers;
c. providing nutritious food to toddlers from underprivileged families;
d. optimizing the role of posyandu for health services for mothers and toddlers;
e. conducting periodic health examinations;
f. providing sanitation facilities and clean water in every school; and
g. providing special breastfeeding facilities or ASI corner rooms in government agencies or businesses.

Article 62
(1) Curative efforts for basic health services as referred to in Article 59 paragraph (2) letter a are aimed at preventing disease from becoming more severe through treatment.
(2) Treatment as referred to in paragraph (1) is carried out free of charge for children from underprivileged families.

Article 63
(1) Rehabilitative efforts for basic health services as referred to in Article 59 paragraph (2) letter a are aimed at maintaining and restoring conditions after illness.
(2) Rehabilitative efforts as referred to in paragraph (1) are carried out through periodic health examinations.

Article 64
The Regional Government is required to organize Social Welfare for:
a. children in conflict with the law;
b. child victims of violence who are economically exploited;
c. children who do not have parents and are cared for by parties or families who are unable;
d. child victims of natural disasters or social disasters; and
e. children with disabilities.

Fifth Section
Fulfillment of Children's Rights in the Field of Manpower

Article 65
The Regional Government conducts periodic supervision of every person/entity that employs children in efforts to protect child workers.

Article 66
(1) Every person/entity is prohibited from employing and involving children in the worst forms of work.
(2) The worst forms of work referred to in paragraph (1) include:
a. all work in the form of slavery or the like;
b. all work that utilizes, provides, or offers children for prostitution, pornography production, pornographic performances, or gambling;
c. all work that utilizes, provides, or involves children for production and trade of alcoholic beverages, narcotics, psychotropics, and other addictive substances; and/or
d. all work that endangers the health, safety, or morals of children.

Sixth Section
Children's Forum

Article 67
(1) In implementing fulfillment of children's rights to express opinions and participate, the Regional Government facilitates the formation of a Children's Forum.
(2) The Children's Forum as referred to in paragraph (1) is a representation of children in the Region.
(3) Representation as referred to in paragraph (2) consists of children's geographical domicile, components of children's socio-cultural groups and children's educational background.
(4) The Children's Forum as referred to in paragraph (1) can be formed at the Regional, Sub-district and Nagari levels.
(5) In every formulation of policies related to children, the Regional Government is required to pay attention to and accommodate children's opinions submitted through the Children's Forum.
(6) The formation of the Children's Forum as referred to in paragraph (1) is stipulated by Regent's Decree.

CHAPTER VIII
CHILD-FRIENDLY REGENCY

Article 68
(1) In order to fulfill children's rights in an integrated, systematic and sustainable manner, the Regional Government establishes a KLA Development policy.
(2) The KLA Development policy as referred to in paragraph (1) contains:
a. KLA concept concerning objectives, strategies and roles of related parties;
b. children's rights; and
c. KLA development approach.

Article 69
(1) KLA development policy is directed at fulfilling children's rights divided into 5 (five) groups, including:
a. civil rights and freedoms;
b. family environment and alternative care;
c. basic health and welfare;
d. education, leisure time utilization, and cultural activities; and
e. special protection.
(2) Further provisions regarding KLA development policy as referred to in paragraph (1) are regulated by Regent's Regulation.

Article 70
(1) In order to effectively implement KLA policy in the Region, a KLA Task Force is formed.
(2) The KLA Task Force as referred to in paragraph (1) has the main duties of:
a. coordinating the implementation of KLA policy and development;
b. conducting socialization, advocacy and communication, information and education of KLA policy;
c. collecting basic data for KLA development;
d. conducting needs analysis sourced from basic data;
e. disseminating basic data;
f. determining program focus and priorities in realizing KLA, adjusted to regional potential;
g. preparing a 5 (five) year Child-Friendly City regional action plan and work mechanisms; and
h. conducting monitoring, evaluation and reporting at least once (one) a year.
(3) The formation of the KLA Task Force as referred to in paragraph (1) is stipulated by Regent's Decree.

Article 71
In carrying out its duties, the KLA Task Force as referred to in Article 70 paragraph (1) can coordinate and cooperate with UPTD PPA and/or other institutions that provide Women's and Children's Protection services.

CHAPTER IX
GUIDANCE AND SUPERVISION

Article 72
(1) The Regional Government is required to conduct guidance and supervision of Women's Empowerment and Child Protection.
(2) Guidance as referred to in paragraph (1) includes:
a. formation of policies and rules that serve as guidelines and standards in implementing women's empowerment and child protection;
b. technical guidance and training, including:
1. providing training for government officials and law enforcement on prevention and handling of violence;
2. providing training for religious leaders, community leaders, customary leaders, educators, journalists and media managers in order to prevent violence against women and children;
3. providing training to health workers who provide health services to women and child victims of violence at health centers, integrated service units and general hospitals;
4. providing training to social rehabilitation officers and spiritual guidance officers in order to protect women and child victims of violence; and
5. providing training to PUG working groups and focal points of each Regional Apparatus for preparing activity programs based on gender-responsive planning and budgeting.
c. provision of facilities and infrastructure in implementing women's empowerment and child protection;
d. facilitating the formation of nagari that care for women and children;
e. providing information through print and electronic media related to women's and children's protection.
(3) Supervision as referred to in paragraph (1) includes:
a. monitoring carried out in the form of submission of reports on implementation of women's empowerment and child protection programs by Heads of Regional Apparatus to the Regent periodically once (one) every 3 (three) months; and
b. evaluation of the implementation of women's empowerment and child protection periodically once (one) every 3 (three) months carried out by related Regional Apparatus.
(4) The results of supervision as referred to in paragraph (3) are material for evaluation of women's empowerment and child protection program policies.
(5) Further provisions regarding Guidance and Supervision as referred to in paragraph (1) are regulated by Regent's Regulation.

CHAPTER X
FINANCING

Article 73
Financing for implementation of women's empowerment and child protection activities is sourced from the Regional Revenue and Expenditure Budget and other legitimate and non-binding sources.

CHAPTER XI
CLOSING PROVISIONS

Article 74
Implementation regulations of this Regional Regulation are stipulated at the latest 1 (one) year from the promulgation of this Regional Regulation.

Article 75
This Regional Regulation comes into force on the date of promulgation.
In order that everyone knows of it, orders the promulgation of this Regional Regulation by placing it in the Regional Gazette of Dharmasraya Regency.

I. GENERAL

Children are a trust as well as a gift from Almighty God, which we must always protect because in them is inherent dignity, honor, and rights as human beings that must be upheld. Children's human rights are part of human rights contained in the 1945 Constitution and the United Nations Convention on the Rights of the Child. From the perspective of national and state life, children are the future of the nation and the next generation of the nation's ideals, so that every child has the right to survival, growth, and development, participation and the right to protection from acts of violence and discrimination as well as civil rights and freedoms.

Likewise with women, women are expected to give birth to children who are healthy, strong, resilient, noble, potential and quality. Through women's roles in the family, society and state, tough generations will be born who will continue the nation's ideals. As Indonesian citizens, women also have the right to improve quality and develop themselves according to their abilities, dignity and honor.

Women and children need to be protected from discriminatory actions and acts of violence whether physical, psychological, mental, sexual and neglect. Because of their physical weakness compared to men and adults and because of women's nature itself, women and children often become objects of venting desires, anger, economic exploitation, violence (physical, psychological and sexual), cruelty, neglect, and so on. As assets and hopes of the nation and state, children need to be given protection and safeguarded from all forms of violence that will hinder their growth and development.

To realize women's empowerment and child protection, support from statutory regulations is needed that can guarantee its implementation.

Although the protection of women's and children's rights has been regulated in national level statutory regulations, the fulfillment of constitutional rights of women and children as well as improving the quality of life of women and children is one of the mandatory affairs that is the duty, authority and responsibility of the Regional Government, so in providing protection to women and children, the Regional Government needs to establish a Regional Regulation on Women's Empowerment and Child Protection.

This Regional Regulation on Women's Empowerment and Child Protection regulates the following scope:
a. rights of women and children;
b. obligations;
c. improving women's quality of life;
d. protection of women and children;
e. improving family quality;
f. gender and child data system;
g. fulfillment of children's rights;
h. child-friendly regency; and
i. guidance and supervision.
