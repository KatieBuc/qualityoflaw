CHAPTER I
GENERAL PROVISIONS

Article 1
In this Regional Regulation, what is meant by:
1. Region is Wonogiri Regency.
2. Regional Government is the Regent and Regional Apparatus as administrators of Regional Government.
3. Regent is the Regent of Wonogiri.
4. Violence is any act that results in misery or suffering whether physical, mental, sexual, and economic.
5. Gender-Based Violence is any form of restriction, exclusion, differentiation and all forms of treatment carried out on the basis of gender and aimed at reducing, eliminating the recognition, enjoyment or use of human rights, the consequences of which are and are not limited to physical, sexual, psychological and economic violence.
6. Violence Against Children is any form of restriction, differentiation, exclusion and all forms of treatment carried out against children, the consequences of which are and are not limited to physical, sexual, psychological and economic violence.
7. Domestic Violence is any act against a person, especially women, which results in the emergence of misery or suffering physically, sexually, psychologically, and/or domestic neglect including threats to commit acts, coercion, or unlawful deprivation of liberty within the scope of the household.
8. Physical Violence is any act that results in pain, injury, wounds or defects on a person's body, miscarriage, unconsciousness and or causes death.
9. Psychological Violence is any act that results in fear, loss of self-confidence, loss of ability to act, feelings of helplessness and or severe psychological suffering in a person.
10. Sexual Violence is any act in the form of sexual harassment, forced sexual intercourse, forced unnatural or unwanted sexual intercourse, forced sexual intercourse with others for commercial purposes and or certain purposes.
11. Economic Violence is any act that neglects members in the form of not providing a proper livelihood, care or maintenance.
12. Victim is a person who experiences violence and/or threat of violence within the scope of the household.
13. Victim of Gender-Based Violence is a person who due to their gender experiences physical, psychological, economic, social, sexual suffering, and other losses resulting from state policies, acts of violence and or threats of violence within the scope of the household and community.
14. Child Victim of Violence is a child who receives harsh treatment whether physical, psychological, economic, social, sexual, and other losses resulting from state policies, acts of violence and/or threats of violence within the scope of the household and community.
15. Protection is all efforts aimed at providing a sense of security to victims carried out by family, advocates, social institutions, police, prosecutors, courts, or other parties either temporarily or based on court orders.
16. Temporary Protection is protection directly provided by the police and/or social institutions or other parties, before the issuance of a protection order from the court.
17. Protection Order is a decree issued by the Court to provide protection to victims.
18. Child is a person who has not reached the age of 18 (eighteen) years, including children still in the womb.
19. Child Protection is all activities to guarantee and protect children and their rights so they can live, grow, develop, and participate optimally in accordance with human dignity and dignity, and receive protection from violence and discrimination.
20. Family is the smallest unit in society consisting of husband and wife, husband wife and their children, or father and children, or mother and children, or blood relatives in direct line upward or downward up to the third degree.
21. Parents are biological father and/or mother, or stepfather and/or stepmother, or adoptive father and/or mother.
22. Guardian is a person or body that in fact exercises parental authority as a parent over a child.
23. Rights of the Child are part of human rights that must be guaranteed, protected, and fulfilled by parents, family, community, government, and the state.
24. Community is individuals, families, groups, and social organizations and/or community organizations.
25. Accompaniment is all actions in the form of counseling, psychological therapy, advocacy, and spiritual guidance, for strengthening victims of gender-based violence and violence against children to resolve the problems they face.
26. Integrated Service Center hereinafter abbreviated as PPT is a service provider institution for child victims of violence, managed jointly between the Regional Government and the community in the form of medical care (including medico-legal), psychosocial and legal services.
27. Social Reintegration is the process of preparing the community and victims that supports the reunification of victims into the family environment, family substitutes that can provide protection and fulfillment of victims' needs.
28. Safe house (shelter) is temporary housing used to provide protection to victims in accordance with determined operational standards.
29. Gender is a concept that refers to the differentiation of roles and responsibilities of men and women that occur as a result of and can be changed by the social and cultural conditions of society.
30. Paralegal is an individual, group, association, organization/institution that does not have a legal background that provides assistance to fight for rights and justice in society.
31. Counseling is the process of providing assistance carried out by an expert to individuals experiencing a problem that leads to the resolution of the problem faced by the client.

CHAPTER II
PRINCIPLES AND OBJECTIVES

Article 2
Implementation of Protection for Victims of Gender-Based Violence and Children is carried out based on respect and recognition of equal human rights and dignity, non-discrimination, gender equality and justice as well as protection of human rights and the best interests of victims.

Article 3
Implementation of Protection for Victims of Gender-Based Violence and Children aims to provide protection and services to victims of gender-based violence and children and ensure the fulfillment of children's rights so they can live, grow, develop and participate optimally in accordance with human dignity, the best interests of children and receive protection from violence and discrimination, for the realization of quality, noble and prosperous Indonesian children.

CHAPTER III
SCOPE

Article 4
(1) The scope of gender-based violence includes:
a. husband, wife, and children;
b. people who have family relationships with persons as referred to in letter a due to blood relations, marriage, breastfeeding, foster care, and guardianship, who reside in the household, and/or
c. people who work to help the household and reside in that household.
(2) People who work to help the household and reside in the household as referred to in paragraph (1) letter c are considered as family members for the duration of their stay in the household concerned.

CHAPTER IV
PROHIBITION OF DOMESTIC VIOLENCE

Article 5
Everyone is prohibited from committing domestic violence against people within the scope of their household, by means of:
a. physical violence;
b. psychological violence;
c. economic violence;
d. sexual violence; or
e. domestic neglect.

CHAPTER V
RIGHTS OF VICTIMS

Article 6
(1) Victims of gender-based violence and child victims of violence have the right to obtain:
a. protection from family, police, prosecutors, courts, advocates, social institutions or other parties either temporarily or based on protection orders from the court;
b. protection for the security of themselves, family, and property, and freedom from threats related to testimony that will be, is being, or has been given;
c. participation in the process of selecting and determining the form of protection and security support;
d. freedom from ensnaring questions;
e. information regarding case developments and court decisions;
f. services that are fast, appropriate, comfortable, and according to needs.
g. health services according to medical needs;
h. special handling related to victim confidentiality;
i. accompaniment by social workers and legal assistance at every level of the examination process in accordance with statutory provisions and spiritual guidance services;
j. recovery and social reintegration;
k. legal, psychological, spiritual, economic, and social assistance.
(2) Victims' rights as referred to in paragraph (1) are implemented in accordance with applicable statutory provisions.

CHAPTER VI
OBLIGATIONS AND RESPONSIBILITIES

Article 7
(1) The obligations of the Regional Government include:
a. preventing the occurrence of violence;
b. providing protection for victims of violence;
c. providing recovery services and social reintegration;
d. encouraging and increasing community participation;
e. cooperating with service providers in prevention, protection and recovery efforts for victims of violence; and
f. supervising the implementation of services to victims with service standards involving the community;
(2) To carry out the obligations as referred to in paragraph (1), the Regional Government has the authority to:
a. formulate policies and programs concerning the elimination of gender-based violence and children;
b. establish and facilitate the implementation of integrated services;
c. provide facilities and infrastructure;
d. increase the capacity of service provider institutions;
e. coordinate and cooperate in implementing protection for victims of gender-based violence and children;
f. encourage community participation; and
g. conduct monitoring and evaluation.
(3) The Regional Government in carrying out obligations as referred to in paragraph (1) must pay attention to the rights and obligations of parents, guardians, husbands, wives or other persons who are legally responsible for victims.

CHAPTER VII
IMPLEMENTATION OF GENDER-BASED PROTECTION AND CHILD VICTIMS OF VIOLENCE

Part One
Institutional

Article 8
(1) The Regent through Regional Apparatus establishes a community service work unit that functions as PPT.
(2) Regional Apparatus as referred to in paragraph (1) in implementing Protection for Women and Child Victims of Violence may coordinate with other Regional Apparatus.

Part Two
Integrated Service Center

Article 9
(1) PPT as referred to in Article 8 paragraph (1) is established by the Regional Government in accordance with applicable statutory provisions.
(2) The establishment of PPT as referred to in paragraph (1) consists of members from regional government elements, non-governmental institutions, health service institutions, law enforcement officials, professional personnel, volunteer companions, social workers, religious leaders, and safe house (shelter) managers, and social rehabilitation centers.

Article 10
The duties of PPT are to seek prevention, recovery and social reintegration, provide legal protection, coordinate and seek to increase community participation, as well as monitoring and reporting.

Article 11
Prevention efforts as referred to in Article 10 include:
a. conducting communication, information, and education about gender-based violence and children;
b. socialization of statutory regulations related to gender-based violence and children;

Article 12
Recovery and social reintegration efforts as referred to in Article 10 include:
a. providing physical recovery at health service institutions;
b. assisting victim repatriation;
c. providing temporary protection in safe houses (shelters);
d. providing psychosocial recovery and accompaniment;
e. providing spiritual guidance services;
f. preparing families and communities, economic empowerment, and returning to school and/or other educational institutions.

Article 13
Legal Protection as referred to in Article 10 includes:
a. providing protection in safe houses (shelters);
b. providing accompaniment in legal processes at the police, prosecutor and district court levels;
c. providing special legal protection for child victims of violence can be done by appointing guardianship in accordance with applicable regulations.

Article 14
Coordination and Cooperation as referred to in Article 10 include:
a. coordinating and cooperating in handling violence cases with integrated services;
b. coordinating and cooperating with integrated services between regencies/cities;
c. coordinating and cooperating with institutions providing services for victims of gender-based violence and children;
d. coordinating and consulting with the Regional Government.

Article 15
Increasing Community Participation as referred to in Article 10 includes:
a. fostering community awareness of cases of gender-based violence and children;
b. encouraging the community to actively participate in providing information and reporting gender-based violence and children;
c. fostering local wisdom in handling gender-based violence and children;
d. organizing strengthening of community groups in handling gender-based violence and children;
e. disseminating information about statutory regulations related to gender-based violence and children.

Article 16
Monitoring and Reporting as referred to in Article 10, includes monitoring, documentation and reporting of cases of gender-based violence and children.

Article 17
Implementation of integrated services is coordinated by the Regional Work Unit in charge of Women's Empowerment and Child Protection.

Part Three
Cooperation

Article 18
(1) In implementing protection for victims of gender-based violence and children, the Regional Government may cooperate with the Government, Provincial Government, Regency/Municipal Government and other institutions.
(2) Cooperation between Regional Government and the Government and Provincial Government includes consultation, coordination and reporting.
(3) Cooperation between other Regency/Municipal Governments includes coordination, advocacy, referral, repatriation, social reintegration and facilitation of Integrated Service System development, monitoring, evaluation and reporting.

CHAPTER VIII
HANDLING AND MECHANISMS

Article 19
(1) Implementation of protection for victims is carried out in an integrated manner within the PPT framework.
(2) PPT in handling medical, legal, psychological, and economic protection in its implementation may partner with regional apparatus and social community institutions engaged in protection for victims of gender-based violence and children.
(3) Implementation of protection for victims as referred to in paragraph (1) must pay attention to religious norms as well as the rights and obligations of parents, guardians, husbands/other persons who are legally responsible for victims.
(4) Provisions regarding the mechanism for handling protection as referred to in paragraph (1) are regulated by Regent Regulation.

CHAPTER IX
FORMS OF PROTECTION

Article 20
(1) Every victim of gender-based violence and children must receive protection in accordance with applicable provisions.
(2) Protection for victims of gender-based violence and children as referred to in paragraph (1) is carried out for Wonogiri residents, both carried out within Wonogiri Regency and outside Wonogiri Regency.
(3) Protection for victims of gender-based violence and children in addition to being carried out for Wonogiri residents as referred to in paragraph (2), also for non-Wonogiri residents carried out within Wonogiri Regency.

Article 21
(1) Forms of protection for victims of gender-based violence and children are in the form of medical, legal, medicolegal (forensic medicine), economic, and psychological protection.
(2) Forms of medical protection for victims of gender-based violence and children as referred to in paragraph (1) are in the form of treatment and recovery of physical injuries aimed at restoring the physical condition of victims carried out by medical and paramedical personnel free of charge.
(3) Forms of legal protection for victims of gender-based violence and children as referred to in paragraph (1) are in the form of services and accompaniment to assist victims in undergoing legal and judicial processes.
(4) Forms of medicolegal protection for victims of gender-based violence and children as referred to in paragraph (1) are in the form of medical services for evidentiary purposes in the legal field.
(5) Forms of economic protection for victims of gender-based violence and children as referred to in paragraph (1) are in the form of services for skills and providing economic access so victims can be independent.
(6) Forms of psychological protection for victims of gender-based violence and children as referred to in paragraph (1) are in the form of accompaniment in order to restore the traumatic condition of victims including provision of safe houses to protect victims from various threats and intimidation for victims and provide psychological support so victims have self-confidence, strength and independence in problem solving.

CHAPTER X
COMMUNITY PARTICIPATION

Article 22
(1) The community may participate in helping prevention and handling efforts for victims of gender-based violence and children.
(2) Community participation as referred to in paragraph (1) is realized by:
a. providing information and/or reporting any violence known;
b. providing protection for victims;
c. providing emergency assistance;
d. providing advocacy to victims and/or the community regarding handling cases of gender-based violence and children;
e. assisting in the process of submitting applications for protection orders;
f. assisting in the repatriation and reintegration process.
(3) Community participation may be carried out by individuals, groups or social community organizations.
(4) Social community organizations that participate in implementing protection for victims of gender-based violence and children as referred to in paragraph (3) are provided guidance by the Wonogiri Regency Government through regional apparatus according to their main duties and functions.
(5) Guidance as referred to in paragraph (4) may be in the form of counseling guidance, skills guidance, paralegal training and operational technical guidance.

CHAPTER XI
ACCOMPANIMENT

Article 23
Accompaniment is carried out by persons or institutions that have expertise to conduct counseling, therapy and advocacy for strengthening and recovery of victims of violence and have cooperated with PPT.

Article 24
(1) Accompaniment includes all integrated efforts to restore and strengthen the condition of victims, carried out by the Police, Prosecutors, Courts, Advocates, Social Workers, and Women's or Social Organizations concerned with women and children.
(2) Accompaniment by the Police, Prosecutors, Courts and Advocates as referred to in paragraph (1) is in accordance with applicable statutory provisions.

CHAPTER XII
CONTROL, GUIDANCE AND SUPERVISION

Article 25
(1) Control, guidance and supervision of the implementation of protection for victims of gender-based violence and children is carried out by the Regent.
(2) Implementation of control, guidance and supervision as referred to in paragraph (1) on a daily basis is carried out by the Regional Apparatus in charge of Women's Empowerment and Child Protection.

CHAPTER XIII
FINANCING

Article 26
All activities related to the implementation of protection for victims of gender-based violence and children are charged to the Regional Revenue and Expenditure Budget and/or other lawful sources in accordance with statutory provisions.

CHAPTER XIV
CRIMINAL PROVISIONS

Article 27
Criminal acts in the field of child protection are threatened with criminal punishment as regulated in Law Number 23 Year 2002 concerning Child Protection.

CHAPTER XV
TRANSITIONAL PROVISIONS

Article 28
At the time this Regional Regulation comes into force, all implementing regulations related to the implementation of protection for victims of gender-based violence and children are declared to remain in force insofar as they are not contrary to and/or have not been replaced by new regulations.

CHAPTER XVI
CLOSING PROVISIONS

Article 29
This Regional Regulation comes into force on the date of promulgation.

I GENERAL
Gender-based violence and children is a social phenomenon that has existed since ancient times and has become increasingly prevalent recently. In fact, gender-based violence and children is increasing, both in number and in form and modus operandi which is increasingly diverse. Rape, sexual harassment, trafficking in women and children, domestic violence, sexual exploitation, violence against domestic workers, pornography, exploitation of migrant workers, and neglect, will apparently continue to be encountered in daily life, both in the domestic (household) and public spheres.

The factors causing gender-based violence and children are very complex and interrelated. These factors include, among others, legal instruments that have not been able to provide protection to victims, the concept that women and children are family assets, media that do not support reporting on violence against women and children, public services that are not yet optimal, customs that sometimes legalize violence, poverty issues, misinterpretation of religious teachings, all of which are wrapped in patriarchal culture.

Handling of victims of gender-based violence and violence against children occurring in Wonogiri, in accordance with the provisions of Article 3 of Law Number 23 Year 2002 concerning Child Protection and Article 13 of Law Number 23 Year 2004 concerning Elimination of Domestic Violence, that the Regional Government is obligated to establish and develop a system and mechanism of cooperation for handling violence. Furthermore, based on the provisions of Article 26 paragraph (1) letter b of Law Number 32 Year 2004 concerning Regional Government states that the Deputy Regional Head has the duty to assist the Regional Head in coordinating the activities of vertical agencies in the region, following up on reports and/or findings of supervisory apparatus oversight, implementing women's and youth empowerment, and seeking the development and preservation of socio-culture and the environment. Thus it is necessary to establish a Regional Regulation concerning Implementation of Protection for Victims of Gender-Based Violence and Children.

II ARTICLE BY ARTICLE

Article 1
Sufficiently clear

Article 2
- What is meant by "non-discrimination" is protection for all victims of gender-based violence and children without distinguishing ethnicity, religion, race, class, gender, ethnic culture and language, legal status and physical or mental condition.
- What is meant by "gender justice" is fair treatment given to women and men.
- What is meant by "gender equality" is a condition and position that describes harmonious, aligned and balanced relations between men and women in obtaining opportunities/chances in access, participation, control and benefits in development implementation and enjoying development results in family life, as well as in society, nation and state.
- What is meant by "best interests of victims" is all actions concerning victims carried out by the government, society, legislative bodies and judicial bodies, then the best interests of victims must be the primary consideration.

Article 3
- What is meant by being able to live is the child's right to survival as contained in the principles of the Convention on the Rights of the Child, including the right to identity and the right to enjoy the highest attainable health status.
- What is meant by "grow and develop" for children is as contained in the principles of the Convention on the Rights of the Child, including the right to education, the right to play, the right to create and recreate.
