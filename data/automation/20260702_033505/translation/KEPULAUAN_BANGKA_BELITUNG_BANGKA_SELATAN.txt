BY THE GRACE OF ALMIGHTY GOD
REGENT OF SOUTH BANGKA,

Considering: a. that the number of violence against women and children in South Bangka has experienced an increase in the number of incidents. Meanwhile, protection and services for victims have been provided but are still not optimal;

b. that in South Bangka Regency, an Integrated Service Center for the Protection of Women and Children (P2TP2A) for victims of violence has been established, but the basis for its establishment still uses a Regent Regulation which does not yet include the mechanism or implementation of protection that can facilitate victims and protection providers in implementing it;

c. that based on the considerations as referred to in letters a and b, it is necessary to establish a Regional Regulation concerning the Implementation of Protection for Women and Children Victims of Violence;

CHAPTER I
GENERAL PROVISIONS

Article 1

In this Regional Regulation, what is meant by:
1. Region is South Bangka Regency.
2. Regent is the Regent of South Bangka.
3. Regional Government is the Regent and regional apparatus as elements of regional government administration.
4. Regional People's Representative Council hereinafter abbreviated as DPRD is the Regional People's Representative Council of South Bangka Regency as an element of regional government administration.
5. Principle of Respect and Recognition of Human Rights and Dignity is based on respect for victims' rights, justice and gender equality, non-discrimination and the best interests of victims.
6. Integrated Service Center for the Protection of Women and Children hereinafter abbreviated as P2TP2A is an institution providing services to victims of violence, based in hospitals, managed jointly in the form of medical care (including medico-legal), psychological and legal services.
7. Social Community Institution is a Non-Governmental Organization (NGO) and/or other community organizations.
8. Child is a person who has not yet reached the age of 18 (eighteen) years, including those in the womb.
9. Woman is a person who has female genitalia who can menstruate and become pregnant or has obtained legal status as a woman.

10. Violence is any act that results in or may result in misery or suffering whether physical, sexual, economic, social, psychological to the victim.
11. Violence against women is any act based on gender differences that results in or may result in misery or suffering of women physically, sexually, economically, socially, psychologically including threats of certain actions, coercion or deprivation of freedom, whether occurring in public or private life.
12. Violence against children is any act that results in misery or suffering to children physically, sexually, economically, socially, psychologically including threats of certain actions, coercion or deprivation of freedom.
13. Physical violence is any act that causes pain, injury, wounds or disability to a person's body, miscarriage, unconsciousness and/or causes death.
14. Sexual violence is any act in the form of sexual harassment, forced sexual intercourse, forced unnatural or unwanted sexual intercourse, forced sexual intercourse with others for commercial purposes and/or certain purposes.
15. Economic violence is any act that deliberately neglects family members in the form of not providing a decent living, care or maintenance.
16. Psychological violence is any act that causes fear, loss of self-confidence, loss of ability to act, feelings of helplessness and/or severe psychological suffering to a person.
17. Victim is a woman and child who experiences and/or suffers either directly or indirectly as a result of violence.

18. Protection of women is all activities aimed at providing a sense of security carried out by the police, prosecutors, courts, social institutions, or other parties who know or hear that violence against women has occurred or is occurring.
19. Child protection is all activities to guarantee and protect children and their rights so they can live, grow, develop and participate optimally in accordance with human dignity and receive protection from violence and discrimination.
20. Optimal service is a dynamic effort consisting of various regularly related parts, followed by performance offered by one party to another party by providing benefits, in order to achieve the best goal.
21. Information is a message (speech or expression) or collection of messages consisting of an order sequence of symbols, or meaning that can be interpreted from the message or collection of messages and can be recorded or transmitted.
22. Repatriation is an effort to return victims of violence from the Region to the Region of Origin.

CHAPTER II
PRINCIPLES AND OBJECTIVES

Article 2

Implementation of Protection for Women and Children Victims of Violence is carried out based on the principle of respect and recognition of human rights and dignity.

Article 3

Protection of Women and Children Victims of Violence aims to provide protection and services covering aspects of prevention, service and assistance, rehabilitation, reunification and empowerment.

Article 4

Prevention is an activity carried out to prevent the occurrence of acts of violence against women and children in South Bangka.

Article 5

Rehabilitation is an effort to restore the condition of victims related to health recovery and social recovery so they can carry out their roles and functions normally in society again.

Article 6

Service and Assistance is an activity carried out to serve and protect victims of acts of violence against women and children from the time of reporting through the legal process and psychological recovery until the repatriation process.

Article 7

Reunification and Empowerment is an activity carried out to reunite with family, substitute family, or community that can provide protection and fulfillment of needs for victims and empower victims of violence so they can be economically independent.

CHAPTER III
VICTIMS' RIGHTS

Article 8

Every victim has the right to receive:
a. protection;
b. information;
c. optimal service;
d. continuous handling up to the rehabilitation stage; and
e. confidential handling whether individual, group or institution, both government and non-government.

Article 9

In the event of violence, every victim has the right to receive assistance both psychologically and legally and to receive guarantees of their rights related to their status as wife, mother, child, family member or as a member of society.

CHAPTER IV
DUTIES AND OBLIGATIONS OF REGIONAL GOVERNMENT

Article 10

(1) The Regional Government has the duty to carry out prevention, handling and recovery efforts for victims of violence against women and children, by:
a. collecting data and information about women and children victims of violence;
b. socializing legislation related to the protection of women and children victims of violence;
c. conducting education on anti-violence values against children and women; and
d. conducting monitoring and evaluation of the implementation of protection for women and children victims of violence.

(2) The Regional Government is obliged to carry out prevention, handling and recovery efforts for victims of violence against women and children, providing and organizing services for victims in the form of:
a. providing and facilitating the establishment of integrated services for victims by involving institutions and community elements;
b. encouraging public awareness about the importance of protection for victims; and
c. empowering communities for the prevention of acts of violence.

CHAPTER V
COMMUNITY PARTICIPATION

Article 11

(1) The Regional Government in implementing protection for victims of violence involves community participation.
(2) Community participation as referred to in paragraph (1) may be carried out by individuals, groups or social community organizations.
(3) Community participation as referred to in paragraph (2) may be carried out in the form of:
a. preventing the occurrence of acts of violence against women and children;
b. submitting reports to the authorities if acts of violence against women and children occur; and
c. providing assistance to victims.

Article 12

(1) Social community organizations that participate in implementing protection for victims as referred to in Article 11 paragraph (2), are provided guidance by the Regional Government.
(2) Guidance as referred to in paragraph (1) may be in the form of social guidance, skills guidance and operational technical guidance.
(3) Guidance as referred to in paragraph (2) is carried out by the Institution for the Protection of Women and Children established by the Regional Government.

CHAPTER VI
INSTITUTION IMPLEMENTING PROTECTION FOR VICTIMS

Article 13

(1) The Regional Government establishes an Integrated Service Center for the Protection of Women and Children (P2TP2A) as the Implementing Institution for Prevention and Protection of Women and Children Victims of Violence in an integrated manner.
(2) P2TP2A as referred to in paragraph (1) consists of elements of:
a. health;
b. law enforcement;
c. social and empowerment;
d. education and religion;
e. Non-Governmental Organizations (NGOs); and
f. private sector.
(3) Further provisions regarding P2TP2A as referred to in paragraph (1) are regulated by Regent Regulation.

CHAPTER VII
IMPLEMENTATION AND FORMS OF PROTECTION

Article 14

(1) Implementation of protection for victims is carried out in an integrated manner within the P2TP2A framework.
(2) P2TP2A in handling medical, legal, medicolegal, psychological and economic protection may in its implementation establish partnerships with social community institutions engaged in the protection of women and children.
(3) Implementation of protection for victims as referred to in paragraph (1) must pay attention to religious norms, customs and the rights and obligations of parents/guardians, husbands and/or other persons legally responsible for victims.
(4) The mechanism for handling protection as referred to in paragraph (1) is implemented according to Standard Operating Procedures (SOP) regulated by Regent Regulation.

Article 15

(1) Every victim of violence has the right to receive protection in accordance with the provisions of legislation.
(2) Protection for victims as referred to in paragraph (1) is provided to residents of South Bangka Regency both carried out within the South Bangka Regency area and outside the South Bangka Regency area.
(3) In addition to protection for victims who are South Bangka residents as referred to in paragraph (2), protection is also provided to non-South Bangka residents whose incidents were carried out or occurred within the South Bangka Regency area.

Article 16

(1) Forms of protection provided to victims implemented by P2TP2A are in the form of:
a. medical;
b. legal;
c. medico-legal (forensic medicine);
d. psychological; or
e. economic.

(2) The form of medical protection for victims as referred to in paragraph (1) letter a, is in the form of treatment and recovery of physical injuries aimed at restoring the physical condition of victims.
(3) The form of legal protection for victims as referred to in paragraph (1) letter b, is in the form of services and assistance to help victims undergo legal and judicial processes.
(4) The form of medico-legal protection for victims as referred to in paragraph (1) letter c, is in the form of medical services for legal evidentiary purposes.
(5) The form of psychological protection for victims as referred to in paragraph (1) letter d, is in the form of assistance in order to restore traumatic conditions including provision of safe houses to protect victims from various threats and intimidation for victims and provide psychological support so that victims have self-confidence, strength and independence in problem solving.
(5) The form of economic protection for victims as referred to in paragraph (1), is in the form of services for skills and providing economic access so that victims can be independent.

Article 17

Implementation of protection for victims is carried out free of charge, quickly, safely, empathetically, non-discriminatorily, easily accessible and with guaranteed confidentiality.

Article 18

P2TP2A managers are obliged to organize services as regulated in Article 16.

CHAPTER VIII
FUNDING SOURCES

Article 19

(1) Funding sources for implementing victim protection are charged to the Regional Revenue and Expenditure Budget.
(2) Funding as referred to in paragraph (1) is budgeted at the Agency for Women's Empowerment and Child Protection.
(3) In addition to funding sources as referred to in paragraph (1), funds may come from other legitimate and non-binding sources.

CHAPTER IX
REPORTING

Article 20

(1) P2TP2A is obliged to report in writing the implementation of protection for women and children victims of violence to the Regent.
(2) Reports as referred to in paragraph (1) include:
a. administration;

b. finance;
c. services; and
d. performance.

(3) Submission of reports as referred to in paragraph (2) is carried out at least once every 3 (three) months.

CHAPTER X
SANCTIONS

Article 21

(1) Any person who deliberately does not provide protection to women and children resulting in violence, allows violence to occur, and/or does not report or provide protection to victims, is subject to sanctions in accordance with applicable legislation.
(2) P2TP2A managers who carry out service duties, if they violate service principles, may be subject to sanctions in accordance with P2TP2A internal mechanisms.

CHAPTER XI
CLOSING PROVISIONS

Article 22

Regent Regulations implementing this Regional Regulation shall be established no later than 1 (one) year from the promulgation of this Regional Regulation.

Article 23

This Regional Regulation comes into force on the date of promulgation. In order that everyone may know of it, orders the promulgation of this Regional Regulation by placing it in the Regional Gazette of South Bangka Regency.

I. GENERAL

The National Objective of the Unitary State of the Republic of Indonesia, as regulated in the preamble to the 1945 Constitution in the fourth paragraph states it includes: (1) Protecting all Indonesian people and all Indonesian territory, (2) advancing public welfare, (3) educating the nation's life and (4) participating in implementing world order based on (a) independence, (b) eternal peace, and (c) social justice. To achieve these objectives, in accordance with the teachings of trias politica, there are three state powers namely Executive, Legislative, and Judicial. In Indonesia these three powers complement each other, for the implementation of clean and authoritative government from the national level to the regional level.

The Republic of Indonesia is a State of Law (rechtstaat) and not a State of power (machtstaat), so that all actions of its citizens, including those in the form of power, carried out by both men and women, are also regulated by law.

The rights of women and children are recognized as Human Rights (HAM), which must be upheld and respected by all parties, but in reality there are still many women and children who become victims of violence. The Central Government's commitment to protect children and women is reflected in the legislation below it.

Regional autonomy that is implemented has strategic value so that each region forms legislation (regional regulations), in addition to expressing local wisdom, also to become a guideline for regional governments to implement the legislation above it (the 1945 Constitution, and other Laws) to address legal problems and social problems that occur specifically in each region.

This Regional Regulation concerning Implementation of Protection for Women and Children Victims of Violence is based on Pancasila, and based on the 1945 Constitution, and refers to many Laws especially those regulating the protection of women and children victims of violence, including Law No. 7 Year 1984 concerning Ratification of the Elimination of All Forms of Discrimination Against Women, Law No. 39 Year 1999 Concerning Human Rights, Law No. 23 Year 2004 concerning Elimination of Domestic Violence, Law No. 35 Year 2014 concerning Amendment to Law No. 23 Year 2002 Concerning Child Protection, and other related Laws.

With the provisions of the Law above, we can understand how great the Central Government's attention is to protect its citizens who are in an unpleasant position, namely when becoming victims of violence, but in reality the legal process is still rarely carried out for victims of violence which today are increasingly numerous and complex in their problems. And in South Bangka very few existing problems can be resolved legally due to ignorance of the community and the absence of specific legal regulations that become legal regulations that can be used in the region.

Hopefully with the enactment of this Regional Regulation on Implementation of Protection for Women and Children Victims of Violence, in South Bangka Regency in particular, handling and rehabilitation as well as reunification can be carried out properly, and the process of preventing violence against women and children becomes smoother, so that there is less and eventually no more violence against women and children.
