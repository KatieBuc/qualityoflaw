THE MAYOR OF SEMARANG,

Considering: a. that women are a national asset who play a very important role in the process of continuation and creation of quality generations and need to receive guarantees for the fulfillment of their rights to be empowered so they can actualize their potential optimally and receive guarantees of protection from acts of violence against women;

b. that in order to provide means for women's self-actualization and guarantees of protection in society in the city of Semarang, there needs to be a regional regulation that regulates the empowerment and protection of women;

c. that based on Law Number 23 Year 2014 concerning Regional Government as has been amended several times most recently by Law Number 6 Year 2023 concerning Stipulation of Government Regulation in Lieu of Law Number 2 Year 2022 concerning Job Creation into Law, regional governments have authority in the administration of governmental affairs in the field of empowerment and protection of women;

d. that based on the considerations as referred to in letter a, letter b, and letter c, it is necessary to stipulate a Regional Regulation concerning Empowerment and Protection of Women;

In View of: 1. Article 18 paragraph (6) of the 1945 Constitution of the Republic of Indonesia;

CHAPTER I
GENERAL PROVISIONS

Article 1

In this Regional Regulation, what is meant by:
1. Region is Semarang City.
2. Regional Government is the Mayor as an element of regional government administration who leads the implementation of governmental affairs that are the authority of the autonomous region.
3. Mayor is the Mayor of Semarang.
4. Regional Apparatus is an auxiliary element of the Mayor and Regional People's Representative Council in the administration of governmental affairs that are the authority of the Region.
5. Human Rights are a set of rights inherent in the nature and existence of humans as creatures of Almighty God and are His gift that must be respected, upheld and protected by the state, law, government, and every person for the sake of honor and protection of human dignity and worth.
6. Women's Empowerment is an effort to obtain access and control over economic, political, social, and cultural resources, so that women can manage themselves and increase self-confidence to be able to play a role and participate actively in solving problems, so as to be able to build capacity and self-concept.

-3-

7. Women's Protection is all efforts to protect and provide a sense of security to women and the fulfillment of their rights through consistent, structured, and systematic attention aimed at achieving gender equality.
8. Violence Against Women is any act based on gender differences that results or may result in suffering or hardship for women physically, sexually, and psychologically, including threats of certain actions, arbitrary deprivation of liberty, whether occurring in the private or public sphere.
9. Gender is the values, roles, and responsibilities of women and men that are socially and culturally constructed by society.
10. Gender Equality is an equal condition and position between men and women to obtain their rights as human beings, in accessing and controlling resources, participating in all development processes and decision-making, and obtaining benefits from development.

Article 2

Empowerment and Protection of Women is implemented based on the principles of:
a. respect for human rights;
b. non-discrimination;
c. gender equality and gender justice;
d. justice and legal certainty;
e. benefit;
f. participatory;
g. accountability; and
h. inclusion.

Article 3

Empowerment and Protection of Women aims to:
a. improve the quality of life of women;
b. increase women's participation in the development process;
c. encourage women's leadership and women's bargaining position in decision-making in various development processes, both planning, implementation and monitoring and evaluation;
d. improve the ability of women to manage the economy, both in meeting household needs and in opening productive and independent employment opportunities;
e. provide protection of women's rights from various forms of violence and other discriminatory treatment;
f. prevent all forms of Violence Against Women;

-4-

g. provide services and recovery for women victims of violence.
h. increase community participation in Women's Protection;
i. realize a safe social life free from all forms of Violence Against Women; and
j. increase strengthening and development of Women's Empowerment service provider institutions.

Article 4

The scope of this Regional Regulation includes:
a. authority and responsibility of regional government;
b. women's rights;
c. Women's Empowerment;
d. Women's Protection;
e. strategy for Empowerment and Protection of Women;
f. mechanism for organizing Empowerment and Protection of Women;
g. institutions;
h. participation;
i. guidance and supervision; and
j. funding.

CHAPTER III
WOMEN'S RIGHTS

Article 6

Every Woman has the right to:
a. live and maintain life and improve her standard of living;
b. fulfill her basic needs to grow and develop properly, have a family and continue offspring;
c. develop her personality, to obtain education, and improve her quality of life to become a faithful, pious, responsible, noble, happy, and prosperous human being in accordance with human rights;
d. obtain justice, security, and freedom to express opinions without discrimination;
e. be involved in every stage of the development process;
f. be free from slavery or servitude and threats;
g. obtain protection of self, family, honor, dignity and property rights;
h. obtain welfare and a decent life;
i. participate in politics;
j. perform legal acts; and
k. freely choose a partner in marriage in accordance with the provisions of laws and regulations.

CHAPTER IV
WOMEN'S EMPOWERMENT

Part One
General

Article 7

Women's Empowerment is directed to play a role and participate in the fields of:
a. health;
b. education;
c. economy;
d. social culture;
e. politics and government;
f. law;
g. employment;
h. social security; and
i. environmental preservation.

-6-

Part Two
Health Sector

Article 8

(1) Women's Empowerment in the Health Sector is implemented to increase access to health services for women in all aspects of health.
(2) Women's Empowerment in the Health Sector as referred to in paragraph (1) is implemented through promotive, preventive, curative and rehabilitative approaches.

Part Three
Education Sector

Article 9

Women's Empowerment in the Education Sector through:
a. increasing awareness of average length of schooling, school participation rates, and completed education.
b. organizing formal, non-formal, and informal education for women in the Region.

Part Three
Economic Sector

Article 10

Women's Empowerment in the Economic Sector is implemented through:
a. providing skills and job training;
b. facilitating the formation, strengthening, and development of productive economic and creative economy business groups;
c. facilitating capital; and
d. facilitating the development of marketing networks.

Part Four
Social and Cultural Sector

Article 11

Women's Empowerment in the social and cultural sector is implemented through:
a. increasing knowledge, attitudes and skills to encourage the fulfillment of tiered education according to potential to improve social status;
b. increasing awareness of various social problems in society through communication, information and education services;
c. increasing awareness and knowledge about planning for independent, healthy and prosperous families including access to consultation services and marriage registration; and
d. facilitating and efforts to preserve customs and develop science, technology, arts and culture for the advancement of women.

-7-

Part Five
Politics and Government Sector

Article 12

Women's Empowerment in the politics and government sector is implemented through:
a. involving women in decision-making at various levels;
b. involving women in development planning at various levels;
c. encouraging increased involvement of women in organizations and providing opportunities for women to hold public positions;
d. facilitating women's participation in general elections;
e. self-development through organizations to associate, assemble and express opinions;
f. facilitating capacity building for prospective legislative members, political party members and/or women's community organizations in order to meet women's representation in legislative institutions of at least 30% (thirty percent);
g. facilitating increased women's political participation in legislative institutions;
h. facilitating facilities and infrastructure for women's community organizations or community organizations whose vision and mission is to fight for the fulfillment of women's rights in the Region;
i. conducting socialization in order to increase awareness of women's rights and women's participation in politics; and
j. encouraging the formation of women's organizations and/or forums.

Part Six
Legal Sector

Article 13

(1) Women's Empowerment in the legal sector through:
a. increasing awareness and knowledge in the legal field with communication, information and education services; and
b. facilitating access and legal consultation services as well as Gender-responsive legal aid.
(2) Women's Empowerment in the legal sector as referred to in paragraph (1) is implemented in accordance with laws and regulations.

Part Seven
Employment Sector

Article 14

Women's Empowerment in the employment sector is implemented through:
a. providing access to enter the labor market;
b. freedom for women to become members of labor unions and lead labor unions; and

-8-

c. access for women to be involved in policy-making related to wages, basic rights of workers and other aspects closely related to employment.

Part Eight
Social Security Sector

Article 15

Women's Empowerment in the social security sector is implemented through:
a. facilitating social rehabilitation guidance for women with social welfare problems; and
b. encouraging increased involvement of women in overcoming social problems.

Part Nine
Environmental Preservation Sector

Article 16

Women's Empowerment in the environmental preservation sector is implemented through:
a. increasing the capacity of women, especially related to increasing environmental knowledge so that it can be highly beneficial for environmentally friendly development;
b. increasing environmental preservation education and environmental management in households;
c. increasing waste management education in the surrounding environment; and
d. increasing access for women to participate in environmental development.

Article 17

Further provisions regarding the implementation of Women's Empowerment as referred to in Article 8 through Article 16 are regulated in a Mayor Regulation.

CHAPTER V
WOMEN'S PROTECTION

Part One
General

Article 18

(1) The organization of Women's Protection through:
a. prevention of violence against women;
b. services for women victims of violence; and
c. strengthening Women's Protection institutions.

-9-

(2) The organization of Women's Protection as referred to in Article 18 can be provided to vulnerable women groups.
(3) Vulnerable women groups as referred to in paragraph (2) include:
a. poor women;
b. women with disabilities;
c. women with HIV/AIDS;
d. migrant women workers;
e. elderly women;
f. indigenous women;
g. women in disaster and social conflict situations;
h. women heads of households;
i. informal women workers;
j. women in situations of intolerance;
k. women victims/exposed to NAPZA (narcotics, psychotropics, and addictive substances);
l. women victims/exposed to terrorism, radicalism and extremism; and/or
m. other vulnerable women groups.

Part Two
Prevention of Violence Against Women

Article 19

(1) Prevention of Violence Against Women as referred to in Article 18 letter a can be carried out in the following forms:
a. developing communication media, information, education and public campaigns;
b. developing educational materials and curricula;
c. developing safe transportation systems and public spaces;
d. building early detection systems, security and integrated complaint services in industrial areas, companies, residential areas, educational environments, Islamic boarding schools and other public spaces;
e. providing consultation, information, education, and counseling services;
f. developing policies related to safe and friendly schools/madrasas, boarding schools, Islamic boarding schools, and universities;
g. forming and developing cadres, communities, and groups from among youth, students, santri, university students, journalists, social media influencers, educators, and religious leaders;
h. conducting education and advocacy to owners, managers and/or users of social media;
i. developing protection systems and special support for vulnerable women groups;
j. raising awareness among perpetrators;

-10-

k. increasing the capacity of regional governments, government agencies, institutions, community institutions, business world, Islamic boarding schools, community organizations, media, religious leaders, community leaders and communities;
l. conducting cooperation with religious institutions, social community institutions, service institutions, universities, Islamic boarding schools, media, youth organizations, religious leaders and community leaders; and
m. conducting socialization of laws and regulations.
(2) Forms of prevention of Violence Against Women as referred to in paragraph (1) are implemented in accordance with laws and regulations.

Part Three
Services for Women Victims of Violence

Article 20

Women victims of violence receive the following rights:
a. the right to be respected for dignity and worth as human beings;
b. the right to recovery;
c. the right to determine their own decisions;
d. the right to easy access to information;
e. the right to confidentiality;
f. the right to social rehabilitation;
g. the right to quick, appropriate, comfortable and needs-based complaint handling;
h. the right of victims and their families to receive facilities in the judicial process;
i. the right to assistance; and
j. the right to security.

Article 21

Services for women victims of violence can be carried out through:
a. receiving reports and/or outreach to victims of violence;
b. providing information about the rights of victims of violence;
c. facilitating the provision of health services;
d. facilitating the provision of psychological strengthening services;
e. facilitating the provision of psychosocial services, social rehabilitation, social empowerment, repatriation, and social reintegration;
f. providing legal services;
g. identifying economic empowerment needs;
h. identifying temporary shelter needs for victims of violence and families of victims of violence that need to be met immediately;
i. facilitating the needs of victims of violence with disabilities;

-11-

j. coordinating and cooperating on the fulfillment of the rights of victims of violence with other institutions; and
k. monitoring the fulfillment of the rights of victims of violence by law enforcement officials during the judicial process.

Part Four
Strengthening Women's Protection Institutions

Article 22

(1) Strengthening Women's Protection institutions can be carried out through capacity building, cooperation and integration with:
a. government agencies;
b. educational institutions;
c. religious institutions;
d. health service provider institutions;
e. professional institutions;
f. business world;
g. service institutions;
h. Islamic boarding schools;
i. youth organizations;
j. legal aid organizations;
k. media;
l. religious leaders;
m. community leaders;
n. communities; and
o. families.
(2) Strengthening and developing Women's Protection service provider institutions is implemented through:
a. capacity building of human resources of Women's Protection service provider institutions for victims of violence at the Regional level;
b. providing specific needs for women in emergency situations and special conditions at the Regional level;
c. developing communication, information, and education strategies about women's rights protection at the Regional level;
d. strengthening networks between Women's Protection service provider institutions at the Regional level;
e. facilitating efforts to fulfill standards of Women's Protection service provider institutions at the Regional level;
f. developing Women's Protection service provider institutions at the Regional level.

I. GENERAL

The 1945 Constitution of the Republic of Indonesia, mandates the authority of regional governments to regulate and manage their own governmental affairs directed to accelerate the realization of community welfare through improved services, Empowerment and community participation. The authority of Regional Government is to make regional policies to provide services, increase participation, initiative, and community Empowerment aimed at improving people's welfare.

The implementation of regional autonomy must always be oriented towards improving community welfare by always paying attention to the interests and aspirations that grow in society. Therefore, a governmental affair related to basic services such as basic education, health, fulfillment of minimum living needs, basic environmental infrastructure is mandatory to be fulfilled.

Human rights are basic rights that are inherently attached to human beings, are universal and lasting, so they must be protected, respected, maintained, and may not be ignored, reduced, or seized by anyone. The 1945 Constitution of the Republic of Indonesia, especially Article 27 paragraph (1) which states that "All citizens have equal status in law and government and are obliged to uphold the law and government without exception". Although the 1945 Constitution of the Republic of Indonesia guarantees equal status for every citizen, until now women still experience various obstacles in obtaining their rights in almost all areas of life.

This has an impact, women as part of the national development process, namely as actors as well as beneficiaries of development results, have not been able to obtain optimal benefits in enjoying development results equal to men, especially in the process of policy formulation and decision-making, as well as in the implementation of development in all fields and at all levels. On the other hand, women have special needs compared to men so that in accordance with their nature, in certain conditions, the Regional Government must be able to guarantee that their rights are fulfilled according to those needs.

-17-

Based on this, a regulation is needed that becomes a guideline for all parties, especially the Regional Government in ensuring the implementation of Empowerment and Protection of Women in Semarang City. This Regional Regulation is expected to be able to encourage efforts to improve the physical and mental conditions of women in fulfilling their rights and life needs as part of human rights from various fields of development, increase the commitment and effectiveness of regional governments in implementing policies, programs and activities and encourage the institutional capacity of government and society that improves the quality of life of women.

Article 2
Letter a
What is meant by "Principle of respect for human rights" contains the understanding that the Republic of Indonesia recognizes and upholds human rights and basic human freedoms as rights that are inherently attached to and inseparable from humans, which must be protected, respected, and upheld for the sake of improving human dignity, welfare, happiness, and intelligence as well as justice.

Letter b
What is meant by "Principle of non-discrimination" is that in providing protection to women and children victims of acts of violence, there is no differentiation based on age, gender, race, ethnicity, tribe, religion and between groups.

Letter c
What is meant by "Principle of gender equality and justice" is that gender justice is a process to be fair to men and women. Gender equality is the equality of conditions for men and women to obtain opportunities and their rights as human beings, to be able to play a role and participate in political, economic, social and cultural activities, government and equality in enjoying development results.

Letter d
What is meant by "Principle of justice and legal certainty" contains the understanding that everyone has the right to recognition, guarantee, protection and fair legal treatment and to obtain legal certainty and equal treatment before the law.

-18-

Letter g
What is meant by "Principle of accountability" concerns that the organization of women's empowerment must be accountable and is part of the accountability of government implementation.

Letter h

Letter b
What is meant by basic rights of workers includes:
a. basic right to obtain decent wages.
b. right to get equal opportunities and treatment from the company without discrimination.
c. right to get job training to improve and develop work competence.
d. right to perform work according to the specified time.
e. right to employment placement.
f. right to get protection for occupational health and safety.
g. right to get welfare through workers' social security.
h. right to participate in workers' or laborers' unions.
i. right to get leave: at least 12 working days after the employee has worked for one year continuously.
j. right to rest: workers after working four hours continuously, get a rest opportunity for at least half an hour.
k. right to maternity leave, miscarriage and menstrual leave specifically for female workers/laborers:
Female workers/laborers are entitled to rest for 1.5 (one and a half) months before the time of giving birth and 1.5 (one and a half) months after giving birth according to the calculation of an obstetrician or midwife.
Female workers/laborers who experience miscarriage are entitled to rest for 1.5 (one and a half) months or according to a certificate from an obstetrician or midwife.
Female workers/laborers who feel pain during menstruation and notify the employer, are not required to work on the first and second days during menstruation.
l. right to perform worship.
m. right to conduct strikes.
n. right to get severance pay if termination of employment or layoffs occurs.
