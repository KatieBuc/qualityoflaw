d. that based on the considerations as referred to in letter a, letter b, and letter c, it is necessary to establish a Regional Regulation concerning Protection of Women and Children from Acts of Violence;

CHAPTER I
GENERAL PROVISIONS

Article 1

In this Regional Regulation, what is meant by:
1. Region is Barito Kuala Regency.
2. Regional Government is the Regent together with regional apparatus as elements of Regional Government administration of Barito Kuala Regency.
3. Agency for Family Planning, Women's Empowerment and Child Protection, abbreviated as BKBP3A is the Regional Work Unit of Barito Kuala Regency.
4. Integrated Service Center for Women's Empowerment and Child Protection, hereinafter abbreviated as P2TP2A, is an integrated service unit established by the Regional Government to provide services to women and children victims of violence comprehensively through information services, assistance and legal aid, counseling services, medical services and safe houses through referral.
5. Task Force for Prevention and Handling of Criminal Trafficking in Persons, hereinafter referred to as Task Force is a coordinating institution tasked with coordinating prevention and handling efforts for criminal acts of trafficking in persons in Barito Kuala Regency.
6. Woman is a person who has female genitalia who can menstruate and become pregnant or has obtained legal status as a woman.
7. Child is a person who has not reached 18 (eighteen) years of age, including those in the womb.
8. Discrimination is any distinction, harassment or exclusion that is direct or indirect based on human differentiation on the basis of religion, ethnicity, race, ethnic group, class, social status, socio-economic status, gender, language, political beliefs which results in reduction, deviation or elimination of recognition, implementation or use of human rights and basic freedoms in life both individually and collectively in the political, economic, legal, social, cultural fields and other aspects of life.
9. Violence is any act that results in or can result in misery or suffering whether physical, sexual, economic, social, psychological to the victim;
10. Violence against women is any action based on gender differences that results in or may result in misery or suffering of women physically, sexually, economically, socially, psychologically including threats of certain actions, coercion or deprivation of freedom, whether occurring in public or private life;
11. Violence against children is any action that results in misery or suffering to children physically, sexually, economically, socially, psychologically including threats of certain actions, coercion or deprivation of freedom.
12. Domestic violence is any act against a person, especially women, which results in misery or suffering physically, sexually, psychologically and or domestic neglect including threats to commit acts of coercion or deprivation of freedom unlawfully within the scope of the household;
13. Trafficking in persons is the act of recruitment, transportation, harboring, sending, transfer, or receipt of a person by threat of violence, use of violence, abduction, confinement, forgery, fraud, and abuse of power or vulnerable position, debt bondage or giving payment or benefits so as to obtain consent from the person holding control over that other person, whether conducted within the country or between countries, for the purpose of exploitation or resulting in the person being exploited;
14. Physical violence is any act that causes pain, injury, wounds or disability to a person's body, miscarriage, fainting and or causing death.
15. Sexual violence is any act in the form of sexual harassment, forced sexual intercourse, forced unnatural or unwanted sexual intercourse, forced sexual intercourse with others for commercial purposes and or certain purposes.
16. Economic violence is any act that deliberately neglects family members in the form of not providing livelihood, care or maintenance properly.
17. Psychological violence is any act that causes fear, loss of self-confidence, loss of ability to act, feelings of helplessness and or severe psychological suffering to a person.
18. Victim is a woman and child who experiences and/or suffers either directly or indirectly as a result of violence.
19. Protection of women is all activities aimed at providing security carried out by the Police, Prosecutor's Office, Courts, Social Institutions, or other parties who know or hear or violence against women has occurred.
20. Child protection is all activities to guarantee and protect children and their rights so they can live, grow, develop and participate optimally in accordance with human dignity and receive protection from violence and discrimination.
21. Companion is a person who has expertise in accompanying victims to conduct counseling, therapy and advocacy for strengthening and recovery of victims of violence.
22. Optimal service is a dynamic effort consisting of various regularly related parts, followed by performance offered by one party to another party by providing benefits, in order to achieve the best goal.
23. Information is a message (speech or expression) or collection of messages consisting of order sequences of symbols, or meanings that can be interpreted from messages or collections of messages and can be recorded or transmitted.
24. Parents are biological father and/or mother, or step father and/or mother, or adoptive father and or mother;
25. Family is the smallest unit in society consisting of husband and wife, or husband, wife and their children, or father and his children, and mother and her children and among those in their dependents.
26. Society is individuals, families, groups, social organizations and/or community organizations.

CHAPTER II
PRINCIPLES AND OBJECTIVES

Article 2

Protection of women and children from acts of violence is organized based on the principles of:
a. humanity;
b. justice and gender equality;
c. protection;
d. best interests of women and children; and
e. non-discrimination.

Article 3

The objectives of protection of women and children from acts of violence are to:
a. prevent acts of violence against women and children including trafficking in persons;
b. eliminate all forms of violence and exploitation against women and children;
c. protect, provide security for women and children;
d. provide services to women and children victims of acts of violence, reporters, and witnesses; and
e. facilitate and mediate domestic disputes to realize harmonious and prosperous household integrity.

CHAPTER III
RIGHTS OF WOMEN AND CHILDREN
FROM ACTS OF VIOLENCE

Article 4

Women and children victims of acts of violence receive the following rights:
a. right to be respected for their dignity as human beings;
b. right to health and psychological recovery from suffering experienced by victims;
c. right to determine their own decisions;
d. right to obtain information;
e. right to confidentiality;
f. right to compensation;
g. right to social rehabilitation;
h. right to complaint handling;
i. right of victims and their families to obtain facilities in the judicial process; and
j. right to assistance.

Article 5

Child victims of acts of violence, in addition to receiving the rights as referred to in Article 4, also receive the following special rights:
a. right to respect and full use for survival, growth and development;
b. right to basic services;
c. right to equal protection;
d. right to be free from various stigma; and
e. right to obtain freedom.

CHAPTER IV
OBLIGATIONS AND RESPONSIBILITIES

Article 6

Obligations and responsibilities in organizing protection of women and children from acts of violence are the joint responsibility of:
a. Regional Government;
b. Society;
c. Family;
d. Parents;
e. Related institutions both directly and indirectly.

Article 7

(1) The obligations and responsibilities of regional government as referred to in Article 6 letter a, include:
a. implementing policies for protection of women and children from acts of violence established by the government;
b. establishing policies, programs, and activities for protection of women and children from acts of violence;
c. conducting cooperation in organizing protection of women and children from acts of violence;
d. providing support for facilities and infrastructure for implementation of protection of women and children from acts of violence;
e. allocating budget for organizing protection of women and children from acts of violence according to regional financial capacity; and
f. fostering and supervising the organization of protection of women and children from acts of violence.
(2) In order to carry out obligations and responsibilities as referred to in paragraph (1), the regional government establishes action programs and activities for protection of women and children in 1 (one) Regional Action Plan as a basis for SKPD in implementing protection of women and children from acts of violence.
(3) The Regional Action Plan as referred to in paragraph (2), is part of the Regional Medium-Term Development Plan (RPJMD).
(4) Further provisions regarding the Regional Action Plan as referred to in paragraph (2), are regulated by Regent Regulation.

Article 8

(1) The obligations and responsibilities of society as referred to in Article 6 letter b, are organized in the form of community participation.
(2) Forms of community participation as referred to in paragraph (1), include:
a. preventing acts of violence against women and children;
b. providing information and/or reporting acts of violence against women and children to law enforcement or authorized parties; and
c. participating in providing assistance and/or handling victims of acts of violence.
(3) Forms of community participation as referred to in paragraph (2), are implemented responsibly in accordance with the provisions of legislation.

Article 9

The obligations of family and/or parents as referred to in Article 6 letters c and d, who legally have full responsibility to prevent all forms of violence and protect women and children as family members.

CHAPTER V
PREVENTION OF ACTS OF VIOLENCE

Article 10

(1) To prevent acts of violence against women and children, the regional government conducts empowerment and awareness to families, parents, and communities by providing information, guidance and/or counseling.
(2) In addition to empowerment and awareness as referred to in paragraph (1), the Regional government conducts efforts as follows:
a. increasing the quantity and quality of education both formal and non-formal and informal;
b. opening accessibility to obtain education, training, funding, income improvement and social services;
c. opening employment opportunities for women;
d. building community participation and concern for prevention of protection of women and children from acts of violence;
e. building and providing complete and easily accessible information systems;
f. building networks and cooperation with law enforcement apparatus, government apparatus, universities and various non-governmental organizations that work and/or care about women and children; and
g. opening complaint posts for protection of women and children from acts of violence.

Article 11

(1) Prevention of acts of violence against women and children as referred to in Article 10, is implemented by SKPD whose duties and functions are in the fields of:
a. social affairs;
b. health;
c. education;
d. manpower;
e. women's empowerment and child protection;
f. mental and spiritual; and
g. peace and order.
(2) Prevention of acts of violence by SKPD as referred to in paragraph (1), is implemented in an integrated and continuous manner based on the Regional Action Plan.

CHAPTER VI
SERVICES FOR VICTIMS OF ACTS OF VIOLENCE

Article 12

(1) Forms of services provided to women and children victims of acts of violence, are as follows:
a. complaint services;
b. health services and psychological services;
c. legal aid;
d. repatriation;
e. rehabilitation, social reintegration; and
f. assistance services.
(2) Forms of services as referred to in paragraph (1), are implemented according to minimum service standards established by the government and implemented by SKPD whose duties and functions are in the fields of:
a. social affairs;
b. health;
c. women's empowerment and child protection;
d. mental and spiritual;
e. peace and order
(3) In carrying out duties and functions as referred to in paragraph (1), the Regional Government cooperates with government agencies, other regional governments, and society.
(4) Further provisions regarding procedures for handling services for women and children from acts of violence as referred to in paragraphs (1), (2), and (3), are regulated by Regent Regulation.

Article 13

The regional government is obliged to provide complaint services for protection of women and children from acts of violence.

Article 14

The regional government and society or social service institutions may establish recovery houses or safe houses.

Article 15

Further provisions regarding complaint services and social services as referred to in Articles 13 and 14, are regulated by Regent Regulation.

CHAPTER VII
INSTITUTIONS

Article 16

(1) In the framework of providing protection services to women and children from acts of violence, the regional government establishes P2TP2A as an integrated service center for protection of women and children from acts of violence including victims of acts of violence.
(2) P2TP2A as referred to in paragraph (1), functions as an integrated service center in providing protection to women and children from acts of violence.
(3) Further provisions regarding the Organization and Working Procedures of P2TP2A as referred to in paragraph (1), are stipulated by Regent Regulation.

Article 17

In addition to establishing P2TP2A as referred to in Article 16, to support the implementation of protection of women and children from acts of violence, the Regent establishes:
a. task force for criminal acts of trafficking in persons;
b. regional action committee for elimination of the worst forms of child labor.

Article 18

(1) The task force as referred to in Article 17 letter a, consists of representatives from regional government, law enforcement, community organizations, non-governmental organizations, professional organizations, and researchers/academics.
(2) The task force as referred to in paragraph (1), is a coordinating institution tasked with coordinating prevention and handling of trafficking in persons.
(3) Further provisions regarding the establishment, duties, and functions of the task force as referred to in paragraph (1), are stipulated by Regent Regulation.

Article 19

(1) The committee as referred to in Article 17 letter b, consists of representatives from regional government, law enforcement, community organizations, non-governmental organizations, professional organizations, and researchers/academics.
(2) Further provisions regarding the duties and functions of the committee as referred to in paragraph (1), are stipulated by Regent Decree.

I. GENERAL

The State has an obligation to provide security to its citizens from threats and actions that can disturb and damage psychological (psychic), physical, sexual and economic security. This is philosophically stated in the preamble to the 1945 Constitution, that the purpose of establishing the Unitary State of the Republic of Indonesia is to protect the entire Nation and all Indonesian territory.

Therefore, the Government of Indonesia has signed the Universal Declaration of Human Rights (UDHR 1948) and ratified CEDAW (Law Number 7 Year 1984 concerning Elimination of All Forms of Discrimination Against Women), and is obliged to fulfill these provisions.

Human Rights state that, enforcement and fulfillment of Human Rights is the responsibility of the State, especially the Government. Through Law Number 23 Year 2002 concerning Child Protection, the Government recognizes the existence of children's rights. The inherent rights of children include basic rights as human beings, namely the Right to Life, Right to Growth and Development, Right to Protection and Right to Participation.

Protection is needed to prevent physical, mental and sexual violence and exploitation. To fulfill the right to growth and development, children need space to play, exercise, and education appropriate to their physical and psychological development. In fulfilling children's rights, every government administrator, society and parents must understand and care about children's rights. The existence of legal instruments and legal apparatus that defend children's interests is needed for this protection effort. Legislation that is principled to defend the best interests of the child is needed to realize protection that is legal in nature.

The Government of Barito Kuala Regency has established an Integrated Service Center for Women's Empowerment and Child Protection (P2TP2A) as a center for empowerment of women victims of acts of violence which specifically has the main duties and functions for empowerment of women and children victims of acts of violence.

From the above framework, the Government of Barito Kuala Regency is responsible for taking legal actions to prevent, suppress, reduce, and eliminate all forms of violence against women and children, by creating a Regional Regulation of Barito Kuala Regency concerning Protection of Women and Children from Acts of Violence.

Article 12
Paragraph (1)
Letter a
What is meant by complaint services is a series of actions carried out by the organizer of integrated service institutions to follow up on reports of acts of violence against women and children submitted by victims, families and/or society.
Letter b
What is meant by health services is efforts that include promotive, preventive, curative and rehabilitative aspects.
Letter c
What is meant by legal aid is legal services provided by legal companions or advocates to conduct the process of accompanying witnesses and/or victims of violence against women and children that is gender sensitive.
Letter d
What is meant by repatriation is returning women and children victims of violence from abroad to the debarkation/entry point or from the receiving area to the area of origin.
Letter e
What is meant by social rehabilitation is services aimed at restoring and developing the ability of a person who experiences social dysfunction so they can carry out their social functions properly.
What is meant by social reintegration is an effort to reunite victims with family, substitute family or society that can provide protection and fulfillment of needs for victims.
Letter f
What is meant by assistance services is that victims have the right to be accompanied by social workers at the investigation, prosecution or examination level.
Paragraph (2)
What is meant by minimum service standards is a performance benchmark for integrated service units in providing complaint handling services, health services, social rehabilitation, enforcement and legal aid, as well as repatriation and social reintegration for women and children victims of violence.
