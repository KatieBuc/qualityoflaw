REGENT OF LAMANDAU
PROVINCE OF CENTRAL KALIMANTAN
REGIONAL REGULATION OF LAMANDAU REGENCY
NUMBER 16 YEAR 2015
CONCERNING
INTEGRATED SERVICES FOR WOMEN AND CHILDREN EMPOWERMENT
BY THE GRACE OF ALMIGHTY GOD
REGENT OF LAMANDAU,

Considering : a. that acts of violence against women and children that can cause physical and psychological victims constitute violations of human rights, discrimination against women and children and crimes against human dignity, thus hindering the creation of justice and gender equality;
b. that protection for women and children victims of violence is an effort to provide a sense of security and freedom from all acts of violence guaranteed by laws and regulations;
c. that the regional government is obligated to regulate and manage the handling and provide services for women and children victims of violence covering aspects of law enforcement, legal protection, health services, and spiritual matters;
d. that to improve protection for women and children victims of violence, it is necessary to establish a facility that can provide integrated services;
e. that based on the considerations as stated in letters a, b, c and d, it is necessary to establish a Regional Regulation of Lamandau Regency concerning Integrated Services for Women and Children Empowerment.

Considering : 1. Law Number 4 Year 1979 concerning Child Welfare (State Gazette of the Republic of Indonesia Year 1979 Number 32, Supplement to the State Gazette of the Republic of Indonesia Number 3143);

2. Law Number 7 Year 1984 concerning Ratification of the Convention on the Elimination of All Forms of Discrimination Against Women (Convention On The Elimination Of All Forms Of Discrimination Against Women) (State Gazette of the Republic of Indonesia Year 1984 Number 29, Supplement to the State Gazette of the Republic of Indonesia Number 3668);
3. Law Number 20 Year 1999 concerning Ratification of ILO CONVENTION Number 138 Concerning Minimum Age for Admission to Employment (State Gazette of the Republic of Indonesia Year 1999 Number 56, Supplement to the State Gazette of the Republic of Indonesia Number 3835);
4. Law Number 39 Year 1999 concerning Human Rights (State Gazette of the Republic of Indonesia Year 1999 Number 165, Supplement to the State Gazette of the Republic of Indonesia Number 3886);
5. Law Number 5 Year 2002 concerning Establishment of Katingan Regency, Seruyan Regency, Sukamara Regency, Lamandau Regency, Gunung Mas Regency, Pulang Pisau Regency, Murung Raya Regency, East Barito Regency in Central Kalimantan Province (State Gazette of the Republic of Indonesia Year 2002 Number 18, Supplement to the State Gazette of the Republic of Indonesia Number 4180);
6. Law Number 23 Year 2002 concerning Child Protection (State Gazette of the Republic of Indonesia Year 2002 Number 109, Supplement to the State Gazette of the Republic of Indonesia Number 4235) as amended by Law Number 35 Year 2014 Concerning Amendment to Law Number 23 Year 2002 concerning Child Protection (State Gazette of the Republic of Indonesia Year 2014 Number 297, Supplement to the State Gazette of the Republic of Indonesia Number 5606);
7. Law Number 23 Year 2004 concerning Elimination of Domestic Violence (State Gazette of the Republic of Indonesia Year 2004 Number 95, Supplement to the State Gazette of the Republic of Indonesia Number 4419);
8. Law Number 13 Year 2006 concerning Protection of Witnesses and Victims (State Gazette of the Republic of Indonesia Year 2006 Number 64, Supplement to the State Gazette of the Republic of Indonesia Number 4635);
9. Law Number 21 Year 2007 concerning Eradication of the Criminal Act of Trafficking in Persons (State Gazette of the Republic of Indonesia Year 2007, Number 58, Supplement to the State Gazette of the Republic of Indonesia Number 4720);

10. Law Number 12 Year 2011 concerning Formation of Legislation (State Gazette of the Republic of Indonesia Year 2011 Number 82, Supplement to the State Gazette of the Republic of Indonesia Number 5234);
11. Law Number 23 Year 2014 concerning Regional Government (State Gazette of the Republic of Indonesia Year 2014 Number 244, Supplement to the State Gazette of the Republic of Indonesia Number 5587), as amended several times most recently by Law Number 9 Year 2015 concerning Second Amendment to Law Number 23 Year 2014 concerning Regional Government (State Gazette of the Republic of Indonesia Year 2015 Number 58, Supplement to the State Gazette of the Republic of Indonesia Number 5679);
12. Government Regulation Number 4 Year 2006 concerning Implementation and Cooperation for Recovery of Victims of Domestic Violence (State Gazette of the Republic of Indonesia Year 2006, Number 15, Supplement to the State Gazette of the Republic of Indonesia Number 4604);

HAS DECIDED:

To Establish : REGIONAL REGULATION CONCERNING INTEGRATED SERVICES FOR WOMEN AND CHILDREN EMPOWERMENT.

CHAPTER I
GENERAL PROVISIONS

Article 1
In this Regional Regulation, what is meant by:
1. Region is Lamandau Regency.
2. Regional Government is the administration of Government affairs by the Regional Government and the Regional House of Representatives according to the principles of Autonomy and Co-Administration with the principle of the broadest possible autonomy within the system and principles of the Unitary State of the Republic of Indonesia as referred to in the 1945 Constitution.

3. Regional Government is the Regent together with Regional Apparatus as Elements of Regional Government Administration.
4. Regent is the Regent of Lamandau.
5. Regional House of Representatives is the Regional House of Representatives of Lamandau Regency.
6. Regional Secretary is the Regional Secretary of Lamandau Regency.
7. Child is a person who has not yet reached 18 (eighteen) years of age, and is not yet married, including a child still in the womb.
8. Woman is every person who is physically born as a woman.
9. Victim is a woman and or child who experiences suffering and or hardship either directly or indirectly as a result of an act of violence.
10. Prevention is all efforts made by government or non-government institutions aimed at providing a sense of security to women and children.
11. Protection is all efforts aimed at providing a sense of security to victims carried out by the government, family, advocates, social institutions or other community institutions, police, prosecutors, courts or other parties either temporarily or based on court decisions or determinations, in connection with acts of violence against children and women.
12. Child Protection is all activities carried out to guarantee and protect children and their rights so they can live, grow, develop, and participate optimally in accordance with human rights and dignity, and receive protection from violence and discrimination.
13. Women's Protection is all activities carried out to guarantee and protect women and their rights so they can live, grow, develop, and participate optimally in accordance with human rights and dignity, and receive protection from violence and discrimination.
14. Suffering is any act that results in or can result in suffering whether psychological, physical, sexual, economic, or social to the victim.
15. Physical violence is any act that causes pain, injury, wounds or disability to a person's body, miscarriage, fainting and or causes death.
16. Psychological violence is an act that causes fear, loss of self-confidence, loss of ability to act, feelings of helplessness and or severe psychological suffering to women and or children.
17. Sexual violence is any act in the form of sexual harassment, forced sexual relations with others for commercial purposes and or certain purposes.
18. Service is action taken as soon as possible to the victim, when seeing, hearing and knowing of the impending, ongoing or after occurrence of violence against the victim.
19. Companion is a person or institution that has the ability to accompany victims to provide counseling, therapy, legal services and advocacy, for strengthening victim recovery.
20. Safe House is temporary housing used to provide protection to women and children victims in accordance with the standard operating procedures or abbreviated as SOP determined.

21. Community is individuals, families, groups, social organizations and or other community organizations.
22. Integrated Service Center for Women and Children Empowerment hereinafter abbreviated as P2TP2A is a form of service facility for women and children in efforts to fulfill information and needs in the fields of education, health, economy, politics, law, protection and prevention of violence and trafficking against women and children.

CHAPTER II
PRINCIPLES AND OBJECTIVES

Article 2
Integrated services for women and children empowerment are carried out based on Pancasila, the 1945 Constitution of the Republic of Indonesia, as well as the principles and basic rights as regulated in the Convention on women and children, which include:
a. Respect for human rights.
b. Justice and gender equality.
c. Non-discrimination;
d. The best interests of women and or children;
e. The right to life, survival, and development;
f. Respect for the opinions of women and children;
g. Justice and gender equality; and
h. Victim protection.

Article 3
The objectives of implementing integrated services for women and children empowerment are:
a. To guarantee the fulfillment of the rights of women and children in accordance with applicable laws and regulations.
b. To prevent and protect women and children from violence.
c. To encourage the community to carry out their obligations in the prevention and protection of women and children in accordance with applicable laws and regulations.
d. To restore victims as a result of the violence experienced.
e. To take action against perpetrators of domestic violence.
f. To provide disaggregated data by sex and information on issues of empowerment and protection of women and children for the community that needs it.
g. To encourage the provision of facilities, infrastructure and various types of services in various areas of life for women and children such as data and information centers, counseling, psychological and medical therapy, assistance, education and training, referral centers, skills training and so on as efforts to improve the quality of life of women, welfare and child protection managed by the community independently.

CHAPTER III
RIGHTS AND OBLIGATIONS

Article 4
The rights of children are as follows:
a. Every child has the right to live, grow, develop and participate reasonably in accordance with human dignity and dignity, and receive protection from violence and discrimination;

b. Every child has the right to a name as identity and citizenship status;
c. Every child has the right to worship according to their religion, think and express themselves according to their level of intelligence and age, under parental guidance;
d. Every child has the right to know their parents, be raised, and cared for by their own parents;
e. In the event that for some reason the parents cannot guarantee the child's growth and development, or the child is in a neglected state, then the child has the right to be cared for and adopted as a foster child or adopted child by others in accordance with applicable laws and regulations;
f. Every child has the right to obtain health services and social security in accordance with physical, mental, spiritual and social needs;
g. Every child has the right to obtain education and teaching in the context of personal development and level of intelligence according to their interests and talents;
h. Especially for children with disabilities, they have the right to obtain special education, while children with excellence have the right to obtain special education;
i. Every child has the right to express and be heard their opinion, receive, seek, and provide information according to their level of intelligence and age for their self-development in accordance with the values of decency and propriety;
j. Every child has the right to rest and use leisure time, associate with peers, play, recreate, and create according to their interests, talents, and level of intelligence for self-development;
k. Every child with disabilities has the right to obtain rehabilitation, social assistance, and maintenance of social welfare standards; and
l. Every child while in the care of parents, guardians, or any other party responsible for care, has the right to receive protection from treatment of:
- Discrimination;
- Exploitation, both economic and sexual;
- Neglect;
- Cruelty, violence, and persecution;
- Injustice; and
- Other mistreatment.
m. Every child has the right to be cared for by their own parents, unless there are valid reasons and/or legal rules showing that separation is in the best interests of the child and is a last consideration.
n. Every child has the right to obtain protection from:
- Abuse in political activities;
- Involvement in armed conflict;
- Involvement in social unrest;
- Involvement in events containing elements of violence; and
- Involvement in warfare.
o. Every child has the right to obtain protection from being the target of persecution, torture, or imposition of inhuman punishment.
p. Every child has the right to obtain freedom in accordance with the law.
q. Arrest, detention, or criminal imprisonment of children may only be carried out if in accordance with applicable law and can only be done as a last resort.
r. Every child deprived of liberty has the right to:
- Receive humane treatment and placement separated from adults;

- Obtain legal assistance or other assistance effectively at every stage of applicable legal efforts; and
- Defend themselves and obtain justice before an objective and impartial juvenile court in closed sessions for law.
s. Every child who becomes a victim or perpetrator of sexual violence or who is in conflict with the law has the right to confidentiality.
t. Every child who becomes a victim or perpetrator of a criminal act has the right to obtain legal assistance and other assistance.

Article 5
Victims of domestic violence have the right to obtain:
a. Protection from family, police, prosecutors, courts, legal aid, social institutions, or other parties either temporarily or based on protection order determinations from the court;
b. Health services according to medical needs;
c. Special handling related to victim confidentiality;
d. Assistance by social workers and legal aid at every level of the examination process in accordance with the provisions of laws and regulations; and
e. Spiritual guidance services.

Article 6
Every child is obligated to:
a. Respect parents, guardians, and teachers;
b. Love family, community, and care for friends;
c. Love the homeland, nation and state;
d. Perform worship according to the teachings of their religion; and
e. Implement noble ethics and morals.

Article 7
Everyone is prohibited from committing domestic violence against people within their household scope, by means of:
a. Physical violence;
b. Psychological violence;
c. Sexual violence; and
d. Household neglect.

Article 8
(1) Physical violence is an act that causes pain, illness, or serious injury.
(2) Psychological violence is an act that causes fear, loss of self-confidence, loss of ability to act, feelings of helplessness, and/or severe psychological suffering to a person.
(3) Sexual violence includes:
a. Forced sexual relations committed against persons residing within the household scope.
b. Forced sexual relations against one person within the household scope with others for commercial purposes and/or certain purposes.
(4) Household neglect is an act of not providing livelihood, not providing care, or not providing maintenance to persons within the household scope.

(5) Neglect also applies to everyone who causes economic dependence by limiting and/or prohibiting proper work inside or outside the home so that the victim is under the control of that person.

Article 9
(1) The state, government, community, family, and parents are obligated and responsible for the implementation of child protection.
(2) The government is responsible for efforts to prevent domestic violence.

Article 10
(1) The state, government, community, family, and parents are obligated and responsible for the implementation of child protection.
(2) The government is responsible for efforts to prevent domestic violence.
(3) Everyone who hears, sees or knows of the occurrence of domestic violence is obligated to make efforts according to their capabilities to:
a. Prevent the continuation of criminal acts;
b. Provide protection to victims;
c. Provide emergency assistance; and
d. Assist in the process of submitting applications for protection determinations.

CHAPTER IV
IMPLEMENTATION OF CHILD PROTECTION AND VICTIMS OF DOMESTIC VIOLENCE

Article 11
(1) Every child receives protection to worship according to their religion.
(2) Before the child can determine their choice, the religion embraced by the child follows the religion of their parents.
(3) The state, government, community, family, parents, guardians, and social institutions guarantee the protection of children in embracing their religion.
(4) Protection of children in embracing their religion includes guidance, mentoring, and practice of religious teachings for children.

Article 12
(1) The government is obligated to provide facilities and organize comprehensive health efforts for children, so that every child obtains optimal health status from the womb.
(2) Comprehensive health efforts include promotive, preventive, curative and rehabilitative services for both basic health services and referrals.
(3) Parents and families are responsible for maintaining children's health and caring for children from the womb.
(4) The state, government, family and parents are obligated to protect children from acts of:
a. Removal of child organs and/or child body tissues without regard to the child's health;
b. Sale and purchase of child organs and/or body tissues; and
c. Health research that uses children as research objects without parental consent and does not prioritize the best interests of the child.

Article 13
(1) The regional government is obligated to organize basic education of at least 9 (nine) years for all children.
(2) The state, government, family and parents are obligated to provide the widest possible opportunity for children to obtain education.
(3) Education is directed at:
a. Development of attitudes and abilities of the child's personality, talents, mental and physical abilities to reach their optimal potential;
b. Development of respect for human rights and fundamental freedoms;
c. Development of respect for parents, cultural identity, language and their own values, national values where the child resides, from where the child originates, and civilizations different from their own civilization;
d. Preparation of children for responsible life; and
e. Development of respect and love for the environment.
(4) Children with physical and/or mental disabilities are given equal opportunities and accessibility to obtain regular education and special education.
(5) Children in and around schools must be protected from acts of violence committed by teachers, school administrators or their friends in the school concerned or other educational institutions.

Article 14
(1) The government is obligated to organize maintenance and care for neglected children, both in institutions and outside social institutions.
(2) The government in organizing maintenance and care is obligated to strive for and assist children, so that children can:
a. Participate;
b. Freely express opinions and think according to conscience and religion;
c. Freely receive oral or written information according to the child's age and development stage;
d. Freely associate and assemble;
e. Freely rest, play, recreate, create and engage in cultural arts; and
f. Obtain play facilities that meet health and safety requirements.

Article 15
The government and other state institutions are obligated and responsible to provide special protection to children in emergency situations, children in conflict with the law, children from minority and isolated groups, children exploited economically and/or sexually, children who are trafficked, children who are victims of narcotics, alcohol, psychotropic, and other addictive substances (drugs) abuse, children victims of kidnapping, sale and trafficking, children victims of violence both physical and/or mental, children with disabilities, and children victims of mistreatment and neglect.

Article 16
(1) Within 1x24 (one times twenty-four) hours from knowing or receiving a report of domestic violence, the police must immediately provide temporary protection to the victim.
(2) Temporary protection is provided for a maximum of 7 (seven) days from when the victim is received or handled.
(3) Within 1x24 (one times twenty-four) hours from the provision of protection, the police must request a protection order determination letter from the court.

Article 17
In providing temporary protection, the police can cooperate with health workers, social workers, volunteer companions, and/or spiritual guides to accompany victims.

Article 18
The police are obligated to provide information to victims about the victim's right to receive services and assistance.

Article 19
The police are obligated to immediately conduct an investigation after knowing or receiving a report about the occurrence of domestic violence.

Article 20
The police immediately convey to the victim about:
a. The identity of officers for recognition by the victim;
b. Domestic violence is a crime against human dignity; and
c. The police's obligation to protect victims.

Article 21
In providing health services to victims, health workers must:
a. Examine the victim's health according to professional standards;
b. Make a written report of the examination results on the victim and visum et repertum at the request of the police investigator or a medical certificate that has the same legal force as evidence.

Article 22
In providing services, social workers must:
a. Conduct counseling to strengthen and provide a sense of security for victims;
b. Provide information regarding the rights of victims to obtain protection from the police and protection order determinations from the court;
c. Escort victims to safe houses or alternative residences; and

d. Conduct integrated coordination in providing services to victims with the police, social services, social institutions needed by victims.

Article 23
In providing services, volunteer companions can:
a. Inform victims of their right to obtain one or more companions;
b. Accompany victims at the investigation, prosecution or court examination level by guiding victims to objectively and completely describe the domestic violence they experienced;
c. Listen empathetically to all victim statements so that victims feel safe accompanied by companions; and
d. Actively provide psychological and physical strengthening to victims.

Article 24
In providing services, spiritual guides must provide explanations regarding rights, obligations and provide strengthening of faith and piety to victims.

Article 25
In providing protection and services, advocates are obligated to:
a. Provide legal consultation that includes information regarding victim rights and the judicial process;
b. Accompany victims at the investigation, prosecution, and examination levels in court sessions and help victims to completely describe the domestic violence they experienced; and
c. Coordinate with fellow law enforcers, volunteer companions, and social workers so that the judicial process proceeds as it should.

Article 26
(1) Victims have the right to report domestic violence directly to the police either at the place where the victim is located or at the scene of the case.
(2) Victims can authorize family or others to report domestic violence to the police either at the place where the victim is located or at the scene of the case.
(3) In the event the victim is a child, the report can be made by parents, guardians, caregivers, or the child concerned which is carried out in accordance with applicable laws and regulations.

Article 27
The head of the court within 7 (seven) days from receipt of the application must issue a determination letter containing a protection order for the victim and other family members, unless there are valid reasons.

Article 28
Applications to obtain protection orders can be submitted by:
a. Victims or victim's family;
b. Victim's friends;
c. Police;
d. Volunteer companions; and
e. Spiritual guides.

Article 29
(1) Applications for protection orders are submitted orally or in writing.
(2) In the event the application is submitted orally, the clerk of the local district court is obligated to record the application.
(3) In the event the application for a protection order is submitted by family, victim's friends, police, volunteer companions, or spiritual guides, the victim must give consent. In certain circumstances, applications can be submitted without victim consent.

Article 30
Upon application by the victim or their representative, the court may consider to:
a. Establish a special condition;
b. Change or cancel a special condition of the protection order.

Article 31
(1) Protection orders can be granted for a maximum period of 1 (one) year.
(2) Protection orders can be extended by court determination.
(3) Applications for extension of protection orders are submitted 7 (seven) days before the expiration of their validity period.

Article 32
(1) The court may declare one or more additional protection orders.
(2) In providing additional protection orders, the court is obligated to consider statements from victims, health workers, social workers, volunteer companions, and/or spiritual guides.

Article 33
(1) Based on consideration of possible dangers, the court may declare one or more additional conditions in the protection order.
(2) In granting additional conditions in the protection order, the court is obligated to consider statements from victims, health workers, social workers, volunteer companions, and/or spiritual guides.

Article 34
(1) The police can arrest and subsequently detain without a warrant against perpetrators believed to have violated the protection order, even though the violation was not committed at the place where the police officer is on duty.
(2) Arrest and detention as referred to in paragraph (1), must be given an arrest and detention order letter after 1x24 (one times twenty-four) hours.
(3) Suspension of detention does not apply to detention as referred to in paragraphs (1) and (2).

Article 35
(1) To provide protection to victims, the police can arrest perpetrators with sufficient preliminary evidence for having violated the protection order.
(2) Arrest as referred to in paragraph (1), can be continued with detention accompanied by a detention order letter within 1x24 (one times twenty-four) hours.

Article 36
(1) Victims, police or volunteer companions can submit written reports about alleged violations of the protection order.
(2) In the event the court receives a written report as referred to in paragraph (1), the perpetrator is ordered to appear within 3x24 (three times twenty-four) hours for examination.
(3) Examination as referred to in paragraph (2) is conducted by the court at the place where the perpetrator once lived with the victim at the time the violation was allegedly committed.

Article 37
(1) If the court knows that the perpetrator has violated the protection order and is suspected of committing further violations, the court may require the perpetrator to make a written statement containing a commitment to comply with the protection order.
(2) If the perpetrator still does not heed the written statement as referred to in paragraph (1), the court may detain the perpetrator for a maximum of 30 days.
(3) Detention as referred to in paragraph (2), is accompanied by a detention order letter.

CHAPTER V
VICTIM RECOVERY

Article 38
For recovery purposes, victims can obtain services from:
a. Health workers;
b. Social workers;

c. Volunteer companions; and
d. Spiritual guides.

Article 39
(1) Health workers are obligated to examine victims according to professional standards.
(2) In the event victims require treatment, health workers are obligated to restore and rehabilitate the victim's health.

Article 40
Social workers, volunteer companions, and/or spiritual guides are obligated to provide services to victims in the form of counseling to strengthen and/or provide a sense of security for victims.

CHAPTER VI
INSTITUTIONAL MATTERS

Article 41
(1) In order to provide protection services to women and children victims of violence, the Regional Government establishes P2TP2A.
(2) Membership of P2TP2A as referred to in (1), consists of:
a. regional government elements;
b. community elements; and
c. academic elements.
(3) Membership of P2TP2A as referred to in paragraph (2), is determined by Regent's Decree.

Article 42
(1) The Integrated Service Center for Women and Children Empowerment (P2TP2A) facilitates the provision of various services for the community both physical and non-physical including information, referrals, consultation/counseling, skills training and other activities.
(2) The Integrated Service Center for Women and Children Empowerment (P2TP2A) can also be a place to conduct training for cadres who have great commitment and concern for women's and children's issues in all fields (health, education, economy, politics, law and protection of women and children from violence and human trafficking) to then be able to work together and participate in providing services to the community in a forum for improving quality of life and protection for women and children.

Article 43
Programs that can be carried out by the Integrated Service Center for Women and Children Empowerment (P2TP2A) include:
a. Women's empowerment.

b. Protection of women and children from violence and human trafficking.
c. Communication, information and education.
d. Increasing community member participation.
e. Increasing manager capacity.

Article 44
Success indicators of the Integrated Service Center for Women and Children Empowerment (P2TP2A) are as follows:
a. Formation and functioning of the Integrated Service Center for Women and Children Empowerment (P2TP2A) that provides women's empowerment services, protection of women and children in the fields of law, politics, education, health, economy, acts of violence and human trafficking.
b. Increasing number of women and children utilizing the Integrated Service Center for Women and Children Empowerment (P2TP2A).
c. Increasing number of women and children receiving training.
d. Functioning of dialogue and communication mechanisms between government, community and business world.
e. Existence of cooperation between the Integrated Service Center for Women and Children Empowerment (P2TP2A) with government and business world.

CHAPTER VII
REPORTING

Article 45
(1) The Integrated Service Center for Women and Children Empowerment (P2TP2A) is obligated to report the implementation of activities to the Regent.
(2) Submission of written reports as referred to in paragraph (1), is carried out at least every 3 (three) months.

CHAPTER VIII
FINANCING

Article 46
Financing for the implementation of Integrated Services for Women and Children Empowerment is sourced from:
a. Regional revenue and expenditure budget of Lamandau Regency.
b. Other legal and non-binding sources.

CHAPTER IX
CLOSING PROVISIONS

Article 47
This Regional Regulation comes into force on the date of promulgation.

In order that everyone may know of it, the promulgation of this Regional Regulation is ordered by placement in the Regional Gazette of Lamandau Regency.

I. GENERAL ELUCIDATION

All forms of violence in general and acts of violence against women and or children are a form of violation of human rights and constitute a form of crime against human dignity and discriminatory treatment, therefore it is the obligation of the Government and Regional Government to realize guarantees of security/protection for honor, dignity, property, freedom from torture, or treatment that degrades human dignity and discriminatory treatment.

Current developments show that there are still frequent acts of violence against women and or children within the family environment and in society that clearly result in suffering and hardship for women and or children physically, psychologically, or sexually including threats of certain actions, coercion, or deprivation of rights.

Services and handling, guidance and empowerment of women and or children have not been optimal so far. Various causes mentioned above are that most women and or children victims of violence choose to remain silent and the problem ends with women and children falling into powerlessness.

This regional regulation is intended as an effort by the Government of Lamandau Regency to meet community demands by providing services for the rights of women and children victims of violence comprehensively, clearly and firmly to protect or side with victims.
