Article 1

In this Regional Regulation what is meant by:

1. Central Government is the President of the Republic of Indonesia who holds governmental power of the Unitary State of the Republic of Indonesia who is assisted by the Vice President and ministers as referred to in the 1945 Constitution of the Republic of Indonesia.

2. Region is Pesisir Barat Regency.

3. Regional Government is the administration of Governmental Affairs by the Regional Government and Regional House of Representatives according to the principles of autonomy and assistance tasks with the principle of the broadest possible autonomy within the system and principles of the Unitary State of the Republic of Indonesia as referred to in the 1945 Constitution of the Republic of Indonesia.

5. Regent is the Regent of Pesisir Barat.

6. Regional House of Representatives hereinafter abbreviated as DPRD is a people's representative institution positioned as an element of Regional Government administration.

7. Office of Women's Empowerment, Child Protection and Family Planning hereinafter abbreviated as Dinas PPPAKB is a Regional Apparatus Organization that implements and handles affairs of Protection of Women and Children.

9. 10. Violence is any act unlawfully with or without means against physical and psychological that causes danger to life, body or results in deprivation of a person's freedom.

11. Violence against women is any action based on gender differences that results or may result in suffering or hardship for women physically, sexually or psychologically, economic exploitation, social, including threats of certain actions, coercion or arbitrary deprivation of freedom, whether occurring in the public sphere or in private life.

12. Violence against children is any act against a child that results in suffering or hardship physically, mentally, sexually, psychologically, economic exploitation, social, including neglect and ill-treatment that threatens bodily integrity and degrades the dignity of the child.

13. CHAPTER II
OBJECTIVES AND SCOPE

Part One
Objectives

Article 2

The objectives of protection of women and children from acts of violence are:

a. to prevent all forms of violence against women and children;

b. to provide protection and services to women and children victims of gender-based violence;

c. to provide a sense of security to women and children victims of violence;

d. to restore the physical, psychological and economic condition of women and children victims of violence;

e. the best interests of women and children victims of violence that occurs in the domestic and/or public sphere; and

f. to provide certainty of assistance to victim parties in the legal process against perpetrators of violence against women and children.

Scope

Article 3

d. Organization of protection;

CHAPTER III
FORMS OF VIOLENCE AGAINST
WOMEN AND CHILDREN

Article 4

Forms of violence against women and children include:

a. physical violence;
b. psychological violence;
c. sexual violence;
d. neglect;
e. exploitation; and/or
f. other violence.

Article 5

Physical violence as referred to in Article 4 letter a is an act that causes pain accompanied by injury, wounds or disability to the body, miscarriage, fainting and/or causing death of women and/or children.

Article 6

Psychological violence as referred to in Article 4 letter b is an act that causes fear, loss of self-confidence, loss of ability to act, sense of helplessness and/or psychological suffering to women and/or children.

Article 7

(1) Sexual violence as referred to in Article 4 letter c includes:

a. non-physical sexual harassment;
b. physical sexual harassment;
c. forced contraception;
d. forced sterilization;
e. forced marriage;
f. sexual torture;
g. sexual exploitation;
h. sexual slavery; and
i. electronic-based sexual violence

(2) In addition to Criminal Acts of Sexual Violence as referred to in paragraph (1), Criminal Acts of Sexual Violence also include:

a. rape;
b. indecent acts;
c. intercourse with a Child, indecent acts against a Child, and/or sexual exploitation of a Child;

d. acts violating decency that are contrary to the will of the victim and religious values recognized in Indonesia;

e. pornography involving Children or pornography that explicitly contains violence and sexual exploitation;

f. forced prostitution;

g. criminal acts of trafficking in persons intended for sexual exploitation;

h. sexual violence within the scope of the household;

i. criminal acts of money laundering where the predicate offense is a Criminal Act of Sexual Violence; and

j. other criminal acts explicitly stated as Criminal Acts of Sexual Violence as regulated in the provisions of laws and regulations.

Article 8

Neglect as referred to in Article 4 letter d includes:

a. an act of deliberately neglecting a woman within her household, whereas according to the law applicable to her or due to agreement or contract she is obliged to provide livelihood and care to that woman.

b. an act that results in the non-fulfillment of a child's needs properly, whether physical, mental, spiritual or social, committed by parents, guardians, or other parties or those responsible for their care;

c. an act of deliberately neglecting to care for or look after a child as appropriate, committed by parents, guardians, or any other party responsible for their care.

Article 9

Exploitation as referred to in Article 4 letter e includes:

a. an act of exploiting economically or sexually with the intention of benefiting oneself or others;

b. an act with or without the consent of the victim which includes but is not limited to prostitution, forced labor or service, slavery or similar practices, oppression, extortion, utilization of physical, sexual, reproductive organs, or unlawfully transferring or transplanting organs and/or body tissues or utilizing the labor or ability of a person by another party to obtain benefits either material or immaterial;

Article 10

(1) Other violence as referred to in Article 4 letter f is threats of violence and coercion.

(2) Threats of violence as referred to in paragraph (1) include any unlawful act in the form of speech, writing, images, symbols, or body movements, whether with or without means that cause fear or restrict a person's inherent freedom.

(3) Coercion as referred to in paragraph (1) includes a situation where a person/victim is ordered to do something in such a way that the person does something contrary to their own will.

CHAPTER IV
RIGHTS OF VICTIMS

Article 11

Women and children victims of acts of violence receive the following rights:

a. right to be respected for their dignity as human beings;
b. right to recovery;
c. right to determine their own decisions;
d. right to obtain information;
e. right to confidentiality;
f. right to social rehabilitation;
g. right to appropriate, comfortable and needs-based complaint handling;
h. right of victims and their families to obtain facilities in the judicial process;
i. right to assistance; and
j. to receive continuous handling up to the rehabilitation stage.

Article 12

Child victims of acts of violence in addition to receiving rights as referred to in Article 11 also receive special rights as follows:

a. right to survival, growth, and development;
b. right to basic population services;
c. right to equal protection;
d. right to be free from various stigma; and
e. right to obtain freedom.

I. GENERAL

Acts of violence against women and children are experiencing an increasingly rising trend. Triggering factors such as economic, social, cultural and social environment are increasingly complex along with the influence of technological developments. Real steps are needed to provide protection by all elements of Citizens as a unity of society, as well as the role of the Regional Government as protector of its citizens with various programs to prevent acts of violence against women and children. One effort to prevent violence against women is conducted through women's empowerment and optimization of previously possessed potential, so that it is expected to be able to improve and develop themselves to play a role and be protected from potential acts of violence. This condition will strengthen forms of appreciation and respect for women while maintaining their role and nature as women to play a role in educating and supporting the welfare and happiness of their families.

Efforts to prevent violence against children are the joint obligation of parents, families, communities and the private sector holistically and inseparably from one another. Community service is the front line when there is potential for violence against children. The mechanism for preventing violence and protecting child victims of violence involves multiple sectors coordinated by the Regional Government. These efforts are made with the direction of providing social rehabilitation for child victims of violence, reuniting child victims of violence with their families and/or environment, and increasing the empowerment of child victims of violence.

In connection with this, to realize the provision of protection efforts for women and children victims of violence and for legal certainty in organizing women's empowerment and protection of child victims of violence in the Regional area, it needs to be regulated in a Regional Regulation.

Article 7

a. Non-physical sexual harassment is an act directed at the body, sexual desire, and/or reproductive organs with the intention of degrading the dignity of a person based on their sexuality and/or decency.
