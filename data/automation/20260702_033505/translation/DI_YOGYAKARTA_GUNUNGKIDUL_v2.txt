CHAPTER I
GENERAL PROVISIONS

Article 1

In this Regional Regulation what is meant by:
1. Women are persons who are female by gender.

2. Children are persons who have not yet reached the age of 18 (eighteen) years, including children still in the womb.

3. Family is the smallest unit in society consisting of husband and wife, or husband, wife and their children, or father and his children, or mother and her children, or blood relatives in direct lineage upward or downward up to the third degree.

4. Violence is any act that results in or can result in misery or suffering whether physical, sexual, economic, social, and psychological to the victim.

5. Violence against Women is any action that results in or may result in misery or suffering of women physically, sexually, economically, socially, psychologically, including threats of certain actions, coercion or deprivation of freedom, whether occurring in public or private life.

6. Violence against Children is any action that results in or may result in suffering of children physically, psychologically, sexually, and/or neglect, exploitation, including threats to commit acts, coercion, or deprivation of freedom unlawfully, and other violence.

7. Victims are women and children who experience misery and or suffering either directly or indirectly as a result of violence.

8. Child Victims of Violence are children who experience violence and children in conflict with the law.

9. Protection is all efforts aimed at providing a sense of security to victims carried out by family, advocates, social institutions, police, prosecutors, courts, or other parties either temporarily or based on court determination.

10. Child Protection is all activities to guarantee and protect children and their rights so they can live, grow, develop, and participate optimally in accordance with human dignity and worth, and receive protection from violence and discrimination.

11. Special Protection is protection given to children in emergency situations, children in conflict with the law, children from minority and isolated groups, children who are exploited economically and/or sexually, children who are trafficked, children who are victims of narcotics, alcohol, psychotropic, and other addictive substance (napza) abuse, child victims of kidnapping, sale, trafficking, child victims of physical and/or mental violence, children with disabilities, and child victims of maltreatment and neglect.

12. Society is individuals, groups, and social organizations and/or community organizations.

13. Social Rehabilitation is services aimed at restoring and developing the ability of a person experiencing social dysfunction so they can carry out their social functions properly.

14. Services are immediate activities and actions carried out for victims when seeing, hearing and knowing of the impending, ongoing or completed occurrence of violence against women and children carried out by professionals in accordance with their respective professions in the form of counseling, therapy and advocacy for strengthening and recovery of violence victims.

15. Counseling for Victims is the provision of assistance by an expert or trained person in such a way as to increase psychological understanding and ability in solving problems faced.

16. Assistance is all actions in the form of counseling, psychological therapy, advocacy, and or spiritual guidance, for strengthening victims of violence to resolve problems faced.

17. Technical Implementation Unit hereinafter abbreviated as UPT is the technical implementation unit of the agency formed by the regional government in providing services for women and children who experience violence, discrimination, special protection, and other problems.

18. Technical Implementation Unit of the Agency for Protection of Women and Children hereinafter abbreviated as UPTD PPA is the technical implementation unit of the agency formed by the regional government in providing services for women and children who experience violence, discrimination, special protection, and other problems.

19. Forum for Protection of Violence Victims hereinafter abbreviated as FPKK is a coordination forum for handling victims of violence against women and children whose implementation is carried out in a networked manner and formed at the regency, Kapanewon/sub-district, and Kalurahan/village levels.

20. Safe House is a temporary residence or shelter for victims, provided to give protection during the assistance process, for the safety and comfort of victims from threats and dangers from perpetrators.

21. Government Agencies are collective terms covering work units/organizational units of ministries/departments, Non-Departmental Government Institutions, secretariats of state high institutions, and other government agencies, both central and regional, including State-Owned Enterprises, State-Owned Legal Entities, and Business Entities.

22. Region is Gunungkidul Regency.

23. Regional Government is the Regent and regional apparatus as elements of regional government administration.

24. Regent is the Regent of Gunungkidul.

25. The Agency for Women's Empowerment, Child Protection, and Family Planning, Community and Village Empowerment of Gunungkidul Regency hereinafter referred to as the Agency, is the implementing element of government affairs in the field of women's empowerment and child protection.

Article 2

Protection of women and children aims to:
a. guarantee the fulfillment of the rights of women and children so they can participate optimally in society in accordance with human dignity and worth;

b. provide protection and give a sense of security to women and children from all forms of violence and discrimination;

c. prevent and eliminate all forms of violence and exploitation against women and children including trafficking in persons;

d. provide services to women and children victims of violence, reporters, and witnesses;

e. restore and strengthen the physical and mental condition of women and children victims of violence; and

f. increase awareness and community participation in preventing violence against women and children.

CHAPTER II
REGIONAL GOVERNMENT AUTHORITY

Article 3

(1) The Regent has the authority to implement protection for women and children from violence.
(2) The authority as referred to in paragraph (1) includes:

a. violence prevention;
b. services for violence victims; and
c. institutional strengthening and development.

Article 4

(1) In exercising the authority as referred to in Article 3, the Regent establishes programs and activities for the protection of women and children in the Regional Action Plan.

(2) The Regional Action Plan as referred to in paragraph (1) contains at least:

a. programs and activities for action to protect women and children from violence;
b. programs and activities for regional action to prevent all forms of violence and discrimination against women and children;

c. programs and activities for action to improve and enhance protection services for women and children from violence; and

d. programs and activities for action to empower women from violence.

(3) Provisions regarding the Regional Action Plan as referred to in paragraph (1) shall be further regulated by Regent Regulation.

CHAPTER III
VIOLENCE PREVENTION

Part One
Types of Violence

Article 5

Types of violence include:
a. physical violence;
b. psychological violence;
c. sexual violence;
d. neglect;
e. exploitation; and/or
f. other violence.

Article 6

Physical violence as referred to in Article 5 letter a is an act that results in pain, injury, wounds or disability to a person's body, miscarriage, unconsciousness and/or causes death.

Article 7

Psychological violence as referred to in Article 5 letter b is an act that results in fear, loss of self-confidence, loss of ability to act, feelings of helplessness and/or severe psychological suffering to a person.

Article 8

Sexual violence as referred to in Article 5 letter c includes:
a. acts in the form of sexual harassment:
b. forced sexual intercourse;
c. forced unnatural or unwanted sexual intercourse; and/or
d. forced sexual intercourse with others for commercial purposes and or certain purposes.

Article 9

Neglect as referred to in Article 5 letter d includes:
a. acts that result in the unfulfillment of children's needs properly, whether physical, mental, spiritual or social, carried out by parents, guardians, or any other party responsible for their care;

b. acts of deliberately neglecting to maintain, care for, or take care of children as they should, carried out by parents, guardians, or any other party responsible for their care;

c. acts of neglecting persons within their household scope, whereas according to the law applicable to them or due to agreement or contract they are obliged to provide life, care, or maintenance to that person; and/or

d. acts that result in economic dependence by limiting and/or prohibiting decent work inside or outside the home so that the victim is under the control of that person.

Article 10

Exploitation as referred to in Article 5 letter e is:
a. acts that exploit economically or sexually with the intention of benefiting oneself or others;

b. acts with or without the victim's consent that include but are not limited to prostitution, forced labor or services, slavery or similar practices, oppression, extortion, utilization of physical, sexual, reproductive organs, or unlawfully transferring or transplanting organs and/or body tissues;

c. utilizing the labor or ability of a person by another party to obtain material or immaterial benefits; and/or

d. all forms of utilization of sexual organs or other body organs of victims to obtain benefits, including but not limited to all prostitution or indecency activities.

Article 11

Other violence as referred to in Article 5 letter f namely:
a. threats of violence include any unlawful act in the form of speech, writing, images, symbols, or body movements, whether with or without using means that cause fear or restrict a person's fundamental freedom; and

b. coercion, includes a situation where a person/victim is made to do something in such a way that the person does something contrary to their own will.

Part Two
Prevention Efforts

Article 12

Violence prevention as referred to in Article 3 paragraph (2) letter a is implemented by:
a. Regional Government;
b. families; and
c. Society;
d. mass media; and
e. business sector.

Article 13

(1) Violence prevention efforts carried out by the Regional Government as referred to in Article 12 letter a include:

a. socialization of laws and regulations related to the implementation of protection for women and children;
b. education about the dangers of domestic violence;
c. mapping of violence-prone areas;
d. compilation of data and information about women and children vulnerable to violence; and/or
e. counseling/mental and spiritual guidance.
(2) Prevention efforts as referred to in paragraph (1) are coordinated by the Agency.

Article 14

(1) Families and Society carry out violence prevention efforts independently according to their needs and capabilities.

(2) The Regional Government provides support and facilitation for violence prevention efforts carried out by the community.

CHAPTER IV
PROVISION OF SERVICES FOR WOMEN AND CHILD VICTIMS OF VIOLENCE

Part One
Rights of Violence Victims

Article 15

Women and children victims of violence have the right to receive:
a. Protection;
b. information;
c. excellent service;
d. continuous handling up to the rehabilitation and social reintegration stage;
e. confidential handling;
f. psychological and legal assistance; and
g. guarantees of rights related to status as family members and community members.

Article 16

In addition to receiving rights as referred to in Article 15, child victims of violence also receive special rights in the form of:
a. rights to respect and full use for survival, growth, and development;
b. rights to basic services;
c. rights to equal protection;
d. rights to be free from various stigma; and
e. rights to obtain freedom in accordance with the law.

Article 17

(1) In the event that violence victims require continuous handling, they have the right to stay in a Safe House or alternative residence.

(2) Continuous handling as referred to in paragraph (1) is handling at the Social Rehabilitation stage carried out by individuals, groups or Government and non-Government institutions.

(3) The Safe House as referred to in paragraph (1) provides a safe place and/or temporary shelter for violence victims until their rights are restored.

Article 18

(1) The Regional Government provides a Safe House in the Region.
(2) The operation and/or management of the Safe House as referred to in paragraph (1) is carried out by the Agency.

Article 19

(1) In the event that the Regional Government has not yet provided a Safe House as referred to in Article 18 paragraph (1), the Regional Government may cooperate with third parties who have Safe Houses.

(2) Cooperation with third parties as referred to in paragraph (1) shall be further regulated in a Regent Regulation.

Part Two
Handling of Violence Victims

Article 20

(1) Handling provided to women and children victims of violence is in the form of services, including:

a. complaints;
b. health;
c. legal aid;
d. Social Rehabilitation;
e. medicolegal;
f. psychological; and
g. assistance.

(2) Services as referred to in paragraph (1) are provided with:

a. speed and accuracy;
b. safety and comfort;
c. empathy;
d. non-discrimination;
e. easy accessibility;
f. no charges; and
g. guaranteed confidentiality

Article 21

Complaint services as referred to in Article 20 paragraph (1) letter a include:
a. initial identification or recording of victims; and
b. consent for action to be taken.

Article 22

(1) Health Services as referred to in Article 20 paragraph (1) letter b include:

a. first aid to victims;
b. care and recovery of physical injuries aimed at restoring the physical condition of victims carried out by medical personnel and paramedics; and
c. referral to health facilities in accordance with professional standards;

(2) In the event that women and children victims of violence require treatment, health workers are obliged to provide health recovery and Social Rehabilitation services.

(3) The provision of health recovery and Social Rehabilitation services as referred to in paragraph (2) is implemented in accordance with the provisions of laws and regulations.

Article 23

(1) Legal services as referred to in Article 20 paragraph (1) letter c to assist victims in undergoing the judicial process by:
a. providing legal consultation that includes information regarding victims' rights and the judicial process;

b. accompanying victims at the reporting, investigation, prosecution, and examination levels in court hearings and helping victims to fully describe the violence they experienced; and

c. coordinating with fellow law enforcers, volunteer assistants, and social workers so that the judicial process proceeds properly.

Article 24

(1) Social Rehabilitation services as referred to in Article 20 paragraph (1) letter d, include:

a. motivation and psychosocial diagnosis;
b. care and nurturing;
c. vocational training and entrepreneurship development;
d. mental spiritual guidance;
e. physical guidance;
f. social guidance and psychosocial counseling;
g. accessibility services;
h. social assistance and aid;
i. resocialization guidance;
j. follow-up guidance; and/or
k. referral.

(2) Forms of vocational training and entrepreneurship development as referred to in paragraph (1) letter c, include:

a. skills training;
b. field work practice;
c. apprenticeship;
d. entrepreneurial skills training;
e. facilitation of joint business group formation;
f. business capital in the form of business support tools; and/or
g. business implementation assistance.

Article 25

(1) Medicolegal services as referred to in Article 20 paragraph (1) letter e are medical services to provide optimal professional assistance in utilizing medical science for the purposes of law enforcement and justice.

(2) Medicolegal services as referred to in paragraph (1) include:

a. visum et repertum; and
b. visum et psikiatrikum.

Article 26

(1) Psychological services as referred to in Article 20 paragraph (1) letter f are actions taken to restore the psyche of women and children as victims of violence.

(2) Psychological services as referred to in paragraph (1) include:

a. conducting psychological evaluations for women and children victims of violence referred by police using forensic interviews with various techniques;

b. conducting individual psychological counseling for women and children victims of violence with psychological disorders related to the violence they experienced;

c. implementing family psychological counseling for women and children victims of violence;

d. conducting individual psychotherapy for women and children victims of violence with psychological disorders related to the violence they experienced;

e. conducting group psychotherapy for women and children victims of violence with psychological disorders related to the violence they experienced;

f. providing psychological assistance for children in conflict with the law; and

g. providing testimony as a fact witness in all levels of the legal process (Investigation Report, case review and trial)

(3) In addition to psychological services as referred to in paragraph (1), psychological services may be in the form of spiritual guidance.

(4) Spiritual counselors as referred to in paragraph (3) provide services to women and children victims of violence in the form of counseling to strengthen and provide a sense of security.

Article 27

(1) Assistance services as referred to in Article 20 paragraph (1) letter g are carried out by individuals or institutions that have expertise to conduct counseling, therapy and advocacy for strengthening and recovery of violence victims.

(2) Assistance services as referred to in paragraph (1) include:

a. accompanying victims during the examination and health recovery process;
b. accompanying victims during the medicolegal process;
c. accompanying victims during mediation, examination at the Police, Prosecutor's Office and Court;
d. monitoring the interests and rights of victims in the examination process at the Police, Prosecutor's Office and Court;
e. maintaining the privacy and confidentiality of victims from all parties who have no interest, including reporting by mass media;
f. coordinating with other assistants; and/or
g. providing continuous handling up to the Social Rehabilitation stage.

Article 28

Provisions regarding the handling of violence victims as referred to in Article 20 shall be further regulated in a Regent Regulation.

Part Three
UPT PPA

Article 29

(1) The Regional Government may establish UPT PPA to provide services for women and children who experience violence, discrimination, require special protection, and other problems in accordance with the provisions of Laws and Regulations.

(2) UPT PPA as referred to in paragraph (1) is positioned under and is responsible to the Agency.

Article 30

(1) UPT PPA in carrying out its duties as referred to in Article 29 performs service functions including:
a. public complaints;
b. victim outreach;
c. case management;
d. temporary shelter;
e. mediation; and/or
f. victim assistance.

(2) UPT PPA provides services as referred to in paragraph (1) based on minimum service standards established by laws and regulations.

CHAPTER V
INSTITUTIONAL STRENGTHENING AND DEVELOPMENT
Part One
Institutional Strengthening

Article 31

(1) In implementing violence prevention and services for women and children victims of violence, the Agency may cooperate with regional apparatus/related institutions.

(2) Cooperation as referred to in paragraph (1) is implemented in the form of FPKK.

(3) Regional apparatus/related institutions as referred to in paragraph (1) include regional apparatus/institutions whose duties and functions are in the fields of:

a. education;
b. employment;
c. health;
d. social affairs;
e. women's empowerment and child protection;
f. mental and spiritual;
g. peace and order.
h. population and civil registration;
i. law; and
j. cooperatives and MSMEs.

Part Two
Institutional Development

Article 32

(1) The Regional Government optimizes and facilitates FPKK in prevention and services for women and children victims of violence.

(2) FPKK as referred to in paragraph (1) includes:
a. Regency FPKK;
b. Kapanewon FPKK; and
c. Kalurahan FPKK.

Article 33

(1) The Regent establishes the Regency FPKK.
(2) The establishment of FPKK as referred to in paragraph (1) is stipulated by Regent Decree.

Article 34

Regency FPKK as referred to in Article 32 paragraph (1) letter a has duties including:
a. coordinating and synchronizing efforts to implement protection for women and children from violence carried out by members;
b. building, maintaining, and developing networks and referral systems;

c. communicating, socializing, and educating the community to play an active role in protecting women and children from violence;

d. conducting monitoring and evaluation;

e. coordinating the implementation of handling services for women and children victims of violence at the Regency level;

f. collecting and disseminating data and information related to the implementation of protection for women and children from violence;

g. conducting supervision, guidance, and assistance to Kapanewon FPKK and/or Kalurahan FPKK;

h. receiving referrals from Kapanewon FPKK and/or Kalurahan FPKK; and/or

i. reporting implementation to the Regent periodically.

Article 35

Elements of Regency FPKK as referred to in Article 32 paragraph (2) letter a include:
a. Government Agencies; and
b. community institutions.

Article 36

(1) Government Agencies as referred to in Article 35 letter a at least include Government Agencies that have duties and functions in the fields of:

a. social affairs;
b. handling of women and children;
c. health;
d. education;
e. youth and sports;
f. religion;
g. population;
h. employment; and/or
i. industry and trade.

(2) Community institutions as referred to in Article 35 letter b are grouped based on their field of activity, including:

a. health field;
b. psychology field;
c. law field;
d. social field; and/or
e. economic field.

Article 37

(1) The Panewu establishes Kapanewon FPKK.
(2) The establishment of FPKK as referred to in paragraph (1) is stipulated by Panewu Decree.

Article 38

Kapanewon FPKK as referred to in Article 32 paragraph (2) letter b has duties including:
a. implementing and coordinating the handling of women and children victims of violence within the Kapanewon scope;
b. receiving referrals from Kalurahan FPKK; and/or
c. documenting case handling in accordance with established mechanisms and service standards.

Article 39

Kapanewon FPKK as referred to in Article 32 paragraph (2) letter b consists at least of elements of:
a. health;
b. education;
c. law;
d. psychosocial;
e. Society;
f. spirituality;
g. children's forum; and/or
h. empowerment.

Article 40

(1) Kalurahan FPKK is established by the Lurah.
(2) The establishment of Kalurahan FPKK as referred to in paragraph (1) is stipulated by Lurah Decree.

(3) In the event that Kalurahan FPKK as referred to in paragraph (1) has not been formed, Regency FPKK and/or Kapanewon FPKK may provide facilitation and assistance.

Article 41

Kalurahan FPKK as referred to in Article 32 paragraph (2) letter c consists at least of elements of:
a. health;
b. education;
c. law;
d. community leaders;
e. religious leaders;
f. children's forum/youth organization;
g. Kalurahan officials; and/or
h. other related organizations/institutions.

Article 42

Kalurahan FPKK as referred to in Article 32 paragraph (2) letter c has duties including:

a. preparing programs and activities in efforts to prevent and handle violence against women and children within the Kalurahan scope; and/or

b. referring cases and coordinating with Kapanewon FPKK or Regency FPKK in the event that Kalurahan FPKK experiences obstacles related to case handling.

CHAPTER VI
COMMUNITY PARTICIPATION

Article 43

(1) The community may participate in the implementation of protection for women and children victims of violence.

(2) Community participation as referred to in paragraph (1) includes:

a. forming family partners at the kalurahan level;
b. forming community institutions whose field of work is in the field of protection of women and children;
c. conducting socialization of women's and children's rights independently;
d. conducting prevention efforts if finding indications of violence against women and children in the community;
e. providing first aid to women and children victims of violence; and
f. reporting to authorized agencies if violence against women and children occurs in their environment.

(3) Community participation as referred to in paragraph (1) may be carried out by individuals, community institutions, educational institutions, religious institutions, and the business sector.

Article 44

(1) The Regional Government involves the business sector and private parties to participate in the protection of women and children.

(2) Participation as referred to in paragraph (1) may be in the form of:

a. socialization to employees in the business environment regarding the rights of women and children;
b. socialization to employees in the business environment regarding the dangers of violence against women and children;
c. providing assistance in the implementation of protection for women and children as a form of corporate social responsibility.

CHAPTER VII
FUNDING

Article 45

In order to support the implementation of protection for women and children victims of violence, funding is required which may be sourced from:
a. Regional Revenue and Expenditure Budget (APBD);
b. Kalurahan Revenue and Expenditure Budget (APBKal); and
c. other legal and non-binding sources in accordance with applicable laws and regulations.

CHAPTER VIII
GUIDANCE AND SUPERVISION

Article 46

(1) The Regent conducts guidance and supervision of the implementation of protection for women and children victims of violence.

(2) Guidance and supervision as referred to in paragraph (1) is carried out by the Agency.

(3) Guidance and supervision as referred to in paragraph (1) is carried out in order to realize the objectives of implementing protection for women and children victims of violence in accordance with the provisions of laws and regulations.

(4) Guidance and supervision is carried out at least (two) times a year.

CHAPTER IX
CLOSING PROVISIONS

Article 47

(1) At the time this Regional Regulation comes into force, the provisions of laws and regulations governing the Establishment, Organizational Structure, Duties, Functions, and Work Procedures by the Technical Implementer of Women and Child Protection are declared to remain valid insofar as they do not conflict with the provisions in this Regional Regulation or are not specifically regulated in this Regional Regulation.

(2) At the time this Regional Regulation comes into force, FPKK that has been formed is declared to remain valid insofar as it does not conflict with the provisions in this Regional Regulation or is not specifically regulated in this Regional Regulation.

Article 48

At the time this Regional Regulation comes into force:
a. Regional Regulation of Gunungkidul Regency Number 25 Year 2012 concerning Protection of Women and Children Victims of Violence (Regional Gazette of Gunungkidul Regency Year 2012 Number 25); and

b. Regulation of the Regent of Gunungkidul Number 48 Year 2019 concerning Implementation of Protection for Women and Children Victims of Violence (Regional News of Gunungkidul Regency Year 2019 Number 48);

are revoked and declared invalid.

Article 49

This Regional Regulation comes into force on the date of promulgation.

In order that everyone may know of it, the promulgation of this Regional Regulation is ordered by placing it in the Regional Gazette of Gunungkidul Regency.

I. GENERAL

That women and children as vulnerable groups in family and community life often experience problems that violate human rights, that protection of women and children is a mandatory concurrent affair that is the authority, obligation, and responsibility of the Regional Government, so regulation is needed as a basis for implementing comprehensive and integrated protection for women and children in a Regional Regulation.

For this reason, protection efforts in the Region are needed in the form of institutional support and regulations that can guarantee its implementation. This Regional Regulation regulates protection efforts, especially in terms of prevention, services and empowerment for women and children from violence in Gunungkidul Regency.
