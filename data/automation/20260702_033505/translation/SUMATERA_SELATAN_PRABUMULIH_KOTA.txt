MAYOR OF PRABUMULIH
SOUTH SUMATRA PROVINCE
REGIONAL REGULATION OF PRABUMULIH CITY
NUMBER YEAR 2021
CONCERNING
WOMEN'S EMPOWERMENT AND CHILD PROTECTION WITH THE GRACE OF ALMIGHTY GOD THE MAYOR OF PRABUMULIH,

Considering:
a. that to guarantee Women's Empowerment and Child Protection in accordance with their dignity and to guarantee their right to life in accordance with their nature and destiny without discrimination and to receive Protection from Violence and other violations, legal certainty is necessary;
b. that to provide legal certainty in the implementation of Women's Empowerment and Child Protection and to carry out mandatory Regional government affairs, regulation in the form of Regional regulations is necessary;
c. that based on the considerations as referred to in letter a, and letter b, it is necessary to establish a Regional Regulation on Women's Empowerment and Child Protection;

Recalling:
1. Article 18 paragraph (6) of the 1945 Constitution of the Republic of Indonesia;
2. Law Number 39 of 1999 concerning Human Rights (State Gazette of the Republic of Indonesia Year 1999 Number 165, Supplement to State Gazette of the Republic Number 3886);
3. Law Number of 2001 concerning the Establishment of Prabumulih City (State Gazette of the Republic of Indonesia Year 2001 Number 86, Supplement to State Gazette of the Republic of Indonesia Number 4113);
4. Law Number 23 of 2002 concerning Child Protection (State Gazette of the Republic of Indonesia Year 2002 Number 109, Supplement to State Gazette of the Republic of Indonesia Number 4235) as amended by Law Number 35 of 2014 concerning Amendment to Law Number 23 of 2002 concerning

5. Child Protection (State Gazette of the Republic of Indonesia Year 2014 Number 297, Supplement to State Gazette of the Republic of Indonesia Number 5606);
6. Law Number 11 of 2012 concerning the Juvenile Criminal Justice System (State Gazette of the Republic of Indonesia Year 2012 Number 153, Supplement to State Gazette of the Republic of Indonesia Number 5332);
7. Law Number 23 of 2014 concerning Regional Government (State Gazette of the Republic of Indonesia Year 2014 Number 244, Supplement to State Gazette of the Republic of Indonesia Number 5587) as amended several times, most recently by Law Number of 2015 concerning the Second Amendment to Law Number 23 of 2014 concerning Regional Government (State Gazette of the Republic of Indonesia Year 2015 Number 58, Supplement to State Gazette of the Republic of Indonesia Number 5679);
8. Law Number 11 of 2020 concerning Job Creation (State Gazette of the Republic of Indonesia Year 2020 Number 245, Supplement to State Gazette of the Republic of Indonesia Number 6573);
9. Regulation of the State Minister for Women's Empowerment and Child Protection Number of 2010 Concerning Minimum Service Standards for Integrated Services for Women and Children Victims of Violence (State News of the Republic of Indonesia Year 2010 Number 56);
10. Regulation of the State Minister for Women's Empowerment and Child Protection Number 11 of 2011 Concerning Policy for the Development of Child-Friendly Regencies/Cities (State News of the Republic of Indonesia Year 2012 Number 168);
11. Regulation of the Minister for Women's Empowerment and Child Protection Number of 2018 concerning Guidelines for the Establishment of Regional Technical Implementation Units for Women and Child Protection (State News of the Republic of Indonesia Year 2018 Number 532).

With Joint Approval of
THE REGIONAL HOUSE OF REPRESENTATIVES OF PRABUMULIH CITY
and
THE MAYOR OF PRABUMULIH

DECIDES:

To Establish: REGIONAL REGULATION CONCERNING WOMEN'S EMPOWERMENT AND CHILD PROTECTION.

CHAPTER I
GENERAL PROVISIONS

Article 1
In this Regional Regulation, what is meant by:
1. City is Prabumulih City.
2. City Government is the Government of Prabumulih City.
3. Mayor is the Mayor of Prabumulih City.
4. Deputy Mayor is the Deputy Mayor of Prabumulih City.
5. Regional House of Representatives hereinafter abbreviated as DPRD is the Regional House of Representatives of Prabumulih City.
6. Regional Apparatus hereinafter abbreviated as PD is the Regional Apparatus within the Government of Prabumulih City.
7. Office of Population Control, Family Planning, Women's Empowerment and Child Protection hereinafter abbreviated as DPPKBPPPA is the Office of Population Control, Family Planning, Women's Empowerment and Child Protection of Prabumulih City.
8. Regional General Hospital hereinafter abbreviated as RSUD is the Regional General Hospital RSUD of Prabumulih City.
9. Integrated Service Center for Women's Empowerment and Child Protection hereinafter abbreviated as P2TP2A is the Integrated Service Center for Women's Empowerment and Child Protection of Prabumulih City.
10. Community-Based Integrated Protection hereinafter abbreviated as PATBM is the Community-Based Integrated Protection PATBM of Prabumulih City.
11. Community Health Center hereinafter abbreviated as Puskesmas is the Community Health Center Puskesmas of Prabumulih City.
12. Regional Technical Implementation Unit hereinafter abbreviated as UPTD is the Technical Implementation element of the Office that carries out Technical Operational activities and/or certain Technical Support Activities.
13. Private sector is part of the economy outside government ownership, consisting of Individuals or groups of Individuals with legal entities that carry out Public services in Prabumulih City.
14. Women's Empowerment is an effort to Protect, strengthen human rights, improve quality of life, and increase women's participation in development.
15. Child Protection is all activities to guarantee and protect Children and their rights so they can live, grow, develop, and participate optimally in accordance with human dignity and worth, and receive Protection from Violence and discrimination.
16. Child is a person who has not reached 18 (eighteen) years of age, including a Child still in the womb and not yet married.

17. Prevention is a direct effort carried out by the Regional Government, community and parents and/or family to prevent violations of women's and Children's rights.
18. Violence is any act that results in misery or suffering either physically, sexually, psychologically and/or domestic neglect including threats to commit acts, coercion, or unlawful deprivation of a person's freedom.
19. Children's Forum is a forum for Children's participation formed with the aim of facilitating Children's involvement in decision-making processes and enjoying changes concerning their lives either directly or indirectly, which is carried out with the consent and will of all Children based on awareness and understanding, in accordance with age and level of thinking maturity.
20. Child-Friendly City hereinafter abbreviated as KLA is a City that has a Child rights-based development system through the integration of government, community and business commitments and resources that are planned comprehensively and sustainably in policies, programs and activities to ensure the fulfillment of Children's rights.
21. Gender Mainstreaming is a strategy built to integrate gender into an integral dimension of planning, formulation, implementation, monitoring, and evaluation of policies, programs, and development activities in the Region.
22. Community is individuals, families, groups and social organizations and/or community organizations.
23. Women Victims of Violence are women who experience actions that result in misery or suffering physically, sexually or psychologically, including threats of certain actions, coercion or unlawful deprivation of freedom, whether occurring in the public sphere or in private life.
24. Child Victims of Violence are Children who experience actions that result in misery or suffering physically, mentally, sexually, psychologically or neglect that threatens life, body and degrades human dignity.
25. Human trafficking is the act of recruiting, transporting, harboring, sending, transferring, or receiving a person by threat of Violence, use of Violence, kidnapping, confinement, forgery, fraud, abuse of power or vulnerable position, debt bondage or giving payment or benefits, so as to obtain consent from the person holding control over that other person, whether done within the Country or between Countries, for the purpose of exploitation or resulting in the person being exploited.
26. Complaint handling is a series of actions carried out by integrated service providers to follow up on reports of acts of Violence against women and Children, submitted by victims, families or the community.

27. Social rehabilitation is a service aimed at restoring and developing the ability of a person experiencing social dysfunction to be able to carry out social functions properly.
28. Law enforcement is the action of officials authorized by the state to implement statutory regulations.
29. Legal aid is legal services provided by legal assistants and advocates to carry out the process of assisting witnesses and/or victims of Violence against women and Children that is gender sensitive.
30. Repatriation is an effort to return women and Child victims of Violence from abroad to the debarkation/entry point, or from the receiving Region to the Region of origin.
31. Social reintegration is an effort to reunite victims with family, substitute family, or community that can provide Protection and fulfill the needs of victims.
32. Safe house is a temporary shelter that aims to provide Protection and a sense of security to victims of Violence.
33. Shelter home is a forum for guidance and welfare services for street children that carries out social assistance/guidance activities, maintenance, care, education, and health, skills guidance in order to ensure that Children do not carry out activities on the streets so they can grow and develop normally.
34. Spiritual Guidance is an effort to restore mental health through strengthening the religion adhered to by the victim.
35. Public service provider is every state organizing institution, corporation, independent institution formed based on Law for public service activities, and other legal entities formed solely for public service activities.
36. Community Organization is an organization established and formed by the community voluntarily based on common aspirations, will, needs, interests, activities and goals to participate in development for the achievement of the goals of the Unitary State of the Republic of Indonesia based on Pancasila.
37. Parents are biological father and/or mother, or stepfather and/or stepmother, or adoptive father and/or mother.
38. Family is the smallest unit in society consisting of husband and wife or husband, wife and their Child, or father and his Child, or mother and her Child, or blood family in a straight line up or down to the third degree.
39. Customs are patterns of behavior that are eternal and passed down from one generation to another as heritage, so that they are strongly integrated with community behavior patterns.

Article 2
The implementation of Women's Empowerment and Child Protection is based on the principles of:
a. best interests for women and Children;
b. gender justice and gender equality;
c. local wisdom;
d. non-discrimination;
e. right to life, survival, and development;
f. respect for Children's opinions; and
g. law enforcement and legal certainty.

Article 3
This Regional Regulation aims to:
a. guarantee the fulfillment of women's empowerment and child protection in the City;
b. serve as a guideline in planning, policies and strategies for Women's Empowerment and Child Protection;
c. protect and prevent women and Children from actions, decisions and policies that violate women's and Children's rights;
d. increase the role and value of local wisdom and the role of customs in Women's Empowerment and Child Protection; and
e. serve as a legal umbrella for PD within the Prabumulih City Government that deals with women's empowerment and child protection in order to provide assistance and accompaniment to women and child victims from investigation at the police, prosecution to trial in court.

Article 4
The scope of regulation of Women's Empowerment and Child Protection includes:
a. protection of women;
b. protection of Children;
c. fulfillment of Children's rights;
d. improvement of quality of life of women and children;
e. community participation; and
f. guidance, supervision, accompaniment;
g. gender and child data system, disaggregated data on women and children.

CHAPTER II
IMPROVEMENT OF QUALITY OF LIFE OF WOMEN AND CHILDREN

Section One
General

Article 5
(1) In order to improve the quality of life of women and children, the City Government is responsible for:
a. respecting and guaranteeing the rights of women and children without discrimination in accordance with human dignity and worth;
b. increasing Women's Empowerment and child protection in roles, quality and capacity in the fields of:
1. politics;
2. law;
3. social;
4. economy; and
5. culture.
c. strengthening and developing institutions providing Women's Empowerment and child protection services in the City.
(2) The City Government's responsibility in efforts to improve the quality of life of women as referred to in paragraph (1) is carried out by PD in accordance with their duties and functions.

Section Two
Implementation of Women's Empowerment and Child Protection

Article 6
(1) The Mayor is responsible for the implementation of Women's Empowerment and Child Protection in the City.
(2) The Mayor's responsibility as referred to in paragraph (1) may be delegated to the Deputy Mayor.

Article 7
In implementing Women's Empowerment and Child Protection in the City, the Mayor establishes the PD that deals with Women's Empowerment tasks as coordinator of the implementation of Women's Empowerment and Child Protection.

Section Three
Formation of Working Group for Women's Empowerment and Child Protection in the City

Article 8
(1) In an effort to accelerate the institutionalization of Women's Empowerment and Child Protection in all PD, a working group for Women's Empowerment and Child Protection in the City is formed.
(2) Members of the working group for Women's Empowerment and Child Protection are all heads/leaders of PD.
(3) The Mayor appoints the Head of PD who deals with Women's Empowerment tasks as Chair of the Working Group and Head of the Secretariat of the Regional Women's Empowerment and Child Protection Working Group.
(4) The formation of the City Women's Empowerment and Child Protection working group is established by Mayor's Decree.

Article 9
The City Women's Empowerment and Child Protection Working Group as referred to in Article 8 has the tasks of:
a. promoting and facilitating Women's Empowerment and Child Protection to each PD;
b. carrying out socialization and advocacy of Women's Empowerment and Child Protection to sub-district heads, Village Heads and Village Chiefs;
c. preparing annual work programs;
d. encouraging the realization of planning and budgeting that is Responsive to Women's Empowerment and Child Protection;
e. preparing the work plan of the Women's Empowerment and Child Protection working group every year;
f. being responsible to the Mayor through the Deputy Mayor;
g. formulating policy recommendations to the Mayor;
h. preparing a profile of Regional Women's Empowerment and Child Protection;
i. monitoring the implementation of Women's Empowerment and Child Protection in each agency;
j. establishing a technical team to conduct analysis of the Regional budget;
k. preparing a regional action plan for City Women's Empowerment and Child Protection; and
l. encouraging the implementation of selection and appointment of Focal Points in each PD.

Article 10
(1) The Technical Team as referred to in Article 9 letter j consists of officials who understand gender responsive budget analysis and Women's Empowerment and Child Protection.
(2) The regional action plan for Regional Women's Empowerment and Child Protection as referred to in Article 9 letter k contains:
a. women's empowerment and child protection in statutory regulations in the City;
b. women's empowerment and child protection in the development cycle in the City;
c. strengthening of women's empowerment and child protection institutions in the City; and
d. strengthening community participation in the City.

Article 11
(1) The Focal Point for Women's Empowerment and Child Protection encourages the implementation of selection and appointment of Focal Points in each PD in each PD consisting of officials and/or staff who deal with planning and/or program tasks.
(2) The Focal Point for Women's Empowerment and Child Protection as referred to in paragraph (1), has the tasks of:
a. promoting Women's Empowerment and Child Protection in the work unit;
b. facilitating the preparation of PD work plans and budgeting that is responsive to Women's Empowerment and Child Protection;
c. carrying out training, socialization, advocacy of Women's Empowerment and Child Protection to all officials and staff within the PD;
d. reporting the implementation of Women's Empowerment and Child Protection to PD leadership;
e. encouraging the implementation of Budget analysis for Women's Empowerment and Child Protection; and
f. facilitating the preparation of Women's Empowerment and Child Protection data in each PD.
(3) The Focal Point for Women's Empowerment and Child Protection as referred to in paragraph (1), is selected and appointed by the PD leadership.

Section Four
Increasing Women's Role, Quality, and Capacity in the Political Field

Article 12
Efforts to increase women's role, quality and capacity in the political field as referred to in Article 5 paragraph (1) letter b number 1 include:
a. carrying out socialization to increase women's political participation in general elections;
b. implementing programs that encourage the level of women's involvement in political processes and public positions;
c. facilitating women's self-development in organizing, associating and assembling; and
d. involving women in decision-making concerning women's interests.

Section Five
Increasing Women's Role, Quality, and Capacity in the Legal Field

Article 13
Efforts to increase women's role, quality and capacity in the legal field as referred to in Article 5 paragraph (1) letter b number 2 include:
a. conducting socialization to increase women's legal knowledge and awareness;
b. mapping and reviewing gender-biased policies; and
c. involving women's organizations in the process of formulating and discussing Regional legal products related to women's participation.

Section Six
Increasing Women's Role, Quality, and Capacity in the Social Field

Article 14
Efforts to increase women's role, quality and capacity in the social field as referred to in Article 5 paragraph (1) letter b number 3 include:
a. increasing knowledge and skills to encourage fulfillment of education according to potential;
b. increasing and fulfilling quality health for women;
c. increasing awareness and knowledge about planning independent, healthy and prosperous families including access to consultation services and marriage registration;
d. increasing women's awareness of the impact of early marriage;
e. facilitating and seeking preservation of customs and development of science, technology, arts and culture for women's advancement;
f. increasing women's awareness and knowledge of planning independent, healthy and prosperous families;
g. implementing programs to reduce maternal mortality rates; and
h. encouraging the formation of women's organizations in the City.

Section Seven
Increasing Women's Role, Quality, and Capacity in the Economic Field

Article 15
Efforts to increase women's role, quality and capacity in the economic field as referred to in Article 5 paragraph (1) letter b number 4 include:
a. implementing programs, activities, training to improve women's skills and expertise in order to realize improved women's welfare;
b. facilitating strengthening of business capital for women with weak economies;
c. marketing products from businesses of women with weak economies;
d. training to develop women's capabilities through economic empowerment of pre-prosperous families; and
e. comprehensive entrepreneurship training for women.

CHAPTER III
PROTECTION OF WOMEN AND CHILDREN

Section One
General

Article 16
(1) The City Government in efforts to Protect women and children is responsible for:
a. establishing and implementing policies, programs and activities for the protection of women and children;
b. making efforts to prevent acts of Violence against women and children including the crime of human trafficking;
c. increasing the effectiveness of services for women and child victims of acts of Violence;
d. increasing efforts to Protect women workers;
e. strengthening and developing institutions providing women and child Protection services;
f. cooperating with the Government, other Regional Governments, the private sector, business world and/or NGOs in efforts to prevent acts of Violence against women and children; and
g. involving the active role of families, customary leaders, religious leaders, communities, and institutions in the field of women and child Protection.
(2) The City Government's responsibility in efforts to Protect women as referred to in paragraph (1) is carried out by PD in accordance with their duties and functions and may coordinate with relevant agencies.

Section Two
Prevention of Acts of Violence against Women and Children

Article 17
In order to prevent acts of Violence against women and children, the City Government:
a. organizes socialization regarding prevention of Violence against women and children by optimizing the role of customary, educational, cultural, religious institutions and community organizations;
b. builds networks and cooperation with law enforcement officials, government officials, universities and various non-governmental organizations that work and/or care about women and child victims of Violence;
c. provides public facilities that are comfortable and safe for women and children; and
d. forms an Integrated Service Center for Women's Empowerment and Children P2TP2A established by Mayor's decree and provides a Secretariat for the Integrated Service Center for Women's Empowerment and Children.

Section Three
Effectiveness of Services for Women and Child Victims of Acts of Violence

Part One
General

Article 18
(1) Protection of women and child victims of acts of Violence is carried out through integrated services.
(2) Provision of integrated services to women and child victims of acts of Violence as referred to in paragraph (1) uses the principles of:
a. gender responsive;
b. non-discriminatory;
c. equal and respectful relationships;
d. maintaining confidentiality;
e. providing a sense of security and comfort;
f. respecting individual differences;
g. not judging;
h. respecting the victim's choices and decisions;
i. sensitive to the victim's background and condition and use of language that is appropriate and understood by the victim;
j. fast and simple;
k. empathy; and
l. fulfillment of women's and children's rights.

Article 19
Forms of integrated services for women and child victims of acts of Violence include:
a. complaint handling;
b. health rehabilitation;
c. social rehabilitation;
d. victim accompaniment;
e. law enforcement and legal aid; and
f. repatriation and social reintegration.

Part Two
Complaint Handling

Article 20
Integrated complaint handling services as referred to in Article 19 letter a are carried out by:
a. hospitals;
b. puskesmas;
c. women and child service units;
d. trauma center protection houses;
e. women crisis centres;
f. advisory board for marriage guidance and preservation;
g. police;
h. prosecutor's office;
i. courts;
j. ministry of religion; and/or
k. legal aid institutions.

Article 21
(1) Complaints of acts of Violence against women and children can be made by reporters consisting of:
a. victims;
b. family;
c. community; and/or
d. community organizations in the field of women and child Protection.
(2) Reporters as referred to in paragraph (1) can make complaints:
a. directly;
b. by telephone; and/or
c. by letter.
(3) In addition to handling complaints submitted by reporters, the office dealing with Women and Child Protection, officers at service institutions as referred to in Article 20, are also authorized to provide Accompaniment in handling victims of acts of Violence against women and children obtained through other information.
(4) Further provisions regarding the procedure for complaint handling services as referred to in paragraph (1) and paragraph (2) are regulated by Mayor's Regulation.

Part Three
Health Rehabilitation

Article 22
(1) Health rehabilitation as referred to in Article 19 letter b is an effort that includes aspects of:
a. promotive;
b. preventive;
c. curative; and
d. rehabilitative.
(2) Health rehabilitation as referred to in paragraph (1) is carried out comprehensively, can be accessed 24 (twenty-four) hours, is of quality and is carried out in accordance with health service standards.
(3) Women and child victims of Violence and children in conflict with the law have the right to free health services, including:
a. medical services;
b. medicolegal services;
c. psychosocial counseling; and
d. visum.
(4) Further provisions regarding the procedure for implementing health services as referred to in paragraph (3) are regulated by Mayor's Regulation.

Part Four
Social Rehabilitation

Article 23
(1) Social rehabilitation as referred to in Article 19 letter c is aimed at restoring and developing the ability of women and child victims of Violence who experience social dysfunction to be able to carry out social functions in society properly.
(2) Social rehabilitation as referred to in paragraph (1) includes services for:
a. motivation and psychosocial diagnosis;
b. care and nurturing;
c. vocational training and entrepreneurship guidance;
d. mental spiritual guidance;
e. physical guidance;
f. social guidance and psychosocial counseling;
g. accessibility services;
h. social assistance and assistance;
i. resocialization guidance;
j. follow-up guidance; and/or
k. referral.
(3) The implementation of services as referred to in paragraph (2) is organized by:
a. PD that has duties and functions in the social field;
b. PD that deals with Women's Empowerment and children, and may cooperate with integrated service centers, women crisis centres or existing social community organizations; or
c. ministry of religion.

Article 24
(1) Counseling services as referred to in Article 23 paragraph (2) letter f aim to:
a. help women and child victims of violence recognize their problems and find effective ways to overcome them themselves;
b. empower women and child victims of Violence to determine their future;
c. provide moral support to women and child victims of Violence in facing the process they are going through; and
d. make women and child victims of Violence feel accepted in the community environment.
(2) Counseling services are carried out by gender-responsive social rehabilitation officers, consisting of:
a. counselors;
b. psychologists; and
c. psychiatrists.
(3) Counseling services are carried out in a place that guarantees a sense of security, comfort and confidentiality of information from women and child victims of Violence.
(4) Stages of counseling services include:
a. initial counseling;
b. follow-up counseling;
c. case discussion; and
d. site visits.

Article 25
(1) The City Government may provide safe houses for women and child victims of Violence.
(2) The provision of safe house services as referred to in paragraph (1) aims to protect women and child victims of Violence from intimidation by perpetrators or other parties.
(3) The location of the safe house as referred to in paragraph (1) must be kept confidential.
(4) Criteria for women and child victims of Violence who need safe houses include:
a. life threatened;
b. receiving rejection from family or community;
c. requiring intensive services but their residence is relatively far away; and/or
d. abandoned if not placed in a safe house.
(5) The implementation of safe house services is carried out by social rehabilitation officers who have sensitivity to women's empowerment and child protection and/or gender awareness, consisting of:
a. social workers/volunteers;
b. security officers; and/or
c. community leaders.
(6) Further provisions regarding the implementation of safe house services are regulated by Mayor's Regulation.

Article 26
(1) Spiritual guidance aims at mental recovery.
(2) Spiritual guidance as referred to in paragraph (1) is carried out based on the principles of:
a. not imposing any religion or belief; and
b. leaving the final decision in the hands of the victim (self determination).
(3) Spiritual guidance is carried out by spiritual guidance officers.
(4) Spiritual guidance officers as referred to in paragraph (3) consist of consultants from the Advisory Board for Marriage Guidance and Preservation (BP4), Officers of the Religious Affairs Office, Ustads/Ulamas, Pastors, Priests and other religious leaders who have sensitivity to women's empowerment and child protection and/or gender awareness.

Part Five
Law Enforcement and Legal Aid

Article 27
(1) Law enforcement and legal aid services as referred to in Article 19 letter d are actions by law enforcement officials to implement statutory regulations in handling cases of acts of Violence against women and children, especially to provide sanctions to perpetrators and provide Protection for witnesses and/or victims.
(2) Law enforcement and legal aid services as referred to in paragraph (1) are carried out by prioritizing the best interests of the victim.

Article 28
(1) Law enforcement and legal aid services are aimed at providing accompaniment and Protection to women and child victims of acts of Violence and witnesses with a gender-responsive approach so that victims obtain justice and legal certainty.
(2) Types of legal aid services include:
a. legal aid in the reporting process and preparation of examination reports;
b. legal aid in the prosecution process at the prosecutor's office;
c. legal aid in the examination process in court;
d. legal aid in the court hearing decision process until there is a final legal decision; and
e. legal aid in the mediation process.
(3) The City Government provides legal aid to women and child victims of acts of Violence based on statutory provisions.
(4) In providing legal aid as referred to in paragraph (3), the Regional Government may cooperate with legal aid institutions, women crisis centers, advocates and/or other gender-sensitive legal assistants.

Part Six
Repatriation and Social Reintegration

Article 29
(1) Repatriation is aimed at returning women and child victims of Violence to their Region of origin.
(2) Repatriation of women and child victims of Violence is accompanied by social reintegration efforts.

Article 30
(1) Social reintegration is carried out in order to reunite women and child victims of Violence with family, substitute family, or community that can provide Protection and fulfill the needs of victims.
(2) Social reintegration is aimed so that:
a. women and child victims of Violence can continue their social life; and
b. women and child victims of Violence can have a decent life.
(3) Social reintegration as referred to in paragraph (1) is carried out after the victim receives social rehabilitation services.

Article 31
The City Government carries out repatriation and social reintegration of women and child victims of Violence in accordance with statutory regulations.

Part Seven
Employment Protection

Article 32
(1) Employers who employ women must maintain decency, safety and security while at work.
(2) Employers must place female workers in places that are safe for women's reproductive health.
(3) Employers must fulfill the rights of female workers to receive wages in accordance with statutory provisions.
(4) Employers must provide special breastfeeding facilities in their companies.

Article 33
(1) Female workers/laborers under 18 (eighteen) years of age are prohibited from being employed between 23.00 WIB and 07.00 WIB.
(2) Employers are prohibited from employing pregnant female workers/laborers who according to a doctor's statement are dangerous to the health and safety of their pregnancy.
(3) Employers who employ female workers/laborers between 23.00 WIB and 07.00 WIB, must:
a. provide nutritious food and drinks; and
b. maintain security and health.
(4) Employers must provide shuttle transportation for female workers/laborers who leave and return from work between 23.00 WIB and 05.00 WIB.

CHAPTER IV
IMPROVING FAMILY QUALITY

Article 34
(1) In order to improve the quality of family life, the City Government is responsible for:
a. making efforts to improve family quality to realize gender equality and Children's rights;
b. providing services for families in realizing gender equality and Children's rights; and
c. strengthening and developing institutions providing family quality improvement services in the City.
(2) The City Government's responsibility in efforts to improve family quality as referred to in paragraph (1) is carried out by PD in accordance with their duties and functions.

Article 35
The City Government makes efforts to improve family quality as referred to in Article 34 paragraph (1) letter a including:
a. guidance;
b. counseling;
c. supervision;
d. facilitation of family development implementation; and
e. improving the health quality of pregnant and breastfeeding mothers.

Article 36
Guidance as referred to in Article 35 letter a, includes:
a. growth and development of toddlers;
b. Protection of adolescent reproductive health;
c. Maturation of marriage age;
d. Birth regulation; and
e. Population management.

Article 37
Counseling as referred to in Article 35 letter b includes:
a. conducting outreach to improve the quality of life of women and children; and
b. conducting premarital counseling.

Article 38
Supervision as referred to in Article 35 letter c includes:
a. evaluating data on toddler growth and development; and
b. evaluating post-marriage data.

Article 39
Provision of services for families as referred to in Article 34 paragraph (1) letter b includes:
a. communication, information and education;
b. contraceptive tools and drugs;
c. recording and reporting of family planning services;
d. adequate health service facilities and infrastructure; and
e. lactation rooms in every public facility.

Article 40
Communication, information and education as referred to in Article 39 letter a is a communication activity to increase knowledge and improve the attitudes and behavior of families and communities in population and family planning Programs.

Article 41
(1) In addition to the City Government, private parties that organize Public services must provide lactation rooms.
(2) Lactation rooms as referred to in paragraph (1) are proper and adequate rooms.
(3) Private parties that do not provide lactation rooms as referred to in paragraph (2) are subject to administrative sanctions in the form of:
a. written warning;
b. limitation of business activities;
c. freezing of business activities;
d. administrative fines; and/or
e. revocation of permits.

CHAPTER V
PROTECTION AND FULFILLMENT OF CHILDREN'S RIGHTS

Section One
General

Article 42
(1) The City Government in efforts to protect and fulfill Children's rights is responsible for:
a. establishing and implementing policies and programs/activities for the protection and fulfillment of Children's rights;
b. respecting and guaranteeing the human rights of every Child without distinguishing ethnicity, religion, race, class, gender, ethnicity, culture and language, legal status of the Child, birth order of the Child, and physical and/or mental condition;
c. strengthening and developing institutions providing family quality improvement services in realizing Children's rights;
d. guaranteeing Protection, maintenance, and welfare of Children by taking into account the rights and obligations of parents, guardians, or other persons legally responsible for the Child;
e. providing facilities and infrastructure in the implementation of fulfillment of Children's rights;
f. guaranteeing the right of Children to express opinions according to the age and level of intelligence of the Child;
g. conducting supervision and guidance in the implementation; and
h. cooperating with the private sector in the implementation of Child Protection.
(2) The City Government's responsibility in efforts to Protect Children as referred to in paragraph (1) is carried out by PD in accordance with their duties and functions.

Section Two
Birth Registration

Article 43
(1) The City Government is obliged to organize birth registration of Children with the issuance of birth certificates.
(2) Birth certificates as referred to in paragraph (1) are organized free of charge.

Section Three
Education

Article 44
The City Government guarantees the implementation of a compulsory education program (nine) years for Children aged 7 (seven) to 15 (fifteen) years.

Article 45
(1) Every organizer of an education unit must provide opportunities for Children to obtain education services without discrimination.
(2) Every organizer of an education unit is prohibited from expelling Children from educational institutions unless there is a guarantee for the continuity of the Child's education.
(3) In the event that a Child victim of acts of Violence drops out of school, the organizer of the education unit must coordinate with relevant agencies.

Article 46
(1) Every Child has the right to obtain education and teaching in the context of developing their personality and level of intelligence according to their interests and talents.
(2) Every Child has the right to receive Protection in education units from sexual crimes and Violence committed by educators, education personnel, fellow students, and/or other parties.

Article 47
(1) Children with disabilities and Children with special needs are given equal opportunities to obtain formal, non-formal and informal education services, including inclusive education programs.
(2) The regional government provides teaching staff for inclusive education in every education unit.

Section Four
Health and Social

Article 48
(1) The City Government must organize comprehensive health services for Children, so that every Child obtains optimal health status from the womb.
(2) Comprehensive health services as referred to in paragraph (1) include:
a. promotive, preventive, curative and rehabilitative efforts for basic health services; and
b. exemption from cost burdens for malnourished Children, Children with disabilities, Children with special needs, Children infected with HIV/AIDS, child workers, Children victims of narcotics, alcohol, psychotropic and other addictive substance abuse, Children who are victims of Violence, and Children victims of human trafficking.
(3) The City Government, community, family and parents must endeavor that Children born are free from diseases that threaten survival and/or cause disability.

Article 49
(1) Promotive efforts for basic health services as referred to in Article 48 paragraph (2) letter a are aimed at improving health status more optimally.
(2) Promotive efforts as referred to in paragraph (1) are carried out by providing socialization or outreach to the community regarding healthy lifestyles including:
a. the importance of exclusive breastfeeding for babies;
b. the importance of immunization for toddlers;
c. clean living patterns;
d. the importance of nutritious food; and
e. the dangers of drugs and smoking.
(3) In addition to promotive efforts as referred to in paragraph (2), the City Government may carry out other activities in the context of health implementation.

Article 50
(1) Preventive efforts for basic health services as referred to in Article 48 paragraph (2) letter a are aimed at preventing disease or health problems.
(2) Efforts as referred to in paragraph (1) are carried out by:
a. requiring mothers to provide exclusive breastfeeding to babies up to 6 (six) months of age;
b. providing free immunization and vitamins to babies and toddlers;
c. providing nutritious food to toddlers from underprivileged families;
d. optimizing the role of posyandu for health services for mothers and toddlers;
e. conducting periodic health examinations; and
f. providing sanitation facilities and clean water in every school.

Article 51
(1) Curative efforts for basic health services as referred to in Article 48 paragraph (2) letter a are aimed at preventing disease from becoming more severe through treatment.
(2) Treatment as referred to in paragraph (1) is carried out free of charge for Children from underprivileged families.

Article 52
(1) Rehabilitative efforts for basic health services as referred to in Article 48 paragraph (2) letter a are aimed at maintaining and restoring conditions after illness.
(2) Rehabilitative efforts as referred to in paragraph (1) are carried out through periodic health examinations.

Article 53
(1) Implementation of social welfare is aimed at:
a. individuals;
b. families;
c. groups; and/or
d. communities.
(2) Implementation of social welfare as referred to in paragraph (1) is prioritized for those who have an inhumane life and have social problem criteria:
a. poverty;
b. neglect;
c. disability;
d. remoteness;
e. social disability and behavioral deviation;
f. disaster victims; and/or
g. victims of violence, exploitation and discrimination.
(3) Social welfare as referred to in paragraph (1) may be in the form of service provision:
a. health;
b. education;
c. legal aid;
d. accompaniment;
e. social, mental and spiritual guidance;
f. social rehabilitation; and/or
g. reintegration.

Section Five
Employment

Article 54
The Regional Government conducts regular supervision of every person/entity that employs Children in efforts to Protect child workers.

Article 55
Every person/entity that employs Children must pay attention to the requirements:
a. at least 15 (fifteen) years of age;
b. obtaining written consent from the parents/guardian of the child worker;
c. having a written work agreement between the employer and the parents/guardian of the child worker and obtaining validation from the authorized agency;
d. not employing Children at night; and
e. working time of at most 3 (three) hours per day.

Article 56
In the event that Children are employed together with adult workers, then the workplace for Children must be separated from the workplace for adult workers.

Article 57
(1) Every person/entity is prohibited from employing and involving Children in the worst forms of work.
(2) The worst forms of work referred to in paragraph (1) include:
a. all work in the form of slavery or the like;
b. all work that exploits, provides, or offers Children for prostitution, pornography production, pornographic performances, or gambling;
c. all work that exploits, provides, or involves Children for the production and trade of liquor, narcotics, psychotropics, and other addictive substances; and/or
d. all work that endangers the health, safety, or morals of Children.

Section Six
Facilitation of the Juvenile Justice System

Article 58
(1) Facilitation of the juvenile justice system includes criminal and civil justice.
(2) Facilitation of the criminal justice system as referred to in paragraph (1) includes:
a. provision of assistants or Child volunteers during examination by investigators;
b. provision of legal aid and psychologist services;
c. involving members/police investigators in efforts to prevent criminal acts committed by Children or Children as victims;
d. realizing a restorative justice process for Children in conflict with the law; and
e. rehabilitation and social reintegration services.
(3) Fulfillment of Children's rights in the civil justice system as referred to in paragraph (1) includes the provision and provision of consultation and legal aid services during the judicial process.
(4) In facilitating the juvenile justice system as referred to in paragraph (1) and paragraph (2), the authorized PD is the PD that deals with women's Empowerment and Child Protection and has the obligation to accompany child victims and perpetrators, in police examinations, prosecution and in court.

Section Seven
Children's Forum

Article 59
(1) In implementing the fulfillment of Children's rights to express opinions and participate, the City Government facilitates the formation of a Children's Forum.
(2) The Children's Forum as referred to in paragraph (1) is a representation of Children in the City, both representation of Children's geographic domicile, components of Children's socio-cultural groups and Children's educational background.
(3) In every formulation of policies related to Children, the City Government must pay attention to and accommodate Children's opinions submitted through the Children's participation Forum.
(4) Further provisions regarding the formation of the Children's Forum as referred to in paragraph (1) to paragraph (3) are stipulated by Mayor's Regulation.

Section Eight
Child-Friendly City

Article 60
(1) In order to fulfill Children's rights in an integrated, systematic and sustainable manner, the City Government establishes a KLA Development policy.
(2) The KLA Development policy contains:
a. the KLA concept concerning objectives, strategies and roles of related parties;
b. Children's rights; and
c. the KLA development approach.

Article 61
(1) The KLA development policy is directed at fulfilling Children's rights which are divided into 5 (five) groups, including:
a. civil rights and freedoms;
b. family environment and alternative care;
c. basic health and welfare;
d. education, use of leisure time, and cultural activities; and
e. special Protection.
(2) Further provisions regarding the KLA development policy as referred to in paragraph (1) are regulated by Mayor's Regulation.

Article 62
(1) In order to effectively implement the KLA policy in the Region, a KLA Task Force is formed.
(2) The formation and membership composition of the KLA Task Force is stipulated by Mayor's Decree.
(3) The KLA Task Force is domiciled at the DPPKBPPPA Office.
(4) The KLA Task Force as referred to in paragraph (1), has the main tasks of:
a. coordinating the implementation of KLA policies and development;
b. conducting socialization, advocacy and communication, information and education of KLA policies;
c. collecting basic data for KLA development;
d. conducting needs analysis sourced from basic data;
e. disseminating basic data;
f. determining the focus and priority of programs in realizing KLA, which are adjusted to regional potential;
g. preparing a 5 (five) year Child-Friendly City regional action plan and work mechanism; and
h. conducting monitoring, evaluation and reporting at least 1 (one) year once.

Article 63
In carrying out its duties, the KLA Task Force may coordinate and cooperate with other institutions that provide Child Protection services.

CHAPTER VI
CHILD PROTECTION

Article 64
The City Government, in order to provide protection to Children:
a. implements and supports national policies in the implementation of Child Protection in the City;
b. guarantees Children to use their rights in expressing opinions according to the age and level of intelligence of the Child;
c. prevents Violence against Children by optimizing the role of customary institutions;
d. conducts socialization, seminars and outreach regarding Protection and Children's rights;
e. develops child-friendly school models;
f. provides public facilities that are friendly and safe for Children; and
g. encourages community awareness of the importance of Protection for Children.

Article 65
Service efforts made by the City Government for child victims of acts of Violence as referred to in Article 42 to Article 64 apply mutatis mutandis to service efforts for Child victims of acts of Violence.

CHAPTER VII
PARTICIPATION OF COMMUNITY, AND PARENTS AND/OR FAMILY

Section One
Community

Article 66
(1) Community participation in efforts to Protect women and Children:
a. participating in prevention and providing support for the development of prevention programs for violations of women's and Children's rights;
b. providing information and/or reporting violations of women's and Children's rights to authorized parties; and
c. supervising the law enforcement process for cases of violations of women's and Children's rights.
(2) Community, parents and families are responsible for protecting and preventing violations of women's and Children's rights.
(3) Community participation as referred to in paragraph (1) and paragraph (2) is based on statutory regulations and local wisdom values of the local community.

Article 67
(1) In order to prevent violations of women's rights, the community:
a. monitors various conditions occurring in their environment that can cause violations of women's rights;
b. increases awareness of violations of women's rights;
c. strengthens the role of community, religious organizations and family environment; and
d. supervises the implementation of Women's Empowerment and Child Protection organized by the City Government with a mechanism for conveying aspirations to the Mayor or Regional House of Representatives.
(2) In order to prevent violations of Children's rights, the community:
a. monitors various conditions occurring in their environment that cause violations of Children's rights;
b. increases awareness of violations of Children's rights; and
c. strengthens the role of community, religious organizations and family environment, and prevents early marriage.

Section Two
Parents and/or Family

Article 68
Families in efforts to Protect women's rights play a role in:
a. protecting women and children from conflict and Violence in the household that degrades human dignity;
b. providing equal opportunities for women and children to develop themselves in the fields of education, social, economic, political and cultural; and
c. preventing violations of women's and children's rights in the household.

Article 69
(1) Parents in efforts to Protect Children's rights play a role in:
a. caring for, maintaining, educating, and protecting Children;
b. developing Children according to their abilities, talents, and interests;
c. providing formal and non-formal education;
d. providing character education and instilling moral values in Children;
e. guiding Children to behave in accordance with religion, ethics and morals and customs; and
f. preventing early marriage.
(2) In the event that parents do not exist, or their whereabouts are unknown, or for some reason, cannot carry out their obligations and responsibilities, then the obligations and responsibilities as referred to in paragraph (1) may transfer to the family.

CHAPTER VIII
GUIDANCE AND SUPERVISION

Article 70
(1) The City Government must conduct guidance and supervision of the implementation of Women's empowerment and protection of Child victims of Violence.
(2) Guidance as referred to in paragraph (1), includes:
a. guidelines and fulfillment standards;
b. technical guidance and training;
c. provision of facilities;
d. monitoring; and
e. evaluation.
(3) Guidance as referred to in paragraph (2) is in order to realize the objectives of empowerment and Protection of women and Child victims of Violence according to minimum service standards.

Article 71
(1) Supervision of the implementation of women's empowerment and Child Protection as regulated in this Regional Regulation is carried out by a Supervisory institution specifically formed for that purpose.
(2) In carrying out supervision, the Institution referred to is authorized to recommend cases to law enforcement officials, government agencies and other community institutions as regulated in the Law.
(3) Recommendations submitted must be followed up by law enforcement officials, government agencies, community institutions and other related individuals.

CHAPTER IX
FINANCING

Article 72
Financing for the implementation of Women's Empowerment and Child Protection programs and activities is sourced from:
a. regional revenue and expenditure budget; and
b. other legitimate sources in accordance with statutory provisions.

CHAPTER X
SANCTIONS

Article 73
(1) Every person who intentionally does not provide protection to women and Children so as to cause violence, does not report and does not provide protection to victims, is subject to sanctions as regulated in applicable statutory regulations.
(2) If officials appointed to implement protection do not carry out their obligations and responsibilities, they are subject to sanctions in accordance with applicable statutory provisions.
(3) UPT managers who carry out service duties but violate service principles, may be subject to sanctions in accordance with applicable mechanisms.
(4) Private parties that carry out Public services do not carry out their obligations and responsibilities in accordance with this regulation are subject to sanctions in accordance with applicable statutory provisions.

CHAPTER XI
CLOSING PROVISIONS

Article 74
Mayor's Regulations as implementation of this Regional Regulation are stipulated at the latest 6 (six) months after this Regional Regulation is promulgated.

Article 75
This Regional Regulation comes into force on the date of promulgation.
In order that everyone may know of it, the promulgation of this Regional Regulation is ordered by placing it in the Regional Gazette of Prabumulih City.
