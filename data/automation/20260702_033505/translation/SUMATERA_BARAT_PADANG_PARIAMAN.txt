CHAPTER I
GENERAL PROVISIONS

Article 1

In this Regional Regulation, what is meant by:

1. Region is Padang Pariaman Regency.

2. Regional Government is the Government of Padang Pariaman Regency.

3. Regent is the Regent of Padang Pariaman Regency.

4. Regional Work Unit hereinafter abbreviated as SKPD is the Regional Work Unit within the Government of Padang Pariaman Regency.

5. Women's Empowerment is an effort to Protect, strengthen human rights, improve quality of life, and increase women's participation in development.

6. Child Protection is all activities to guarantee and protect Children and their rights so they can live, grow, develop, and participate optimally in accordance with human dignity, and receive Protection from Violence and discrimination.

7. Child is a person who has not reached 18 (eighteen) years of age, including a Child still in the womb and not yet married.

8. Prevention is a direct effort carried out by the Regional Government, community and parents and/or family to prevent violations of the rights of women and Children.

9. Violence is any act that results in misery or suffering either physically, sexually, psychologically and/or domestic neglect including threats to commit acts, coercion, or unlawful deprivation of a person's freedom.

10. Children's Forum is a forum for Child participation formed with the aim of facilitating the involvement of Children in decision-making processes and enjoying changes relating to their lives either directly or indirectly, which is carried out with the consent and will of all Children based on awareness and understanding, in accordance with age and level of thinking maturity.

11. Child-Friendly Regency hereinafter abbreviated as KLA is a Regency that has a child rights-based development system through the integration of government commitment and resources, community and business world that is planned comprehensively and sustainably in policies, programs and activities to ensure the fulfillment of Children's rights.

12. Gender Mainstreaming is a strategy built to integrate gender into an integral dimension of planning, formulation, implementation, monitoring and evaluation of national development program policies.

13. Community is individuals, families, groups and social organizations and/or community organizations.

14. Women Victims of Violence are women who experience actions that result in misery or suffering physically, sexually or psychologically, including threats of certain actions, coercion or arbitrary deprivation of freedom, whether occurring in the public sphere or in private life.

15. Child Victims of Violence are Children who experience actions that result in misery or suffering physically, mentally, sexually, psychologically or neglect that threatens life, body and degrades human dignity.

16. Human trafficking is the act of recruiting, transporting, harboring, sending, transferring, or receiving a person by threat of Violence, use of Violence, kidnapping, confinement, forgery, fraud, abuse of power or vulnerable position, debt bondage or giving payment or benefits, so as to obtain consent from the person holding control over that other person, whether done within the Country or between Countries, for the purpose of exploitation or resulting in the person being exploited.

17. Complaint handling is a series of actions taken by integrated service providers to follow up on reports of acts of Violence against women and Children, submitted by victims, families or the community.

18. Social rehabilitation is a service aimed at restoring and developing the ability of a person experiencing social dysfunction to be able to carry out their social functions normally.

19. Law enforcement is the action of apparatus authorized by the state to implement statutory regulations.

20. Legal aid is legal services provided by legal assistants and advocates to carry out the process of assisting witnesses and/or victims of Violence against women and Children that is gender sensitive.

21. Repatriation is an effort to return women and Child victims of Violence from abroad to the debarkation/entry point, or from the receiving Region to the Region of origin.

22. Social reintegration is an effort to reunite victims with family, substitute family, or community that can provide Protection and fulfillment of needs for victims.

23. Safe house is a temporary shelter that aims to provide Protection and a sense of security to victims of Violence.

24. Transit house is a forum for guidance and welfare services for street children that carries out assistance/social guidance activities, maintenance, care, education, and health, skills guidance in order to ensure that Children do not carry out activities on the streets so they can grow and develop normally.

25. Spiritual Guidance is an effort to restore the soul through strengthening the religion adhered to by the victim.

26. Gender Mainstreaming is a strategy built to integrate gender into an integral dimension of planning, formulation, implementation, monitoring, and evaluation of policies, programs, and development activities in the Region.

27. Gender responsive is consistent and systematic attention to the differences between women and men in society accompanied by efforts to eliminate structural and cultural barriers in achieving gender equality.

28. Gender Sensitivity or Gender Awareness is the ability to identify gaps in power relations between women and men in the family and in the community, the impact of gender-based division of labor on women and men, and the experiences, problems, needs, interests and aspirations of women and men are different.

29. Public service providers are every state organizing institution, corporation, independent institution formed based on Law for public service activities, and other legal entities formed solely for public service activities.

30. Community Organizations are organizations established and formed by the community voluntarily based on common aspirations, will, needs, interests, activities and goals to participate in development for the achievement of the goals of the Unitary State of the Republic of Indonesia based on Pancasila.

31. Parents are biological father and/or mother, or step father and/or mother, or adoptive father and/or mother.

32. Family is the smallest unit in society consisting of husband and wife or husband, wife and their Child, or father and his Child, or mother and her Child, or blood family in a straight line up or down to the third degree.

33. Customs are eternal and hereditary conduct patterns from one generation to another as heritage, so that it is strongly integrated with community behavior patterns.

Article 2

The implementation of Women's Empowerment and Child Protection is based on the principles of:

a. best interests for women and Children;

b. gender justice and gender equality;

c. local wisdom;

d. law enforcement and legal certainty.

e. non-discrimination;

f. right to life, survival, and development;

g. respect for the opinion of the Child; and

h. law enforcement and legal certainty.

Article 3

This Regional Regulation aims to:

a. guarantee the fulfillment of the rights of women and Children in the Region

b. serve as a guideline for the Region in planning, policies and strategies for Women's Empowerment and Child Protection;

c. protect and prevent women and Children from actions, decisions and policies that violate the rights of women and Children;

d. increase the role and value of local wisdom and the role of customs in Women's Empowerment and Child Protection;

Article 4

The scope of regulation of Women's Empowerment and Child Protection includes:

a. improvement of women's quality of life;

b. Protection of women;

c. gender and Child data system;

d. fulfillment of Children's rights;

e. Child Protection;

f. community participation; and

g. guidance and supervision.

CHAPTER II
IMPROVEMENT OF WOMEN'S QUALITY OF LIFE

Part One
General

Article 5

(1) In order to improve the quality of life of women, the regional government is responsible for:

a. respecting and guaranteeing women's rights without discrimination in accordance with human dignity;

b. implementing Gender Mainstreaming in the Region;

c. forming a Gender Mainstreaming working group in the Region;

d. increasing Women's Empowerment in roles, quality and capabilities in the fields of;

1. politics;

2. law;

3. social; and

4. economy.

e. strengthening and developing institutions providing Women's Empowerment services in the Region;

(2) The responsibility of the Regional Government in efforts to improve the quality of life of women as referred to in paragraph (1) is carried out by SKPD in accordance with their duties and functions.

Part Two
Implementation of Gender Mainstreaming in the Region

Article 6

(1) The Regent is responsible for the implementation of Gender Mainstreaming in the Region.

(2) The responsibility of the Regent as referred to in paragraph (1) may be delegated to the Vice Regent.

Article 7

In implementing Gender Mainstreaming in the Region as referred to in Article 5 paragraph (1) letter b, the Regent appoints the SKPD that handles Women's Empowerment tasks as coordinator of Gender Mainstreaming implementation.

Part Three
Formation of Gender Mainstreaming Working Group in the Region

Article 8

(1) In an effort to accelerate the institutionalization of Gender Mainstreaming in all Regional SKPDs, a Gender Mainstreaming working group is formed in the Region.

(2) Members of the Gender Mainstreaming working group are all heads/leaders of SKPD.

(3) The Regent appoints the head of Bappeda as chairman of the Regional Gender Mainstreaming working group and the head of SKPD that handles Women's Empowerment tasks as head of the secretariat of the Regional Gender Mainstreaming working group.

(4) The formation of the Regional Gender Mainstreaming working group is established by Regent's decree.

Article 9

The Regional Gender Mainstreaming working group as referred to in Article 14 has the tasks of:

a. promoting and facilitating Gender Mainstreaming to each SKPD;

b. carrying out socialization and advocacy of Gender Mainstreaming to sub-district heads and village heads;

c. preparing annual work programs;

d. encouraging the realization of gender-responsive planning and budgeting;

e. preparing the work plan of the Gender Mainstreaming working group every year;

f. being responsible to the Regent through the Vice Regent;

g. formulating policy recommendations to the Regent;

h. preparing Regional Gender profiles;

i. monitoring the implementation of Gender Mainstreaming in each agency;

j. establishing a technical team to conduct analysis of Regional budgets;

k. preparing a regional action plan for Regional Gender Mainstreaming; and

l. encouraging the implementation of selection and appointment of focal points in each SKPD.

Article 10

(1) The Technical Team as referred to in Article 9 letter j consists of apparatus who understand gender responsive budget analysis.

(2) The regional action plan for Regional Gender Mainstreaming as referred to in Article 9 letter k contains:

a. Gender Mainstreaming in statutory regulations in the Region;

b. Gender Mainstreaming in the development cycle in the Region;

c. strengthening Gender Mainstreaming institutions in the Region; and

d. strengthening community participation in the Region.

Article 11

(1) The Gender Mainstreaming Focal Point in each SKPD consists of officials and/or staff who handle planning and/or program tasks.

(2) The Gender Mainstreaming Focal Point as referred to in paragraph (1), has the tasks of:

a. promoting Gender Mainstreaming in the work unit;

b. facilitating the preparation of work plans and SKPD budgeting that is gender responsive;

c. carrying out training, socialization, advocacy of Gender Mainstreaming to all officials and staff within the SKPD;

d. reporting the implementation of Gender Mainstreaming to SKPD leadership;

e. encouraging the implementation of gender analysis of policies, programs, and activities in the work unit; and

f. facilitating the preparation of gender data in each SKPD;

(3) The Gender Mainstreaming Focal Point as referred to in paragraph (1), is selected and appointed by the SKPD leadership.

Part Four
Increasing the Role, Quality, and Capability of Women
Political Field

Article 12

Efforts to increase the role, quality and capability of women in the political field as referred to in Article 5 paragraph (1) letter d number 1 include:

a. carrying out socialization to increase women's political participation in general elections;

b. implementing programs that encourage the level of women's involvement in political processes and public positions;

c. facilitating women's self-development in organizing, associating and assembling;

d. involving women in decision-making concerning women's interests;

Part Five
Increasing the Role, Quality, and Capability of Women
Legal Field

Article 13

Efforts to increase the role, quality and capability of women in the legal field as referred to in Article 5 paragraph (1) letter d number 2 include:

a. conducting socialization to increase women's legal knowledge and awareness;

b. mapping and reviewing gender-biased policies;

c. involving women's organizations in the process of drafting and discussing Regional legal products related to women's participation;

Part Six
Increasing the Role, Quality, and Capability of Women
Social Field

Article 14

Efforts to increase the role, quality and capability of women in the social field as referred to in Article 5 paragraph (1) letter d number 3 include:

a. increasing knowledge and skills to encourage fulfillment of education in accordance with potential;

b. increasing and fulfilling quality of health for women;

c. increasing awareness and knowledge about independent, healthy and prosperous family planning including access to consultation services and marriage registration;

d. increasing women's awareness of the impact of early marriage;

e. facilitating and seeking preservation of customs and development of science, technology, arts and culture for women's advancement;

f. increasing women's awareness and knowledge of independent, healthy and prosperous family planning;

g. implementing programs to reduce maternal mortality rates;

h. encouraging the formation of women's organizations in the Region;

Part Seven
Increasing the Role, Quality, and Capability of Women
Economic Field

Article 15

Efforts to increase the role, quality and capability of women in the economic field as referred to in Article 5 paragraph (1) letter d number 4 include:

a. implementing programs, activities, training to improve women's skills and expertise in order to realize improvement of women's welfare;

b. facilitating business capital strengthening for women with weak economies;

c. marketing products from businesses of women with weak economies;

d. training to develop women's capabilities through empowerment of pre-prosperous family economies;

e. comprehensive entrepreneurship training for women.

CHAPTER III
PROTECTION OF WOMEN

Part One
General

Article 16

(1) The Regional Government in efforts to Protect women is responsible for:

a. establishing and implementing policies, programs and activities for Protection of women;

b. making efforts to prevent acts of Violence against women including criminal acts of human trafficking;

c. increasing the effectiveness of services for women victims of acts of Violence;

d. increasing efforts to Protect women workers;

e. strengthening and developing institutions providing women's Protection services;

f. cooperating with the Government, other Regional Governments, private parties, business world and/or NGOs in efforts to prevent acts of Violence against women;

g. involving the active role of families, ninik mamak, bundo kanduang, customary leaders, religious leaders, community, and institutions in the field of Protection of women;

(2) The responsibility of the Regional Government in efforts to Protect women as referred to in paragraph (1) is carried out by SKPD in accordance with their duties and functions and may coordinate with related agencies.

Part Two
Prevention of Acts of Violence against Women

Article 17

In order to prevent acts of Violence against women, the Regional Government:

a. conducts socialization regarding prevention of Violence against women by optimizing the role of customary, cultural, religious institutions and community organizations;

b. builds networks and cooperation with law enforcement apparatus, government apparatus, universities and various non-governmental organizations that work and/or care about women and children victims of Violence;

c. provides public facilities that are comfortable and safe for women;

Part Three
Effectiveness of Services for Women Victims of Acts of Violence

Paragraph 1
General

Article 18

(1) Protection of women victims of acts of Violence is carried out through integrated services.

(2) Provision of integrated services to women victims of acts of Violence as referred to in paragraph (1) uses the principles of:

a. gender responsive;

b. non-discrimination;

c. equal and respectful relationships;

d. maintaining confidentiality;

e. providing a sense of security and comfort;

f. respecting individual differences;

g. non-judgmental;

h. respecting the victim's choices and decisions;

i. sensitive to the victim's background and condition and use of language that is appropriate and understood by the victim;

j. fast and simple;

k. empathy; and

l. fulfillment of women's rights.

Article 19

Forms of integrated services for women victims of acts of Violence include:

a. complaint handling;

b. health rehabilitation;

c. social rehabilitation;

d. law enforcement and legal aid; and

e. repatriation and social reintegration.

Paragraph 2
Complaint Handling

Article 20

Integrated complaint handling services as referred to in Article 19 letter a are carried out by:

a. hospitals;

b. community health centers;

c. women and child service units;

d. trauma center protection houses;

e. women crisis centres;

f. marriage counseling, guidance and preservation advisory boards;

g. police;

h. prosecutor's office;

i. courts;

j. ministry of religious affairs; and/or

k. legal aid institutions;

Article 21

(1) Complaints of acts of Violence against women can be made by reporters consisting of:

a. victims,

b. family,

c. community; and/or

d. community organizations in the field of Protection of women and Children, such as: P2TP2A, Safe Houses etc.)

(2) Reporters as referred to in paragraph (1) may file complaints:

a. directly;

b. by telephone; and/or

c. by letter.

(3) In addition to handling complaints submitted by reporters, officers at service institutions as referred to in Article 20, are also authorized to handle victims of acts of Violence against women obtained through other information.

(4) Further provisions regarding procedures for complaint handling services as referred to in paragraphs (1) and (2) are regulated by Regent's Regulation.

Paragraph 3
Health Rehabilitation

Article 22

(1) Health rehabilitation as referred to in Article 19 letter b is an effort that includes aspects of:

a. promotive;

b. preventive;

c. curative; and

d. rehabilitative.

(2) Health rehabilitation as referred to in paragraph (1) is carried out comprehensively, can be accessed 24 (twenty-four) hours, is of quality and is carried out in accordance with health service standards.

(3) Women victims of acts of Violence have the right to free health services, including:

a. medical services;

b. medicolegal services;

c. psychosocial counseling; and

d. visum.

(4) Further provisions regarding procedures for implementing health services as referred to in paragraph (3) are regulated by Regent's Regulation.

Paragraph 4
Social Rehabilitation

Article 23

(1) Social rehabilitation as referred to in Article 19 letter c is aimed at restoring and developing the ability of women victims of acts of Violence who experience social dysfunction to be able to carry out their social functions in society normally.

(2) Social rehabilitation as referred to in paragraph (1) includes services:

a. counseling;

b. provision of safe houses; and

c. spiritual guidance.

(3) Implementation of services as referred to in paragraph (2) is carried out by:

a. SKPD that has duties and functions in the social field;

b. SKPD that handles Women's Empowerment, and may cooperate with integrated service centers, women crisis centres or existing community organizations in the social field; or

c. ministry of religious affairs.

Article 24

(1) Counseling services as referred to in Article 23 paragraph (2) letter a aim to:

a. help women victims of acts of Violence recognize their problems and find effective ways to overcome them themselves;

b. empower women victims of acts of Violence to determine their future;

c. provide moral support to women victims of acts of Violence in facing the process they are going through; and

d. make women victims of acts of Violence feel accepted in the community environment.

(2) Counseling services are provided by gender-responsive social rehabilitation officers, consisting of:

a. counselors;

b. psychologists; and

c. psychiatrists.

(3) Counseling services are provided in a place that guarantees a sense of security, comfort and confidentiality of information from women victims of acts of Violence.

(4) Stages of counseling services include:

a. initial counseling;

b. follow-up counseling;

c. case discussion; and

d. site visits.

Article 25

(1) The Regional Government may provide safe houses for women victims of acts of Violence.

(2) Provision of safe house services as referred to in Article 23 paragraph (2) letter b aims to protect women victims of acts of Violence from intimidation by perpetrators or other parties.

(3) The location of the safe house as referred to in paragraph (1) must be kept confidential.

(4) Criteria for women victims of acts of Violence who need safe houses include:

a. life threatened;

b. receiving rejection from family or community;

c. requiring intensive services but their residence is relatively far away; and/or

d. abandoned if not placed in a safe house.

(5) Implementation of safe house services is carried out by social rehabilitation officers who have gender sensitivity or gender awareness, consisting of:

a. social workers/volunteers;

b. security officers; and/or

c. community leaders.

(6) Further provisions regarding the implementation of safe house services are regulated by Regent's Regulation.

Article 26

(1) Spiritual guidance as referred to in Article 23 paragraph (2) letter c aims for psychological recovery.

(2) Spiritual guidance as referred to in paragraph (1) is carried out based on the principles of:

a. not imposing any religion or belief; and

b. leaving the final decision in the hands of the victim (self determination).

(3) Spiritual guidance is provided by spiritual guidance officers.

(4) Spiritual guidance officers as referred to in paragraph (3) consist of consultants from the Marriage Advisory, Guidance and Preservation Board (BP4), Religious Affairs Office Officers, Ustad/Ulama, Pastors, Ministers and other religious leaders who have gender sensitivity or gender awareness.

Paragraph 5
Law Enforcement and Legal Aid

Article 27

(1) Law enforcement and legal aid services as referred to in Article 19 letter d are actions by law enforcement apparatus to implement statutory regulations in handling cases of acts of Violence against women, especially to provide sanctions against perpetrators and provide Protection for witnesses and/or victims.

(2) Law enforcement and legal aid services as referred to in paragraph (1) are carried out by prioritizing the best interests of the victim.

Article 28

(1) Law enforcement and legal aid services are aimed at providing assistance and Protection to women victims of acts of Violence and witnesses with a gender-responsive approach so that victims obtain justice and legal certainty.

(2) Types of legal aid services include:

a. legal aid in the reporting process and preparation of examination reports;

b. legal aid in the prosecution process at the prosecutor's office;

c. legal aid in the examination process in court;

d. legal aid in the court decision process until there is a final legal decision; and

e. legal aid in the mediation process.

(3) The Regional Government provides legal aid to women victims of acts of Violence based on statutory provisions.

(4) In providing legal aid as referred to in paragraph (3), the Regional Government may cooperate with legal aid institutions, women crisis centers, advocates and/or other gender-sensitive legal assistants.

Paragraph 6
Repatriation and Social Reintegration

Article 29

(1) Repatriation is aimed at returning women victims of acts of Violence to their Region of origin.

(2) Repatriation of women victims of acts of Violence is accompanied by social reintegration efforts.

Article 30

(1) Social reintegration is carried out in order to reunite women victims of acts of Violence with family, substitute family, or community that can provide Protection and fulfillment of needs for victims.

(2) Social reintegration is aimed so that:

a. women victims of acts of Violence can continue their social life; and

b. women victims of acts of Violence can have a decent life.

(3) Social reintegration as referred to in paragraph (1) is carried out after the victim receives social rehabilitation services.

Article 31

The Regional Government carries out repatriation and social reintegration of women victims of acts of Violence in accordance with statutory regulations.

Part Four
Employment Protection

Article 32

(1) Employers who employ women must maintain decency, safety and security while at work.

(2) Employers must place women workers in places that are safe for women's reproductive health.

(3) Employers must fulfill the rights of women workers to receive wages in accordance with statutory provisions.

(4) Employers must provide special breastfeeding facilities in their companies.

Article 33

(1) Women workers/laborers under 18 (eighteen) years of age are prohibited from being employed between 23.00 WIB to 07.00 WIB.

(2) Employers are prohibited from employing pregnant women workers/laborers who according to a doctor's statement are dangerous to the health and safety of their pregnancy.

(3) Employers who employ women workers/laborers between 23.00 WIB to 07.00 WIB, must:

a. provide nutritious food and drinks; and

b. maintain decency and security while at work.

(4) Employers must provide shuttle transportation for women workers/laborers who leave and return from work between 23.00 WIB to 05.00 WIB.

CHAPTER V
IMPROVEMENT OF FAMILY QUALITY

Article 34

(1) In order to improve family quality, the Regional Government is responsible for:

a. making efforts to improve family quality to realize gender equality and Children's rights;

b. providing services for families in realizing gender equality and Children's rights.

c. strengthening and developing institutions providing family quality improvement services in the Region; and

(2) The responsibility of the Regional Government in efforts to improve family quality as referred to in paragraph (1) is carried out by SKPD in accordance with their duties and functions.

Article 35

The Regional Government makes efforts to improve family quality as referred to in Article 34 paragraph (1) letter a including:

a. guidance;

b. counseling;

c. supervision;

d. facilitation of family development implementation;

e. improving the health quality of pregnant and breastfeeding mothers;

Article 36

Guidance as referred to in Article 35 letter a, includes:

a. growth and development of toddlers;

b. Protection of adolescent reproductive health;

c. Maturation of marriage age;

d. Birth regulation; and

e. Population management.

Article 37

Counseling as referred to in Article 35 letter b includes:

a. conducting outreach to improve women's quality of life;

b. conducting premarital counseling;

Article 38.

Supervision as referred to in Article 35 letter c includes:

a. Evaluating toddler growth and development data;

b. Evaluating post-marriage data;

Article 39

Provision of services for families as referred to in Article 34 paragraph (1) letter b includes:

a. communication, information and education;

b. contraceptive tools and drugs;

c. recording and reporting of family planning services;

d. adequate health service facilities and infrastructure;

e. breastfeeding corner rooms in every public facility.

Article 40

Communication, information and education as referred to in Article 39 letter a is a communication activity to increase knowledge and improve the attitudes and behavior of families and communities in Population and family planning Programs.

Article 41

(1) In addition to the Regional Government, private parties (private parties include companies that provide public services) must provide breastfeeding corner rooms.

(2) Breastfeeding corner rooms as referred to in paragraph (1) are proper and adequate rooms.

(3) Private parties that do not provide breastfeeding corner rooms as referred to in paragraph (2) are subject to administrative sanctions in the form of:

a. written warning;

b. limitation of business activities;

c. freezing of business activities;

d. administrative fines; and/or

e. revocation of permits.

CHAPTER VI
FULFILLMENT OF CHILDREN'S RIGHTS

Part One
General

Article 42

(1) The Regional Government in efforts to fulfill Children's rights is responsible for:

a. establishing and implementing policies and programs/activities for fulfillment of Children's rights;

b. respecting and guaranteeing the human rights of every Child without distinguishing ethnicity, religion, race, class, gender, ethnicity, culture and language, legal status of the Child, birth order of the Child, and physical and/or mental condition;

c. strengthening and developing institutions providing family quality improvement services in realizing Children's rights;

d. guaranteeing Protection, maintenance, and welfare of Children by taking into account the rights and obligations of parents, guardians, or other persons legally responsible for the Child;

e. providing facilities and infrastructure in implementing fulfillment of Children's rights;

f. guaranteeing the right of Children to express opinions in accordance with the age and level of intelligence of the Child;

g. conducting supervision and guidance in the context of implementing fulfillment of Children's rights;

h. cooperating with private parties and/or NGOs in implementing Child Protection;

(2) The responsibility of the Regional Government in efforts to Protect Children as referred to in paragraph (1) is carried out by SKPD in accordance with their duties and functions.

Part Two
Birth Registration

Article 43

(1) The Regional Government is obliged to organize birth registration of Children with the issuance of birth certificates.

(2) Birth certificates as referred to in paragraph (1) are organized free of charge.

Part Three
Education

Article 44

The Regional Government guarantees the implementation of a 9 (nine) year compulsory education program for Children aged 7 (seven) to 15 (fifteen) years.

Article 45

(1) Every education unit organizer must provide opportunities for Children to obtain education services without discrimination.

(2) Every education unit organizer is prohibited from expelling Children from educational institutions unless there is a guarantee for the continuity of the Child's education.

(3) In the event that a Child victim of acts of Violence experiences dropping out of school, the education unit organizer must coordinate with related agencies.

Article 46

(1) Every Child has the right to obtain education and teaching in the context of developing their personality and level of intelligence in accordance with their interests and talents.

(2) Every Child has the right to receive Protection in education units from sexual crimes and Violence committed by educators, education personnel, fellow students, and/or other parties.

Article 47

(1) Children with disabilities and Children with special needs are given equal opportunities to obtain formal, non-formal and informal education services, including inclusive education programs.

(2) The regional government provides teaching staff for inclusive education in every education unit.

Part Four
Health and Social

Article 48

(1) The Regional Government must organize comprehensive health services for Children, so that every Child obtains optimal health status from the womb.

(2) Comprehensive health services as referred to in paragraph (1) include:

a. promotive, preventive, curative and rehabilitative efforts for basic health services;

b. exemption from cost burdens for Children with malnutrition, Children with disabilities, Children with special needs, Children infected with HIV/AIDS, child workers, Children victims of narcotics, alcohol, psychotropic and other addictive substance abuse, Children who are victims of Violence, and Children victims of human trafficking; and

(3) The Regional Government, community, family and parents must ensure that Children born are free from diseases that threaten survival and/or cause disability.

Article 49

(1) Promotive efforts for basic health services as referred to in Article 46 paragraph (2) letter a are aimed at improving health status more optimally.

(2) Promotive efforts as referred to in paragraph (1) are carried out by providing socialization or outreach to the community regarding healthy lifestyles including:

a. the importance of exclusive breastfeeding for babies;

b. the importance of immunization for toddlers;

c. clean living patterns;

d. the importance of nutritious food;

e. dangers of drugs and smoking; and

(3) In addition to promotive efforts as referred to in paragraph (2), the Regional Government may carry out other activities in the context of health implementation.

Article 50

(1) Preventive efforts for basic health services as referred to in Article 46 paragraph (2) letter a are aimed at preventing the occurrence of disease or health problems.

(2) Efforts as referred to in paragraph (1) are carried out by:

a. requiring mothers to provide exclusive breastfeeding to babies up to 6 (six) months of age;

b. providing free immunization and vitamins to babies and toddlers;

c. providing nutritious food to toddlers from underprivileged families;

d. optimizing the role of posyandu for health services for mothers and toddlers;

e. conducting regular health examinations; and

f. providing sanitation facilities and clean water in every school.

Article 51

(1) Curative efforts for basic health services as referred to in Article 46 paragraph (2) letter a are aimed at preventing disease from becoming more severe through treatment.

(2) Treatment as referred to in paragraph (1) is carried out free of charge for Children from underprivileged families.

Article 52

(1) Rehabilitative efforts for basic health services as referred to in Article 46 paragraph (2) letter a are aimed at maintaining and restoring conditions after illness.

(2) Rehabilitative efforts as referred to in paragraph (1) are carried out through regular health examinations.

Article 53

(1) The Regional Government must organize Social Welfare for:

a. Children in conflict with the law;

b. Child victims of Violence who are exploited economically and sexually, neglected Children, Child victims of kidnapping and Child victims of human trafficking;

c. Children who do not have parents and are cared for by parties or families who are unable;

d. Child victims of natural disasters or social disasters; and

e. Children with disabilities;

(2) Social welfare as referred to in paragraph (1) may be in the form of providing services:

a. health;

b. education;

c. legal aid;

d. assistance;

e. social, mental and spiritual guidance;

f. social rehabilitation; and/or

g. reintegration.

Part Five
Employment

Article 54

The Regional Government conducts regular supervision of every person/entity that employs Children in efforts to Protect child workers.

Article 55

Every person/entity that employs Children must pay attention to the requirements:

a. at least 15 (fifteen) years of age;

b. obtain written consent from the parents/guardian of the child worker;

c. have a written work agreement between the employer and the parents/guardian of the child worker and receive validation from the authorized agency;

d. not employ Children at night;

e. working time of at most 3 (three) hours per day;

Article 56

In the event that Children are employed together with adult workers, the workplace for Children must be separated from the workplace for adult workers.

Article 57

(1) Every person/entity is prohibited from employing and involving Children in the worst forms of work.

(2) The worst forms of work referred to in paragraph (1) include:

a. all work in the form of slavery or the like;

b. all work that utilizes, provides, or offers Children for prostitution, pornography production, pornographic performances, or gambling;

c. all work that utilizes, provides, or involves Children for the production and trade of liquor, narcotics, psychotropics, and other addictive substances; and/or

d. all work that endangers the health, safety, or morals of Children.

Part Six
Facilitation of the Juvenile Justice System

Article 58

(1) Facilitation of the juvenile justice system includes criminal and civil justice.

(2) Facilitation of the criminal justice system as referred to in paragraph (1) includes:

a. provision of assistants or Child volunteers during examination by investigators;

b. provision of legal aid and psychologist services;

c. involving members/police investigators in efforts to prevent criminal acts committed by Children or Children as victims;

d. realizing a restorative justice process for Children in conflict with the law; and

e. rehabilitation and social reintegration services;

(3) Fulfillment of Children's rights in the civil justice system as referred to in paragraph (1) includes provision and provision of consultation and legal aid services during the judicial process.

(4) In facilitating the juvenile justice system as referred to in paragraphs (1) and (2), the authorized SKPD is the SKPD that handles Protection of women and Children

Part Seven
Children's Forum

Article 59

(1) In organizing fulfillment of Children's rights to express opinions and participate, the Regional Government facilitates the formation of a Children's Forum.

(2) The Children's Forum as referred to in paragraph (1) is a representation of Children in the Region, both representation of Children's geographical domicile, components of Children's socio-cultural groups and Children's educational background.

(3) In every formulation of policies related to Children, the Regional Government must pay attention to and accommodate the opinions of Children submitted through the Children's participation Forum.

(4) Further provisions regarding the formation of the Children's Forum as referred to in paragraphs (1) to (3) are established by Regent's Decree.

Part Eight
Child-Friendly Regency

Article 60

(1) In the context of fulfilling Children's rights in an integrated, systematic and sustainable manner, the Regional Government establishes a KLA Development policy.

(2) The KLA Development policy contains:

a. the KLA concept concerning objectives, strategies and roles of related parties;

b. Children's rights; and

c. KLA development approach.

Article 61

(1) The KLA development policy is directed at fulfilling Children's rights which are divided into 5 (five) groups, including:

a. civil rights and freedoms;

b. family environment and alternative care;

c. basic health and welfare;

d. education, use of leisure time, and cultural activities; and

e. special Protection.

(2) Further provisions regarding the KLA development policy as referred to in paragraph (1) are regulated by Regent's Regulation.

Article 62

(1) In order to effectively implement the KLA policy in the Region, a KLA Task Force is formed.

(2) The formation and membership composition of the KLA Task Force is established by Regent's Decree.

(3) The KLA Task Force is located at the Office of Women's Empowerment and Family Planning.

(4) The KLA Task Force as referred to in paragraph (1), has the main tasks of:

a. coordinating the implementation of policies and development of KLA;

b. conducting socialization, advocacy and communication information and education of KLA policies;

c. collecting basic data for KLA development;

d. conducting needs analysis sourced from basic data;

e. disseminating basic data;

f. determining the focus and priorities of programs in realizing KLA, which are adjusted to regional potential;

g. preparing a 5 (five) year Child-Friendly City regional action plan and work mechanisms; and

h. conducting monitoring, evaluation and reporting at least once (1) a year.

Article 63

In carrying out its duties, the KLA Task Force may coordinate and cooperate with PPT institutions and other institutions that provide Child Protection services.

CHAPTER VII
CHILD PROTECTION

Article 64

The Regional Government, in order to provide protection to Children:

a. implements and supports national policies in implementing Child Protection in the Region;

b. guarantees Children to use their rights in expressing opinions in accordance with the age and level of intelligence of the Child;

c. prevents Violence against Children by optimizing the role of customary institutions;

d. conducts socialization, seminars and outreach regarding Protection and rights of Children;

e. develops child-friendly school models;

f. provides public facilities that are friendly and safe for Children

g. encourages community awareness of the importance of Protection of Children;

Article 65

Service efforts carried out by the Regional Government for child victims of acts of Violence as referred to in Articles 42 to 64 apply mutatis mutandis to service efforts for Child victims of acts of Violence.

CHAPTER VIII
PARTICIPATION OF COMMUNITY, AND PARENTS AND/OR FAMILY

Part One
Community

Article 66

(1) Community participation in efforts to Protect women and Children:

a. participate in prevention and provide support for the development of prevention programs for violations of the rights of women and Children;

b. provide information and/or report violations of the rights of women and Children to the authorities; and

c. supervise the law enforcement process for cases of violations of the rights of women and Children.

(2) Ninik mamak, religious scholars and wise scholars as well as bundo kanduang are responsible for protecting and preventing violations of the rights of women and Children.

(3) Community participation as referred to in paragraphs (1) and (2) is based on statutory regulations and local wisdom values of the local community.

Article 67

(1) In order to prevent violations of women's rights, the community:

a. monitors various conditions occurring in their environment that can cause violations of women's rights;

b. increases awareness of violations of women's rights;

c. strengthens the role of community, religious organizations and family environment;

d. conducts supervision of the implementation of Women's Empowerment and Child Protection organized by the Regional Government with a mechanism for conveying aspirations to the Regent or regional people's representative council;

(2) In order to prevent violations of Children's rights, the community:

a. monitors various conditions occurring in their environment that cause violations of Children's rights;

b. increases awareness of violations of Children's rights;

c. strengthens the role of community, religious organizations and family environment; and

prevents early marriage.

Part Two
Parents and/or Family

Article 68

The family in efforts to Protect women's rights plays a role in:

a. protecting women from conflict and Violence in the household that degrades women's dignity;

b. providing equal opportunities for women to develop themselves in the fields of education, social, economic, political and cultural; and

c. preventing violations of women's rights in the household.

Article 69

(1) Parents in efforts to Protect Children's rights play a role in:

a. caring for, maintaining, educating, and protecting Children;

b. developing Children according to their abilities, talents, and interests;

c. providing formal and non-formal education;

d. providing character education and instilling moral values in Children;

e. guiding Children to behave in accordance with religion, ethics and morals as well as customs; and

f. preventing early marriage.

(2) In the event that parents do not exist, or their whereabouts are unknown, or for some reason, cannot carry out their obligations and responsibilities, then the obligations and responsibilities as referred to in paragraph (1) may transfer to the family.

CHAPTER IX
GUIDANCE AND SUPERVISION

Article 70

(1) The Regional Government must conduct guidance and supervision of the implementation of Protection of women and Child victims of Violence.

(2) Guidance as referred to in paragraph (1), includes:

a. guidelines and fulfillment standards;

b. technical guidance and training;

c. provision of facilities;

d. monitoring; and

e. evaluation.

(3) Guidance as referred to in paragraph (2) is in order to realize the objectives of Protection of women and Child victims of Violence according to minimum service standards.

Article 71

(1) Supervision of the implementation of Child Protection as regulated in this Regional Regulation is carried out by the Regional Government Supervisory Agency of Padang Pariaman Regency and P2TP2A;

(2) In carrying out supervision, P2TP2A is authorized to recommend cases to legal apparatus, government agencies and other community institutions as regulated in the Law

(3) Recommendations submitted by P2TP2A must be followed up by legal apparatus, government agencies, community institutions and other related individuals.

CHAPTER X
FINANCING

Article 72

Financing for the implementation of Women's Empowerment and Child Protection programs and activities is sourced from:

a. regional revenue and expenditure budget; and

b. other legitimate sources in accordance with statutory provisions.

CHAPTER XI
CLOSING PROVISIONS

Article 73

Implementing regulations of this Regional Regulation are established at the latest 6 (six) months from the promulgation of this Regional Regulation.

Article 74

This Regional Regulation comes into force on the date of promulgation, so that everyone knows it, orders the promulgation of this Regional Regulation by placing it in the Regional Gazette of Padang Pariaman Regency.
