REGENT OF BANTUL
REGIONAL REGULATION OF BANTUL REGENCY
NUMBER 15 YEAR 2013
CONCERNING
PROTECTION OF CHILDREN AND WOMEN VICTIMS OF VIOLENCE BY THE GRACE OF GOD ALMIGHTY
REGENT OF BANTUL,

Considering: a. that every child and woman has the right to be free from torture or treatment that degrades human dignity and has the right to obtain a sense of security, free from all forms of violence, discrimination and violations of the rights of women and children;

b. that acts of violence against children and women continue to increase, so comprehensive and maximum protection efforts are needed, concrete actions, formal institutional support, regulations that can guarantee implementation from the Regional Government in increasing broad community participation;

c. that based on the considerations as referred to in letter a, letter b, it is necessary to establish a Regional Regulation concerning Protection of Children and Women Victims of Violence;

CHAPTER I
GENERAL PROVISIONS

Article 1
In this Regional Regulation what is meant by:
1. Government is the Central Government.
2. Region is Bantul Regency.
3. Regional Government is the Regent and Regional Apparatus as elements of regional government administration.

4. Regional People's Representative Council hereinafter abbreviated as DPRD is the Regional People's Representative Council of Bantul Regency as an element of Regional Government administration.
5. Regent is the Regent of Bantul.
6. Regional Apparatus Work Unit hereinafter abbreviated as SKPD is the Regional Apparatus Work Unit of Bantul Regency.
7. Woman is a person who is female by sex and a person who is legally recognized as a woman.
8. Child is a person who is not yet 18 years old, including a child still in the womb.
9. Violence is any act that results in or can result in misery or suffering whether physical, sexual, economic, social, and psychological, threats of certain actions, coercion or arbitrary deprivation of freedom, whether occurring in public or in private life against victims.
10. Violence against women is any action that results in or may result in misery or suffering of women physically, sexually, economically, socially, psychologically, including threats of certain actions, coercion or deprivation of freedom, whether occurring in public or private life.
11. Violence against children is any action that results in or may result in suffering of children physically, psychologically, sexually, neglect, exploitation, economically, and other violence.
12. Physical Violence is any act that causes pain, injury, wounds or disability to a person's body, miscarriage, fainting and or causes death.
13. Psychological Violence is any act that causes fear, loss of self-confidence, loss of ability to act, feelings of helplessness and or severe psychological suffering to a person.
14. Sexual Violence is any act in the form of sexual harassment, forced sexual intercourse, forced unnatural or unwanted sexual intercourse, forced sexual intercourse with others for commercial purposes and or certain purposes.
15. Economic Violence is any act that neglects family members in the form of not providing livelihood, care or maintenance in a proper manner.
16. Exploitation of Children is any act involving children in activities that can harm the welfare and growth and development or endanger the safety of children with the aim of enabling others to obtain economic, sexual, social, or political benefits, including if it contains restrictions or elimination of opportunities for children to obtain their rights.
17. Sexual harassment is any action with sexual nuances conveyed through physical or non-physical contact that targets sexual body parts or a person's sexuality, including by using whistles, flirting, comments or statements with sexual nuances, displaying pornographic materials and sexual desires, pinching or touching body parts, movements or gestures that are sexual in nature resulting in feelings of discomfort, offense, feeling degraded in dignity, and possibly causing health and safety problems.
18. Victim is a Child and Woman who experiences misery and or suffering either directly or indirectly as a result of violence occurring in the area of Bantul Regency.

19. Prevention is a direct effort made by the Regional Government to prevent acts of violence against women and children.
20. Protection is all service actions to guarantee and protect the rights of victims of violence organized by the Integrated Service Center.
21. Protection of women is all activities aimed at providing a sense of security for women carried out by family members, police, prosecutors, courts, social institutions, or other parties who know or hear that violence against women will or has occurred.
22. Protection of children is all activities to guarantee and protect children and their rights so they can live, grow, develop and participate optimally in accordance with human dignity and receive protection from violence and discrimination.
23. Service is action taken as soon as possible to victims when seeing, hearing and knowing that violence against victims will, is, or has occurred.
24. Women's Empowerment is any effort to improve physical, mental, spiritual, social abilities, knowledge, and skills so that women are ready to be utilized according to their respective abilities.
25. Companion is a person or representative from an institution who has expertise in accompanying victims to conduct counseling, therapy and advocacy for strengthening and recovery of victims of violence.
26. Spiritual guidance is accompaniment in the form of services for spiritual strengthening so that sick people can rise to be closer to God so that a sense of calm, security arises, and mental health is achieved.
27. Judicial body is a general court that has the authority to receive, examine, and adjudicate, as well as resolve every case submitted to it, to realize law enforcement and justice.
28. Prosecution is the action of the public prosecutor to transfer a case to the competent District Court in the manner and according to the procedure regulated in the Law with a request to be examined and decided by a judge in a court hearing.
29. Lawsuit is notification accompanied by a request by the interested party to the judge to act according to law against someone who has harmed him in a civil manner.
30. Institution is an agency/office/body/office within the Regional Government and/or Non-Governmental Organization (NGO) that provides assistance to victims of violence.
31. Integrated Service Center hereinafter abbreviated as PPT is a functional work unit that organizes integrated services for victims of violence based in hospitals, and is managed jointly in the form of medical services (including medico-legal), psychosocial and legal services.
32. Regional Action Plan is the foundation and guideline for related agencies, vertical agencies, and the community, in order to carry out activities for the prevention and protection of children and women victims of violence.
33. Safe House is a shelter for victims, during the accompaniment process, for the safety and comfort of victims from threats and dangers of perpetrators in accordance with predetermined standards.

34. Social Reintegration is the process of preparing the community and victims that supports the reunification of victims into the family environment, family substitutes who can provide protection and fulfillment of victims' needs.
35. Standard Operating Procedure hereinafter abbreviated as SOP is the standard steps that must be taken in protecting victims starting from complaints/identification, rehabilitation, health, social rehabilitation, legal services up to repatriation and social reintegration of witnesses and/or victims.
36. Family is the smallest unit in society consisting of husband and wife, or husband-wife and their children, or father and his children, or mother and her children, or blood relatives in a straight line up or down to the third degree.
37. Household is husband, wife, and children, people who have family relationships due to blood relations, marriage, breastfeeding, foster care, and representation, and/or domestic workers and reside in that household.
38. Community is individuals, families, groups, social organizations and/or community organizations.
39. Regional Revenue and Expenditure Budget hereinafter abbreviated as APBD is the Regional Revenue and Expenditure Budget of Bantul Regency
40. Gender responsive budget is a budget that provides and accommodates justice and equality for women and men to access and obtain access, as well as benefits from development programs and participate in decision-making processes and have control over resources.
41. Violence Victim Protection Forum hereinafter abbreviated as FPKK is a coordination forum for handling victims of violence against Children and Women whose implementation is carried out in a networked manner.

Article 2
The principles of organizing protection of Children and Women from violence are:
a. respect and fulfillment of victims' rights;
b. justice and gender equality;
c. non-discrimination; and
d. empowerment that is best for victims.

Article 3
(1) The objectives of protecting Children and Women from violence are:
a. to prevent and eliminate all forms of violence and exploitation against children and women that occur in the family and community environment;
b. to seek recovery and social reintegration for children and women victims of violence;
c. to provide services to children and women victims of violence, reporters, and witnesses;
d. to empower women victims of violence; and
e. to increase community participation.

(2) The organization of protection of children and women from violence as referred to in paragraph (1), includes aspects of:
a. prevention;
b. service and accompaniment;
c. social reintegration; and
d. empowerment.

CHAPTER II
VICTIMS' RIGHTS

Article 4
Every victim of violence receives the following rights:
a. the right to have their dignity respected as a human being;
b. the right to health and psychological recovery from the suffering experienced by victims;
c. the right to determine their own decisions;
d. the right to obtain information;
e. the right to confidentiality of their identity;
f. the right to optimal service;
g. the right to compensation;
h. the right to continuous handling up to the social rehabilitation stage;
i. the right to complaint handling;
j. the right to obtain facilities in the judicial process;
k. the right to psychological and legal accompaniment; and
l. the right to legal protection and services.

Article 5
Child victims of violence in addition to receiving the rights as referred to in Article 4, also receive the following rights:
a. the right to respect for survival, growth and development;
b. the right to basic services in the field of education;
c. the right to basic services in the field of health;
d. the right to equal protection;
e. the right to be free from various stigmas; and
f. the right to obtain freedom.

CHAPTER III
OBLIGATIONS AND RESPONSIBILITIES

Article 6
Obligations and responsibilities in organizing protection for children and women victims of violence are:
a. Regional Government;
b. community;
c. family; and
d. parents.

Article 7
(1) To implement the provisions as referred to in Article 6 letter a, the Regional Government carries out victim protection efforts, in the form of:
a. formulating policies on the protection of Children and Women from all forms of violence and discrimination;
b. preparing program and activity planning;
c. providing infrastructure and facilities support;
d. allocating special gender-responsive budgets;
e. establishing PPT and/or Safe Houses;
f. establishing and facilitating FPKK; and
g. conducting monitoring and evaluation.

(2) In implementing the obligations of the Regional Government as referred to in paragraph (1), the Regional Government prepares a Regional Action Plan as a basis for SKPD in implementing protection for victims.
(3) The Regional Action Plan as referred to in paragraph (2), is part of the Regional Medium-Term Development Plan (RPJMD).
(4) Further provisions regarding the Regional Action Plan as referred to in paragraph (2), are further regulated by Regent Regulation.

Article 8
The Regional Government organizes services for victims in the form of:
a. facilitating the establishment of PPT;
b. facilitating PPT facilities and infrastructure according to capacity; and
c. facilitating FPKK as a forum for victim handling networks.

Article 9
The Regional Government is obliged and responsible to:
a. supervise the organization of services for victims according to minimum service standards; and
b. provide funds for victim protection through the APBD.

Article 10
In carrying out the obligations and responsibilities of the Regional Government as referred to in Article 7, Article 8, and Article 9, the Regent grants authority to SKPD that has duties and functions in the field of women's empowerment and child protection.

Article 11
The obligations and responsibilities of the community towards the protection of children and Women Victims of violence are:
a. preventing violence against children and women;
b. providing education on peace culture and gender equality;
c. providing information and/or reporting if violence occurs against children and women to law enforcement officials or authorized parties;
d. protecting victims; and

e. providing emergency assistance.

Article 12
The obligations of the family and/or parents as referred to in Article 6 letters c and d, legally have full responsibility to prevent all forms of violence and protect children and women as family members.

CHAPTER IV
ORGANIZATION OF PROTECTION

Part One
Institutions

Article 13
(1) In order to provide services and protection to victims of violence, the Regional Government establishes PPT.
(2) PPT as referred to in paragraph (1) has the task, principal, and function of providing protection to Children and Women from violence, consisting of fields.
(3) PPT as referred to in paragraph (2), is established by Regent Decree.
(4) PPT as referred to in paragraph (2) is tasked with providing services and temporary protection in the form of safe houses for victims of violence.
(5) In the event that PPT does not have a safe house (shelter) as referred to in paragraph (3), then victims of violence are referred to other institutions.

Article 14
(1) The organizational structure of PPT established by the Regent as referred to in Article 12 paragraph (1) consists of:
a. Chairman or other equivalent designation;
b. Secretary;
c. Treasurer; and
d. Executive Chairman who oversees the fields.

(2) The Fields as referred to in paragraph (1) letter d, at least include:
a. complaint service field;
b. health service field;
c. social rehabilitation service field;
d. repatriation and social reintegration field;
e. legal aid service field;
f. spiritual field; and
g. psychology field

Article 15
The complaint service field as referred to in Article 14 paragraph (2) letter a has the task of:
a. conducting interviews and observations of the victim's condition;
b. making recommendations for follow-up services;
c. coordinating and referring to follow-up services and related parties; and
d. administering the complaint process.

Article 16
The health service field as referred to in Article 14 paragraph (2) letter b has the task of:
a. conducting examinations, treatment, and follow-up care for victims;
b. coordinating the implementation of health rehabilitation and medico-legal;
c. conducting medico-legal examinations including collection of evidence on victims, making visum et repertum and visum et psychiatricum;
d. conducting supporting examinations and laboratory tests on evidence;
e. consulting with expert doctors or making referrals; and
f. making case reports.

Article 17
The social rehabilitation service field as referred to in Article 14 paragraph (2) letter c has the task of:
a. providing accompaniment during the case handling process;
b. conducting counseling; and
c. carrying out social functions.

Article 18
The repatriation and social reintegration field as referred to in Article 14 paragraph (2) letter d has the task of:
a. coordinating with related agencies for victim repatriation;
b. making progress reports on the repatriation and social rehabilitation accompaniment process; and
c. conducting monitoring for a maximum of 3 (three) months after the victim is returned to their family.

Article 19
The legal aid service field as referred to in Article 14 paragraph (2) letter e has the task of:
a. accompanying and defending every legal process; and
b. making progress reports on legal handling.

Article 20
The spiritual field as referred to in Article 14 paragraph (2) letter f has the task of:
a. providing spiritual guidance to victims;
b. coordinating with related parties; and

c. making reports and recommendations for follow-up services;

Article 21
The psychology field as referred to in Article 14 paragraph (2) letter g has the task of:
a. providing psychological guidance to victims;
b. making recommendations for follow-up services; and
c. coordinating and referring to follow-up services

Article 22
(1) PPT has at least 5 (five) counselors.
(2) Counselors as referred to in paragraph (1) include:
a. counselor in the legal field;
b. counselor in the health field;
c. counselor in the psychology field;
d. counselor in the social field; and
e. counselor in the spiritual field.

Article 23
The organizational structure of PPT established by the Regent involves related SKPD with community institutions adjusted to needs.

Article 24
Further provisions regarding the development, mechanisms and networked working relationships of PPT are further regulated by Regent Regulation.

CHAPTER V
FORMS AND MECHANISMS OF PROTECTION

Part One
Prevention

Article 25
(1) Efforts to prevent violence against Children and Women are carried out in an integrated manner by the Regional Government coordinated by the agency that has the main tasks and functions of coordination in the field of women's empowerment and child protection.
(2) Prevention efforts as referred to in paragraph (1) are implemented by:
a. forming work networks in efforts to prevent violence and handle acts of violence through coordination, integration, synchronization of violence prevention based on partnership patterns;
b. forming violence prevention systems, mapping locations or areas prone to violence;
c. organizing communication, information, and critical education about the rights of children and women for the community;
d. socializing laws and regulations related to the protection of children and women; and

e. prevention efforts as referred to in paragraph (1) are carried out in an integrated manner coordinated by SKPD in charge of women's empowerment and child protection.

Article 26
Prevention efforts are also carried out by:
a. community;
b. family; and
c. parents.

Part Two
Services

Article 27
(1) The organization of services for victims is carried out in an integrated manner by PPT.
(2) PPT as referred to in paragraph (1) can receive and send case referrals from or to other service units in a networked manner.

Article 28
The organization of services for victims is implemented with:
a. no fees charged;
b. fast;
c. safe and comfortable;
d. empathy;
e. non-discrimination;
f. integrated;
g. easily accessible; and
h. guaranteed confidentiality.

Article 29
Forms of services for victims include:
a. complaint, consultation, and counseling services;
b. accompaniment services;
c. health services;
d. social rehabilitation services;
e. legal services;
f. repatriation and social reintegration services; and
g. psychology services.

Article 30
Complaint, consultation, and counseling services as referred to in Article 29 letter a include:
a. initial identification or recording of victims; and
b. consent to take action.

Article 31
Accompaniment services as referred to in Article 29 letter b include:
a. accompanying victims during the examination and health recovery process;
b. accompanying victims during the medicolegal process;
c. accompanying victims during the examination process at the Police, Prosecutor's Office and court;
d. monitoring the interests and rights of victims in the examination process at the Police, Prosecutor's Office and Court;
e. maintaining the privacy and confidentiality of victims from all parties who are not interested, including coverage by the mass media;
f. coordinating with other companions; and
g. providing continuous handling up to the rehabilitation stage.

Article 32
Health services as referred to in Article 29 letter c include:
a. first aid to victims;
b. treatment and recovery of physical injuries aimed at restoring the physical condition of victims carried out by medical and paramedical personnel; and
c. referral to health services.

Article 33
Social rehabilitation services as referred to in Article 29 letter d are services provided by companions in order to restore the traumatic condition of victims, including providing safe houses to protect victims from various threats and intimidation for victims and providing social support so that victims have self-confidence, strength, and independence in solving their problems, by:
a. providing spiritual guidance to victims; and
b. psychological recovery of victims.

Article 34
Legal services as referred to in Article 29 letter e to assist victims in undergoing the judicial process by:
a. providing legal consultation that includes information regarding victims' rights and the judicial process;
b. accompanying victims at the investigation, prosecution, and examination levels in court hearings and helping victims to fully describe the violence they experienced; and
c. coordinating with fellow law enforcers, volunteer companions, and social workers so that the judicial process runs as it should.

Article 35
(1) Repatriation and social reintegration services as referred to in Article 29 letter f aim to return victims to their families and social environment.

(2) Repatriation and social reintegration services as referred to in paragraph (1) are carried out by the Regional Government in coordination with:
a. other Regional Governments; and
b. related agencies and institutions both government and non-government.

Article 36
Psychology services as referred to in Article 29 letter g aim to:
a. provide psychological accompaniment and services; and
b. provide psychological recovery of victims from trauma.

Part Three
Empowerment

Paragraph 1
Empowerment of Women Victims of Violence

Article 37
Forms of empowerment of women victims of violence include:
a. job training, including:
1. skills training;
2. field work practice; and
3. apprenticeship.
b. productive economic enterprises and joint business groups, including:
1. entrepreneurial skills training;
2. facilitation of joint business group formation; and
3. business implementation accompaniment.
c. capital assistance, including:
1. work facilities and infrastructure assistance; and
2. facilitation of working capital assistance.

Paragraph 2
Protection and Fulfillment of Children's Rights

Article 38
(1) Protection for children in conflict with the law is implemented through:
a. treatment of children in a humane manner in accordance with the dignity and rights of children;
b. provision of special child companion officers from an early stage;
c. provision of special facilities and infrastructure;
d. imposition of appropriate sanctions for the best interests of children;
e. continuous monitoring and recording of the development of children in conflict with the law.
f. provision of guarantees to maintain relationships with parents or family; and
g. protection from identity coverage through mass media and to avoid labeling.

(2) Forms of fulfillment of the rights of child victims of violence, include:
a. rehabilitation efforts both in institutions and outside institutions;
b. provision of safety guarantees for victim witnesses and expert witnesses both physically, mentally, and socially; and

c. provision of accessibility to obtain information regarding case development.
(3) PPT and the community are obliged to fulfill the rights of child victims of violence.

Article 39
(1) In an effort to guarantee the fulfillment of children's rights, the Regional Government cooperates with the Children's Forum that has been formed.
(2) The Children's Forum is an organization whose members are children who are administrators of children's organizations, studios or children's activity groups and the like which are generally based on talent development, interests, abilities and use of free time.
(3) The children's forum has the task of:
a. socializing about violence to peers;
b. being actively involved in planning policies, programs and activities related to child protection from violence;
c. being a source of information on violence incidents in their environment; and
d. consulting the needs and desires of child victims of violence to partners from related sectors.

Part Four
Coordination of Services and Protection of Victims

Article 40
(1) In an effort to provide and organize handling of services and protection for victims, PPT establishes FPKK.
(2) FPKK as referred to in paragraph (1) aims to:
a. coordinate and synchronize PPT service handling;
b. maintain and develop networks and referral systems; and
c. collect, compile and present violence reports.
(3) The management and membership of FPKK as referred to in paragraph (1) is established by Decree of the PPT Chairman.
(4) FPKK membership as referred to in paragraph (3) is grouped into the following roles:
a. health role;
b. psychology role;
c. legal role;
d. social role; and
e. economic role.
(5) Further provisions regarding the establishment, tasks, principles, and functions as well as FPKK membership as referred to in paragraph (1) are further regulated by Regent Regulation.

CHAPTER VI
COOPERATION AND PARTNERSHIP

Part One
Cooperation

Article 41
(1) In order to achieve the objectives of protecting children and women from violence as referred to in Article 3, the Regional Government cooperates with:
a. Government;
b. Provincial Government;
c. other Regencies/Cities; and
d. non-Government institutions.

(2) Cooperation as referred to in paragraph (1) includes:
a. exchange of data and information;
b. rehabilitation of victims of violence;
c. repatriation and social reintegration; and
d. provision of evidence and witnesses, and followed up in accordance with applicable law.
(3) Cooperation as referred to in paragraphs (1) and (2) is set forth in the form of a Joint Agreement.

Part Two
Partnership

Article 42
(1) The Regional Government establishes partnerships with the business world in protecting children and women from violence.
(2) Partnerships as referred to in paragraph (1) are carried out through:
a. job opportunity information systems for women victims of violence;
b. education and training for children and women victims of violence;
c. educational assistance for children and women victims of violence who are vulnerable to dropping out of school; and
d. fostering and increasing the economic independence of women victims of violence.
(3) Partnerships as referred to in paragraphs (1) and (2) are set forth in the form of a joint agreement.

CHAPTER VII
GUIDANCE AND SUPERVISION

Article 44
(1) The Regional Government conducts guidance and supervision of the implementation of PPT Minimum Service Standards.

(2) Guidance and Supervision as referred to in paragraph (1) include:
a. coordination;
b. guidance;
c. education and training; and
d. monitoring and evaluation.
(3) Coordination as referred to in paragraph (2) letter a covers aspects related to planning and implementation.
(4) Guidance as referred to in paragraph (2) letter b covers aspects related to planning, implementation, procedures, financing, quality, control and supervision.
(5) Education and training as referred to in paragraph (2) letter c is carried out by SKPD in charge of women's empowerment and child protection.
(6) Monitoring and evaluation as referred to in paragraph (2) letter d is carried out periodically.

CHAPTER VIII
REPORTING

Article 43
(1) PPT and FPKK make reports on the protection of Children and Women victims of violence to the Regent.
(2) Reporting as referred to in paragraph (1) is submitted in writing, including:
a. administration;
b. finance;
c. services; and
d. performance.
(3) Submission of written reports as referred to in paragraph (2), is carried out at least every 3 (three) months and is organized in accordance with the provisions of laws and regulations.

CHAPTER IX
FUNDING

Article 45
(1) Funding is carried out by the Regional Government for protection activities for victims due to violence charged to the APBD.
(2) Management of funding allocation as referred to in paragraph (1) is adjusted to the needs of PPT by coordinating between SKPDs.

CHAPTER X
COMMUNITY PARTICIPATION

Article 46
(1) The community participates in organizing protection for Victims of violence.
(2) Community participation as referred to in paragraph (1) is in the form of:
a. forming family partners at the village/sub-district level by the community;
b. forming Child and Women protection units within community organizations;
c. conducting socialization of Child and Women's rights independently;
d. providing first aid to victims; and
e. reporting to authorized agencies if violence against victims occurs in their environment.
(3) Community participation as referred to in paragraph (1) is carried out by individuals, social community institutions, non-governmental organizations, educational institutions, religious institutions, private sector, and mass media.

CHAPTER XI
CLOSING PROVISIONS

Article 47
This Regional Regulation comes into force on the date of promulgation.

In order that everyone may know, orders the promulgation of this Regional Regulation by placing it in the Regional Gazette of Bantul Regency.

I. GENERAL
One of the objectives of the formation of the state is to provide protection to every citizen with a sense of security and comfort, this is explicitly stated in the Preamble to the 1945 Constitution of the Republic of Indonesia Paragraph IV. It is stated that the national goal of the Indonesian nation is to protect all Indonesian people and all Indonesian bloodshed, advance general welfare, educate the nation's life and participate in implementing world order based on independence, eternal peace and social justice.

To achieve this, sustainable development efforts have been carried out which are a series of comprehensive, directed and integrated development, including development in promoting gender equality and women's empowerment. This is as affirmed in the Millennium Development Goals (MDGs). The implementation of gender equality and women's empowerment has become a priority that is always sought for improvement from year to year, especially since 2000, when Presidential Instruction Number 9 Year 2000 concerning Gender Mainstreaming (PUG) was issued at the urging of the State Minister for Women's Empowerment (Meneg PP) at that time.

This Presidential Instruction affirms that all Ministries and non-Ministerial government institutions in the National Government, Provinces, and Regencies/Cities must carry out gender mainstreaming in planning, implementation, monitoring, and evaluation of development policies and programs. This is a manifestation of efforts to fulfill the human rights of every Indonesian citizen, also intended so that gender equality and women's empowerment in Indonesia are getting better.

Indonesia as a country that has ratified the International Convention, namely the Women's Convention; Convention of Elimination and Discrimination Against Women (CEDAW) in the form of Law Number 7 Year 1984 concerning Ratification of the Convention on the Elimination of All Forms of Discrimination Against Women, has thereby bound itself to guarantee through laws and regulations, policies, programs and temporary special actions (affirmative action), to realize equality and justice between women and men, as well as the elimination of all forms of discrimination against women, including acts of violence against women and children.

It is a breath of fresh air for Indonesian women, when clearly explained in the Women's Convention which establishes principles and provisions to eliminate gaps, subordination and actions that violate women's rights and harm the position of women in law, family and society. These principles are clearly contained in the substantive articles of the Women's Convention (articles 1-16).

Through Law Number 23 Year 2002 concerning Child Protection, the Government recognizes the existence of children's rights. Human rights inherent in children, include basic rights as humans, namely the Right to Life, Right to Growth and Development, Right to Protection and Right to Participation.

To fulfill the Right to life, children need food in sufficient quantities, healthy and nutritious, as well as optimal health access. Protection from violence that threatens their safety and health must be provided from an early age. Protection is needed to prevent physical, mental and sexual violence and exploitation. To fulfill the right to growth and development, children need space to play, exercise, education that is appropriate to their physical and psychological development. In fulfilling children's rights, every government administrator, community and parents must understand and care about children's rights. The existence of legal instruments and law enforcement officials who defend children's interests is needed for this protection effort. Laws and regulations that are principled to defend the best interests of children are needed to realize protection that is legal in nature. Furthermore, it is not uncommon to find cases of women and children who experience problems of physical, psychological, sexual and social violence. For example, women and children victims of abuse, neglect, eviction and mistreatment by parents or others. For women and children who experience these problems, the government must provide Safe Houses or Integrated Service Centers (PPT) for women and children victims of violence. Safe Houses or PPT are safe and friendly places for women and children. The PPT is equipped with companions from educators, doctors, psychologists, clerics/spiritual leaders and networked with lawyers for women and children as well as RPK Units at the Police.

The fate of women and children victims of violence must be considered by the government. Many cases of violence against women and children are often caused by factors that develop in society, for example low levels of economy, education, environments in the industrial sector. Therefore, victims of violence like this need to receive protection in accordance with the principles of justice, truth, legal certainty, equality and upholding human rights.

For empowerment for victims of violence, regional governments can establish Integrated Service Centers and Violence Victim Protection Forums (FPKK) as centers for empowerment of women victims of violence that specifically have the main tasks and functions for empowerment of children and women victims of violence.

From the above framework, the Government is responsible for taking actions both legally, politically, economically and socially to prevent, suppress, reduce, and eliminate all forms of violence against women and children. From these various efforts, a legal framework will be realized in organizing protection for children and women from violence.

What is meant by the "prevention" aspect is a protection strategy effort through:
a. Primary prevention, all people, families, communities and the state in efforts to increase knowledge, understanding and maintain so that violence against women and children does not occur, including socialization of policies, adequate services, supportive workplace policies, as well as life skills training for women and children. What is meant by life skills training includes conflict resolution without violence, stress management skills, resource management, effective decision making, effective interpersonal communication, guidance on psychosocial development of women and children.
