REGIONAL REGULATION OF PANGANDARAN REGENCY
NUMBER YEAR 2019
CONCERNING
EMPOWERMENT AND PROTECTION OF WOMEN
BY THE GRACE OF ALMIGHTY GOD
REGENT OF PANGANDARAN,

Considering: a. that in order to improve the position, role and quality of women is an effort to realize gender equality and justice in family, community, national and state life;

b. that all forms of violence against women constitute violations of human rights so that women need to be protected in their dignity and honor and guaranteed their right to life in accordance with their nature and destiny without discrimination and empowered so that they can actualize their potential optimally;

c. that to protect the community and realize equity and justice, as an obligation of the regional government to provide protection and welfare for women, a legal foundation is needed in the implementation of empowerment and protection of women;

d. that based on the considerations as referred to in letter a, letter b and letter c, it is necessary to establish a Regional Regulation on Empowerment and Protection of Women.

In View of: 1. Article 18 paragraph (6) of the 1945 Constitution of the Republic of Indonesia;

2. Law Number 7 of 1984 concerning Ratification of the Convention on the Elimination of All Forms of Discrimination Against Women (State Gazette of the Republic of Indonesia Year 1984 Number 29, Supplement to the State Gazette of the Republic of Indonesia Number 3277);

CHAPTER I
GENERAL PROVISIONS

Article 1

In this Regional Regulation what is meant by:
1. Regency Area is Pangandaran Regency.
2. Regent is the Regent of Pangandaran.
3. Regency Regional Government is the Regent as an element of regional government administration who leads the implementation of government affairs that are the authority of the autonomous region.
4. Regional Apparatus is an assistant element to the Regent and Regional House of Representatives in the administration of government affairs that are the authority of the Region.
5. Women are adult humans of female gender and persons recognized by law as Women.
6. Prevention is activities carried out to prevent the occurrence of acts of violence against Women.
7. Women's Empowerment is every effort to improve physical, mental spiritual, social abilities, knowledge and skills so that Women are ready to be utilized according to their respective abilities.
8. Protection is all efforts aimed at providing a sense of security to victims carried out by family, advocates, social institutions, police, prosecutors, courts, or other parties either temporarily.
9. Women's Protection is all activities aimed at providing a sense of security carried out by the Regional Government, community, and/or other parties who know or hear that violence against Women will or has occurred.
10. Victim is a Woman who experiences suffering and/or hardship either directly or indirectly as a result of violence and life vulnerability.
11. Violence is any act that results in or can result in suffering or hardship both physical, sexual, economic, social and psychological to the victim.
12. Victim of Violence is an individual or group of people who experience physical, mental or emotional suffering, economic loss, or experience neglect, reduction or deprivation of their basic rights, as a result of human rights violations or criminal acts as well as acts of Violence committed either by state apparatus, Regional Government apparatus, or individuals.
13. Violence Against Women is any action that results in or may result in suffering or hardship to Women physically, sexually, economically, socially, psychologically including threats of certain actions, coercion or deprivation of freedom, whether occurring in public or private life.
14. Physical Violence is any act that causes pain, injury, wounds or defects to a person's body, miscarriage or fainting and/or causes death.
15. Psychological Violence is an act that results in fear, loss of self-confidence, loss of ability to act, distrust or severe psychological suffering to a person.
16. Sexual Violence is any act in the form of sexual harassment, forced sexual relations, either unnaturally or unwanted with others for commercial purposes and/or certain purposes.
17. Domestic Neglect is someone who neglects people within their household scope, whereas according to the law applicable to them or because of an agreement or contract they are obliged to provide life, care, or maintenance to that person. In addition, neglect also applies to everyone who causes economic dependence by limiting and/or prohibiting proper work inside or outside the home so that the victim is under the control of that person.
18. Human Trafficking is the act of recruiting, transporting, harboring, sending, transferring or receiving a person by threat of violence, use of violence, kidnapping, confinement, forgery, fraud, abuse of power or vulnerable position, debt bondage or giving payment or benefits, so as to obtain consent from the person holding control over that other person, whether carried out within the country or between countries, for the purpose of exploitation or resulting in the person being exploited.
19. Exploitation is an act that exploits economically or sexually with the intention of benefiting oneself or others, acts with or without the victim's consent which include but are not limited to prostitution, forced labor or service, slavery or similar practices, oppression, extortion, utilization of physical, sexual, reproductive organs, or unlawfully transferring or transplanting organs and/or body tissues or utilizing the labor or ability of a person by another party to obtain material or immaterial benefits and/or all forms of utilization of sexual organs or other body organs of the victim to obtain benefits, including but not limited to all prostitution or indecency activities.

20. Domestic Violence is any act against a Woman that results in suffering or hardship physically, sexually, psychologically and/or Domestic Neglect including threats to commit acts, coercion or deprivation of freedom unlawfully within the household environment.
21. Family is a person who has a blood relationship in a straight line upward or downward and collateral line up to the third degree, or who has a marital relationship, or a person who is a dependent of the Woman.
22. Parents are biological father and/or mother, or stepfather and/or stepmother, or adoptive father and/or adoptive mother.
23. Discrimination Against Women is any distinction, exclusion or restriction made on the basis of sex which has the effect or purpose of reducing or eliminating the recognition, enjoyment or exercise of human rights and fundamental freedoms in the political, economic, social, cultural, civil or any other field by Women, regardless of their marital status on the basis of equality between men and Women.
24. All Forms of Discrimination Against Women are all forms of discrimination that include territorial dimensions, age dimensions, and special dimensions.
25. Gender is a concept that refers to the differentiation of roles and responsibilities of men and Women that occur as a result of and can be changed by social and cultural conditions of society.
26. Gender Equality is the equality of conditions for men and Women to obtain opportunities and rights as human beings so that they are able to play a role and participate in political, economic, socio-cultural, defense and security activities, and equality in enjoying the results of development.
27. Gender Justice is a process to be fair to men and Women.
28. Health Rehabilitation is an effort to restore the Victim's condition including physical, psychological, and sexual health so that the Victim can carry out their role again properly and naturally both in the family and in society.
29. Social Rehabilitation is a service aimed at restoring and developing the ability of a person experiencing social dysfunction so that they can carry out their social functions properly.
30. Repatriation is an effort to return Victims of Violence to their area of origin.
31. Social Reintegration is an effort to reunite Victims with family, substitute family, or community that can provide Protection and fulfillment of needs for Victims.
32. Integrated Service Center for Women and Children Empowerment hereinafter referred to as P2TP2A is an integrated service unit established by the Regional Government to carry out the functions and main tasks of integrated services for Women and child victims of violence, including complaint services, Health Rehabilitation, Social Rehabilitation, Repatriation, Social Reintegration, and legal assistance.
33. Minimum Service Standards hereinafter abbreviated as SPM is a performance benchmark for integrated service units in providing services for handling reports, complaints, health services, Social Rehabilitation, law enforcement and legal assistance, as well as Repatriation and Social Reintegration for Women Victims of Violence.

CHAPTER II
WOMEN'S RIGHTS

Part One
General

Article 2

Every Woman has the right:
a. to life;
b. to have a family and continue offspring;
c. to self-development;
d. to obtain justice;
e. to personal freedom;
f. to a sense of security;
g. to welfare; and
h. to participate in government.

Part Two
Rights of Women with Disabilities

Article 3

Every Woman with disabilities has the right to obtain:
a. education at all units, paths, types and levels of education;
b. work and decent livelihood according to the type and degree of disability, education and ability;
c. equal treatment to play a role in development and enjoy its results;
d. accessibility in the context of their independence;
e. rehabilitation, social assistance and maintenance of social welfare standards; and
f. equal rights to develop talents, abilities and social life.

Part Three
Rights of Women Victims of Violence

Article 4

Every woman victim of violence has the right:
a. to be respected for her dignity as a human being;
b. to health rehabilitation;
c. to determine her own decisions;
d. to obtain information;
e. to confidentiality;
f. to compensation;
g. to social rehabilitation;
h. to complaint handling;
i. to obtain facilities in the judicial process; and
j. to assistance.

CHAPTER III
WOMEN'S EMPOWERMENT

Article 5

Women's empowerment is directed to obtain opportunities and rights as human beings so that they are able to play a role and participate in the fields of:
a. economy;
b. socio-culture;
c. politics and government;
d. law;
e. education;
f. health;
g. employment; and
h. social security.

Part One
Economic Field

Article 6

Women's empowerment in the economic field is implemented through:
a. provision of skills and job training;
b. facilitation of the formation of productive economic business groups;
c. facilitation of strengthening and development of productive economic business groups;
d. facilitation and capital assistance; and
e. facilitation of marketing network development.

Part Two
Socio-Cultural Field

Article 7

Women's empowerment in the socio-cultural field is implemented through:
a. increasing knowledge, attitudes and skills to encourage the fulfillment of tiered education according to potential to improve social status;
b. increasing awareness, willingness and ability to overcome health problems through quality promotive, preventive, curative, and rehabilitative efforts, especially in the field of reproductive health;
c. increasing awareness and knowledge about independent, healthy and prosperous family planning including access to consultation services and marriage registration; and
d. facilitation and efforts to preserve customs and develop science, technology, arts and culture for the advancement of women.

Part Three
Politics and Government Field

Article 8

(1) Women's empowerment in the field of politics and government includes:
a. involvement of women in decision-making at various levels;
b. providing opportunities for women to hold public office;
c. participation in general elections; and
d. self-development through organizations to associate, assemble and express opinions.
(2) Women's empowerment in the field of politics and government as referred to in paragraph (1) is implemented in accordance with statutory regulations.
(3) The Regional Government provides guarantees to women to have the right to vote and/or be elected in general elections, regional head elections, village head elections and/or other political office elections based on equal rights through direct, general, free, secret, honest and fair voting according to statutory regulations.
(4) The Regional Government provides opportunities for women to be appointed as regional government officials and occupy strategic positions in regional government; and
(5) The Regional Government and/or Political Parties are responsible for providing political education for women.

Part Four
Legal Field

Article 9

(1) Women's empowerment in the legal field includes:
a. increasing awareness and knowledge in the legal field through communication, information and education services; and
b. facilitation of access and legal consultation services.
(2) Women's empowerment in the legal field as referred to in paragraph (1) is implemented in accordance with statutory regulations.

Part Five
Education Field

Article 10

Women's empowerment in the education field through:
a. implementation of formal and non-formal education for women in the Region.
b. non-formal education aimed at improving women's abilities.
c. non-formal education as referred to in letter b, includes women's empowerment education, skills education and job training.
d. formal and non-formal education as referred to in letter a, is regulated by a separate Regional Regulation.

Part Six
Health Field

Article 11

(1) Women have the right to obtain health services to undergo reproductive life and sexual life that is healthy, safe, and free from coercion and/or violence.
(2) Health services as referred to in paragraph (1) include:
a. health services before pregnancy, childbirth and after giving birth;
b. pregnancy regulation, contraceptive services and sexual health; and
c. reproductive health services.
d. health services implemented through promotive, preventive, curative and rehabilitative approaches.

Part Seven
Employment Field

Article 12

(1) Women have equal opportunities without discrimination to obtain employment.
(2) Employers who use female workers must provide protection that includes welfare, safety and health.

Part Eight
Social Security Field

Article 13

(1) The Regional Government is responsible for providing guidance facilities for women with social problems.
(2) The Regional Government provides the right to social security for women with social problems.
(3) The responsibility for providing guidance facilities for women with social problems is under the regional apparatus organization that handles women's empowerment affairs.

CHAPTER IV
WOMEN'S PROTECTION

Part One
General

Article 14

Implementation of women's protection is given to:
a. female workers/laborers;
b. elderly women;
c. women with disabilities;
d. homeless women;
e. domestic workers;
f. women as family economic supporters;
g. women former inmates of correctional institutions;
h. women disaster victims;
i. commercial sex workers; and
j. women victims of violence.

Part One
Female Workers/Laborers

Article 15

(1) Implementation of protection for female workers/laborers to provide protection from acts of economic exploitation and cruel, inhumane treatment and to seek proper appreciation for work.
(2) Female workers/laborers as referred to in paragraph (1) receive protection for:
a. recognition of rights, wages and decent working conditions;
b. health insurance and employment social security;
c. access to information and legal consultation services;
d. opportunities to obtain knowledge and skills to improve status.

Part Two
Elderly Women

Article 16

(1) Implementation of protection for elderly women in order to extend life expectancy so that they can carry out their social functions and play an active role naturally in their social life.
(2) Elderly women as referred to in paragraph (1) receive the right to Protection for:
a. guarantee of continuity and personal self-development;
b. participation in the fields of education, health, work, politics, arts and culture as well as utilization of information and communication technology;
c. accessibility to information, legal assistance, mobility, social services; and
d. early intervention efforts including treatment and rehabilitation to improve their function and capacity.

Part Three
Women with Disabilities

Article 17

(1) Implementation of Protection for women with disabilities in order to provide protection and prevention against forms of discrimination including freedom from cruel, inhumane treatment, degrading human dignity and freedom from exploitation;
(2) Women with disabilities as referred to in paragraph (1) receive the right to protection for:
a. guarantee of continuity and personal self-development;
b. participation in the fields of education, health, work, politics, sports, arts and culture as well as utilization of information and communication technology;
c. accessibility to information, legal assistance, mobility, social services; and
d. early intervention efforts including treatment and rehabilitation to improve their function and capacity.

Part Four
Homeless Women

Article 18

(1) Implementation of protection for homeless women in order to provide protection and prevention against forms of violence caused by the absence of shelter or permanent residence.
(2) Homeless women as referred to in paragraph (1) receive the right to protection for:
a. decent housing;
b. health and social security;
c. population administration registration;
d. opportunities to obtain skills to improve economic status; and
e. access to information and legal consultation services.

Part Five
Domestic Workers

Article 19

(1) Implementation of Protection for domestic workers in order to provide protection and prevention against forms of discrimination, cruel, inhumane treatment, degrading human dignity, and exploitation in employment relationships.
(2) Domestic workers as referred to in paragraph (1) receive protection for:
a. recognition of rights, wages, and decent working conditions;
b. health insurance and social security;
c. access to information and legal consultation services; and
d. opportunities to obtain knowledge and skills to improve economic status.

Part Six
Women as Family Economic Supporters

Article 20

(1) Implementation of protection for women as family economic supporters in order to provide protection and prevention against forms of discrimination, negative stigma and economic empowerment for women as family economic supporters.
(2) Women as family economic supporters as referred to in paragraph (1) receive the right to protection for:
a. discriminatory actions and efforts to weaken women's function as family economic supporters;
b. strengthening capacity and recognition as family economic supporters in their environment; and
c. accessibility to economic and financial institutions.

Part Seven
Women Former Inmates of Correctional Institutions

Article 21

(1) Implementation of Protection for women former inmates of Correctional Institutions in order to provide protection from obstacles to adjusting to community life, so that they can carry out life normally.
(2) Women former inmates of Correctional Institutions as referred to in paragraph (1) receive protection for:
a. rehabilitation and social reintegration; and
b. opportunities to obtain knowledge and skills to improve economic status.

Part Eight
Women Disaster Victims

Article 22

(1) Implementation of Protection for women disaster victims in order to provide protection during disasters and post-disaster.
(2) Women disaster victims as referred to in paragraph (1) receive protection for:
a. access to decent housing;
b. health services;
c. access to information and legal consultation services; and
d. post-disaster recovery.

Part Nine
Commercial Sex Workers

Article 23

(1) Implementation of Protection for commercial sex workers in order to provide health protection and improvement of life skills as capital to improve social status.
(2) Protection for commercial sex workers as referred to in paragraph (1) includes:
a. providing life skills;
b. facilitating reproductive health services; and
c. preventing human trafficking.

Part Ten
Women Victims of Violence

Article 24

(1) Forms of violence against Women include:
a. physical violence;
b. psychological violence;
c. sexual violence;
d. domestic neglect;
e. trafficking of women; and
f. exploitation.
(2) Implementation of protection for women victims of violence in order to provide legal protection and/or health recovery
(3) Protection for women victims of violence as referred to in paragraph (2) includes:
a. legal assistance;
b. assistance after the occurrence of acts of violence;
c. rehabilitation and social reintegration; and
d. health services.

CHAPTER V
OBLIGATIONS AND RESPONSIBILITIES

Part One
General

Article 25

Empowerment and protection of women is a joint obligation and responsibility between:
a. Regional Government;
b. community,
c. family; and
d. parents.

Part Two
Obligations and Responsibilities of Regional Government

Article 26

(1) The Regional Government is obliged and responsible for the empowerment and protection of women.
(2) Efforts for empowerment and protection of women as referred to in paragraph (1), include:
a. determination of policies, programs, and activities;
b. determination of implementation guidelines;
c. provision of services; and
d. coordination of policies, programs and activities.
(3) The obligations and responsibilities of the Regional Government as referred to in Article 25 letter a include:
a. implementing policies for the Protection of Women from all forms of discrimination and acts of violence;
b. establishing policies, programs, and activities for Women's Empowerment and Protection that have been determined;
c. collecting data and information about Women Victims of Violence and Discrimination as well as supporting statutory regulations;
d. conducting education on matters related to the Protection of Women Victims of Violence and Discrimination;
e. conducting socialization of statutory regulations related to the Protection of Women Victims of Violence and Discrimination;
f. conducting monitoring and evaluation of the implementation of Protection of Women Victims of Violence and Discrimination.
(4) To anticipate the occurrence of acts of Violence, the Regional Government must provide and organize services for Victims in the form of:
a. establishing and facilitating the implementation of integrated service institutions for Victims by involving community elements;
b. facilitating legal assistance and legal services according to the needs of Victims;
c. providing safe houses or alternative residences along with handling mechanisms, psychosocial and spiritual services;
d. conducting continuous handling up to the rehabilitation and social reintegration stage;
e. conducting monitoring and evaluation of the implementation of Victim protection; and
f. encouraging public awareness of the importance of Protection for Victims.
(5) In carrying out obligations as referred to in paragraph (3) and paragraph (4) the Regional Government must pay attention to the rights and obligations of Parents, guardians, husbands or other persons who are legally responsible for Victims.
(6) To carry out the obligations and responsibilities as referred to in paragraph (1) through paragraph (4) the Regional Government prepares a regional action plan for Protection of Women Victims of Violence.
(7) Provisions regarding the regional action plan for Protection of Women Victims of Violence as referred to in paragraph (6) are regulated by Regent Regulation.

Article 27

(1) The Regent integrates policies, programs and activities for women's empowerment and protection into regional development planning.
(2) Integration of programs and activities as referred to in paragraph (1) is incorporated into planning and budget documents which include:
a. regional long-term development plan;
b. regional medium-term development plan;
c. strategic plan of Regional Apparatus;
d. regional government work plan; and
e. work plan and budget of Regional Apparatus work units.
(3) The Regional Apparatus that handles Women facilitates the integration of policies, programs, and activities for Women's Protection into the preparation of planning and budget documents as referred to in paragraph (2).
(4) In carrying out activities as referred to in paragraph (3) the Regional Apparatus that handles Women coordinates with Regional Apparatus that have direct relevance to the field of Women.

Article 28

(1) The Regent implements policies, programs, and activities for Women's Protection to eliminate all forms of discrimination and acts of Violence Against Women.
(2) The Regional Apparatus handling Women implements policies, programs, and activities for Women's Protection that have been determined and their implementation is in cooperation and coordination with related Regional Apparatus, law enforcers, community institutions, universities, and the community.
(3) Implementation of policies, programs, and activities for Women's Protection as referred to in paragraph (2) in the form of:
a. policy analysis;
b. advocacy;
c. coordination and communication;
d. socialization;
e. education;
f. training;
g. facilitation;
h. provision of services; and
i. other forms.

Article 29

(1) The Regent in implementing policies, programs, and activities for women's protection as referred to in Article 28 makes efforts to:
a. coordinate implementation between Regional Apparatus;
b. cooperate with the central government, provincial government, and other regency/city governments in accordance with statutory provisions;
c. strengthen gender institutional capacity;
d. facilitate Women's Protection services;
e. provide Women's Protection services;
f. implement affirmative action for Women's Protection; and
g. prepare a Women's Protection data system including a Violence Against Women data system.
(2) Implementation of policies, programs, and activities as referred to in paragraph (1) is coordinated by the Vice Regent.

Part Three
Obligations and Responsibilities of Community, Family, and Parents

Article 30

(1) The obligations and responsibilities of the community as referred to in Article 25 letter b are organized in the form of community participation.
(2) Forms of community participation as referred to in paragraph (1) include:
a. forming family partners at the sub-district and/or village level;
b. forming women's protection units within community organizations;
c. conducting independent socialization of women's rights;
d. providing first aid to victims; and
e. reporting to the competent authority if violence against victims occurs in their environment;
(3) Community participation as referred to in paragraph (1) is carried out by individuals, social community institutions, non-governmental organizations, educational institutions, religious institutions, private sector, and mass media.
(4) Provisions regarding forms of community participation as referred to in paragraph (2) are implemented responsibly in accordance with statutory provisions.

Article 31

The obligations of family and parents as referred to in Article 25 letter c and letter d are in the form of full responsibility to prevent all forms of violence and protect women as family members.

CHAPTER VI
STRATEGY FOR WOMEN'S EMPOWERMENT AND PROTECTION

Article 32

(1) Women's empowerment and protection is implemented by integrating gender mainstreaming and gender-disaggregated data in an integrated manner based on statutory regulations.
(2) In women's empowerment and protection, a Working Group and Integrated Services are formed as determined by Regent Decree.
(3) Women's empowerment and protection is coordinated by the Agency that handles Women's Empowerment.

CHAPTER VII
PREVENTION, PROTECTION SERVICES, AND RECOVERY OF WOMEN VICTIMS OF VIOLENCE

Part One
Prevention of Violence Against Women

Article 33

The Regional Government in preventing the occurrence of acts of violence against women:
a. conducts preparation and socialization regarding matters concerning violence against women;
b. conducts socialization of Women's rights;
c. seeks to improve education for Women;
d. opens skills courses for Women to support family economic needs;
e. creates employment opportunities for Women;
f. provides counseling related to Violence Against Women;
g. empowers social community organizations and Women's organizations at the Regional, sub-district, and village levels to increase Women's knowledge and insight;
h. builds Women's Protection complaint posts; and
i. forms a network in efforts to prevent Violence Against Women.

Part Two
Protection Services

Article 34

(1) Protection services to women are carried out in the form of:
a. protection of the victim's personal security from physical and mental threats;
b. health services according to medical needs;
c. medical services for the purposes of legal evidence;
d. maintaining the confidentiality of the victim's identity;
e. providing testimony during examination at trial without facing the suspect;
f. spiritual guidance services; and/or
g. protection of Women who work as laborers or workers in accordance with statutory provisions.
(2) Protection of the victim's personal security from physical and mental threats as referred to in paragraph (1) letter a is carried out by family, law enforcement officers, social institutions, and other parties either temporarily or based on a court Protection order.

Article 35

(1) In providing protection services as referred to in Article 34 paragraph (1) the Regional Government:
a. provides services and handling as quickly as possible to witnesses and/or victims;
b. provides convenience, comfort, safety, and free of charge for witnesses and/or victims;
c. maintains the confidentiality of witnesses and/or victims;
d. guarantees justice and legal certainty for witnesses and/or Victims;
e. provision of apparatus, health workers, social workers, and spiritual counselors;
f. creation and development of cooperation program systems and mechanisms involving parties easily accessible to victims;
g. provides protection for assistants, witnesses, families, and friends of victims;
h. provides decent work and livelihood according to the type and degree of disability, education, and ability; and
i. provides protection for female workers or laborers in accordance with the provisions of protection for laborers or workers in accordance with statutory provisions.
(2) The Regional Government in providing protection services as referred to in paragraph (1) may cooperate with the central government, provincial government, other regency/city governments, the community, and/or other social institutions.
(3) Cooperation as referred to in paragraph (2) is set forth in a cooperation agreement.

Article 36

(1) Provision of Women's Protection services as referred to in Article 35 consists of Victim identification, legal assistance, medical rehabilitation, psychosocial rehabilitation, Social Reintegration, Repatriation assistance, or other forms.
(2) Provision of Women's Protection services as referred to in paragraph (1) is provided for Women Victims of Violence including Victims of Human Trafficking, Women in disaster-prone areas, Women workers, elderly Women, Women with disabilities, Women Victims of sexual exploitation, Women heads of households, and other vulnerable Women groups.

Article 37

Every person related to the implementation of Women's Protection who knows of the occurrence of acts of Violence against Women but allows it, does not provide Protection to Victims, and/or does not report to related agencies may be subject to administrative sanctions in the form of:
a. written warning;
b. temporary suspension from activities;
c. termination of cooperation;
d. revocation of registration certificate;
e. revocation of operational license;
f. withdrawal of facilities; and
g. administrative fines.

Part Three
Recovery

Article 38

(1) For the purposes of recovery, victims have the right to obtain:
a. health services;
b. victim assistance;
c. counseling;
d. spiritual guidance; and
e. resocialization.
(2) For the purposes of recovery as referred to in paragraph (1) the Regional Government must provide:
a. expert and professional personnel;
b. service centers and safe houses; and
c. other facilities and infrastructure needed for victim recovery.
(3) Expert and professional personnel as referred to in paragraph (2) letter b consist of:
a. health workers;
b. social workers;
c. volunteer assistants; and/or
d. spiritual counselors.
(4) Health workers as referred to in paragraph (3) letter a must examine victims according to their professional standards, restore, and rehabilitate the health of victims if victims require treatment.
(5) Social workers, volunteer assistants, and spiritual counselors as referred to in paragraph (3) letter b, letter c, and letter d must provide services to victims in the form of counseling to strengthen and/or provide a sense of security for victims.
(6) Violations of the provisions as referred to in paragraph (4) and paragraph (5) may be subject to administrative sanctions in the form of:
a. written warning;
b. temporary suspension from activities;
c. termination of cooperation;
d. revocation of registration certificate;
e. revocation of operational license;
f. withdrawal of facilities; and
g. administrative fines.
(7) Provisions regarding procedures for applying administrative sanctions as referred to in paragraph (6) are regulated in Regent Regulation.

CHAPTER VIII
INSTITUTIONS

Article 39

(1) P2TP2A in the implementation of women's protection services.
(2) Women's protection services by P2TP2A as referred to in paragraph (1) involve elements of:
a. health;
b. psychology;
c. law;
d. clergy;
e. social; and
f. economy.
(3) Further provisions regarding the establishment, membership composition, duties, and functions of P2TP2A as referred to in paragraph (1) and paragraph (2) are determined by Regent Decree.

Article 40

(1) The Regional Government forms a forum for the protection of women victims of violence at every sub-district level.
(2) The forum for the protection of women victims of violence as referred to in paragraph (1) has the task of:
a. coordinating and synchronizing the handling of protection services for women victims of violence;
b. facilitating and providing more optimal services and reaching the lower level of society;
c. maintaining and developing networks and referral systems; and
d. compiling a database regarding the development of protection of women victims of violence.
(3) Further provisions regarding the establishment, membership composition, duties, and functions of the forum for Protection of Women Victims of Violence as referred to in paragraph (1) and paragraph (2) are determined by Regent Decree.

CHAPTER IX
SUPERVISION, EVALUATION AND GUIDANCE

Part One
Supervision

Article 41

(1) The Regent supervises the implementation of policies, programs, and activities for women's empowerment and protection.
(2) To ensure synergy, continuity and effectiveness in an integrated manner in the implementation of supervision as referred to in paragraph (1) the Regent conducts monitoring through the Regional Apparatus that handles the field of Women.
(3) Monitoring as referred to in paragraph (2) is carried out from planning to implementation of policies, programs and activities for women's empowerment and protection for the current year.
(4) The results of monitoring as referred to in paragraph (3) are reported to the Regent periodically 2 (two) times in 1 (one) year.

Part Two
Evaluation

Article 42

(1) The Regional Government conducts an evaluation of the implementation of policies, programs, and activities for women's protection at the end of each fiscal year.
(2) The results of the evaluation as referred to in paragraph (1) are used as input material for the preparation of policies, programs, and activities for women's protection for the following year.
(3) Evaluation as referred to in paragraph (1) is implemented in accordance with statutory provisions.

Part Three
Guidance

Article 43

The Regent provides guidance on the implementation of policies, programs, and activities for women's protection to related Regional Apparatus and sub-districts.

Article 44

Further provisions regarding supervision, evaluation and guidance of policies, programs and activities for women's protection as referred to in Article 41 through Article 43 are regulated by Regent Regulation.

CHAPTER X
FINANCING SOURCES

Article 45

Financing for the implementation of Women's Protection is sourced from:
a. regional revenue and expenditure budget; and
b. other legal and non-binding sources of funds.

Article 46

(1) The Regional Government may provide financial assistance to community organizations, social organizations, or non-governmental organizations and universities that implement protection for women.
(2) Financial assistance as referred to in paragraph (1) is provided according to the financial capacity of the Region and is implemented in accordance with statutory provisions in the field of regional finance.

CONCERNING
EMPOWERMENT AND PROTECTION OF WOMEN

I. GENERAL

Basically the Region is formed to improve public services and realize community welfare, therefore in the implementation of autonomy, regions have obligations including protecting the community, maintaining unity, integrity and national harmony.

The unequal distribution of opportunities in all areas of life including in the fields of economy, socio-culture, politics and government, law, education, health, employment and social security, which can cause various social problems especially for women. The reality shows that in the Region there are still acts of violence both physically, psychologically, sexually and this is a violation of human rights.

Violence against women is a violation of human rights so they need to be protected in their dignity and honor and guaranteed their right to life in accordance with their nature and destiny without discrimination. The fate of women victims of violence must be given attention by the regional government. The many cases of violence against women are often caused by factors that develop in society, for example low economic levels, education, environments in the industrial sector. Therefore, victims of violence like this need to receive protection in accordance with the principles of justice, truth, legal certainty, equality and upholding human rights.

The Regional Government is obliged and responsible to implement efforts to protect women victims of violence through: implementing policies for the protection of women victims of violence applied by the government, establishing policies, programs, and activities for the protection of women victims of violence, establishing P2TP2A, ensuring the implementation of service convenience for victims, seeking effectiveness and efficiency for the victim recovery process, and seeking the creation of cooperation and coordination in victim recovery efforts.
