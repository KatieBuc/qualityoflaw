PROTECTION FOR WOMEN AND CHILDREN
BY THE GRACE OF ALMIGHTY GOD
REGENT OF DONGGALA,

Considering:
a. that women and children are creations of Almighty God who constitute important elements of Indonesian society and nation;
b. that Indonesian women have an important role in community activities, development and family life and have the obligation to care for, protect and raise Indonesian children to grow and develop as the nation's next generation;
c. that Indonesian children are Indonesian human resources who will later become actors in all fields of social and state life;
d. that in community life, women and children in Donggala Regency often experience violence or unpleasant treatment so it is considered important to protect them;
e. based on the considerations as referred to in letter a, letter b, letter c, and letter d, a Regional Regulation of Donggala Regency concerning Protection for Women and Children is established.

CHAPTER I
GENERAL PROVISIONS

Article 1

In this Regional Regulation, what is meant by:
1. Regent is the Regent of Donggala.
2. Regional People's Representative Council hereinafter abbreviated as DPRD, is the regional people's representative institution of Donggala Regency as an element of regional government administration.
3. Regional Government is the Regent of Donggala and Regional Apparatus as elements of regional government administration.
4. Regional Apparatus Work Unit hereinafter abbreviated as SKPD, is the regional apparatus work unit of Donggala Regency that handles the interests of women and children.
5. Regional Apparatus Work Unit hereinafter abbreviated as UKPD, is the work unit of regional apparatus located within the SKPD of Donggala Regency.
6. Child is a human being who is not yet 18 years old, including children who are still in the womb.
7. Woman is a human being who is female by gender and a person who is recognized by law.
8. Prevention is activities carried out to prevent the occurrence of acts of violence against women and children.
9. Protection is all actions and services to fulfill rights, as well as guarantee and protect the rights of victims of acts of violence.
10. Child protection is all efforts to guarantee and protect children and their rights so that they can live, grow, develop and participate optimally in accordance with human dignity and worth, and receive protection from violence and discrimination.
11. Referral services are services referred to protect victims from all forms of violence, suffering and distress both physically and psychologically.
12. Violence is any act that results in physical, sexual, psychological misery and suffering, neglect, including threats of certain actions, coercion, or arbitrary deprivation of liberty, whether occurring in public or in private life.
13. Physical violence is an act that results in pain, injury, or causes serious injury, miscarriage or fainting and/or death.
14. Psychological violence is an act that results in fear, loss of self-confidence, loss of ability to act, feelings of helplessness or severe psychological suffering in a person.
15. Sexual violence is forced sexual relations, forced sexual relations with another person for commercial purposes and/or certain purposes.
16. Victim of acts of violence hereinafter referred to as victim is an individual or group of people who experience suffering either physically, mentally or emotional suffering, economic loss or deprivation of basic rights (Human Rights), or criminal acts and acts of violence that occur both by state apparatus or government apparatus or by others.
17. Service is immediate activities and actions carried out by professional personnel in accordance with their respective professions in the form of counseling, therapy and advocacy for strengthening and recovery of victims of violence.
18. Assistance is activities and actions carried out by assistants during the service process.
19. Repatriation is an effort to return victims of acts of violence to the family, substitute family, or community that can provide protection and fulfill their needs.
20. Institution is a non-governmental organization (NGO) that provides assistance.
21. Regional Action Plan is a foundation and guideline for related agencies and the community, in order to carry out activities for the implementation of prevention and protection of women and children who are victims of acts of violence.
22. Community is individuals; families, groups, social organizations and/or community organizations.
23. Family is a person who has a blood relationship in a straight line upward or downward and sideways up to the third degree, or who has a marital relationship, or a person who is the dependent of a woman and/or child.
24. Parents are biological father and/or mother, or stepfather and/or stepmother, or adoptive father and/or mother.
25. Supervision is an activity of observing, listening, examining, reviewing, assessing the implementation of protection of women and children.

CHAPTER II
PRINCIPLES, OBJECTIVES AND SCOPE

Article 2

The implementation of protection of women and children is carried out based on the principles of:
a. respect for the rights of women and children;
b. non-discrimination;
c. justice.

Article 3

The objectives of protection of women and children are to:
a. provide and increase a sense of security for women and children;
b. help prevent acts of violence against women and children;
c. help prevent exploitation activities against women and children;
d. provide services to women and children in facing problems.
e. help improve the quality of life of children

Article 4

The scope of regulation of protection for women and children includes:
a. Rights of women and children;
b. Duties and authorities
c. Handling of acts of violence;
d. Financing;
e. Supervision;
f. Community participation.

CHAPTER III
FULFILLMENT OF CHILDREN'S AND WOMEN'S RIGHTS

Part One
Children's Rights

Article 5

In the implementation of child protection in the region, children have the right:
a. to live, grow and develop and participate reasonably in accordance with human dignity and worth and receive guidance and protection from violence, negligence and discrimination;
b. to a valid birth certificate obtained and/or arranged by their parents in accordance with applicable laws and regulations;
c. to worship according to their religion, think and express themselves according to their level of intelligence and age under parental guidance;
d. to know, be raised and cared for by their own parents;
e. in the event that for some reason parents cannot guarantee the child's growth and development or the child is in a neglected state, then the child has the right to be cared for or adopted as a foster child or adopted child by another person in accordance with the provisions of laws and regulations;
f. to obtain health services and social security in accordance with physical, mental, spiritual and social needs;
g. to obtain education and teaching according to their abilities, interests and talents;
h. to express opinions and have their opinions heard;
i. to rest and utilize time to develop their talents and abilities;
j. to receive protection from family, community and Regional Government against discriminatory treatment and exploitation both economically and socially, neglect, cruelty, violence, persecution, injustice and other mistreatment;
k. to regain formal or non-formal education for children who drop out of school;
l. to receive exclusive breast milk (ASI) as a priority;
m. to receive guidance
n. to obtain other rights in accordance with the provisions of laws and regulations.

Part Two
Women's Rights

Article 6

In the implementation of protection of women in the region, women have the right to:
a. basic needs;
b. comfort;
c. a sense of security;
d. maximum health services in facing acts of violence;
e. assistance services, legal aid and spiritual guidance in facing acts of violence; and
f. confidentiality regarding the problems faced.

CHAPTER IV
DUTIES AND AUTHORITIES

Part One
Duties

Paragraph 1
Duties of the Government in the Implementation of Child Protection

Article 7

The duties of the Regional Government in the implementation of Child Protection include:
a. preparation of long-term, medium-term, and short-term strategic plans for Child Protection as an integrated part of the Development Planning Document;
b. implementation and supervision of the implementation of Child Protection.

Article 8

The regional government provides guidance and guidance for children to embrace religion in accordance with the religion and beliefs of their parents.

Article 9

(1) The regional government provides facilities and organizes comprehensive health efforts for children, so that every child obtains optimal health status from the womb through community health centers and hospitals.
(2) Comprehensive health efforts as referred to in paragraph (1) include promotive, preventive, curative, and rehabilitative efforts, both for basic health services and referrals.
(3) Comprehensive health efforts as referred to in paragraph (2) are provided free of charge for families who are unable or poor families.
(4) The regional government carries out activities to support breastfeeding to babies.

Article 10

(1) The regional government organizes basic and secondary education for children.
(2) The implementation of education as referred to in paragraph (1) is free for students from families who are unable or poor families or neglected children.
(3) The regional government is obliged to provide education costs or free education for students from families who are unable or poor families or neglected children.

Article 11

(1) The regional government is obliged to accommodate and care for children who are neglected by their parents.
(2) Accommodation and care for children as referred to in paragraph (1) can be carried out through cooperation with institutions, social foundations or orphanages.

Article 12

The regional government provides special protection for children who are victims of riots or disasters by providing basic needs consisting of clothing, shelter, education, health, social welfare and recreation, security guarantees, and equality before the law.
(2) The regional government provides special protection for children with disabilities and children experiencing psychosocial disorders.

Article 13

(1) The regional government carries out prevention of exploitation of children's body organs to other parties and acts of violence against children.
(2) Prevention efforts as referred to in paragraph (1) are carried out in the form of activities that include:
a. Early detection and intervention
b. Socialization and education;
c. Seminars or workshops;
d. Preparation of an effective information system;
e. Cooperation with institutions that have activities in preventing acts of violence against children.

Article 14

The regional government is obliged to assist in handling acts of violence against children.

Paragraph 2
Duties of the Government in the Implementation of Protection of Women

Article 15

The duties of the Regional Government in the Implementation of Protection of Women include:
a. preparation of long-term, medium-term, and short-term strategic plans for Protection of Women as an integrated part of the Development Planning Document;
b. implementation and supervision of the Implementation of Protection of Women.

Article 16

(1) The regional government carries out prevention of acts of violence against women.
(2) Prevention efforts for acts of violence against women are carried out in the form of activities that include:
a. Regional Action Plan
b. Socialization;
c. Seminars or workshops;
d. Preparation of an effective information system;
e. Cooperation with institutions that have activities in preventing acts of violence against women.

Article 17

The regional government is obliged to assist in handling acts of violence against women.

Part Two
Authority

Article 18

(1) The Regent may cooperate with related parties in assisting efforts to accommodate and/or care for neglected children or handle acts of violence against women and children.
(2) The Regent in carrying out cooperation as referred to in paragraph (1) may assign officials within the regional government.
(2) Officials assigned as referred to in paragraph (2) act for and on behalf of the Regent.
(3) Assignment to officials as referred to in paragraph (2) is stipulated by Regent's Decree.

Article 19

(1) Related parties with whom the Regent cooperates as referred to in Article 14 paragraph (1) include:
a. Social community organizations, institutions or orphanages that have activities related to protection, care, shelter, guidance, empowerment of children and/or women;
b. Social workers or volunteers;
c. Advocates and
d. Religious leaders
(2) Cooperation as referred to in paragraph (1) is carried out in the form of a cooperation agreement.

CHAPTER V
HANDLING OF ACTS OF VIOLENCE AGAINST WOMEN AND CHILDREN

Article 20

(1) The regional government is obliged to prepare medical personnel at community health centers (Puskesmas) or hospitals who specifically handle victims.
(2) Medical personnel as referred to in paragraph (1) are obliged to provide complete health services to victims.

Article 21

The regional government is obliged to assist in providing adequate special rooms for victims at the Police Resort (Polres) and at every Police Sector office.

Article 22

(1) Social workers or volunteer personnel as referred to in Article 19 paragraph (1) letter b are tasked with accompanying victims in facing the process of resolving acts of violence.
(2) Social workers or volunteer personnel in addition to being tasked as referred to in paragraph (1) carry out activities including:
a. counseling or guidance to strengthen and provide a sense of security for victims;
b. providing information regarding victims' rights to obtain protection from the police and determination of protection orders from the court;
c. escorting victims to safe houses or alternative residences; and
d. conducting integrated coordination in providing services to victims with agencies or related parties needed by victims.

Article 23

(1) The regional government is obliged to prepare a Safe House to be used in temporary handling for victims of violence against women and children.
(2) The Safe House as referred to in paragraph (2) is provided in every Sub-district.

Article 24

(1) Advocates as referred to in Article 19 paragraph (1) letter c assist in providing legal services for victims or provide legal consultation in the form of information regarding victims' rights and the judicial process;
(2) Advocates are obliged to accompany and/or represent victims at the investigation, prosecution, and examination levels in court hearings and help victims to fully describe the violence they experienced.
(3) Information about violence from victims that is personal in nature must be kept confidential by advocates.
(3) Advocates may coordinate with fellow law enforcers, volunteer assistants, and social workers so that the judicial process proceeds properly.

Article 25

(1) Religious leaders as referred to in Article 19 paragraph (1) letter d are tasked with providing spiritual guidance or strengthening faith and piety to victims.
(2) In addition to providing spiritual guidance, religious leaders are obliged to provide explanations regarding the rights and obligations of victims.

CHAPTER VI
FINANCING

Article 26

(1) Activity costs related to the implementation of protection of women and children are charged to the Regional Revenue and Expenditure Budget as well as other legitimate receipts in accordance with applicable laws and regulations.
(2) Activity expenditures sourced from the Regional Revenue and Expenditure Budget are budgeted at the Regional Apparatus Work Unit that handles the affairs of implementing protection of women and children in accordance with regional needs and financial capacity.
(3) Budget allocation as referred to in paragraph (1) must be budgeted by the Regent and DPRD and stipulated every fiscal year in the APBD.

CHAPTER VII
SUPERVISION AND GUIDANCE

Article 27

(1) Supervision and guidance of the implementation of protection of women and children is carried out by the Regent.
(2) Daily supervision and guidance activities as referred to in paragraph (1) are carried out by the Regional Apparatus Work Unit that handles child protection.
(3) Results of supervision and guidance activities by the Regional Apparatus Work Unit must be reported to the Regent.

CHAPTER VIII
COMMUNITY PARTICIPATION

Article 28

(1) The community has the right to obtain the widest possible opportunity to play a role in assisting/supporting efforts to protect women and children.
(2) Community participation as referred to in paragraph (1) is carried out by individuals, child protection institutions, social community institutions, non-governmental organizations, educational institutions, religious institutions, business entities, and mass media.

Article 29

(1) Forms of community participation can be:
a. Supervision; or
b. Provision of assistance;
(2) Community supervision includes activities:
a. observing or seeing the condition of fulfillment of women's and children's rights; and
b. providing information on every condition that tends to lead to acts of violence against women and children to police officers and/or regional government.
(3) Provision of assistance as referred to in paragraph (1) letter b is in the form of:
a. temporary security or shelter for victims;
b. financial support to meet the needs of victims according to community capacity;
c. Financial assistance and/or facilities to the regional government in supporting the implementation of protection of women and children.

CHAPTER IX
CLOSING PROVISIONS

Article 30

This Regional Regulation comes into force on the date of promulgation.

In order that everyone may know of it, the promulgation of this Regional Regulation is ordered by placing it in the Regional Gazette of Donggala Regency.

I. GENERAL

The state through the government and regional government currently places great attention on the existence of children. Based on their existence as the nation's next generation who are qualified and dignified, the government and regional government are obliged to recognize the existence of children's rights in their human rights. The inherent human rights of children include basic rights as human beings, namely the Right to Life, the Right to Growth and Development, the Right to Protection and the Right to Participation. To fulfill the Right to life, children need adequate, healthy and nutritious food in sufficient quantities, as well as optimal health access. In supporting the realization of children to grow and develop, children need various adequate educational facilities and infrastructure, development of talents and interests. In order to realize the right to protection and the right to participation, children need serious attention from various parties, both government, regional government and society.

Specifically regarding protection efforts from violence that threatens the safety and health of children and women must be given from an early age. Protection is needed to prevent physical, mental and sexual violence and exploitation. For example, women and children who are victims of persecution, neglect, eviction and mistreatment by parents or others. For women and children experiencing such problems, the government must provide Safe Houses and adequate budgets, the role of various parties to help handle violence problems faced by women and children including social community organizations, volunteers, social workers, medical personnel, advocates and religious leaders.

The condition of children's decline along with their rights and the condition of violence against women and children that often occurs in the region should be a common concern for all stakeholders, including the regional government, to play a role in preventing and overcoming such decline and violence.

For the regional government of Donggala Regency in facing the universe of conditions above, various actions are taken to fulfill children's rights and prevent and assist in handling acts of violence against women and children which are endeavored by establishing a Regional Regulation on Protection for Women and Children.
