# MAYOR OF PAYAKUMBUH
WEST SUMATRA PROVINCE
REGIONAL REGULATION OF PAYAKUMBUH CITY
NUMBER tl YEAR 2016
CONCERNING
PROTECTION OF WOMEN AND CHILDREN

WITH THE GRACE OF GOD ALMIGHTY

MAYOR OF PAYAKUMBUH,

Considering
a. that the Unitary State of the Republic of Indonesia guarantees the welfare of every citizen, including the protection of Women and Children from all forms of violence and discrimination as mandated in the 1945 Constitution of the Republic of Indonesia;

b. that the rampant crimes and violence against women and children in society requires increased commitment and coordination from the Government, Regional Government and Society as well as all stakeholders in order to carry out handling and prevention measures;

c. that the fulfillment of the constitutional rights of women and children as well as improving the quality of life of women and children is one of the mandatory affairs that becomes the duty, authority and responsibility of the Regional Government;

d. that based on the considerations as referred to in letter a, letter b and letter c, it is necessary to establish a Regional Regulation on the Protection of Women and Children.

Remembering
1. Article 18 paragraph (6) of the 1945 Constitution of the Republic of Indonesia;

2. Law Number 8 of 1956 concerning the Establishment of Small City Autonomous Regions Within the Central Sumatra Provincial Region jo. 6. Law Number 23 of 2004 concerning the Elimination of Domestic Violence (State Gazette of the Republic of Indonesia year 2004 Number 95, Supplement to the State Gazette of the Republic of Indonesia Number 4419);

To Establish: REGIONAL REGULATION CONCERNING THE PROTECTION OF WOMEN AND CHILDREN

CHAPTER I
GENERAL PROVISIONS

Article 1
In this Regional Regulation, what is meant by:
1. Region is Payakumbuh City.
2. Regional Government is the Government of Payakumbuh City.
3. Mayor is the Mayor of Payakumbuh.
4. Regional Apparatus Work Unit hereinafter abbreviated as SKPD is the Regional Apparatus Work Unit within the Payakumbuh City Government.
5. Protection of Women is all efforts to guarantee and protect the rights of women so that they can live healthily and participate optimally in accordance with human dignity and receive protection from neglect, violence and discrimination, whether in the form of preventive, repressive or post-remedial protection (assistance and treatment).
6. Child protection is all activities to guarantee and protect children and their rights so that they can live, grow, develop and participate optimally in accordance with human dignity and receive protection from violence and discrimination.
7. Prevention is direct efforts made by the Regional Government, community and Parents and/or families to prevent violations of the rights of women and children.
8. Children's Forum is a Children's Participation Forum formed with the aim of facilitating the involvement of children in the decision-making process and enjoying changes concerning their lives both directly and indirectly which are carried out with the approval and will of all children based on awareness and understanding, in accordance with age and level of thinking maturity.
9. Child-Friendly Regency/City hereinafter abbreviated as KLA is a Regency City that has a child rights-based development system through the integration of commitment and resources of government, society and the business world that are planned comprehensively and sustainably in policies, programs and activities to ensure the fulfillment of children's rights.
10. Child is someone who has not reached the age of 18 (eighteen) years including children still in the womb.

11. Neglected child is a child whose needs are not properly fulfilled, whether physical, mental, spiritual or social.
12. Female victim of violence is a woman who experiences actions that result in misery or suffering physically, sexually or psychologically, including threats of certain actions, coercion or arbitrary deprivation of freedom, whether occurring in the public sphere or in private life.
13. Child victim of violence is a child who experiences actions that result in misery or suffering physically, mentally, sexually, psychologically or neglect that threatens life, body and degrades human dignity.
14. Trafficking in persons is the act of recruiting, transporting, harboring, sending, transferring or receiving someone with threats of violence, use of violence, kidnapping, confinement, fraud, abuse of power or vulnerable position, debt bondage or giving payment or benefits so as to obtain consent from the person who holds control over that other person, whether carried out within the country or between countries, for the purpose of exploitation or resulting in the person being exploited.
15. Complaint Handling is a series of actions taken by integrated service providers to follow up on reports of acts of violence against women and children, submitted by victims, families and the community.
16. Social rehabilitation is a service aimed at restoring and developing the ability of someone experiencing social dysfunction so that they can carry out their social functions properly.
17. Law enforcement is the action of state-authorized apparatus to implement statutory regulations.
18. Legal assistance is legal services provided by legal assistants and advocates to carry out the process of accompanying witnesses and/or violence against women and children that is gender-sensitive.
19. Repatriation is an effort to return women and children victims of violence from abroad to the debarkation point/entry point or from the receiving area to the area of origin.
20. Social reintegration is an effort to reunite victims with family, substitute family or community that can provide protection and fulfill the needs of victims.

21. Safe house is a temporary shelter that aims to provide protection and a sense of security to victims of violence.
22. Shelter house is a guidance or welfare service facility for street children that carries out social assistance/guidance activities, maintenance, care, education and health, skills guidance in order to ensure that children do not carry out activities on the streets so that they can grow and develop normally.
23. Spiritual guidance is an effort to restore the soul through strengthening the religion adhered to by the victim.
24. Gender Responsive is consistent and systematic attention to the differences between women and men in society accompanied by efforts to eliminate structural and cultural barriers in achieving gender equality.
25. Parents are biological father and/or mother, or adoptive father and/or mother.
26. Family is the smallest unit in society consisting of husband and wife or husband, wife and their children, or father and children, mother and children, or blood relatives in a straight line up or down to the third degree.
27. Customs are permanent and hereditary conduct from one generation to another as a legacy, so that it is strongly integrated with the behavior patterns of society.

CHAPTER II
PRINCIPLES, OBJECTIVES AND SCOPE

Article 2
(1) The implementation of women's protection is based on the principles of:
a. Best interests of women
b. Gender justice and gender equality
c. Local wisdom
d. Legal Certainty

(2) The implementation of child protection is based on the principles of:
a. Non-Discrimination
b. Best interests of the child
c. Right to life, survival and development
d. Respect for the child's opinion
e. Legal Certainty

Article 3
The regulation of the protection of women and children aims to:
a. serve as a guideline for the Regional Government in planning, policies and strategies for the protection of women and children.
b. protect women and children from actions, decisions and policies that violate the rights of women and children.
c. prevent violations of the rights of women and children.
d. increase empowerment of women.
e. increase the role and value of local wisdom and the role of customs in the protection of women and children, and
f. increase the role of government and non-government institutions in efforts to protect women and children.

Article 4
The scope of regulation of the protection of women and children includes:
a. Prevention;
b. Implementation of protection of women and children;
c. Handling;
d. Empowerment, and
e. Guidance and supervision;

CHAPTER III
RIGHTS OF WOMEN AND CHILDREN

First Section
Rights of Women

Article 5
Every woman has the right to:
a. Obtain protection from discriminatory actions;
b. Obtain protection from economic exploitation and sexual exploitation;
c. Obtain protection from physical, psychological, sexual violence and neglect;
d. Obtain decent work according to her ability;
e. Obtain work leave with wages/salary in accordance with statutory regulations;
f. Obtain education and teaching at all types, levels and paths of education;
g. Obtain special protection in carrying out work or profession against things that can threaten safety and/or health related to women's reproductive function, and
h. Ownership and management of joint property in marriage in accordance with statutory regulations.

Second Section
Rights of Children

Article 6
Every child has the right to:
a. Live, grow, develop and participate properly in accordance with human dignity;
b. Obtain protection from discriminatory actions;
c. Obtain protection from economic exploitation and sexual exploitation;
d. Obtain protection from physical, psychological, sexual violence and neglect;
e. A name as self-identity and citizenship status;
f. Obtain a birth certificate free of charge;
g. Embrace religion and worship according to the teachings of their religion with the guidance of parents/guardians;
h. Not be forced to change religion by foster parents, adoptive parents and by anyone;
i. Be recognized and know their parents, be raised and cared for by their own parents;
j. Obtain health services and social security in accordance with physical, mental, spiritual and social needs;
k. Obtain education and teaching in accordance with their interests and talents;
l. Obtain care, education and training, improve themselves and the ability to participate in community and state life for children with special needs.
m. Express and be heard their opinions, and
n. Rest and utilize leisure time for self-development.

CHAPTER IV
RESPONSIBILITIES OF REGIONAL GOVERNMENT, COMMUNITY, PARENTS AND/OR FAMILY

First Section
Regional Government

Article 7
(1) The Regional Government in efforts to protect women is responsible for:
a. Guaranteeing women's rights without discrimination in accordance with statutory regulations;
b. Establishing and implementing policies, programs and activities for the protection of women's rights;
c. Cooperating with the community and/or community organizations in implementing women's protection in accordance with statutory regulations;
d. Conducting rehabilitation and empowerment of women who are victims of violence;
e. Providing special protection to women victims of violence and trafficking in persons, and
f. Taking preventive actions in order to prevent violations of women's rights.

(2) The responsibility of the Regional Government in efforts to protect women as referred to in paragraph (1) is carried out by SKPD in accordance with their respective duties and functions.

Article 8
(1) The Regional Government in efforts to protect children is responsible for:
a. Guaranteeing the fulfillment of children's rights without discrimination in accordance with statutory regulations;
b. Providing support for facilities and infrastructure in implementing child protection;
c. Guaranteeing the right of children to express opinions in accordance with the age and intelligence level of the child;
d. Establishing and implementing policies and programs/activities for the protection of children's rights;
e. Cooperating with the community and/or community organizations in implementing child protection in accordance with statutory regulations;
f. Conducting rehabilitation of children who are victims of violence;
g. Providing special protection to children in emergency situations, children in conflict with the law, children victims of violence and trafficking in persons, children from minority and isolated groups, children exploited economically and/or sexually, children who are victims of drug abuse, alcohol, psychotropics and children with HIV/AIDS;
h. Organizing inclusive education for children with special needs, and
i. Taking preventive actions in order to prevent violations of children's rights.

(2) The responsibility of the Regional Government in efforts to protect children as referred to in paragraph (1) is carried out by SKPD in accordance with their respective duties and functions.

Article 9
(1) In order to carry out its responsibilities, the Regional Government establishes programs and activities for the protection of women and children with a Regional Action Plan as a basis for SKPD in implementing the protection of women and children in accordance with their duties and functions.

(2) Further provisions regarding the Regional Action Plan as referred to in paragraph (1) are regulated by Mayor's Regulation.

Second Section
Community

Article 10
(1) Community responsibility in efforts to protect women and children:
a. Participate in prevention and provide support for the development of prevention programs for violations of the rights of women and children;
b. Provide information and/or report violations of the rights of women and children to the competent authorities, and
c. Supervise the law enforcement process for cases of violations of the rights of women and children.

(2) Ninik mamak, religious scholars, intellectuals and bundo kanduang are responsible for protecting and preventing violations of the rights of women and children.

(3) Community responsibility as referred to in paragraphs (1) and (2) is based on statutory regulations and local wisdom values of the local community.

Third Section
Parents/Guardians and/or Family

Paragraph 1
Responsibility for the Protection of Women

Article 11
Families in efforts to protect women's rights are responsible for:
a. Protecting women from conflicts and domestic violence that degrades the dignity of women;
b. Providing equal opportunities for women to develop themselves in the fields of education, social, economic, political and cultural affairs, and
c. Preventing violations of women's rights in the household.

Paragraph 2
Responsibility for Child Protection

Article 12
(1) Parents in efforts to protect children's rights are responsible for:
a. Caring for, maintaining, educating and protecting children;
b. Developing children according to their abilities, talents and interests;
c. Providing formal and non-formal education;
d. Guiding children to behave in accordance with religion, ethics and morals as well as customs; and
e. Preventing marriage at child age.

(2) In the event that parents do not exist, or their whereabouts are unknown, or for some reason, cannot carry out their obligations and responsibilities, then the obligations and responsibilities as referred to in paragraph (1) may transfer to the family.

CHAPTER V
PREVENTION

First Section
Regional Government

Article 13
(1) In order to prevent violations of women's rights, the Regional Government:
a. Conducts socialization regarding the prevention of violence against women by optimizing the role of customary, cultural, religious institutions and community organizations;
b. Provides guidelines on handling acts of violence against women;
c. Involves community participation in recovery and social reintegration;
d. Develops an integrated database and information system on acts of violence against women;
e. Develops family empowerment programs;
f. Strengthens the function of community organizations working in the field of women's empowerment; and
g. Provides comfortable and safe public facilities for women.

(2) In order to prevent violations of children's rights, the Regional Government:
a. Makes efforts to prevent acts of violence against children by optimizing the role of customary, cultural, religious institutions and community organizations;
b. Develops child-friendly schools;
c. Encourages increased child participation through the establishment of children's forums;
d. Provides guidelines on handling acts of violence against children;
e. Involves community participation in recovery services and reintegration of child victims of violence.
f. Develops an integrated database and information system on acts of violence against children;
g. Strengthens the function of community organizations working in the field of child protection; and
h. Realizes the development of a Child-Friendly City.
i. Realizes the development of Child-Friendly Open Public Spaces (RPTRA).

Second Section
Community

Article 14
(1) In order to prevent violations of women's rights, the community:
a. Monitors various conditions occurring in their environment that can lead to violations of women's rights;
b. Strengthens the role of community, religious organizations and the family environment; and
c. Prevents under-the-table/unregistered marriages

(2) In order to prevent violations of children's rights, the community:
a. Monitors various conditions occurring in the environment that lead to violations of children's rights;
b. Strengthens the role of community, religious organizations and the family environment; and
c. Prevents marriage at child age.

Third Section
Parents, Family and/or Guardians

Article 15
(1) In order to prevent violations of children's rights, parents or guardians:
a. Provide good examples to children according to religious teachings and customs;
b. Supervise children in their activities inside or outside the home;
c. Provide opportunities for children to dialogue and express opinions democratically; and
d. Protect children from various violations of children's rights in the household.

(2) In order to prevent violations of women's rights, families:
a. Protect women from various violations of rights in the household; and
b. Encourage the formation of harmonious, educative and democratic families.

CHAPTER VI
IMPLEMENTATION OF PROTECTION OF WOMEN AND CHILDREN

First Section
Implementation of Women's Protection

Paragraph 1
General

Article 16
Women's protection is implemented by the Regional Government by involving the active role of families, ninik mamak, bundo kanduang, customary leaders, religious leaders, the community and community organizations in the field of Women's Protection.

Paragraph 2
Marriage

Article 17
During marriage or after divorce, a woman has the same rights as the man who is/was her husband over all matters related to the consequences of marriage and/or divorce.

Paragraph 3
Health Sector

Article 18
The Regional Government provides adequate health and maternity facilities for women.

Article 19
Public service providers must provide special breastfeeding facilities.

Paragraph 4
Employment Sector

Article 20
(1) Everyone is prohibited from employing and involving women in bad jobs.

(2) Bad jobs as referred to in paragraph (1) include:
a. All work in the form of slavery or the like.
b. All work that exploits, provides or offers women for prostitution, pornography production, pornographic performances or gambling.
c. All work that exploits, provides, or involves women in the production and trade of alcoholic beverages, narcotics, psychotropics, and other addictive substances and/or
d. All work that endangers the health, safety and morals of women.

(3) Employers who employ women must maintain decency, safety and security while at the workplace;

(4) Employers must place female workers in places that are safe for women's reproductive health;

(5) Employers must fulfill the rights of female workers to receive wages in accordance with the provisions of statutory regulations;

(6) Employers must provide special breastfeeding facilities at their companies.

Article 21
(1) Female workers/laborers under the age of 18 (eighteen) years are prohibited from being employed between 23.00 WIB and 07.00 WIB.

(2) Employers are prohibited from employing pregnant female workers/laborers who according to a doctor's statement are dangerous to the health and safety of their pregnancy or themselves if they work between 23.00 WIB and 07.00 WIB.

(3) Employers who employ female workers/laborers between 23.00 WIB and 07.00 WIB must:
a. Provide nutritious food and drinks; and
b. Maintain decency and security while at the workplace.

(4) Employers must provide shuttle transportation for female workers/laborers who depart and return from work between 23.00 WIB and 05.00 WIB.

Paragraph 5
Education Sector

Article 22
Women are guaranteed freedom to obtain education at every level of formal and non-formal education.

Paragraph 6
Social Sector

Article 23
(1) The regional government organizes empowerment of women experiencing socio-economic vulnerability through productive economic business activities.

(2) Community organizations can carry out empowerment for women experiencing socio-economic vulnerability in accordance with statutory regulations.

Second Section
Implementation of Child Protection

Paragraph 1
Health Sector

Article 24
In implementing Child Protection in the Health Sector, the Regional Government must:
(1) Provide free health services for children from poor families.
(2) Organize optimal health efforts for children, so that every child obtains a good degree of health from the womb.
(3) Encourage babies to receive exclusive breastfeeding from their mothers properly and naturally.
(4) Motivate children to receive adequate nutrition.
(5) Protect children from attempts to transplant their body organs to other parties.
(6) Conduct screening on newborns.
(7) Monitor the growth and development of toddlers and preschool-age children.

Paragraph 2
Education Sector

Article 25
Children in formal and non-formal educational institutions must be protected from acts of violence.

Article 26
The Regional Government and community organize Early Childhood Education (PAUD).

Article 27
For children aged 7 (seven) to 18 (eighteen) years who have not completed formal education/dropped out of school can pursue education through non-formal education units, namely:
a. Package A Study Group equivalent to Elementary School and Madrasah Ibtidaiyah.
b. Package B Study Group equivalent to Junior High School and Madrasah Tsanawiyah.
c. Package C Study Group equivalent to Senior High School and Madrasah Aliyah and Vocational High School.

Article 28
(1) Children with special needs and/or who have potential intelligence and/or special talents are given equal opportunities and facilities to obtain regular education or inclusive education.
(2) Inclusive education as referred to in paragraph (1) is organized by the Regional Government and community.

Article 29
Every formal education provider is prohibited from expelling children without guaranteeing the continuity of the child's education.

Article 30
(1) The Regional Government provides scholarships and/or educational assistance for children from poor families, neglected children.
(2) The implementation of scholarship programs and/or educational assistance as referred to in paragraph (1) is supported by community participation and community organizations.
(3) The implementation of the provisions as referred to in paragraph (1) is carried out in accordance with statutory regulations.

Article 31
Children requiring special protection are protected in their right to obtain education, namely:
a. Children in emergency situations;
b. Children in conflict with the law;
c. Children from minority and isolated groups;
d. Children exploited economically and/or sexually;
e. Children who are victims of narcotics, alcohol, psychotropics, and other addictive substance abuse;
f. Children who are victims of pornography;
g. Children with HIV/AIDS;
h. Children victims of kidnapping, sale, and/or trafficking;
i. Children victims of physical and/or psychological violence;
j. Children victims of sexual crimes;
k. Children victims of terrorism networks;
l. Children with Disabilities;
m. Children victims of mistreatment and neglect;
n. Children with deviant social behavior;
o. Children who are victims of stigmatization from labeling related to their Parents' condition.

Paragraph 3
Employment Sector

Article 32
(1) Employers are prohibited from employing children.
(2) Employers are prohibited from forcing the use of religious attributes that are not in accordance with the religion adhered to by the worker.

Article 33
(1) The provisions as referred to in Article 32 paragraph (1) may be exempted for children aged between 13 (thirteen) years to 15 (fifteen) years, to perform light work as long as it does not interfere with physical, mental and social development and health.

(2) Employers who employ children in light work as referred to in paragraph (1) must meet the requirements:
a. Written permission from parents or guardians;
b. Work agreement between the employer and parents or guardians.
c. Maximum working time of 3 (three) hours
d. Performed during the day and does not interfere with school time.
e. Occupational safety and health.
f. Clear employment relationship.
g. Receive wages in accordance with applicable statutory provisions.

(3) The provisions as referred to in paragraph (2) letters a, b, f and g are exempted for children working in family businesses.

Article 34
In the event that a company employs children as referred to in Article 33, the children's workplace must be separated from the workplace of adult workers.

Article 35
Children may perform work at a workplace that is part of an educational or training curriculum validated by the competent official.

Article 36
(1) Everyone is prohibited from employing and involving children in bad jobs.

(2) Bad jobs as referred to in paragraph (1) include:
a. All work in the form of slavery or the like.
b. All work that exploits, provides or offers children for prostitution, pornography production, pornographic performances or gambling.
c. All work that exploits, provides, or involves children in the production and trade of alcoholic beverages, narcotics, psychotropics, and other addictive substances and/or
d. All work that endangers the health, safety and morals of children.

Paragraph 4
Social Sector

Article 37
(1) The Regional Government must organize care for neglected children.
(2) Care as referred to in paragraph (1) may be carried out by Community Organizations in the social field.
(3) In the event that a child is neglected because for some reason the parents neglect, the Regional Government, Community Organizations as referred to in paragraph (2) may submit an application to the Court to determine the child as a neglected child.
(4) The Court determination as referred to in paragraph (3) simultaneously determines the place of shelter and care for the neglected child concerned.

Article 38
(1) The Regional Government organizes skills training for street children and neglected children.
(2) Skills training as referred to in paragraph (1) is organized by SKPD in accordance with their respective duties and functions.

Article 39
(1) The Regional Government provides shelter houses to guide street children and social institutions for neglected children.
(2) Shelter houses as referred to in paragraph (1) may be organized in cooperation with community organizations.
(3) Further provisions regarding shelter houses as referred to in paragraph (1) are regulated by Mayor's Regulation.

Article 40
The implementation of protection of women and children is organized by cross-sectoral SKPD whose duties and functions are related to the protection of women and children.

CHAPTER VII
HANDLING AND SERVICES

First Section
Handling of Women and Children Victims of Violence

Article 41
(1) Complaints of acts of violence against women and children can be made by reporters consisting of victims, families, the community and/or community organizations in the field of protection of women and children.

(2) Reporters as referred to in paragraph (1) may file complaints:
a. Directly.
b. By telephone; and
c. By letter.

(3) In addition to handling complaints submitted by reporters, officers at service institutions as referred to in Article 43 are also authorized to handle victims of violence against women and children obtained through other information.

(4) Further provisions regarding the procedures for handling complaint services as referred to in paragraphs (1) and (2) are regulated by Mayor's regulation.

Article 42
The implementation of handling women and children victims of violence includes:
a. Handling complaints of victims of violence.
b. Health services
c. Social rehabilitation
d. Law enforcement and legal assistance, and
e. Repatriation and social reintegration.

Article 43
(1) The implementation of handling women and children victims of violence is carried out through integrated services;

(2) Integrated services for women and children victims of violence use the principles of:
a. Gender responsive
b. Non-discrimination.
c. Equal and respectful relationships.
d. Maintaining confidentiality.
e. Providing a sense of security and comfort.
f. Respecting individual differences.
g. Non-judgmental.
h. Respecting the victim's choices and decisions.
i. Sensitive to the victim's background and condition and use of language that is appropriate and understood by the victim.
j. Fast and simple.
k. Empathy, and
l. Fulfillment of the rights of women and children.

Article 44
Complaint handling services for victims of violence against women and children are carried out by:
a. Hospitals.
b. Community health centers.
c. Integrated Service Center for Women's Empowerment and Child Protection (P2TP2A)
d. Women and Children Service Unit (UPPA)
e. Trauma Center Shelter (RPTC)
f. Child Social Shelter (RPSA)
g. Women Crisis Centre (WCC)
h. Marriage Counseling, Guidance and Preservation Agency (BP4)
i. Police
j. Prosecutor's Office
k. Legal Aid Institute (LBH) and
l. Child Protection Institution.

Second Section
Health Services for Women and Children Victims of Violence

Article 45
(1) Health services for women and children victims of violence are efforts that include promotive, preventive, curative and rehabilitative aspects.

(2) Health services for women and children victims of violence are carried out comprehensively, accessible 24 (twenty-four) hours, quality and carried out in accordance with health service standards.

(3) Health services specifically for child victims of violence must prioritize the best interests of the child.

(4) Women and children victims of violence have rights to health services, including:
a. Medical services
b. Medicolegal services
c. Psychosocial Counseling.

(5) Further provisions regarding the procedures for implementing health services as referred to in paragraph (4) are regulated by Mayor's Regulation.

Third Section
Social Rehabilitation Services for Women and Children Victims of Violence

Article 46
(1) Social Rehabilitation Services for Women and Children Victims of Violence are aimed at restoring and developing the abilities of victims experiencing social dysfunction to be able to carry out their social functions in society properly.

(2) Social Rehabilitation Services as referred to in paragraph (1) include counseling services, provision of safe houses and spiritual guidance.

(3) The implementation of services as referred to in paragraph (2) is organized by:
a. SKPD that has duties and functions in the social field.
b. SKPD in charge of women's empowerment and child protection.
c. Service handling institutions.

Article 47
(1) Counseling services as referred to in Article 46 paragraph (2) aim to:
a. Help victims recognize problems and find effective ways to overcome them themselves;
b. Empower victims to determine their future;
c. Provide moral support for victims in facing the process they are going through, and
d. Make victims feel accepted in the community environment.

(2) Counseling services are carried out by gender-responsive social rehabilitation officers, consisting of:
a. Counselors;
b. Psychologists, and
c. Psychiatrists.

(3) Counseling services are carried out in places that guarantee a sense of security, comfort and confidentiality of information from victims.

(4) Stages of counseling services include:
a. Initial counseling
b. Follow-up counseling.
c. Case conference; and
d. Home visit.

Article 48
(1) The Regional Government may provide safe houses for women and children victims of violence.

(2) Safe houses aim to protect women and children victims of violence from intimidation by perpetrators and other parties.

(3) The location of safe houses as referred to in paragraph (1) must be kept confidential.

(4) Criteria for women and children victims of violence who need safe houses include:
a. Life threatened
b. Receiving rejection from family or community
c. Requiring intensive services but their residence is relatively far; and/or
d. Neglected if not placed in a safe house.

(5) The implementation of safe house services is carried out by gender-responsive social rehabilitation officers, consisting of:
a. Social workers
b. Volunteers
c. Security officers and
d. Community leaders

(6) Further provisions regarding the implementation of safe house services are regulated by Mayor's Regulation.

Article 49
(1) Women and children victims of violence are provided with spiritual guidance aimed at psychological recovery.

(2) Spiritual guidance as referred to in paragraph (1) is carried out based on the principles of:
a. Not forcing any religion and belief; and
b. Leaving the final decision in the hands of the victim/self determination

(3) Spiritual guidance is carried out by spiritual guidance officers

(4) Spiritual guidance officers as referred to in paragraph (3) consist of Consultants of the Marriage Counseling, Guidance and Preservation Agency (BP4), Officers of the Office of Religious Affairs (KUA), Ustads/Ulama, Pastors, Ministers and other religious leaders who are gender responsive.

Fourth Section
Law Enforcement and Legal Assistance Services

Article 50
(1) Law enforcement and legal assistance services are actions by law enforcement apparatus to implement statutory regulations in handling cases of violence against women and children, especially to provide sanctions to perpetrators and provide protection for witnesses and/or victims.

(2) Law enforcement and legal assistance services as referred to in paragraph (1) are carried out by prioritizing the best interests of victims.

Article 51
(1) Law enforcement and legal assistance services are aimed at providing assistance and protection to women and children victims of violence and witnesses with a gender-responsive approach so that victims obtain justice and certainty of their legal rights.

(2) Types of legal assistance services include:
a. Legal assistance in the reporting process and preparation of BAP.
b. Legal assistance in the prosecution process at the Prosecutor's Office.
c. Legal assistance in the examination process at the Court.
d. Legal assistance in the court hearing decision process until there is a final legal decision; and
e. Legal assistance in the Mediation process.
f. Legal assistance in the Diversion process.

(3) The Regional Government provides legal assistance to children and women victims of violence.

(4) In providing legal assistance as referred to in paragraph (3), the Regional Government may cooperate with Legal Aid Institutes (LBH), Women Crisis Centres (WCC), Advocates and/or other gender-responsive legal assistants.

Fifth Section
Repatriation and Social Reintegration Services

Article 52
(1) Repatriation of victims of violence is aimed at returning women and children victims of violence to their area of origin.

(2) Repatriation of victims of violence against women and children is accompanied by social reintegration efforts.

(3) The Regional Government implements repatriation and social reintegration of women and children victims of violence in accordance with statutory regulations.

I. GENERAL

Children are a trust and gift from God Almighty that we must always protect because in them are inherent dignity and rights as human beings that must be upheld. Children's human rights are part of human rights included in the 1945 Constitution and the United Nations Convention on the Rights of the Child. From the perspective of national and state life, children are the future of the nation and the next generation of the nation's ideals, so that every child has the right to survival, growth and development, participation and the right to protection from acts of violence and discrimination as well as civil rights and freedoms.

Likewise with women, women are expected to give birth to healthy, strong, resilient, noble, potential and quality children. Through the role of women in the family, society and state, resilient generations will be born who will continue the nation's ideals.

As Indonesian citizens, women also have the right to improve quality and develop themselves according to their abilities, dignity and worth.

As assets and hope of the nation and state, children need to be given protection and safeguarded from all forms of violence that will hinder their growth and development. Likewise with women, women need to be protected from discriminatory actions and from acts of violence whether physical, psychological, mental, sexual and neglect. Because of their physical weakness compared to men and adults and because of women's own dignity, women and children often become objects, outlets for desires, anger, economic exploitation, violence (physical, psychological and sexual), cruelty, neglect and so on.

To realize the protection of women and children, support is needed, statutory regulations that can guarantee its implementation.

The Convention on the Rights of the Child emphasizes that preventive efforts must be made so that children are protected from kidnapping, smuggling and sale. In following up on the Convention on the Rights of the Child, the government established Law Number 23 of 2002 concerning Child Protection.

Law Number 39 of 1999 concerning Human Rights has included Children's Rights, the implementation of obligations and responsibilities of parents, families, communities, government and the State. Child protection in all its aspects is part of National development activities, especially in advancing national and state life.

Parents, families and communities are responsible for safeguarding and maintaining these human rights in accordance with the obligations imposed by law. Likewise in the context of implementing child protection, the State, government and Regional Government are responsible for providing facilities and accessibility for children, especially in ensuring their growth and development optimally and in a directed manner.

Although the protection and rights of women and children have been regulated in national-level statutory regulations, because the fulfillment of the constitutional rights of women and children is one of the mandatory affairs that becomes the duty, authority and responsibility of the Regional Government, in providing protection to women and children, the Payakumbuh City Government needs to establish a Regional Regulation on the Protection of Women and Children.

Letter a
What is meant by Gender Responsive is that all service officers must be gender sensitive when exploring problems experienced by victims and can empower victims.

Letter b
What is meant by Non-Discrimination is that every woman and child without exception has the right to receive services related to the violence they experience, no one may be rejected or given priority over others except on the basis of certain emergency considerations.

Letter c
What is meant by equal and respectful relationships is that whoever the victim is, service provision for victims must be carried out with respect for victims without distinguishing their beliefs, values and social status.

Letter d
What is meant by maintaining confidentiality is that services must be provided in a place that guarantees the victim's privacy. Every piece of information revealed in the service provision process must be kept confidential and known only to people relevant to service provision. Officers must convey this principle to victims.

Letter e
What is meant by providing a sense of security and comfort is that service providers must ensure that victims are in a safe and comfortable state in telling their problems.

Letter f
What is meant by respecting individual differences is that each individual must be viewed as unique, each person has a different background and life experience so that one victim should not be compared with another victim in any way.

Letter g
What is meant by non-judgmental is that service providers must ensure that whatever the victim's condition or information coming from the victim will not be assessed or judged.

Letter h
What is meant by respecting the victim's choices and decisions is that service provision must be done with the victim's consent, starting from the interview process, data recording, to the handling/actions to be taken.

Letter i
What is meant by being sensitive to the victim's background and condition and using language that is appropriate and understood by the victim is that sometimes victims come from different regions or economic, educational and other backgrounds from the officers. It must be ensured that victims are served in language understood by the victim. Recently there are increasingly many disabled women who become victims of violence, whether deaf, blind or mentally retarded. For this category of victims, interpreters must also be provided, for example, who can be taken from special school teachers.

Letter j
What is meant by fast and simple is that service provision must be given immediately without unnecessary delay. Perhaps some interventions require various parties, but with the proactive nature of service officers, victims must be guaranteed to be able to go through everything with a simple process. If the victim comes on referral from another service provider, the receiving officer must first read the referral letter. Efforts should be made so that victims are not asked repeatedly about the same things related to identity or case narrative.

Letter k
What is meant by empathy is that officers must apply an attitude of empathy, namely the ability to put oneself in the position of others (in this case the victim). Thus victims feel accepted, understood and can openly tell their problems.

Letter l
What is meant by fulfillment of the rights of women and children is that victims are entitled to respect and full use of their rights to survive, development, protection, freedom from all forms of discrimination and participation, as regulated in the Convention on the Rights of the Child and the Convention on the Elimination of All Forms of Discrimination Against Women.
