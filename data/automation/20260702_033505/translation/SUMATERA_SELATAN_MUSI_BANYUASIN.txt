REGIONAL REGULATION CONCERNING PROTECTION OF RIGHTS OF WOMEN AND CHILDREN.

CHAPTER I
GENERAL PROVISIONS
Part One
Definitions, Meanings and Terminology
Article 1
In this Regional Regulation what is meant by:
1. Regency is Musi Banyuasin Regency.
2. Regency Government is the Government of Musi Banyuasin Regency.
3. Regent is the Regent of Musi Banyuasin.
4. Regional Apparatus hereinafter abbreviated as PD is the Regional Apparatus within the Government of Musi Banyuasin Regency.
5. Office of Women's Empowerment and Child Protection hereinafter abbreviated as DPPPA is the Regional Apparatus that administers the affairs of Women's Empowerment and Child Protection in Musi Banyuasin Regency.
6. Protection of Women is all efforts to guarantee and protect the rights of Women so they can live healthily and participate optimally in accordance with human dignity and receive protection from neglect, violence and discrimination, whether in the form of preventive, repressive or post-remedial protection (assistance and treatment).
7. Child Protection is all activities to guarantee and protect Children and their rights so they can live, grow, develop, and participate, optimally in accordance with human dignity, and receive protection from violence and discrimination.
8. Child is someone who has not reached 18 (eighteen) years of age, including a Child who is still in the womb.
9. Child with Disability is a Child who has physical, mental, intellectual, or sensory limitations for a long period of time which in interacting with the environment and community attitudes can encounter obstacles that make it difficult to participate fully and effectively based on equal rights.
10. Child with Excellence is a Child who has extraordinary intelligence or has potential and/or special talents not limited to intellectual abilities, but also in other fields.
11. Prevention is direct efforts carried out by the Regency Government, community and parents and/or family to prevent violations of Women's rights and Children's Rights.
12. Children's Forum is a forum for Children's participation formed with the aim of facilitating Children's involvement in decision-making processes and enjoying changes concerning their lives either directly or indirectly, which is carried out with the consent and will of all Children based on awareness and understanding, in accordance with age and level of thinking maturity.
13. Child-Friendly Regency hereinafter abbreviated as KLA is a Regency/City that has a Child rights-based development system through integration of government commitment and resources, community and business world that is planned comprehensively and sustainably in policies, programs and activities to ensure the fulfillment of Children's rights.
14. Neglected Child is a Child whose needs are not properly fulfilled, whether physical, mental, spiritual, or social.
15. Women victim of violence is a Woman who experiences actions that result in misery or suffering physically, sexually or psychologically, including threats of certain actions, coercion or arbitrary deprivation of freedom, whether occurring in the public sphere or in private life.
16. Child victim of violence is a Child who experiences actions that result in misery or suffering physically, mentally, sexually, psychologically or neglect that threatens life, body and degrades human dignity.
17. Human trafficking is the act of recruitment, transportation, harboring, sending, transfer, or receipt of a person by threat of violence, use of violence, kidnapping, confinement, forgery, fraud, abuse of power or vulnerable position, debt bondage or giving payment or benefits, so as to obtain consent from the person who holds control over that other person, whether done within the Country or between Countries, for the purpose of exploitation or resulting in the person being exploited.
18. Complaint handling is a series of actions carried out by integrated service providers to follow up on reports of acts of violence against Women and Children, submitted by victims, families or the community.
19. Social rehabilitation is services aimed at restoring and developing the abilities of someone experiencing social dysfunction so they can carry out their social functions normally.
20. Law enforcement is action by officials authorized by the state to implement statutory regulations.
21. Legal aid is legal services provided by legal assistants and advocates to conduct the process of assisting witnesses and/or victims of violence against Women and Children that is gender sensitive.
22. Repatriation is an effort to return Women and Children victims of violence from abroad to the debarkation point/entry point, or from the receiving area to the area of origin.
23. Social reintegration is an effort to reunite victims with family, substitute family, or community that can provide protection and fulfillment of needs for victims.
24. Safe house is a temporary shelter that aims to provide protection and a sense of security to victims of violence according to established standards.
25. Shelter house is a forum for guidance and welfare services for street Children that carries out activities of social assistance/guidance, maintenance, care, education, and health, skills guidance in order to ensure that Children do not carry out activities on the streets so they can grow and develop normally.
26. Spiritual guidance is an effort to restore the psyche through strengthening the religion adhered to by the victim.
27. Gender responsive is consistent and systematic attention to differences between Women and men in society accompanied by efforts to eliminate structural and cultural barriers in achieving gender equality.
28. Gender Sensitivity or Gender Awareness is the ability to recognize power relationship gaps between Women and men in the family and in the community, the impact of gender-based division of labor on Women and men, and the experiences, problems, needs, interests and aspirations of Women and men are different.
29. Public service provider is every state organizing institution, corporation, independent institution formed based on Law for public service activities, and other legal entities formed solely for public service activities.
30. Community Organization is an organization established and formed by the community voluntarily based on common aspirations, will, needs, interests, activities and goals to participate in development for the achievement of the goals of the Unitary State of the Republic of Indonesia based on Pancasila.
31. Parents are biological father and/or mother, or step father and/or mother, or adoptive father and/or mother.
32. Family is the smallest unit in society consisting of husband and wife or husband, wife and their Child, or father and his Child, or mother and her Child, or blood family in a straight line up or down to the third degree.
33. Integrated Service Center for Women's Empowerment and Children, hereinafter abbreviated as P2TP2A is an integrated service center in efforts to empower Women in various fields of development, as well as protection of Women and Children from various types of discrimination and acts of violence, including human trafficking, formed by the government or community-based.

Part Two
Definitions, Meanings and Terminology
Article 2
(1) Implementation of Protection of Women is based on principles:
a. best interests of Women;
b. gender justice and gender equality;
c. local wisdom; and
d. legal certainty.
(2) Implementation of Child Protection is based on principles:
a. non-discrimination;
b. best interests of the Child;
c. right to life, survival, and development;
d. respect for the Child's opinion; and
e. legal certainty.

Article 3
Regulation of Protection of Women and Children aims to:
a. serve as a guide for the Regency Government in planning, policies and strategies for Protection of Women and Children;
b. protect Women and Children from actions, decisions and policies that violate the rights of Women and Children;
c. prevent violations of Women's Rights and Children's Rights;
d. increase empowerment of Women and Children;
e. increase the role and value of local wisdom in Protection of Women and Children; and
f. increase the role of Regional Apparatus and non-Government in the Regency in efforts to Protect Women and Children.

Article 4
The scope of regulation of Protection of Women and Children includes:
a. prevention;
b. implementation of Protection of Women's Rights and Children's Rights;
c. handling;
d. empowerment; and
e. guidance and supervision.

CHAPTER II
WOMEN'S RIGHTS AND CHILDREN'S RIGHTS
Part One
Women's Rights
Article 5
Every Woman has the right to:
a. obtain protection from acts of discrimination;
b. obtain protection from economic exploitation and sexual exploitation;
c. obtain protection from physical, psychological, sexual violence, and neglect;
d. obtain decent work according to her abilities;
e. obtain work leave while receiving wages/salary in accordance with statutory regulations;
f. obtain education and teaching in all types, levels and paths of education;
g. obtain special protection in carrying out work or profession against things that can threaten safety and/or health related to Women's reproductive function; and
h. ownership and management of joint property in marriage in accordance with statutory provisions.

Part Two
Children's Rights
Article 6
(1) Every Child has the right to:
a. be able to live, grow, develop, and participate normally in accordance with human dignity, and receive protection from violence and discrimination;
b. obtain a name as personal identity and citizenship status;
c. worship according to their religion, think, and express themselves according to their level of intelligence and age under the guidance of Parents or Guardians;
d. obtain education and teaching in the context of personal development and level of intelligence according to interests and talents and receive protection in educational units from sexual crimes and Violence committed by educators, education personnel, fellow students, and/or other parties;
e. obtain protection from being targets of persecution, torture, or imposition of inhumane punishment;
f. obtain freedom in accordance with law and arrest, detention, or criminal imprisonment of Children can only be done if in accordance with applicable law and can only be done as a last resort;
g. be cared for by their own Parents, unless there are reasons and/or valid legal rules showing that the separation is in the best interests of the Child and is a last consideration;
h. obtain protection from abuse in political activities, involvement in armed conflict, involvement in social unrest, involvement in events containing elements of Violence, involvement in warfare and sexual crimes;
i. participate, express opinions and think according to conscience and religion;
j. receive oral or written information according to the Child's age stage and development;
k. associate and assemble; and
l. rest, play, recreate, create, and work in arts and culture, obtain play facilities that meet health and safety requirements and are adapted to the Child's age, Child's ability level, and environment so as not to hinder and disturb.
(2) Every child who is deprived of freedom has the right to:
a. receive humane treatment;
b. placement separated from adults;
c. obtain legal assistance or other assistance effectively at every stage of applicable legal efforts; and
d. defend themselves and obtain justice before an objective and impartial Children's court in closed sessions for the public.
(3) Every child who becomes a victim or perpetrator of sexual violence or who is in conflict with the law has the right to confidentiality.
(4) Every Child with Disability in addition to receiving rights as referred to in paragraph (1) also has the right to obtain:
a. special education/special education;
b. rehabilitation;
c. social assistance; and
d. maintenance of social welfare standards.
(5) Every Child who has excellence has the right to receive special education.

CHAPTER III
OBLIGATIONS AND RESPONSIBILITIES
Part One
Regency Government
Article 7
(1) The Regency Government in efforts to protect Women is obligated and responsible to:
a. guarantee Women's Rights without discrimination in accordance with statutory provisions;
b. establish and implement policies, programs and activities for protection of Women;
c. cooperate with the community and/or Community Organizations in implementing Protection of Women in accordance with statutory provisions;
d. conduct rehabilitation and empowerment of Women who become victims of violence;
e. provide special protection to Women victims of violence and human trafficking; and
f. take preventive actions in order to prevent violations of Women's rights.
(2) The responsibility of the Regency Government in efforts to protect Women's Rights as referred to in paragraph (1) is carried out by DPPPA.

Article 8
(1) The Regency Government in efforts to protect Children is obligated and responsible to:
a. respect the fulfillment of Children's rights without distinguishing ethnicity, religion, race, class, gender, ethnicity, culture and language, legal status, birth order, and physical and/or mental condition;
b. provide support for facilities, infrastructure, and availability of human resources in implementing Child Protection;
c. guarantee protection, maintenance, and welfare of Children taking into account the rights and obligations of Parents, Guardians, or other persons legally responsible for Children;
d. guarantee Children to convey their right to express opinions according to the Child's age and level of intelligence;
e. establish and implement policies and programs/activities for protection of Children's rights;
f. cooperate with the community and/or Community Organizations in implementing child protection in accordance with statutory provisions;
g. conduct rehabilitation of Children who become victims of violence;
h. provide special protection to Children:
1. in emergency situations victims of disasters and conflicts;
2. in conflict with the law;
3. victims of violence, neglect and human trafficking;
4. from minority and isolated groups;
5. who are exploited economically and/or sexually/victims of pornography;
6. who become victims of narcotics, alcohol, psychotropic abuse; and
7. identified with HIV/AIDS;
i. organize inclusive education for Children with special needs;
j. take preventive actions in order to prevent violations against Children; and
k. implement and support National policies in implementing Child Protection in the Regency carried out through efforts to realize Child-Friendly Regency, Child-Friendly Sub-district and Child-Friendly Village;
(2) The responsibility of the Regency Government in efforts to protect Children as referred to in paragraph (1) is carried out by DPPPA.

Article 9
(1) The Regency Government in order to carry out its responsibilities, establishes programs and activities for protection of Women and Children with a Regional Action Plan as a basis for Regional Apparatus in implementing protection of Women and Children.
(2) Further provisions regarding the Regional Action Plan as referred to in paragraph (1) are regulated by Regent Regulation.

Part Two
Community
Article 10
(1) The community is obligated and responsible in implementing protection of Women and Children including:
a. participation in prevention and providing support for development of prevention programs for violations of Women's and Children's rights;
b. providing information and/or reporting violations of Women's and Children's rights to authorized parties; and
c. supervising the law enforcement process for cases of violations of Women's and Children's rights.
(2) Community responsibility as referred to in paragraph (1) is in accordance with statutory provisions and local wisdom values of the local community.

Part Three
Parents and/or Family
Paragraph 1
Responsibility for Protection of Women's Rights
Article 11
Family responsibility in efforts to protect women includes:
a. protecting women from conflict and domestic violence that degrades women's dignity;
b. providing equal opportunities for women to develop themselves in the fields of:
1. education;
2. social;
3. economic; and
4. politics and culture.
c. preventing violations of women's rights in the household.

Paragraph 2
Responsibility for Protection of Children's Rights
Article 12
(1) Parents in efforts to protect Children are responsible to:
a. care for, maintain, educate, and protect Children;
b. develop Children according to abilities, talents, and interests;
c. provide formal and non-formal education;
d. guide Children to behave according to religion, ethics and morals as well as customs; and
e. prevent marriage at Child age.
(2) In the event that parents do not exist, or their whereabouts are unknown, or due to some reason, cannot carry out obligations and responsibilities, then the obligations and responsibilities as referred to in paragraph (1) may transfer to the family.

CHAPTER IV
PREVENTION
Part One
Regency Government
Article 13
(1) Efforts made by the Regency Government in order to prevent violations of Women's rights include:
a. organizing socialization regarding prevention of violence against Women by optimizing the role of customary, cultural, religious institutions and community organizations;
b. providing guidelines on handling acts of violence against Women;
c. involving community participation in recovery and reintegration;
d. developing databases and integrated information systems regarding acts of violence against Women;
e. developing family empowerment programs;
f. strengthening the function of community organizations working in the field of Women's empowerment; and
g. providing comfortable and safe public facilities for Women.
(2) Efforts made by the Regency Government in order to prevent violations of Children's rights include:
a. preventing acts of violence against Children by optimizing the role of customary, cultural, religious institutions and community organizations;
b. developing child-friendly schools;
c. encouraging increased child participation through formation of Children's Forums;
d. providing guidelines on handling acts of violence against Children;
e. involving community participation in recovery and reintegration services for child victims of violence;
f. developing databases and integrated information systems regarding acts of violence against Children;
g. strengthening the function of community organizations working in the field of child protection; and
h. encouraging the formation of Child-Friendly Regency.

Part Two
Community
Article 14
(1) Efforts made by the community in order to prevent violations of Women's rights include:
a. monitoring various conditions occurring in their environment that can cause violations of Women's rights;
b. strengthening the role of community, religious organizations and family environment; and
c. preventing unregistered marriage/siri marriage.
(2) Efforts made in order to prevent violations of Children's rights include:
a. monitoring various conditions occurring in their environment that cause violations of Children's rights;
b. strengthening the role of community, religious organizations and family environment; and
c. preventing marriage at Child age.

Part Three
Parents and/or Family
Article 15
(1) Efforts made by the family in order to prevent violations of Women's rights include:
a. protection of women from various rights violations in the household; and
b. encouraging the formation of harmonious, educative and democratic families;
(2) Efforts made by parents in order to prevent violations of Children's rights include:
a. providing good examples to children according to religious teachings and customs;
b. supervising children in activities inside or outside the home;
c. providing opportunities for children to dialogue and express opinions democratically; and
d. protecting children from various acts of violation of Children's Rights in the household.

CHAPTER V
IMPLEMENTATION OF PROTECTION OF WOMEN AND CHILDREN
Part One
Implementation of Protection of Women
Paragraph 1
General
Article 16
The Regency Government implements Protection of Women by involving:
a. family;
b. community leaders;
c. religious leaders;
d. community;
e. community organizations in the field of Women's protection.

Paragraph 2
Marriage
Article 17
During marriage or after divorce, a Woman has equal rights with the man who is her husband/former husband over all matters related to the consequences of marriage and/or divorce.

Paragraph 3
Health
Article 18
(1) The Regency Government provides adequate health and maternity facilities for pregnant and nursing mothers.
(2) Public service providers must provide special facilities for nursing mothers.

Paragraph 4
Employment
Article 19
(1) Employers who employ Women must maintain decency, safety and security while at work.
(2) Employers must place Women workers in places that are safe for Women's reproductive health.
(3) Employers must fulfill the rights of Women workers to receive wages in accordance with statutory provisions.
(4) Employers provide special facilities for nursing mothers at their companies.

Article 20
(1) Women workers/laborers aged at most 18 (eighteen) years are prohibited from being employed between 23.00 WIB until 07.00 WIB.
(2) Employers are prohibited from employing pregnant Women workers/laborers who according to doctor's statement are dangerous to the health and safety of their pregnancy or themselves during working hours from 23.00 WIB until 07.00 WIB.
(3) Employers who employ Women workers/laborers between 23.00 WIB until 07.00 WIB, must:
a. provide nutritious food and drinks; and
b. maintain decency and security while at work.
(4) Employers must provide shuttle transportation for Women workers/laborers who depart and return from work between 23.00 WIB until 05.00 WIB.

Paragraph 5
Education
Article 21
Women are guaranteed the right to obtain education at every level of formal and non-formal education.

Paragraph 6
Social
Article 22
(1) The Regency Government implements empowerment of Women experiencing socio-economic vulnerability through productive economic business activities.
(2) Community Organizations can empower Women experiencing socio-economic vulnerability as referred to in paragraph (1) in accordance with statutory provisions.

Part Two
Implementation of Protection of Children's Rights
Paragraph 1
Health
Article 23
(1) Efforts by the Regency Government to provide Child protection in the health field include:
a. free health services for Children from poor families;
b. organizing optimal health for Children to obtain good health status from the womb;
c. ensuring that babies receive exclusive breastfeeding from their mothers properly and naturally;
d. ensuring Children receive adequate nutrition;
e. ensuring newborn Children undergo screening; and
f. ensuring provision of growth and development monitoring for toddlers and pre-school age children.
(2) The Regency Government, family, and parents protect children from attempts to transplant their body organs to other parties.

Paragraph 2
Education
Article 24
Children in formal and non-formal educational institution environments must be protected from acts of violence.

Article 25
Every Child aged 7 (seven) to 18 (eighteen) years who has not completed education, exceeding 3 (three) years beyond the formal education age limit can pursue education through non-formal education units:
a. Package A equivalency program equivalent to Elementary School and Madrasah Ibtidaiyah;
b. Package B equivalency program equivalent to Junior High School and Madrasah Tsanawiyah; and
c. Package C equivalency program equivalent to Senior High School, Madrasah Aliyah and Vocational High School.

Article 26
(1) Children with special needs and/or who have potential intelligence and/or special talents are given equal opportunities and facilities to obtain regular education or inclusive education.
(2) Inclusive education as referred to in paragraph (1) is organized by the Regency Government and community.

Article 27
Every formal education provider is prohibited from expelling Children without guaranteeing the continuity of the Child's education.

Article 28
(1) The Regency Government provides scholarships and/or educational assistance for:
a. children from poor families;
b. neglected children; and
c. children residing in remote areas.
(2) Implementation of scholarship programs and/or educational assistance as referred to in paragraph (1) is supported by community participation and community organizations.
(3) Further provisions regarding provision of scholarships and or educational assistance as referred to in paragraph (1) are regulated by Regent Regulation.

Article 29
Children in conflict with the law, Children victims of violence and sexual abuse and Children victims of HIV/AIDS transmission are protected in their rights to obtain education.

Article 30
The Regency Government and community organize Early Childhood Education (PAUD).

Paragraph 3
Employment
Article 31
Employers are prohibited from employing Children.

Article 32
(1) The provisions as referred to in Article 31 may be exempted for Children aged between 13 (thirteen) years to 15 (fifteen) years, to perform light work as long as it does not interfere with physical, mental, and social development and health.
(2) Employers who employ Children in light work as referred to in paragraph (1) must meet requirements including:
a. written permission from parents or guardians;
b. work agreement between employer and parents or guardian;
c. maximum working time of 3 (three) hours;
d. carried out during the day and does not interfere with school time;
e. occupational safety and health;
f. clear employment relationship; and
g. receive wages in accordance with statutory provisions.
(3) The provisions as referred to in paragraph (2) letters a, b, f, and g are exempted for Children working in family businesses.

Article 33
In the event that a company employs Children as referred to in Article 32, then the Children's workplace must be separated from the adult workers' workplace.

Article 34
(1) Children can perform work at workplaces that are part of the education or training curriculum validated by authorized officials.
(2) Children as referred to in paragraph (1) are at least 14 (fourteen) years old.

Article 35
(1) Everyone is prohibited from employing and involving Children in the worst forms of work.
(2) The worst forms of work as referred to in paragraph (1) include:
a. all work in the form of slavery or the like;
b. all work that utilizes, provides, or offers Children for
1. prostitution;
2. pornography production;
3. pornographic performances;
4. gambling;
5. production and trade of alcoholic beverages;
6. narcotics;
7. psychotropics; and
8. other addictive substances; and/or
c. all work that endangers the health, safety, or morals of Children.

Paragraph 4
Social
Article 36
(1) The Regency Government is obligated to organize maintenance of neglected Children.
(2) Maintenance as referred to in paragraph (1) may be carried out by Community Organizations in the social field.
(3) In the event that a Child is neglected because for some reason the parents neglect their obligations, then the Family or Regency Government or Community Organization as referred to in paragraph (2) may submit an application to the court to determine the Child as a neglected Child.
(4) The court determination as referred to in paragraph (3) simultaneously determines the shelter and maintenance place for the neglected Child concerned.

Article 37
(1) The Regency Government organizes skills training for street Children and neglected Children.
(2) Skills training as referred to in paragraph (1) is organized by Regional Apparatus whose duties and functions are in the social and employment fields and can cooperate with Community Organizations.

Article 38
(1) The Regency Government provides shelter houses to guide street Children and social institutions for neglected Children.
(2) Shelter houses as referred to in paragraph (1) can be organized in cooperation with Community Organizations.
(3) Further provisions regarding shelter houses as referred to in paragraph (1) are regulated by Regent Regulation.

Article 39
Implementation of protection of Women and Children is organized by Regional Apparatus whose duties and functions are related to protection of Women and Children.

CHAPTER VI
HANDLING
Part One
Handling of Women and Children Victims of Violence
Article 40
Implementation of handling of Women and Children victims of violence includes:
a. handling complaints of victims of violence;
b. health services;
c. social rehabilitation;
d. law enforcement and legal assistance; and
e. repatriation and social reintegration.

Article 41
(1) Implementation of handling of Women and Children victims of violence as referred to in Article 40 is carried out through integrated services.
(2) Integrated services for Women and Children victims of violence as referred to in paragraph (1) use principles:
a. gender responsive;
b. non-discriminatory;
c. equal and respectful relationships;
d. maintaining confidentiality;
e. providing a sense of security and comfort;
f. respecting individual differences;
g. non-judgmental;
h. respecting victims' choices and decisions;
i. sensitive to victims' background and condition and use of appropriate language understood by victims;
j. fast and simple;
k. empathetic;
l. fulfillment of Women's and Children's rights; and
m. free of charge.

Article 42
Services for handling complaints of victims of violence against Women and Children are carried out by:
a. hospitals;
b. community health centers;
c. P2TP2A;
d. Women and Children Service Unit (UPPA);
e. Trauma Center Shelter (RPTC);
f. Child Social Shelter (RPSA);
g. Women Crisis Centre (WCC)
h. Marriage Advisory, Guidance and Preservation Board (BP4);
i. Police;
j. Prosecutor's Office;
k. Court;
l. Legal Aid Institute (LBH); and
m. Child Protection Institute (LPA).

Article 43
(1) Complaints of acts of violence against Women and Children as referred to in Article 42 can be made by reporters consisting of:
a. Victims;
b. Family;
c. Community; and/or
d. community organizations in the field of protection of Women and Children.
(2) Reporters as referred to in paragraph (1) can make complaints:
a. directly;
b. by telephone; and
c. by letter.
(3) In addition to handling complaints submitted by reporters, officers at service institutions as referred to in Article 42 are authorized to handle victims of violence against Women and Children obtained through other information.
(4) Further provisions regarding procedures for complaint handling services as referred to in paragraphs (1) and (2) are regulated by Regent Regulation.

Part Two
Health Services for Women and Children Victims of Violence
Article 44
(1) Health services for Women and Children victims of violence include aspects:
a. Promotive;
b. Preventive;
c. Curative;
d. Rehabilitative.
(2) Health services for Women and Children victims of violence are carried out
a. Comprehensively;
b. accessible 24 (twenty-four) hours;
c. quality; and
d. in accordance with health service standards.
(3) Special health services for Child victims of violence, must prioritize the best interests of the Child.
(4) Women and Children victims of violence are entitled to health services, including
a. medical services;
b. medicolegal services; and
c. psychosocial counseling.
(5) Further provisions regarding procedures for implementing health services as referred to in paragraph (4) are regulated by Regent Regulation.

Part Three
Social Rehabilitation Services for Women and Children Victims of Violence
Article 45
(1) Social rehabilitation services for Women and Children victims of violence are aimed at restoring and developing the abilities of victims experiencing social dysfunction to be able to carry out their social functions in society normally.
(2) Social rehabilitation services as referred to in paragraph (1) include:
a. counseling services;
b. provision of safe houses;
c. spiritual guidance.
(3) Implementation of services as referred to in paragraph (2) is organized by:
a. Regional Apparatus that has duties and functions in the social field;
b. DPPPA;
c. P2TP2A;
d. Women Crisis Centre (WCC);
e. community organizations in the social field; and/or
f. religious institutions.

Article 46
(1) Counseling services as referred to in Article 45 paragraph (2) letter a aim to:
a. help victims recognize their problems and find effective ways to overcome them themselves;
b. empower victims to determine their future;
c. provide moral support for victims in facing the process they are going through; and
d. make victims feel accepted in the community environment.
(2) Counseling services are carried out by gender-responsive social rehabilitation officers, consisting of:
a. counselors;
b. psychologists; and
c. psychiatrists.
(3) Counseling services are carried out in places that guarantee a sense of security, comfort and confidentiality of information from victims.
(4) Stages of counseling services include:
a. initial counseling;
b. follow-up counseling;
c. case conference; and
d. home visit.

Article 47
(1) The Regency Government may provide Safe houses for Women and Children victims of violence.
(2) Safe houses as referred to in paragraph (1) aim to protect Women and Children victims of violence from intimidation by perpetrators or other parties.
(3) The location of Safe houses as referred to in paragraph (1) must be kept confidential.
(4) Criteria for Women and Children victims of violence who need Safe houses include:
a. life threatened;
b. receiving rejection from family or community;
c. requiring intensive services but residence is relatively far; and/or
d. neglected if not placed in a safe house.
(5) Implementation of Safe house services is carried out by social rehabilitation officers who have gender sensitivity or gender awareness, consisting of:
a. social workers/volunteers;
b. security officers; and
c. community leaders.
(6) Further provisions regarding implementation of Safe house services are regulated by Regent Regulation.

Article 48
(1) Every Woman and Child victim of violence is given spiritual guidance aimed at psychological recovery.
(2) Spiritual guidance as referred to in paragraph (1) is carried out based on principles:
a. not imposing any religion or belief; and
b. leaving the final decision in the hands of the victim (self determination).
(3) Spiritual guidance as referred to in paragraph (2) is carried out by spiritual guidance officers.
(4) Spiritual guidance officers as referred to in paragraph (3) consist of:
a. Marriage Advisory, Guidance and Preservation Board (BP4) consultants;
b. Religious Affairs Office (KUA) Officers;
c. Ustad or Ulama;
d. Priests;
e. Pastors; and
f. other religious leaders who have gender sensitivity or gender awareness.

Part Four
Law Enforcement and Legal Assistance Services
Article 49
(1) Law enforcement and legal assistance services are actions by law enforcement officials provided to Women and Children victims of violence and/or witnesses.
(2) Law enforcement and legal assistance services as referred to in paragraph (1) are carried out by prioritizing the best interests of victims.

Article 50
(1) Law enforcement and legal assistance services as referred to in article 49 are aimed at providing assistance and protection to Women and Children victims of violence and witnesses with a gender-responsive approach so that victims obtain justice and certainty of legal rights.
(2) Types of legal assistance services as referred to in paragraph (1) include:
a. legal assistance in the reporting process and making of BAP;
b. legal assistance in the prosecution process at the prosecutor's office;
c. legal assistance in the examination process in court;
d. legal assistance in the court hearing decision process until there is a final legal decision; and
e. legal assistance in the mediation process.
(3) The Regency Government provides legal assistance to Children and Women victims of violence free of charge.
(4) In providing legal assistance as referred to in paragraph (3) the Regency Government can cooperate with:
a. Legal Aid Institute (LBH);
b. Women Crisis Center (WCC);
c. Advocates; and/or
d. other legal assistants who are gender sensitive.

Part Five
Repatriation and Social Reintegration Services
Article 51
(1) Repatriation of victims of violence is aimed at returning Women and Children victims of violence to their area of origin.
(2) Repatriation of victims of violence against Women and Children as referred to in paragraph (1) is accompanied by social reintegration efforts.
(3) The Regency Government implements repatriation and social reintegration of Women and Children victims of violence in accordance with statutory provisions.

CHAPTER VII
WOMEN'S EMPOWERMENT
Article 52
(1) The Regency Government guarantees the implementation of empowerment for Women.
(2) Implementation of Women's empowerment as referred to in paragraph (1) is supported by community participation and Community Organizations.

Article 53
(1) The Regency Government provides skills training to increase Women's capacity.
(2) In providing training as referred to in paragraph (1) the Regency Government can cooperate with training institutions organized by community organizations.
(3) The Regency Government expands access and opportunities for Women in obtaining skills and employment.

Article 54
(1) The Regency Government facilitates:
a. provision of business capital assistance for Women with weak economies;
b. marketing of Women's business products;
c. organizing training to develop community capabilities through economic empowerment of pre-prosperous families;
d. expanding access, information and providing knowledge to Women regarding product marketing efforts; and
e. providing comprehensive entrepreneurship training to Women.
(2) The Regency Government in facilitating activities as referred to in paragraph (1) cooperates with the community and/or community organizations.

CHAPTER VIII
CHILDREN'S PARTICIPATION
Article 55
(1) Every Child has the right to participate in expressing opinions, ideas, and thoughts related to life based on awareness and understanding, according to age and level of thinking maturity.
(2) Children's participation as referred to in paragraph (1) can be done through Children's Forums starting from village, sub-district to regency level.
(3) Children's Forums as referred to in paragraph (2) represent Children, both geographic domicile representation, socio-cultural groups and Children's educational background.
(4) Formation of Children's Forums as referred to in paragraph (2) is facilitated by the Regency Government.
(5) Formation of Children's Forums as referred to in paragraph (4) is determined by Regent Decree.

Article 56
(1) In every formulation of policies related to Children, the Regency Government pays attention to and accommodates Children's Opinions submitted through Children's Forums.
(2) The Regency Government includes representatives of Children's Forums in the Regency Development Planning Deliberation.

CHAPTER IX
CHILD-FRIENDLY REGENCY
Article 57
(1) The Regency Government conducts guidance to Sub-district Governments in developing Child-Friendly Regency.
(2) Guidance as referred to in paragraph (1) includes:
a. coordination;
b. facilitation;
c. guidance;
d. supervision;
e. consultation; and
f. education and training.
(3) Provisions regarding Child-Friendly Regency are further regulated by Regent Regulation.

CHAPTER X
GUIDANCE AND SUPERVISION
Article 58
(1) The Regent conducts guidance and supervision of implementation of Protection of Women and Children.
(2) Guidance as referred to in paragraph (1) can be carried out in the form of:
a. training for government officials and law enforcers on prevention and handling of violence;
b. training for religious leaders, community leaders, customary leaders, educators, journalists and media managers in order to prevent violence against Women and Children;
c. training for health workers who provide health services to Women and Children victims of violence at community health centers, integrated service units, and general hospitals;
d. training for social rehabilitation officers and spiritual guidance officers in order to protect Women and Children victims of violence; and
f. increasing professionalism of officers, managers of violence case data against Women and Children at community health centers, integrated service units and hospitals.
(3) The Regent in conducting guidance and supervision as referred to in paragraph (1) may delegate to the Head of Office.

Article 59
(1) The Head of DPPPA is obligated to submit reports on implementation of Women and Children protection programs to the Regent.
(2) Reports as referred to in paragraph (1) are submitted periodically 1 (one) time in 3 (three) months.

Article 60
(1) The Regent conducts supervision and evaluation of implementation of Protection of Women and Children periodically 1 (one) time in 3 (three) months.
(2) Evaluation results as referred to in paragraph (1) constitute evaluation material for Women and Children protection program policies in the Regency.

CHAPTER XI
FINANCING
Article 61
Financing for implementation of protection of Women and Children is sourced from:
a. Regional Revenue and Expenditure Budget;
b. State Revenue and Expenditure Budget; and
c. other legitimate sources in accordance with statutory provisions.

CHAPTER XII
CLOSING PROVISIONS
Article 62
Implementation regulations of this Regional Regulation are established at the latest 1 (one) year from the promulgation of this Regional Regulation.

Article 63
This Regional Regulation comes into force on the date of promulgation.
