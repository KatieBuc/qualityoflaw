REGENT OF NORTH CENTRAL TIMOR
PROVINCE OF EAST NUSA TENGGARA
REGIONAL REGULATION OF NORTH CENTRAL TIMOR REGENCY NUMBER 3 OF 2020
CONCERNING
IMPLEMENTATION OF WOMEN'S EMPOWERMENT
BY THE GRACE OF ALMIGHTY GOD
THE REGENT OF NORTH CENTRAL TIMOR,

Considering: a. that every person has equal dignity and worth to live in society, nation and state and has the right to obtain protection of human rights and basic human freedoms without discrimination as an effort to realize gender equality and justice in national and regional development;

b. that in order to realize gender equality and justice in development in North Central Timor Regency, there is a need for increased effectiveness and optimization of the implementation of women's empowerment sub-affairs as an inseparable part of the functional activities of government and non-government institutions in order to improve the position, role and quality of women in development in North Central Timor Regency;

c. that to guarantee legal certainty and to fill the legal vacuum in order to realize gender equality and justice in development in North Central Timor Regency, there is a need for regulation in a Regional Regulation concerning the implementation of women's empowerment;

d. that based on the considerations as referred to in letter a, letter b, and letter c, it is necessary to establish a Regional Regulation concerning the Implementation of Women's Empowerment;

In View of: 1 Article 18 paragraph (6) of the 1945 Constitution of the Republic of Indonesia;

2. Law Number 69 of 1958 concerning the Establishment of Level II Regions within the Level I Regions of Bali, West Nusa Tenggara and East Nusa Tenggara (State Gazette of the Republic of Indonesia Year 1958 Number 122 Supplement to the State Gazette of the Republic of Indonesia Number 1655);

3. Law Number 23 of 2014 concerning Regional Government (State Gazette of the Republic of Indonesia Year 2014 Number 244, Supplement to the State Gazette of the Republic of Indonesia Number 5587); As has been amended several times most recently by Law Number 9 of 2015 concerning the Second Amendment to Law Number 23 of 2014 concerning Regional Government (State Gazette of the Republic of Indonesia Year 2015 Number 58 Supplement to the State Gazette of the Republic of Indonesia Number 5679);

With Joint Approval:
THE REGIONAL HOUSE OF REPRESENTATIVES OF NORTH CENTRAL TIMOR REGENCY and
THE REGENT OF NORTH CENTRAL TIMOR

DECIDES:

To Establish: REGIONAL REGULATION CONCERNING THE IMPLEMENTATION OF WOMEN'S EMPOWERMENT.

CHAPTER I
GENERAL PROVISIONS

Article 1

In this Regional Regulation, what is meant by:
1. Region is North Central Timor Regency.
2. Regional Government is the Government of North Central Timor Regency.
3. Regent is the Regent of North Central Timor.
4. Regional House of Representatives hereinafter abbreviated as DPRD is the Regional House of Representatives of North Central Timor Regency.
5. Regional secretary is the Regional Secretary of North Central Timor Regency.
6. Agency is the Regional Apparatus that administers governmental affairs in the field of women's empowerment and Child Protection.
7. Head of agency is the Head of Regional Apparatus that administers governmental affairs in the field of women's empowerment.
8. Regional Apparatus hereinafter abbreviated as PD is the Regional Apparatus in North Central Timor Regency.
9. Head of Regional Apparatus is the Head of Regional Apparatus in North Central Timor Regency.
10. Board is the Regional Apparatus that administers Government affairs in the field of development planning and regional research.
11. Head of Board is the Regional Apparatus that administers Government affairs in the field of planning.
12. Women's Empowerment is every effort to improve physical, mental spiritual, social capacity, knowledge, and skills so that women are ready to be utilized according to their respective abilities.
13. Gender Mainstreaming, hereinafter abbreviated as PUG is a strategy built to integrate gender into an integral dimension of planning, implementation, monitoring and evaluation of regional development policies and programs.
14. Gender is a concept that refers to the roles and responsibilities of men and women that occur as a result of social construction and can change due to social and cultural circumstances of society.
15. Gender Equality is the equality of conditions for men and women to obtain opportunities and their rights as human beings so they are able to play a role and participate in the development process.
16. Gender Justice is a process to be fair to men and women.
17. Gender Mainstreaming Working Group, hereinafter referred to as Pokja PUG is a consultation forum for implementers and drivers of gender mainstreaming from various agencies/institutions in the region.
18. Gender Mainstreaming Focal Point, hereinafter referred to as Focal Point PUG is PD apparatus who have the ability to conduct gender mainstreaming in their respective work units.
19. Regional Medium-Term Development Plan, hereinafter abbreviated as RPJMD is the elaboration of the vision, mission and program of the Regent whose preparation is guided by the Regional Long-Term Development Plan, which contains regional development strategies, general regional policies, regional programs and macroeconomic framework that includes an overview of the economy as a whole including the direction of fiscal policy in a work plan in the form of a regulatory framework and indicative funding framework.
20. Gender Analysis is a process built systematically to identify and understand the division of labor or roles of men and women, access and control over development resources, participation in development and the benefits they enjoy.
21. Gender Responsive Budget is a performance-based budget allocation for certain programs or activities whose input, output, and outcome indicators can guarantee the fulfillment of the aspirations and needs of women and men fairly.
22. Communication, Information and Education hereinafter abbreviated as KIE is the delivery of messages in the form of information, ideas or facts related to gender mainstreaming that need to be known by the community directly or indirectly through communication channels to message recipients to obtain effects, as a process of behavioral change towards gender responsiveness.
23. Village/Kelurahan Development Planning Deliberation hereinafter referred to as Musrembangkel is a forum for annual village/kelurahan development planning deliberation involving development actors in the Village/kelurahan.
24. Sub-district Development Planning Deliberation hereinafter referred to as Musrembangcam is a deliberation forum among development actors to discuss and agree on steps to handle priority program activities listed in the list of proposed village development planning activities integrated with development priorities of North Central Timor Regency in the sub-district area coordinated by the Regional Development Planning Board and implemented by the Sub-district Head.
25. Development planning deliberation for the preparation of the Regional Medium-Term Development Plan hereinafter referred to as Musrembang RPJMD is a deliberation forum among stakeholders to discuss, sharpen and agree on the draft Regional Medium-Term Development Plan.
26. Strategic Plan hereinafter referred to as Renstra is a five-year planning document containing the vision, mission, objectives, strategies, policies, programs and development activities in accordance with the duties and functions of the organization as an elaboration of the RPJMD.
27. Work Plan hereinafter referred to as Renja is a one-year planning document containing development policies, programs, and activities both planned directly by the Regional Apparatus and pursued by encouraging community participation as an elaboration of the Renstra.
28. Regional Government Work Plan hereinafter abbreviated as RKPD is a one-year planning document containing development policies, programs, and activities both planned directly by the Regional Government and pursued by encouraging community participation as an elaboration of the Renstra.
29. Regional Action Plan hereinafter abbreviated as RAD is an activity planning document prepared by a technical team to be implemented in a certain period/time.
30. Gender Analysis Pathway hereinafter abbreviated as GAP is a gender analysis tool that can be used to help planners in mainstreaming gender in planning development policies/programs/activities with the aim of identifying gender gaps and gender issues as well as simultaneously preparing policy/program/activity plans aimed at reducing or eliminating such gender gaps.
31. Technical Team is a Team formed by pokja PUG with the task of conducting gender perspective budget analysis and preparing RAD PUG.

CHAPTER II
SCOPE

Article 2

The scope of implementation of Women's Empowerment includes:
a. quality of life of women;
b. protection of women;
c. quality of family; and
d. gender data system.

CHAPTER III
QUALITY OF LIFE OF WOMEN

Part One
General

Article 3

Quality of Life of Women includes:
a. institutionalization of PUG;
b. Women's Empowerment in the political, legal, social and economic fields in community organizations; and
c. strengthening and development of women's empowerment service provider institutions.

Part Two
Institutionalization of PUG

Paragraph 1
General

Article 4

Institutionalization of PUG includes:
a. appointment of PUG coordinator;
b. pokja PUG;
c. Focal Point PUG;
d. Budgeting;
e. gender-based development planning;
f. monitoring and evaluation;
g. reporting; and
h. supervision.

Paragraph 2
PUG Coordinator

Article 5

(1) The Regent has the authority to appoint the Head of PD who administers governmental affairs in the field of Women's Empowerment as coordinator of PUG implementation.
(2) The appointment as referred to in paragraph (1) shall be by Regent's Decree.

Paragraph 3
Pokja PUG

Article 6

(1) The Regent has the authority to form Pokja PUG.
(2) The formation of Pokja PUG as referred to in paragraph (1) shall be stipulated by Regent's Decree.
(3) The Regent's Decree as referred to in paragraph (2) shall be valid for 5 (five) years.
(4) The management of Pokja PUG consists of:
a. chairman by the head of the board;
b. secretariat by the head of the agency;
c. members include:
1. head of PD;
2. technical team consisting of:
a) apparatus who have attended training or education in gender perspective budget analysis of at most 5 (five) people.
b) representatives of university elements of at most 5 (five) people;
c) press representatives of at most 5 (five) people; and
d) representatives of non-governmental organizations that have a scope of orientation in the field of gender of at most 5 (five) people.

Article 7

The duties of Pokja PUG include:
a. promoting and facilitating dialogue between work units within the scope of regional government;
b. developing work networks in accordance with the main tasks, functions and authorities given by the leadership in an effort to realize gender equality and justice;
c. assigning the technical team to prepare RAD PUG in order to realize gender equality and justice adjusted to the periodization of RPJMD;
d. elaborating and following up on policies and implementation programs to be gender responsive that are implicit and explicit in the RPJMD;
e. coordinating the formation of Focal Point PUG in each PD or work unit;
f. promoting PUG in each PD;
g. strengthening the capacity of Focal Point PUG in preparing gender responsive budgets by the technical team;
h. overseeing the preparation of gender responsive planning and budgeting in their respective PDs;
i. including gender indicators in the guidelines for preparing the APBD;
j. ensuring that gender responsive budgets become a consideration in the discussion and determination of the APBD together with the DPRD;
k. holding coordination meetings every 6 (six) months to ensure the implementation of PUG in the Region;
l. making and submitting reports on the implementation of PUG to the regent every 6 (six) months; and
m. holding coordination meetings before the preparation of RKPD.

Paragraph 4
Focal Point PUG

Article 8

(1) The Regional Secretary has the authority to form Focal Point PUG within the Regional Secretariat.
(2) The formation of Focal Point PUG as referred to in paragraph (1) shall be stipulated by the Decree of the Regional Secretary.
(3) The management of Focal Point PUG as referred to in paragraph (1) consists of:
a. person in charge by the regional secretary;
b. coordinator by echelon IIB official who oversees governmental affairs in the field of law;
c. vice coordinator by echelon IIIA official of each section in the regional secretariat except echelon IIB official as referred to in letter b; and
d. members by representatives of officials and/or staff from all sections in the regional secretariat.

Article 9

(1) The Head of PD must form Focal Point PUG PD.
(2) The formation of Focal Point PUG as referred to in paragraph (1) shall be by Decree of the Head of PD.
(3) The management of Focal Point PUG as referred to in paragraph (1) consists of:
a. person in charge by the head of PD;
b. coordinator by echelon III A official who oversees planning affairs at the PD;
c. Vice Coordinator by Echelon IIIB Official who has competence in PUG; and
d. Members by representatives of Officials or staff from fields and secretariat at the PD.

Article 10

(1) The Hospital Director has the authority to form Focal Point PUG Hospital.
(2) The formation of Focal Point PUG as referred to in paragraph (1) shall be stipulated by the decree of the Hospital Director.
(3) The management of Focal Point PUG as referred to in paragraph (1) consists of:
a. person in charge by the hospital director;
b. coordinator by echelon IIIB official who oversees hospital planning;
c. vice coordinator by echelon IVA official who has competence in PUG; and
d. members by representatives of officials or staff in fields or sections in the hospital.

Article 11

(1) The Head of the Community Health Center has the authority to form Focal Point PUG Puskesmas.
(2) The formation of Focal Point PUG as referred to in paragraph (1) shall be stipulated by the Decree of the Head of the Community Health Center.
(3) The management of Focal Point PUG puskesmas consists of:
a. person in charge by the Head of Puskesmas;
b. coordinator by echelon IV B official who oversees Puskesmas planning; and
c. members consisting of Puskesmas staff.

Article 12

The duties of Focal Point PUG include:
a. assisting policy makers of work units within the scope of duties, main tasks and functions of their agencies to take planned steps fully if there are gender gaps;
b. encouraging and assisting work units to evaluate and improve mandates, policies, programs, projects, activities and budgets to be more gender responsive;
c. facilitating the implementation of gender sensitivity training, gender analysis training and developing gender work networks with agencies or institutions or organizations and other work units, both government and non-government;
d. seeking the implementation of gender analysis as one of the stages in every development process starting from the planning, implementation, monitoring and evaluation stages;
e. elaborating and following up on policies and development implementation programs to be gender responsive that are implicit in the PD Renstra, PD Renja and RKPD;
f. participating in various activities organized by Pokja PUG and/or national PUG working groups; and
g. making periodic activity reports and reporting to the regent through the chairman of pokja PUG.

Paragraph 5
Gender-Based Regional Development Planning

Article 13

(1) The Regional Government is responsible for preparing planning for Gender perspective development policies, programs and activities.
(2) The responsibility as referred to in paragraph (1) shall be coordinated by the PD that administers governmental affairs in the field of development planning, research and Regional development.
(3) Planning as referred to in paragraph (1) includes stages:
a. Musrenbangdus;
b. Musrembangdes/kel;
c. Musrembangcam;
d. Musrenbang at the regional level;
e. preparation of RPJMD;
f. preparation of PD Renstra;
g. preparation of PD Renja; and
h. RKPD;
i. preparation of RAPBD;
(4) Planning as referred to in paragraph (1) shall be carried out through Gender analysis using the Gender Analysis Pathway method or other analytical methods.
(5) Implementation of Gender analysis of RPJMD and PD Renstra may cooperate with Higher Education institutions or other parties who have capability in their field.

Article 14

(1) The Board is responsible for preparing guidelines for gender-based development planning.
(2) The guidelines as referred to in paragraph (1) must be used in facilitating:
a. Musrenbangdus;
b. Musrembangdes/kel;
c. Musrembangcam;
d. Musrenbang at the regional level;
e. preparation of RPJMD;
f. preparation of PD Renstra; and
g. preparation of PD Renja,
that are gender responsive.
(3) The preparation of guidelines as referred to in paragraph (1) may cooperate with the Pokja PUG Technical Team.

Article 15

(1) The Gender Analysis Pathway method as referred to in Article 13 paragraph (4) is used as a way to make planning in each PD.
(2) The method as referred to in paragraph (1) includes:
a. stage I analysis of gender responsive policies;
b. stage II formulation of gender responsive policies;
c. stage III gender responsive action plan;
d. stage IV implementation of activities that have been prepared; and
e. stage V monitoring and evaluation of each stage and step taken.

Paragraph 6
Budgeting

Article 16

(1) The Regional Government must allocate a budget for the implementation of PUG in the Region.
(2) The implementation budget as referred to in paragraph (1) shall be sourced from:
a. APBD; and
b. non-binding assistance from other parties, as long as it is in accordance with the provisions of applicable laws and regulations.
(3) The budget as referred to in paragraph (1) and paragraph (2), shall be used to finance the activities of Pokja PUG and Focal Point PUG PD in integrating Gender at the level of planning, preparation, implementation, monitoring and evaluation of policies and development in the Region.

Paragraph 7
Monitoring and Evaluation

Article 17

(1) The Regent has the authority to monitor and evaluate the implementation of PUG.
(2) The Regent may delegate the implementation of monitoring and evaluation to Pokja PUG.
(3) Monitoring and evaluation shall be carried out on each PD and/or other institutions that implement PUG efforts.
(4) Monitoring and evaluation shall be carried out at least every 6 (six) months.

Article 18

Monitoring of PUG implementation shall be carried out through:
a. monitoring the preparation of gender responsive development planning documents both at the PD level and at the regional level;
b. monitoring RAD PUG; and
c. verification of RAD PUG implementation Reports.

Article 19

Evaluation of PUG implementation shall be carried out through:
a. evaluation of achievements and challenges in implementing RAD PUG;
b. preparation of RAD PUG for the next period;
c. evaluation of achievements and challenges in implementing PUG every 6 (six) months; and
d. preparation of PUG implementation plan for the next 6 (six) months.

Paragraph 8
PUG Reporting

Article 20

(1) Pokja PUG is responsible for preparing PUG Implementation Reports every 6 (six) months and RAD PUG Implementation Reports every five years.
(2) The reporting framework as referred to in paragraph (1) shall be guided by the reporting framework as referred to in the laws and regulations concerning General Guidelines for PUG Implementation in Regions.
(3) The Pokja PUG report as referred to in paragraph (1) shall be reported to the Regent.
(4) The Regent is responsible for examining and stating approval of the Pokja PUG Report as referred to in paragraph (1).
(5) The Regent is responsible for sending the pokja PUG report as referred to in paragraph (1) to the Minister who administers Home Affairs.

Paragraph 9
Supervision

Article 21

(1) The Regent is responsible for supervising the implementation of PUG.
(2) Supervision as referred to in paragraph (1) shall be in the form of:
a. Gender responsive planning and budgeting carried out by the regional government budget team;
b. implementation of coordination between Pokja PUG and Focal Point gender; and
c. progress in achieving RAD PUG every year.
(3) The responsibility as referred to in paragraph (1) may be technically and operationally carried out by the Chairman of Pokja PUG and the Head of the Pokja PUG Secretariat.
(4) The implementation of the responsibility as referred to in paragraph (3) shall be reported to the Regent.

Part Three
Women's Empowerment in the Political, Legal, Social Cultural and Economic Fields in Community Organizations

Paragraph 1
General

Article 22

Efforts to Empower Women in the political, legal, social cultural and economic fields in community organizations include stages:
a. planning;
b. implementation; and
c. reporting and evaluation.

Paragraph 2
Planning

Article 23

(1) The Board is responsible for facilitating the planning of Women's Empowerment in the political, legal, social cultural and economic fields in community organizations.
(2) Planning as referred to in paragraph (1) shall be carried out in an integrated manner in development planning deliberations based on gender analysis pathway instruments.

Paragraph 3
Implementation

Article 24

The implementation of Women's Empowerment in community organizations shall be carried out by PD in accordance with the main tasks and functions carried out in accordance with regional financial capacity.

Article 25

(1) The PD that administers governmental affairs in the field of national unity and politics is responsible for facilitating women's empowerment in the political field.
(2) Empowerment as referred to in paragraph (1) includes:
a. implementing education and training for women in order to improve competence especially in the political field; and
b. encouraging women's representation in every management of community organizations.

Article 26

(1) The PD that administers governmental affairs in the legal field is responsible for facilitating Women's Empowerment in the legal field.
(2) The implementation of Women's Empowerment in the legal field includes:
a. increasing awareness and knowledge in the legal field through communication information and education services; and
b. facilitation of access and legal consultation services.

Article 27

(1) The PD that administers governmental affairs in the social field is responsible for facilitating Women's Empowerment in the social cultural field.
(2) Women's Empowerment in the social cultural field through:
a. increasing knowledge, attitudes and skills to encourage the fulfillment of tiered education according to potential to improve social status;
b. increasing awareness, willingness and ability to overcome health problems through quality promotive, preventive, curative, and rehabilitative efforts, especially in the field of reproductive health;
c. increasing awareness and knowledge about independent, healthy and prosperous family planning including access to consultation services and marriage registration; and
d. facilitation and efforts to preserve customs and develop science, technology, arts and culture for the advancement of women.

Article 28

(1) The PD that administers governmental affairs in the field of cooperatives, small and medium enterprises and industrial trade is responsible for facilitating Women's Empowerment in the economic field.
(2) The implementation of Women's Empowerment in the economic field shall be carried out through:
a. providing skills and job training;
b. facilitating the formation of productive economic business groups;
c. facilitating the strengthening and development of productive economic business groups;
d. facilitating and providing capital assistance; and
e. facilitating the development of marketing networks.

Paragraph 4
Reporting and Evaluation

Article 29

(1) Each PD is responsible for making reports on the implementation of Women's Empowerment activities that have been carried out to the Regent through the Agency.
(2) The Agency after receiving the report submitted by the PD shall evaluate the implementation of activities.
(3) The results of the evaluation of the implementation of activities as referred to in paragraph (2) shall be reported to the Regent.
(4) The evaluation results as referred to in paragraph (3) shall be used for budget planning for the following year.

b. conducting socialization on women's rights;
c. seeking to improve education for women;
d. providing counseling to immediately report to local authorities if Violence against women occurs;
e. empowering social community organizations and women's organizations to increase women's knowledge and insight; and

a. improving the competence of service institution members; and/or

(3) Development of Service Institutions as referred to in paragraph (1) letter b shall be carried out through:

I. GENERAL

The presence of Law Number 23 of 2014 concerning Regional Government as has been amended several times, most recently by Law Number 9 of 2015 concerning the Second Amendment to Law Number 23 of 2014 concerning Regional Government and Law Number 33 of 2004 concerning Financial Balance Between the Central Government and Regional Governments is an entry point for Women's Empowerment in regional development. Women's empowerment is the right development strategy to realize gender equality and justice through gender-sensitive and gender-responsive development policies and programs in the future, especially in North Central Timor Regency.

In North Central Timor Regency there are several conditions that need to be intervened with the formation of a Regional Regulation on Women's Empowerment. This occurs because there is much discrimination against women in developing themselves and opportunities for the fulfillment of their rights. Women are only considered as second-class people. Their task is to take care of the household and do not need to work. This is what needs to be fought, this stigma becomes a problem that must be solved with empowerment programs.

The above paradigm is reflected in the data on the existence of women working in Government, data shows that women are not in strategic positions to participate in making decisions or policies, especially in Government affairs through ASN data based on gender available in the field.

Based on this thinking, it is time for North Central Timor Regency to form a Regional Regulation on Women's Empowerment in North Central Timor Regency. This Regional Regulation will regulate the implementation of gender mainstreaming clearly, firmly, balanced and comprehensively to realize gender equality and justice as well as provide understanding about the importance of women's empowerment as a gender analysis strategy in planning, preparation, implementation, monitoring and evaluation of development policies and programs.
