DECIDES:

To Establish: REGIONAL REGULATION CONCERNING PROTECTION OF WOMEN AND CHILDREN.

CHAPTER I
GENERAL PROVISIONS

Article 1

In this Regional Regulation, what is meant by:
1. Regency is Muara Enim Regency.
2. Regency Government is Muara Enim Regency.
3. Regent is the Regent of Muara Enim.
4. Regional Apparatus is the Regional Apparatus within the Muara Enim Regency Government.
5. Women Empowerment is an effort to strengthen Protection of human rights, improve quality of life, and increase women's participation in development.

(4)

6. Protection of Women is all activities aimed at providing a sense of security carried out by the Regional Government, police, prosecutors, courts, social institutions, or other parties who know or hear that violence against women will or has occurred;

7. Child Protection is all activities to guarantee and protect Children and their rights so they can live, grow, develop, and participate optimally in accordance with human dignity and dignity, and receive Protection from Violence and discrimination.

7. Child is someone who has not reached the age of 18 (eighteen) years, including Children still in the womb and not yet married.

8. Prevention is direct efforts carried out by the Regional Government, community and parents and/or family to prevent violations of the rights of women and Children.

9. Violence is any act that results in misery or suffering either physically, sexually, psychologically and/or domestic neglect including threats to commit acts, coercion, or deprivation of a person's freedom unlawfully.

10. Children's Forum is a forum for Child participation formed with the aim of facilitating the involvement of Children in decision-making processes and enjoying changes concerning their lives either directly or indirectly, which is carried out with the approval and will of all Children based on awareness and understanding, in accordance with age and level of thinking maturity.

11. Child-Friendly Regency hereinafter abbreviated as KLA is a Regency that has a child rights-based development system through integration of government commitment and resources, community and business world that is planned comprehensively and sustainably in policies, programs and activities to ensure fulfillment of Children's rights.

12. Gender Mainstreaming is a strategy built to integrate gender into an integral dimension of planning, formulation, implementation, monitoring and evaluation of national development program policies.

(5)

13. Community is individuals, families, groups and social organizations and/or community organizations.

14. Women Victims of Violence are women who experience actions that result in misery or suffering physically, sexually or psychologically, including threats of certain actions, coercion or deprivation of freedom arbitrarily, whether occurring in the public sphere or in private life.

15. Child Victims of Violence are Children who experience actions that result in misery or suffering physically, mentally, sexually, psychologically or neglect that threatens life, body and degrades human dignity.

16. Human trafficking is the act of recruiting, transporting, harboring, sending, transferring, or receiving a person by threat of Violence, use of Violence, kidnapping, confinement, forgery, fraud, abuse of power or vulnerable position, debt bondage or giving payment or benefits, so as to obtain consent from the person holding control over that other person, whether carried out within the Country or between Countries, for the purpose of exploitation or resulting in the person being exploited.

17. Complaint handling is a series of actions carried out by integrated service providers to follow up on reports of acts of Violence against women and Children, submitted by victims, families or the community.

18. Social rehabilitation is services aimed at restoring and developing the ability of a person experiencing social dysfunction to be able to carry out social functions normally.

19. Law enforcement is the action of apparatus authorized by the state to implement statutory regulations.

20. Legal aid is legal services provided by legal assistants and advocates to carry out the process of assisting witnesses and/or victims of Violence against women and Children that is gender sensitive.

(6)

21. Repatriation is an effort to return women and Child victims of Violence from abroad to the debarkation/entry point, or from the receiving Region to the region of origin.

22. Social reintegration is an effort to reunite victims with family, substitute family, or community that can provide Protection and fulfillment of needs for victims.

23. Safe house is a temporary shelter that aims to provide Protection and a sense of security to victims of Violence.

24. Shelter house is a forum for guidance and welfare services for street children that carries out assistance/social guidance activities, maintenance, care, education, and health, skills guidance in order to ensure that Children do not carry out activities on the streets so they can grow and develop normally.

25. Spiritual Guidance is an effort to restore the soul through strengthening the religion adhered to by the victim.

26. Gender Mainstreaming is a strategy built to integrate gender into an integral dimension of planning, formulation, implementation, monitoring, and evaluation of policies, programs, and development activities in the Region.

27. Gender responsive is consistent and systematic attention to the differences between women and men in society accompanied by efforts to eliminate structural and cultural barriers in achieving gender equality.

28. Gender Sensitivity or Gender Awareness is the ability to recognize gaps in power relations between women and men in families and in communities, the impact of gender-based division of labor on women and men, and the experiences, problems, needs, interests and aspirations of women and men are different.

(7)

29. Public Service Provider is every state organizing institution, corporation, independent institution formed based on Law for public service activities, and other legal entities formed solely for public service activities.

30. Community Organization is an organization established and formed by the community voluntarily based on similarity of aspirations, will, needs, interests, activities and goals to participate in development for the achievement of the goals of the Unitary State of the Republic of Indonesia based on Pancasila.

31. Parents are biological father and/or mother, or stepfather and/or stepmother, or adoptive father and/or adoptive mother.

32. Family is the smallest unit in society consisting of husband and wife or husband, wife and their Children, or father and his Children, or mother and her Children, or blood family in a straight line up or down to the third degree.

33. Customs are eternal conduct patterns passed down from one generation to another as heritage, so that they are strongly integrated with community behavior patterns.

32. Best interests are the best interests for women and children.

Article 2

Implementation of Protection of Women and Children is based on the principles of:

a. best interests for women and Children;
b. gender justice and gender equality;
c. local wisdom;
d. law enforcement and legal certainty.
e. non-discrimination;
f. right to life, survival, and development;
g. respect for the Child's opinion; and
h. law enforcement and legal certainty.

Article 3

This Regional Regulation aims to:

a. guarantee the fulfillment of the rights of women and Children in the Regency;

(8)

b. serve as a guide for the Region in planning, policies and strategies for Protection of Women and Children;

c. protect and prevent women and Children from actions, decisions and policies that violate the rights of women and Children;

d. increase the role and value of local wisdom and the role of customs in Protection of Women and Children;

e. serve as a legal umbrella for regional apparatus in charge of protection of women and children in order to provide assistance and support to women and child victims.

Article 4

The scope of regulation of Protection of Women and Children includes:

a. protection of women;
b. protection of Children;
c. fulfillment of Children's rights;
d. improvement of women's quality of life;
e. gender and Child data system, disaggregated data on women and children, and strengthening of Gender Mainstreaming institutions through Gender Responsive Planning and Budgeting;
f. community participation; and
g. guidance, supervision, assistance.

CHAPTER II
IMPROVEMENT OF WOMEN'S QUALITY OF LIFE

Part One
General

Article 5

(1) In order to improve the quality of women's lives, the regional government is responsible for:

a. respecting and guaranteeing women's rights without discrimination in accordance with human dignity;
b. implementing Gender Mainstreaming in the Regency;
c. forming a Gender Mainstreaming working group in the Region;
d. increasing Women's Empowerment in roles, quality and capacity in the fields of

(9)

1. politics;
2. law;
3. social;
4. economy; and
5. culture.

e. strengthening and developing Women's Empowerment service provider institutions in the Regency.

(2) The responsibility of the Regency Government in efforts to improve the quality of women's lives as referred to in paragraph (1) is carried out by regional apparatus in accordance with their duties and functions.

Part Two
Increasing the Role, Quality and
Capacity of Women in the Political Field

Article 6

Efforts to increase the role, quality and capacity of women in the political field as referred to in Article 5 paragraph (1) letter d number 1 include:

a. conducting socialization to increase women's political participation in general elections;
b. implementing programs that encourage the level of women's involvement in political processes and public positions;
c. facilitating women's self-development in organizing, associating and assembling; and
d. involving women in decision-making concerning women's interests.

Part Three
Increasing the Role, Quality, and
Capacity of Women in the Legal Field

Article 7

Efforts to increase the role, quality and capacity of women in the legal field as referred to in Article 5 paragraph (1) letter d number 2 include:

a. conducting socialization to increase women's legal knowledge and awareness;

(10)

b. mapping and reviewing gender-biased policies; and
c. involving women's organizations in the process of formulating and discussing Regional legal products related to women's participation.

Part Four
Increasing the Role, Quality, and Capacity of
Women in the Social Field

Article 8

Efforts to increase the role, quality and capacity of women in the social field as referred to in Article 5 paragraph (1) letter d number 3 include:

a. increasing knowledge and skills to encourage fulfillment of education according to potential possessed;
b. increasing and fulfilling quality of health for women;
c. increasing awareness and knowledge about planning for independent, healthy and prosperous families including access to consultation services and marriage registration;
d. increasing women's awareness of the impact of early marriage;
e. facilitating and seeking preservation of customs and development of science, technology, arts and culture for women's advancement;
f. increasing women's awareness and knowledge of planning for independent, healthy and prosperous families;
g. implementing programs to reduce maternal mortality rates; and
h. encouraging the formation of women's organizations in the Regency.

Part Five
Increasing the Role, Quality, and
Capacity of Women in the Economic Field

Article 9

Efforts to increase the role, quality and capacity of women in the economic field as referred to in Article 5 paragraph (1) letter d number 4 include:

(11)

a. implementing programs, activities, training to improve women's skills and expertise in order to realize improvement of women's welfare;
b. facilitating strengthening of business capital for women with weak economies;
c. marketing products from businesses of women with weak economies;
d. training to develop women's capacity through economic empowerment of pre-prosperous families; and
e. comprehensive entrepreneurship training for women.

CHAPTER III
PROTECTION OF WOMEN

Part One
General

Article 10

(1) The Regency Government in efforts to protect women is responsible for:

a. establishing and implementing policies, programs and activities for protection of women;
b. making efforts to prevent acts of Violence against women including the crime of human trafficking;
c. increasing the effectiveness of services for women victims of acts of Violence;
d. increasing protection efforts for women workers;
e. strengthening and developing women's protection service provider institutions;
f. cooperating with the Government, other Regional Governments, private parties, business world and/or Non-Governmental Organizations in efforts to prevent acts of Violence against women;
g. involving the active role of families, customary leaders, religious leaders, communities, and institutions in the field of women's protection.

(12)

(2) The responsibility of the Regency Government in efforts to protect women as referred to in paragraph (1) is carried out by regional apparatus in accordance with their duties and functions and can coordinate with relevant agencies.

Part Two
Prevention of Acts of Violence Against Women

Article 11

In order to prevent acts of Violence against women, the Regency Government:

a. conducts socialization regarding prevention of violence against women by optimizing the role of customary, cultural, religious and community organizations;
b. builds networks and cooperation with law enforcement apparatus, government apparatus, universities and various Non-Governmental Organizations that work and/or care about women and child victims of Violence;
c. provides comfortable and safe public facilities for women;
d. forms Regional Technical Service Unit for Protection of Women and Children and provides secretariat for Regional Technical Service Unit for Protection of Women and Children.

Part Three
Effectiveness of Services for Women Victims of Acts of Violence

Paragraph 1
General

Article 12

(1) Protection of women victims of acts of Violence is carried out through integrated services.

(2) Provision of integrated services to women victims of acts of Violence as referred to in paragraph (1) uses the principles of:

a. gender responsive;
b. non-discrimination;

(13)

c. equal and respectful relationships;
d. maintaining confidentiality;
e. providing a sense of security and comfort;
f. respecting individual differences;
g. non-judgmental;
h. respecting the victim's choices and decisions;
i. sensitive to the victim's background and condition and use of language that is appropriate and understood by the victim;
j. fast and simple;
k. empathy; and
l. fulfillment of women's rights.

Article 13

Forms of integrated services for women victims of acts of violence include:

a. complaint handling;
b. health services;
c. social rehabilitation;
d. victim assistance, law enforcement and legal aid; and
e. repatriation and social reintegration.

Paragraph 2
Complaint Handling

Article 14

Integrated complaint handling services as referred to in Article 13 letter a are carried out by:

a. Hospitals, Community Health Centers and other health service facilities;
b. Technical Implementation Unit for Protection of Women and Children;
c. Trauma Center Protection House,
d. Women crisis centre;
e. Advisory Board for Marriage Guidance and Preservation;
f. Police;
g. Prosecutor's Office;
h. Court;
i. Ministry of Religious Affairs; and/or
j. Legal Aid Institute.

(14)

Article 15

(1) Complaints of acts of violence against women can be made by reporters consisting of:

a. victims;
b. family;
c. community; and/or
d. community organizations in the field of protection of women and children.

(2) Reporters as referred to in paragraph (1) can make complaints:

a. directly;
b. by telephone; and/or
c. by letter.

(3) In addition to handling complaints submitted by reporters, the agency in charge of Protection of Women and Children, officers at service institutions as referred to in Article 13, are also authorized to provide assistance in handling victims of acts of violence against women obtained through other information.

(4) Further provisions regarding procedures for complaint handling services as referred to in paragraph (1) and paragraph (2) are regulated by Regent Regulation.

Paragraph 3
Health Rehabilitation

Article 16

(1) Health rehabilitation as referred to in Article 13 letter b is an effort that includes aspects of:

a. promotive;
b. preventive;
c. curative; and
d. rehabilitative.

(2) Health rehabilitation as referred to in paragraph (1) is carried out comprehensively, can be accessed 24 (twenty-four) hours, is of quality and is carried out in accordance with health service standards.

(15)

(3) Women and child victims of acts of violence and children in conflict with the law have the right to free health services, including:

a. medical services;
b. medicolegal services;
c. psychosocial counseling; and
d. visum.

(4) Further provisions regarding procedures for implementing health services as referred to in paragraph (3) are regulated by Regent Regulation.

Paragraph 4
Social Rehabilitation

Article 17

(1) Social rehabilitation as referred to in Article 13 letter c is aimed at restoring and developing the ability of women victims of acts of Violence who experience social dysfunction to be able to carry out social functions in society normally.

(2) Social rehabilitation as referred to in paragraph (1) includes services for:

a. motivation and psychosocial diagnosis;
b. care and nurturing;
c. vocational training and entrepreneurship development;
d. spiritual mental guidance;
e. physical guidance;
f. social guidance and psychosocial counseling;
g. accessibility services;
h. social assistance and assistance;
i. resocialization guidance;
j. follow-up guidance; and/or
k. referral.

(3) Implementation of services as referred to in paragraph (2) is carried out by:

a. regional apparatus that has duties and functions in the social field;

(16)

b. regional apparatus in charge of Women's Empowerment, and can cooperate with integrated service centers, women crisis centers or existing social community organizations; or
c. Ministry of Religious Affairs.

Article 18

(1) Counseling services as referred to in Article 17 paragraph (2) letter a aim to:

a. help women victims of acts of violence recognize their problems and find effective ways to overcome them themselves;
b. empower women victims of acts of violence to determine their future;
c. provide moral support to women victims of acts of violence in facing the process they are going through; and
d. make women victims of acts of violence feel accepted in the community environment.

(2) Counseling services are carried out by gender-responsive social rehabilitation officers, consisting of:

a. counselors;
b. psychologists; and
c. psychiatrists.

(3) Counseling services are carried out in places that guarantee a sense of security, comfort and confidentiality of information from women victims of acts of violence.

(4) Stages of counseling services include:

a. initial counseling;
b. follow-up counseling;
c. case discussion; and
d. site visits.

Article 19

(1) The Regency Government can provide safe houses for women victims of acts of violence.

(17)

(2) Provision of safe house services as referred to in Article 17 paragraph (2) letter b aims to protect women victims of acts of violence from intimidation by perpetrators or other parties.

(3) The location of safe houses as referred to in paragraph (1) must be kept confidential.

(4) Criteria for women victims of acts of violence who need safe houses include:

a. their lives are threatened;
b. receiving rejection from family or community;
c. requiring intensive services but their residence is relatively far away; and/or
d. abandoned if not placed in a safe house.

(5) Implementation of safe house services is carried out by social rehabilitation officers who have gender sensitivity or gender awareness, consisting of:

a. social workers/volunteers;
b. security officers; and/or
c. community leaders.

(6) Further provisions regarding implementation of safe house services are regulated in Regent Regulation.

Article 20

(1) Spiritual guidance as referred to in Article 17 paragraph (2) letter d aims at psychological recovery.

(2) Spiritual guidance as referred to in paragraph (1) is carried out based on the principles of:

a. not imposing any religion or belief; and
b. leaving the final decision in the hands of the victim (self determination).

(3) Spiritual guidance is carried out by spiritual guidance officers.

(4) Spiritual guidance officers as referred to in paragraph (3) consist of consultants from the Advisory, Guidance and Marriage Preservation Board, Officers of the Religious Affairs Office, Ustads/Ulama, Pastors, Ministers and other religious leaders who have gender sensitivity or gender awareness.

(18)

Paragraph 5
Victim Assistance, Law Enforcement and Legal Aid

Article 21

(1) Victim assistance, law enforcement and legal aid services as referred to in Article 13 letter d are actions by law enforcement apparatus to implement statutory regulations in handling cases of acts of violence against women, especially to provide sanctions to perpetrators and provide protection for witnesses and/or victims.

(2) Victim assistance, law enforcement and legal aid services as referred to in paragraph (1) are carried out by prioritizing the best interests of victims.

Article 22

(1) Victim assistance, law enforcement and legal aid services are aimed at providing assistance and protection to women victims of acts of violence and witnesses with a gender-responsive approach so that victims obtain justice and legal certainty.

(2) Types of legal aid services include:

a. legal aid in the reporting process and preparation of examination reports;
b. legal aid in the prosecution process at the prosecutor's office;
c. legal aid in the examination process in court;
d. legal aid in the court hearing decision process until there is a final legal decision; and
e. legal aid in the mediation process.

(3) The Regional Government provides legal aid to women victims of acts of violence based on statutory provisions.

(4) In providing legal aid as referred to in paragraph (3), the Regional Government can cooperate with legal aid institutions, women crisis centers, advocates and/or other gender-sensitive legal assistants.

(19)

Paragraph 6
Repatriation and Social Reintegration

Article 23

(1) Repatriation is aimed at returning women victims of acts of violence to their region of origin.

(2) Repatriation of women victims of acts of violence is accompanied by social reintegration efforts.

Article 24

(1) Social reintegration is carried out in order to reunite women victims of acts of Violence with family, substitute family, or community that can provide Protection and fulfillment of needs for victims.

(2) Social reintegration is aimed so that:

a. women victims of acts of violence can continue their social lives; and
b. women victims of acts of violence can have a decent life.

(3) Social reintegration as referred to in paragraph (1) is carried out after victims receive social rehabilitation services.

Article 25

The Regency Government carries out repatriation and social reintegration of women victims of acts of violence in accordance with statutory regulations.

Part Four
Employment Protection

Article 26

(1) Employers who employ women must maintain decency, safety and security while at work.

(2) Employers must place women workers in places that are safe for women's reproductive health.

(20)

(3) Employers must fulfill the rights of women workers to receive wages in accordance with statutory provisions.

(4) Employers must provide special breastfeeding facilities at their companies.

Article 27

(1) Women workers/laborers under the age of 18 (eighteen) years are prohibited from being employed between 23.00 WIB and 07.00 WIB.

(2) Employers are prohibited from employing pregnant women workers/laborers who according to a doctor's statement are dangerous to the health and safety of their pregnancy.

(3) Employers who employ women workers/laborers between 23.00 WIB and 07.00 WIB, must:

a. provide nutritious food and drinks; and
b. provide shuttle transportation for women workers/laborers who leave and return from work between 23.00 WIB and 05.00 WIB.

CHAPTER IV
IMPROVEMENT OF FAMILY QUALITY

Article 28

(1) In order to improve family quality of life, the Regency Government is responsible for:

a. making efforts to improve family quality to realize gender equality and children's rights;
b. providing services for families in realizing gender equality and children's rights; and
c. strengthening and developing family quality improvement service provider institutions in the Regency.

(2) The responsibility of the Regency Government in efforts to improve family quality as referred to in paragraph (1) is carried out by regional apparatus in accordance with their duties and functions.

(21)

Article 29

The Regional Government makes efforts to improve family quality as referred to in Article 28 paragraph (1) letter a including:

a. guidance;
b. counseling;
c. supervision;
d. facilitation of family development implementation; and
e. improving the health quality of pregnant and breastfeeding mothers.

Article 30

Guidance as referred to in Article 29 letter a, includes:

a. growth and development of toddlers;
b. protection of adolescent reproductive health;
c. maturation of marriage age;
d. birth regulation; and
e. population management.

Article 31

Counseling as referred to in Article 28 letter b includes:

a. conducting outreach to improve women's quality of life; and
b. conducting premarital counseling.

Article 32

Supervision as referred to in Article 28 letter c includes:

a. evaluating toddler growth and development data; and
b. evaluating post-marriage data.

Article 33

Provision of services for families as referred to in Article 28 paragraph (1) letter b includes:

a. communication, information and education;
b. contraceptive tools and medicines;
c. recording and reporting of family planning services;
d. adequate health service facilities and infrastructure;
e. lactation rooms in every public facility.

(22)

Article 34

Communication, information and education as referred to in Article 33 letter a are communication activities to increase knowledge and improve the attitudes and behavior of families and communities in population and family planning programs.

Article 35

(1) In addition to the Regency Government, private parties (private parties include companies that provide public services) must provide lactation rooms.

(2) Lactation rooms as referred to in paragraph (1) are proper and adequate rooms.

(3) Private parties that do not provide lactation rooms as referred to in paragraph (2) are subject to administrative sanctions in the form of:

a. written warning;
b. limitation of business activities;
c. freezing of business activities;
d. administrative fines; and/or
e. revocation of permits.

CHAPTER V
FULFILLMENT OF CHILDREN'S RIGHTS

Part One
General

Article 36

(1) The Regency Government in efforts to fulfill children's rights is responsible for:

a. establishing and implementing policies and programs/activities for fulfillment of children's rights;
b. respecting and guaranteeing the human rights of every child without distinguishing ethnicity, religion, race, class, gender, ethnicity, culture and language, legal status of children, birth order of children, and physical and/or mental condition;

(23)

c. strengthening and developing family quality improvement service provider institutions in realizing children's rights;
d. guaranteeing protection, maintenance, and welfare of children by taking into account the rights and obligations of parents, guardians, or other persons legally responsible for children;
e. providing facilities and infrastructure in implementing fulfillment of children's rights;
f. guaranteeing children's rights to express opinions in accordance with the child's age and level of intelligence;
g. conducting supervision and guidance in the framework of implementation; and
h. cooperating with private parties and/or Non-Governmental Organizations in implementing Child Protection.

(2) The responsibility of the Regency Government in efforts to protect children as referred to in paragraph (1) is carried out by regional apparatus in accordance with their duties and functions.

Part Two
Birth Registration

Article 37

(1) The Regency Government is obliged to conduct birth registration of children with issuance of birth certificates.

(2) Birth certificates as referred to in paragraph (1) are conducted free of charge.

Part Three
Education

Article 38

The Regency Government guarantees the implementation of a 9 (nine) year compulsory education program for children aged 7 (seven) to 15 (fifteen) years.

(24)

Article 39

(1) Every education unit provider must provide opportunities for children to obtain education services without discrimination.

(2) Every education unit provider is prohibited from expelling children from educational institutions unless there is a guarantee for the continuity of the child's education.

(3) In the event that child victims of acts of Violence drop out of school, education unit providers must coordinate with relevant agencies.

Article 40

(1) Every child has the right to obtain education and teaching in the framework of developing their personality and level of intelligence according to their interests and talents.

(2) Every child has the right to receive protection in education units from sexual crimes and violence committed by educators, education personnel, fellow students, and/or other parties.

Article 41

(1) Children with disabilities and children with special needs are given equal opportunities to obtain formal, non-formal and informal education services, including inclusive education programs.

(2) The Regency Government provides teaching staff for inclusive education at every education unit.

Part Four
Health and Social

Article 42

(1) The Regency Government must provide comprehensive health services for children, so that every Child obtains an optimal degree of health from the womb.

(2) Comprehensive health services as referred to in paragraph (1) include:

(25)

a. promotive, preventive, curative and rehabilitative efforts for basic health services;
b. exemption from cost burdens for Children with malnutrition, children with disabilities, children with special needs, children infected with HIV/AIDS, child workers, children victims of narcotics abuse, alcohol, psychotropics and other addictive substances, children who are victims of violence, and child victims of human trafficking; and

(3) The Regency Government, community, family and parents must ensure that children born are protected from diseases that threaten survival and/or cause disability.

Article 43

(1) Promotive efforts for basic health services as referred to in Article 42 paragraph (2) letter a are aimed at improving health status more optimally.

(2) Promotive efforts as referred to in paragraph (1) are carried out by providing socialization or outreach to the community regarding healthy lifestyles including:

a. the importance of exclusive breastfeeding for babies;
b. the importance of immunization for toddlers;
c. clean living patterns;
d. the importance of nutritious food; and
e. dangers of drugs and smoking.

(3) In addition to promotive efforts as referred to in paragraph (2), the Regional Government can carry out other activities in the framework of health implementation.

Article 44

(1) Preventive efforts for basic health services as referred to in Article 42 paragraph (2) letter a are aimed at preventing the occurrence of diseases or health problems.

(2) Efforts as referred to in paragraph (1) are carried out by:

a. requiring mothers to provide exclusive breastfeeding to babies up to 6 (six) months of age;

(26)

b. providing free immunization and vitamins to babies and toddlers;
c. providing nutritious food to toddlers from underprivileged families;
d. optimizing the role of posyandu for health services for mothers and toddlers;
e. conducting regular health examinations; and
f. providing sanitation facilities and clean water at every school.

Article 45

(1) Curative efforts for basic health services as referred to in Article 42 paragraph (2) letter a are aimed at preventing diseases from becoming more severe through treatment.

(2) Treatment as referred to in paragraph (1) is carried out free of charge for Children from underprivileged families.

Article 46

(1) Rehabilitative efforts for basic health services as referred to in Article 42 paragraph (2) letter a are aimed at maintaining and restoring conditions after illness.

(2) Rehabilitative efforts as referred to in paragraph (1) are carried out through regular health examinations.

Article 47

(1) Implementation of social welfare is aimed at:

a. individuals;
b. families;
c. groups; and/or
d. communities.

(2) Implementation of social welfare as referred to in paragraph (1) is prioritized for those who have lives that are not humanly decent and have social problem criteria:

(27)

a. poverty;
b. neglect;
c. disability;
d. isolation;
e. social deviance and behavioral deviation;
f. disaster victims; and/or
g. victims of acts of violence, exploitation and discrimination.

(3) Social welfare as referred to in paragraph (1) can be in the form of providing services for:

a. health;
b. education;
c. legal aid;
d. assistance;
e. social, mental and spiritual guidance;
f. social rehabilitation; and/or
g. reintegration.

Part Five
Employment

Article 48

The Regency Government conducts regular supervision of every person/entity that employs children in efforts to Protect child workers.

Article 49

Every person/entity that employs children must pay attention to the requirements:

a. at least 15 (fifteen) years old;
b. obtain written consent from parents/guardians of child workers;
c. have a written work agreement between the employer and the parents/guardians of child workers and obtain validation from the authorized agency;
d. not employ children at night;
e. working time of at most 3 (three) hours per day.

(28)

Article 50

In the event that children are employed together with adult workers, the workplace for children must be separated from the workplace for adult workers.

Article 51

(1) Every person/entity is prohibited from employing and involving children in the worst forms of work.

(2) The worst forms of work referred to in paragraph (1) include:

a. all work in the form of slavery or the like;
b. all work that utilizes, provides, or offers children for prostitution, pornography production, pornographic performances, or gambling;
c. all work that utilizes, provides, or involves children for production and trade of alcoholic beverages, narcotics, psychotropics, and other addictive substances; and/or
d. all work that endangers the health, safety, or morals of children.

Part Six
Facilitation of Juvenile Justice System

Article 52

(1) Facilitation of the juvenile justice system includes criminal and civil justice.

(2) Facilitation of the criminal justice system as referred to in paragraph (1) includes:

a. provision of assistants or Child volunteers during examination by investigators;
b. provision of legal aid and psychologist services;
c. involving members/police investigators in efforts to prevent criminal acts committed by children or children as victims;

(29)

d. realizing a restorative justice process for children in conflict with the law; and
e. rehabilitation and social reintegration services.

(3) Fulfillment of children's rights in the civil justice system as referred to in paragraph (1) includes provision and provision of consultation and legal aid services during the judicial process.

(4) In facilitating the Child justice system as referred to in paragraph (1) and paragraph (2), the authorized regional apparatus is the regional apparatus in charge of Protection of Women and Children and the Integrated Service Center for Women and Child Empowerment has the obligation to accompany child victims and perpetrators, in police, prosecutor's office and court examinations.

Part Seven
Children's Forum

Article 53

(1) In implementing fulfillment of children's rights to express opinions and participate, the Regency Government facilitates the formation of a Children's Forum.

(2) The Children's Forum as referred to in paragraph (1) is a representation of children in the Region, both representation of children's geographical domicile, components of children's socio-cultural groups and children's educational background.

(3) In every formulation of policies related to children, the Regional Government must pay attention to and accommodate children's opinions submitted through children's participation forums.

(4) Further provisions regarding the formation of the Children's Forum as referred to in paragraph (1) through paragraph (3) are stipulated by Regent Decree.

(30)

Part Eight
Child-Friendly Regency

Article 54

(1) In order to fulfill children's rights in an integrated, systematic and sustainable manner, the Regency Government establishes a KLA Development policy.

(2) The KLA Development policy contains:

a. KLA concept concerning objectives, strategies and roles of related parties;
b. children's rights; and
c. KLA development approach.

Article 55

(1) KLA development policy is directed at fulfillment of children's rights divided into 5 (five) groups, including:

a. civil rights and freedoms;
b. family environment and alternative care;
c. basic health and welfare;
d. education, leisure time utilization, and cultural activities; and
e. special protection.

(2) Further provisions regarding KLA development policy as referred to in paragraph (1) are regulated by Regent Regulation.

Article 56

(1) In order to ensure effectiveness of KLA policy implementation in the Regency, a KLA Task Force is formed.

(2) Formation and membership composition of the KLA Task Force is stipulated by Regent Decree.

(3) The KLA Task Force is domiciled at the Office of the Agency for Women's Empowerment and Child Protection.

(4) The KLA Task Force as referred to in paragraph (1), has the main tasks of:

a. coordinating the implementation of KLA development policies and programs;
b. conducting socialization, advocacy and communication, information and education on KLA policies;

(31)

c. collecting basic data for KLA development;
d. conducting needs analysis sourced from basic data;
e. disseminating basic data;
f. determining program focus and priorities in realizing KLA, adjusted to regional potential;
g. preparing a 5 (five) year KLA regional action plan and work mechanisms; and
h. conducting monitoring, evaluation and reporting at least once (1) a year.

Article 57

In carrying out its duties, the KLA Task Force can coordinate and cooperate with PPT institutions and other institutions that provide child protection services.

CHAPTER VI
CHILD PROTECTION

Article 58

The Regency Government, in order to provide protection for children:

a. implements and supports national policies in implementing child protection in the Regency;
b. guarantees children to use their rights in expressing opinions in accordance with the child's age and level of intelligence;
c. prevents violence against children by optimizing the role of customary institutions;
d. conducts socialization, seminars and outreach regarding protection and children's rights;
e. develops child-friendly school models;
f. provides public facilities that are friendly and safe for children; and
g. encourages community awareness of the importance of protection for children.

(32)

Article 59

Service efforts carried out by the Regency Government for child victims of acts of violence as referred to in Article 36 through Article 58 apply mutatis mutandis to service efforts for child victims of acts of violence.

CHAPTER VII
SPECIAL PROTECTION FOR CHILDREN

Article 60

(1) Special protection for children is given to:

a. children in emergency situations;
b. children in conflict with the law;
c. children from minority and isolated groups;
d. children who are exploited economically and/or sexually;
e. children who are victims of narcotics abuse, alcohol, psychotropics, and other addictive substances;
f. children who are victims of pornography;
g. children with HIV/AIDS;
h. child victims of kidnapping, sale, and/or trafficking;
i. child victims of physical and/or psychological violence;
j. child victims of sexual crimes;
k. child victims of terrorism networks;
l. children with disabilities;
m. child victims of abuse and neglect;
n. children with deviant social behavior; and
o. children who are victims of stigmatization from labeling related to their parents' condition.

(2) The Regency Government in implementing handling of special protection for children as referred to in paragraph (1) in accordance with statutory provisions.

Article 61

Children in emergency situations as referred to in Article 60 paragraph (1) letter a consist of:

(33)

a. children who become refugees;
b. child victims of riots;
c. child victims of natural disasters; and
d. children in armed conflict situations.

Article 62

Special protection for children who become refugees as referred to in Article 61 paragraph (1) letter a is implemented in accordance with statutory provisions.

Article 63

Special protection for child victims of riots, disaster victims, and children in armed conflict as referred to in Article 61 paragraph (1) letters b, c, and d, is implemented through:

a. fulfillment of basic needs consisting of food, clothing, housing, education, health, learning and recreation, security guarantees, and equal treatment; and
b. fulfillment of special needs for children with disabilities and children experiencing psychosocial disorders.

Article 64

Special Protection for children in conflict with the law as referred to in Article 60 paragraph (1) letter b is carried out through:

a. humane treatment taking into account needs according to age;
b. separation from adults;
c. provision of legal aid and other assistance effectively;
d. implementation of recreational activities;
e. freedom from torture, punishment, or other treatment that is cruel, inhuman and degrades dignity and degree;
f. avoidance of imposition of death penalty and/or life imprisonment;

(34)

g. avoidance of arrest, detention or imprisonment, except as a last resort and for the shortest possible time;
h. provision of justice before a juvenile court that is objective, impartial, and in closed hearings;
i. avoidance of publication of identity;
j. provision of assistance from Parents/Guardians and persons trusted by the child;
k. provision of social advocacy;
l. provision of private life;
m. provision of accessibility, especially for Children with Disabilities;
n. provision of education;
o. provision of health services; and
p. provision of other rights in accordance with statutory provisions.

Article 65

Special Protection for Children from minority and isolated groups as referred to in Article 60 paragraph (1) letter c is carried out through provision of infrastructure and facilities to be able to enjoy their own culture, acknowledge and practice their own religious teachings, and use their own language.

Article 66

Special Protection for children who are exploited economically and/or sexually as referred to in Article 60 paragraph (1) letter d is carried out through:

a. dissemination and/or socialization of statutory provisions related to protection of children who are exploited economically and/or sexually.
b. involvement of various companies, labor unions, Non-Governmental Organizations, and communities in eliminating exploitation of children economically and/or sexually.

(35)

Article 67

Special protection for Children who are victims of narcotics abuse, alcohol, psychotropics, and other addictive substances as referred to in Article 60 paragraph (1) letter e and children involved in their production and distribution is carried out through supervision, prevention, treatment, and rehabilitation efforts.

Article 68

(1) Every Person must protect children from the influence of pornography and prevent children's access to information containing pornographic elements.

(2) Special Protection for children who are victims of pornography as referred to in Article 60 paragraph (1) letter f is implemented through guidance, assistance, and social, physical and mental health recovery efforts.

(3) Guidance, assistance, and social, physical and mental health recovery as referred to in paragraph (1) is implemented in accordance with statutory provisions.

Article 69

Special protection for Children with HIV/AIDS as referred to in Article 60 paragraph (1) letter f is carried out through supervision, prevention, treatment, care, and rehabilitation efforts.

Article 70

Special protection for child victims of kidnapping, sale, and/or trafficking as referred to in Article 60 paragraph (1) letter h is carried out through supervision, protection, prevention, care, and rehabilitation efforts.

Article 71

Special protection for child victims of physical and/or psychological violence as referred to in Article 60 paragraph (1) letter i is carried out through efforts to:

(36)

a. disseminate and socialize statutory provisions that protect child victims of acts of violence; and
b. monitor, report, and impose sanctions.

Article 72

Special Protection for child victims of sexual crimes as referred to in Article 60 paragraph (1) letter j is carried out through efforts to:

a. educate about reproductive health, religious values, and moral values;
b. social rehabilitation;
c. psychosocial assistance during treatment until recovery; and
d. provide protection and assistance at every level of examination from investigation, prosecution, to examination in court hearings.

Article 73

Special Protection for child victims of terrorism networks as referred to in Article 60 paragraph (1) letter k is carried out through efforts to:

a. educate about education, ideology, and nationalism values;
b. counsel about the dangers of terrorism;
c. social rehabilitation; and
d. social assistance.

Article 74

Special Protection for children with disabilities as referred to in Article 60 paragraph (1) letter l is carried out through efforts to:

a. treat children humanely in accordance with the dignity and rights of children;
b. fulfill special needs;
c. treat equally with other children to achieve full social integration and individual development;
d. social assistance.

(37)

Article 75

Special Protection for child victims of abuse and neglect as referred to in Article 60 paragraph (1) letter m is carried out through supervision, prevention, care, counseling, social rehabilitation, and social assistance efforts.

Article 76

Special Protection for children with deviant social behavior as referred to in Article 60 paragraph (1) letter n is carried out through guidance on religious and social values, counseling, social rehabilitation, and social assistance.

Article 77

Special Protection for children who are victims of stigmatization from labeling related to their parents' condition as referred to in Article 60 paragraph (1) letter o is carried out through counseling, social rehabilitation, and social assistance.

Article 78

(1) Every child who becomes a victim as referred to in Article 60 paragraph (1) letters b, d, f, h, i and j has the right to submit to the Court in the form of the right to restitution which is the responsibility of the perpetrator of the crime.

(2) Further provisions regarding implementation of restitution as referred to in paragraph (1) are regulated by Regent Regulation.

Article 79

Further provisions regarding Special Protection for Children as referred to in Article 60 are regulated by Regent Regulation.

(38)

CHAPTER VIII
PARTICIPATION OF COMMUNITY, AND PARENTS
AND/OR FAMILY

Part One
Community

Article 80

(1) Community participation in efforts to protect women and children:

a. participate in prevention and provide support for development of prevention programs for violations of the rights of women and children;
b. provide information and/or report violations of the rights of women and children to authorized parties; and
c. supervise the law enforcement process for cases of violations of the rights of women and children.

(2) Community participation as referred to in paragraph (1) is based on statutory regulations and local wisdom values of the local community.

Article 81

(1) In order to prevent violations of women's rights, the community:

a. monitors various conditions occurring in their environment that can cause violations of women's rights;
b. increases awareness of violations of women's rights;
c. strengthens the role of community, religious and family environment organizations;
d. conducts supervision of the implementation of Protection of Women and Children carried out by the Regional Government with a mechanism for conveying aspirations to the Regent or Regional House of Representatives.

(39)

(2) In order to prevent violations of children's rights, the community:

a. monitors various conditions occurring in their environment that cause violations of children's rights;
b. increases awareness of violations of children's rights;
c. strengthens the role of community, religious and family environment organizations; and
d. prevents early marriage.

Part Two
Parents and/or Family

Article 82

Families in efforts to protect women's rights play a role in:

a. protecting women from conflict and domestic violence that degrades women's dignity;
b. providing equal opportunities for women to develop themselves in the fields of education, social, economic, political and cultural; and
c. preventing violations of women's rights in the household.

Article 83

(1) Parents in efforts to protect children's rights play a role in:

a. caring for, maintaining, educating, and protecting children;
b. developing children according to their abilities, talents, and interests;
c. providing formal and non-formal education;
d. providing character education and instilling moral values in children;
e. guiding children to behave in accordance with religion, ethics and morals as well as customs; and
f. preventing early marriage.

(40)

(2) In the event that parents do not exist, or their whereabouts are unknown, or due to some reason, cannot carry out their obligations and responsibilities, then the obligations and responsibilities as referred to in paragraph (1) can be transferred to the family.

CHAPTER IX
GUIDANCE AND SUPERVISION

Article 84

(1) The Regency Government must conduct guidance and supervision of the implementation of protection of women and child victims of violence.

(2) Guidance and supervision as referred to in paragraph (1), includes:

a. guidelines and fulfillment standards;
b. technical guidance and training;
c. provision of facilities;
d. monitoring; and
e. evaluation.

(3) Guidance as referred to in paragraph (2) in order to realize the objectives of protection of women and child victims of violence according to minimum service standards implemented by the agency in charge of Protection of Women and Children.

(4) The agency in charge of Protection of Women and Children must report regularly to the Regent regarding the implementation of protection of women and child victims of violence.

CHAPTER X
FINANCING

Article 85

Financing for implementation of programs and activities for Protection of Women and Children is sourced from:

a. regional revenue and expenditure budget; and
b. other legitimate sources in accordance with statutory provisions.

(41)

CHAPTER XI
CLOSING PROVISIONS

Article 86

Implementing regulations of this Regional Regulation are stipulated at the latest 6 (six) months from the promulgation of this Regional Regulation.

Article 87

This Regional Regulation comes into force on the date of promulgation.

In order that everyone may know of it, orders the promulgation of this Regional Regulation by placing it in the Regional Gazette of Muara Enim Regency.
