REGIONAL REGULATION OF TASIKMALAYA REGENCY
NUMBER 5 OF 2019
CONCERNING
WOMEN'S EMPOWERMENT AND CHILD PROTECTION
BY THE GRACE OF ALMIGHTY GOD
THE REGENT OF TASIKMALAYA,

Considering : a. that every citizen has equal rights to live a dignified life in accordance with the principles of humanity, equality and justice;

b. that women and children are groups of society vulnerable to acts of injustice and inequality as well as acts of violence that can harm their rights and dignity as human beings;

c. that the rampant cases of violence against women and children in Tasikmalaya Regency require handling and concrete action from the Regional Government to eliminate all forms of violence against women and children;

d. that based on the considerations as referred to in letter a, letter b, and letter c, it is necessary to establish a Regional Regulation on Women's Empowerment and Child Protection;

CHAPTER I
GENERAL PROVISIONS

Article 1

In this Regional Regulation, what is meant by:
1. Regent is the Regent of Tasikmalaya.
2. Regency Area is the Regency Area of Tasikmalaya.
3. Regency Regional Government is the Regent as an element of regional government administration who leads the implementation of government affairs that are the authority of the autonomous region.
4. Regional House of Representatives hereinafter abbreviated as DPRD is a regional people's representative institution positioned as an element of regional government administration.
5. Regency Regional Apparatus is an assistant element to the Regent and Regency DPRD in administering government affairs that are the authority of the Regency area.
6. Women's Empowerment is every effort to improve physical, mental, spiritual, social, knowledge, and skills capabilities so that women are ready to be utilized according to their respective abilities.
7. Protection is a series of fulfillment of the rights of women and child victims of violence and discrimination starting from prevention efforts, services and social rehabilitation.
8. Child is someone who has not reached the age of 18 (eighteen) years, including children still in the womb.
9. Women's Protection is all efforts aimed at protecting women and providing a sense of security in fulfilling their rights by providing consistent and systematic attention aimed at achieving gender equality.
10. Child Protection is all activities to guarantee and protect children and their rights so they can live, grow, develop and participate optimally in accordance with human dignity and receive protection from violence and discrimination.
11. Violence is any act that results in suffering or pain physically, psychologically, sexually, and/or neglect, including threats to commit acts, coercion, or unlawful deprivation of liberty.
12. Violence Against Women is any action based on gender differences that results in or may result in suffering or pain to women physically, sexually or psychologically, including threats of certain actions, coercion or arbitrary deprivation of liberty, whether occurring in the public sphere or in private life.
13. Violence Against Children is any act against children that results in suffering or pain physically, mentally, sexually, psychologically, including neglect and maltreatment that threatens bodily integrity and degrades the dignity of children.
14. Special Protection is a form of protection received by Children in certain situations and conditions to obtain a guarantee of security against threats that endanger themselves and their lives in their growth and development.
15. Domestic Violence hereinafter referred to as KDRT is any act against a person, especially women, that results in suffering or pain physically, sexually, psychologically, and/or household neglect including threats to commit acts of coercion, or unlawful deprivation of liberty within the household scope.
16. Parents are biological father and/or mother, or stepfather and/or stepmother, or adoptive father and/or adoptive mother.
17. Guardian is a person or body who in reality exercises custody as a Parent over a Child.
18. Family is the smallest unit in society consisting of husband and wife, or husband-wife and their children, or father and children, or mother and children, or blood relatives in direct line upward or downward up to the third degree.
19. Community is individuals, families, groups, and/or community organizations.
20. Non-Governmental Organization, hereinafter referred to as LSM is an organization/institution formed by citizens of the Republic of Indonesia voluntarily of their own will and interested and engaged in the field of social welfare business established by the organization/institution as a manifestation of community participation in efforts to improve community welfare that emphasizes voluntary service.
21. Victim is a woman and child who experiences suffering and or pain either directly or indirectly as a result of violence occurring in the Tasikmalaya Regency area.
22. Prevention is an effort to develop the capacity and mechanism of the Regional Government and community in creating conditions that can prevent the occurrence of violence, maltreatment, KDRT, exploitation and/or neglect.
23. Handling is action that includes identification, rescue, rehabilitation and reintegration of women and children who are victims of acts of violence, maltreatment, KDRT, exploitation, maltreatment or neglect.
24. Repatriation is an effort to return women and child victims of violence from abroad to the debarkation point/entry point, or from the receiving area to the area of origin.
25. Service is action taken as soon as possible to victims when seeing, hearing and knowing that violence against victims will, is, or has occurred.
26. Assistance is an effort made by a person or representative from an institution that has the expertise to provide assistance to victims to conduct counseling, therapy and advocacy for the strengthening and recovery of victims of violence.
27. Rehabilitation is recovery from disturbances to physical, psychological, and social conditions in order to carry out their roles again properly both in the family and in society.
28. Social Rehabilitation is a service aimed at restoring and developing the ability of a person experiencing social dysfunction to carry out social functions properly.
29. Social Reintegration is an effort to reunite victims with family, substitute family, or community that can provide protection and fulfillment of needs for victims.
30. Regional Technical Implementation Unit for Women and Child Protection hereinafter abbreviated as UPTD PPPA is a technical implementer to carry out operational technical activities in its working area in providing services for women and children experiencing problems of violence, discrimination, special protection and other problems;
31. Legal Aid is legal services provided by legal assistants and advocates to conduct the process of assisting witnesses and/or victims of violence against women and children that is gender sensitive.
32. Integrated Service Center for Women's Empowerment and Child Protection, hereinafter abbreviated as P2TP2A, is an integrated service center in efforts to empower women in various fields of development, as well as protection of women and children from various types of discrimination and acts of violence, including trafficking in persons, established by the Regional Government or community-based, which includes: referral centers, business consultation centers, reproductive health consultation centers, legal consultation centers, Integrated Crisis Centers (PKT), Integrated Service Centers (PPT), trauma recovery centers (trauma centers), women's crisis handling centers (women crisis centers), training centers, Science and Technology Information Centers (PIPTEK), safe houses (shelters), transit houses, or other forms.
33. Women and Child Protection Task Force is a functional Work Unit that organizes integrated protection services and handling of cases of violence against children and women at the Sub-district level.
34. Women and Child Protection Task Unit is a functional Work Unit that organizes integrated protection services and handling of cases of violence against children and women at the Village level.
35. Child-Friendly Regency is a Regency that has a child rights-based development system through integration of government, community and business world commitments and resources that are planned comprehensively and sustainably in policies, programs and activities to ensure the fulfillment of children's rights.
36. Child-Friendly Regency Task Force is a coordinating institution at the regency level that coordinates policy efforts, programs and activities to realize a child-friendly regency.
37. Regional Action Plan for Child-Friendly Regency Development is a document containing policies, programs and activities to realize a child-friendly regency.

CHAPTER II
WOMEN'S EMPOWERMENT

Article 2

(1) The Regional Government is obligated and responsible for the implementation of women's empowerment.
(2) Women's empowerment efforts as referred to in paragraph (1) include:
a. determination of policies, budgets, programs, and activities;
b. determination of implementation guidelines;
c. service delivery; and
d. coordination of policies, programs and activities.

Article 3

Women's empowerment is directed to obtain opportunities and rights as human beings to be able to play a role and participate in the fields of:
a. economy;
b. socio-culture;
c. politics; and
d. law.

Article 4

Implementation of women's empowerment in the economic field as referred to in Article 3 letter a is carried out through:
a. provision of skills and job training;
b. facilitation of formation, strengthening and development of productive economic business groups;
c. facilitation and capital assistance;
d. facilitation of marketing network development; and
e. Economic Empowerment of Women Heads of Households (PEKKA).

Article 5

Women's empowerment in the socio-cultural field as referred to in Article 3 letter b is carried out through:
a. increasing knowledge, attitudes and skills to encourage fulfillment of the right to education in stages according to potential to improve women's social status;
b. increasing awareness, willingness and ability to overcome health problems through promotional, preventive, curative, and rehabilitative efforts that are affordable and quality, especially in the field of reproductive health;
c. increasing awareness and knowledge about independent, healthy and prosperous family planning including access to consultation services and marriage registration; and
d. facilitation and efforts to preserve customs and develop science, technology, arts and culture for the advancement of women.

Article 6

Implementation of women's empowerment in the political field as referred to in Article 3 letter c includes:
a. political education for women;
b. involvement of women in decision-making at various levels of government;
c. providing opportunities for women to hold public office;
d. participation in general elections; and
e. self-development through organizations to associate, assemble and express opinions.

Article 7

Implementation of women's empowerment in the legal field as referred to in Article 3 letter d includes:
a. increasing knowledge and awareness in the legal field through communication, information and education services; and
b. facilitation of access and consultation services as well as free legal aid.

CHAPTER III
WOMEN'S PROTECTION

Article 8

(1) Women have the right to receive protection from acts of domestic violence, protection of women's rights in employment, protection of women in emergency situations and special conditions as well as protection of women's rights from criminal acts of trafficking in persons.
(2) Protection of women's rights from acts of domestic violence in the form of physical and psychological prevention, sexual violence and neglect, handling, service and empowerment of victims of domestic violence.
(3) Protection of women's rights in employment rights inside or outside the country receive protection in prevention and handling of cases of neglect of women's rights in terms of employment.
(4) Protection of women's rights in emergency conditions and special conditions, includes protection of women in emergency situations, in conflict areas, disaster areas, protection of the rights of women with disabilities, and protection of the rights of elderly women with social problems.
(5) Protection of women's rights from acts of trafficking in persons includes prevention and handling, services and empowerment of victims of criminal acts of trafficking in persons.

Article 9

(1) The Regional Government is obligated to provide protection to women so they can realize and enjoy a proper standard of living.
(2) Provisions regarding Protection as referred to in paragraph (1) are regulated by Regent Regulation.

CHAPTER IV
CHILD PROTECTION

Part One
General Child Protection

Article 10

Child Protection includes the fields of:
a. Child Protection in the field of Religion and Belief;
b. Child Protection in the field of Civil and Freedom;
c. Child Protection in the field of Health;
d. Child Protection in the field of Education;
e. Child Protection in the field of Social; and
f. Child Protection in the field of Special Protection.

Part Two
Child Protection in the field of Religion and Belief

Article 11

(1) Every child receives protection to worship according to their respective religion and belief.
(2) Before the child can determine their choice, the religion embraced by the child follows the religion of their parents.
(3) In the case of child adoption, prospective adoptive parents must be of the same religion as the religion embraced by the prospective adopted child.
(4) If the origin of the child as referred to in paragraph (3) is not known, then the child's religion is adjusted to the religion of the majority of the local population.

Part Three
Child Protection in the Field of Civil and Freedom

Article 12

Child protection in the Civil field as referred to in Article 10 letter b includes:
a. guarantee that every child has the right to obtain a name and self-identity from birth in the form of a birth certificate;
b. making birth certificates is the responsibility of the Government;
c. making birth certificates is not charged and is borne by the Regional Government; and
d. provisions for making birth certificates are further regulated in Regent Regulation.

Article 13

(1) Child protection in the field of civil and freedom helps children so that children can:
a. participate in all fields;
b. freely express opinions and think according to conscience and religion in accordance with the norms prevailing in the region;
c. freely receive oral or written information according to the child's age stage and development;
d. freely associate and assemble in accordance with noble norms and ethics;
e. freely rest, play, recreate, and create arts and culture without neglecting obligations as a child; and
f. obtain play facilities that meet health and safety requirements.
(2) Efforts as referred to in paragraph (1), are developed and adjusted to the age, level of ability of the child, and their environment so as not to hinder and disturb the child's development.

Part Four
Child Protection in the Field of Health

Article 14

Child protection in the field of Health as referred to in Article 10 letter c, includes:
a. providing facilities and organizing comprehensive health efforts for children, so that every child obtains an optimal degree of health from the womb carried out by the Regional Government;
b. organizing comprehensive and free health efforts for all children including those from poor families;
c. providing health insurance for child victims of violence, exploitation, neglect and maltreatment;
d. being responsible for maintaining health and caring for children from the womb carried out by family and parents; and
e. endeavoring that children born are protected from diseases that threaten the continuity of life and or cause disability carried out by the Regional Government, community, family and parents.

Part Five
Child Protection in the Field of Education

Article 15

Implementation of child protection in the field of education as referred to in Article 10 letter d, includes:
a. providing the widest possible opportunity for children to obtain education carried out by the Regional Government, family and parents;
b. every education provider is prohibited from expelling children from Educational Institutions without guarantees for the continuity of children's education;
c. implementation of a 12 (twelve) year compulsory education program, regulated in a separate Regional Regulation;
d. providing equal opportunities and accessibility to obtain regular education and special education for children with disabilities; and
e. protecting their rights to obtain education for child victims of violence, exploitation, neglect and maltreatment.

Part Six
Child Protection in the Social Field

Article 16

Implementation of child protection in the social field as referred to in Article 10 letter e is carried out in the form of:
a. Prevention services, include:
1) Making policies on child protection from all forms of violence, exploitation, neglect and maltreatment in all children's lives;
2) Making policies on social welfare guarantees for children experiencing violence, exploitation, neglect and maltreatment; and
3) Organizing training to increase capacity for family welfare consultation institutions.
b. Risk Reduction Services, include:
1) Conducting early identification, complaint services and assessment of children and families who are victims of violence, exploitation, neglect and maltreatment;
2) Providing services for children and families who are victims of violence, exploitation, neglect and maltreatment including:
1) guidance and counseling on parenting patterns and behavior change;
2) mediation to related parties;
3) psychosocial services;
4) economic assistance and life skills;
5) temporary shelter;
6) preparation of social reintegration for child victims of violence, exploitation, neglect and maltreatment; and
7) protecting the identity of victims to prevent bad stigma for children.
c. Case Handling Services, include:
1) Making immediate rescue efforts for victims in dangerous conditions for their safety;
2) Providing referrals according to victim needs;
3) Providing counseling and family support;
4) Providing social rehabilitation and reintegration services;
5) Monitoring the condition of children in long-term alternative care; and
6) Creating a database of alternative care places for children.

Part Seven
Child Protection in the Field of Special Protection

Article 17

Child Protection in the field of Special Protection as referred to in Article 10 letter f, includes:
a. children in emergency situations;
b. children in conflict with the law (ABH);
c. children exploited economically and or sexually;
d. children who are victims of abuse of narcotics, alcohol, psychotropics, and other addictive substances (NAPZA);
e. child victims of kidnapping, sale and trafficking;
f. child victims of violence both physical and/or mental, and children with disabilities; and/or
g. child victims of maltreatment and neglect.

Paragraph 1
Protection of Children in Emergency Situations

Article 18

(1) Children in emergency situations as referred to in Article 17 letter a, consist of:
a. children who become refugees;
b. child victims of riots;
c. child victims of natural disasters; and
d. children in armed conflict situations.
(2) Implementation of child protection in emergency situations as referred to in paragraph (1) is carried out in the form of:
a. Prevention Services, include:
1) Making policies on child protection from all forms of violence, exploitation, neglect and maltreatment in emergency situations;
2) Making policies on fulfillment of basic needs consisting of food, clothing, housing, education, health, learning and recreation, security guarantees, and equal treatment for children who become refugees, victims of violence, victims of natural disasters and children in armed conflict situations; and
3) Making disaster management policies and mechanisms that take into account the interests of children.
b. Risk Reduction Services, include:
1) Conducting early identification, complaint services and assessment of children and families who are victims of violence, exploitation, neglect and maltreatment.
2) Providing services for children and families who are victims of violence, exploitation, neglect and maltreatment, which include:
a) legal consultation;
b) mediation to related parties;
c) psychosocial services;
d) economic assistance and life skills;
e) safe houses; and
f) transit houses.
c. Case Handling Services, include:
1) Making immediate rescue efforts for victims in dangerous conditions for their safety;
2) Providing referrals according to victim needs;
3) Providing counseling and family support;
4) Treating children in emergency situations humanely in accordance with the dignity and rights of children;
5) Providing a sense of security and protection for children who become refugees, victims of violence, victims of natural disasters and children in armed conflict situations; and
6) Fulfilling special needs for children in emergency situations experiencing psychosocial disorders.

Paragraph 2
Protection for ABH

Article 19

(1) Special protection for ABH as referred to in Article 17 letter b includes children in conflict with the law and child victims of criminal acts, is the obligation and responsibility of the Regional Government and community.
(2) implementation of special protection for ABH as referred to in paragraph (1) is carried out in the form of:
a. Prevention services, include:
1) Making policies on education guarantees for ABH;
2) Providing socialization and increasing awareness of parents and/or community, press, educators, government, law enforcement officials about protecting the identity of ABH to avoid bad stigma for children;
3) Providing socialization and increasing awareness of parents/guardians and/or community, press, educators, Regional Government and law enforcement officials about alternative forms of enforcing discipline without physical and psychological punishment;
4) Providing socialization to the community about understanding restorative justice or justice that restores;
5) Organizing training to increase community capacity in advocating to law enforcement institutions to make distinctions in handling problems related to ABH;
6) Organizing training to increase community capacity in advocating for the juvenile justice process to provide alternative punishments in the form of actions; and
7) Making complaint mechanisms for handling problems related to ABH.
b. Risk Reduction Services, include:
1) Conducting early identification, complaint services and assessment of children and families at risk;
2) Providing services for children and families such as:
a) guidance and counseling on parenting patterns and behavior change;
b) legal consultation;
c) mediation to related parties;
d) psychosocial services; and
e) protecting the identity of victims.
c. Case Handling Services, include:
1) Providing counseling and need for family support;
2) Treatment of children humanely in accordance with the dignity and rights of children;
3) Providing guarantees of legal aid/special assistance for children in conflict with the law;
4) Providing a sense of security and protection from news about the identity of ABH to avoid bad stigma for children;
5) Providing special facilities and infrastructure for ABH;
6) Providing accessibility to obtain information regarding case developments;
7) Providing social rehabilitation and reintegration services; and
8) Conducting continuous monitoring and recording of the development of children in conflict with the law.

Paragraph 3
Protection of Exploited Children
Economically and/or Sexually

Article 20

(1) Special protection for children exploited economically and/or sexually as referred to in Article 17 letter c, is the obligation and responsibility of the Regional Government and community.
(2) Implementation of special protection for children exploited economically and or sexually as referred to in paragraph (1) is carried out in the form of:
a. Prevention Services, include:
1) Making policies on child protection from all forms of violence, exploitation, and maltreatment in all situations of children's lives;
2) Making policies on fulfillment of special needs for children with disabilities and children experiencing psychosocial disorders who are victims of economic and/or sexual exploitation;
3) Providing protection guarantees for child victims of economic and/or sexual exploitation;
4) Providing protection guarantees from all forms of violence, exploitation, neglect and maltreatment for children exploited economically and/or sexually;
5) Providing socialization and increasing awareness of parents and/or community, press, educators, by government and law enforcement officials about the bad impacts of violence and economic and/or sexual exploitation;
6) Providing socialization and increasing awareness of parents and/or community, press, educators, by government and law enforcement officials about protecting the identity of child victims of economic and/or sexual exploitation; and
7) Providing socialization regarding provisions of laws and regulations related to protection of children exploited economically and/or sexually.
b. Risk Reduction Services, which include:
1) Conducting early identification, complaint services and assessment of children and families at risk of experiencing economic and/or sexual exploitation;
2) Providing services for children and families who are victims of violence, and economic and/or sexual exploitation including:
a) legal consultation;
b) legal aid to victims of sexual violence;
c) mediation to related parties;
d) psychosocial services;
e) economic assistance and life skills;
f) temporary shelter; and
g) protecting the identity of victims.
c. Case Handling Services, include:
1) Making immediate rescue efforts for victims in dangerous conditions for their safety;
2) Providing referrals according to victim needs;
3) Providing counseling and family support;
4) Treating child victims of economic and/or sexual exploitation humanely in accordance with the dignity and rights of children;
5) Providing a sense of security and protection for child victims of economic and/or sexual exploitation from news coverage; and
6) Providing social rehabilitation and reintegration services.

Paragraph 4
Protection of Children Who Are Victims
of Abuse of Narcotics, Alcohol, Psychotropics,
and Other Addictive Substances (NAPZA)

Article 21

(1) Supervision, prevention, treatment, and rehabilitation of children who are victims of abuse of narcotics, alcohol, psychotropics, and other addictive substances (NAPZA) as referred to in Article 17 letter d and involved in their production and distribution, is the obligation and responsibility of the Regional Government and community.
(2) Implementation of protection for children who are victims of abuse of narcotics, alcohol, psychotropics, and other addictive substances (NAPZA) as referred to in paragraph (1) is carried out in the form of:
a. Prevention Services, include:
1. Making policies on protection of child victims of abuse of narcotics, alcohol, psychotropics and other addictive substances (NAPZA);
2. Making policies on special protection for children who are victims of abuse of narcotics, alcohol, psychotropics, and other addictive substances (NAPZA); and
3. Providing socialization and increasing awareness of parents and/or community and children about the bad impacts of abuse of narcotics, alcohol, psychotropics and other addictive substances (NAPZA).
b. Risk Reduction Services, which include:
1. Conducting early identification, complaint services and assessment of children and families at risk;
2. Providing services for children and families who are victims of abuse of narcotics, alcohol, psychotropics, and other addictive substances (NAPZA) including:
a. guidance and counseling on parenting patterns and behavior change;
b. legal consultation; and
c. psychosocial services.
c. Case Handling Services, include:
1. Making immediate rescue efforts for victims in dangerous conditions for their safety;
2. Providing referrals according to the needs of victims of abuse of narcotics, alcohol, psychotropics and other addictive substances (NAPZA);
3. Conducting counseling and family support;
4. Treating child victims of abuse of narcotics, alcohol, psychotropics, and other addictive substances (NAPZA) humanely in accordance with the dignity and rights of children; and
5. Providing social rehabilitation and reintegration services.

Paragraph 5
Protection of Child Victims of Kidnapping,
Sale and Trafficking

Article 22

(1) Special Protection for child victims of kidnapping, sale and trafficking as referred to in Article 17 letter e is carried out through supervision, protection, prevention, treatment and rehabilitation efforts by the Regional Government and community.
(2) Implementation of special protection for children who are victims of kidnapping, sale and trafficking as referred to in paragraph (1) is carried out in the form of:
a. Prevention Services, include:
1. Providing protection guarantees for child victims of kidnapping, sale and trafficking; and
2. Providing complaint and case handling mechanisms.
b. Risk Reduction Services, include:
1. Conducting early identification, complaint services and assessment of children and families at risk; and
2. Providing services for children and victims of kidnapping, sale and trafficking which include:
a. guidance and counseling on parenting patterns and behavior change;
b. legal consultation;
c. mediation to related parties;
d. psychosocial services;
e. economic assistance and life skills;
f. temporary shelter; and
g. protecting the identity of victims.
c. Case Handling Services, include:
1. Making immediate rescue efforts for victims in dangerous conditions for their safety;
2. Providing referrals according to victim needs;
3. Conducting counseling and family support;
4. Treating child victims of kidnapping, sale and trafficking humanely in accordance with the dignity and rights of children;
5. Providing accessibility to obtain information regarding case developments;
6. Providing social rehabilitation and reintegration services; and
7. Creating a database of child victims of kidnapping, sale and trafficking.

Paragraph 6
Protection of Child Victims of Violence
both Physical and/or Mental and Children with Disabilities

Article 23

(1) Special Protection for child victims of violence both physical and/or mental and children with disabilities as referred to in Article 17 letter f is carried out through efforts to:
a. disseminate and socialize provisions of laws and regulations that protect children from becoming victims of violence;
b. obtain equal treatment with other children to achieve full social integration and individual development; and
c. fulfill special needs.
(2) Implementation of special protection for children who are victims of violence both physical and/or mental and children with Disabilities as referred to in paragraph (1) is carried out in the form of:
a. Prevention Services, which include:
1. making policies on fulfillment of special needs for children with disabilities and children experiencing psychosocial disorders;
2. providing education guarantees for child victims of violence both physical and/or mental and children with disabilities;
3. providing protection guarantees from all forms of violence, exploitation, neglect and maltreatment for children with disabilities;
4. providing socialization and increasing awareness of parents and/or community, press, educators, by government and law enforcement officials about the bad impacts of violence;
5. providing socialization and increasing awareness of parents and/or community, press, educators, by government about protecting the identity of children who are victims of violence both physical and/or mental and children with disabilities from bad stigma; and
6. preparing complaint mechanisms for case handling.
b. Risk Reduction Services, include:
1. conducting early identification, complaint services and assessment of children and families at risk;
2. providing services for children and families who are victims of violence which include:
a. guidance and counseling on parenting patterns and behavior change;
b. legal consultation;
c. mediation to related parties;
d. psychosocial services;
e. economic assistance and life skills;
f. temporary shelter; and
g. protecting the identity of victims.
c. Case Handling Services, which include:
1. making immediate rescue efforts for victims in dangerous conditions for their safety;
2. providing referrals according to victim needs;
3. conducting counseling and family support;
4. treatment of children humanely in accordance with the dignity and rights of children;
5. providing a sense of security and protection from news coverage to avoid bad stigma;
6. providing special facilities and infrastructure for children with disabilities;
7. providing social rehabilitation and reintegration services; and
8. creating a database of child victims of violence both physical and/or mental and children with disabilities.

Paragraph 7
Protection of Children
Victims of Maltreatment and Neglect

Article 24

(1) Protection of child victims of maltreatment and neglect as referred to in Article 17 letter g is carried out through supervision, prevention, treatment and rehabilitation efforts by the Regional Government and community.
(2) Implementation of protection for children who are victims of maltreatment and neglect as referred to in paragraph (1) is carried out in the form of:
a. Prevention Services, include:
1. providing protection guarantees for child victims of maltreatment and neglect;
2. providing protection guarantees from all forms of neglect and maltreatment for children;
3. providing socialization and increasing awareness of parents and/or community, press, educators, government, law enforcement officials about the bad impacts of maltreatment and neglect; and
4. preparing complaint mechanisms for case handling.
b. Risk Reduction Services, include:
1. conducting early identification, complaint services and assessment of children and families at risk;
2. providing services for children and families who are victims of neglect and maltreatment including:
a. guidance and counseling on parenting patterns and behavior change;
b. legal consultation;
c. mediation to related parties;
d. psychosocial services; and
e. temporary shelter.
c. Case Handling Services, include:
1. making immediate rescue efforts for victims in dangerous conditions for their safety;
2. providing referrals according to victim needs;
3. conducting counseling and family support;
4. treatment of children humanely in accordance with the dignity and rights of children; and
5. conducting continuous monitoring and recording of the development of child victims of maltreatment and neglect.
(4) Procedures, mechanisms and service standards for special protection for child laborers, working children and children who work as referred to in paragraph (1) are further regulated by Regent Regulation.

CHAPTER V
FORMS OF VIOLENCE AGAINST WOMEN AND CHILDREN

Article 25

Forms of violence against women and children, include:
a. physical violence;
b. psychological violence;
c. sexual violence;
d. neglect;
e. exploitation; and/or
f. other violence.

Article 26

(1) Physical violence as referred to in Article 25 letter a is an act that results in pain accompanied by injury, wounds or defects on the body, miscarriage, fainting and/or causing death of women and/or children.
(2) Psychological violence as referred to in Article 25 letter b is an act that results in fear, loss of self-confidence, loss of ability to act, feelings of helplessness and/or psychological suffering to women and/or children.
(3) Sexual violence as referred to in Article 25 letter c, includes:
a. acts in the form of sexual harassment, consisting of:
1. physical harassment;
2. verbal harassment;
3. gesture harassment;
4. written or pictorial harassment; and
5. psychological/emotional harassment.
b. forced sexual intercourse;
c. forced sexual intercourse in an unnatural or unwanted manner; and/or
d. forced sexual intercourse with others for commercial purposes and/or certain purposes.
(4) Neglect as referred to in Article 25 letter d, includes:
a. acts that result in the unfulfillment of children's needs properly, whether physical, mental, spiritual or social carried out by parents, guardians, or any other party responsible for their care;
b. acts of deliberately neglecting to maintain, care for, or take care of children as they should be carried out by parents, guardians, or any other party responsible for their care; and/or
c. acts of deliberately neglecting women within their household scope, whereas according to the law applicable to them or because of an agreement or contract they are obligated to provide life, care, or maintenance to the woman.
(5) Exploitation as referred to in Article 25 letter e, includes:
a. acts of exploiting economically or sexually with the intention of benefiting oneself or others;
b. acts with or without the victim's consent which include but are not limited to prostitution, forced labor or services, slavery or similar practices, oppression, extortion, utilization of physical, sexual, reproductive organs, or unlawfully transferring or transplanting organs and/or body tissues or utilizing the labor or ability of someone by other parties to obtain material or immaterial benefits; and
c. all forms of utilization of sexual body organs or other body organs of victims to obtain benefits, but not limited to prostitution or indecency activities.
(6) Other violence as referred to in Article 25 letter f is threats of violence and coercion.
(7) Threats of violence as referred to in paragraph (6) include any unlawful act in the form of words, writing, images, symbols, or body movements, whether with or without using means that cause fear or restrict a person's fundamental freedom.
(8) Coercion as referred to in paragraph (6) includes a situation where a person or victim is ordered to do something in such a way that the person does something contrary to their own will.

CHAPTER VI
RIGHTS OF VICTIMS OF VIOLENCE

Article 27

Women and child victims of violence receive the following rights:
a. right to be respected for dignity as human beings;
b. right to recovery of health and psychology from the suffering experienced by victims;
c. right to determine their own decisions;
d. right to obtain information;
e. right to confidentiality;
f. right to compensation;
g. right to social rehabilitation;
h. right to complaint handling;
i. right to assistance and legal aid;
j. right to obtain visum et repertum free of charge; and
k. right of victims and their families to obtain facilities in the judicial process.

Article 28

Child victims of violence, in addition to receiving the rights as referred to in Article 27 are given special rights, as follows:
a. right to respect and full use for survival, growth and development;
b. right to basic services;
c. right to equal protection;
d. right to be free from various stigma;
e. right to obtain freedom; and
f. right to be tried based on the principle of restorative justice.

CHAPTER VII
OBLIGATIONS AND RESPONSIBILITIES

Part One
General

Article 29

Obligations and responsibilities in implementing protection for women and children are the joint responsibility of:
a. Regional Government;
b. village government;
c. Community; and
d. parents, Guardians and/or Family.

Part Two
Regional Government

Article 30

(1) The Regional Government as referred to in Article 29 letter a, is obligated and responsible to:
a. respect, protect and fulfill the rights of victims and special rights of child victims of violence;
b. prepare and implement programs and activities related to women and child protection;
c. implement protection of women and children from violence;
d. supervise services to victims of violence based on minimum service standards in accordance with provisions of laws and regulations;
e. allocate budget for protection of women and children from violence according to regional financial capacity and budgeted in each fiscal year; and
f. realize a Child-Friendly Regency.
(2) In order to prepare and implement effective women and child protection programs as referred to in paragraph (1) letter b, the Regional Government organizes:
a. gender and child disaggregated data system; and
b. gender responsive and child-friendly planning and budgeting.
(3) The Regional Government may establish a Regional Technical Implementation Unit for Women's Empowerment and Child Protection to reach services in the Region.

Part Three
Village Government

Article 31

(1) Village Government as referred to in Article 29 letter b, is obligated and responsible to:
a. prepare and implement programs and activities related to women and child protection;
b. implement protection of women and children from violence;
c. realize a Child-Friendly village; and
d. allocate budget for protection of women and children from violence according to village financial capacity in each fiscal year.
(2) In order to carry out obligations and responsibilities as referred to in paragraph (1), village government prepares programs and activities for women and child protection action in the village action plan which is part of the medium-term village development plan.

Part Four
Community

Article 32

(1) Community as referred to in Article 29 letter c is obligated and responsible for the protection of women and children through community participation activities in the protection of women and children.
(2) Forms of community participation as referred to in paragraph (1), include:
a. preventing the occurrence of acts of violence against women and children;
b. providing information and/or reporting acts of violence against women and children to law enforcement or authorized parties; and
c. participating in handling victims of acts of violence.

Part Five
Parents, Guardians and/or Family

Article 33

(1) Parents, guardians and/or family as referred to in Article 29 letter d legally have full responsibility to prevent all forms of violence and report if violence occurs and protect victims.
(2) In the event that parents, guardians and/or family as referred to in paragraph (1) do not exist or their whereabouts are unknown or for some reason cannot carry out their obligations and responsibilities, then the obligations and responsibilities may be transferred to the Regional Government and other family members carried out in accordance with provisions of laws and regulations.

CHAPTER VIII
IMPLEMENTATION OF WOMEN AND CHILD PROTECTION

Part One
General

Article 34

The Regional Government carries out activities for implementing women's protection and child protection, which include:
a. prevention;
b. services; and
c. social rehabilitation.

Part Two
Prevention

Article 35

(1) Prevention as referred to in Article 34 letter a, is carried out by:
a. forming a network in efforts to prevent violence;
b. conducting coordination, integration, synchronization of violence prevention based on partnership patterns;
c. forming a violence prevention system;
d. conducting socialization of laws and regulations related to women and child protection; and
e. providing knowledge about prevention and mechanisms for overcoming violence against women and children.
(2) Prevention as referred to in paragraph (1) is carried out in an integrated manner by Regency Regional Apparatus and related agencies that have main tasks and functions in administering affairs of:
a. women's empowerment and child protection;
b. education;
c. health;
d. social;
e. employment;
f. youth and sports; and
g. mental and spiritual.
(3) In addition to being carried out by Regency Regional Apparatus and related agencies as referred to in paragraph (2), prevention is also carried out by:
a. family and/or closest relatives;
b. community, LSM, community organizations, social organizations and religious organizations; and
c. business world.

Part Three
Services

Article 36

Services as referred to in Article 34 letter b, take into account:
a. service principles; and
b. forms of services to victims.

Article 37

Service principles as referred to in Article 36 letter a, consist of:
a. fast;
b. safe and comfortable;
c. sense of empathy;
d. non-discrimination;
e. easily accessible;
f. not charged; and
g. guaranteed confidentiality.

Article 38

Forms of services to victims as referred to in Article 36 letter b, consist of:
a. services to women and child victims of acts of violence, include:
1. complaint, consultation and counseling services;
2. assistance services;
3. health services;
4. legal aid services; and
5. repatriation and social reintegration services.
b. special services to women victims of acts of domestic violence or trafficking in persons, include:
1. complaint services;
2. pick-up services;
3. medical and psychological referral services;
4. shelter services;
5. assistance and legal aid services; and
6. repatriation and social reintegration services.

Article 39

Complaint, consultation and counseling services as referred to in Article 38 letter a number 1, include:
a. initial identification or recording of victims; and
b. consent to take action (informed consent).

Article 40

Assistance services as referred to in Article 38 letter a number 2 include:
a. accompanying victims during the examination and health recovery process;
b. accompanying victims during the psychological recovery process;
c. accompanying victims during the medicolegal process;
d. accompanying victims during the examination process at the police, prosecutor's office and court;
e. monitoring the interests and rights of victims in the examination process at the police, prosecutor's office and court;
f. maintaining the privacy and confidentiality of victims from all parties who are not interested, including news coverage by mass media;
g. coordinating with other assistants; and
h. providing continuous handling up to the rehabilitation stage.

Article 41

Health services as referred to in Article 38 letter a number 3 include:
a. first aid to victims by competent officers;
b. treatment and recovery of physical injuries aimed at recovering the physical condition of victims carried out by medical and paramedical personnel; and
c. referral to health services according to needs.

Article 42

(1) Legal aid services as referred to in Article 38 letter a number 4 to help victims go through the judicial process.
(2) Legal aid services as referred to in paragraph (1) are carried out by:
a. providing legal consultation that includes information regarding the rights of victims and the judicial process;
b. accompanying victims at the investigation, prosecution, and examination levels in court hearings and helping victims to fully explain the violence they experienced; and
c. coordinating with fellow law enforcers, volunteer assistants, and social workers so that the judicial process runs as it should.

Article 43

(1) Repatriation and social reintegration services as referred to in Article 38 letter a number 5 are carried out to return victims to their family and social environment.
(2) Repatriation and social reintegration services as referred to in paragraph (1) are carried out by the Regional Government in coordination with:
a. Regency/City Government within one Provincial area or outside the Province; and/or
b. related agencies and institutions both Government and non-Government.
(3) Forms of services as referred to in Article 38, are carried out according to minimum service standards established by the Government/Regional Government and carried out by Regency Regional Apparatus that has tasks and functions in administering affairs of:
a. women's empowerment and child protection;
b. social;
c. employment;
d. health;
e. education;
f. security and order; and
g. mental and spiritual.
(4) In carrying out tasks and functions as referred to in paragraph (1) the Regional Government cooperates with the Government, Provincial Government, other Regency/City Governments, and the community.

Part Four
Social Rehabilitation

Article 44

(1) Social rehabilitation as referred to in Article 34 letter c is a service provided by assistants to restore the traumatic condition of victims.
(2) Social rehabilitation as referred to in paragraph (1) is carried out by:
a. providing guidance and counseling to victims and parents of victims;
b. psychological recovery of victims;
c. accompanying victims and parents of victims in the family and community environment; and
d. providing safe houses for victims of violence who need continuous handling.
(3) Special social rehabilitation for women victims of domestic violence and trafficking in persons is carried out by:
a. providing guidance and counseling;
b. psychological recovery of victims; and
c. accompanying victims in the family and community environment.
(4) Social rehabilitation as referred to in paragraph (1) is carried out by Regency Regional Apparatus that has main tasks and functions in administering affairs of:
a. social;
b. women's empowerment and child protection;
c. health; and
d. mental and spiritual.
(5) In addition to being carried out by Regency Regional Apparatus as referred to in paragraph (4), social rehabilitation can also be carried out by the community or social service institutions.

CHAPTER IX
INSTITUTIONS

Part One
General

Article 45

To assist women's empowerment and child protection, the Regional Government may establish:
a. Regional Technical Implementation Unit for Women's Empowerment and Child Protection;
b. Integrated Service Center for Women's Empowerment and Child Protection at the Regional level;
c. Women and Child Protection Task Force at Sub-district Level;
d. Women and Child Protection Task Unit at Village Level;
e. Regional Indonesian Child Protection Commission;
f. Regional Children's Forum; and
g. Other institutions or organizations in accordance with provisions of laws and regulations.

Part Two
Regional Technical Implementation Unit
for Women's Empowerment and Child Protection

Article 46

UPTD PPPA as referred to in Article 45 letter a is a technical implementer to carry out operational technical activities in its working area in providing services for women and children experiencing problems of violence, discrimination, special protection and other problems.

Part Three
Integrated Service Center for Women's Empowerment and Child Protection

Article 47

(1) P2TP2A as referred to in Article 45 letter b, is a community-based women's empowerment and child protection service container that functions as an integrated service center in providing protection to women and children.
(2) The composition of P2TP2A members as referred to in paragraph (1) among others consists of:
a. Regional Government elements;
b. academics;
c. legal experts;
d. psychologists;
e. psychiatrists;
f. religious leaders; and
g. community elements.
(3) The main tasks of P2TP2A as referred to in paragraph (1) are:
a. providing protection to women and children; and
b. improving the quality of life of women and children.
(4) Further provisions regarding the establishment of P2TP2A are stipulated by Regent Decree.

Part Four
Women and Child Protection Task Force at Sub-district Level

Article 48

(1) Women and Child Protection Task Force at Sub-district Level as referred to in Article 45 letter c, is a functional work unit that organizes integrated protection services and handling of cases of violence against children and women at the Sub-district level.
(2) Management of Women and Child Protection Task Force at Sub-district Level as referred to in paragraph (1) consists of:
a. Sub-district apparatus elements;
b. agencies or institutions at Sub-district level;
c. Sector Police elements;
d. Military Rayon Command elements;
e. community leaders or religious leaders;
f. youth elements; and
g. Sub-district Family Welfare Development Driving Team elements.
(3) The main tasks of Women and Child Protection Task Force at Sub-district Level as referred to in paragraph (1) are:
a. providing services in the context of women and child protection;
b. receiving community complaints regarding cases of violence against women and children in the Sub-district and Village areas;
c. facilitating women and children who are victims of violence in resolving their problems;
d. socializing to the community about women and child protection; and
e. providing assistance to victims or referring to P2TP2A, sector police/resort police, hospitals or other institutions as needed.
(4) Further provisions regarding the establishment, tasks, and functions of women and child protection task force at sub-district level as referred to in paragraph (1), are stipulated by Sub-district Head Decree.

Part Five
Women and Child Protection Task Unit at Village Level

Article 49

(1) Women and Child Protection Task Unit at Village Level as referred to in Article 45 letter d is a functional work unit that organizes integrated protection services and handling of cases of violence against children and women at the Village level.
(2) Management of Women and Child Protection Task Unit at Village Level as referred to in paragraph (1) consists of:
a. Village apparatus elements;
b. Village or Sub-district Guidance Non-Commissioned Officer elements;
c. Community Guidance Non-Commissioned Officer elements;
d. community leaders or religious leaders;
e. youth elements; and
f. Village Family Welfare Development Driving Team elements.
(3) The main tasks of Women and Child Protection Task Unit at Village Level as referred to in paragraph (1) are:
a. providing services in the context of women and child protection;
b. receiving community complaints regarding cases of violence against women and children in the Village area;
c. facilitating women and children who are victims of violence in resolving their problems;
d. socializing to the community about women and child protection; and
e. providing assistance to victims or referring to women and child protection task force at Sub-district level.
(4) Further provisions regarding the establishment, tasks and functions of women and child protection task unit at village level as referred to in paragraph (1) are stipulated by village head decree.

Part Six
Regional Indonesian Child Protection Commission

Article 50

(1) Regional Indonesian Child Protection Commission as referred to in Article 45 letter e is a partner of the Government in efforts to fulfill children's rights and child protection and is positioned at the Regency level.
(2) Regional Indonesian Child Protection Commission is stipulated by Regent decree.
(3) Membership of Regional Indonesian Child Protection Commission consists of 1 (one) chairperson, 1 (one) vice chairperson, and 7 (seven) members.
(4) Membership of Regional Indonesian Child Protection Commission comes from elements of Regional Government, religious leaders, community leaders, community organizations, business world, and community groups concerned with child protection.

Article 51

(1) Regional Indonesian Child Protection Commission as referred to in Article 45 letter e has detailed tasks including:
a. conducting supervision of the implementation of child protection and fulfillment of children's rights;
b. providing input and proposals in formulating policies on child protection implementation;
c. collecting data and information regarding child protection;
d. receiving and conducting reviews of community complaints regarding violations of children's rights;
e. conducting mediation on disputes over violations of children's rights;
f. conducting cooperation with institutions formed by the community in the field of child protection; and
g. other tasks in accordance with provisions of laws and regulations.
(2) In the event of changes to laws and regulations regarding Regional Indonesian Child Protection Commission, then the regulation regarding Regional Indonesian Child Protection Commission is guided by laws and regulations further regulated by Regent Regulation.

Part Seven
Regional Children's Forum

Article 52

(1) Regional Children's Forum as referred to in Article 45 letter f is an organization or social institution used as a container or institution for child participation for children under 18 (eighteen) years of age where its members are representatives from child groups or child activity groups managed by children and fostered by the government as a medium to hear and fulfill children's aspirations, voices, opinions, desires and needs in the development process.
(2) Regional Children's Forum as referred to in paragraph (1) is formed in tiers at all levels namely:
a. Regency Level;
b. Sub-district Level; and
c. Village Level.

Paragraph 1
Children's Forum at Regency Level

Article 53

(1) Children's Forum at Regency Level as referred to in Article 52 paragraph (2) letter a is a container for child participation in the Regency area whose members are representatives of Children's Forum at Sub-district Level at least 1 (one) girl and 1 (one) boy.
(2) Children's Forum at Regency Level is stipulated by Regent Decree with a management period of 2 (two) years.
(3) In order for Children's Forum at Regency Level to run well, a secretariat must be formed.
(4) Formation of the secretariat as referred to in paragraph (3) may involve at most 40% (forty percent) unmarried adults.
(5) Involvement of adults as referred to in paragraph (4) is intended to guide and foster new members at Children's Forum at Regency Level.
(6) Regency Regional Apparatus that handles women's empowerment and child protection as Supervisor of Children's Forum at Regency Level facilitates the formation of Children's Forum at Regency Level starting from the selection stage, validation of management and coaching in capacity building of members of Children's Forum at Regency Level.

Paragraph 2
Children's Forum at Sub-district Level

Article 54

(1) Children's Forum at Sub-district Level as referred to in Article 52 paragraph (2) letter b is a container for child participation in the sub-district area whose members are representatives of Children's Forum at Village Level at least 1 (one) girl and 1 (one) boy.
(2) Children's Forum at Sub-district Level is stipulated by Sub-district Head Decree with a management period of 2 (two) years.
(3) Sub-district Government as Supervisor of Children's Forum at Sub-district Level facilitates the formation of Children's Forum at Sub-district Level starting from the selection stage, validation of management and coaching in capacity building of members of Children's Forum at Sub-district Level.

Paragraph 3
Children's Forum at Village Level

Article 55

(1) Children's Forum at Village Level as referred to in Article 52 paragraph (2) letter c is a container for child participation in the Village area whose members are representatives from Neighborhood Units and Community Units at least 1 (one) girl and 1 (one) boy.
(2) Children's Forum at Village Level is stipulated by Village Head Decree with a management period of 2 (two) years.
(3) Village Government as Supervisor of Children's Forum at Village Level facilitates the formation of Children's Forum at Village Level starting from the selection stage, validation of management and coaching in capacity building of members of Children's Forum at Village Level.
(4) Community institutions in the Village are obligated to accommodate and foster child participation in development and in fighting for children's rights.

CHAPTER X
CHILD-FRIENDLY REGENCY

Article 56

(1) The Regional Government is obligated to organize a Child-Friendly Regency implementation system in order to fulfill children's rights.
(2) Development of Child-Friendly Regency is carried out based on the following principles:
a. good governance, namely transparency, accountability, participation, information openness, and rule of law;
b. non-discrimination, namely not distinguishing ethnicity, race, religion, gender, language, political views, national origin, economic status, physical or psychological condition of children, or other factors;
c. best interests of children, namely making what is best for children as the main consideration in every policy, program, and activity;
d. right to life, survival, and development of children, namely guaranteeing the right to life, survival and development of children to the maximum extent possible; and
e. respect for children's views, namely recognizing and ensuring that every child who has the ability to express their opinion, is given the opportunity to express their views freely on all matters affecting them.
(3) Equipment for implementing Child-Friendly Regency as referred to in paragraph (1) includes:
a. development and public service system;
b. Child-Friendly Health Services;
c. Child-Friendly Family;
d. Child-Friendly School; and
e. Child-Friendly Environment;
f. Child-Friendly Islamic Boarding School.
(4) The Regional Government in organizing Child-Friendly Regency as referred to in paragraph (1) must pay attention to Child-Friendly Regency Indicators which include:
a. institutions; and
b. child rights clusters.
(5) Institutional strengthening as referred to in paragraph (4) letter a includes:
a. existence of laws and regulations and policies for fulfillment of children's rights;
b. percentage of budget for fulfillment of children's rights, including budget for institutional strengthening;
c. number of laws and regulations, policies, programs and activities that receive input from Children's Forum and other child groups;
d. availability of trained Human Resources (HR) in Convention on the Rights of the Child (KHA) and able to apply children's rights into policies, programs, and activities;
e. availability of disaggregated child data by gender, age, and sub-district;
f. involvement of community institutions in fulfillment of children's rights; and
g. involvement of business world in fulfillment of children's rights.

Article 57

Child rights clusters as referred to in Article 56 paragraph (4) letter b which include:
a. Civil rights and freedoms which include:
1) children's right to identity;
2) right to identity protection;
3) right to express and issue opinions;
4) right to think, have conscience, religion, and belief;
5) right to organize and assemble peacefully;
6) right to protection of private life;
7) right to access appropriate information; and
8) right to be free from torture and other cruel, inhuman, or degrading punishment or treatment.
b. Right to family environment and care which include:
1) parental guidance and responsibility;
2) children separated from parents;
3) reunification;
4) illegal transfer of children;
5) welfare support;
6) children forced to be separated from family environment;
7) child adoption;
8) periodic placement review; and
9) violence and neglect.
c. Children's rights in basic health and welfare which include:
a. children with disabilities receive access to health services and welfare;
b. health and health services;
c. social security health services and facilities; and
d. standard of living;
d. Children's rights in education, leisure time use and cultural activities which include:
1) education;
2) goals of education; and
3) holiday activities, cultural activities, and sports.
e. Protection for children in special protection which includes:
1) children in emergency situations;
2) children in conflict with the law;
3) children in exploitation situations; and
4) children in minority and isolated groups.

Article 58

(1) To make the development of Child-Friendly Regency effective, a Child-Friendly Regency Task Force is formed which is stipulated by Regent Decree.
(2) Membership of Child-Friendly Regency Task Force as referred to in paragraph (1) may come from Regional Government, child representatives, business world, religious leaders, community leaders and other parties as needed.
(3) In carrying out main tasks, members of Child-Friendly Regency Task Force as referred to in paragraph (2) have detailed tasks including:
a. coordinating various efforts to develop Child-Friendly Regency;
b. preparing Regional Action Plan for Child-Friendly Regency Development;
c. conducting socialization, advocacy and communication for Child-Friendly Regency development;
d. monitoring the implementation of policies, programs and activities in the Regional Action Plan for Child-Friendly Regency Development;
e. conducting evaluation at the end of each year on the implementation of policies, programs and activities in the Regional Action Plan for Child-Friendly Regency Development; and
f. submitting reports to the Regent.

Article 59

(1) Regional Action Plan as referred to in Article 58 paragraph (3) letter b is prepared for a period of 5 (five) years or according to needs that are integrated with the medium-term regional development plan, and regional apparatus work plan.
(2) Regional Action Plan has an annual program focus that refers to the stages of achieving Child-Friendly Regency indicators.
(3) Regional Action Plan for Child-Friendly Regency must be based on problems in the field and their comprehensive resolution.
(4) Regional Action Plan is socialized to all Regional Apparatus, child stakeholders, families, and the community.
(5) Further provisions regarding Regional Action Plan for Child-Friendly Regency are stipulated by Regent Regulation.

CHAPTER XI
MONITORING, EVALUATION, AND REPORTING

Article 60

Monitoring is carried out regularly and in an integrated manner every 6 (six) months by Regional Apparatus that administers government affairs in the field of women's empowerment and child protection in coordination with Regional Apparatus or other units that handle women's empowerment and child protection.

Article 61

Evaluation of women's empowerment and child protection implementation is carried out by Regional Apparatus that administers government affairs in the field of women's empowerment and child protection at least 2 (two) times in 1 (one) year.

Article 62

(1) Reporting on women's empowerment and child protection implementation is carried out by:
a. P2TP2A to the Regent;
b. Women and Child Protection Task Force at Sub-district Level to the Sub-district Head;
c. Women and Child Protection Task Unit at Village Level to the Village Head; and
d. Regional Indonesian Child Protection Commission to the Regent through the Head of Regional Apparatus that administers government affairs in the field of women's empowerment and child protection.
(2) Reporting as referred to in paragraph (1) is submitted in writing, including:
a. administration;
b. finance;
c. services; and
d. performance.
(3) Submission of written reports as referred to in paragraph (2), is carried out at least every 6 (six) months.

CHAPTER XII
FUNDING

Article 63

Funding for women's empowerment and child protection is sourced from:
a. regional revenue and expenditure budget;
b. village revenue and expenditure budget; and
c. other legitimate sources in accordance with provisions of laws and regulations.

CHAPTER XIII
SANCTIONS

Article 64

(1) Every person who deliberately violates the provisions of Article 10 or does not provide protection against forms of violence against women and children as referred to in Article 25 and Article 26, and does not provide the rights of women and children as referred to in Article 27 and Article 28, thus causing violence, allowing violence to occur, and/or not reporting and not providing protection to victims, is subject to sanctions as regulated in laws and regulations.
(2) Regency Regional Apparatus that has tasks and functions to administer women's empowerment and child protection that does not carry out its obligations and responsibilities is subject to administrative sanctions.
(3) Management of P2TP2A, Women and Child Protection Task Force at Sub-district Level, Women and Child Protection Task Unit at Village Level and other institutions or organizations in accordance with provisions of laws and regulations that carry out protection tasks, if they violate protection principles, may be subject to sanctions in accordance with mechanisms applicable within each institution or organization.

CHAPTER XIV
CLOSING PROVISIONS

Article 65

This Regional Regulation comes into force on the date of promulgation.
In order that everyone may know it, orders the promulgation of this Regional Regulation by placing it in the Regional Gazette of Tasikmalaya Regency.
