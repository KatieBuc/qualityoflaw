PROTECTION OF WOMEN AND CHILDREN

REGENT OF MUARA ENIM

PROVINCE OF SOUTH SUMATRA

REGIONAL REGULATION OF MUARA ENIM REGENCY

NUMBER 5 OF 2023

CONCERNING

PROTECTION OF WOMEN AND CHILDREN

BY THE GRACE OF ALMIGHTY GOD

REGENT OF MUARA ENIM,

Considering:

a. that to guarantee the Protection of Women and Children in accordance with their dignity and to guarantee their right to life in accordance with their nature and disposition without discrimination and to receive Protection from violence and other violations, legal certainty is necessary;
b. that to provide legal certainty in the implementation of Protection of Women and Children and to carry out mandatory regional government affairs, regulation in the form of a Regional Regulation is necessary;
c. that based on the considerations as referred to in letter a and letter b, it is necessary to establish a Regional Regulation concerning the Protection of Women and Children;

In View of:
1. Article 18 paragraph (6) of the 1945 Constitution of the Republic of Indonesia;

REGIONAL REGULATION CONCERNING PROTECTION OF WOMEN AND CHILDREN.

CHAPTER I

GENERAL PROVISIONS

Article 1

In this Regional Regulation, what is meant by:
1. Regency is Muara Enim Regency.
2. Regency Government is Muara Enim Regency.
3. Regent is the Regent of Muara Enim.
4. Regional Apparatus is the Regional Apparatus within the Muara Enim Regency Government.
5. Women's Empowerment is the strengthening of human rights, enhancement of Protection efforts, quality of life, and enhancement of women's participation in development.
6. Women's Protection is all activities aimed at providing a sense of security carried out by the Regional Government, police, prosecutor's office, courts, social institutions, or other parties who know or hear that violence against women will or has occurred;
7. Child Protection is all activities to guarantee and protect Children and their rights so that they can live, grow, develop, and participate optimally in accordance with human dignity and worth, and receive Protection from Violence and discrimination.
7. Child is a person who has not yet reached the age of 18 (eighteen) years, including a Child who is still in the womb and has not married.
8. Prevention is direct efforts carried out by the Regional Government, community and parents and/or family to prevent violations of the rights of women and Children.
9. Violence is any act that results in misery or suffering whether physical, sexual, psychological and/or domestic neglect including threats to commit acts, coercion, or deprivation of a person's freedom unlawfully.
10. Child Forum is a forum for Child participation formed with the aim of facilitating the involvement of Children in decision-making processes and enjoying changes relating to their lives either directly or indirectly, which is carried out with the consent and will of all Children based on awareness and understanding, in accordance with age and level of thinking maturity.
11. Child-Friendly Regency hereinafter abbreviated as KLA is a Regency that has a Child rights-based development system through the integration of government, community and business commitments and resources that are planned comprehensively and sustainably in policies, programs and activities to ensure the fulfillment of Children's rights.
12. Gender Mainstreaming is a strategy built to integrate gender into an integral dimension of planning, formulation, implementation, monitoring and evaluation of national development program policies.
13. Community is individuals, families, groups and social organizations and/or community organizations.
14. Women Victims of Violence are women who experience actions that result in misery or suffering physically, sexually or psychologically, including threats of certain actions, coercion or deprivation of freedom arbitrarily, whether occurring in the public sphere or in private life.
15. Child Victims of Violence are Children who experience actions that result in misery or suffering physically, mentally, sexually, psychologically or neglect that threatens life, body and degrades human dignity.
16. Human trafficking is the act of recruitment, transportation, harboring, sending, transfer, or receipt of a person by threat of Violence, use of Violence, kidnapping, confinement, forgery, fraud, abuse of power or position of vulnerability, debt bondage or giving payment or benefits, so as to obtain consent from the person holding control over that other person, whether carried out within the Country or between Countries, for the purpose of exploitation or resulting in the person being exploited.
17. Complaint handling is a series of actions carried out by integrated service providers to follow up on reports of acts of Violence against women and Children, submitted by victims, families or the community.
18. Social rehabilitation is services aimed at restoring and developing the ability of a person experiencing social dysfunction so that they can carry out their social functions normally.
19. Law enforcement is the action of officials authorized by the state to implement laws and regulations.
20. Legal aid is legal services provided by legal assistants and advocates to carry out the process of assisting witnesses and/or victims of Violence against women and Children that is gender-sensitive.
21. Repatriation is an effort to return women and Child victims of Violence from abroad to the debarkation point/entry point, or from the receiving Region to the Region of origin.
22. Social reintegration is an effort to reunite victims with family, substitute family, or community that can provide Protection and fulfillment of needs for victims.
23. Safe house is a temporary shelter that aims to provide Protection and a sense of security to victims of Violence.
24. Shelter house is a forum for guidance and welfare services for street children that carries out assistance/social guidance activities, maintenance, care, education, and health, skills guidance in order to ensure that Children do not carry out activities on the streets so that they can grow and develop normally.
25. Spiritual Guidance is an effort to restore the psyche through strengthening the religion adhered to by the victim.
26. Gender Mainstreaming is a strategy built to integrate gender into an integral dimension of planning, formulation, implementation, monitoring, and evaluation of development policies, programs, and activities in the Region.
27. Gender responsive is consistent and systematic attention to the differences between women and men in society accompanied by efforts to eliminate structural and cultural barriers in achieving gender equality.
28. Gender Sensitivity or Gender Awareness is the ability to identify gaps in power relations between women and men in the family and in the community, the impact of gender-based division of labor on women and men, and the experiences, problems, needs, interests and aspirations of women and men are different.
29. Public service providers are every state organizing institution, corporation, independent institution formed based on Law for public service activities, and other legal entities formed solely for public service activities.
30. Community Organization is an organization established and formed by the community voluntarily based on common aspirations, will, needs, interests, activities and goals to participate in development for the achievement of the goals of the Unitary State of the Republic of Indonesia based on Pancasila.
31. Parents are biological father and/or mother, or step father and/or mother, or adoptive father and/or mother.
32. Family is the smallest unit in society consisting of husband and wife or husband, wife and their Child, or father and his Child, or mother and her Child, or blood relatives in a straight line upward or downward up to the third degree.
33. Customs are eternal patterns of behavior passed down from one generation to another generation as heritage, so that they are strongly integrated with community behavior patterns.
32. Best interests are the best interests for women and children.

Article 2

The implementation of Protection of Women and Children is based on the principles of:
a. best interests of women and Children;
b. gender justice and gender equality;
c. local wisdom;
d. law enforcement and legal certainty.
e. non-discrimination;
f. right to life, survival, and development;
g. respect for the opinion of Children; and
h. law enforcement and legal certainty.

Article 3

This Regional Regulation aims to:
a. guarantee the fulfillment of the rights of women and Children in the Regency;
b. serve as a guideline for the Region in planning, policy and strategy for the Protection of Women and Children;
c. protect and prevent women and Children from actions, decisions and policies that violate the rights of women and Children;
d. enhance the role and value of local wisdom as well as the role of custom in the Protection of Women and Children;
e. serve as a legal umbrella for regional apparatus in charge of the protection of women and children in order to provide assistance and support to women and child victims.

Article 4

The scope of regulation of the Protection of Women and Children includes:
a. protection of women;
b. protection of Children;
c. fulfillment of the rights of Children;
d. improvement of the quality of life of women;
e. gender and Child data system, disaggregated data on women and children, and institutional strengthening of Gender Mainstreaming through Gender Responsive Planning and Budgeting;
f. community participation; and
g. guidance, supervision, assistance.

CHAPTER II

IMPROVEMENT OF THE QUALITY OF LIFE OF WOMEN

Part One

General

Article 5

1. In order to improve the quality of life of women, the regional government is responsible for:
a. respecting and guaranteeing the rights of women without discrimination in accordance with human dignity;
b. implementing Gender Mainstreaming in the Regency;
c. establishing a Gender Mainstreaming working group in the Region;
d. improving the Empowerment of Women in their role, quality and capacity in the fields of (9)
1\. politics;
2\. law;
3\. social;
4\. economy; and
5\. culture.
e. strengthening and developing institutions providing Women's Empowerment services in the Regency.
2. The responsibility of the Regency Government in efforts to improve the quality of life of women as referred to in paragraph (1) shall be carried out by regional apparatus in accordance with their duties and functions.

Part Two

Improvement of the Role, Quality and Capacity of Women in the Political Field

Article 6

Efforts to improve the role, quality and capacity of women in the political field as referred to in Article 5 paragraph (1) letter d number 1 include:
a. conducting socialization to increase the political participation of women in general elections;
b. implementing programs that encourage the level of involvement of women in political processes and public office;
c. facilitating the self-development of women in organizing, associating and assembling; and
d. involving women in decision-making concerning the interests of women.

Part Three

Improvement of the Role, Quality, and Capacity of Women in the Legal Field

Article 7

Efforts to improve the role, quality and capacity of women in the legal field as referred to in Article 5 paragraph (1) letter d number 2 include:
a. conducting socialization to increase the legal knowledge and awareness of women;
b. mapping and reviewing gender-biased policies; and
c. involving women's organizations in the process of drafting and discussing Regional legal products related to the participation of women.

Part Four

Improvement of the Role, Quality, and Capacity of Women in the Social Field

Article 8

Efforts to improve the role, quality and capacity of women in the social field as referred to in Article 5 paragraph (1) letter d number 3 include:
a. improvement of knowledge and skills to encourage the fulfillment of education in accordance with the potential possessed;
b. improvement and fulfillment of health quality for women;
c. improvement of awareness and knowledge about independent, healthy and prosperous family planning including access to consultation services and marriage registration;
d. improvement of women's awareness of the impact of early marriage;
e. facilitating and seeking the preservation of customs and the development of science, technology, arts and culture for the advancement of women;
f. improving the awareness and knowledge of women regarding independent, healthy and prosperous family planning;
g. implementing programs to reduce maternal mortality rates; and
h. encouraging the formation of women's organizations in the Regency.

Part Five

Improvement of the Role, Quality, and Capacity of Women in the Economic Field

Article 9

Efforts to improve the role, quality and capacity of women in the economic field as referred to in Article 5 paragraph (1) letter d number 4 include:
a. implementing programs, activities, training to improve the skills and expertise of women in order to realize the improvement of women's welfare;
b. facilitating the strengthening of business capital for women with weak economies;
c. marketing products from the businesses of women with weak economies;
d. training to develop the capacity of women through economic empowerment of pre-prosperous families; and
e. comprehensive entrepreneurship training for women.

CHAPTER III

PROTECTION OF WOMEN

Part One

General

Article 10

1. The Regency Government in its efforts to Protect women is responsible for:
a. establishing and implementing policies, programs and activities for the protection of women;
b. making efforts to prevent acts of Violence against women including criminal acts of trafficking in persons;
c. increasing the effectiveness of services for women victims of acts of Violence;
d. increasing efforts to protect women workers;
e. strengthening and developing institutions providing women's protection services;
f. cooperating with the Government, other Regional Governments, the private sector, the business world and/or Non-Governmental Organizations in efforts to prevent acts of Violence against women;
g. involving the active role of families, customary leaders, religious leaders, the community, and institutions in the field of women's protection.
2. The responsibility of the Regency Government in efforts to protect women as referred to in paragraph (1) shall be carried out by regional apparatus in accordance with their duties and functions and may coordinate with relevant agencies.

Part Two

Prevention of Acts of Violence Against Women

Article 11

In order to prevent acts of Violence against women, the Regency Government:
a. conducts socialization regarding the prevention of violence against women by optimizing the role of customary, cultural, religious and community organizations;
b. builds networks and cooperation with law enforcement officials, government officials, universities and various Non-Governmental Organizations that work and/or care about women and children victims of Violence;
c. provides public facilities that are comfortable and safe for women;
d. establishes a Regional Technical Service Unit for the Protection of Women and Children and provides a secretariat for the Regional Technical Service Unit for the Protection of Women and Children.

Part Three

Effectiveness of Services for Women Victims of Acts of Violence

Paragraph 1

General

Article 12

1. Protection of women victims of acts of Violence is implemented through integrated services.
2. The provision of integrated services to women victims of acts of Violence as referred to in paragraph (1) uses the principles of:
a. gender responsiveness;
b. non-discrimination;
c. equal and respectful relationships;
d. maintaining confidentiality;
e. providing a sense of security and safety;
f. respecting individual differences;
g. non-judgmental;
h. respecting the choices and decisions of victims;
i. sensitivity to the background and condition of victims and the use of appropriate language understood by victims;
j. fast and simple;
k. empathy; and
l. fulfillment of women's rights.

Article 13

Forms of integrated services for women victims of acts of violence include:
a. complaint handling;
b. health services;
c. social rehabilitation;
d. victim assistance, law enforcement and legal aid; and
e. repatriation and social reintegration.

Paragraph 2

Complaint Handling

Article 14

Integrated services for complaint handling as referred to in Article 13 letter a are implemented by:
a. Hospitals, Community Health Centers and other health service facilities;
b. Technical Implementation Unit for the Protection of Women and Children;
c. Trauma Center Shelter Homes,d. Women crisis centre;
e. Advisory Board for Marriage Guidance and Preservation;
f. Police;
g. Prosecutor's Office;
h. Courts;
i. Ministry of Religious Affairs; and/or
j. Legal Aid Institutions.

{14)

Article 15

1. Complaints of acts of violence against women may be made by reporters consisting of:
a. victims;
b. family;
c. community; and/or
d. community organizations in the field of protection of women and children.
2. Reporters as referred to in paragraph (1) may file complaints:
a. directly;
b. by telephone; and/or
c. by letter.
3. In addition to handling complaints submitted by reporters, the agency responsible for the Protection of Women and Children, officers at service institutions as referred to in Article 13, are also authorized to provide assistance in handling victims of acts of violence against women obtained through other information.
4. Further provisions regarding procedures for complaint handling services as referred to in paragraphs (1) and (2) shall be regulated by Regent Regulation.

Paragraph 3

Health Rehabilitation

Article 16

1. Health rehabilitation as referred to in Article 13 letter b constitutes efforts that include aspects of:
a. promotive;
b. preventive;
c. curative; and
d. rehabilitative.
2. Health rehabilitation as referred to in paragraph (1) shall be conducted comprehensively, accessible 24 (twenty-four) hours, of quality and conducted in accordance with health service standards.
3. Women and children victims of acts of violence and children in conflict with the law have the right to free health services, including:
a. medical services;
b. medicolegal services;
c. psychosocial counseling; and
d. visum.
4. Further provisions regarding procedures for implementing health services as referred to in paragraph (3) shall be regulated by Regent Regulation.

Paragraph 4

Social Rehabilitation

Article 17

1. Social rehabilitation as referred to in Article 13 letter c is aimed at restoring and developing the capacity of women victims of acts of Violence who experience social dysfunction to be able to carry out their social functions in society normally.
2. Social rehabilitation as referred to in paragraph (1) includes services for:
a. motivation and psychosocial diagnosis;
b. care and nurturing;
c. vocational training and entrepreneurship development;
d. spiritual mental guidance;
e. physical guidance;
f. social guidance and psychosocial counseling;
g. accessibility services;
h. social assistance and aid;
i. resocialization guidance;
j. follow-up guidance; and/or
k. referral.
3. The provision of services as referred to in paragraph (2) shall be organized by:
a. regional apparatus having duties and functions in the social field;
b. regional apparatus responsible for Women's Empowerment, and may cooperate with integrated service centers, women crisis centres or existing social community organizations; orc. Ministry of Religious Affairs.

Article 18

1. Counseling services as referred to in Article 17 paragraph (2) letter a aim to:
a. assist women victims of acts of violence in recognizing their problems and finding effective ways to overcome them themselves;
b. empower women victims of acts of violence to determine their future;
c. provide moral support to women victims of acts of violence in facing the process they are undergoing; and.
d. make women victims of acts of violence feel accepted in the community environment.
2. Counseling services are provided by gender-responsive social rehabilitation officers, consisting of:
a. counselors;
b. psychologists; and
c. psychiatrists.
3. Counseling services shall be conducted in a place that guarantees a sense of security, comfort and confidentiality of information from women victims of acts of violence.
4. Stages of counseling services include:
a. initial counseling;
b. follow-up counseling;
c. case discussion; and
d. site visits.

Article 19

1. The Regency Government may provide safe houses for women victims of acts of violence.
2. The provision of safe house services as referred to in Article 17 paragraph (2) letter b aims to protect women victims of acts of violence from intimidation by perpetrators or other parties.
3. The location of safe houses as referred to in paragraph (1) must be kept confidential.
4. Criteria for women victims of acts of violence who require safe houses include:
a. life threatened;
b. receiving rejection from family or community;
c. requiring intensive services but their residence is relatively far; and/or
d. abandoned if not placed in a safe house.
4. The provision of safe house services shall be carried out by social rehabilitation officers who have gender sensitivity or gender awareness, consisting of:
a. social workers/volunteers;
b. security officers; and/or
c. community leaders.
5. Further provisions regarding the provision of safe house services shall be regulated in a Regent Regulation.

Article 20

1. Spiritual guidance as referred to in Article 17 paragraph (2) letter d aims at psychological recovery.
2. Spiritual guidance as referred to in paragraph (1) shall be implemented based on the principles of:
a. not imposing any religion or belief; and
b. leaving the final decision in the hands of the victim (self determination).
3. Spiritual guidance shall be provided by spiritual guidance officers.
4. Spiritual guidance officers as referred to in paragraph (3) consist of consultants from the Advisory Board for Marriage Guidance and Preservation, Officers of the Office of Religious Affairs, Ustads/Ulama, Priests, Pastors and other religious leaders who have gender sensitivity or gender awareness.

Paragraph 5

Victim Assistance, Law Enforcement and Legal Aid

Article 21

1. Victim assistance, law enforcement and legal aid services as referred to in Article 13 letter d constitute actions by law enforcement officials to implement laws and regulations in handling cases of acts of violence against women, especially to impose sanctions on perpetrators and provide protection for witnesses and/or victims.
2. Victim assistance, law enforcement and legal aid services as referred to in paragraph (1) shall be conducted by prioritizing the best interests of victims.

Article 22

1. Victim assistance, enforcement and legal aid services are intended to provide assistance and protection to women victims of violence and witnesses with a gender-responsive approach so that victims obtain justice and legal certainty.
2. Types of legal aid services include:
a. legal aid in the reporting process and preparation of official examination reports;
b. legal aid in the prosecution process at the prosecutor's office;
c. legal aid in the examination process at court;
d. legal aid in the court hearing decision process until a final legal decision is reached; and
e. legal aid in the mediation process.
3. The Regional Government provides legal aid to women victims of violence based on the provisions of laws and regulations.
4. In providing legal aid as referred to in paragraph (3), the Regional Government may cooperate with legal aid institutions, women crisis centers, advocates and/or other legal assistants who are gender sensitive.

Paragraph 6

Repatriation and Social Reintegration

Article 23

1. Repatriation is intended to return women victims of violence to their region of origin.
2. Repatriation of women victims of violence is accompanied by social reintegration efforts.

Article 24

1. Social reintegration is carried out in order to reunite women victims of violence with family, substitute family, or community that can provide protection and fulfillment of needs for victims.
2. Social reintegration is intended so that:
a. women victims of violence can resume their social life; and
b. women victims of violence can have a decent life.
3. Social reintegration as referred to in paragraph (1) is carried out after the victim receives social rehabilitation services.

Article 25

The Regency Government implements repatriation and social reintegration of women victims of violence in accordance with laws and regulations.

Part Four

Employment Protection

Article 26

1. Employers who employ women must maintain decency, safety and security while at the workplace.
2. Employers must place female workers in places that are safe for women's reproductive health.
3. Employers must fulfill the rights of female workers to receive wages in accordance with the provisions of laws and regulations.
4. Employers must provide special breastfeeding facilities at their company.

Article 27

1. Female workers/laborers under the age of 18 (eighteen) years are prohibited from being employed between 23.00 WIB until 07.00 WIB.
2. Employers are prohibited from employing pregnant female workers/laborers who according to a doctor's statement are dangerous to the health and safety of their pregnancy.
3. Employers who employ female workers/laborers between 23.00 WIB until 07.00 WIB, must:
a. provide nutritious food and drinks; and
b. provide shuttle transportation for female workers/laborers who depart and return from work between 23.00 WIB until 05.00 WIB.

CHAPTER IV

IMPROVING FAMILY QUALITY

Article 28

1. In order to improve the quality of family life, the Regency Government is responsible for:
a. making efforts to improve family quality to realize gender equality and children's rights;
b. providing services for families in realizing gender equality and children's rights; and
c. strengthening and developing service provider institutions for improving family quality in the Regency.
2. The responsibility of the Regency Government in efforts to improve family quality as referred to in paragraph (1) is implemented by regional apparatus in accordance with their duties and functions.

Article 29

The Regional Government makes efforts to improve family quality as referred to in Article 28 paragraph (1) letter a including:
a. guidance;
b. counseling;
c. supervision;
d. facilitation of family development implementation; and
e. improving the health quality of pregnant and breastfeeding mothers.

Article 30

Guidance as referred to in Article 29 letter a, includes:
a. growth and development of toddlers;
b. protection of adolescent reproductive health;
c. maturation of marriage age;
d. birth regulation; and
e. population management.

Article 31

Counseling as referred to in Article 28 letter b includes:
a. conducting outreach to improve the quality of life of women; and
b. conducting premarital counseling.

Article 32

Supervision as referred to in Article 28 letter c includes:
a. conducting evaluation of toddler growth and development data; and
b. conducting evaluation of post-marriage data.

Article 33

Provision of services for families as referred to in Article 28 paragraph (1) letter b includes:
a. communication, information and education;
b. contraceptive tools and medicines;
c. recording and reporting of family planning services;
d. adequate health service facilities and infrastructure;
e. lactation rooms in every public facility.

Article 34

Communication, information and education as referred to in Article 33 letter a is a communication activity to increase knowledge and improve the attitudes and behavior of families and communities in population and family planning programs.

Article 35

1. In addition to the Regency Government, private parties (private parties include companies that provide public services) must provide lactation rooms.
2. Lactation rooms as referred to in paragraph (1) are proper and adequate rooms.
3. Private parties that do not provide lactation rooms as referred to in paragraph (2) are subject to administrative sanctions in the form of:
a. written warning;
b. limitation of business activities;
c. freezing of business activities;
d. administrative fines; and/or
e. revocation of permits.

CHAPTER V

FULFILLMENT OF CHILDREN'S RIGHTS

Part One

General

Article 36

1. The Regency Government in efforts to fulfill children's rights is responsible for:
a. establishing and implementing policies and programs/activities for the fulfillment of children's rights;
b. respecting and guaranteeing the human rights of every child without distinguishing ethnicity, religion, race, class, gender, ethnicity, culture and language, legal status of the child, birth order of the child, and physical and/or mental condition;
c. strengthening and developing service provider institutions for improving family quality in realizing children's rights;
d. guaranteeing the protection, care, and welfare of children with due regard to the rights and obligations of parents, guardians, or other persons legally responsible for the child;
e. providing facilities and infrastructure in the implementation of the fulfillment of children's rights;
f. guaranteeing the right of children to express opinions in accordance with the age and level of intelligence of the child;
g. conducting supervision and guidance in the context of implementation; and
h. cooperating with private parties and/or Non-Governmental Organizations in the implementation of child protection.
2. The responsibility of the Regency Government in efforts to protect children as referred to in paragraph (1) is implemented by regional apparatus in accordance with their duties and functions.

Part Two

Birth Registration

Article 37

1. The Regency Government is obliged to organize birth registration of children with the issuance of birth certificates.
2. Birth certificates as referred to in paragraph (1) are organized free of charge.

Part Three

Education

Article 38

The Regency Government guarantees the implementation of a 9 (nine) year compulsory education program for children aged 7 (seven) to 15 (fifteen) years.

Article 39

1. Every education unit organizer is obliged to provide opportunities for children to obtain educational services without discrimination.
2. Every education unit organizer is prohibited from expelling children from educational institutions unless there is a guarantee for the continuity of the child's education.
3. In the event that a child victim of Violence experiences dropping out of school, the education unit organizer is obliged to coordinate with relevant agencies.

Article 40

1. Every child has the right to obtain education and teaching in the context of developing their personality and level of intelligence in accordance with their interests and talents.
2. Every child has the right to receive protection in education units from sexual crimes and violence committed by educators, education personnel, fellow students, and/or other parties.

Article 41

1. Children with disabilities and children with special needs are given equal opportunities to obtain formal, non-formal and informal educational services, including inclusive education programs.
2. The Regency Government provides teaching personnel for inclusive education at every education unit.

Part Four

Health and Social

Article 42

1. The Regency Government is obliged to provide comprehensive health services for children, so that every Child obtains optimal health status from the womb.
2. Comprehensive health services as referred to in paragraph (1) include:
a. promotive, preventive, curative and rehabilitative efforts for basic health services;
b. exemption from cost burdens for malnourished Children, children with disabilities, children with special needs, children infected with HIV/AIDS, child workers, children victims of narcotics abuse, alcohol, psychotropics and other addictive substances, children who are victims of violence, and child victims of human trafficking; and
3. The Regency Government, community, family and parents are obliged to ensure that children who are born are protected from diseases that threaten survival and/or cause disability.

Article 43

1. Promotive efforts for basic health services as referred to in Article 42 paragraph (2) letter a are aimed at improving health status more optimally.
2. Promotive efforts as referred to in paragraph (1) are carried out by providing socialization or counseling to the community regarding healthy lifestyle patterns which include:  a. the importance of exclusive breastfeeding for infants;
b. the importance of immunization for toddlers;
c. clean living patterns;
d. the importance of nutritious food; and
e. the dangers of drugs and smoking.
3. In addition to promotive efforts as referred to in paragraph (2), the Regional Government may carry out other activities in the context of health provision.

Article 44

1. Preventive efforts for basic health services as referred to in Article 42 paragraph (2) letter a are aimed at preventing the occurrence of disease or health problems.
2. Efforts as referred to in paragraph (1) are carried out by:
a. requiring mothers to provide exclusive breastfeeding to infants up to the age of 6 (six) months;
b. providing free immunization and vitamins to infants and toddlers;
c. providing nutritious food to toddlers from underprivileged families;
d. optimizing the role of posyandu for health services for mothers and toddlers;
e. conducting periodic health examinations; and
f. providing sanitation facilities and clean water at every school.

Article 45

1. Curative efforts for basic health services as referred to in Article 42 paragraph (2) letter a are aimed at preventing disease from becoming more severe through treatment.
2. Treatment as referred to in paragraph (1) is carried out free of charge for Children who come from underprivileged families.

Article 46

1. Rehabilitative efforts for basic health services as referred to in Article 42 paragraph (2) letter a are aimed at maintaining and restoring conditions after illness.
2. Rehabilitative efforts as referred to in paragraph (1) are carried out through periodic health examinations.

Article 47

1. The provision of social welfare is directed to:
a. individuals;
b. families;
c. groups; and/ord. communities.
2. The provision of social welfare as referred to in paragraph (1) is prioritized for those who have lives that are not humanely decent and have social problem criteria:
a. poverty;
b. neglect;
c. disability;
d. remoteness;
e. social dysfunction and behavioral deviation;
f. disaster victims; and/or
g. victims of violence, exploitation and discrimination.
3. Social welfare as referred to in paragraph (1) may be in the form of providing services:
a. health;
b. education;
c. legal aid;
d. assistance;
e. social, mental and spiritual guidance;
f. social rehabilitation; and/or
g. reintegration.

Part Five

Employment

Article 48

The Regency Government conducts periodic supervision of every person/entity that employs children in efforts to Protect child workers.

Article 49

Every person/entity that employs children must pay attention to the requirements:
a. at least 15 (fifteen) years of age;
b. obtain written consent from the parent/guardian of the child worker;
c. have a written employment agreement between the employer and the parent/guardian of the child worker and obtain validation from the authorized agency;
d. not employ children at night;
e. working time of at most 3 (three) hours per day.

Article 50

In the event that a child is employed together with adult workers, then the child's workplace must be separated from the adult workers' workplace.

Article 51

1. Every person/entity is prohibited from employing and involving children in the worst forms of work.
2. The worst forms of work referred to in paragraph (1) include:
a. all work in the form of slavery or the like;
b. all work that exploits, provides, or offers children for prostitution, pornography production, pornographic performances, or gambling;
c. all work that exploits, provides, or involves children for the production and trade of alcoholic beverages, narcotics, psychotropics, and other addictive substances; and/or
d. all work that endangers the health, safety, or morals of children.

Part Six

Facilitation of the Child Justice System

Article 52

1. Facilitation of the child justice system includes criminal and civil justice.
2. Facilitation of the criminal justice system as referred to in paragraph (1) includes:
a. provision of companions or Child volunteers during examination by investigators;
b. provision of legal aid and psychologist services;
c. involving members/police investigators in efforts to prevent criminal acts committed by children or children as victims;
d. realizing a restorative justice process for children in conflict with the law; and
e. rehabilitation and social reintegration services.
3. Fulfillment of children's rights in the civil justice system as referred to in paragraph (1) includes the provision and delivery of consultation and legal aid services during the judicial process.
4. In facilitating the Child justice system as referred to in paragraph (1) and paragraph (2), the authorized regional apparatus is the regional apparatus in charge of Women's and Children's Protection and the Integrated Service Center for Women's and Children's Empowerment has the obligation to accompany child victims and perpetrators, in police, prosecutor's examinations and in court.

Part Seven

Children's Forum

Article 53

1. In organizing the fulfillment of children's rights to express opinions and participate, the Regency Government facilitates the establishment of a Children's Forum.
2. The Children's Forum as referred to in paragraph (1) represents children in the Region, both representing the geographical domicile of children, components of children's socio-cultural groups and children's educational backgrounds.
3. In every formulation of policies related to children, the Regional Government is obliged to consider and accommodate the opinions of children submitted through the children's participation forum.
4. Further provisions regarding the establishment of the Children's Forum as referred to in paragraph (1) through paragraph (3) shall be stipulated by Regent's Decree.
30.

Part Eight

Child-Friendly Regency

Article 54

1. In the framework of fulfilling children's rights in an integrated, systematic and sustainable manner, the Regency Government establishes a CFC Development policy.
2. The CFC Development policy contains:
a. the CFC concept concerning objectives, strategies and roles of related parties;
b. children's rights; and
c. the CFC development approach.

Article 55

1. The CFC development policy is directed at fulfilling children's rights which are divided into 5 (five) groups, comprising:
a. civil rights and freedoms;
b. family environment and alternative care;
c. basic health and welfare;
d. education, leisure time utilization, and cultural activities; and
e. special protection.
2. Further provisions regarding the CFC development policy as referred to in paragraph (1) shall be regulated by Regent's Regulation.

Article 56

1. In order to ensure the effectiveness of implementing the CFC policy in the Regency, a CFC Task Force is established.
2. The establishment and membership composition of the CFC Task Force shall be stipulated by Regent's Decree.
3. The CFC Task Force is domiciled at the Office of the Women's Empowerment and Child Protection Agency.
4. The CFC Task Force as referred to in paragraph (1) has the following principal duties:
a. coordinating CFC development;
b. conducting socialization, advocacy and communication, information and education on CFC policy;
c. collecting basic data for CFC development;
d. conducting needs analysis sourced from basic data;
e. disseminating basic data;
f. determining the focus and priority of programs in realizing CFC, which are adjusted to regional potential;
g. preparing a 5 (five) year regional CFC action plan and work mechanism; and
h. conducting monitoring, evaluation and reporting at least once (1) a year.

Article 57

In carrying out its duties, the CFC Task Force may coordinate and cooperate with PPT institutions and other institutions that provide child protection services.

CHAPTER VI

CHILD PROTECTION

Article 58

The Regency Government, in order to provide protection to children:
a. implements and supports national policy in the implementation of child protection in the Regency;
b. guarantees children to exercise their rights in expressing opinions in accordance with the age and level of intelligence of the child;
c. prevents violence against children by optimizing the role of customary institutions;
d. conducts socialization, seminars and counseling regarding child protection and rights;
e. develops a child-friendly school model;
f. provides public facilities that are friendly and safe for children; and
g. encourages public awareness of the importance of child protection.

Article 59

Service efforts carried out by the Regency Government for child victims of violence as referred to in Article 36 through Article 58 apply mutatis mutandis to service efforts for child victims of violence.

CHAPTER VII

SPECIAL PROTECTION FOR CHILDREN

Article 60

1. Special protection for children is provided to:
a. children in emergency situations;
b. children in conflict with the law;
c. children from minority and isolated groups;
d. children who are exploited economically and/or sexually;
e. children who are victims of narcotics, alcohol, psychotropic, and other addictive substance abuse;
f. children who are victims of pornography;
g. children with HIV/AIDS;
h. child victims of abduction, sale, and/or trafficking;
i. child victims of physical and/or psychological violence;
j. child victims of sexual crimes;
k. child victims of terrorism networks;
1. children with disabilities;
m. child victims of abuse and neglect;
n. children with deviant social behavior; and
o. children who are victims of stigmatization from labeling related to their parents' condition.
2. The Regency Government in implementing the handling of special protection for children as referred to in paragraph (1) shall be in accordance with the provisions of Laws and Regulations.

Article 61

Children in emergency situations as referred to in Article 60 paragraph (1) letter a consist of:
a. children who become refugees;
b. child victims of riots;
c. child victims of natural disasters; and
d. children in armed conflict situations.

Article 62

Special protection for children who become refugees as referred to in Article 61 paragraph (1) letter a shall be implemented in accordance with the provisions of Laws and Regulations.

Article 63

Special protection for child victims of riots, disaster victims, and children in armed conflict as referred to in Article 61 paragraph (1) letter b, letter c, and letter d, shall be implemented through:
a. fulfillment of basic needs consisting of food, clothing, housing, education, health, learning and recreation, security guarantees, and equal treatment; and
b. fulfillment of special needs for children with disabilities and children experiencing psychosocial disorders.

Article 64

Special Protection for children in conflict with the law as referred to in Article 60 paragraph (1) letter b shall be carried out through:
a. humane treatment with due regard to needs according to their age;
b. separation from adults;
c. provision of legal assistance and other assistance effectively;
d. implementation of recreational activities;
e. freedom from torture, punishment, or other treatment that is cruel, inhuman and degrading to their dignity and degree;
f. avoidance of imposition of the death penalty and/or life imprisonment;
g. avoidance of arrest, detention or imprisonment, except as a last resort and for the shortest possible time;
h. provision of justice before a children's court that is objective, impartial, and in a closed hearing to the public;
i. avoidance of publication of their identity;
j. provision of accompaniment by Parents/Guardians and persons trusted by the child;
k. provision of social advocacy;
l. provision of private life;
m. provision of accessibility, especially for Children with Disabilities;
n. provision of education;
o. provision of health services; and
p. provision of other rights in accordance with the provisions of Laws and Regulations.

Article 65

Special Protection for Children from minority and isolated groups as referred to in Article 60 paragraph (1) letter c shall be carried out through the provision of infrastructure and facilities to be able to enjoy their own culture, acknowledge and practice their own religion, and use their own language.

Article 66

Special Protection for children who are exploited economically and/or sexually as referred to in Article 60 paragraph (1) letter d shall be carried out through:
a. dissemination and/or socialization of the provisions of Laws and Regulations relating to the protection of children who are exploited economically and/or sexually.
b. involvement of various companies, labor unions, Non-Governmental Organizations, and the community in the elimination of economic and/or sexual exploitation of children.

Article 67

Special protection for Children who are victims of narcotics, alcohol, psychotropic substances, and other addictive substances abuse as referred to in Article 60 paragraph (1) letter e and children involved in their production and distribution shall be carried out through supervision, prevention, treatment, and rehabilitation efforts.

Article 68

1. Every Person is obliged to protect children from the influence of pornography and prevent children's access to information containing pornographic elements.
2. Special Protection for children who are victims of pornography as referred to in Article 60 paragraph (1) letter f shall be implemented through guidance, assistance, and social, physical and mental health recovery efforts.
3. Guidance, assistance, and social, physical and mental health recovery as referred to in paragraph (1) shall be implemented in accordance with the provisions of Laws and Regulations.

Article 69

Special protection for Children with HIV/AIDS as referred to in Article 60 paragraph (1) letter f shall be carried out through supervision, prevention, treatment, care, and rehabilitation efforts.

Article 70

Special protection for child victims of kidnapping, sale, and/or trafficking as referred to in Article 60 paragraph (1) letter h shall be carried out through supervision, protection, prevention, care, and rehabilitation efforts.

Article 71

Special protection for child victims of physical and/or psychological violence as referred to in Article 60 paragraph (1) letter i shall be carried out through the following efforts: (36)
a. dissemination and socialization of the provisions of Laws and Regulations that protect child victims of acts of violence; and
b. monitoring, reporting, and imposition of sanctions.

Article 72

Special Protection for child victims of sexual crimes as referred to in Article 60 paragraph (1) letter j shall be carried out through the following efforts:
a. education on reproductive health, religious values, and moral values;
b. social rehabilitation;
c. psychosocial assistance during treatment until recovery; and
d. provision of protection and assistance at every level of examination starting from investigation, prosecution, to examination in Court hearings.

Article 73

Special Protection for child victims of terrorism networks as referred to in Article 60 paragraph (1) letter k shall be carried out through the following efforts:
a. education on education, ideology, and nationalism values;
b. counseling on the dangers of terrorism;
c. social rehabilitation; and
d. social assistance.

Article 74

Special Protection for children with disabilities as referred to in Article 60 paragraph (1) letter l shall be carried out through the following efforts:
a. treatment of children in a humane manner in accordance with the dignity and rights of the child;
b. fulfillment of special needs;
c. equal treatment with other children to achieve full social integration and individual development as much as possible;
d. social assistance.

Article 75

Special Protection for child victims of mistreatment and neglect as referred to in Article 60 paragraph (1) letter m shall be carried out through supervision, prevention, care, counseling, social rehabilitation, and social assistance efforts.

Article 76

Special Protection for children with deviant social behavior as referred to in Article 60 paragraph (1) letter n shall be carried out through guidance on religious and social values, counseling, social rehabilitation, and social assistance.

Article 77

Special Protection for children who are victims of stigmatization from labeling related to their parents' condition as referred to in Article 60 paragraph (1) letter o shall be carried out through counseling, social rehabilitation, and social assistance.

Article 78

1. Every child who becomes a victim as referred to in Article 60 paragraph (1) letter b, letter d, letter f, letter h, letter i and letter j has the right to file with the Court in the form of the right to restitution which is the responsibility of the perpetrator of the crime.
2. Further provisions regarding the implementation of restitution as referred to in paragraph (1) shall be regulated by Regent Regulation.

Article 79

Further provisions regarding Special Protection for Children as referred to in Article 60 shall be regulated by Regent Regulation.

CHAPTER VIII

PARTICIPATION OF THE COMMUNITY, AND PARENTS AND/OR FAMILY

Part One

Community

Article 80

1. Community participation in efforts to protect women and children:
a. participate in prevention and provide support for the development of prevention programs for violations of the rights of women and children;
b. provide information and/or report violations of the rights of women and children to the competent authorities; and c. supervise the law enforcement process for cases of violations of the rights of women and children.
2. Community participation as referred to in paragraph (1) shall be based on laws and regulations and the values of local wisdom of the local community.

Article 81

1. In order to prevent violations of women's rights, the community:
a. monitors various conditions occurring in their environment that may cause violations of women's rights;
b. increases awareness of violations of women's rights;
c. strengthens the role of community organizations, religious organizations and the family environment;
d. supervises the implementation of Women and Children Protection organized by the Regional Government with a mechanism for conveying aspirations to the Regent or Regional People's Representative Council.
2. In order to prevent violations of children's rights, the community:
a. monitors various conditions occurring in their environment that may cause violations of children's rights;
b. increases awareness of violations of children's rights;
c. strengthens the role of community organizations, religious organizations and the family environment; and
d. prevents early marriage.

Part Two

Parents and/or Family

Article 82

The family in efforts to protect women's rights has the role of:
a. protecting women from conflict and domestic violence that degrades the dignity of women;
b. providing equal opportunities for women to develop themselves in the fields of education, social, economic, political and cultural affairs; and
c. preventing violations of women's rights in the household.

Article 83

1. Parents in efforts to protect children's rights have the role of:
a. caring for, maintaining, educating, and protecting children;
b. developing children according to their abilities, talents, and interests;
c. providing formal and non-formal education;
d. providing character education and instilling moral values in children;
e. guiding children to behave in accordance with religion, ethics and morals as well as customs; and
f. preventing early marriage.
2. In the event that parents do not exist, or their whereabouts are unknown, or due to a certain reason, cannot carry out their obligations and responsibilities, then the obligations and responsibilities as referred to in paragraph (1) may be transferred to the family.

CHAPTER IX

GUIDANCE AND SUPERVISION

Article 84

1. The Regency Government is obliged to conduct guidance and supervision of the implementation of protection for women and children victims of violence.
2. Guidance and supervision as referred to in paragraph (1), includes:
a. guidelines and fulfillment standards;
b. technical guidance and training;
c. provision of facilities;
d. monitoring; and
e. evaluation.
3. Guidance as referred to in paragraph (2) in order to realize the objectives of protection for women and children victims of violence in accordance with minimum service standards implemented by the agency in charge of Women and Children Protection.
4. The agency in charge of Women and Children Protection is obliged to report regularly to the Regent regarding the implementation of protection for women and children victims of violence.

CHAPTER X

FINANCING

Article 85 Financing for the implementation of programs and activities for the Protection of Women and Children is sourced from:
a. regional revenue and expenditure budget; and
b. other legitimate sources in accordance with the provisions of laws and regulations.

CHAPTER XI

CLOSING PROVISIONS

Article 86

Implementing regulations of this Regional Regulation shall be stipulated no later than 6 (six) months from the promulgation of this Regional Regulation.

Article 87

This Regional Regulation shall come into force on the date of promulgation.
