NUMBER 4 OF 2018 CONCERNING

IMPLEMENTATION OF PROTECTION FOR WOMEN AND CHILDREN VICTIMS OF VIOLENCE

BY THE GRACE OF ALMIGHTY GOD

REGENT OF MUSI RAWAS.

Considering:

a. that women and children are among vulnerable groups who tend to experience violence;

b. that violence against women and children continues to increase, thus requiring protection efforts;

c. that all forms of violence against women and children constitute violations of law and human rights and therefore their dignity and honor must be protected and their right to life guaranteed in accordance with their nature and destiny without discrimination;

d. that based on the considerations as referred to in letter a, letter b, and letter c, it is necessary to establish a Regional Regulation on the Implementation of Protection for Women and Children Victims of Violence;

# CHAPTER I

GENERAL PROVISIONS

#### Article 1

In this Regional Regulation, what is meant by:

1. Regency is Musi Rawas Regency;

2. Regency Government is the Government of Musi Rawas Regency;

3. Regent is the Regent of Musi Rawas.

4. Domestic Violence is any act against a person, especially women, which results in misery or suffering physically, sexually, psychologically, and/or domestic neglect including threats to commit acts, coercion, or unlawful deprivation of liberty within the scope of the household.

5. Elimination of Domestic Violence is a guarantee provided by the state to prevent the occurrence of domestic violence, prosecute perpetrators of domestic violence, and protect victims of domestic violence.

6. Trafficking in Persons is the act of recruitment, transportation, harboring, transfer, relocation, or receipt of a person by means of threat of violence, use of violence, abduction, confinement, forgery, fraud, abuse of power or position of vulnerability, debt bondage or giving payments or benefits, so as to obtain the consent of a person having control over another person, whether carried out within the country or between countries, for the purpose of exploitation or resulting in a person being exploited.

7. Victim of Trafficking in Persons is a person who experiences psychological, mental, physical, sexual, economic, and/or social suffering, resulting from the criminal act of trafficking in persons.

8. Violence is any act that results in or may result in misery or suffering whether physical, sexual, economic, psychological to the victim.

9. Physical violence is any act that causes pain, injury, wounds, or disability to a person's body, miscarriage, fainting and/or causes death.

10. Psychological violence is an act that causes fear, loss of self-confidence, loss of ability to act, feelings of helplessness and/or severe psychological suffering to a person.

11. Sexual violence is an act in the form of sexual harassment, forced sexual intercourse, forced unnatural or unwanted sexual intercourse, forced sexual intercourse with others for commercial purposes and/or certain purposes.

12. Victim is a woman and child who experiences misery and/or suffering either directly or indirectly as a result of violence.

13. Women in contact with the law are women in conflict with the law, women as victims, women as witnesses or women as parties.

14. Child is a person under the age of 18 years, including those in the womb.

15. Family is the smallest unit in society consisting of husband and wife, or husband and wife and their children, or father and his children, or mother and her children, or blood relatives in direct line upward or downward up to the third degree.

16. Parents are biological father and/or mother, or step father and/or mother, or adoptive father and/or mother.

17. Guardian is a person or body who in fact exercises custody as a parent over a child.

18. Neglected child is a child whose needs are not properly fulfilled, whether physical, mental, spiritual, or social.

19. Child with Disability is a child who experiences physical and/or mental障碍 so as to interfere with his or her growth and development properly.

20. Child with excellence is a child who has extraordinary intelligence, or has special potential and/or talent.

21. Adopted child is a child whose rights are transferred from the environment of the authority of the family of parents, legal guardian, or other person responsible for the care, education, and raising of the child, into the family environment of the adoptive parents based on a court decision or determination.

22. Foster child is a child who is cared for by a person or institution, to be given guidance, maintenance, care, education, and health, because his or her parents or one of his or her parents is unable to guarantee the child's proper growth and development.

23. Custody is the power of parents to care for, educate, maintain, foster, protect, and develop the child in accordance with the religion professed and the child's abilities, talents, and interests.

24. Rights of the child are part of human rights that must be guaranteed, protected, and fulfilled by parents, family, community, government, and the state.

25. Protection is all efforts aimed at providing a sense of security to victims carried out by family, advocates, social institutions, police, prosecutors, courts, or other parties either temporarily or based on court determination.

26. Child protection is all activities to guarantee and protect children and their rights so that they can live, grow, develop, and participate, optimally in accordance with human dignity and dignity, and receive protection from violence and discrimination.

27. Special protection is protection given to children in emergency situations, children in contact with the law, children from minority and isolated groups, children who are exploited economically and/or sexually, children who are trafficked, children who are victims of narcotics, alcohol, psychotropic, and other addictive substances (drugs) abuse, children who are victims of kidnapping, sale, trafficking, children who are victims of violence both physical and/or mental, children with disabilities, and children who are victims of maltreatment and neglect.

28. Service is an action taken as soon as possible to the victim when seeing, hearing and knowing that violence against the victim will, is or has occurred.

29. Recovery of victims is all efforts to strengthen victims of domestic violence to be more empowered both physically and psychologically.

30. Implementation of Recovery is all actions that include services and assistance to victims of domestic violence.

31. Recovery implementation officer is a health worker, social worker, volunteer companion, and/or spiritual counselor.

32. Companion is a person who has the expertise to accompany victims to conduct counseling, therapy and advocacy for the strengthening and recovery of victims of violence.

33. Assistance is all actions in the form of counseling, psychological therapy, advocacy, and spiritual guidance, for the self-strengthening of victims of domestic violence to resolve the problems faced.

34. Integrated Service Center for the Empowerment of Women and Children (P2TP2A) hereinafter abbreviated as P2TP2A is an institution providing services to victims of violence, based in hospitals, managed jointly in the form of medical services (including medico-legal), psycho-social and legal services.

35. Cooperation is a systematic and integrated way among recovery implementers in providing services to recover victims of domestic violence.

36. Safe house is a temporary residence used to provide protection to victims in accordance with determined operational standards.

37. Community is individuals, families, groups, and social organizations and/or community organizations.

38. Every person is an individual person or corporation.

39. Household is family members, relatives (grandchildren, nieces/nephews, siblings, grandparents, cousins and so on) and non-relatives (servants, drivers and so on) who live and eat from one kitchen and reside in one house.

#### Article 2

The implementation of protection for women victims of violence is carried out based on the principles of:

a. respect for human rights;

b. justice and gender equality;

c. non-discrimination; and

d. best interests of the victim.

#### Article 3

The implementation of child protection is based on Pancasila and grounded in the 1945 Constitution of the Republic of Indonesia as well as the basic principles of the Convention on the Rights of the Child, including:

a. collecting data and information about women and children victims of violence;

b. conducting education on anti-violence values towards women and children;

c. socializing laws and regulations related to the implementation of protection for women and children victims of violence;

d. providing facilities necessary for victim recovery, including:

1. expert and professional personnel;

2. service centers, safe houses; and

3. other facilities and infrastructure necessary for victim recovery.

e. monitoring and evaluation of the implementation of protection for women and children victims of violence.

#### Article 7

For the implementation of services to victims, Government Agencies and Regional Governments in accordance with their respective functions and duties may undertake efforts to:

a. provide special service rooms at police stations;

b. provide apparatus, health workers, social workers, and spiritual counselors;

c. create and develop cooperation systems and mechanisms for service programs involving parties easily accessible to victims; and

d. provide protection for companions, witnesses, families, and friends of victims.

#### Article 8

To implement the efforts as referred to in Article 7, the government and regional governments in accordance with their respective functions and duties, may cooperate with the community or other social institutions.

a. non-discrimination;

b. the best interests of the child;

c. the right to life, survival, and development; and

d. respect for the views of the child.

#### Article 4

The purpose of establishing this Regional Regulation is to provide protection and services to women and children victims of gender-based violence and the best interests of the child occurring in the household and/or public. As well as to guarantee the fulfillment of children's rights so they can live, grow, develop, and participate optimally in accordance with human dignity, and receive protection from violence and discrimination, for the realization of quality, noble, and prosperous Indonesian children.

# CHAPTER II

RIGHTS OF VICTIMS

#### Article 5

Every woman and child victim of violence has the right to receive

a. Protection from family, police, prosecution, courts, advocates, social institutions, or other parties either temporarily or based on a protection order from the court;

b. health services in accordance with medical needs;

c. special handling related to victim confidentiality;

d. assistance by social workers and legal aid at every level of the examination process in accordance with the provisions of laws and regulations; and

e. spiritual guidance services.

# CHAPTER III

OBLIGATIONS AND RESPONSIBILITIES

#### Article 6

The Regency Government and the Regency community are obliged and responsible to implement efforts to prevent the occurrence of violence, in the form of:

#### Article 9

Every person who hears, sees, or knows of the occurrence of domestic violence is obliged to make efforts according to their capabilities to:

a. prevent the continuation of criminal acts;

b. provide protection to victims;

c. provide emergency assistance; and

d. assist in the process of submitting applications for protection orders.

# CHAPTER IV

IMPLEMENTATION OF PROTECTION Part One Institutions

#### Article 10

(1) For the implementation of Protection for women and children, the Regency Government is obliged to provide services for victims in the form of:

a. establishing and facilitating the implementation of integrated service institutions for victims involving community elements; and

b. encouraging community awareness of the importance of protection for victims.

(2). The Regency Government in carrying out the obligations as referred to in paragraph (1), must pay attention to the rights and obligations of parents, guardians, husbands or other persons legally responsible for the victim.

(3). The implementation of Protection Institutions for victims is carried out in an integrated manner within the P2TP2A framework.

(4) Further provisions regarding P2TP2A are regulated in a Regent Regulation.

## Part Two

Forms and Mechanisms of Service

#### Article 11

(1) The form of Integrated Services for victims organized by P2TP2A includes:

a. medical services, in the form of treatment and recovery of physical injuries aimed at restoring the physical condition of victims carried out by medical and paramedical personnel;

b. medico-legal services are forms of medical services for evidentiary purposes in the legal field;

c. psychosocial services are services provided by companions in order to recover the traumatic condition of victims, including the provision of space and victim houses to protect victims from threats and intimidation for victims;

d. legal services to assist victims in undergoing the judicial process;

e. economic independence services in the form of services for skills training and providing economic access so that victims can be independent.

(2) The service mechanism as referred to in paragraph (1) is organized based on Standard Operating Procedures.

(3) Provisions regarding Standard Operating Procedures as referred to in paragraph (2) are regulated by Regent Regulation.

## Part Three

Service Principles

#### Article 12

The implementation for women and children victims is carried out with:

a. speed;

b. safety;

c. empathy;

d. non-discrimination;

e. easy accessibility;

f. free of charge; and

g. guarantee of confidentiality.

# CHAPTER V

IMPLEMENTATION OF CHILD PROTECTION

## Part One

Religion

#### Article 13

(1) Every child receives protection to worship according to their religion.

(2) Before the child can determine their choice, the religion embraced by the child follows the religion of their parents.

#### Article 14

(1) The state, government, society, family, parents, guardians, and social institutions guarantee the protection of children in embracing their religion.

(2) Protection of children in embracing their religion as referred to in paragraph (1) includes guidance, counseling, and practice of religious teachings for children.

## Part Two

Health

#### Article 15

(1) The Government is obliged to provide facilities and organize comprehensive health efforts for children, so that every child obtains optimal health status from the womb.

(2) The provision of facilities and organization of comprehensive health efforts as referred to in paragraph (1) is supported by community participation.

(3) Comprehensive health efforts as referred to in paragraph (1) include promotive, preventive, curative, and rehabilitative efforts, both for basic health services and referrals.

(4) Comprehensive health efforts as referred to in paragraph (1) are organized free of charge.

(5) The implementation of provisions as referred to in paragraph (1), paragraph (2), paragraph (3), and paragraph (4) is adjusted to the provisions of applicable laws and regulations.

#### Article 16

(1) Parents and families are responsible for maintaining children's health and caring for children from the womb.

(2) In the event that parents and families are unable to carry out the responsibilities as referred to in paragraph (1), then the government is obliged to fulfill them.

(3) The obligations as referred to in paragraph (2), their implementation is carried out in accordance with the provisions of applicable laws and regulations.

#### Article 17

The state, government, family, and parents are obliged to ensure that children born are protected from diseases that threaten the continuity of life and/or cause disability.

#### Article 18

(1) The state, government, family, and parents are obliged to protect children from attempts to transplant their body organs for other parties.

(2) The state, government, family, and parents are obliged to protect children from acts of:

a. removal of children's body organs and/or children's body tissue without regard to the health of the child;

b. buying and selling of children's body organs and/or tissue; and

c. health research that uses children as research subjects without parental consent and does not prioritize the best interests of the child.

## Part Three

Education

#### Article 19

The government is obliged to provide basic education for a minimum of 9 (nine) years for all children.

#### Article 20

The state, government, family, and parents are obliged to provide the widest possible opportunity for children to obtain education.

#### Article 21

Education as referred to in Article 20 is directed at:

a. development of the child's attitudes and personality abilities, talents, mental and physical abilities to achieve their optimal potential;

b. development of respect for human rights and fundamental freedoms;

c. development of respect for parents, cultural identity, language and their own values, national values where the child resides, from where the child originates, and civilizations that are different from one's own civilization;

d. preparation of the child for a responsible life; and

e. development of respect and love for the environment.

#### Article 22

Children with physical and/or mental disabilities are given equal opportunity and accessibility to obtain regular education and special education.

#### Article 23

Children who have excellence are given opportunity and accessibility to obtain special education.

#### Article 24

(1) The government is responsible for providing education costs and/or free assistance or special services for children from underprivileged families, neglected children, and children residing in remote areas.

(2) The government's responsibility as referred to in paragraph (1) also includes encouraging the community to play an active role.

#### Article 25

Children inside and in the school environment must be protected from acts of violence committed by teachers, school administrators or their friends within the school concerned, or other educational institutions.

## Part Four

Social

#### Article 26

(1) The government is obliged to provide care and maintenance for neglected children, both in institutions and outside institutions.

(2) The provision of care as referred to in paragraph (1) may be carried out by community institutions.

(3) To provide care and maintenance for neglected children, government institutions and community institutions, as referred to in paragraph (2), may cooperate with various related parties.

(4) In the case of providing care and maintenance as referred to in paragraph (3), it is carried out by the Social Service.

#### Article 27

(1) The government in providing care and maintenance is obliged to strive for and assist children, so that children can:

a. participate;

b. freely express opinions and think in accordance with their conscience and religion;

c. freely receive oral or written information in accordance with the child's age and development stage;

d. freely associate and assemble;

e. freely rest, play, create, create, and engage in cultural arts; and

f. obtain play facilities that meet health and safety requirements.

(2) The efforts as referred to in paragraph (1) are developed and adapted to the child's age, level of ability, and environment so as not to hinder and disturb the child's development.

#### Article 28

In the event that a child is neglected because for some reason the parents neglect their obligations, then the institution as referred to in Article 27, family, or authorized official may submit an application to the court to determine the child as a neglected child.

#### Article 29

(1) The court determination as referred to in Article 40 simultaneously determines the place of shelter, care, and maintenance for the neglected child concerned.

(2) The government or authorized institution is obliged to provide a place as referred to in paragraph (1).

## Part Five

Special Protection

#### Article 30

The government and other state institutions are obliged and responsible to provide special protection to children in emergency situations, children in conflict with the law, children from minority and isolated groups, children exploited economically and/or sexually, children who are trafficked, children who are victims of narcotics, alcohol, psychotropic, and other addictive substance abuse (napza), children who are victims of kidnapping, sale and trafficking, children who are victims of violence both physical and/or mental, children with disabilities, and children who are victims of maltreatment and neglect.

#### Article 31

Children in emergency situations as referred to in Article 30 consist of:

a. children who become refugees;

b. children who are victims of unrest;

c. children who are victims of natural disasters; and

d. children in armed conflict situations.

#### Article 32

Special protection for children who become refugees as referred to in Article 30 letter a is implemented in accordance with the provisions of humanitarian law.

#### Article 33

Special protection for children who are victims of unrest, victims of disasters, and children in armed conflict situations as referred to in Article 30 letters b, c, and d, is implemented through:

a. fulfillment of basic needs consisting of food, clothing, housing, education, health, learning and recreation, security guarantees, and equal treatment; and

b. fulfillment of special needs for children with disabilities and children experiencing psychosocial disorders.

#### Article 34

Everyone is prohibited from recruiting or exploiting children for military and/or other interests and leaving children without protection of life.

#### Article 35

(1) Special protection for children in conflict with the law as referred to in Article 30 includes children in conflict with the law and children who are victims of criminal acts, which is the obligation and responsibility of the government and society.

(2) Special protection for children in conflict with the law as referred to in paragraph (1) is implemented through:

a. treatment of children in a humane manner in accordance with the dignity and rights of children;

b. provision of special accompanying officers for children from an early stage;

c. provision of special facilities and infrastructure;

d. imposition of appropriate sanctions for the best interests of the child;

e. continuous monitoring and recording of the development of children in conflict with the law;

f. provision of guarantees to maintain relationships with parents or family; and

g. protection from disclosure of identity through the mass media and to avoid labeling.

(3) Special protection for children who become victims of criminal acts as referred to in paragraph (1) is implemented through:

a. rehabilitation efforts, both in institutions and outside institutions;

b. protection efforts from disclosure of identity through the mass media and to avoid labeling;

c. provision of safety guarantees for victim witnesses and expert witnesses, whether physical, mental, or social; and

d. provision of accessibility to obtain information regarding case developments.

#### Article 36

(1) Special protection for children from minority and isolated groups as referred to in Article 30 is carried out through the provision of infrastructure and facilities to be able to enjoy their own culture, acknowledge and practice their own religious teachings, and use their own language.

(2) Everyone is prohibited from obstructing children as referred to in paragraph (1) from enjoying their own culture, acknowledging and practicing their religion, and using their own language without neglecting access to community and cultural development.

#### Article 37

(1) Special protection for children who are exploited economically and/or sexually as referred to in Article 30 constitutes an obligation and responsibility of the government and society.

(2) Special protection for children who are exploited as referred to in paragraph (1) is carried out through:

a. Dissemination and/or socialization of provisions of laws and regulations related to the protection of children who are exploited economically and/or sexually;

b. monitoring, reporting, and imposition of sanctions; and

c. involvement of various government agencies, companies, labor unions, non-governmental organizations, and society in the elimination of economic and/or sexual exploitation of children.

(3) Every person is prohibited from placing, allowing, committing, ordering to commit, or participating in committing exploitation of children as referred to in paragraph (1).

#### Article 38

(1) Special protection for children who are victims of abuse of narcotics, alcohol, psychotropic substances, and other addictive substances (napza) as referred to in Article 30, and involved in their production and distribution, is carried out through efforts of supervision, prevention, treatment, and rehabilitation by the government and society.

(2) Every person is prohibited from intentionally placing, allowing, involving, ordering to involve children in the abuse, production and distribution of napza as referred to in paragraph (1).

#### Article 39

(1) Special protection for child victims of abduction, sale, and trafficking of children as referred to in Article 30 is carried out through efforts of supervision, protection, prevention, treatment, and rehabilitation by the government and society.

(2) Every person is prohibited from placing, allowing, committing, ordering to commit, or participating in committing abduction, sale, or trafficking as referred to in paragraph (1).

#### Article 40

(1) Special protection for child victims of violence as referred to in Article 30 includes: physical, psychological, and sexual violence carried out through efforts of: dissemination and socialization of provisions of laws and regulations that protect child victims of acts of violence; and monitoring, reporting, and imposition of sanctions.

(2) Every person is prohibited from placing, allowing, committing, ordering to commit, or participating in committing violence as referred to in paragraph (1).

#### Article 41

(1) Special protection for children with disabilities as referred to in Article 30 is carried out through efforts of:

a. treatment of children in a humane manner in accordance with the dignity and rights of the child;

b. fulfillment of special needs; and

c. obtaining equal treatment with other children to achieve full social integration and individual development as much as possible.

(2) Every person is prohibited from treating children by disregarding their views in a discriminatory manner, including labeling and equalization in education for children with disabilities.

#### Article 42

(1) Special protection for child victims of mistreatment and neglect as referred to in Article 30 is carried out through supervision, prevention, treatment, and rehabilitation by the government and society.

(2) Every person is prohibited from placing, allowing, involving, ordering to involve children in situations of mistreatment, and neglect as referred to in paragraph (1).

# CHAPTER VI

RIGHTS OF PERSONS WITH DISABILITIES

## Part One

General

#### Article 43

Persons with Disabilities have the right to:

a. life;

b. freedom from stigma;

c. privacy;

d. justice and legal protection;

e. education;

f. employment, entrepreneurship, and cooperatives;

g. health;

h. politics;

i. religion;

j. sports;

k. culture and tourism;

l. social welfare;

m. accessibility;

n. Public services;

o. protection from disasters;

p. habilitation and rehabilitation;

q. concessions;

r. data collection;

s. live independently and be involved in society;

t. express, communicate, and obtain information;

u. move and citizenship; and

v. freedom from acts of Discrimination, neglect, torture, and exploitation.

#### Article 44

In addition to the rights of Persons with Disabilities as referred to in paragraph (1), women with disabilities have the right to:

a. reproductive health;

b. accept or refuse the use of contraceptives;

c. receive greater Protection from multiple Discrimination; and

d. receive greater Protection from acts of violence, including sexual violence and exploitation.

#### Article 45

In addition to the rights of Persons with Disabilities as referred to in paragraph (1), children with disabilities have the right to:

a. receive special Protection from Discrimination, neglect, harassment, exploitation, as well as sexual violence and crimes;

b. receive family care and upbringing or substitute family to grow and develop optimally;

c. have their interests protected in decision-making;

d. treatment of children in a humane manner in accordance with the dignity and rights of the child;

e. Fulfillment of special needs;

f. equal treatment with other children to achieve social integration and individual development; and

g. receive social assistance.

## Part Two

Right to Life

#### Article 46

The right to life for Persons with Disabilities includes the right to:

a. Respect for integrity;

b. not be deprived of life;

c. receive care and upbringing that ensures their survival;

d. freedom from neglect, shackling, confinement, and isolation;

e. freedom from threats and various forms of exploitation; and

f. freedom from torture, other cruel, inhuman, and degrading treatment and punishment.

## Part Three

Right to Freedom from Stigma

#### Article 47

The right to freedom from stigma for Persons with Disabilities includes the right to freedom from harassment, insult, and negative labeling related to their disability condition.

## Part Four

Right to Privacy

#### Article 48

The right to privacy for Persons with Disabilities includes the right to:

a. be recognized as a personal human being who can demand and obtain equal treatment and Protection in accordance with human dignity in public;

b. form a family and continue offspring through lawful marriage;

c. Respect for home and family;

d. receive Protection of personal and family life; and

e. have confidentiality of personal data, correspondence, and other forms of personal communication protected, including health data and information.

## Part Five

Right to Justice and Legal Protection

#### Article 49

The right to justice and legal protection for Persons with Disabilities includes the right to:

a. equal treatment before the law;

b. be recognized as a legal subject;

c. own and inherit movable or immovable property;

d. control financial matters or appoint a person to represent their interests in financial affairs;

e. obtain access to banking and non-banking services;

f. obtain provision of Accessibility in judicial services;

g. Protection from all pressure, violence, persecution, discrimination, and/or deprivation or seizure of property rights;

h. choose and appoint a person to represent their interests in civil matters inside and outside the court; and

i. have their intellectual property rights protected.

## Part Six

Right to Education

#### Article 50

The right to education for Persons with Disabilities includes the right to:

a. receive quality education at educational units in all types, paths, and levels of education in an inclusive and special manner;

b. have Equal Opportunity to become an educator or educational personnel at educational units in all types, paths, and levels of education;

c. have Equal Opportunity as an organizer of quality education at educational units in all types, paths, and levels of education; and

d. receive Reasonable Accommodation as students.

# CHAPTER VII

IMPLEMENTATION OF PROTECTION OF WOMEN

Rights of Women

#### Article 51

Women have the right to obtain education and training in work, positions, and professions in accordance with the requirements that have been determined.

#### Article 52

(1) Women have the right to choose, be chosen, be appointed, in work, positions, and professions in accordance with the requirements and laws and regulations.

(2) Women have the right to obtain special protection in the implementation of their work or profession against matters that may threaten their safety and or health in relation to Women's reproductive function.

(3) Special rights attached to Women due to their reproductive function, are guaranteed and protected by law.

#### Article 53

Women who are adults and or are married have the right to perform legal acts independently, unless otherwise determined by their religious law.

#### Article 54

In the examination of cases of Women Facing the Law, judges must not:

a. show attitudes or issue statements that demean, blame and/or intimidate Women Facing the Law.

b. justify the occurrence of Discrimination Against Women by using culture, customary rules and other traditional practices or by using expert interpretations that are Gender biased;

c. question and/or consider the victim's sexual experience or background as a basis to acquit the perpetrator or mitigate the perpetrator's punishment; and

d. issue statements or views that contain Gender stereotypes.

#### Article 55

During the course of trial examination, judges shall prevent and/or reprimand the parties, legal advisors, public prosecutors and/or legal counsel who behave or make statements that demean, blame, intimidate and/or use the sexual experience or background of Women Facing the Law.

#### Article 56

If Women Facing the Law experience physical and psychological obstacles so that they require assistance, the Judge may grant the request of Women Facing the Law to present a Companion.

#### Article 57

The judge on his own initiative and/or at the request of the parties, public prosecutor, legal advisor and/or victim may order Women Facing the Law to have their testimony heard through examination with remote audio visual communication at the local court or at another place, if:

1) The mental/psychological condition of Women Facing the Law is unhealthy due to fear/psychological trauma based on the assessment of a doctor or psychologist;

2) Based on the judge's assessment, the safety of Women Facing the Law is not guaranteed if they are in a public and open place; or

3) Based on the decision of the Witness and Victim Protection Agency (LPSK), Women Facing the Law are declared to be in the witness and/or victim protection program according to LPSK and cannot attend the trial to give testimony either for security reasons or due to physical and psychological obstacles.

# CHAPTER VIII

COMMUNITY ROLE

#### Article 58

(1) The community has the right to obtain the widest possible opportunity to participate in the protection of women and children.

(2) The community role as referred to in paragraph (1) is carried out by individuals, child protection institutions, social community institutions, non-governmental organizations, educational institutions, religious institutions, business entities, and mass media.

#### Article 59

The community role is implemented in accordance with the provisions of applicable laws and regulations.

# CHAPTER IX

IMPLEMENTATION OF VICTIM RECOVERY

#### Article 60

(1) The implementation of recovery activities for women and child victims includes:

a) Health services;

Health services are carried out by health workers at health facilities owned by the government, regional government, and the community, including the private sector by providing treatment services and health recovery for victims.

b) Victim assistance;

Victim assistance is carried out by health workers, social workers, volunteer assistants, and/or spiritual counselors by providing counseling, therapy, spiritual guidance and advocacy for the strengthening and recovery of victims.

c) Counseling;

Counseling is provided by social workers, volunteer assistants, by listening empathetically and exploring problems for the psychological strengthening of victims.

d) Spiritual guidance;

Spiritual guidance is carried out by spiritual counselors by providing explanations regarding their rights and obligations, as well as strengthening faith and piety in accordance with the religion and beliefs they adhere to.

e) Resocialization.

Resocialization of victims is carried out by social agencies and social institutions so that victims can return to performing their social functions in society.

# CHAPTER IX

CRIMINAL PROVISIONS

#### Article 61

Any person who intentionally does not provide protection to women and children so that:

a. causes violence to occur;

b. allows violence to occur; and/or

c. does not report and does not provide protection to victims, shall be subject to sanctions in accordance with the provisions of laws and regulations.

#### I. GENERAL

The National Objectives of the Unitary State of the Republic of Indonesia, as stipulated in the Preamble of the 1945 Constitution in the fourth paragraph, are stated to include: (1) protecting the entire Indonesian nation and all Indonesian territory, (2) advancing public welfare, (3) educating the life of the nation and (4) participating in implementing world order based on: (a) independence, (b) lasting peace, and (c) social justice. To achieve these objectives, in accordance with the doctrine of trias politica, there are three State powers, namely executive power for the administration of government, legislative power as the maker of laws and regulations and judicial power as the implementer of laws and regulations. In Indonesia, these three powers complement each other, for the implementation of clean and authoritative governance, from the national level to the regional level.

The Republic of Indonesia is a State of Law (rechtstaat) and not a State of Power (machtstaat), so that all actions of its citizens, including those in the form of violence, committed by both men and women, are also regulated by law.

The Rights of Women and Children are recognized as Human Rights (HAM), which must be upheld and respected by all parties, but in reality there are still many women and children who become victims of violence. The commitment of the Central Government to protect children and women is reflected in the legislation below it.

Regional Autonomy that is implemented has strategic value so that each region forms legislation (Regional Regulations), not only to express local wisdom, but also to become a guideline for Regional Governments to implement the legislation above it (the 1945 Constitution, and Laws) to address legal problems and social problems that occur specifically in each region.

This Regional Regulation on the Protection of Women and Children is based on Pancasila, and the 1945 Constitution, and refers to Laws that regulate the protection of women and children victims of violence, including Law Number 7 of 1984 concerning Ratification of the Elimination of All Forms of Discrimination against Women, Law Number 39 of 1999 concerning Human Rights, Law Number 23 of 2002 concerning Child Protection as amended by Law Number 35 of 2014 concerning Amendment to Law Number 23 of 2002 concerning Child Protection, Law Number 23 of 2004 concerning the Elimination of Domestic Violence, Law Number 21 of 2007 concerning the Eradication of the Crime of Trafficking in Persons, Law Number 11 of 2012 concerning the Juvenile Justice System, Law Number 17 of 2016 concerning the Stipulation of Government Regulation in Lieu of Law Number 1 of 2016 concerning the Second Amendment to Law Number 23 of 2002 concerning Child Protection into Law, and other related Laws, Law Number 8 of 2016 concerning Persons with Disabilities, Supreme Court Regulation Number 3 of 2017 concerning Guidelines for Adjudicating Cases of Women Facing the Law,

The principles of this Regional Regulation are principles contained in previous laws and regulations, for example the principle of respect for Human Rights and gender equality and justice, are actually already contained in Article 27

of the 1945 Constitution, which essentially is a legal rule on gender equality and justice, while also reflecting recognition of human rights, because what is referred to as every Citizen certainly consists of men and women, having equal standing in the field of law and government.

Also in, Article 28 G paragraph (1) of the 1945 Constitution which essentially stipulates that every person, certainly both men and women, has the right to protection and freedom from fear. Article 28H paragraph (2) which essentially regulates the right of citizens to obtain facilities and special treatment to obtain equality and justice, certainly including women and children victims of violence.

Violence whose victims are women and children occurs both within marriage and outside marriage. Whereas Law Number 1 of 1974 concerning Marriage, which in Article 1 regulates the definition of marriage as a physical and spiritual bond, between a man and a woman to build a household based on Belief in the One and Only God. Article 30, stipulates that the Obligation of husband and wife is to be faithful, love, and help each other.

In this Marriage Law, it is regulated regarding who becomes a biological child, as well as joint property, including how to continue its ownership or inheritance to the children. If this Law is obeyed, it can prevent and eliminate Domestic Violence, unfortunately what happens in society is that Domestic Violence (KDRT) is increasingly rampant, precisely with increasingly complex modus operandi and causing increasingly horrifying consequences.

The principle of non-discrimination is in line with the recognition of human rights, therefore it is very appropriate that the Indonesian Government by enacting Law Number 7 of 1984 concerning Ratification of the Convention on the Elimination of All Forms of Discrimination Against Women (CEDAW), in Article 1 states the definition of discrimination against women and all its consequences if discrimination against women occurs.

Basically working is only for adults, while children, namely people under the age of 18, are obliged to seek knowledge and play. However, due to family economic limitations, there are some children who have to work. Therefore Law Number 20 of 1999, concerning the Minimum Age Limit for Children to be able to work by providing provisions that parents and employers should pay attention to the provisions of this Law, so that even though children are forced to work, they do not lose their youth and can still attend school to ensure their future. Children who are forced to work are found in all regions, both in urban and rural areas. In order for children to obtain their rights, socialization of Law Number 20 of 1999 is urgent to be carried out.

As a consequence of the Unitary State of the Republic of Indonesia as a State that is a signatory to the Universal Declaration of Human Rights, Law Number 39 of 1999 concerning Human Rights is the answer. Considering that even though since Indonesia's Independence, the 1945 Constitution also stated the recognition of human rights as regulated in Articles 27 to 34, but in reality there are still many women and children who become victims of violence which clearly constitutes a violation of human rights. In Law Number 39 of 1999 it is reaffirmed that women's rights are human rights (Article 45) and Article 52 paragraph (2), which states that Children's rights are Human Rights and for the benefit of children's rights it is recognized and protected by law even from the womb.

This means that children are recognized as having freedom and liberty in accordance with their dignity as human beings, so that all forms of violence whatsoever must not occur to children, because it constitutes a violation of Human Rights.

The commitment and political will of the Government of the Unitary State of the Republic of Indonesia to protect children is reinforced by the enactment of Law Number 23 of 2002 concerning Child Protection as amended by Law

Number 35 of 2014 concerning Amendment to Law Number 23 of 2002 concerning Child Protection. In accordance with the types of crimes against children that are increasingly occurring today, we need to study the provisions of articles 77 to 90, which already provide sufficiently severe criminal sanctions (threats). Examples related to this regional regulation for cases that occur in society are those that violate Articles 81, 82 and 83. For this reason, let us examine the contents of Articles 81, 82, 83 so that child victims of violence or sexual crimes in society receive legal protection in accordance with a sense of justice.

Article 81 states: (1) Any person who intentionally commits violence or threat of violence forcing a child to have intercourse with him or with another person, shall be punished with imprisonment for a maximum of 15 (fifteen) years and a minimum of 5 (five) years and a fine of at most Rp.300,000,000,- (three hundred million rupiah) and at least Rp.5,000,000,000.- (Five Billion rupiah). (2) The Criminal provisions as referred to in paragraph (1) also apply to any person who intentionally commits fraud, a series of lies, or persuades a child to have intercourse with him or with another person. (3) The Criminal provisions as referred to in paragraph (1) committed by Parents, Guardians, Child Caregivers, Educators or Education Personnel, then the punishment is increased by 1/3 (one third) of the punishment as referred to in paragraph (1)

Article 82 states: (1) Any person who intentionally commits violence or threat of violence, coercion, fraud, a series of lies, or persuades a child to commit or allow obscene acts to be committed, shall be punished with imprisonment for a maximum of 15 (fifteen) years and a minimum of 5 (five) years and a fine of at most Rp. 5,000,000,000,- (Five billion rupiah). (2) The Criminal provisions as referred to in paragraph (1) committed by Parents, Guardians, Child Caregivers, Educators or Education Personnel, then the punishment is increased by 1/3 (one third) of the punishment as referred to in paragraph (1)

Article 83 states: Any person who traffics, sells, or kidnaps a child for himself or to be sold, shall be punished with imprisonment for a maximum of 15 (fifteen) years and a minimum of 3 (three) years and a fine of at most Rp. 300,000,000,- (three hundred million rupiah) and at least Rp. 60,000,000,- (sixty million rupiah).

These three Articles are very reformative Articles when compared with the provisions in the Criminal Code, which in articles 281 to 296 regulate provisions, where the heaviest criminal threat is Article 295, namely 12 years. When compared, the Child Protection Law has been quite severe in establishing criminal threats and fines as regulated in Articles 81 to 83.

The problem is, even though the Law has established severe punishment threats or sanctions, there are still Law Enforcement Officials who have not optimally applied them, so it is still far from reality, between law in book and law in action. In cases as regulated in Articles 81 to 83, Law Enforcement Officials often conduct investigation and inquiry processes that are quite heavy and exhausting for child victims of violence, but in the prosecution and judge's verdict it is still light so it is not in accordance with justice.

Violence against women and children also occurs frequently within the household sphere, even though we have had a law for 13 years, namely Law Number 23 of 2004 concerning the Elimination of Domestic Violence (KDRT), but nowadays KDRT is increasing in number. For the prevention and handling of KDRT in Musi Rawas Regency at the Office of Women's Empowerment and Child Protection of Musi Rawas Regency, an Integrated Service Center for the Protection of Women and Children (P2TP2A) of Musi Rawas Regency has been established, based on the Decree of the Regent of Musi Rawas Number 703/KPTS/KPP/2015 namely concerning the Establishment of the Integrated Service Center for Women's Empowerment and Child Protection (P2TP2A) of Musi Rawas Regency.

The Integrated Service Center for Women's Empowerment and Child Protection (P2TP2A) becomes a place for reporting, handling, rehabilitation and reintegration of victims of violence. If there is KDRT or other violence that befalls women and children, it must be reported to the P2TP2A, but the existence of P2TP2A is not yet widely known by the community.

Law Number 23 of 2004 concerning the Elimination of KDRT, in essence is to realize a household that is sakinah, mawadah, and warokhmah, this can be read from the objectives of eliminating domestic violence, namely: (a) preventing all forms of domestic violence (KDRT); (b) protecting victims of KDRT, (c) prosecuting perpetrators of KDRT and (d) maintaining the integrity of a harmonious and prosperous household. In order for us to better understand the prohibition of KDRT, the important matters of Law Number 23 of 2004 concerning KDRT will be briefly described as follows:

Types of KDRT, include:

a. physical violence, is an act that results in (1) pain, (2) falling ill, or (3) serious injury;

b. psychological violence, is an act that results in (1) fear, (2) loss of self-confidence, (3) loss of ability to act, (4) feeling of helplessness, (5) and/or severe psychological suffering to a person;

c. sexual violence includes: (1) forced sexual relations committed against a person including a child, who resides within the household sphere, (2) forced sexual relations against one person within the household sphere with another person including a child, for commercial purposes and/or certain purposes;

d. household neglect, includes (1) neglecting a person including a child within the household sphere, whereas according to the law applicable to him or because of an agreement or contract he is obliged to provide life, care, or maintenance to that person or child, (2) resulting in economic dependence by restricting and/or prohibiting proper work inside or outside the home so that the victim is under the control of that person.

The rights of KDRT victims are to obtain:

(1) protection from family, police, prosecutors, courts, advocates, social institutions, or other parties either temporarily or based on a court order;

(2) health services in accordance with medical needs;

(3) special handling related to victim confidentiality;

(4) assistance by social workers and legal aid at every level of the examination process in accordance with the provisions of laws and regulations and

(5) spiritual guidance services. All parties both from government elements, Law Enforcement Officials (APH) and NGOs, have become members of the P2TP2A of Musi Rawas Regency.
