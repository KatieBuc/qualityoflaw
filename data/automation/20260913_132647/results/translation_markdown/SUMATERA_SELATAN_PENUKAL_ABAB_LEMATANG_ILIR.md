REGENT OF PENUKAL ABAB LEMATANG ILIR.

Considering : 

a. that all forms of violence or treatment that degrade the degree and dignity of women and children constitute a violation of human rights, therefore it is necessary to protect their honor and dignity and guarantee their right to life in accordance with their nature and destiny without discrimination;

b. that violence and treatment that degrades the degree and dignity of women and children must be subject to protection efforts;

c. that based on the considerations as referred to in letters a and b, it is necessary to establish a Regional Regulation on the Implementation of Protection for Women and Children.

In view of : 1. Article 18 Paragraph (6) of the 1945 Constitution of the Republic of Indonesia;

2. Law Number 4 of 1979 concerning Child Welfare (State Gazette of the Republic of Indonesia of 1979 Number 32, Supplement to the State Gazette of the Republic of Indonesia Number 3143);

# CHAPTER I 

GENERAL PROVISIONS

#### Article 1

In this Regional Regulation, what is meant by :

1. Regency is Penukal Abab Lematang Ilir Regency.

2. Regency Government is the Regent as an element of the regency government administration who leads the implementation of government affairs that are the authority of the autonomous region.

3. Regent is the Regent of Penukal Abab Lematang Ilir.

4. Regency Regional Apparatus is an auxiliary element of the Regent and the Regency Regional House of Representatives in the administration of government affairs that are the authority of the regency.

5. Child is a person who has not yet reached 18 (eighteen) years of age, including a child still in the womb.

6. Woman is a human being of female sex and a person who is recognized by law as a woman.

7. Fulfillment of Children's Rights is all activities to guarantee and protect children and their rights so that they can live, grow, develop and participate optimally in accordance with human dignity and receive protection from violence and discrimination.

8. Violence is any act that results in misery or suffering whether physical, sexual, psychological, and/or domestic neglect including threats to commit acts, coercion, or deprivation of a person's freedom unlawfully.

9. Victim is a woman and/or child who experiences misery and/or suffering either directly or indirectly as a result of violence occurring in the territory of Penukal Abab Lematang Ilir Regency.

10. Protection of women is all activities aimed at providing a sense of security carried out by the police, prosecutor's office, court, social institutions, or other parties who know or hear that violence against women will or has occurred.

11. Child protection is all activities to guarantee and protect children and their rights so that they can live, grow, develop and participate optimally in accordance with human dignity and receive protection from violence and discrimination.

12. Prevention is activities carried out to prevent the occurrence of acts of violence against women and children.

13. Service is action taken as soon as possible to victims when seeing, hearing and knowing that violence against victims will, is or has occurred.

14. Empowerment is the strengthening of women victims of violence to be able to try and work independently after they are recovered and provided with health and social rehabilitation services.

15. Health Rehabilitation is an effort to restore the condition of victims including physical, psychological and social health so that victims can carry out their roles again properly and reasonably both in the family and in society.

16. Social rehabilitation is a service aimed at restoring and developing the ability of a person experiencing social dysfunction so that they can carry out their social functions properly.

17. Repatriation is an effort to return victims of violence from the regional territory to their area of origin.

18. Social reintegration is an effort to reunite victims with family, substitute family or community that can provide protection and fulfillment of needs for victims.

19. Integrated Service Center, hereinafter abbreviated as PPT, is a unit that organizes integrated services for victims of violence and is integrated with PPT that organizes integrated services for witnesses and/or victims of criminal acts of human trafficking.

#### Article 2

The Implementation of Protection for Women and Children is carried out based on the principles of:

a. respect and protection of human rights;

b. justice and gender equality;

c. non-discrimination;

d. the best interests of the Child;

e. the right to life, survival, and development;

f. Empowerment; and

g. legal certainty.

#### Article 3

Protection of Women and Children aims to :

a. guarantee the fulfillment of the rights of Women and Children;

b. maintain the integrity of the household in order to realize a harmonious family;

c. prevent violence against Women and Children;

d. protect and provide a sense of security for Women and Children;

e. provide services to Women and Children victims of acts of violence;

f. provide protection to reporters and witnesses; and

g. carry out empowerment for Women victims of violence.

# CHAPTER II 

PROTECTION OF WOMEN AND CHILDREN

## Part One 

Women's Rights

#### Article 4

Every Woman has the right:

a. to live, sustain life and her existence;

b. to form a family and continue offspring through lawful marriage;

c. to develop herself through the fulfillment of her basic needs;

d. to obtain education and benefit from science and technology, arts and culture, in order to improve the quality of her life and for the welfare of humankind;

e. to advance herself in fighting for her rights collectively to build society, nation and state;

f. to recognition, guarantee, protection and fair legal certainty as well as equal treatment before the law;

g. to work and receive fair and proper remuneration and treatment in employment relations;

h. to obtain equal opportunities in government;

i. to freedom of association, assembly and expression of opinion;

j. to protection of her person, family, honor, dignity and property under her control, and the right to a sense of security and protection from threats of fear to do or not do something which constitutes a basic right;

k. to be free from torture or treatment that degrades human dignity and the right to obtain political asylum from another country;

l. to live in physical and spiritual prosperity, to reside and obtain a good and healthy living environment, and the right to obtain health services;

m. to social security that enables her full development as a dignified human being;

n. to equality of position in law and government;

o. to work and a livelihood worthy of humanity;

p. in efforts to defend the state;

q. to communicate and obtain information to develop her personal and social environment, and the right to seek, obtain, possess, store, process and convey information using all types of available channels;

r. to embrace her respective religion and to worship according to that religion and belief; and

s. to obtain education.

## Part Two

Fulfillment of Children's Rights

### Paragraph 1

Rights and Obligations of Children

#### Article 5

Every Child has the right:

a. to birth registration;

b. to obtain health;

c. to obtain education; and

d. to social welfare.

#### Article 6

Every Child has the obligation to:

a. respect parents, guardians and teachers;

b. love family, community and care for friends;

c. love the homeland, nation and state;

d. perform worship in accordance with their religion and belief;

e. study and develop themselves in accordance with their abilities and talents, interests; and

f. have noble character.

### Paragraph 2

Birth Registration

#### Article 7

For birth registration of children as referred to in Article 5 letter a, the Regency Government is obligated to issue a birth certificate in accordance with the provisions of Laws and Regulations.

### Paragraph 3

Health

#### Article 8

(1) The implementation of Fulfillment of Children's Rights in the field of health as referred to in Article 5 letter b, the Regency Government is obligated to provide facilities and organize comprehensive health services for children.

(2) Comprehensive health services as referred to in paragraph (1) include:

a. promotive, preventive, curative and rehabilitative efforts for basic health services; and

b. exemption from cost burden for malnourished children, Children with disabilities, children with special needs, street children, children infected with HIV/AIDS, child laborers, children victims of narcotics, alcohol, psychotropic and other addictive substances (NAPZA) abuse, Children who are victims of violence, such as neglect, economically and sexually exploited and victims of human trafficking.

(3) Exemption from cost burden for Children as referred to in paragraph (2) letter b is for Children from poor families unless otherwise regulated in accordance with the health financing program established by the government.

#### Article 9

The Regency Government together with the community, family and parents are obligated to ensure that Children who are born are protected from diseases that threaten the continuity of life and/or cause disability.

### Paragraph 4

Education

#### Article 10

(1) The implementation of Fulfillment of Children's Rights in the field of education as referred to in Article 5 letter c, the Regency Government guarantees the implementation of the 9 (nine) year compulsory education program.

(2) Every organizer of an education unit is obligated to provide opportunities for Children to obtain education services without discrimination.

#### Article 11

Every organizer of an education unit is prohibited from expelling Children from educational institutions unless there is a guarantee for the continuity of the Child's education.

#### Article 12

Every organizer of an education unit is obligated to coordinate with relevant child protection agencies if they find Children dropping out of school due to being victims of acts of violence.

#### Article 13

Children with disabilities and Children with special needs are given equal opportunities to obtain education services:

a. formal;

b. non-formal;

c. informal;

d. special education; and

e. inclusive.

#### Article 14

Early Childhood Education for ages 0 (zero) to 3 (three) years and 4 (four) to 6 (six) years may be organized by:

a. Posyandu institutions;

b. Posyandu gardens;

c. Early Childhood Education Posts; and

d. similar Early Childhood Education unit institutions.

### Paragraph 5

Social Welfare

#### Article 15

(1) The implementation of Child Protection and Empowerment in the field of social welfare as referred to in Article 5 letter d includes:

a. children in conflict with the law;

b. child victims of violence who are economically and sexually exploited;

c. neglected children;

d. child victims of abduction and child victims of human trafficking;

e. child victims of narcotics, alcohol, psychotropic and other addictive substances (NAPZA) abuse;

f. children infected with HIV/AIDS;

g. children who do not have parents and are cared for by other parties or families who are unable;

h. street children;

i. child victims of natural disasters or social disasters;

j. children with physical and mental disabilities;

k. children of migrant worker families;

l. children living in or around prostitution locations; and

m. child victims of other mistreatment.

(2) Social welfare as referred to in paragraph (1) may be in the form of ease of access to services:

a. health;

b. education;

c. legal aid;

d. assistance;

e. social, mental and spiritual guidance;

f. social rehabilitation;

g. empowerment;

h. social assistance; and/or

i. reintegration.

(3) Special infrastructure for persons with disabilities in public places.

#### Article 16

(1) The Regency Government is obliged to protect the fulfillment of the rights of Child workers in the informal sector.

(2) Child workers in the informal sector as referred to in paragraph (1) include:

a. domestic workers;

b. shoe shiners;

c. street vendors;

d. scavengers;

e. parking attendants;

f. children who work in agriculture, animal husbandry and plantations;

g. children who work in mining; and

h. other informal sector work that employs Children.

(3) Every person who employs Children in the informal sector must pay attention to the requirements:

a. minimum age of 15 (fifteen) years, except for the category of Worst Forms of Child Labor minimum 18 (eighteen) years;

b. obtain written consent from the parents/guardian of the Child worker;

c. have a written employment agreement between the employer and the parent/guardian of the Child worker and obtain validation from the authorized agency;

d. not employed at night;

e. working time of at most 3 (three) hours per day;

f. not employed in a place or environment that can disrupt the child's growth and development process, whether physical, mental, moral and intellectual or the Child's health;

g. provide opportunities to obtain education in accordance with their talents and interests;

h. employ for light types of work; and

i. provide one day off per week.

#### Article 17

The fulfillment of the rights of Child workers in the informal sector as referred to in Article 16 paragraph (1) includes:

a. counseling to stakeholders about the rights of Children;

b. psychological, medical and legal service assistance and socio-economic reintegration;

c. empowering families through providing training, business capital stimulus and assistance;

d. scholarships to continue formal education to higher levels; and

e. non-formal education and skills training for those who do not pursue formal education.

## Part Three

Forms of Violence

#### Article 18

Forms of violence against Women and Children include:

a. physical violence;

b. psychological violence;

c. sexual violence;

d. neglect;

e. exploitation; and/or

f. other violence.

## Part Four

Prevention of Violence against Women and Children

#### Article 19

(1) Efforts to prevent violence against Women and Children are carried out in an integrated manner by the Regency Government and coordinated by the technical Regional Apparatus.

(2) Prevention efforts as referred to in paragraph (1) are implemented by:

a. conducting guidance towards harmonious household integrity;

b. forming work networks in efforts to prevent violence;

c. conducting coordination, integration, synchronization of violence prevention based on partnership patterns;

d. establishing a violence prevention system;

e. conducting socialization regarding laws and regulations related to the protection of Women and Children victims of violence; and

f. providing critical education regarding the rights of Women and Children to the community.

#### Article 20

Efforts to prevent violence against Women and Children as referred to in Article 19 paragraph (1) in addition to the Regency Government are also implemented by:

a. family and/or closest relatives;

b. community;

c. educational institutions; and

d. Non-Governmental Organizations.

## Part Five

Handling of Women and Children Victims of Violence

#### Article 21

The implementation of Handling of Women and Children victims of violence is carried out with:

a. speed;

b. safety and comfort;

c. sense of empathy;

d. non-discrimination;

e. easy accessibility;

f. no charges; and

g. guaranteed confidentiality.

#### Article 22

Forms of handling of Women and Children victims of violence as referred to in Article 21 include services for:

a. complaints, consultation, and counseling;

b. assistance;

c. medical rehabilitation;

d. social rehabilitation;

e. legal; and

f. social reintegration.

## Part Six

Financing

#### Article 23

Financing required for the implementation of Protection of Women and Children is charged to:

a. Regional Revenue and Expenditure Budget; and

b. Other lawful and non-binding sources of funds in accordance with the provisions of applicable Laws and Regulations.

# CHAPTER III

INTEGRATED SERVICE CENTER (PPT)

#### Article 24

(1) The implementation of services for Women and Children victims of violence is carried out in an integrated manner by the PPT.

(2) The PPT as referred to in paragraph (1) is an integrated part and constitutes one unit with the PPT that provides integrated services for witnesses and/or victims of human trafficking crimes.

(3) The implementation of services for Women and Children victims of violence as referred to in paragraph (1) includes:

a. providing fast and free services to victims;

b. providing protection and fulfillment of victims' rights to health rehabilitation, social rehabilitation, repatriation, social reintegration and legal assistance;

c. cooperating with certain institutions in providing translators and volunteer assistants needed for victims;

d. networking with government or private hospitals for treatment and health recovery of victims and cooperating with witness and victim protection institutions, social protection houses or trauma centers owned by the government, community or other institutions for health recovery of victims;

e. providing convenience, comfort and safety for victims;

f. maintaining confidentiality of victims; and

g. providing fulfillment of legal assistance for victims.

(4) Further provisions regarding procedures and mechanisms for implementing the PPT are regulated by Regent Regulation.

# CHAPTER IV

RIGHTS OF WOMEN AND CHILDREN VICTIMS OF VIOLENCE

#### Article 25

Every Woman and Child victim of violence has the right:

a. to complaint handling;

b. to have their dignity respected as human beings;

c. to receive protection from family, community, regional government and/or other parties either temporarily or based on protection determination from the court;

d. to health and psychological recovery from the suffering experienced by the victim;

e. to determine their own decisions;

f. to special handling related to victim confidentiality;

g. to obtain information;

h. to confidentiality of their identity;

i. to social rehabilitation;

j. to assistance by social workers and legal aid at every level of the examination process in accordance with the provisions of Laws and Regulations; and

k. to spiritual guidance services.

#### Article 26

Women and children victims of violence in addition to having rights as referred to in Article 25, also receive special rights as follows:

a. right to survival;

b. to grow and develop;

c. to receive equal protection; and

d. to participate in community life.

# CHAPTER V

MONITORING AND EVALUATION

#### Article 27

(1) The Regency Government conducts monitoring to ensure synergy, balance and effectiveness of integrated steps in the implementation of policies, programs, activities, Protection of Women and Children.

(2) Monitoring as referred to in paragraph (1) is carried out by Regional Apparatus and PPT.

(3) Monitoring as referred to in paragraph (1) is to determine the progress and obstacles in the implementation of policies, programs and activities for Protection of Women and Children.

(4) Monitoring is carried out periodically through coordination and direct monitoring of Regional Apparatus that implement policies, activities for Protection of Women and Children.

(5) Monitoring stages as referred to in paragraph (1) include:

a. planning

b. policy implementation:

c. program implementation; and

d. Protection of Women and Children activities every year.

#### Article 28

(1) Evaluation of the implementation of policies, programs and activities for the Protection of Women and Children shall be conducted at the end of each Fiscal Year or when necessary according to needs.

(2) The results of the evaluation of the implementation of policies, programs and activities for the Protection of Women and Children as referred to in paragraph (1) shall be used as input material for the formulation of policies, programs and activities for the following year.

(3) Evaluation as referred to in paragraph (1) shall be implemented in accordance with the provisions of laws and regulations.

# CHAPTER VI

GUIDANCE AND SUPERVISION

#### Article 29

(1) The Regent may appoint officials or related Regional Apparatus to conduct guidance and supervision of the implementation of the Protection of Women and Children.

(2) Guidance and supervision as referred to in paragraph (1) shall include:

a. provision of implementation instructions;

b. guidance;

c. supervision;

d. monitoring; and

e. evaluation of the Implementation of the Protection of Women and Children.

# CHAPTER VII

COMMUNITY PARTICIPATION

#### Article 30

(1) Community participation in the implementation of Protection of Women and Children shall be as follows:

a. forming family partners at the Village/Sub-district level by the community;

b. conducting socialization of the rights of women and children independently;

c. providing first aid to victims; and

d. reporting to the competent authority if violence against victims occurs in their environment.

(2) Community participation as referred to in paragraph (1) shall be carried out by:

a. individuals;

b. community institutions;

c. non-governmental organizations;

d. educational institutions;

e. religious institutions;

f. private sector; and/or

g. mass media.

# CHAPTER VIII

PROVISIONS ON INVESTIGATION

#### Article 31

(1) Certain Civil Servant Officials within the Regency Government who are granted special authority as Investigators to conduct investigations of criminal acts as regulated in the provisions of laws and regulations.

(2) Investigators as referred to in paragraph (1) are certain Civil Servants within the Regency Government who are appointed by authorized officials in accordance with the provisions of laws and regulations.

(3) The authority of Investigators as referred to in paragraph (1) shall include:

a. receiving reports or complaints from a person regarding the existence of a criminal act;

b. taking first action at that time at the scene and conducting examination;

c. ordering a suspect to stop and examining the suspect's identity card;

d. seizing objects or documents;

e. taking fingerprints and photographing a person;

f. summoning persons to be heard and examined as suspects or witnesses;

g. bringing in experts needed in connection with the examination of the case;

h. conducting cessation of investigation after receiving instructions from the Investigator that there is insufficient evidence or the incident does not constitute a criminal act and subsequently through the investigator notifying this matter to the Public Prosecutor, the suspect or their family;

i. conducting other actions according to law that can be accounted for.

(4) Investigators as referred to in paragraph (1) shall notify the commencement of the investigation and submit the results of their investigation to the Public Prosecutor through the Investigator

Official of the Police of the Republic of Indonesia, in accordance with the provisions stipulated in the Criminal Procedure Code.

# CHAPTER IX

CRIMINAL PROVISIONS

#### Article 32

Any person who intentionally commits or allows the occurrence of violence against women and children and/or does not report the occurrence of violence against women and children shall be subject to sanctions in accordance with the provisions stipulated in Law Number 35 of 2014 concerning Amendments to Law Number 23 of 2004 concerning Child Protection and Law Number 23 of 2003 concerning the Elimination of Domestic Violence.

# CHAPTER X

CLOSING PROVISIONS

#### Article 33

This Regional Regulation shall come into force on the date of promulgation. In order that everyone may know of it, the promulgation of this Regional Regulation is ordered by its Placement in the Regional Gazette of Penukal Abab Lematang Ilir Regency.

#### I. GENERAL

Violence against women and children constitutes a violation of human rights so that their dignity and honor need to be protected and their right to life guaranteed in accordance with their nature and disposition without discrimination. The fate of women and children victims of violence must be given attention by the regional government. The high number of cases of violence against women and children is often caused by factors that develop in society, for example low levels of economy, education, and environments located in the industrial sector. Therefore, victims of violence such as this need to receive protection in accordance with the principles of justice, truth, legal certainty, equality and upholding human rights.

For the empowerment of victims of violence, the regency government establishes an Integrated Service Center (PPT) which is a generic term that can be used by various institutions that provide services for victims of violence such as the Integrated Service Center for Women and Children Empowerment (P2TP2A), Safe House, Child Social Protection House (RPSA), Trauma Center Protection House (RPTC), Women's Social Protection House (RPSW), Shelter House, etc.

The Regency Government is obligated and responsible to implement efforts to protect women and children through implementing protection policies for women and children applied by the government, establishing policies, programs, and activities for the protection of women and children, establishing PPT, ensuring the implementation of ease of service to victims, seeking effectiveness and efficiency for the victim recovery process, and seeking the creation of cooperation and coordination in efforts to protect women and children.

Article 18

Letter a

What is meant by "physical violence" is an act that results in pain, injury, wounds or disability to a person's body, miscarriage, fainting and/or causing death.

Letter b

What is meant by "psychological violence" is an act that results in fear, loss of self-confidence, loss of ability to act, feelings of helplessness and/or severe psychological suffering to a person.

Letter c

What is meant by "sexual violence" is an act in the form of sexual harassment, forced sexual intercourse, forced unnatural or unwanted sexual intercourse and/or forced sexual intercourse with others for commercial purposes and/or certain purposes.

Letter d

What is meant by "neglect" is an act that results in the failure to meet the needs of children properly, whether physical, mental, spiritual or social, carried out by parents, guardians, or any other party responsible for their care; the act of deliberately neglecting to maintain, care for, or look after a child as appropriate carried out by parents, guardians, or other parties responsible for their care; the act of neglecting a person within the scope of their household, whereas according to the law applicable to them or because of an agreement or contract they are obliged to provide life, care, or maintenance to that person; and/or an act that results in economic dependence by restricting and/or prohibiting proper work inside or outside the home so that the victim is under the control of that person.

Letter e

What is meant by "exploitation" is an act that exploits economically or sexually with the intention of benefiting oneself or others; an act with or without the consent of the victim including prostitution, forced labor or service, slavery or similar practices, oppression, extortion, utilization of physical, sexual, reproductive organs, or unlawfully moving or transplanting organs and/or body tissues or utilizing the labor or ability of a person by another party to obtain material or immaterial benefits; and/or any form of utilization of sexual organs or other body organs of the victim to obtain benefits, including but not limited to all activities of prostitution or indecency.

Letter f

What is meant by "other violence" is the threat of violence including: any unlawful act in the form of speech, writing, images, symbols, or body movements, whether with or without using means that cause fear or restrict a person's fundamental freedom; and coercion, including: a situation where a person/victim is ordered to do something in such a way that the person does something contrary to their own will.

Article 22

Letter a

What is meant by "complaint, consultation, and counseling services" includes initial identification or recording of victims; case identification; and consent to take action.

Letter b

What is meant by "assistance services" includes accompanying victims during the examination and health recovery process; accompanying victims during the examination process at the police, prosecutor's office and court; monitoring the interests and rights of victims in the examination process at the police, prosecutor's office and court; maintaining the privacy and confidentiality of victims from all parties who have no interest, including reporting by the mass media; providing a sense of security to victims; and providing continuous handling up to the rehabilitation stage.

Letter c

What is meant by "medical rehabilitation services" includes first aid to victims; treatment and recovery of physical injuries aimed at restoring the physical condition of victims carried out by medical personnel and paramedics; and referral to health services.

Letter d

What is meant by "social rehabilitation services" is services provided in order to restore the traumatic condition of victims. The form of service is carried out through social support through spiritual guidance to victims; and psychological recovery of victims.

Letter e

What is meant by "legal services" is to assist victims in undergoing the judicial process by providing legal consultation that includes information regarding the rights of victims and the judicial process; accompanying victims at the investigation, prosecution, and examination levels in court hearings and helping victims to fully describe the violence they experienced; and coordinating with fellow law enforcers, volunteer assistants, and social workers so that the judicial process proceeds as it should.

Letter f

What is meant by "social reintegration services" is aimed at returning victims to their families and social environment. Social reintegration services are carried out by the Regency Government by coordinating with relevant government and non-government agencies and institutions; and village community institutions and/or local community leaders.
