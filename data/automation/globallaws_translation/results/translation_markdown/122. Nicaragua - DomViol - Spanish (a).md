# Law No. 779

Law No. 779

The President of the Republic of Nicaragua
To its inhabitants, Know ye:

That,

THE NATIONAL ASSEMBLY CONSIDERING

I
That the existing regulations to curb gender-based violence against women have not obtained the desired results for the effective protection of their life, liberty and personal integrity, therefore it is indispensable to promulgate an autonomous Law of special character, which addresses this problem in a comprehensive manner, typifying and sanctioning the different manifestations of violence towards women.
II
The State of Nicaragua has signed and ratified various international instruments such as the "Convention on the Elimination of All Forms of Discrimination against Women", the "Inter-American Convention to Prevent, Punish and Eradicate Violence Against Women", the "Convention on the Rights of the Child", and the "International Convention on the Rights of Persons with Disabilities", among others. These instruments oblige the State to establish special norms that ensure effective equality before the Law, to eliminate discrimination and explicitly prohibit violence towards women in any of its manifestations.
III
That the Political Constitution of the Republic of Nicaragua enshrines the constitutional recognition of human rights, individual rights, the right to life, physical, mental and moral integrity, not to be subjected to torture, to honor, to dignity, to personal liberty, security, legal capacity; it also broadly recognizes the rights of detained and prosecuted persons; however, it is necessary to establish minimum guarantees for victims of crimes.
THEREFORE
In the exercise of its powers,
HAS ENACTED
The following:

COMPREHENSIVE LAW AGAINST VIOLENCE TOWARDS WOMEN AND AMENDMENTS TO LAW No. 641, "PENAL CODE"

## TITLE I

GENERAL PROVISIONS AND PRINCIPLES

### Chapter I

Of the object, scope and policies

#### Article 1 Object of the Law

The purpose of this Law is to act against violence exercised towards women, with the purpose of protecting the human rights of women and guaranteeing them a life free of violence, that favors their development and well-being in accordance with the principles of equality and non-discrimination; to establish comprehensive protection measures to prevent, punish and eradicate violence and provide assistance to women victims of violence, promoting changes in the sociocultural and patriarchal patterns that sustain power relations.

#### Art. 2. Scope of application of the Law

This Law shall apply both in the public and private spheres to whoever exercises violence against women in a specific manner or repeatedly. The effects of this Law shall be applicable to whoever is or has been linked by relationship of consanguinity, affinity, subjects to guardianship, spouse, ex-spouse, cohabitant in stable de facto union, ex-cohabitant in stable de facto union, boyfriends, ex-boyfriends, affective relationship, strangers, as well as any other interpersonal relationship that may generate this type of violence.
Violence in the public sphere: That which by willful or negligent action or omission, takes place in the community, in the workplace and institutional sphere or any other place, that is perpetrated against the rights of women by any person or by the State, authorities or public officials.
Violence in the private sphere: That which occurs within the family sphere or in any other interpersonal relationship, whether the aggressor shares or has shared the same domicile as the woman.

#### Art. 3. Public policies of comprehensive protection towards the victim of violence

The State through the competent body must:

a)	Guarantee to all women, the effective exercise of their rights, ensuring their rapid, transparent and efficient access to the services established for that purpose.
b)	Strengthen and promote dissemination, awareness-raising and consciousness-raising campaigns on violence towards women, informing about the

rights, resources and public and private services to prevent, punish and eradicate it.

c)	Improve public policies for the prevention of violence towards women and the eradication of gender discrimination; develop, implement and monitor an action plan for the prevention, punishment, attention and eradication of violence towards women.
d)	Guarantee economic, professional, technological, scientific and any other nature resources, to State institutions, to ensure the attention, prevention and eradication of violence against women, as well as the adequate punishment of those guilty of it and the implementation of socio-educational measures that prevent their recidivism.
e)	Generate and reinforce the minimum standards for early detection and approach to violence, in accordance with the object of the Law, in information, attention, emergency, protection, support, shelter and comprehensive recovery services, as well as establish a system for the most efficient coordination of existing services at the municipal, departmental, regional and national level.
f)	Promote the collaboration and participation of entities, associations and organizations that act against violence towards women from civil society.

g)	Promote permanent training and specialization of justice operators, who intervene in the process of information, attention and protection to victims.
h)	Promote permanent training and specialization of officials of the Women's and Children's Police Station, and of the Public Prosecutor's Office.
i)	Establish and strengthen emergency and precautionary protection measures that guarantee the rights protected in this Law, as well as the personal, physical, emotional, labor and patrimonial protection of women victims of violence.
j)	Open a free and accessible telephone line connected to police instances and the Public Prosecutor's Office, intended to provide information and offer advice on existing resources in matters of prevention of violence towards women and assistance to those who suffer from it.

### Chapter II Principles, Sources and Rights

#### Art. 4. Guiding Principles of the Law

The guiding principles contained in this article are established in order to guarantee the legal equality of persons, in accordance with the international instruments signed and ratified by the Republic of Nicaragua:
a) Principle of access to justice: State Institutions, operators of the justice system and communal authorities must guarantee women, without any distinction, effective access to the services and resources they provide, eliminating all types of barriers and obstacles of any kind that impede this access.
b) Principle of celerity: The procedure established by this Law must be processed with agility, celerity and without any delay, until obtaining a resolution within the established time periods; failure to comply with the responsibilities of officials leads to their being subject to administrative measures or sanctions as applicable.
c) Principle of concentration: Once the trial has begun, it must conclude on the same day when all the evidence provided by the parties is presented. If this is not possible, it shall continue for the fewest number of consecutive days in accordance with the provisions of articles 288 and 289 of Law No. 406, "Code of Criminal Procedure of the Republic of Nicaragua".
d) Principle of interinstitutional coordination: To ensure that service providers of the Women's and Children's Police Station, Public Prosecutor's Office, Public Defender's Office, Institute of Legal Medicine, Judicial Branch, Special Women's Ombudsman Office, Special Children's Ombudsman Office, Ministry of Education, Ministry of Health, Ministry of Family, Adolescence and Childhood, Nicaraguan Women's Institute, National Penitentiary System and communal authorities coordinate the actions required for the protection of persons affected by violence.
e) Principle of real equality: All actions of the justice system shall seek to achieve equality of persons without any distinction based on gender, age, ethnicity and disability. Ensuring respect and protection of human rights, taking into account the cultural, economic, physical and social differences that prevail among them, in order to resolve with criteria of equality.
f) Principle of comprehensiveness: The protection of women experiencing violence requires medical, legal, psychological and social care in a comprehensive and timely manner to detect, protect and restore rights.

g) Principle of due diligence of the State: The State has the obligation to act with due diligence to prevent, investigate, punish and eradicate violence against women, in order to guarantee the life, safety and protection of victims of violence.
h) Principle of the best interests of the child: The best interests of the girl, boy and adolescent is understood to mean everything that favors their full physical, psychological, moral, cultural and social development, in accordance with the evolution of their faculties that benefits them to the maximum degree and especially the recognition, validity, satisfaction and enjoyment of their rights, freedoms and guarantees in a comprehensive manner.
i) Principle of non-discrimination: It is the elimination of all distinction, exclusion or restriction based on birth, nationality, political creed, race, age, sex, language, religion, opinion, origin, economic position, social condition, disability, that has as its object or result, the impairment or annulment of the recognition, enjoyment or exercise of human rights and fundamental freedoms. Also constituting discrimination are actions or omissions that do not have the intention to discriminate but do have a discriminatory result.
j) Principle of non-secondary victimization: The State must guarantee that the authorities that make up the justice system and other institutions that attend to, prevent, investigate and punish violence, must deploy special prevention measures to avoid situations of incomprehension, unnecessary repetitions and annoyances that may be applied to victims.
k) Principle of non-violence: Violence against women constitutes a violation of fundamental freedoms, totally or partially limiting the recognition, enjoyment and exercise of human rights.
l) Principle of full gender equality: Gender relations must be based on the full equality of men and women, and should not be founded on a relationship of power or domination, in which the man subordinates, subjugates or attempts to control the woman.
m) Principle of protection of victims: Victims of the punishable acts described herein have the right to access the organs of justice free of charge and must be attended to expeditiously, without undue delays or useless formalities and obtain a resolution within the time periods established by Law, without prejudice to the rights of accused or charged persons.

n) Principle of publicity: The trial shall be public, unless at the request of the victim of violence, the court decides that it be held totally or partially behind closed doors, the victim having to be informed in advance and in a timely manner that she may exercise this right.
ñ) Principle of compensation: The administration of justice shall guarantee the necessary mechanisms to ensure that the victim of violence has effective access to compensation and reparation for damages or other fair and effective means of compensation as part of the process of restoring her well-being.

#### Art. 5. Sources of Interpretation

The Political Constitution of the Republic of Nicaragua, Codes, Laws and International Human Rights Instruments signed and ratified by the State of Nicaragua constitute sources of interpretation of this Law.
In particular, the following shall be sources of interpretation of this Law:

a) The Convention on the Elimination of All Forms of Discrimination against Women; and
b) The Inter-American Convention to Prevent, Punish and Eradicate Violence against Women.

#### Art. 6. Participation of Society

Society through its organizations has the right and the duty to participate in a leading role to achieve the full and effective validity of this Law.

#### Art. 7. Protected Rights of Women

All women have the right both in the public and private spheres to live a life free of violence, to their sexual and reproductive freedom and integrity, as well as to the recognition, enjoyment, exercise and protection of all their human rights and freedoms enshrined in the Political Constitution of the Republic of Nicaragua, in the national legal system and International Human Rights Instruments.
These rights include, among others:

a) The right to have their life respected; and to live without violence and without discrimination;
b) The right to health and education;

c) The right to have their physical, psychological, moral, sexual, patrimonial or economic integrity respected;

d) The right to freedom, to personal security, to privacy;
e) The right to freedom of beliefs and thought;

f) The right not to be subjected to torture, or to cruel or degrading treatment;
g) The right to have the dignity inherent to their person respected and to have their family protected;

h) The right to equal protection before the Law and of the Law;
i) The right to receive adequate information and advice;

j) The right to a simple and expeditious remedy before the institutions of the justice system and other State Institutions to protect them against acts that violate their rights; and
k) The right to have equality in public office and to participate in public affairs including decision-making.

#### Art. 8. Forms of violence against women

Violence towards women in any of its forms and settings must be considered a manifestation of discrimination and inequality that women experience in power relations, recognized by the State as a public health problem, a citizen security problem and in particular:
a) Misogyny: Conduct of hatred towards women manifested in violent and cruel acts against her for the fact of being a woman.
b) Physical violence: Any action or omission that endangers or harms the bodily integrity of the woman, resulting in physical injury.
c) Violence in the exercise of public office against women: That carried out by authorities or public officials, professionals, personnel and agents belonging to any public body or institution, whose purpose is to delay, obstruct, deny or prevent women from having access to justice and public policies.
d) Labor violence against women: That which discriminates against women in public or private work settings and obstructs their

access to employment, hiring, decent and equitable salary, promotion, stability or permanence in the same, requiring requirements regarding marital status, maternity, surgical sterilization, age, physical appearance, performance of pregnancy test or Human Immunodeficiency Virus HIV/AIDS test or other test on the health condition of the woman. It also constitutes violence against women in the labor sphere to violate the right to equal remuneration for equal task or function. Likewise, it includes psychological harassment in a systematic manner against a particular female worker with the purpose of achieving her labor exclusion.
e) Patrimonial and economic violence: Action or omission that implies damage, loss, theft, destruction, retention or diversion of objects, personal documents, valuables, patrimonial rights or economic resources intended to satisfy her needs, property of a woman and her own or shared resources in the family or couple setting.

Patrimonial and economic violence also constitutes control of property and financial resources, thus maintaining dominance over the woman, refusal to provide necessary resources in the home, failure to recognize the economic value of the woman's domestic work within the home and the demand that she abandon or not begin paid work.
f) Psychological violence: Action or omission intended to degrade or control the actions, behaviors, decisions and beliefs of the woman through intimidation, manipulation, coercion, destructive comparisons, occasional or permanent surveillance, insults, direct or indirect threat, humiliation, isolation or any other conduct that implies harm to mental health, self-determination or her personal development.
g) Sexual violence: Any action that compels the woman to maintain sexual contact, physical or verbal, or participate in other sexual interactions through the use of force, intimidation, coercion, blackmail, bribery, manipulation, threat or any other mechanism that nullifies or limits her will or sexual freedom, regardless of whether the aggressor may have a conjugal, couple, affective or kinship relationship with the woman.

## TÍTULO II

OFFENSES AND PENALTIES
Capítulo Único
Offenses of violence against women and their penalties

#### Art. 9 Femicide

A man commits the offense of femicide who, within the framework of unequal power relations between men and women, causes the death of a woman whether in the public or private sphere, in any of the following circumstances:
a) Having unsuccessfully attempted to establish or reestablish a relationship of couple or intimacy with the victim;

b) Maintaining at the time the act is perpetrated, or having maintained with the victim, family, conjugal, cohabitation, intimacy or dating relationships, friendship, companionship, labor relationship, educational or guardianship;
c) As a result of repeated manifestation of violence against the victim;
d) As a result of group rituals, gangs, using or not using weapons of any type;

e) By contempt for the victim's body to satisfy sexual instincts, or the commission of acts of genital mutilation or any other type of mutilation;
f) By misogyny;

g) When the act is committed in the presence of the victim's daughters or sons;
h) When any of the aggravating circumstances contemplated in the offense of murder in the Penal Code concur.
When the act occurs in the public sphere the penalty shall be fifteen to twenty years of imprisonment. If it occurs in the private sphere the penalty shall be twenty to twenty-five years of imprisonment. In both cases if two or more of the circumstances mentioned in the preceding subsections concur, the maximum penalty shall be applied.
The penalties established in the preceding paragraph shall be increased by one third when any of the circumstances of murder concur, up to a maximum of thirty years of imprisonment.

#### Art. 10. Physical violence

If as a consequence of physical violence exercised by the man within the framework of unequal power relations between men and women, he causes the woman any of the physical injuries typified in this Law, the following penalty shall be applied:

a) If minor injuries are caused, he shall be sanctioned with a penalty of eight months to one year and four months of imprisonment;

b) If serious injuries are caused, he shall be sanctioned with a penalty of two years and eight months to six years and eight months of imprisonment;
c) If very serious injuries are caused, he shall be sanctioned with a penalty of seven years and six months to thirteen years and four months of imprisonment.

#### Art. 11. Psychological violence

Whoever through action or omission with the purpose of denigrating, controlling the actions, behaviors and beliefs of the woman who has been or is his spouse, ex-spouse, cohabitant in stable de facto union, ex-cohabitant in stable de facto union, boyfriend, ex-boyfriend, ascendant, descendant, collateral relatives by consanguinity, affinity and any other interpersonal relationship; exercises direct or indirect threat, intimidation, manipulation, humiliation, isolation, offenses, surveillance, destructive comparisons, blackmail, harassment, stalking and any other analogous circumstance that results in harm to psychological health, through devaluation of her self-esteem or personal development, shall be sanctioned as follows:
a) If damage is caused to her psychological integrity requiring psychotherapeutic treatment, he shall be sanctioned with a penalty of eight months to one year and four months of imprisonment;

b) If dysfunction is caused in any of the areas of personal, labor, school, family or social functioning requiring specialized mental health treatment, he shall be sanctioned with a penalty of two years and eight months to six years and eight months of imprisonment;
c) If a mental illness is caused such that even with specialized intervention the person cannot recover her mental health permanently, he shall be sanctioned with a penalty of seven years and six months to thirteen years and four months of imprisonment.

#### Art. 12. Patrimonial and economic violence

Patrimonial and economic violence is the action or omission exercised by a man against a woman with whom he is or has been linked by a relationship of consanguinity, affinity, spouses, ex-spouses, cohabitants in a stable de facto union, ex-cohabitants in a stable de facto union, girlfriends, ex-girlfriends, affective relationship, and which results in any of the following conducts:
a)	Patrimonial misappropriation: Whoever misappropriates any property or asset from the possession or patrimony of a woman or misappropriates property,

regardless of its ownership, shall be punished with a penalty of two to five years of imprisonment. All of this provided that the value of the property or properties misappropriated is greater than the sum resulting from one monthly minimum wage of the industrial sector.
b)	Patrimonial damage: Whoever destroys, renders useless, causes to disappear or deteriorates in any way a property or properties regardless of possession, ownership or holding, shall be punished with a penalty of two to five years of imprisonment. All of this provided that the value of the property or properties is greater than the sum resulting from one monthly minimum wage of the industrial sector.
c)	Limitation on the exercise of the right to property: Whoever prevents, limits or prohibits the use, enjoyment, administration, transformation or disposition of one or more properties that form part of the family patrimony or the patrimony of the woman, shall be punished with a penalty of one to three years of imprisonment.
d)	Misappropriation of profits from family economic activities: Whoever misappropriates the profits derived from a family economic activity or disposes of them for his exclusive personal benefit and to the detriment of the rights of the woman, shall be punished with a penalty of two to four years of imprisonment.
e)	Economic exploitation of the woman: Whoever through violence, threats, intimidation or any type of coercion, has himself maintained, totally or partially, shall be punished with a penalty of one to three years of imprisonment.
f)	Denial of the right to alimony and to work: Whoever refuses to provide the necessary resources in the home or obliges the woman to abandon or not initiate paid work, shall be punished with a penalty of one to three years of imprisonment.

#### Art. 13. Intimidation or threat against the woman

The man who through verbal expressions, writings, electronic messages or any other means intimidates or threatens a woman with whom he is or has been linked by a relationship of consanguinity, affinity, subjects to guardianship, spouses, ex-spouses, cohabitants in a stable de facto union, ex-cohabitants in a stable de facto union, boyfriends, ex-boyfriends, affective relationship; with causing her serious and probable harm of a physical, psychological, sexual, labor or patrimonial nature, shall be punished with imprisonment of six months to one year.
The penalty shall be six months to two years of imprisonment, when one of the following circumstances occurs:

a)	If the intimidation or threat is carried out at the domicile or residence of the woman, at the domicile of relatives, friends or any place where she has taken refuge;
b)	If the act is committed in the presence of the daughters or sons of the victim;
c)	If the perpetrator of the crime avails himself of his position as a public official or of his membership in the police or military corps;
d)	If the act is committed with sharp-edged weapons, blunt weapons, firearms or objects capable of causing harm to physical integrity or health.

#### Art. 14. Abduction of sons or daughters

When the father or another relative exercises or has exercised violence against the woman and as a means of continuing to exercise violence toward her, abducts her son or daughter from the power of her mother who is legally in charge of custody, from the guardian or person in charge of his or her upbringing and retains him or her without her consent, he shall be punished with a penalty of two to four years of imprisonment.

#### Art. 15. Labor violence

Whoever prevents or limits the exercise of the right to work of women, through the establishment of requirements referring to sex, age, physical appearance, marital status, condition of being a mother or not, subjection to laboratory tests, test for Human Immunodeficiency Virus (HIV/AIDS) or of another nature to rule out pregnancy, obstructs or conditions access, salary, promotion or stability in the employment of women, shall be punished with one hundred to three hundred days fine.
If it is an employment policy of a public or private institution, whoever exercises discrimination, the maximum penalty shall be imposed. All of this without prejudice to the co-responsibility established in article 125 of Law No. 641, "Penal Code."

#### Art. 16. Violence in the exercise of public function against the woman

Whoever in the exercise of public function, regardless of his position, in a willful manner, delays, obstructs, denies due attention or prevents the woman from accessing the right to timely response in the institution to which she goes, for the purposes of processing any procedure related to the rights guaranteed by this Law, shall be punished with a penalty of two hundred to five hundred days fine and special disqualification in the exercise of the position for a period of three to six months. Without prejudice to the corresponding administrative responsibilities.

If the previous acts are committed through negligence the penalty shall be one hundred to two hundred days fine and disqualification from the position for a maximum period of three months.
If as a result of the aforementioned conducts, the life and integrity of the woman are placed in concrete danger, the penalty shall be six months to one year of imprisonment and special disqualification to exercise the position for the same period.

#### Art. 17. Omission to report

Persons who according to criminal procedural legislation have an obligation to report crimes of public action, once they have knowledge that a woman, boy, girl or adolescent has been a victim of violence, must report the act to the National Police or the Public Prosecutor's Office within a period of forty-eight hours. Whoever incurs in this omission shall be punished with a penalty of two hundred to five hundred days fine.

#### Art. 18. Obligation to report act of sexual harassment

Any hierarchical authority in employment centers, education centers or of any other nature, who has knowledge of acts of sexual harassment carried out by persons who are under his or her responsibility or direction and does not report it to the National Police or the Public Prosecutor's Office, shall be punished with a penalty of fifty to one hundred days fine.

## TITLE III

ON MEASURES OF ATTENTION, PROTECTION, SANCTION, PRECAUTIONARY AND INTERIM MEASURES

### Chapter I

On measures of attention, protection and sanction

#### Art. 19. Care and prevention measures

The care and prevention measures to be established are the set of measures and actions to protect victims of violence, as part of the State's obligation to guarantee women their safety and the full exercise of their human rights. Such models must take into consideration:
a) Providing care services, legal advice and specialized and free psychological treatment to victims that repair the damage caused by violence.
b) Providing comprehensive, specialized and free re-educational services to the aggressor, to eradicate violent conduct, through an

education that eliminates stereotypes of male supremacy and the sexist patterns that generated their violence.

c) Ensuring that the care received by the victim and the aggressor is not provided by the same person and in the same place. Under no circumstances may care be provided by persons who have been sanctioned for exercising any type of violence.
d) Guaranteeing the separation and distancing of the aggressor from the victim.
e) Enabling and strengthening the installation and maintenance of shelters for victims and their daughters and sons, which will provide specialized and free psychological and legal support.

#### Art. 20. Measures for care of victims

The measures for care of victims are as follows:

a) Promoting the existence of public and private services that provide comprehensive, interdisciplinary care for women victims of violence;
b) Ensuring that public and private intake or referral services provide victims with a safe, dignified service, in an environment of privacy and trust, that takes into account the situation of physical and emotional vulnerability of the victims;
c) Providing comprehensive health services for women, particularly to treat illnesses caused by gender-based violence.
d) Detecting, documenting and providing information to the competent authority on the physical and psychological findings caused by violence in victims who come to public health and justice services for the sanction and recovery of damages.
e) Providing victims in health, investigation, advisory or accompaniment services with information on the consequences of the acts of violence experienced, and must refer her without delay to the health or justice service required.

#### Art. 21. Protection and sanction measures

For protection and sanction measures the following must be done:
a) Complying with the obligation to inform the victim of the scope of filing her complaint; it is the responsibility of the personnel who receive and investigate complaints of violence against women to take preventive measures and request protection measures in the shortest time possible, in accordance with the provisions of this Law;

b) Ensuring the execution of precautionary and interim measures ordered by the competent authorities, implementing controls for the aggressor, telephone reports from victims, mandatory attendance controls for professional treatment;
c) Guaranteeing that the national forensic system complies with the standards that provide the technical and scientific elements for comprehensive and interdisciplinary forensic examination of persons affected by gender-based violence;
d) Expanding access to justice through free legal, medical and psychological assistance for women in situations of violence;
e) Training personnel and officials who make up the justice system from a gender perspective;
f) Strengthening awareness and training programs with a gender perspective aimed at justice operators that ensure quality care and eliminate institutional violence against women;
g) Incorporating specific measures into citizen security policies to prevent, investigate, sanction and eradicate femicide, understood as the most extreme form of gender-based violence against women;
h) Promoting shelters, self-help groups and damage recovery aimed at protecting women in families and in the community; and

i) Adopting the necessary and effective measures to prevent, investigate, sanction and eradicate all forms of trafficking, trafficking of women, girls, adolescents for sexual and labor exploitation.

#### Art. 22. Program actions

Programs to prevent, address, sanction and eradicate violence against women shall contain the following actions:
a) Promoting and encouraging knowledge and exercise of women's human rights;
b) Influencing the transformation of sociocultural models of conduct of women and men, including the formulation of formal and non-formal education programs and actions at all educational and instructional levels, with the purpose of preventing, addressing, sanctioning and eradicating

stereotyped conduct that permits, encourages and tolerates violence against women;

c) Providing them with instruments that allow them to address and adjudicate with a gender perspective, in accordance with the provisions of this Law, educating, training and raising awareness on women's human rights for:
1. Operators of the justice system, including Judges, personnel of the Judicial Branch, prosecutors, police; and
2. Officials in charge of policies for prevention, care, sanction and eradication of violence against women.
d) Providing specialized and free services of care and protection to victims, through public or private authorities and institutions;
e) Promoting that the media not use the image of women as a commercial sexual object, nor encourage violence against women, to contribute to the eradication of all types of violence against women and strengthen respect for human rights and the dignity of women;
f) Designing care and training programs for victims that allow them to participate fully in all areas of life;
g) Guaranteeing research and the preparation of statistical diagnoses on causes, frequencies and consequences of violence against women, in order to define the measures to be implemented to prevent, address, sanction and eradicate all types of violence;
h) Publishing semi-annually general and statistical information on cases of violence against women; and

i) Promoting a culture of reporting violence against women within the framework of the effectiveness of institutions, to guarantee their safety and integrity.
State institutions within the framework of their competence must comply with the measures established in this Law, without detriment to the participation of civil society in complying with them.

### Capítulo II

Nature and actions of precautionary and interim measures

#### Art. 23. Preventive nature

Precautionary and interim measures are preventive in nature, to protect the victim woman assaulted in her physical, psychological, sexual and patrimonial integrity, as well as from any action that violates or threatens the rights contemplated in this Law, thus avoiding new acts of violence.

#### Art. 24. Precautionary Measures

When in the presence of actions or omissions that may constitute crimes referred to in this Law, the National Police through the Women's and Children's Office, the heads of district and municipal delegations or the Public Prosecutor's Office, may order and adopt the following precautionary measures:
a) Order the immediate abandonment of the home by the alleged aggressor, regardless of ownership, insofar as the violence is a risk to the physical, psychological, sexual integrity and property of the woman. The aggressor may not remove household goods or household items. He shall only be authorized to take his personal belongings, instruments, work and study tools;
b) Prohibit or restrict the presence of the alleged aggressor at the dwelling, workplace, place of study, places habitually frequented by the woman or any place where she may be, within a minimum radius of two hundred meters. When the alleged aggressor and the victim work or study at the same center, this measure shall be ordered adapting it to guarantee the integrity of the woman;
c) Order the return of the woman to the domicile where she is prevented from entering or from which she was expelled with violence, intimidation or any means of coercion, regardless of the ownership of the real property. In the same resolution the departure of the alleged aggressor shall be ordered;
d) Guarantee the victim the necessary medical, psychological and psychiatric care;
e) Order the medical, psychological and social examination of children and adolescents who are direct and indirect victims in acts of violence and provide them with due care;
f) Request the intervention of the Ministry of the Family, Adolescence and Children in case of a complaint of violation of the rights of children. Like

wise, the collaboration of specialized organizations that provide support, protection, advice, counseling and necessary follow-up may be requested;

g) Prohibit the alleged aggressor from carrying out acts of intimidation, persecution, harassment or disturbance against the woman, any member of the family group or persons related to the complainant, whether by himself or through third parties, by any electronic, written and audiovisual means;
h) Seize and immediately retain firearms or sharp and blunt weapons that are in the hands of the alleged aggressor, regardless of whether or not he carries a permit; and of his profession or occupation. In all cases the retained weapons must be remitted to the National Police and their destination shall be determined in accordance with the provisions of Law No. 510, "Special Law for the Control and Regulation of Firearms, Ammunition, Explosives and other Related Materials", Law No. 228, "Law of the National Police", Law No. 406, "Code of Criminal Procedure of the Republic of Nicaragua" and Law No. 641, "Penal Code";
i) Prohibit the alleged aggressor from introducing or keeping weapons in the dwelling to intimidate, threaten or cause harm to any of the members of the family group.
j) Make an inventory of the movable property existing in the family domicile, in order to safeguard the property of the woman and her children. This measure shall be executed when the measure of literal a) and c) of this article is applied; and
k) Order that the woman may take with her those goods that guarantee her well-being and that of the family group, when she decides, for security reasons, to leave the home she shares with the aggressor.
The foregoing measures may only be adopted observing criteria of proportionality, rationality, necessity and urgency.

#### Art. 25. Precautionary Measures

The Judge or Court at the request of the Public Prosecutor's Office or of the victim constituted as a private prosecutor, may decree the following precautionary measures:
a) Order the alleged aggressor to submit to the psychological or psychiatric care that the judge deems necessary;
b) Impose on the alleged aggressor the provision of sufficient guarantees as determined by the Judge to compensate for the possible damages caused to the woman;

c) Grant provisionally the guardianship of children, adolescents or persons with disabilities to whomever he or she considers suitable for such function, if it was entrusted to the alleged aggressor, in case they are involved at the time of the commission of any of the crimes contained in this Law;
d) Impose on the alleged aggressor the obligation to provide the woman victim of violence with the necessary support to guarantee her subsistence, in case she does not have economic means for it and there is a relationship of dependence with the alleged aggressor. The application of this measure shall be of a provisional nature according to the time fixed for its validity in this Law;
e) Impose on the alleged aggressor the obligation to provide the sons and daughters with provisional support that guarantees their subsistence, until the competent authority dictates the manner of assessing the support in harmony with what is established in the Law on the matter;
f) Suspend the alleged aggressor's right to visit his sons and daughters and interfere in the exercise of guardianship, care, upbringing and education, when they have been victims of violence or when they are under the guardianship of the mother who has been a victim of violence, whether they are in her home, shelter or in any other place that provides them with security;
g) Issue a judicial order of protection and assistance directed to the police authority. The victim shall carry a copy of this order so that she may go to the nearest authority in case of threat of aggression outside or inside her domicile;
h) Guarantee the exercise of legal actions in matters of support by prohibiting the aggressor from entering into acts and contracts on movable and immovable property, as well as the displacement of movable property from the common residence to any other place. The judge shall make an inventory of such property, both at the time of issuing these measures and upon suspending them;
i) Prohibit the aggressor from approaching the protected person in any place where she may be, as well as approaching her domicile, her workplace or any other that is frequented by her. The judge shall set a minimum distance between the aggressor and the victim that may not be exceeded under warning of incurring criminal liability. The restraining measure may be agreed upon regardless of whether the affected person or those whom it is intended to protect had previously left the place;

j) Prohibit the aggressor from all kinds of communication with the persons indicated, under warning of incurring criminal liability;
k) Disqualify the aggressor person from carrying weapons;
l) Suspend the investigated person in the performance of his public office, when the act for which he is being investigated has to do with the functions he performs; and
m) Order the migratory retention of the alleged aggressor.

## TITLE IV

PROCEDURE AND APPLICATION OF PRECAUTIONARY AND PRECAUTIONARY MEASURES

### Chapter I

On the Duration of Precautionary Measures

#### Art. 26. Duration of precautionary measures

Precautionary measures shall be applied at the request of the victim or offended party or by any person or institution acting on her behalf, preventively for a maximum period of twenty days, which may be extended only once. The resolution ordering the measures or their extension must be issued in a reasoned manner.
Once the corresponding proceeding has been initiated, whether in criminal or family proceedings, at the request of a party the Judge shall rule on the maintenance of all or some of the precautionary measures applied, according to the nature of the proceeding that is the subject of his or her jurisdiction.
In his or her resolution, the Judge, upon ratifying the precautionary measures and ordering the protective measures, shall do so with due reasoning, justifying that they are proportionate and necessary, establishing the duration, which may not exceed one year.
The Judge must examine the need to maintain the measures every three months and when the grounds for his or her resolution cease or are substantially modified, shall replace them with less onerous ones. At any time, extraordinary review of measures may proceed, at the request of a party.

### Chapter II

On the application, implementation and jurisdiction of precautionary measures

#### Art. 27. On the application for precautionary measures

In the same act of filing the complaint, the victim or offended party, any person or institution acting on her behalf, may request orally or in writing the application of precautionary measures before the competent authority; in both cases the authority receiving it shall prepare a record that must contain:
a)	Names, surnames and domicile of the victim or offended party;
b)	Identification data of the alleged aggressor, and domicile if known;

c)	Account of the facts reported and indication of the elements of evidence supporting it;
d)	Description of the applicable precautionary measures; and

e)	Place to receive notifications.

#### Art. 28. Application of precautionary measures

Once the application is submitted, the competent authority shall immediately order the application of any of the requested measures. Notwithstanding, without prejudice to what is requested by the party, the competent authority may order ex officio the application of other measures based on the protection of the physical, psychological, sexual and patrimonial integrity of the victim.
The resolution ordering the application of a precautionary measure must be notified and executed within twenty-four hours of being issued and no appeal shall lie against it.
The resolution shall be notified to the defendant or accused, personally through the Women's and Children's Police Station or the Judicial Assistance Directorate of the National Police or the Public Prosecutor's Office. Notification may be made at the domicile or at any place where the alleged aggressor is found and at any time for the crimes established in this Law.

#### Art. 29

Competent body for the execution and monitoring of precautionary and protective measures
During the time of execution of precautionary and protective measures, the authority issuing them must monitor them.
For the execution and compliance with protective measures issued by the judicial authority, it shall be assisted by the Women's and Children's Police Station or the Judicial Assistance Directorate of the National Police.

In case of non-compliance with any of the measures established in this Law by the alleged aggressor, an investigation shall be opened for the crime of disobedience or contempt of authority.

## TITLE V

COMPETENT BODIES IN MATTERS OF VIOLENCE AGAINST WOMEN

### Chapter I

On the creation and jurisdiction of Specialized Bodies

#### Art. 30. Specialized bodies

The Specialized District Courts on Violence are hereby created, composed of a Judge specialized in the matter. There must be at least one Specialized District Court on Violence in each departmental capital and Autonomous Regions and in municipalities where, due to their location, access to the Courts located in the departmental capitals is difficult.
Attached to the Specialized District Courts on Violence, interdisciplinary teams shall be created composed of at least one psychologist and one social worker, responsible for providing specialized assistance to victims, in support of the jurisdictional function in hearings; and to provide monitoring and control of the protection measures imposed by the court.

#### Art. 31. Competent jurisdictional bodies

The following jurisdictional bodies shall be competent to hear and resolve:
a)	It corresponds to the Single Local Courts to hear in first instance up to the order of remission to trial of the crimes indicated in this Law whose penalty to be imposed is less serious. Once the order of remission to trial is issued, the proceedings must be remitted to the Specialized District Court on Violence of the corresponding territorial jurisdiction.
b)	It corresponds to the Local Criminal Courts of the municipalities to hear in first instance up to the order of remission to trial of the crimes indicated in this Law whose penalty to be imposed is less serious. Once the order of remission to trial is issued, the proceedings must be remitted to the Specialized District Court on Violence of the corresponding territorial jurisdiction.
c)	The Specialized District Judges on Violence shall hear and resolve in first instance the crimes indicated in this Law, whose penalty to be imposed is less serious and serious. In the case of less serious and serious crimes committed in the territory of their jurisdiction, said

Judges shall hear from the preliminary and initial hearing up to the oral and public trial hearing.

d)	The Specialized Criminal Chamber of the Courts of Appeals shall be competent to hear Appeals regarding interlocutory orders and dismissal judgments, which based on the grounds contemplated in article 155 of Law No. 406, "Code of Criminal Procedure of the Republic of Nicaragua", have been issued by Single Local Judges and Local Criminal Judges in cases for less serious crimes.
The Specialized Criminal Chambers of the Courts of Appeals shall also be competent to hear resolutions issued by Specialized District Judges on Violence in cases for less serious and serious crimes.
e)	The Criminal Chamber of the Supreme Court of Justice shall be competent to hear in Cassation the judgments for serious crimes heard and resolved on appeal by the Specialized Criminal Chambers of the Courts of Appeals.

#### Art. 33. Specialization of officials

All institutions that make up the criminal justice system must guarantee that the personnel who handle the investigation and processing of proceedings relating to violence against women are specially trained in the matter through initial, continuing and specialized training programs that they will promote institutionally and inter-institutionally.
For this purpose, the Public Prosecutor's Office shall have prosecutors specialized in the matter and the Supreme Court of Justice shall appoint specialized Judges and Magistrates in Violence, in accordance with Law No. 501, "Judicial Career Law", and shall provide that in each Criminal Chamber of the Court of Appeals, at least one Magistrate must be a specialist in the matter.
There is hereby created in the Court of Appeals of the Department of Managua, the Specialized Criminal Chamber in Violence and Adolescent Criminal Justice, which shall hear on appeal, the resolutions issued by the Specialized District Courts in Violence, and the District Criminal Courts for Adolescents. In the rest of the country's jurisdictions this Specialized Criminal Chamber shall be created according to demand and capacity of the Judicial Branch.
In the Autonomous Regions of the Atlantic Coast, it shall be ensured that the specialized personnel appointed by the Supreme Court of Justice are natives of the region.

### Chapter II

On inhibition or recusal

#### Art. 34. Grounds for inhibition or recusal

The grounds for inhibition and recusal for judicial authorities in charge of specialized criminal justice in violence against women, as well as the procedures and deadlines shall be those established in Law No. 406, "Code of Criminal Procedure of the Republic of Nicaragua."
When recusals or excuses are declared with place, the Court of Appeals shall remit the case to the respective Substitute Judge, so that he or she may continue its processing until final resolution.

If the Substitute Judge inhibits himself or herself or is recused, the case shall be remitted to the Court of Appeals, which shall resolve by assigning the case to another Specialized Court in Violence Against Women, located in the seat closest to that of the court.
If the person who inhibits himself or herself or is recused is a member of a collegiate court, the other members of said court shall resolve. If all members inhibit themselves or are recused, another chamber of the same hierarchy shall hear the case.

#### Art. 35. Opportunity to recuse

Recusal shall be filed at any time during the proceedings, verbally or in writing before the Judge of the case, Magistrate of the Criminal Chambers of the Courts of Appeal and of the Supreme Court of Justice, and must offer the evidence that supports it.

#### Art. 36. Effects of the recusal incident

The recused Judge does not lose his or her jurisdiction until the recusal or inhibition incident is resolved.

### Chapter III

On the Women's and Children's Police Station

#### Art. 37. Strengthening of the Women's and Children's Police Station

The Women's and Children's Police Station Directorate depends hierarchically on the General Director of the National Police. The Women's and Children's Police Stations of the National Police that exist in departmental, district and municipal delegations, shall depend functionally on the Women's and Children's Police Station Directorate.
The Women's and Children's Police Station Directorate of the National Police is a specialty in charge of the investigation, prevention and treatment of criminal offenses referred to in this Law. The Chief of the Municipal Delegations of the National Police shall conduct investigations of criminal offenses, as long as new Women's and Children's Police Stations are not established in said municipalities. The preventive work and specialized treatment of victims of violence shall be carried out in coordination with State institutions applying approved action protocols.
The Women's and Children's Police Station Directorate of the National Police is the body empowered to decide on the entry, permanence, transfer and departure of human resources that will work in the specialty. Likewise, it shall decide on the use and management of material and technical resources intended for comprehensive care of crime victims.
The State must guarantee sufficient resources for the operation of the Police Station and specialized training on the issue of violence against women.
The Women's and Children's Police Station Directorate must guarantee the necessary technical resources and the permanence of its personnel twenty-four hours every day of the week, avoiding that they be assigned to other activities.
The specialties of Judicial Assistance, Detectives, Intelligence and Public Security of the National Police shall support and prioritize the Women's and Children's Police Stations in the clarification of crimes linked to violence against women and children.
For the comprehensive operation of the Women's and Children's Police Station, the State shall allocate the necessary resources in the Budget of the National Police.

#### Art. 38

Strengthening of the Specialized Unit for Crimes Against Gender Violence
The Public Prosecutor's Office, as representative of society and of the victim of the crime in criminal proceedings, shall exercise criminal prosecution with a gender perspective. For this purpose, the Specialized Unit for Crimes against Gender Violence which is under the hierarchical dependence of the Attorney General of the Republic, shall be

the body in charge of the investigation and prosecution of crimes provided for and punished in this Law.

This Specialized Unit with national jurisdiction, for the fulfillment of its purposes and powers shall provide advice, legal technical assistance, accompaniment and monitoring to the Departmental, Regional and Municipal offices of the Public Prosecutor's Office and shall have the specialized personnel required in each Department, Region or Municipality of the national territory.
For the purposes of prevention, care, protection, investigation and punishment of the crimes contained in this Law, the Specialized Unit for Crimes against Gender Violence of the Public Prosecutor's Office shall coordinate with related institutions.
For the comprehensive operation of the Specialized Unit, the State shall allocate the necessary resources in the Budget of the Public Prosecutor's Office.

## TITLE VI

ON CRIMINAL PROCEDURE IN CRIMES REGULATED IN THIS LAW

### Chapter I

On the regime in the procedure

#### Art. 39. Regime in the procedure

The prosecution of the crimes established in this Law shall be governed by the principles, procedural institutes and the procedure established in Law No. 406, "Code of Criminal Procedure of the Republic of Nicaragua" in the forms and time periods indicated for serious and less serious crimes as applicable, provided that they do not contradict the provisions of this Law.

#### Art. 40. Exercise of criminal action

The Public Prosecutor's Office must exercise criminal action in all crimes indicated in this Law.
The victim may exercise private prosecution in accordance with the provisions of article 78 of Law No. 406, "Code of Criminal Procedure of the Republic of Nicaragua" and article 564 of Law No. 641, "Criminal Code". In the latter case, the Public Prosecutor's Office must assist the victim during all stages of the process.

#### Art. 41. Minor victim

When the victim is a minor or disabled, the facts may be reported by their legal representatives, by the victim or by welfare, social and educational institutions or any authority or person who has knowledge of the facts.

#### Art. 42. Accompaniment of victims in the process

During appearances in the process, the victim may be accompanied by a psychologist, psychiatrist or any person, for the purpose of assisting them in the event of a possible crisis resulting from their state of emotional vulnerability.

#### Art. 43. Data protection and limitations on publicity

In the proceedings and procedures related to this Law, the privacy of victims shall be protected; with regard to their personal data, those of their descendants and those of any other person who is under their guardianship. The media, to protect the identity of victims in sexual crimes and other aspects that may expose them to re-victimization, must act in accordance with the highest standards of professional journalistic ethics.

#### Art. 44. Jurisdictional advance of evidence

The Prosecutor or the private prosecuting attorney may request the advance of personal evidence, in the crimes indicated in this Law, when:
a)	The victim or witness is in danger of being exposed to pressure through violence, threat, offer or promise of money or other similar benefits;

b)	Due to reasons of rescheduling, suspension or interruption of the oral and public trial, the victim is unable to appear or prolong their stay at the seat of the court to attend the new trial summons, when the victim's domicile is far from the seat of the court, there is little access to means of transport because they are limited and because they do not have sufficient economic resources to guarantee their stay and food.
This provision must be applied following the procedure established in article 202 of Law No. 406 "Code of Criminal Procedure of the Republic of Nicaragua" and without prejudice to the assumptions indicated in the same article.

#### Art. 45. Bodily investigation

Bodily investigation and extraction of biological fluids must be carried out immediately in crimes against life and in crimes against the freedom and sexual integrity of the victim, only in those cases where it is pertinent due to the finding of evidence that can be analyzed and compared with biological fluids of the person under investigation. The authorization of said investigative act must be ordered by the judicial authority following criteria of proportionality, provided that it does not endanger the health of the person

under investigation and when it is indispensable to identify the alleged person responsible for the act.

#### Art. 46. Prohibition of mediation

Mediation shall not proceed in the crimes indicated in this Law.

#### Art. 47. Right to exercise civil action

The victim of the crimes indicated in this Law who decides to exercise civil action in criminal proceedings in accordance with the provisions of Law No. 406, "Code of Criminal Procedure of the Republic of Nicaragua", may do so directly, through a private attorney or request from the Public Prosecutor's Office advice or legal representation for the exercise of their right to restitution, reparation of damage and compensation for losses.

### Capítulo II

De las diligencias policiales y de la ejecución de pena

#### Art. 48. Police report

The Women's and Children's Police Stations at the Departmental, District or Municipal Delegation level shall prepare the investigative file, which shall be signed by the Head of the Women's and Children's Police Station, for subsequent referral to the corresponding authorities. In municipalities where there are no Police Stations, the Report shall be signed by the Police Chief.

#### Art. 49. Detention order

The Heads of the Women's and Children's Police Station or, as the case may be, the Police Chief, under their personal responsibility, may issue a detention order, with expression of the reasons that make it indispensable, against those for whom there is founded probability of the commission of a crime punished in this Law that carries a custodial sentence, within twelve hours of becoming aware of the act. However, these cases shall not be considered as cases of actual and immediate pursuit of an offender for the purpose of search of domicile.
In other cases, a judicial warrant shall be required to proceed with the detention.
When the detention of a person occurs, police officials must inform the Public Prosecutor's Office within a period not exceeding twelve hours of the proceedings carried out and present the accused before the competent judge within the constitutional period.

#### Art. 50. Execution of Sentence

Those found guilty of crimes of violence against women, girls, boys and adolescents, must participate mandatorily in programs

of orientation, care and prevention aimed at modifying their violent conduct and avoiding recidivism. The conviction shall establish the modality and duration, according to the limits of the sentence imposed. The National Penitentiary System must have adequate conditions for the development of the treatment and orientation programs provided for in this Law.

## TÍTULO VII

POLÍTICAS DE PREVENCIÓN, ATENCIÓN Y PROTECCIÓN A LA MUJER

### Capítulo I

De los mecanismo para la implementación de las medidas de prevención, atención y protección a la mujer

#### Art. 51

Creation of the National Interinstitutional Commission to Combat Violence Against Women
The National Interinstitutional Commission to Combat Violence Against Women is hereby created, which shall be composed of the heads of the following institutions: Supreme Court of Justice, Commission on Women's Affairs, Youth, Children and Family of the National Assembly, Special Women's Procurator of the Procurator for the Defense of Human Rights, Directorate of Women's and Children's Commissary of the National Police, Directorate of Judicial Assistance of the National Police, Public Prosecutor's Office, Public Defender's Office, Institute of Legal Medicine, Ministry of Education, Ministry of Health, Ministry of the Family, Adolescence and Children, Ministry of Labor, Nicaraguan Women's Institute, and National Penitentiary System.
The Commission shall elect annually from its structure a male or female coordinator and a male or female secretary and shall meet quarterly in ordinary session and extraordinarily when it so considers.
When it deems necessary, the commission may invite to participate in its sessions with voice but without vote, representatives of civil society organizations or other public or private institutions that work in defense against violence toward women.
At the departmental and municipal level, interinstitutional coordination commissions shall be organized and shall function, composed of representatives of the institutions that make up the National Interinstitutional Commission to Combat Violence Against Women and the municipal mayoralties. These commissions shall elect a coordinator and a secretary, shall meet once a month and extraordinarily when they so determine.

#### Art. 52

Functions of the National Interinstitutional Commission to Combat Violence Against Women

1.	Coordination:
a)	Promote and adopt measures for budgetary allocation for programs of prevention, attention and punishment of violence toward women in institutional budgets;
b)	Manage the creation of the special State fund to repair damages to victims of violence, in recovery services and restitution of rights;
c)	Create, guide, promote and execute interinstitutional plans to implement the measures of the policies to combat violence toward women.
2.	Monitoring and evaluation:

a)	Create the observatory on violence toward women, attached to the National Interinstitutional Commission to Combat Violence Against Women, which shall be responsible for advising, evaluating, institutional collaboration, preparing reports, studies and proposals for action on violence toward women, with the participation of municipal bodies and women's organizations;
b)	Design the statistical information system to monitor and follow up on the behavior of violence toward women;

c)	Propose complementary measures that are required to improve the system of prevention, attention, investigation, prosecution, punishment, reeducation, control and eradication of violence toward women.

#### Art. 53. Participation of non-governmental institutions

The Commission shall meet at least once every six months with organizations that work on issues of violence against women, in order to hear the suggestions, proposals or recommendations they present, with the purpose of strengthening its work.
The Commission must provide information to the organizations on the plans to implement the policies to combat violence toward women and the statistical monitoring and evaluation reports.

### Capítulo II

On the elaboration and objective

#### Art. 54. Elaboration of the policy

The Institutional Commission must prepare within a period of one hundred eighty days after the entry into force of this Law, the policy of prevention, attention and protection for women victims of violence.

#### Art. 55. Objective

The objective of this policy is to guarantee measures to prevent, attend to, protect, guide, train and provide due follow-up to women victims of violence.

### Capítulo III

Female or Male technical judge and computation of the statute of limitations period for the exercise of criminal action

#### Art. 56. Female or Male technical judge

Trials for the crimes referred to in this Law shall be conducted with a female or male technical judge.

#### Art. 57. Computation of the period

In the event that criminal action is not exercised in a timely manner in crimes of violence toward women, the statute of limitations period for criminal action shall begin from the day on which cohabitation, marital relationship, stable de facto union, courtship or any other interpersonal relationship between the victim and the aggressor ceases.

"Art. 155	Domestic or intrafamily violence
Whoever exercises any type of force, violence or physical or psychological intimidation, to the detriment of whoever has been their spouse or cohabitant in a stable de facto union or against the person to whom they are or have been stably linked by an affective relationship, children, adolescents, older adults, persons with disabilities, on the daughters and

sons of the spouse, cohabitant or on ascendants, descendants, collateral relatives by consanguinity, affinity, adoption, or subjects to guardianship. In the case of children and adolescents, the right of disciplinary correction may not be alleged.
Those responsible for this crime shall be imposed the following penalties:
a)	Minor injuries, the penalty shall be one to two years of prison;
b)	Serious injuries, the penalty shall be three to seven years of prison;
c)	Very serious injuries, the penalty shall be five to twelve years of prison.

In addition to the prison sentences previously indicated, those responsible for intrafamily violence shall be imposed special disqualification for the same period of the rights derived from the relationship between mother, father and children, or with the person subject to guardianship."
e)	Article 162 of Law No. 641, "Criminal Code", is reformed, which shall read as follows:
"Art. 162	Provocation, conspiracy and proposal
Provocation, conspiracy and proposal to commit the crimes of homicide, femicide, parricide, murder, genetic manipulation and cell cloning, genetic manipulation for production of biological weapons, minor injuries, serious injuries and very serious injuries, provided for in the previous chapters, shall be punished with a penalty whose maximum limit shall be the lower limit of the penalty provided in the Law, for the crime in question and whose minimum limit shall be half of the former."
f)	Article 182 of Law No. 641, "Criminal Code", is reformed, which shall read as follows:
"Art. 182	Trafficking in persons
Commits the crime of trafficking in persons, whoever finances, directs, organizes, promotes, facilitates, induces or by any means executes the proposal, capture, recruitment, contracting, transport, transfer, retention, reception or harboring of

persons, with the purpose of subjecting them to: sexual exploitation, servile marriage, forced marriage or simulated marriage, prostitution, labor exploitation, forced labor, slavery or practices analogous to slavery, servitude, trafficking or extraction of organs, or illegal adoption, so that it may be exercised within or outside the national territory, even with the consent of the victim shall be punished with a prison sentence of seven to ten years.
A prison sentence of ten to twelve years shall be imposed when:
1.	When the author commits the crime taking advantage of their position of power or using a situation of vulnerability of the victim, by means of threats, intimidation, use of force or other forms of coercion;
2.	When the act is carried out by means of kidnapping, deception, blackmail or threat, offer of gifts or any type of good or pecuniary value, to obtain the consent of a person;
3.	When the author of the crime is an authority, public official or employee.
A prison sentence of twelve to fourteen years shall be imposed when:
1.	The victim is a person under eighteen years of age, or an incapacitated person or the act was committed by relatives within the fourth degree of consanguinity or second degree of affinity, guardian or person in charge of the education, guardianship, spiritual guidance or permanently shares the victim's home, or there is a relationship of trust.
2.	The author acquires, possesses, offers, sells, delivers, transfers or accepts a girl, boy or adolescent in which there is or is not, payment or reward for purposes of exploitation provided for in this article."
g)	Article 183 of Law No. 641, Criminal Code, is reformed, which shall read as follows:
"Art. 183	Common provisions
When the author of aggravated rape, aggravated statutory rape, sexual abuse, sexual exploitation, sexual acts with adolescents through payment and pornography, promotion of

tourism for purposes of sexual exploitation, aggravated procuring, pimping or trafficking in persons is the father, mother or legal guardian responsible for the care of the victim, the penalty of special disqualification shall also be imposed for the period established for the prison sentence of the rights derived from the mother, father and children relationship, or with the person subject to guardianship.
Provocation, conspiracy and proposal to commit the crimes of sexual exploitation, sexual acts with adolescents through payment and pornography, promotion of tourism for purposes of sexual exploitation, procuring, pimping or trafficking in persons or sexual exploitation, provided for in the previous chapters, shall be punished with an attenuated penalty whose maximum limit shall be the lower limit of the penalty provided in the Law for the crime in question and whose minimum limit shall be half of the former."

#### Art. 60. Incorporation

The additions and reforms approved in Title VIII of this Law must be incorporated into the text of each of the articles of Law No. 641, "Penal Code" to which they refer.

## TITLE IX

REPEALING, TRANSITIONAL AND FINAL PROVISIONS
Single Chapter
Repealing, transitional and final provisions

#### Art. 61. Repeals

The following provisions are repealed:

a)	The second paragraph of article 21 of Law No. 228, "Law of the National Police", published in La Gaceta, Official Gazette No. 162, of August 28, 1996; and
b)	Article 63 of Decree No. 26-96, Regulations of Law No. 228, Law of the National Police, published in La Gaceta, Official Gazette No. 32, of February 14, 1997.

#### Art. 62. Transitional Provisions

Crimes and misdemeanors committed prior to the entry into force of this Law shall be judged in accordance with Law No. 641, "Penal Code" in force, with the Courts maintaining their jurisdiction according to the rules of objective and functional jurisdiction established therein.

#### Art. 63. Appendix to the Penal Code

This Law shall be Appendix No. 1 to Law No. 641, "Penal Code". The appendix must be included in editions of the Penal Code prepared by publishing houses, printing presses or any other entity dedicated to the publication of legal texts, subject to prior authorization by the competent authority.

#### Art. 64. Supplementary Application

Matters not provided for in this Law shall be regulated by the provisions of Law No. 641, "Penal Code" and Law No. 406, "Code of Criminal Procedure of the Republic of Nicaragua."

#### Art. 65. Entry into Force

This Law shall enter into force one hundred twenty days after its publication in La Gaceta, Official Gazette.
