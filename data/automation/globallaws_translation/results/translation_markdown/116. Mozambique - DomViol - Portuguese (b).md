SUMMARY
Assembly of the Republic:
Law no. 24/2019:
Law Revising the Penal Code and revoking article 2 of Decree-Law no. 182/74, of 2 May and the Penal Code approved by Law no. 35/2014 of 31 December..

ASSEMBLY OF THE REPUBLIC

Law no. 24/2019
of 24 December
The Penal Code, approved by Law no. 35/2014, of 31 December, brought great innovations by introducing new legal types of crime, changes in wording and in penal frameworks and incorporation of matters that were contained in separate legislation. Above all, it adopted the decriminalisation movement and the preference for non-custodial sentences over imprisonment, placing Man at its maximum dimension.
However, substantive reasons, reflected in the limitation on the approach to its axiological values and the need for particular legal treatment, namely in terms of articulation between specific substantive and procedural norms, came to justify the systematic allocation of lapses and omissions by a legal vicissitude.
Precisely to conform the primary core of fundamental rights, freedoms and guarantees, the aim is to establish in the Penal Code a true repository of the essential values of contemporary Mozambican society.
In these terms, under number 1 of article 178 of the Constitution of the Republic, the Assembly of the Republic determines:

###### ARTICLE 1

(Approval)
The Penal Code is approved, in annex, which forms an integral part of this Law.

###### ARTICLE 2

(Revocation)
1.	The following are revoked:
a)	article 2 of Decree-Law no. 182/74, of 2 May;
b)	the Penal Code, approved by Law no. 35/2014, of 31 December.
2.	All other legislation that contradicts this Law is revoked.

###### ARTICLE 3

(Repristination)
The following are repristinated:
a)	articles 4, 16, 17, 30, 31, 32 and 33 of Law no. 9/87, of 19 September;
b)	article 38 of Law no. 17/87, of 21 December;
c)	articles 204, 205, 206 and 207 of Law no. 2/2006, of 22 March.

###### ARTICLE 4

(Imprisonment)
1.	The reference to major imprisonment contained in previous legislation is understood to correspond to imprisonment exceeding 2 years.
2.	Whenever criminal law refers to the comminatory measure of imprisonment without specifying the limits, it is understood to be imprisonment ranging from 3 days to 2 years.

###### ARTICLE 5

(Minimum wage)
For the purposes of this Law, minimum wage shall be understood as the wage in force in the Public Service.

###### ARTICLE 6

(Cross-references)
Cross-references contained in separate legislation to provisions of the previous version of the Penal Code are considered to be made to the corresponding provisions of the Penal Code, in the version approved by this Law.

###### ARTICLE 7

(Promotion of release)
All preventive detainees and convicts for acts that, by effect of this Law, cease to constitute a crime must immediately be restored to freedom.

###### ARTICLE 8

(Entry into force)
This Law enters into force 180 days after its publication.

PENAL CODE BOOK ONE GENERAL PART

### TITLE I

Guarantees and Application of Criminal Law
SOLE CHAPTER
General Provisions

###### ARTICLE 1

(Principle of legality)
1.	No act, whether consisting of action or omission, may be judged a crime unless a law, at the time of its commission, qualifies it as such.
2.	Criminal measures or penalties that are not provided for in law may not be applied.

###### ARTICLE 2

(Moment of commission of the act)
The act is considered committed at the moment when the agent acted or, in the case of omission, should have acted, regardless of the moment when the typical result was produced.

###### ARTICLE 3

(Application of criminal law in time)
1.	Criminal law has no retroactive effect, except for the particularities contained in the following numbers.
2.	An infraction punishable by law in force at the time it was committed ceases to be so if a new law eliminates it from the number of infractions.
3.	If there has already been a final conviction, the penalty is extinguished, whether or not its enforcement has begun.
4.	When the penalty established in the law in force at the time the infraction is committed is different from that established in subsequent laws, the penal framework that, concretely, proves most favourable to the perpetrator of the crime is always applied. If, however, there has been a conviction, even if final, the execution and its penal effects cease as soon as the part of the penalty that has been served reaches the maximum limit of the penalty provided for in the subsequent law, if this is favourable.
5.	The provisions of the law on the effects of the penalty have retroactive effect, in everything that is favourable to the perpetrator of the crime, even if the perpetrator is convicted by final judgment at the time of the promulgation of that law, except for the rights of third parties.

6.	Acts committed during the validity of a temporary law are judged by it, unless legally provided otherwise.

###### ARTICLE 4

(Principle of territoriality)
Except as otherwise provided in an international convention or agreement in the field of judicial cooperation, Mozambican criminal law is applicable to acts committed:
a)	in Mozambique, whatever the nationality of the agent; or
b)	on board a ship or aircraft registered in Mozambique.

###### ARTICLE 5

(Acts committed outside the national territory)
1. Unless otherwise provided by treaty, international convention or international judicial cooperation agreement, Mozambican criminal law is applicable to acts committed outside the national territory by a Mozambican when they constitute crimes against life, internal or external security of the State, violation of State secrets, counterfeiting of currency, banknotes and State securities, passing counterfeit currency, slavery, trafficking in persons, abduction, prostitution, sexual abuse and pornography of minors, trafficking in prohibited fauna and flora species products, damage to the environment and pollution, money laundering, corruption and related crimes, where the offender has not been tried in the country where the offence was committed or has evaded total or partial enforcement of the sentence imposed in that country.
2. Mozambican criminal law is also applicable to a foreigner who commits any of the crimes referred to in the preceding paragraph, provided that he is or appears in Mozambican territory, or his surrender can be obtained.
3. Mozambican criminal law is applicable to any other crime committed by a Mozambican in a foreign country, where the following requirements are met:
a) the offender being found in Mozambique;
b) the act also being classified as a crime by the legislation of the country where it was committed;
c) the offender not having been tried in the country where he committed the crime.
4. Mozambican criminal law also applies to crimes committed by or against a legal person or equivalent entity having its headquarters in national territory.
5. Where the crime committed outside the national territory is punishable by imprisonment not exceeding 2 years, the Public Prosecutor does not exercise criminal proceedings without a complaint from the injured party or official notification from the authority of the country where the said crime was committed.
6. If the offender, having been convicted in the place of the crime, has evaded enforcement of all or part of the sentence, new proceedings are instituted before the Mozambican courts which, if they find the crime proven, apply the corresponding penalty provided for in Mozambican legislation, deducting the time of sentence actually served.
7. Even if Mozambican law is applicable, under the terms of the preceding paragraphs, the act is judged according to the law of the country in which it was committed whenever that law is concretely more favourable to the offender. The applicable penalty is converted into that which corresponds to it in the Mozambican system, or, where there is no direct correspondence, into that which Mozambican law provides for the act.

###### ARTICLE 6

(Place of commission of the act)
The act is considered committed in the place where, totally or partially, and in any form of participation, the offender acted, or, in the case of omission, should have acted, as well as in the place where the typical result occurred.

###### ARTICLE 7

(Interpretation and integration of criminal law)
Extensive interpretation or recourse to analogy or induction by parity or a fortiori reasoning is not admissible to classify any act as a crime, define a state of dangerousness or determine the penalty or security measure corresponding thereto.

###### ARTICLE 8

(Reservation of civil legislation)
1. The provisions of civil laws which, by the commission or omission of certain acts, modify the exercise of certain civil rights or establish condemnations relating to private interests are not considered altered for the purposes of this Code without express derogation and only give rise to action in civil proceedings.
2. The age of majority established in the Civil Code produces all its effects in criminal law relations, when minority is the basis for determining the crime and, whenever the same law refers, in general, to majority or minority.

###### ARTICLE 9

(Subsidiary application of the Criminal Code)
Unless otherwise provided, the provisions of this Code apply subsidiarily to acts punishable under special legislation.

### TITLE II

Criminality and Agents of Crime

#### CHAPTER I

Prerequisites for Punishment

###### ARTICLE 10

(Commission by action and by omission)
1. Unless the intention of the law is otherwise, the crime provides for the punishment not only of action adequate to produce the typical result, but also of the omission of action adequate to prevent it.
2. Omission is only punishable when a legal or contractual legal duty rests upon the person who omits, personally obliging him to prevent that result.
3. In the case provided for in paragraph 2 of this article, the penalty may be specially mitigated.

###### ARTICLE 11

(Subjective imputation)
Only an act committed with intent or, in cases specially provided for by law, with negligence is punishable.

###### ARTICLE 12

(Intent)
1. A person acts with intent who, representing an act that fulfils a legal type of crime, acts with the intention of carrying it out.

2. A person also acts with intent who represents the commission of an act that fulfils a type of crime as a necessary consequence of his conduct.
3. When the commission of an act that fulfils a type of crime is represented as a possible consequence of the conduct, there is intent if the offender acts accepting its commission.

###### ARTICLE 13

(Negligence)
1. A person acts with negligence who, by not proceeding with the care to which, according to the circumstances, he is obliged and of which he is capable:
a) represents as possible the commission of an act that fulfils a type of crime, but acts without accepting that commission; or
b) does not even represent the possibility of commission of an act that fulfils a type of crime.
2. The punishment of negligence, in cases specially determined by law, is based on the voluntary omission of a duty.

###### ARTICLE 14

(Contravention)
1. A contravention is considered to be a punishable voluntary act that solely consists in the violation of or failure to observe the preventive provisions of laws and regulations, independently of any malicious intention.
2. In contraventions negligence is always punished.

#### CHAPTER II

Criminality

###### ARTICLE 15

(Forms of appearance of crime)
Not only the consummated crime is punishable, but also the attempt.

###### ARTICLE 16

(Consummated crime)
Whenever the law designates the penalty applicable to a crime without declaring whether it is a consummated crime or an attempt, it is understood to impose it on the consummated crime.

###### ARTICLE 17

(Attempt)
1. There is an attempt when the offender performs acts of execution of a crime he has decided to commit, without it being consummated.
2. Acts of execution are:
a) those that fulfil a constituent element of a type of crime;
b) those that are suitable to produce the typical result; and
c) those that, according to common experience and except for unforeseeable circumstances, are of a nature to lead to the expectation that acts indicated in the preceding subparagraphs will follow.

###### ARTICLE 18

(Punishment of attempt)
1. Unless otherwise provided, an attempt is only punishable if the respective consummated crime carries a penalty exceeding 2 years' imprisonment.

2. An attempt is punishable with the penalty applicable to the consummated crime, specially mitigated.
3. An attempt is not punishable when the unsuitability of the means employed by the offender or the non-existence of the object essential to the consummation of the crime is manifest.

###### ARTICLE 19

(Autonomous punishment of acts constituting attempt)
Even if the attempt is not punishable, the acts that constitute it are punishable if they are classified as crimes by law, or as contraventions by law or regulation.

###### ARTICLE 20

(Withdrawal)
1. An attempt ceases to be punishable when the agent voluntarily desists from continuing the execution of the crime, or prevents its consummation, or, notwithstanding consummation, prevents the occurrence of the result comprised in the legal type of crime.
2. When consummation or the occurrence of the result are prevented by a fact independent of the conduct of the person who desists, the attempt is not punishable if the person makes a serious effort to prevent one or the other.
3. If several agents participate in the commission of the act, the attempt of the one who voluntarily prevents the consummation or the occurrence of the result is not punishable, nor is that of the one who makes a serious effort to prevent one or the other, even if the other participants continue the execution of the crime or consummate it.

###### ARTICLE 21

(Irrelevance of suspension of execution in single-execution offences)
In special cases where the law qualifies as a consummated crime the attempt of a crime, the suspension of the execution of this crime by the will of the criminal is not a justifying cause.

###### ARTICLE 22

(Concept of preparatory acts)
1. Preparatory acts are external acts conducive to facilitating or preparing the execution of the crime that do not yet constitute the beginning of execution.
2. Preparatory acts are not punishable, but to the facts that enter into their constitution the provisions of article 19 are applicable.

#### CHAPTER III

Agents of Crime

###### ARTICLE 23

(Agents of crime)
The agents of crime are perpetrators and accomplices.

###### ARTICLE 24

(Perpetrator)
The following is punishable as a perpetrator:
a) one who executes the act, by himself or through another; or
b) one who takes direct part in its execution, by agreement or together with another or others; or
c) one who, intentionally, induces another person to commit the act, provided there is execution or beginning of execution.

###### ARTICLE 25

(Accomplice)
1. One who, intentionally and in any manner, provides material or moral assistance to the commission by another of an intentional act is punishable as an accomplice.
2. The penalty provided for the perpetrator, specially mitigated, is applicable to the accomplice.

###### ARTICLE 26

(Participation)
1. If the unlawfulness or the degree of unlawfulness of the act depends on certain special qualities or relations of the agent, it is sufficient, to make the respective penalty applicable to all participants, that those qualities or relations are verified in any of them, except if the intention of the incriminating norm is otherwise.
2. Each participant is punished according to his guilt, independently of the punishment or degree of guilt of the other participants.

###### ARTICLE 27

(Non-punishment in contraventions)
In contraventions, complicity is not punishable.

#### CHAPTER IV

Criminal Liability

###### ARTICLE 28

(Criminal liability)
Criminal liability consists in the obligation to repair the damage caused to the legal order of society, by serving the penalty or measure established in the law.

###### ARTICLE 29

(Personal character of liability)
Except as provided in the following article and in cases specially provided for in the law, only natural persons are susceptible to criminal liability.

###### ARTICLE 30

(Criminal liability of legal persons and equivalent entities)
1. Legal persons and equivalent entities, with the exception of the State, legal persons in the exercise of prerogatives of public power and international public law organizations, are liable for crimes provided for in this Code and other specific legislation, when committed:
a) in their name and in the collective interest by persons who occupy a management position in them; or
b) by one who acts under the authority of the persons referred to in the preceding paragraph by virtue of a violation of the duties of supervision or control incumbent upon them.
2. Included in the concept of legal persons in the exercise of prerogatives of public power are public business entities, entities holding public service concessions, regardless of their ownership, public institutes and others so defined by law.
3. For purposes of criminal liability, entities equivalent to legal persons are considered to be de facto associations and civil and commercial partnerships.

4. The liability of legal persons and equivalent entities does not exclude the individual liability of the respective agents nor does it depend on their being held liable.

###### ARTICLE 31

(Vicissitudes of legal persons and equivalent entities)
1. Division and merger do not determine the extinction of the criminal liability of the legal person or equivalent entity, with liability for the commission of the crime falling on:
a) the legal person or equivalent entity in which the merger was effected; and
b) the legal persons or equivalent entities that resulted from the division.
2. The liability of legal persons or equivalent entities is excluded when the agent has acted against express orders or instructions from the competent authority.

###### ARTICLE 32

(Acting on behalf of another)
1. One who acts voluntarily as holder of the organ of a legal person or equivalent entity, or in legal or voluntary representation of another, is punishable even when the respective type of crime requires:
a) certain personal elements and these are only verified in the person of the represented party; or
b) that the agent commit the act in his own interest and the representative acts in the interest of the represented party.
2. The ineffectiveness of the act that serves as the basis for the representation does not prevent the application of the provisions of the preceding number.

###### ARTICLE 33

(Extent of liability)
1. Without prejudice to the right of recourse, persons who occupy a management position are subsidiarily liable for payment of the fines and compensation to which the legal person or equivalent entity is sentenced, in relation to crimes:
a) committed during the period of exercise of their office, without their express opposition; or
b) committed previously, when the final decision to apply them was notified during the period of exercise of their office and the failure to pay is attributable to them.
2. It is understood that those who occupy a management position are the organs and representatives of the legal person or equivalent entity and whoever has authority in it to exercise control over its activity.
3. Where several persons are liable under the terms of the preceding numbers, their liability is joint and several.
4. If fines or compensation are applied to an entity without legal personality, the common patrimony is liable and, in its absence or insufficiency, the patrimony of each of the associates is jointly and severally liable.

###### ARTICLE 34

(Independence of Criminal liability in relation to Civil liability)
Exemption from criminal liability does not entail exemption from civil liability, when it takes place.

###### ARTICLE 35

(Error regarding circumstantial elements and consent of the victim)
1.	The following do not exempt from criminal liability:
a)	ignorance of criminal law;
b)	the illusion regarding the criminality of the act;
c)	error regarding the person or thing to which the punishable act is directed;
d)	personal conviction of the legitimacy of the purpose or motives that determined the act;
e)	consent of the victim, except in cases specified by law;
f)	the intention to commit a different crime, even if the planned crime was of lesser gravity;
g)	culpable error regarding the unlawfulness of the punishable act;
h)	error regarding the prerequisites of a cause of justification or exculpation;
i)	in general, any facts or circumstances, when the law does not expressly declare that they exempt from criminal liability.
2.	The circumstances designated in paragraphs a) and b) of number 1 of this article do not in any way contribute to mitigating criminal liability.
3.	Error regarding the person to whom the punishable act is directed aggravates or mitigates criminal liability, according to the circumstances.
4.	The circumstance designated in paragraph f) of number 1 cannot in any case exempt criminal intent, and consequently the crime cannot for that reason be classified as merely negligent.

###### ARTICLE 36

(Aggravating or mitigating circumstances)
Criminal liability is aggravated or mitigated when aggravating or mitigating circumstances concur in the crime or in the agent. This aggravation or mitigation is correlative to the aggravation or mitigation of the penalty.

###### ARTICLE 37

(Circumstances inherent to the agent)
Aggravating or mitigating circumstances inherent to the agent only aggravate or mitigate the liability of that agent.

###### ARTICLE 38

(Circumstances relating to the incriminated act)
Aggravating circumstances relating to the incriminated act only aggravate the liability of agents who had knowledge of them or who should have foreseen them, before the crime or during its execution.

###### ARTICLE 39

(Aggravation and mitigation for contraventions)
Criminal liability for contraventions is aggravated or mitigated according to the gravity of the act, the fault, the economic situation of the agent and the economic benefit derived from the practice of the contravention.

###### ARTICLE 40

(Enumeration of aggravating circumstances)
The following are solely aggravating circumstances when the crime has been committed:
1.	ª With premeditation;
2.	ª For a trivial motive;
3.	ª By means of reward, remuneration or its promise;
4.	ª To facilitate or ensure the execution, concealment, impunity or advantage of another crime;
5.	ª For reasons of racial, national, ethnic, ideological, religious, sexual discrimination, or discrimination based on illness or physical or mental disability;
6.	ª Against a minor, elderly person, pregnant woman or sick person;
7.	ª By means of convocation, pact or execution between two or more persons;
8.	ª With the aid of persons who could facilitate or ensure impunity;
9.	ª With waiting, ambush, disguise, surprise, betrayal, treachery, abuse of power, breach of trust or any fraud;
10.	ª With breaking in, scaling or false keys;
11.	ª By means of poison, instrument or weapon whose carrying and use is prohibited;
12.	ª On the occasion of fire, explosion, shipwreck, earthquake, flood, death, accident or breakdown of automobile, air and rail transport, any public calamity or particular misfortune of the victim;
13.	ª With the simultaneous use of various means or with insistence on consummating it, after the first efforts have failed;
14.	ª The agent entering or attempting to enter the victim's house;

15.	ª In the dwelling house of the agent, when there is no provocation by the victim;
16.	ª In places intended for religious worship, in cemeteries or in public offices;
17.	ª On a road or deserted place;
18.	ª At night, if the gravity of the crime does not increase by reason of scandal arising from publicity;
19.	ª By any means of publicity or so that its execution may be witnessed, in cases where the gravity of the crime increases with the scandal of publicity;
20.	ª With disregard for the status of public servant, in the exercise of their functions;
21.	ª With any acts of cruelty, spoliation or destruction, unnecessary for the consummation of the crime;
22.	ª With abuse of authority or the agent taking advantage of domestic relations, cohabitation or hospitality;
23.	ª With abuse of power or violation of duty inherent to a position, office or profession;
24.	ª The agent having a special obligation not to commit it, to prevent it from being committed or to contribute to its punishment;
25.	ª The agent having received benefits from the victim, when the latter has not provoked the offense that gave rise to the perpetration of the crime;
26.	ª Against ascendants, descendants, relatives up to the third degree of the collateral line, or in-laws, spouse or person in an analogous situation;
27.	ª With manifest superiority by reason of physical build, age or weapons;

28.	ª The victim being under the immediate protection of public authority;
29.	ª There being recidivism of crimes; or
30.	ª There being concurrence of crimes.

###### ARTICLE 41

(Premeditation)
Premeditation consists of the design, formed at least twenty-four hours before, to perform an act with criminal relevance, even if this design is dependent on some circumstance or some condition.

###### ARTICLE 42

(Recidivism)
1.	Recidivism occurs when the agent, having been convicted by final judgment for some crime, commits another of the same nature before eight years have passed since the conviction, even if the penalty for the first crime has been prescribed, pardoned or commuted.
2.	When the first crime has been amnestied, recidivism does not occur.
3.	If one of the crimes is intentional and the other negligent, there is no recidivism.
4.	The circumstance that the agent was the author of one of the crimes and accomplice of the other does not exclude recidivism.
5.	The crimes may be of the same nature, even if both or some of them have not been consummated.
6.	Convictions handed down by foreign courts count for recidivism under the terms of the preceding numbers, provided that the act constitutes a crime under Mozambican law.
7.	In contraventions, recidivism occurs when the agent, convicted for a contravention, commits an identical or different contravention before six months have elapsed, counted from said punishment.

###### ARTICLE 43

(Concurrence of crimes)
1.	There is concurrence of crimes when the agent commits more than one crime on the same occasion, or when, having perpetrated one, commits another before having been convicted for the previous one by final judgment.
2.	When the same act is provided for and punished in two or more legal provisions, as constituting different crimes, there is no concurrence of crimes.

###### ARTICLE 44

(Continued crime)
1.	A continued crime is constituted by the various conducts of the same agent that violate the same norm or different norms that protect the same legal interest or legal interests of identical nature which, by the conditions of time, place and manner of execution, the subsequent ones can be considered as mere continuation of the previous ones.
2.	Criminal continuation does not occur when legal interests inherent to the person are violated.

###### ARTICLE 45

(Mitigating circumstances)
The following are mitigating circumstances of the criminal liability of the agent:
1. Prior good conduct;

2. The provision of relevant services to society;
3. Being under eighteen or over sixty years of age;
4. The agent's conduct having been determined by an honourable motive, by strong temptation or solicitation by the victim himself or herself, or by unjust provocation or undeserved offence;
5. The intention to avoid a harm or to produce a lesser harm;
6. Imperfect knowledge of the wrongfulness of the crime;
7. Physical coercion, being resistible;
8. Lack of foresight or imperfect knowledge of the harmful results of the crime;
9. Spontaneous confession of the crime;
10. Acts demonstrating sincere repentance by the agent, namely, reparation, to the extent possible, of the damage caused;
11. The order or advice of his or her ascendant, adopter, guardian or educator, the agent being a minor and not emancipated;
12. Compliance with an order of the agent's hierarchical superior, when it is not sufficient for his or her justification;
13. The agent having committed the crime to vindicate himself or herself, his or her spouse, ascendant, descendant, siblings, uncles, nephews or relatives in the same degrees, adopter or adoptee from some insult, dishonour or offence, immediately after the affront;
14. Sudden passion aroused by some cause that excites just public indignation;
15. Resistible fear;
16. Resistance to the orders of his or her hierarchical superior, if obedience is not due and if compliance with the order would constitute a more serious crime;
17. Excess in legitimate defence;
18. Voluntary surrender to the authorities;
19. The reparable nature of the damage caused or its minor seriousness;
20. The discovery of the other agents, the instruments of the crime or the corpus delicti, the revelation being true and useful to the action of justice;
21. The agent having acted under reverential fear;
22. Those expressly qualified as such in the special cases provided for by law;
23. In general, any other circumstances that precede, accompany or follow the crime, if they weaken the culpability of the agent or diminish in any way the seriousness of the criminal act or its results.

###### ARTICLE 46

(Cessation of effect of aggravating circumstances)
1. The circumstances indicated as aggravating cease to be so when:
a) the law expressly considers them as a constituent element of the crime;
b) they are so inherent to the crime that, without them, the criminal act punished by law cannot be committed;
c) the law expressly declares, or the circumstances and special nature of the crime indicate, that they should not aggravate or should mitigate the criminal liability of the agents in whom they concur.
2. When any of the circumstances indicated in article 40 constitutes a crime, it shall not aggravate the criminal liability of the agent, except by reason of concurrence of crimes.

###### ARTICLE 47

(Exempting circumstances)
The following are exempting circumstances of criminal liability:
a) lack of imputability; and
b) justification of the act and exclusion of culpability.

###### ARTICLE 48

(Absolute non-imputability)
The following are not susceptible to imputation:
a) minors who have not completed 16 years of age; and
b) those who suffer from a psychic anomaly without lucid intervals.

###### ARTICLE 49

(Relative non-imputability)
1. The following are relatively non-imputable:
a) minors who, being over 16 years of age and under 21, have acted without discernment;
b) those who suffer from a psychic anomaly who, although they have lucid intervals, commit the act in that state; and
c) those who, for any other reason independent of their will, are accidentally deprived of the exercise of their intellectual faculties at the moment of committing the punishable act.
2. Negligence or fault is always considered as an act or omission dependent on the will.

###### ARTICLE 50

(Non-imputability by reason of psychic anomaly)
1. A person is non-imputable who, by reason of a psychic anomaly, is incapable, at the moment of committing the act, of assessing the unlawfulness thereof or of determining himself or herself in accordance with that assessment.
2. The regime set out in the preceding paragraph is applicable to cases of complete intoxication due to the consumption of alcoholic beverages, narcotics, psychotropic or toxic substances or others that produce similar effects.
3. Imputability is not excluded when the psychic anomaly or the situation described in the preceding paragraph has been provoked by the agent with the intention of committing the act or when the commission of the act has been foreseen or should have been foreseen by the agent.

#### CHAPTER V

Causes that Exclude Unlawfulness and Culpability

###### ARTICLE 51

(Exclusion of unlawfulness and culpability)
1. The following constitute causes of exclusion of unlawfulness, justifying the act:
a) state of necessity;
b) legitimate defence of oneself or others;
c) conflict of duties;
d) obedience legally due to his or her legitimate superiors, except if there is excess in the acts or in the form of execution;
e) legal authorisation in the exercise of a right or in the fulfilment of an obligation, if he or she has acted with due diligence, or the act is a merely casual result;
f) consent of the victim.

2. The following constitute causes of exclusion of culpability:
a) those who commit the act compelled by any external, physical and irresistible force;
b) those who commit the act dominated by insurmountable fear of an equal or greater harm, imminent or in the beginning of execution;
c) those who commit an act whose criminality derives solely from the special circumstances that concur in the victim or in the act, if they are unaware and have no obligation to know of the existence of those special circumstances; and
d) in general, those who have acted without criminal intent and without fault.

###### ARTICLE 52

(State of necessity)
Justification of the act may only be verified under the terms of subparagraph a) of paragraph 1 of the preceding article, when the following requirements concur:
a) reality of the harm;
b) impossibility of resorting to the public force;
c) impossibility of legitimate defence;
d) lack of any other means less harmful than the act committed; and
e) probability of the efficacy of the means employed.

###### ARTICLE 53

(Legitimate defence)
1. Justification of the act may only be verified, under the terms of subparagraph b) of paragraph 1 of article 51, when the following requirements concur:
a) unlawful aggression in execution or imminent, which is not motivated by provocation, offence or any current crime committed by the person defending;
b) impossibility of resorting to the public force;
c) rational necessity of the means employed to prevent or suspend the aggression.
2. Excess in legitimate defence due to excusable disturbance or fear of the agent is not punishable.

###### ARTICLE 54

(Conflict of duties)
1. The act of a person who, in case of conflict in the fulfilment of legal duties or legitimate orders of authority, satisfies a duty or order of equal or superior value to that of the duty or order sacrificed is not unlawful.
2. The duty to comply with superior orders ceases when they lead to the commission of a crime.

###### ARTICLE 55

(Excusable undue obedience)
A public servant who complies with an order without knowing that it leads to the commission of a crime acts without culpability, this not being evident in the framework of the circumstances represented by him or her.

###### ARTICLE 56

(Consent of the injured party)
1. In addition to cases specifically provided for by law, consent excludes the unlawfulness of the act when it refers to freely available legal interests and the act does not offend good morals.

2. Consent may be expressed by any means that reflects a serious, free and informed will of the holder of the legally protected interest and may be freely revoked until the execution of the act.
3. Consent is only effective if given by a person over 16 years of age who possesses the necessary discernment to assess its meaning and scope at the time it is given.
4. If consent is not known to the agent, the agent is punishable with the penalty applicable to an attempt.

###### ARTICLE 57

(Presumed consent)
1. Presumed consent is equivalent to actual consent.
2. There is presumed consent when the situation in which the agent acts reasonably allows one to suppose that the holder of the legally protected interest would have effectively consented to the act if he or she had known the circumstances in which it is carried out.

###### ARTICLE 58

(Inexigibility)
Without prejudice to the provisions of the preceding articles of this chapter, a person acts without guilt who acts in circumstances such that it would not be reasonable to demand and expect different behaviour from him or her.

### TITLE III

Penalties, Criminal Measures and Effects

#### CHAPTER I

General Provisions

###### ARTICLE 59

(Purposes of penalties and security measures)
1. Without prejudice to their repressive nature, the application of penalties and security measures aims at the protection of legal interests, the reparation of damages caused, the resocialisation of the agent and the prevention of recidivism.
2. The penalty may not in any case exceed the measure of guilt.
3. A security measure may only be applied if it is proportionate to the seriousness of the act and the dangerousness of the agent.

###### ARTICLE 60

(Limits of penalties and security measures)
1. There may be no death penalty nor penalties or security measures involving deprivation of liberty of a perpetual nature or of unlimited or indefinite duration.
2. In the case of dangerousness based on serious mental abnormality, security measures may be, by judicial decision, successively extended as long as that state persists, without ever exceeding the maximum limit of the penalty corresponding to the crime committed.
3. Penalties are not transferable.

#### CHAPTER II

Natural Persons

##### SECTION I

Principal Penalties, Penalties of imprisonment and fine
SUBSECTION I
Penalty of imprisonment

###### ARTICLE 61

(Duration of the penalty of imprisonment)
1. The penalty of imprisonment has, as a rule, a minimum duration of 3 days and a maximum duration of 24 years.
2. Exceptionally, the maximum limit provided by law for the penalty of imprisonment may reach 30 years, and may not in any case be exceeded.
3. The calculation of the periods of the penalty of imprisonment is made according to the criteria established in the criminal procedural law and, in its absence, in the civil law.

###### ARTICLE 62

(Execution of the penalty of imprisonment)
1. The execution of the penalty of imprisonment aims, without prejudice to its repressive nature, at the regeneration of the convicted persons and their social readaptation.
2. The execution of the penalty of imprisonment is regulated by specific legislation, in which the duties and rights of prisoners are established.
SUBSECTION II
Penalty of fine

###### ARTICLE 63

(Fine)
1. The penalty of fine consists of the payment:
a) of a determined amount or to be fixed between a minimum and a maximum declared by law;
b) of an amount proportional to the income of the convicted person and his or her personal expenses, fixed in days, the minimum limit being as a rule 3 days and the maximum 2 years, each day corresponding to an amount between one hundredth of the minimum wage and 1 minimum wage.
2. The limits established in the preceding paragraph are doubled:
a) if the offence has been committed for profit;
b) if, by virtue of the economic and financial situation of the defendant, the fine within the normal limits should be deemed ineffective.
3. The amount of the fine fixed in the judgment may not be increased by any additions.
4. The value of the fine reverts to the State.

###### ARTICLE 64

(Payment of the fine in instalments)
1. The fine and costs are paid within 10 working days from the date on which the decision becomes final.
2. At the request of the convicted person, if the amount of the applicable fine exceeds 10 minimum wages and the economic and financial situation of the convicted person justifies it, the court may authorise payment of the fine in instalments within a period not exceeding 1 year.

3. Failure to pay one of the instalments results in the maturity of the remaining instalments.

###### ARTICLE 65

(Personal nature of the penalty of fine)
When the law decrees the penalty of fine, if the offence is committed by several defendants, that penalty must be imposed on each of them.

##### SECTION II

Substitution of penalties

###### ARTICLE 66

(Principle of legality)
No penalty may be substituted by another, except in cases where the law authorises it.

###### ARTICLE 67

(Prevalence of non-custodial penalties and measures)
1. In the individualising function of fixing the penalty, substitute measures to the penalty of imprisonment are privileged, with emphasis on their resocialisation character, placing, whenever possible, in accordance with the law, the agent at liberty monitored by the State and the community.
2. Deprivation of liberty only occurs or is maintained when, through the application of other non-custodial measures or penalties, it is not possible to prevent the future commission of crimes by the offender or to guarantee the protection of legal interests.

###### ARTICLE 68

(Conditions for application)
1. Non-custodial penalties apply in cases where the agent, cumulatively:
a) is a first-time offender;
b) proceeds to the restitution of the goods of which he or she has appropriated, if applicable; or has fully repaired the damages and losses caused to the victim or the community by the commission of the crime or; in the case of partial reparation, undertakes to continue the reparation still outstanding within the period and conditions judicially fixed;
c) submits to the measures, duties and rules of conduct provided for the conditions of provisional suspension of proceedings, and which the court will fix in the decision.
2. Whenever it is concluded that, by that means, the purposes of the conviction are achieved, non-custodial penalties are applicable, provided their general and special conditions are met.

###### ARTICLE 69

(Prohibition of application)
1. The application of non-custodial penalties is prohibited whenever the agent has committed any of the following crimes:
a) crime against humanity and cultural identity;
b) intentional homicide;
c) rape of a minor;
d) kidnapping or trafficking in persons;
e) trafficking in narcotics or psychotropic substances;
f) terrorism or other type of organised crime or criminal association;
g) committed with the use of firearms or with violence or committed with serious threats against persons;

h) committed against a child, incapacitated person, elderly person or pregnant woman;
i) money laundering, corruption and related crime;
j) serious physical violence committed against a spouse, person with whom one lives as such, ex-spouse, partner or ex-partner, boyfriend or ex-boyfriend and family member;
k) traffic accident resulting in death, committed in a state of intoxication equal to or greater than 1.2 mg/l or under the effect of a psychotropic or narcotic substance; and
l) hunting, slaughter or fishing of protected or prohibited flora and fauna species.
2. The application of non-custodial penalties is also prohibited in cases where, in the last three years, the agent:
a) has been convicted of committing an intentional crime;
b) having been subjected to deprivation of liberty, even preventively, has evaded its enforcement; or
c) has benefited from a previous application of a non-custodial penalty.

###### ARTICLE 70

(Choice of non-custodial penalty)
Where the requirements provided for in the preceding articles are met, the court, having regard to the concrete circumstances, shall employ a substitute measure for a non-custodial penalty that best ensures the purposes of the conviction.

###### ARTICLE 71

(Non-custodial penalties)
The following are non-custodial penalties:
a) fine;
b) provision of socially useful work; and
c) temporary prohibition of rights.

###### ARTICLE 72

(Fine)
1. A prison sentence applied in a measure not exceeding 2 years, without prejudice to what the law provides for special cases, may always be replaced by a corresponding fine.
2. The replacement of a prison sentence by a fine is made according to the criterion established in paragraph b) of number 1 of article 63 and in the remaining numbers of the same article.
3. The provisions of articles 64 and 65 are correspondingly applicable.
4. If the offence is punishable by a prison sentence up to the limit indicated in number 1 and a fine, the court that decides to replace the prison sentence shall apply a single fine, equivalent to the sum of the fine directly imposed and that resulting from the conversion of the prison sentence.

###### ARTICLE 73

(Replacement of fine by work)
A fine penalty, in the absence of payment or of sufficient and unencumbered assets, may be replaced by the provision of socially useful work.

###### ARTICLE 74

(Conversion of unpaid fine into subsidiary imprisonment)
1. If the fine penalty, which has not been replaced by work, is not paid voluntarily or coercively, imprisonment shall be served for the time corresponding to the ratio of 1 day of subsidiary imprisonment corresponding to 2 days of fine penalty, even if the crime was not punishable by imprisonment.
2. When the fine is of an amount fixed by law, it is converted into imprisonment at a weighted ratio, its duration not exceeding 2 years in the case of a fine applied for any crime, six months in the case of a fine applied to contraventions provided for in laws, and one month in the case of a fine applied to contraventions provided for in regulations or by-laws.
3. The daily rate of conversion of the fine into imprisonment shall not, however, be less than that resulting from the division of its total by the maximum time into which the fine penalty may be converted.
4. The convicted person may at any time avoid, totally or partially, the execution of the imprisonment referred to in the preceding numbers by paying, in full, the fine to which he was sentenced.
5. The provisions of the preceding numbers are correspondingly applicable to cases in which the convicted person, culpably, does not fulfil the days of work by which, at his request, the fine was replaced; if the non-fulfilment is not attributable to him, the provisions of the preceding number are correspondingly applicable.

###### ARTICLE 75

(Socially useful work)
1. If a prison sentence not exceeding 3 years is to be applied to the offender, the court shall replace it with the provision of work for the benefit of the community, this consisting of the gratuitous provision of an activity, service or task to the community, to the productive sectors of penitentiary services or to other public and private entities that pursue purposes of public or community interest.
2. Each day of imprisonment fixed in the sentence is replaced by one hour of work, up to a maximum of 600 hours.
3. Work for the benefit of the community may be provided on Saturdays, Sundays and public holidays, as well as on working days, but in the latter case the work periods may not prejudice the offender's normal working day, nor exceed it.
4. In choosing the activity to be performed within the framework of the provision of socially useful work, the literary and professional qualifications of the offender or convicted person, his availability of time, as well as his physical condition and health must be taken into consideration on a case-by-case basis.
5. Among other activities, the following are considered to be covered by the concept of socially useful work:
a) tasks performed in welfare establishments, schools, orphanages, hospitals, homes for the elderly or for persons with disabilities and other similar establishments;
b) the provision of work in the context of the construction, conservation or maintenance of public roads and public sanitation;
c) services provided in the field of afforestation, conservation and protection of the environment, wild fauna and flora;
d) tasks relating to the supply and distribution of water, gas, electricity and other energy sources;
e) activities relating to the construction, conservation or maintenance of public infrastructure or infrastructure of social interest;

f) tasks of general cleaning, conservation and maintenance of gardens, parks and other public spaces or infrastructure or infrastructure of public interest.

###### ARTICLE 76

(Temporary prohibition of rights)
1. Temporary prohibition of rights consists of the actual limitation of the individual rights of a person who has committed any crime with abuse or violation of the duties inherent to the position, function, profession, activity or occupation.
2. A prison sentence applied in a measure not exceeding 2 years may be replaced by prohibition, for a period between a minimum of 6 months and a maximum of 3 years, of the right whose immoderate use gave rise to the offence.

###### ARTICLE 77

(Violation of impositions, prohibitions or interdictions)
1. If the offender violates the impositions, prohibitions or interdictions determined in the sentence fixing the non-custodial penalty, the court, having regard to the circumstances, may revoke it.
2. Revocation determines the fulfilment of the prison sentence fixed in the sentence, without the convicted person being able to demand the restitution of any benefit he may have made.

##### SECTION III

Waiver of penalty

###### ARTICLE 78

(Waiver of penalty)
Except in specially provided cases, when the crime is punishable by a prison sentence not exceeding three months, the court may declare the defendant guilty, but not apply any penalty if:
a) the unlawfulness of the act and the guilt of the offender are minimal;
b) the damage has been repaired; and
c) reasons of prevention do not oppose the waiver of penalty.

##### SECTION IV

Accessory penalties and effects of penalties

###### ARTICLE 79

(General principles)
No penalty involves as a necessary effect the loss of civil, professional or political rights, nor does it deprive the convicted person of his fundamental rights, except for the limitations imposed by law inherent to the meaning of the conviction and the specific requirements of its execution.

###### ARTICLE 80

(Accessory penalties)
The accessory penalties are:
a) rule of conduct;
b) loss of mandate or temporary prohibition from exercising public functions;
c) suspension from exercising public functions;
d) prohibition from driving; and
e) inhibition from exercising parental authority, guardianship or curatorship.

###### ARTICLE 81

(Rule of conduct)
1. The court may order the offender to adopt certain measures, in particular those necessary to bring the unlawful activity to an end or to avoid its consequences.
2. In particular, the court may impose on the convicted person that, for between 2 to 6 years:
a) he not exercise a certain profession, activity or occupation that depends on special qualification, licence or authorisation from the public authority;
b) he not frequent certain environments or places;
c) he not reside in certain locations;
d) he not accompany, lodge or receive certain persons;
e) he not frequent certain associations or participate in certain meetings;
f) he not have in his possession objects capable of facilitating the commission of crimes; or
g) he present himself periodically before the court, the social reintegration technician or entities of interest.
3. The time during which the offender is deprived of liberty by judicial decision does not count towards the period of prohibition.

###### ARTICLE 82

(Loss of mandate or temporary prohibition from exercising public functions)
1. Without prejudice to special regimes provided for in law, the public servant who, in the exercise of the activity for which they were appointed or designated, commits a crime punishable by imprisonment exceeding 2 years, loses their mandate or is prohibited from exercising those functions for a period of 2 to 6 years, when the act:
a) is committed with serious abuse of function or with manifest and serious violation of the duties inherent thereto;
b) reveals unworthiness in the exercise of office; or
c) implies the loss of confidence necessary for the exercise of the function.
2. The provisions of the preceding number are correspondingly applicable to professions or activities whose exercise depends on a public title or on authorization or approval by public authority.
3. In the case of elective office, the loss of mandate shall occur in relation to the mandate that the convicted person is performing, regardless of whether the acts were committed in the performance of another mandate.
4. Whenever the public servant is convicted of committing an intentional crime, the court communicates the conviction to the authority on which they depend.
5. Unless otherwise provided, the prohibition from exercising public function determines the loss of rights and benefits attributed to the public servant for the corresponding period.
6. The provisions of number 3 of article 81 are correspondingly applicable.

###### ARTICLE 83

(Prohibition from driving)
1. Whoever is convicted of a crime committed while driving a motorized vehicle may also be sentenced to prohibition from driving a motorized vehicle, for a period to be fixed between 3 months and 2 years, if the crime was committed with serious violation of road traffic rules.

2. The prohibition implies, for the holder of the driving licence, the obligation to surrender it at the court registry or at the service determined by the court.
3. The prohibition takes effect from the final judgment of the decision and may cover the driving of any category of motorized vehicles or a specific category.
4. The provisions of number 3 of article 81 are correspondingly applicable.

###### ARTICLE 84

(Incapacity to exercise parental authority, guardianship or curatorship)
1. Whoever is convicted of a crime against the life, physical, moral or patrimonial integrity of a minor may be prohibited from exercising parental authority, guardianship or curatorship, for a period between 1 and 5 years, taking into account the seriousness of the act and its consequences, the prior conduct and the personality of the agent.
2. The provisions of number 3 of article 81 are correspondingly applicable.

#### CHAPTER III

Legal Persons and Equivalent Entities

##### SECTION I

Principal penalties, Penalties of dissolution and fine

###### ARTICLE 85

(Applicable penalties)
For crimes committed by legal persons and equivalent entities, the principal penalties of dissolution or fine are applied.

###### ARTICLE 86

(Dissolution of legal person or equivalent entity)
1. The penalty of dissolution is decreed by the court when the legal person or equivalent entity has been created with the exclusive or predominant intention of committing the crime or when repeated criminal practice shows that it is being used for that purpose by those who occupy a management position therein.
2. Dissolution entails the cessation of all activities, the cancellation of licences, the inventory of assets that are property of the convicted entity and liquidation in charge of a suitable person appointed by the court.

###### ARTICLE 87

(Fine)
1. The fine penalty applicable to legal persons and equivalent entities is fixed between a minimum of 3 days and a maximum of 2 years at a daily rate between 1 to 5 minimum wages, without prejudice to what the law provides for special cases.
2. Upon expiry of the deadline for payment of the fine or any of its instalments without payment having been made, execution against the assets of the legal person or equivalent entity proceeds.
3. The fine that is not paid voluntarily or coercively cannot be converted into subsidiary imprisonment.

###### ARTICLE 88

(Payment of fine in instalments)
1. At the request of the convicted entity, the court may, if the amount of the fine exceeds 50 minimum wages and the economic and financial situation of the legal person or equivalent entity justifies it, authorize payment of the fine in instalments over a period not exceeding 1 year.

2. The provisions of numbers 2, 3 and 4 of article 63 are correspondingly applicable.

###### ARTICLE 89

(Publicity and communication of the conviction decision)
1. The decision convicting the legal person or equivalent entity is published, and must be by extract containing the identification of the legal person or equivalent entity, the elements of the offence, the sanctions applied and their duration.
2. Publicity of the conviction decision is effected at the expense of the convicted entity, in a means of social communication to be determined by the court, as well as through the posting of a notice, for a period of not less than 30 days, at the commercial or industrial establishment itself or at the place where the activity is carried out, in a manner clearly visible to the public.
3. The judicial conviction decision is also communicated to the administrative licensing entity of the legal person or equivalent entity.

##### SECTION II

Accessory penalties

###### ARTICLE 90

(Accessory penalties)
For crimes committed by legal persons and equivalent entities, the following accessory penalties may be applied, individually or cumulatively:
a) rules of conduct;
b) surety for good conduct;
c) temporary prohibition from exercising a certain activity or from contracting;
d) deprivation of the right to subsidies, subventions or public incentives; or
e) closure of establishment.

###### ARTICLE 91

(Rules of conduct)
The court may order the legal person or equivalent entity to, for a period of 1 to 5 years, adopt certain measures, namely those necessary to cease the unlawful activity or avoid its consequences.

###### ARTICLE 92

(Temporary prohibition from exercising a certain activity or from contracting)
1. Temporary prohibition from exercising certain activities may be ordered by the court, for a period of 3 months to 5 years, when the crime was committed in the exercise of those activities.
2. For a period of 1 to 5 years, the court may prohibit the legal person or equivalent entity from entering into a certain contract or contracts with the State and certain public and private entities.
3. In the cases provided for in the preceding numbers, the court may rehabilitate the legal person or equivalent entity, if it has conducted itself, for a period of 5 years after serving the principal penalty, in a manner that makes it reasonable to suppose that it will not commit new crimes.

###### ARTICLE 93

(Deprivation of the right to subsidies, subventions and public incentives)
Deprivation of the right to subsidies, subventions and incentives granted by the State and other public legal persons is applicable, for a period of 1 to 5 years, to the legal person or equivalent entity.

###### ARTICLE 94

(Closure of establishment)
1. The closure of an establishment may be ordered by the court, for a period of 3 months to 5 years, when the offence has been committed within the scope of its activity.
2. When the legal person or equivalent entity commits a crime punishable by a fine exceeding 600 days, the court may order the definitive closure of the establishment.
3. In the case provided for in the preceding number, the court may rehabilitate the legal person or equivalent entity and authorise the reopening of the establishment if it has conducted itself, for a period of 5 years after serving the principal penalty, in such a way as to make it reasonable to suppose that it will not commit new crimes.
4. The transfer of the establishment or the assignment of rights of any nature, related to the exercise of the activity, carried out after the institution of proceedings or after the commission of the crime, does not prevent the application of the penalty of closure, unless the acquirer is in good faith.
5. The closure of the establishment does not constitute just cause for the dismissal of workers nor grounds for the suspension or reduction of payment of their respective remuneration.

#### CHAPTER IV

Security Measures

###### ARTICLE 95

(Security measures)
The security measures are:
a) confinement of persons lacking criminal capacity;
b) outpatient treatment of persons lacking criminal capacity;
c) confinement in an open penitentiary centre;
d) supervised liberty; and
e) surety for good conduct.

###### ARTICLE 96

(Confinement of persons lacking criminal capacity)
Whoever has committed an unlawful typical act and is considered to lack criminal capacity under the terms of article 50 is ordered by the court to be confined in a cure, treatment or security establishment whenever, by virtue of the psychic anomaly and the seriousness of the act committed, there is well-founded fear that he will commit other acts of the same kind.

###### ARTICLE 97

(Execution of confinement and imprisonment)
1. The measure of confinement is executed before the prison sentence to which the offender has been sentenced and is deducted therefrom, according to the provisions of article 127, number 1.
2. As soon as the measure of confinement must cease, the court places the offender on conditional release if the time corresponding to the provisions of article 153 has been served and the release proves compatible with the defence of the legal order and social peace.

3. If conditional release is revoked, under the terms of article 154, the court decides whether the offender must serve the remainder of the sentence or continue confinement for the same period.

###### ARTICLE 98

(Minimum duration of confinement)
1. When the act committed by the person lacking criminal capacity corresponds to a crime against the person or a crime of common danger punishable by imprisonment exceeding 8 years, the confinement has a minimum duration of 3 years; from this duration is deducted the period for which the offender has suffered deprivation of liberty by reason of the same act.
2. In other cases the minimum corresponds to half the duration of the sentence.

###### ARTICLE 99

(Prior psychic anomaly)
1. When the offender is not declared to lack criminal capacity and is sentenced to imprisonment, but it is shown that, by virtue of a psychic anomaly from which he already suffered at the time of the crime, the regime of common establishments will be prejudicial to him or that he will seriously disturb that regime, the court orders his confinement in an establishment intended for persons lacking criminal capacity, for the time corresponding to the duration of the sentence.
2. The confinement provided for in the preceding number does not prevent the granting of conditional release under the terms of article 153, nor the placement of the offender in a common establishment, for the time of deprivation of liberty remaining to be served, as soon as the cause determining the confinement ceases.

###### ARTICLE 100

(Subsequent psychic anomaly)
1. If a psychic anomaly with the effects provided for in article 96 or in the preceding article supervenes to the offender after the commission of the crime, the court orders confinement in an establishment intended for persons lacking criminal capacity for the time corresponding to the duration of the sentence.
2. To the confinement referred to in the preceding number, resulting from a psychic anomaly with the effects provided for in the preceding article, the regime provided for in numbers 1 and 2 of article 98 applies.
3. The confinement referred to in number 1, resulting from a psychic anomaly with the effects provided for in article 96, is deducted from the sentence, the provisions of article 97 being correspondingly applicable.

###### ARTICLE 101

(Extension of confinement)
While the situation of dangerousness persists, the court may extend the duration of the measure of confinement, never exceeding the maximum limit of the sentence corresponding to the type of crime committed by the person lacking criminal capacity.

###### ARTICLE 102

(Experimental liberty)
1. Except in cases where the maximum duration of confinement has been reached, the definitive release of a confined person is preceded by a period of experimental liberty, fixed between a minimum of 2 years and a maximum of 5, but which cannot exceed the time remaining until the maximum limit of the duration of confinement.

2. If, at the end of the period of experimental liberty, there are no grounds leading to its revocation, the measure of confinement is declared extinct.
3. If, at the end of the period of experimental liberty, proceedings or an incident that may lead to its revocation are pending, the measure of confinement is only declared extinct when the proceedings or the incident end and there is no ground for revocation.

###### ARTICLE 103

(Revocation of experimental liberty)
1. Experimental liberty is revoked when:
a) the conduct of the offender reveals that confinement is indispensable; or
b) the offender is sentenced to a custodial penalty and the conditions for suspension of its execution, under the terms of number 1 of article 142, are not met.
2. Revocation determines immediate reconfinement.

###### ARTICLE 104

(Outpatient treatment of person lacking criminal capacity)
In cases where the offender is declared to lack criminal capacity by reason of psychic anomaly and there is no well-founded danger of continuing to commit unlawful typical acts of the same kind, the court subjects him to outpatient treatment for the period of time deemed appropriate but never exceeding half the maximum sentence corresponding to the type of crime in question.

###### ARTICLE 105

(Review of the situation)
1. If the existence of a cause justifying the cessation of confinement is invoked, the court may at any time consider the matter.
2. Consideration is mandatory, independently of application, after 2 years from the commencement of confinement or from the decision that has maintained it.
3. In any case, the minimum period of confinement fixed in article 98 is reserved.

###### ARTICLE 106

(Simulation of psychic anomaly)
Alterations to the normal regime of execution of the sentence, based on the provisions of the preceding precepts of this chapter, lapse as soon as it is proven that the psychic anomaly of the offender was simulated.

###### ARTICLE 107

(Application of security measures)
1. Security measures are also applicable to:
a) individuals suspected of habitually acquiring or serving as intermediaries in the acquisition or sale of stolen objects, or proceeds of crimes, even if they have not been convicted as receivers, if they have not complied with legal determinations or police instructions intended for the supervision of receivers;
b) those who are habitual alcoholics and predisposed by alcoholism to the commission of crimes, or abuse narcotics.

2. To the individuals indicated in paragraph a) of number 1, the bond of good conduct or supervised release shall be imposed for the first time, and for the second time, supervised release with the bond doubled, or internment.
3. The individuals indicated in paragraph b) of number 1 may serve the sentence to which they have been condemned and be interned after such service in a special establishment or open penitentiary centre.
4. Internment may only be ordered in the sentence that has convicted the offender.
5. In relation to foreigners, security measures may be replaced by expulsion from the national territory.
6. Non-custodial security measures may be reduced in their duration when such reduction proves convenient for the social rehabilitation of the convicted person and half of the period fixed by the conviction sentence has already elapsed.
7. In general, more serious security measures may be replaced, during execution, by less serious security measures that prove adequate for the social rehabilitation of the offenders.

###### ARTICLE 108

(Internment in open penitentiary centre)
Internment in an open penitentiary centre is understood to be for an indeterminate period from 6 months to 3 years. This regime is considered extensive to any internment measures provided for in special legislation.

###### ARTICLE 109

(Supervised release)
1. Supervised release shall be established for a period of 2 to 5 years and implies compliance with the obligations imposed by judicial decision.
2. In the event of failure to comply with the conditions of supervised release, its conditions may be altered or supervised release may be replaced by internment in open centres for an indeterminate period, but not exceeding, at its maximum, the period of supervised release not yet served.

###### ARTICLE 110

(Bond of good conduct)
1. The bond of good conduct shall be provided by deposit of the amount fixed by the judge, for a period of 2 to 5 years.
2. If the bond cannot be provided, it shall be replaced by supervised release for the same period.
3. The bond shall be forfeited in favour of the Court Treasury if the person who provided it has conduct incompatible with the bonded obligations, within the period established, or if, within the same period, he gives cause for the application of another security measure.

###### ARTICLE 111

(Competence to modify or replace sentences and security
measures)
Decisions intended to modify or replace sentences or security measures during their execution, both in duration and in prison regime, are within the competence of the sentence execution courts, unless by law they belong to any other.

#### CHAPTER V

Determination of Sentence

###### ARTICLE 112

(Determination of the measure of sentence)
1. The determination of the measure of sentence, within the limits defined by law, is made according to the guilt of the offender and the requirements of criminal prevention.
2. In determining the measure of sentence, the court considers all circumstances that, not forming part of the type of crime, speak in favour of or against the offender, considering in particular:
a) the degree of unlawfulness of the act, the manner of its execution and the seriousness of its consequences, as well as the degree of violation of the duties imposed on the offender;
b) the intensity of intent or negligence;
c) the feelings manifested in committing the crime and the purposes or motives that determined it;
d) the personal conditions of the offender and his economic situation;
e) the conduct prior to the act and subsequent to it, especially when the latter is intended to repair the consequences of the crime;
f) the lack of preparation to maintain lawful conduct, manifested in the act, when such lack must be censured through the application of the sentence.

###### ARTICLE 113

(Replacement of sentences)
No sentence may be replaced by another, except in cases where the law authorises it.

###### ARTICLE 114

(Sentence applied to several convicted persons)
1. When the law fixes a prison sentence, if the offence is committed by several convicted persons, each of them must be imposed a sentence according to the mode of participation.
2. If the law fixes a fine sentence, there being several offenders, each must be imposed that sentence.

###### ARTICLE 115

(Duty to give reasons)
In the judgment, the grounds for determining the sentence are expressly stated.

#### CHAPTER VI

Application of Sentences When There Are Aggravating or Mitigating Circumstances

###### ARTICLE 116

(General aggravation or mitigation of prison sentences)
1. The prison sentence is aggravated and mitigated by fixing its duration within the limits determined by law for the offence.
2. If in cases where prison sentences are applicable, aggravating or mitigating circumstances concur, which are not specially and expressly considered in the law to qualify the greater or lesser seriousness of the crime, determining the corresponding sentence, it is to be aggravated or mitigated, as to duration, within the maximum and minimum of the same sentences.

###### ARTICLE 117

(Extraordinary aggravation of sentences)
1. There shall be grounds for extraordinary aggravation of sentences in the terms described in articles 101 and 109, without, however, exceeding the limits established therein.
2. If in the same conduct more than one of the general aggravating circumstances and other special circumstances concur, only that which has the strongest aggravating effect is considered for the purpose of determining the applicable sentence, the other or others being valued in the measure of the sentence.

###### ARTICLE 118

(Special mitigation of sentences)
1. The court specially mitigates the sentence, beyond the cases expressly provided for by law, when there exist circumstances prior to or subsequent to the crime, or contemporaneous with it, that markedly diminish the unlawfulness of the act, the guilt of the offender or the need for the sentence.
2. For the purposes of the provisions of the preceding number, the following circumstances are considered, among others:
a) the offender having acted under the influence of serious threat or under the ascendancy of a person on whom he depends or to whom he owes obedience;
b) the conduct of the offender having been determined by honourable motive, by strong solicitation or temptation of the victim himself or by unjust provocation or undeserved offence;
c) there having been acts demonstrative of sincere repentance of the offender, namely the reparation, as far as was possible for him, of the damage caused; or
d) much time having elapsed since the commission of the crime, the offender maintaining good conduct.
3. The circumstance that, by itself or together with other circumstances, gives rise simultaneously to a mitigation specially provided for by law and to that provided for in this article may only be taken into account once.

###### ARTICLE 119

(Terms of special mitigation)
1. Whenever there are grounds for special mitigation of the sentence, the following is observed with respect to the limits of the applicable sentence:
a) the maximum limit of the prison sentence is reduced by one third;
b) the minimum limit of the prison sentence is reduced to one fifth if it is equal to or greater than two years and to the legal minimum if it is less;
c) the maximum limit of the fine is reduced by one third and the minimum limit reduced to the legal minimum;
d) if the maximum limit of the prison sentence does not exceed two years, it may be replaced by a fine.
2. The specially mitigated sentence that has been fixed in concreto is subject to replacement and suspension, under the general terms.

###### ARTICLE 120

(Simultaneous concurrence of aggravating and mitigating circumstances)
When aggravating circumstances and mitigating circumstances concur simultaneously, the penalty shall be aggravated or mitigated in a balanced manner, according to which ones predominate.

###### ARTICLE 121

(Qualifying circumstances)
1.	When a circumstance qualifies the greater or lesser seriousness of the crime, specially determining the measure of the

penalty, it is in relation to the penalty fixed by reason of the qualification that the aggravation or mitigation resulting from the concurrence of other circumstances is established.
2.	In the concurrence of qualifying circumstances that aggravate the penalty of the crime to a special measure expressly considered in the law, only the aggravation resulting from the most serious qualifying circumstance shall take place, the other circumstances of that kind being assessed as if they were of a general character.

###### ARTICLE 122

(Equivalence between penalties of fine and imprisonment)
The equivalence between penalties of fine and imprisonment follows what is set out in article 74.

#### CHAPTER VII

Application of Penalties in Some Special Cases

##### SECTION I

Punishment of concurrence of crimes and continued crime

###### ARTICLE 123

(Concurrence of crimes)
If the same act simultaneously constitutes a crime and a contravention, the agent is punished as a crime, without prejudice to the application of the accessory sanctions provided for the contravention.

###### ARTICLE 124

(Rules for punishment of concurrence)
1.	When someone has committed several crimes before the conviction for any of them becomes final, he is sentenced to a single penalty, the facts and the personality of the agent being considered together in determining the penalty.
2.	The applicable penalty has as its maximum limit the sum of the penalties concretely applied to the various crimes, and may not, in the case of imprisonment, exceed 30 years; and as its minimum limit the highest of the penalties concretely applied to the various crimes.
3.	If the penalties concretely applied to the crimes in concurrence are some of imprisonment and others of fine, a single penalty of imprisonment is applicable, in accordance with the criteria established in the preceding numbers, and a single fine considering the rule of material accumulation.
4.	Accessory penalties and security measures are always applied to the agent, even if provided for by only one of the applicable laws.

###### ARTICLE 125

(Supervening knowledge of concurrence)
1.	If, after a conviction becomes final, it is shown that the agent committed, prior to that conviction, another crime or other crimes, the rules of the preceding article are applicable, the penalty that has already been served being deducted in the service of the single penalty applied to the concurrence of crimes.
2.	The provisions of the preceding number are only applicable in relation to crimes whose conviction has become final.
3.	The accessory penalties and security measures applied in the previous sentence are maintained, except when they prove unnecessary in view of the new decision; if they are applicable only to the crime that remains to be assessed, they are only decreed if they are still necessary in the face of the previous decision.

###### ARTICLE 126

(Punishment of continued crime)
1.	A continued crime is punishable with the penalty applicable to the most serious conduct that forms part of the continuation.
2.	If, after a conviction becomes final, a more serious conduct that forms part of the continuation becomes known, the penalty applicable to it replaces the previous one.

##### SECTION II

Deduction

###### ARTICLE 127

(Procedural measures)
1.	The detention and preventive imprisonment suffered by the accused in the proceedings in which he comes to be convicted are deducted in full in the service of the penalty of imprisonment imposed on him.
2.	If a penalty of fine is imposed, the detention and preventive imprisonment are deducted at the rate of 1 day of deprivation of liberty for 2 days of fine.

###### ARTICLE 128

(Previous penalty)
1.	If the penalty imposed by a final decision is subsequently replaced by another, the previous penalty is deducted from it, to the extent that it has already been served.
2.	If the previous penalty and the subsequent one are of different nature, the deduction that seems equitable is made in the new penalty.

###### ARTICLE 129

(Procedural measure or penalty suffered abroad)
Any procedural measure or penalty that the agent has suffered, for the same act or acts, outside Mozambique is deducted, in accordance with the preceding articles.

##### SECTION III

Punishment of recidivism, of minor, of culpable crime and of agent with deprivation of intelligence

###### ARTICLE 130

(Punishment of recidivism)
1.	If the applicable penalty is imprisonment exceeding 2 years, the aggravation corresponding to recidivism is equal to half the difference between the maximum and minimum limits. The measure of aggravation may, however, be reduced, if the circumstances relating to the personality of the agent so advise, to an increase of penalty equal to the duration of the penalty applied in the previous conviction.
2.	If the applicable penalty is imprisonment up to 2 years, the aggravation consists of increasing the maximum and minimum of the penalty by half the maximum duration of the applicable penalty.
3.	In contraventions, the aggravation for recidivism consists of doubling the limits of the penalty; in the case of second and subsequent recidivisms, the measure of the penalty is successively raised to double the increase thus determined.

###### ARTICLE 131

(Penalty applicable to minors)
1.	If the agent has not completed 21 years of age at the time of perpetration of the crime, no penalty exceeding 12 years of imprisonment shall be imposed.
2.	If the agent has not completed 18 years at the time of perpetration of the crime, no penalty more severe than 8 years of imprisonment shall ever be imposed on him.

3.	Minors under 16 years of age are subject to the jurisdiction of juvenile courts and, in relation to them, only measures of assistance, education or correction provided for in special legislation may be taken.

###### ARTICLE 132

(Punishability of culpable crimes)
Merely culpable crimes are only punishable in the special cases declared in the law and to these crimes penalties exceeding 2 years of imprisonment shall never be applicable.

###### ARTICLE 133

(Punishment of agent with voluntary and accidental deprivation of intelligence)
The provisions of the preceding article are extended to the agent of the crime in which any of the circumstances specified in article 50 concur.

#### CHAPTER VIII

Effects of Penalties

###### ARTICLE 134

(Effects of penalties. Production ope legis)
The effects of penalties take place by virtue of law, independently of declaration in the conviction sentence.

###### ARTICLE 135

(Effects of conviction)
The conviction sentence of the agent of the crime, as soon as it becomes final, has only the effects declared in the following articles.

###### ARTICLE 136

(Effects of conviction to penalty of imprisonment)
Except for the limitations imposed by law, inherent to the meaning of the conviction and the specific requirements of its execution, conviction to a penalty of imprisonment does not involve as a necessary effect the loss of civil, professional or political rights, nor does it deprive the convicted person of his fundamental rights.

###### ARTICLE 137

(Loss of objects)
1.	Objects that have served or were intended to serve for the commission of a typical unlawful act, or that have been produced by it, are declared forfeited in favour of the State when, by their nature or by the circumstances of the case, they endanger the safety of persons or public morals or order, or offer a serious risk of being used for the commission of new typical unlawful acts.
2.	The provisions of the preceding number take place even if no person can be punished for the act.
3.	If the law does not fix a special destination for the objects declared forfeited under the terms of the preceding numbers, the judge may order that they be totally or partially destroyed or placed outside commerce.

###### ARTICLE 138

(Objects belonging to third parties)
1. Without prejudice to the provisions of the following numbers, forfeiture does not take place if the objects did not belong, at the date of the act, to any of its agents or beneficiaries, or do not belong to them at the time the forfeiture is ordered.

2. Even if the objects belong to a third party, forfeiture is ordered when the holders of the objects have contributed, in a culpable manner, to their use or production, or have derived advantages from the act, or when the objects are, by any title, acquired after the commission of the act, with the acquirers knowing their provenance.
3. If the objects consist of inscriptions, representations or records written on paper, on another medium or in audiovisual means of expression, belonging to a third party in good faith, forfeiture does not take place, proceeding with restitution after the inscriptions, representations or records that constitute the typical unlawful act have been erased; if this is not possible, the court orders destruction, with compensation being due under the terms of civil law.

###### ARTICLE 139

(Forfeiture of things, rights or advantages)
1. Any reward given or promised to the agents of a typical unlawful act, for them or for others, is forfeited in favour of the State.
2. Also forfeited in favour of the State, without prejudice to the rights of the victim or of a third party in good faith, are the things, rights or advantages that, through the typical unlawful act, have been directly acquired, for themselves or for others, by the agents.
3. The provisions of the preceding numbers apply to things or rights obtained through transaction or exchange with the things or rights directly obtained by means of the typical unlawful act.
4. If the reward, things, rights or advantages referred to in the preceding numbers cannot be appropriated in kind, forfeiture is replaced by payment to the State of the respective value.

###### ARTICLE 140

(Deferred or instalment payment and mitigation)
1. When the application of the preceding article results, in concrete terms, in the payment of a pecuniary sum, the court, when supervening reasons justify it, may authorise payment within a period not exceeding 1 year, or permit payment in instalments, with the last instalment not going beyond 2 years following the date on which the conviction becomes final.
2. If, given the socioeconomic situation of the person concerned, the application of number 4 of the preceding article proves unjust or excessively severe, the court may equitably reduce the value referred to in that provision.

###### ARTICLE 141

(Civil effects of conviction)
The defendant definitively convicted, whatever the penalty, also incurs the obligation to:
a) restore to the victim the things of which the crime deprived him, or pay him their legally verified value, if restitution is not possible, and the victim or his heirs request such payment;
b) compensate the victim for the damage caused, when the victim or his heirs request compensation; and
c) pay the costs of the proceedings and the expenses of expiation.

#### CHAPTER IX

Suspension of Execution of Prison Sentence

###### ARTICLE 142

(Requirements and duration)
1. Provided it is not for any of the crimes referred to in article 69, the court may, for a period of 1 to 5 years, suspend the execution of a prison sentence imposed in a measure not exceeding 2 years if, having regard to the personality of the agent, the conditions of his life, his conduct before and after the crime and the circumstances thereof, it concludes that the mere censure of the act and the threat of imprisonment adequately and sufficiently achieve the purposes of punishment.
2. The court, if it deems it convenient and appropriate to achieve the purposes of punishment, subordinates the suspension of execution of the prison sentence, under the terms of the following articles, to the fulfilment of duties or the observance of rules of conduct, or determines that the suspension be accompanied by a probation regime.
3. The duties, rules of conduct and probation regime may be imposed cumulatively.
4. The conviction decision always specifies the grounds for the suspension and its conditions.

###### ARTICLE 143

(Duties)
1. The suspension of execution of the prison sentence may be subordinated to the fulfilment of duties imposed on the convicted person and intended to repair the harm of the crime, namely:
a) pay within a certain period, in whole or in the part that the court considers possible, the compensation due to the injured party or guarantee its payment by means of suitable security;
b) give the injured party adequate moral satisfaction;
c) deliver to the State or to social solidarity institutions a monetary contribution or benefit in kind of equivalent value.
2. The duties imposed may not in any case represent obligations for the convicted person whose fulfilment cannot reasonably be required of him.
3. The duties imposed may be modified until the end of the suspension period whenever relevant supervening circumstances occur or of which the court only subsequently becomes aware.

###### ARTICLE 144

(Rules of conduct)
1. The court may impose on the convicted person compliance, for the duration of the suspension, with one or more rules of conduct intended to facilitate his reintegration into society.
2. The court may also, having obtained the prior and express consent of the convicted person, determine his subjection to medical treatment or cure in an appropriate institution.

###### ARTICLE 145

(Revocation of suspension)
1. The suspension of execution of the prison sentence is revoked whenever, during its course, the convicted person:
a) grossly or repeatedly infringes the duties or rules of conduct imposed, or
b) commits a crime for which he is convicted and reveals that the purposes underlying the suspension could not, by means thereof, be achieved.

2. Revocation determines the serving of the prison sentence fixed in the judgment, without the convicted person being able to demand restitution of benefits he may have made.

###### ARTICLE 146

(Extinction of sentence)
1. If there are no grounds that may lead to revocation of the suspension, once the duration thereof has ended, the sentence is declared extinct.
2. If, at the end of the suspension period, proceedings are pending for a crime that may determine its revocation or incident for failure to comply with duties, rules of conduct or the social readaptation plan, the sentence is only declared extinct when the proceedings or incident end and there is no ground for revocation or extension of the suspension period.

###### ARTICLE 147

(Prescription)
Prescription does not run during the period of suspension of execution of the sentence.

#### CHAPTER X

Execution of Sentences and Security Measures

###### ARTICLE 148

(Basis of sentences and security measures)
1. The execution of sentences and security measures is based exclusively on a final judgment.
2. Save provision to the contrary, sentences fall solely on the person of the agent of the crime or contravention.

###### ARTICLE 149

(Prohibition of imprisonment for failure to pay judicial charges)
There is no imprisonment for failure to pay court tax, costs or stamps.

###### ARTICLE 150

(Commencement of serving of sentences and security measures)
1. The execution of sentences and security measures involving deprivation of liberty begins on the day on which the conviction judgment becomes final whenever the convicted person is in custody.
2. Number 1 of article 97 is correspondingly applicable.

###### ARTICLE 151

(Deferral of the execution of sentences and security measures)
The commencement of execution of custodial sentences and security measures is deferred:
a) in cases of legal impossibility of capture;
b) if the convicted person is affected by mental illness after conviction, until he recovers all his faculties;
c) during the presumed last three months of duly proven pregnancy and up to three months after childbirth; but, if the conviction is to a prison sentence exceeding eight years, the judge may order confinement, under custody, in an appropriate establishment; and
d) if the convicted person must first serve another sentence.

###### ARTICLE 152

(Interruption of continuous execution of sentences)
1. Save for the exceptions provided for by law, the execution of sentences is continuous.
2. The execution of custodial sentences and security measures is interrupted:
a) by physical illness or mental abnormality requiring hospital confinement;
b) by escape of the convicted person and during the time he remains a fugitive;
c) by decision of the Supreme Court, when revision of the sentence is admitted.

#### CHAPTER XI

Conditional Release

###### ARTICLE 153

(Conditional release)
1. Those convicted to custodial sentences of duration exceeding six months may be placed on conditional release for the time remaining to complete the sentence, when they have served half of it and show capacity and willingness to adapt to an honest life.
2. Excluded from the provisions of the preceding paragraph are those convicted of one of the crimes provided for in article 69, who may only be placed on conditional release if they have served at least three quarters of the sentence, in addition to showing repentance and aptitude to follow an honest life.

###### ARTICLE 154

(Revocation of conditional release)
1. Conditional release is revoked if the conditionally released person commits another crime of the same nature as that for which he was convicted or any intentional crime for which he comes to suffer a custodial sentence.
2. Conditional release may be revoked or made conditional if the released person does not have good behaviour or does not fulfil any of the obligations that have been imposed on him.
3. When conditional release is revoked the convicted person must complete the service of the sentence, with no deduction for the time spent on conditional release.

#### CHAPTER XII

Extinction of Criminal Liability

###### ARTICLE 155

(Extinction of criminal proceedings, sentences and security measures)
1. Criminal proceedings, sentences and security measures are extinguished, not only in the cases provided for in article 3, but also:
a) by the death of the perpetrator of the crime;
b) by prescription of criminal proceedings, even if not alleged by the defendant or if he retains any object as a result of the crime;
c) by amnesty;
d) by pardon of the party, or by renunciation of the right to file a complaint in court, when they take place;
e) by voluntary payment, in contraventions punishable only by fine;

f) by annulment of the conviction in revision proceedings;
g) by lapse of the conditional conviction; or
h) in special cases provided for by law.
2. The death of the perpetrator of the crime and amnesty do not prejudice the civil action for damages caused, nor do they have retroactive effect with regard to rights legitimately acquired by third parties.
3. Criminal proceedings prescribe after 15 years, if a prison sentence with a maximum limit exceeding 8 years is applicable to the crime; after 5 years, if a prison sentence of less than 8 years or a security measure is applicable to it; after 2 years, in cases of contraventions; after 1 year in the case of electoral offences.
4. If the criminal proceedings relate to a legal person or equivalent entity, the time limits provided for in the preceding paragraph are determined taking into account the prison sentence, before proceeding with the conversion.
5. If, for criminal proceedings to take place, a complaint by the victim or third parties is indispensable, the right to file a complaint prescribes after 2 years, if the crime corresponds to a prison sentence with a maximum limit exceeding 2 years and after 1 year, if the sentence corresponds to a crime punishable by a lesser sentence.
6. For the purposes of the provisions of paragraphs 3, 4 and 5, in determining the maximum sentence applicable to each crime, the elements that belong to the type of crime are taken into account, but not the aggravating or mitigating circumstances.
7. The prescription of criminal proceedings is counted from the day on which the crime was committed, except in the following cases:
a) from the day on which the consummation ceases, in permanent crimes;
b) from the day of the practice of the last act, in continued crimes and habitual crimes;
c) from the day of the last act of execution, in non-consummated crimes;
d) from the day of the occurrence of the result, when the occurrence of that result not included in the legal type of crime is relevant.
8. In the case of complicity, the perpetrator's act is always taken into account for the purposes of prescription.
9. The prescription of criminal proceedings does not run:
a) from the accusation in court and while the proceedings for the respective crime are pending;
b) after the institution of the action on which the instruction of the criminal proceedings depends and while the respective judgment does not become final;
c) with the declaration of contumacy;
d) with the notification of the order designating a day for the hearing in the absence of the defendant.
10. The prescription begins to run again from the day on which the cause of suspension ceases.
11. Concerning the civil action resulting from the crime, the provisions of paragraphs 3, 4 and 5 shall be complied with, insofar as applicable, if it has been cumulated with the criminal action and the time limits established in those paragraphs are longer than those of civil law, but in all other cases it shall prescribe, as well as the restitution or civil reparation ordered by final criminal judgment, according to the rules of civil law.
12. Pardon by the party only extinguishes the criminal liability of the defendant, when there are no criminal proceedings without denunciation or without private prosecution, except if the respective conviction has already become final and also in special cases declared by law. If the party is an unemancipated minor or interdicted for a cause that prevents him from managing his person, the pardon shall only produce effects when legitimately authorised.

###### ARTICLE156

(Other causes of extinction of sentences and security measures)
1. The sentence and the security measure are also extinguished:
a) by their execution;
b) by pardon;
c) by prescription;
d) by rehabilitation.
2. Pardon is the competence of the Head of State.
3. Effective prison sentences exceeding 8 years prescribe after 20 years; lesser sentences, after 10 years; sentences for contraventions and electoral crimes, after 1 year and security measures, after 5 years.
4. The prescription of the principal sentence involves the prescription of the accessory sentence that has not been executed, as well as the effects of the sentence that have not yet occurred.
5. The prescription of the sentence or security measure begins to run on the day on which the decision that applied the sentence becomes final.
6. If the convicted person escapes and has served part of the sentence, the prescription of the sentence or security measure is counted from the day of escape.
7. In those convicted in absentia, the prescription begins to be counted from the date on which the conviction was delivered.
8. The prescription of the sentence or security measure does not run while the convicted person is shown to be legally imprisoned for another reason.
9. In mixed sentences, the lighter sentences prescribe with the more serious sentence; but the causes of extinction referred to in subparagraphs a), b) and c) of paragraph 1 do not extinguish the effects of the conviction.
10. Save for provision to the contrary, criminal proceedings and sentences are only extinguished in relation to those to whom the causes of their extinction refer.

###### ARTICLE 157

(Rehabilitation)
1. Rehabilitation extinguishes the criminal effects of the conviction.
2. Rehabilitation by operation of law occurs after periods equal to the limitation periods for penalties or double the limitation period for security measures have elapsed, after the latter have been extinguished, if in the meantime there has been no new conviction.
3. Judicial rehabilitation, full or limited to one or some of the effects of the conviction, may be requested and granted after the extinction of the penalty and the security measure without a new conviction, when the good behaviour of the applicant is proven, the obligation to compensate the victim has been fulfilled or otherwise extinguished or its fulfilment is impossible, and the following periods have elapsed:
a) 6 years, in the case of offenders difficult to reform;
b) 1 year, in the case of those convicted of negligent crimes or intentional crimes punished with imprisonment up to 6 months or other less serious penalty;
c) 4 years, in cases not specified.
4. If rehabilitation is refused for lack of good behaviour of the applicant, it may only be requested again after the periods referred to in paragraph 3 have elapsed.
5. Rehabilitation does not benefit the convicted person with regard to the definitive losses resulting from the conviction, does not prejudice the rights that arose from it for the victim or for third parties, nor does it remedy, by itself, the nullity of acts performed by the convicted person during his or her incapacity.

6. The following convictions shall be cancelled from the criminal record and shall not appear therein for any purposes:
a) those annulled in review proceedings and convictions for amnestied crimes;
b) those prior to rehabilitation by operation of law or full judicial rehabilitation;
c) conditional convictions when the resolutive condition of the judgment has been fulfilled.

###### ARTICLE 158

(Civil liability)
The attribution and graduation of civil liability connected with criminal acts are governed by civil law.

BOOK TWO SPECIAL PART TITLE I
Crimes Against Persons

#### CHAPTER I

Crimes Against Life

##### SECTION I

Voluntary homicide

###### ARTICLE 159

(Simple voluntary homicide)
Whoever voluntarily kills another person is punished with imprisonment from 16 to 20 years.

###### ARTICLE 160

(Aggravated homicide)
Imprisonment from 20 to 24 years is applied to whoever causes the death of another in circumstances that reveal special censure or perversity, namely when any of the following circumstances concur:
a) there having been premeditation;
b) being an ascendant, descendant, adopter, adoptee, stepfather, stepchild, spouse, ex-spouse or person with whom one lives as such, even without cohabitation;
c) being committed for the extraction or consumption of organs, tissues or parts of the human body;
d) preceding, accompanying or following another crime, consummated or attempted, to which corresponds imprisonment whose maximum limit is greater than two years;
e) committing the act in the presence of a minor under sixteen years of age or against a particularly defenceless person, by reason of age, disability, illness or pregnancy;
f) committing the act together with at least two other persons or using a particularly dangerous means or employing torture or act of cruelty to increase the suffering of the victim;
g) committing the act motivated by racial, religious, political hatred or generated by the colour, ethnic or national origin, sex, sexual orientation or gender identity of the victim;
h) committing the act against an agent of security forces and services, public servant, civil or military, agent of public force or citizen entrusted with a

public service, teacher, examiner or member of the school community, or minister of religious worship, in the exercise of their functions or because of them;
i) being a public servant and committing the act with abuse of power of authority; or
j) committing the act against an infant at the time of its birth, or within fifteen days after its birth.

###### ARTICLE 161

(Privileged homicide)
Whoever kills another person dominated by comprehensible violent emotion, compassion, despair or motive of relevant social or moral value, which significantly diminishes his or her guilt, is punished with imprisonment up to 2 years and a fine.

###### ARTICLE 162

(Poisoning)
1. Whoever commits the crime of poisoning is punished with imprisonment from 20 to 24 years.
2. Every attempt against the life of any person by means of substances that can cause death more or less promptly is qualified as poisoning, in whatever manner these substances are employed or administered and whatever the consequences.

###### ARTICLE 163

(Infanticide)
The mother who kills her child during or up to fifteen days after giving birth, still under its disturbing influence, is punished with imprisonment from 1 to 5 years.

###### ARTICLE 164

(Incitement, assistance and propaganda to suicide)
1. Whoever incites another person to commit suicide, or, upon serious, insistent and express request made by that person, provides assistance for that purpose, is punished with imprisonment from 2 to 8 years, if the suicide comes to be attempted or consummated.
2. If the person incited or to whom assistance was provided is a minor under 16 years of age or has, for any reason, his or her capacity for evaluation or determination significantly diminished, the agent is punished with imprisonment from 8 to 12 years.
3. Whoever, by any means, makes propaganda or advertising of a product, object or method advocated as a means to produce death, in a manner suitable to provoke suicide, is punished with imprisonment up to 2 years and a corresponding fine.

###### ARTICLE 165

(Transport, detention, possession and commercialisation of human organs)
1. Except in cases permitted by law, whoever transports, detains, possesses or commercialises human organs, internal or external, blood, blood products or tissues of the human body, is punished with imprisonment from 12 to 16 years, if a more serious penalty does not apply.
2. Imprisonment from 16 to 20 years is applied to whoever, with promise of success in sentimental life, business or of any other nature, or enticement with payment of money, delivery of movable or immovable property or performance of an act, instigates the agent to commit the acts referred to in the preceding paragraph.

##### SECTION II

Crimes against intrauterine life

###### ARTICLE 166

(Termination of pregnancy)
1. Whoever, on purpose, causes a pregnant woman to abort, employing for this purpose violence or drink, or medicine, or any other means, if the act is committed without the woman's consent, is sentenced to imprisonment from 3 months to 2 years; if it is with the woman's consent, is punished with imprisonment up to 1 year.
2. The woman who consents and makes use of the means provided, or who voluntarily procures abortion for herself is punished with imprisonment up to 2 years.
3. The doctor, pharmacist, nurse or any other health professional who, abusing his or her profession, has voluntarily contributed to the execution of the abortion, indicating or providing the means, incurs imprisonment up to 2 years.

###### ARTICLE 167

(Aggravation)
When from the abortion or from the means employed results serious bodily harm or death of the pregnant woman, as a direct and necessary consequence, the penalty applicable to whoever caused her to abort is imprisonment from 8 to 12 years, depending on the seriousness of the results.

###### ARTICLE 168

(Non-punishable termination of pregnancy)
The termination of pregnancy carried out by a doctor or other health professional qualified for that purpose, or under their direction, in an official or officially recognised health establishment and with the consent of the pregnant woman, does not constitute a crime when, according to the state of knowledge and experience of medicine:
a) the foetus is not viable;
b) there is reliable reason to predict that the unborn child will suffer, in an incurable manner, from a serious disease or congenital malformation, and it is carried out within the first twenty-four weeks of pregnancy;
c) it proves to be the indicated means to avoid danger of death or serious and lasting injury to the body or physical health of the pregnant woman or is advisable in the case of chronic-degenerative diseases;
d) the pregnancy resulted from a crime of sexual violation or incestuous relations and the abortion takes place within the first sixteen weeks; or
e) it is performed, by express choice of the woman, within the first twelve weeks of pregnancy.

###### ARTICLE 169

(Formalities for non-punishable termination of pregnancy)
1. Consent is given:
a) in a written document signed by the pregnant woman or at her request and, whenever possible, with a minimum advance notice of three days in relation to the date of the intervention;
b) if the pregnant woman is under sixteen years of age or mentally incapable, respectively and successively, as the case may be, by the legal representative, by an ascendant or descendant or, in their absence, by any relatives of the collateral line.

2. If it is not possible to obtain consent under the terms of the preceding number and the performance of the abortion is urgent, the doctor shall decide in conscience in view of the situation, resorting, whenever possible, to the opinion of one or more other doctors.
3. The verification of the circumstances that make the abortion non-punishable is certified by a medical certificate, written and signed before the intervention by two health professionals different from the one by whom, or under whose direction, the abortion will be performed.

##### SECTION III

Involuntary homicide

###### ARTICLE 170

(Involuntary homicide)
1. Homicide that someone commits or causes through their lack of skill, thoughtlessness, negligence, lack of dexterity or failure to observe any regulation, is punished with a prison sentence of 1 month to 2 years and corresponding fine.
2. Homicide that is a consequence of an unlawful act, or of a lawful act, performed at an unlawful time, place or manner, has the same penalty, unless a more serious penalty should be applied to the unlawful act, in which case only that is applied.

#### CHAPTER II

Crimes Against Physical Integrity

##### Section I

Voluntary bodily harm

###### ARTICLE 171

(Simple voluntary bodily harm)
1. Whoever, voluntarily, with some bodily harm mistreats any person, without any of the circumstances set out in the following articles being present, is punished with a prison sentence of up to 3 months.
2. Criminal proceedings depend on a complaint, except when the harm is committed against agents of security forces and services, in the exercise of their functions or because of them.

###### ARTICLE 172

(Voluntary bodily harm resulting in illness or temporary incapacity for work)
1. If, as a necessary effect of the harm, illness or temporary incapacity for professional work or work of any nature results, it is punished with a prison sentence of up to 2 years and corresponding fine.
2. A prison sentence of between 1 to 5 years is applied if the harm results in cutting, deprivation, maiming or disablement of any limb or organ of the body.

###### ARTICLE 173

(Voluntary bodily harm resulting in deprivation of reason or permanent incapacity to work)
1. If, as a necessary effect of the harm, the victim is deprived of reason or rendered incapable for life of working, the penalty is imprisonment of 2 to 8 years.
2. The same aggravated penalty is applied if the bodily harm is committed voluntarily, but without intent to kill, and nevertheless causes death.

###### ARTICLE 174

(Bodily harm resulting in death by accidental circumstance)
If the wounding, beating or harm is not mortal, nor aggravates or produces mortal illness, and it is proven that some accidental circumstance, independent of the agent's will, and which was not a consequence of their act, is the cause of death, the penalty for the crime is not aggravated by the circumstance of death.

###### ARTICLE 175

(Privileged bodily harm)
If harm to physical integrity is produced in the circumstances provided for in article 161, the agent is punished with a prison sentence of up to 6 months and corresponding fine.

###### ARTICLE 176

(Administration of substances harmful to health)
The provisions of the preceding articles apply to whoever, voluntarily and with intent to cause harm, administers to another, in any manner, substances which, by their nature, are not capable of causing death, but are harmful to health.

###### ARTICLE 177

(Bodily harm qualified by the person of the victim)
If the crimes declared in the preceding articles of this section are committed against an ascendant or descendant, adopter or adoptee, stepfather, stepmother or stepchild, relative or in-law up to the second degree of the agent, the penalty is aggravated by one third in its minimum and maximum limits.

###### ARTICLE 178

(Bodily harm qualified by the person of the agent)
If the crimes declared in this section are committed by an individual invested with public authority, known as such or intentionally wearing a uniform that makes them appear as such, the penalty is aggravated by half in its minimum and maximum limits.

###### ARTICLE 179

(Castration and genital mutilation)
1. Whoever voluntarily amputates from another any genital organ or organ necessary for procreation or sexual enjoyment is punished with a prison sentence of 8 to 12 years.
2. If the death of the victim results within forty days after the act, as an effect of the injuries produced, the penalty is imprisonment of 16 to 20 years.

###### ARTICLE 180

(Voluntary inhibition for military service)
1. Whoever voluntarily mutilates themselves to render themselves unfit for military service, is punished with a prison sentence of 3 months to 1 year.
2. If the accomplice is a doctor or other health professional, they are sentenced to the same penalty.

###### ARTICLE 181

(Medical-surgical interventions and treatments)
1. Interventions and treatments which, according to the state of knowledge and experience of medicine, prove to be indicated and are carried out in obedience to the theoretical and practical rules of prophylaxis, diagnosis and treatment appropriate to

the specific case, by a doctor or other legally authorised person, with the intention of preventing, diagnosing, combating or alleviating disease, suffering, injury or bodily fatigue, or mental disturbance, are not considered harm to physical integrity.
2. The persons indicated in the preceding number who, in view of the purposes pointed out therein, carry out interventions or treatments violating the rules set out there and thereby create a danger to life or danger of serious harm to the body or health are punished with a prison sentence of up to 2 years or with a corresponding fine, if a more serious penalty does not apply.

##### SECTION II

Ill-treatment

###### ARTICLE 182

(Ill-treatment)
1. Anyone who, having in their care, custody, under the responsibility of their direction or education or in their service, a minor or particularly defenceless person, by reason of age, disability, illness or pregnancy or economic dependence, who cohabits with the agent, and:
a) inflicts, repeatedly or not, physical or psychological ill-treatment, including corporal punishment, deprivation of liberty and sexual offences, or treats them cruelly;
b) employs them in dangerous, inhuman or prohibited activities; or
c) overburdens them with excessive work.
shall be punished with imprisonment from 1 to 5 years and a fine, if a more serious penalty is not applicable by virtue of another legal provision.
2. The same penalty applies to anyone who, by not observing legal or regulatory provisions, subjects any worker to danger to life or serious harm to body or health.
3. If from the facts provided for in the preceding numbers there results:
a) harm to physical integrity, the agent is punished with imprisonment from 2 to 8 years; or
b) death, the agent is punished with imprisonment from 20 to 24 years.

###### ARTICLE 183

(Standing)
Health, social action, education and culture units are especially obliged to report to the Public Prosecutor's Office or police authorities all offences described in the preceding article of which they become aware in the exercise of their functions.

##### SECTION III

Involuntary bodily harm

###### ARTICLE 184

(Involuntary bodily harm)
1. Anyone who, in the circumstances set out in article 170, harms the body, physical integrity or health of another person, is punished with imprisonment up to 6 months and a corresponding fine, or is only obliged to make reparation, according to the circumstances, except for the penalty for a contravention, if applicable.
2. Criminal proceedings only take place upon complaint by the victim.
3. In the absence of this complaint, any contravention that has been committed is nevertheless punishable.

#### CHAPTER III

Provisions Applicable to Previous Chapters
SOLE SECTION
Special cases of exclusion of guilt

###### ARTICLE 185

(Legitimate defence)
1. The rule established in subparagraph b) of number 1 of article 51 includes cases in which homicide or bodily harm are committed:
a) repelling the scaling or breaking into an inhabited house or its outbuildings, which may give access to entry into the same house;
b) defending oneself against the perpetrators of robberies or destruction carried out with violence.
2. If, in the case of subparagraph b) of number 1 of article 51, any person exceeds the limits set in article 53, they are, according to the nature and circumstances of the excess, either punished with imprisonment up to 2 years and a fine, or acquitted of the penalty, remaining only subject to civil reparation for their fault.

###### ARTICLE 186

(Concealment or hiding of corpse)
If, in the case of homicide or death as a consequence of wounds, beatings or other bodily harm dealt with in articles 161 and 175, someone conceals or hides the body of a deceased person, they are punished with imprisonment from 3 months to 2 years.

###### ARTICLE 187

(Provocation constituted by insult, defamation or threat)
Verbal insults, defamation and threats are not included in the causes of privilege set out in articles 161 and 175, for the purpose of the qualification provided for therein, except as provided in number 4 of article 45.

#### CHAPTER IV

Participation in Affray

###### ARTICLE 188

(Participation in affray)
1. Anyone who intervenes or takes part in an affray of two or more persons, from which death or serious bodily harm results, is punished with imprisonment from 2 to 8 years.
2. If, from the provision of the preceding number, neither homicide nor injury results, the penalty is imprisonment up to 6 months.
3. Participation in an affray is not punishable when it is determined by a non-reprehensible motive, in cases where it aims to react against an attack, defend another person or separate the combatants.

###### ARTICLE 189

(Use of weapons in affray)
Anyone who, in an affray, has made use of a firearm or bladed weapon against the adversary, without homicide or injury resulting, is punished with imprisonment from 2 months to 1 year and a corresponding fine, if a more serious penalty is not applicable by virtue of the use of a prohibited weapon.

#### CHAPTER V

Crimes Against Humanity, Cultural Identity and Personal Integrity

###### ARTICLE 190

(Genocide)
1. Anyone who, with intent to destroy, in whole or in part, a national, ethnic, racial or religious group, as such, commits:
a) homicide of members of the group;
b) serious harm to the physical integrity of members of the group;
c) subjection of the group to conditions of existence or to cruel, degrading or inhuman treatment, likely to bring about its destruction, total or partial;
d) forcible transfer of children of the group to another group; or
e) prevention of procreation or births within the group.
shall be punished with imprisonment from 20 to 24 years.
2. Anyone who, publicly and directly, incites genocide is punished with imprisonment from 12 to 16 years.
3. Agreement with a view to committing genocide is punished with imprisonment from 8 to 12 years.

###### ARTICLE 191

(Racial, ethnic or religious discrimination)
1. Anyone who founds or establishes an organisation or develops organised propaganda activities that incite discrimination, hatred or violence against a person or group of persons because of their race, colour, ethnic or national origin, religion, sex or gender identity, or that encourage it, is punished with imprisonment from 1 to 8 years.
2. The penalty of the preceding number is imposed on anyone who participates in the organisation or in the activities referred to or provides assistance to them, including their financing.
3. Anyone who, in a public meeting, by writing intended for dissemination or through any means of social communication:
a) provokes acts of violence against a person or group of persons because of their race, colour, ethnic or national origin, religion, sex or gender identity; or
b) defames or insults a person or group of persons because of their race, colour, ethnic or national origin, religion, sex or gender identity, namely through the denial of war crimes or crimes against peace and humanity, with the intention of inciting discrimination or encouraging it; or
c) threatens a person or group of persons because of their race, colour, ethnic or national origin, religion, sex or gender identity.
is punished with imprisonment from 6 months to 5 years.

###### ARTICLE 192

(War crimes against civilians)
1. Anyone who, violating norms or principles of general or common international law, in time of war, armed conflict or occupation, commits against the civilian population, against the wounded, sick or prisoners of war:
a) intentional homicide;
b) torture or cruel, degrading or inhuman treatment;
c) serious harm to physical integrity;
d) taking of hostages;
e) coercion to serve in enemy armed forces;
f) deportation; or
g) serious, prolonged and unjustified restrictions on the liberty of persons.
shall be punished with imprisonment from 20 to 24 years.
2. The penalty is aggravated by one quarter in its minimum and maximum limits when the acts referred to in the preceding number are committed against members of a humanitarian institution.

###### ARTICLE 193

(Destruction of monuments)
Anyone who, violating norms or principles of general or common international law, in time of war, armed conflict or occupation, destroys or damages, without military necessity, cultural or historical monuments or establishments devoted to science, the arts, culture, religion or humanitarian purposes is punished with imprisonment from 5 to 10 years.

###### ARTICLE 194

(Torture and other cruel, degrading or inhuman treatment)
1. Anyone who, having the function of preventing, prosecuting, investigating or adjudicating criminal, administrative or disciplinary offences, executing sanctions of the same nature or protecting, guarding or supervising a detained or imprisoned person, tortures or treats in a cruel, degrading or inhuman manner in order to:
a) obtain from that person or another person a confession, testimony, statement or information;
b) punish that person for an act committed or allegedly committed by that person or another person; or
c) intimidate that person or to intimidate another person,
shall be punished with imprisonment from 2 to 8 years, if a more serious penalty is not applicable.
2. The same penalty shall be incurred by anyone who, on their own initiative or by superior order, usurps the function referred to in the preceding paragraph to commit any of the acts described therein.
3. Torture, cruel, degrading or inhuman treatment shall be considered to be an act consisting of beatings, electric shocks, mock executions or hallucinatory substances or inflicting acute physical or psychological suffering, severe physical or psychological fatigue or the use of chemical products, drugs or other means, natural or artificial, with the intention of disturbing the victim's capacity for determination or free expression of will.
4. If the facts described in this article or in the preceding article result in suicide or death of the victim, the perpetrator shall be punished with imprisonment from 16 to 20 years.

#### CHAPTER VI

Crimes Against the Liberty of Persons

##### SECTION I

Crimes against personal liberty

###### ARTICLE 195

(Threat)
1. Anyone who, by any means, threatens another person with the commission of a crime against physical integrity, personal liberty, sexual liberty and self-determination or property, in such a way as to cause fear or unease or to prejudice their freedom of determination, shall be punished with imprisonment up to 1 year and corresponding fine.
2. A death threat shall be punished with imprisonment from 6 months to 2 years and corresponding fine.
3. Criminal proceedings depend on a complaint.

###### ARTICLE 196

(Physical coercion)
1. Anyone who, by means of physical violence, compels another person to an action or omission, or to endure an activity, shall be punished with imprisonment from 1 month to 1 year and fine up to 1 year.
2. If the coercion is carried out using a bladed or firearm weapon or any instrument capable of endangering the life, physical integrity or property of the person, the penalty shall be imprisonment from 2 to 8 years, if a more serious penalty is not applicable by virtue of another legal provision.
3. Attempt is punishable.
4. The act is not punishable:
a) If the use of the means to achieve the intended end is not censurable; or
b) If it aims to prevent suicide or the commission of a typical unlawful act.
5. If the act takes place between ascendants and descendants, adopters and adoptees, criminal proceedings depend on a complaint.

###### ARTICLE 197

(Abduction)
1. Anyone who, by means of violence, threat or any fraud, abducts another person with the purpose of subjecting them to extortion, rape, obtaining ransom, reward, compelling the State, an international organization, a legal person, a group of persons or a natural person to an action or omission, or to endure an activity, shall be punished with imprisonment from 16 to 20 years.
2. The penalty provided for in the preceding paragraph shall be aggravated, under general terms, if the abduction is:
a) preceded or accompanied by serious offence to the physical integrity of the victim;
b) accompanied by torture or other cruel, degrading or inhuman treatment;
c) committed against a defenceless person by reason of age, illness, disability or pregnancy;
d) committed by simulating the status of public authority, by a public servant with serious abuse of authority;
e) accompanied by a crime against the sexual liberty of the victim;
f) followed by suicide of the victim.
3. If the deprivation of liberty results in the death of the victim, the perpetrator shall be punished with imprisonment from 20 to 24 years.

###### ARTICLE 198

(Kidnapping)
1. Anyone who unlawfully detains, imprisons, keeps imprisoned or detained any person or in any way unlawfully deprives them of their liberty, up to 24 hours, shall be punished with imprisonment from 1 to 2 years and corresponding fine.
2. The perpetrator shall be punished with imprisonment from 2 to 10 years if the deprivation of liberty:
a) lasts for more than two days;
b) is preceded or accompanied by serious offence to physical integrity, torture or other cruel, degrading or inhuman treatment;
c) is committed under the false pretext that the victim suffered from a mental disorder;
d) results in suicide or serious offence to the physical integrity of the victim;

e) is committed against a particularly defenceless person, by reason of age, disability, illness or pregnancy;
f) is committed against an agent of security forces and services, public servant, civilian or military, agent of public force or citizen entrusted with a public service, teacher, examiner or member of a school community, or minister of religious worship, in the exercise of their functions or because of them;
g) is committed by simulating public authority or by a public servant with serious abuse of authority.
3. If the deprivation of liberty results in the death of the victim, the perpetrator shall be punished with imprisonment from 20 to 24 years.

###### ARTICLE 199

(Special mitigation)
1. In the case of the crimes provided for in articles 197 and 198, if the perpetrator voluntarily renounces their claim and releases the victim, or makes a serious effort to achieve it, the penalty may be specially mitigated.
2. The release of the victim referred to in the preceding paragraph must not be determined by the reactive action of public authorities.
3. In the case of complicity, it is sufficient that the perpetrator of the crime has made a serious effort to achieve the release of the victim.

##### SECTION II

Other crimes against personal liberty

###### ARTICLE 200

(Illegal capture and violence against detainees)
1. Except in cases where the law permits private individuals to arrest someone, anyone who arrests any person to present them to the authority shall be punished with imprisonment of 1 month and corresponding fine.
2. In cases where the law permits private individuals to detain someone, if acts of violence qualified as crimes by law are employed, those acts of violence shall be punished with the corresponding penalties.

#### CHAPTER VII

Crimes Against Sexual Liberty

##### SECTION I

Crimes against sexual liberty

###### ARTICLE 201

(Rape)
Anyone who has intercourse, anal or oral coitus, vaginal or anal penetration with body parts or objects with any person, of either sex, against their will, by means of physical violence or vehement intimidation or when the victim is deprived of the use of reason or senses, commits the crime of rape and shall be punished with imprisonment from 2 to 8 years.

###### ARTICLE 202

(Sexual intercourse with a child under twelve years)
Anyone who has sexual intercourse with a child under twelve years shall be punished with imprisonment from 16 to 20 years.

###### ARTICLE 203

(Other sexual acts with minors)
1. Anyone who, by means of violence or serious threat, commits a sexual act with a minor under sixteen years or causes it to be committed by the minor with another person shall be sentenced to imprisonment from 8 to 12 years.

2. If violence is not proven, the penalty shall be imprisonment from 2 to 8 years and fine up to 1 year.

###### ARTICLE 204

(Indecent assault)
1. Anyone who importunes another person, by performing acts of an exhibitionist nature before them, making proposals of a sexual nature or compelling them to sexual contact not consummated, shall be punished with imprisonment up to 2 years and corresponding fine, if a more serious penalty is not applicable by virtue of another legal provision.
2. If the offended person is a minor under sixteen years, the penalty shall be aggravated by half at the maximum limit.

###### ARTICLE 205

(Sexual harassment)
1. Whoever, abusing the authority conferred by their functions or taking advantage of their condition as hierarchical superior or ascendancy inherent to the exercise of employment, office or function, coerces someone with the intent to obtain sexual advantage or favour, is punished with a penalty of imprisonment up to 2 years and corresponding fine.
2. The same penalty is incurred by whoever sexually coerces someone with a promise of benefit of any nature, the agent taking advantage of domestic relations, cohabitation or hospitality.

###### ARTICLE 206

(Sexual fraud)
Whoever, fraudulently taking advantage of an error about their personal identity, performs a sexual act with another person is punished with a penalty of imprisonment up to 2 years and fine up to 1 year.

###### ARTICLE 207

(Non-consensual artificial procreation)
Whoever performs an act of artificial procreation on a woman without her consent is punished with a penalty of imprisonment from 2 to 8 years.

###### ARTICLE 208

(Aggravation by result)
In the crimes dealt with in this section, the penalties are aggravated by half in their maximum limits, if:
a) except as provided in article 205, the victim is in a relationship of hierarchical, economic or work dependence on the agent and the crime is committed by taking advantage of this relationship; or
b) the victim is an ascendant, descendant, adopter, adoptee, relative, affine up to the second degree of the agent or is in a relationship of guardianship or curatorship; or
c) pregnancy, offence to physical integrity, transmission of a pathogenic agent that creates danger to life, suicide or death of the victim results.

###### ARTICLE 209

(Standing)
In the crimes provided for in this section, criminal action is public, except as provided in articles 201, 204, number 1, 205, 206 and 207, in which there is no place for criminal proceedings without prior complaint by the offended party, or their parents or adopters, grandparents, spouse or person with whom they live as such, siblings, guardians or curators.

###### ARTICLE 210

(Accessory penalties)
1. Whoever is convicted of crimes provided for in this section, whenever referring to minors under sixteen years of age, may, for a period of 1 to 5 years, having regard to the concrete gravity of the fact and its connection with the function exercised by the agent, be:
a) inhibited from the exercise of parental power, guardianship or curatorship; or
b) prohibited from the exercise of profession, function or activity that implies having minors under their responsibility, education, treatment or supervision.
2. Judicial decisions that apply accessory penalties are communicated to the competent entities.

##### SECTION II

Child pornography

###### ARTICLE 211

(Child pornography)
For the purposes of this section, child pornography means any material, whatever the medium or platform, that visually represents a minor or person appearing to be a minor involved in sexually explicit behaviour.

###### ARTICLE 212

(Use of minors in pornography)
1. It is punished with a penalty of imprisonment from 1 to 5 years, whoever:
a) uses a minor under 18 years of age in pornographic photography, film or recording, regardless of its medium, or entices them for that purpose; or
b) uses a minor under 18 years of age in a pornographic show or entices them for that purpose.
2. Whoever commits the acts described in the preceding number using a minor under 12 years of age incurs the penalty of 2 to 8 years of imprisonment.

###### ARTICLE 213

(Distribution or possession of child pornography)
1. Whoever distributes, imports, exports, disseminates, exhibits or transfers professionally or with the purpose of profit, under any title or by any means, materials of pornographic photography, film or recording of minors under eighteen years of age is punished with imprisonment up to 2 years and fine up to 1 year.
2. The mere sharing, exhibition, transfer, import, export or distribution of the material dealt with in the preceding number, when it does not have lucrative or professional purposes, gives rise to the penalty of imprisonment from 1 to 2 years and corresponding fine.
3. Whoever, regardless of medium or platform, acquires, holds or keeps the materials referred to in this article, even for personal use, incurs the penalty of imprisonment up to 1 year and corresponding fine.
4. The attempt is punishable.

##### SECTION III

Prostitution

###### ARTICLE 214

(Prostitution)
1. Whoever, professionally or with lucrative intent promotes, favours or facilitates the exercise of prostitution by another person, is punished with a penalty of imprisonment from 1 to 2 years and corresponding fine.

2. It is punished with the penalty of imprisonment from 2 to 8 years, whoever commits the crime provided for in the preceding number:
a) by means of violence or serious threat;
b) by fraudulent means;
c) with abuse of authority resulting from a family relationship, guardianship or curatorship, or hierarchical, economic or work dependence; or
d) taking advantage of the psychic incapacity or situation of special vulnerability of the victim.

###### ARTICLE 215

(Prostitution of minors)
Whoever, habitually, excites, favours or facilitates the debauchery of any minor under 18 years of age, to satisfy the sexual desires of others, shall be punished with a penalty of imprisonment from 2 to 8 years of imprisonment and fine up to 1 year.

###### ARTICLE 216

(Accessory penalties)
The provisions of numbers 1 and 2 of article 210 are correspondingly applicable.

##### SECTION IV

Public outrage to decency

###### ARTICLE 217

(Public outrage to decency)
1. Whoever, even though there is no individual offence to the sexual dignity of any person, performs a material act of sexually explicit character in a public place, or open or exposed to the public, shall be punished with a penalty of imprisonment up to 6 months and fine up to 1 month.
2. The same penalty is incurred by whoever:
a) makes, imports, acquires, places on vehicles, buildings, walls, partitions or in any public place or place of public access writing, drawing, painting, print or any sexually explicit object for the purpose of commerce, distribution or public exhibition; or
b) broadcasts, exhibits or disseminates, in a public place or accessible to the public, theatrical, radio, television or cinematographic representation of sexually explicit character or performs any show with the same character.

#### CHAPTER VIII

Placing Persons in Danger

###### ARTICLE I

Placing persons in danger

###### ARTICLE 218

(Exposure of person to danger)
1. Whoever voluntarily places or exposes another person to immediate danger to life or serious offence to their integrity, through the use of particularly dangerous or insidious means, is punished with a penalty of imprisonment from 1 to 3 years, if a more serious penalty is not applicable by virtue of another legal provision.
2. If the danger provided for in the preceding number is created by negligence, the agent is punished with imprisonment up to 6 months and fine up to 3 months.

###### ARTICLE 219

(Placing a person in a state of being unable to protect themselves)
Whoever abandons a person who is not in a situation to protect or defend themselves by reason of age, illness or their physical or psychic state, in such a way as to create for them actual danger to life or serious offence to their integrity, is punished with a penalty of imprisonment from 1 to 5 years, whenever the agent had the duty to guard, watch over or assist them.

###### ARTICLE 220

(Exposure of others to disease by sexual act and danger of contagion of serious disease)
Whoever performs an act, sexual or not, by which they transmit or there is susceptibility of transmitting to others venereal disease or other serious disease, knowing or having reason to know that they are infected, contaminated or suffer from that disease, is punished with a penalty of imprisonment from 1 to 5 years.

##### SECTION II

Impediment to assistance and failure to provide aid

###### ARTICLE 221

(Impediment to the provision of rescue and failure to provide assistance)
1. Whoever prevents the arrival or provision of rescue intended for a person in danger of death, serious harm to their integrity or freedom, or to combat a disaster or accident that presents danger to the safety of persons, is punished with a prison sentence of 1 to 5 years, if a more serious penalty is not applicable by virtue of another legal provision.
2. Whoever, in a case of serious necessity, namely caused by disaster, accident, public calamity or situation of common danger, that endangers the life, physical integrity or freedom of another person, refrains from providing them with assistance, which, without serious risk to their own person or to third parties, they could provide, through their personal action or by requesting rescue, is punished with a prison sentence of 1 to 2 years and corresponding fine.
3. If the situation referred to in the preceding number was created by the omitter, the penalty is imprisonment of 2 to 8 years.

###### ARTICLE 222

(Refusal by health professional)
1. The doctor, nurse or other health professional who, in an urgent case, refuses the assistance of their profession in a case of danger to life or serious danger to the physical integrity of another person, which cannot be removed in another manner, is punished with a prison sentence of 2 months to 1 year and corresponding fine.
2. Failure to appear, without legitimate excuse, at the place and time for which they were summoned or notified, shall be considered as refusal for all purposes of what this article provides.

##### SECTION III

Crimes against public health

###### ARTICLE 223

(Sale or display of poisonous or abortive substances)
Whoever displays for sale, sells or supplies poisonous or abortive substances, without legitimate authorization and without the formalities required by the respective laws and regulations, is punished with a prison sentence of up to 6 months and corresponding fine.

###### ARTICLE 224

(Adulteration of goods intended for public consumption)
1. Whoever, in any manner, adulterates goods intended for public consumption, in such a way that they become harmful to health and displays them for sale thus adulterated, and, likewise, whoever, in the same manner, adulterates goods intended for the consumption of one or some persons, or who sells corrupt goods, or manufactures or sells objects, the use of which is necessarily harmful to health, is punished with a prison sentence of 1 to 5 years.
2. In any place where the deteriorated goods, or the aforementioned objects, are found, they are seized and rendered useless.
3. The following is punished with the same penalty as number 1:
a) whoever conceals or removes, or sells, or buys items intended to be destroyed or disinfected;
b) whoever throws into a fountain, cistern, river, stream, lake, whose water serves for drinking, anything that makes the water impure or harmful to health;
c) whoever imports, distributes, transports, stores or holds for commercial purposes spoiled, falsified or corrupt goods.
4. The following is punished with a prison sentence of 2 to 8 years:
a) if the foodstuffs are by their nature or quality likely to harm the health of the consumer;
b) if the quantities of transmissible toxic products are harmful to the health of the consumer.

##### SECTION IV

Weapons

###### ARTICLE 225

(Definition)
Belonging to the category of weapons are objects produced or adapted with the exclusive purpose of cutting, piercing or striking and those which, habitually serving the ordinary uses of life, are employed with the purpose of killing, wounding, beating or destroying.

###### ARTICLE 226

(Prohibited weapons)
1. Whoever, without due licensing, manufactures, imports, acquires, transfers, alienates or disposes by any title, and likewise transports, keeps, holds or uses weapons intending them or having to know that they were intended to be used as instruments of aggression, is punished with a penalty of 8 to 12 years of imprisonment, if a more serious penalty does not apply.
2. If the manufacture, importation, acquisition, transfer, alienation, disposal, transport, keeping, holding or use of weapons, means and instruments referred to in this article simply contravenes the regulations and prescriptions of the competent authorities and is not intended to serve as a means for the commission of any crime, the penalty is imprisonment of up to 2 years and fine of up to 6 months.
3. The same penalty as the preceding number is imposed on individuals whose respective license for use and carrying of a weapon has been revoked and who, notwithstanding, continue using it as if it were in force.
4. Simple illegal holding in the residence of the holder, or in another place, is punished with imprisonment of up to 6 months and corresponding fine.
5. Not included in the provisions of this article are weapons that must be considered as objects of art and ornamentation and the instruments of daily life that are not intended for the ultimate purpose of aggression.

6. In all cases declared in this article, the weapons are seized and forfeited in favour of the State.

###### ARTICLE 227

(Abusive use of weapons)
1. Whoever, being a license holder, unjustifiably discharges a firearm at another person, without intention to kill and without resulting from the act any wound or contusion, is punished with a prison sentence of 1 to 2 years.
2. Whoever throws a projectile at a moving vehicle, of transport by air, water or land, is punished with a prison sentence of up to 2 years and corresponding fine, if a more serious penalty is not applicable by virtue of another legal provision.

##### SECTION V

Crimes against the safety of communications

###### ARTICLE 228

(Capture or diversion of aircraft, ship, train or collective passenger transport vehicle)
1. Whoever seizes or diverts from its normal route, an aircraft in flight, or ship in the course of navigation, or train in circulation, or collective passenger transport vehicle, in which persons are found, is punished with a prison sentence of 8 to 12 years.
2. The following is considered:
a) an aircraft in flight from the moment when, after boarding is completed, all external doors have been closed until the moment when one of those doors is opened for disembarkation. In case of forced landing the flight is considered as being in progress until the competent authority takes responsibility for the aircraft, as well as for the persons and goods on board;
b) a ship in the course of navigation from the moment when the ground personnel or the crew begin the preparatory operations for a particular voyage until arrival at the place of destination;
c) a train in the course of circulation from the moment when, after boarding of passengers is completed, the journey begins until the moment when disembarkation is to take place;
d) a collective passenger transport vehicle in transit from the moment when, after boarding of passengers is completed, the journey begins until the moment when disembarkation is to take place.

###### ARTICLE 229

(Attack on the safety of transport by air, water, railway or land)
1. Whoever attacks the safety of transport by air, water, railway or land is punished with a prison sentence of 2 to 8 years:
a) by destroying, suppressing, damaging or rendering unusable installation, material, installation or signalling;
b) by placing an obstacle to operation or circulation;
c) by giving false notice or signal; or
d) by performing an act from which disaster may result.
2. If, through the conduct referred to in the preceding number, the agent creates danger to the life or physical integrity of others, or to the property of others, they are punished with a prison sentence of 8 to 12 years.

3. If the danger referred to in the preceding numbers is created by negligence, the agent is punished with a prison sentence of 1 to 2 years.

###### ARTICLE 230

(Driving under the influence)
1. Whoever drives a vehicle, with or without a motor, on a public road or equivalent, with a blood alcohol level or exhaled air equal to or greater than 1.2 mg/l, or not being in a condition to do so safely due to being under the influence of narcotics, psychotropic substances or products with analogous effects that impair physical, mental or psychological aptitude, is punished with imprisonment up to 1 year and corresponding fine, if a more severe penalty is not applicable by virtue of another legal provision.
2. The same penalty aggravated by half in its minimum and maximum limits is applied in the case of driving under the same circumstances of transport by air, water or railway.

###### ARTICLE 231

(Dangerous driving of land transport vehicle)
1. Whoever drives a vehicle, with or without a motor, on a public road or equivalent, and carries out unauthorised activities thereon, of a sporting or analogous nature, that grossly violate the rules relating to road safety provided for in road laws and regulations, namely concerning road traffic, priority, the obligation to stop, overtaking, change of direction, pedestrian crossings, reversal of direction of travel, reversing, speed limits or the obligation to drive in the left lane, is punished with imprisonment up to 2 years and corresponding fine, without prejudice to the contraventional penalties that may apply.
2. If, through the conduct referred to in the preceding paragraph, the agent creates real danger to the life or physical integrity of another, or to another's property, he is punished with imprisonment from 2 to 8 years and fine up to 1 year.
3. When the crime provided for is committed in the exercise of the respective activity by a driver of school transport vehicles, light hire vehicles, for public hire transport, semi-collective vehicles, heavy passenger or goods vehicles or transport of dangerous goods, rescue or emergency vehicles, the agent is punished with the penalty that would apply to the case, aggravated by one third in its minimum and maximum limits.
4. If the danger referred to in the preceding paragraphs is created by negligence, the agent is punished with imprisonment never less than 18 months.

###### ARTICLE 232

(Dangerous driving of transport vehicle by air, water or railway)
1. Whoever drives a vehicle intended for transport by air, water or railway, not being in a condition to do so safely or grossly violating the rules of driving, and thereby creates danger to the life or physical integrity of another or to another's property, is punished with imprisonment from 2 to 8 years.
2. If, through the conduct referred to in the preceding paragraph, the agent creates real danger to the life or physical integrity of another, or to another's property, he is punished with imprisonment from 5 to 10 years and fine up to 1 year.
3. If the danger referred to in the preceding paragraph is created by negligence, the agent is punished with imprisonment up to 2 years.

#### CHAPTER IX

Crimes Against the Dignity of Persons

##### SECTION I

Crimes against honour
SUBSECTION I
Defamation and insult

###### ARTICLE 233

(Defamation)
1. Whoever defames another publicly, by word of mouth, by published writing or drawing or by any other means of publication, imputing to him a fact offensive to his honour and consideration, forwarding or reproducing the imputation, is punished with imprisonment up to 1 year and corresponding fine.
2. The conduct is not punishable when:
a) the imputation is made to realise legitimate interests; and
b) the agent proves the truth of the same imputation or had serious grounds to, in good faith, consider it true.
3. The provisions of the preceding paragraph do not apply in the case of imputation of a fact relating to the intimacy of private or family life.
4. The good faith referred to in subparagraph b) of paragraph 2 is excluded when the agent has not fulfilled the duty of information that the circumstances of the case required concerning the truth of the imputation.

###### ARTICLE 234

(Insult)
1. The crime of insult, without imputing any specific fact, if committed against any person publicly, by gestures, by word of mouth, or by published drawing or writing, or by any means of publication, is punished with imprisonment up to 6 months and corresponding fine.
2. In prosecution for insult, proof of the truth of facts that form part of the reserve of intimacy of private life is not admitted.

###### ARTICLE 235

(Defamation and insult committed without publicity)
If, in the crimes provided for in the preceding articles, there is no publicity, the penalty is a fine up to 3 months.

###### ARTICLE 236

(Bodily harm with intent to insult)
If any bodily harm is publicly committed against any person with the intention of insulting him, it shall be punished with the penalty of defamation, committed with aggravating circumstances, unless the harm corresponds to a more severe penalty, which in this case shall be applied as if aggravating circumstances also concurred in the crime.

###### ARTICLE 237

(Offence to the honour of the President of the Republic and other entities)
1. Whoever insults or defames the President of the Republic or whoever constitutionally substitutes him in that capacity, is punished with imprisonment from 1 to 2 years.
2. The crimes dealt with in the preceding paragraph when committed against the holders of sovereign bodies and members of judicial administration bodies are punished with imprisonment up to 2 years.

###### ARTICLE 238

(Defamation or insult against ascendants)
1. The crimes declared in this chapter, committed against the father or mother, or any of the ascendants, are always punished with the maximum penalty.
3. If the same crimes are accompanied by other aggravating circumstances, the general rules shall be observed.

###### ARTICLE 239

(Defamation or insult against deceased person)
1. The crime of defamation or insult, committed against a person already deceased, is punished with imprisonment up to 6 months and corresponding fine, if there is a complaint by the ascendant or descendant, or spouse, or sibling or heir of that person.
2. The offence is not punishable when more than fifty years have elapsed since the death.

###### ARTICLE 240

(Offence to body, service or legal person)
Whoever, without having grounds to, in good faith, consider them true, affirms or spreads untrue facts, capable of offending the credibility, prestige, trust or good name that are due to a body or service that exercises public authority or not, institution or corporation, legal person, company or equivalent entity, is punished with imprisonment up to 6 months and corresponding fine.
SUBSECTION II
Common provisions

###### ARTICLE 241

(Aggravation)
The penalties provided for in the preceding articles are increased by half in their maximum limits if the act is committed:
a) to obtain reward or enrichment, for the agent or for another person, or to cause harm to another person or to the State; or
b) through a means of social communication.

###### ARTICLE 242

(Legitimacy for criminal action)
Criminal proceedings for the crimes provided for in this chapter depend on private prosecution.

###### ARTICLE 243

(Exemption from penalty)
1. The agent is exempted from penalty when he gives clarifications or explanations of the offence of which he was accused, if the offended person, whoever represents him or integrates his will as holder of the right to complaint or private prosecution, accepts them as satisfactory.
2. The court may also exempt from penalty if the offence was provoked by unlawful or reprehensible conduct of the offended person.
3. If the offended person responds, in the same act, with an offence to another offence, the court may exempt from penalty both agents or only one of them, according to the circumstances.

###### ARTICLE 244

(Public knowledge of conviction sentence)
1. In case of conviction, even with exemption from penalty,
the court may order, at the expense of the agent, adequate

public knowledge of the sentence, if so requested, until the closing of the hearing at first instance, by the holder of the right to file a complaint or private prosecution.
2. The court shall fix the specific terms in which the public knowledge of the sentence must take place.

##### SECTION III

Crimes against respect due to the dead

###### ARTICLE 245

(Violation of tombs and disrespect to the dead)
1. Whoever commits violation of tombs or graves, performing before or after burial any acts directly tending to break the respect due to the dead, is punished with a prison sentence of up to 1 year or corresponding fine.
2. The same penalty is imposed on whoever performs any acts directly tending to break the respect due to the memory of the deceased or the dead, without violation of the tomb or grave.
3. If the crime provided for in number 2 consists of an act that, if performed against a living person, would constitute a crime provided for in the last part of article 201, it is punished with imprisonment of up to 2 years and corresponding fine. The violation of a grave shall for this purpose be considered as an aggravating circumstance of the consummated crime.
4. The attempt is punishable.

###### ARTICLE 246

(Exclusion of unlawfulness)
The provisions of the preceding article do not include cases in which, under the terms of laws or regulations and by order of the competent authority, one proceeds to the transfer of a corpse from one tomb or grave to another in the same or different cemetery or burial place, to the improvement of the tomb or grave, and other similar cases.

##### SECTION IV

Crimes against religious feelings

###### ARTICLE 247

(Outrage on grounds of religious belief)
1. Whoever publicly offends another person or mocks them on account of their religious belief or function, in a manner adequate to disturb public peace, is punished with a prison sentence of up to 1 year or with a fine of up to 6 months.
2. The same penalty is incurred by one who desecrates a place or object of worship or religious veneration, in a manner adequate to disturb public peace.

###### ARTICLE 248

(Prevention, disturbance or outrage to an act of worship)
It is punished with a prison sentence of up to 1 year or with a fine of up to 6 months, whoever:
a) by means of violence or threat of serious harm prevents or disturbs the legitimate exercise of worship of a religion; or
b) publicly vilifies an act of worship of a religion or mocks it.

##### SECTION V

Abuse and exploitation of faith

###### ARTICLE 249

(Material enticement through faith)
Whoever, by means of artifice or misleading advertising, entices believers of a religion or cult to alienate or deliver money or

property in exchange for their participation or promise for enrichment, is punished with a prison sentence of 1 month to 2 years and corresponding fine.

#### CHAPTER X

Crimes Against the Privacy of Private Life

##### SECTION I

Crimes against the privacy of private life

###### ARTICLE 250

(Violation of domicile)
1. Whoever, without consent, enters the dwelling of another person or courtyards, gardens or fenced spaces attached to that dwelling or remains there after being ordered to withdraw, is punished with a prison sentence of up to 1 year and corresponding fine.
2. If the crime provided for in the preceding number is committed by means of violence or threat of violence, with use of a weapon or by means of breaking in, scaling or false key, at night or in an isolated place or by two or more persons, the agent is punished with a sentence of 1 to 2 years imprisonment and corresponding fine, if a more serious penalty is not applicable by virtue of another legal provision.

###### ARTICLE 251

(Entry into place closed to the public)
1. Whoever, without consent or authorization from the rightful person, enters or remains in a closed place intended for public service or enterprise, for transport service or for the exercise of professions or activities, or in any other closed place not freely accessible to the public, is punished with a prison sentence of up to 3 months and corresponding fine.
2. Unauthorized entry into an automobile or other motorized vehicle, aircraft, boat, train or other means of transport, whether publicly or privately owned, is punished with imprisonment of up to 3 months and corresponding fine.

###### ARTICLE 252

(Invasion of private life)
1. It is punished with a prison sentence of up to 1 year and corresponding fine, whoever, without consent and with intent to invade the private life of persons, namely the intimacy of family or sexual life:
a) intercepts, records, registers, uses, transmits or discloses conversation, telephone communication, image, photograph, video, audio, itemized billing, email messages, social network messages or other data transmission platform;
b) captures, photographs, films, manipulates, records or discloses images of persons or of intimate objects or spaces;
c) observes or listens secretly to persons who are in a private place; or
d) discloses facts relating to the private life or serious illness of another person.
2. The act provided for in paragraph d) of the preceding number is not punishable when performed as an adequate means to realize a legitimate and relevant public interest.

###### ARTICLE 253

(Violation of correspondence or communications)
1. Whoever, without consent, opens a parcel, letter or any other writing that is closed and not addressed to them, or gains knowledge, by technical processes,

of its content, or prevents, by any means, it from being received by the addressee, is punished with a prison sentence of up to 1 year and corresponding fine.
2. The same penalty is incurred by whoever, without consent, interferes with the content of telecommunication or gains knowledge of it.
3. Whoever, without consent, discloses the content of letters, parcels, closed writings, or contents of telecommunications referred to in the preceding number, is punished with a sentence of 6 months to 1 year imprisonment and corresponding fine.

###### ARTICLE 254

(Automated database)
Whoever, without due consent, or outside the cases established by law, creates, maintains or uses an automated file of individually identifiable data relating to political, philosophical or ideological convictions, religious faith, party or union affiliation and private life, is punished with a prison sentence of up to 2 years and corresponding fine.

###### ARTICLE 255

(Common provisions)
1. Except as provided in number 2 of article 250 and article 254, criminal proceedings for the crimes provided for in this section depend on a complaint or report by the victim.
2. Criminal proceedings depend on private prosecution when, in the same crimes, the agent is the spouse, ascendant or descendant, adopter or adoptee or relative up to the third degree of the collateral line of the victim or person living with them in conditions analogous to those of spouses.
3. The penalties are increased by half in their maximum limits, if the act is performed:
a) to obtain reward or enrichment, for the agent or for another person, or to cause harm to another person or to the State; or
b) through a means of social communication or computer system intended for dissemination.

##### SECTION II

Invasion of other legal interests

###### ARTICLE 256

(Illegal access)
1. Whoever, without legal permission or without being authorised by the owner or other holder of the right to the system or part thereof, invades another person's device, fixed or mobile, connected or not to a computer network, with the purpose of obtaining non-public information from mail or private electronic communications, access to private data, commercial or industrial secrets, confidential information or unauthorised remote access to the device, is punished with imprisonment from 1 to 2 years and a fine of up to 1 year.
2. The same penalty is incurred by whoever, illegally, produces, sells, distributes or in any other way disseminates or introduces into one or more computer systems devices, programs, an executable set of instructions, a code or other computer data intended to produce the unauthorised actions described in the preceding paragraph.
3. Criminal proceedings depend on a complaint.

###### ARTICLE 257

(Illegal recordings)
1. Whoever, against the will and outside the cases permitted by law, is punished with a penalty of imprisonment of up to 1 year and a corresponding fine:
a) records words uttered by another person and not intended for the public, even if addressed to them;
b) uses or permits the use of the recordings referred to in the preceding subparagraph, even if lawfully produced.
2. Criminal proceedings depend on a complaint by the victim.

###### ARTICLE 258

(Violation and undue exploitation of secrets)
1. Whoever, without consent, reveals another person's secret of which they have become aware by reason of their status, office, employment, profession or art is punished with a penalty of imprisonment of up to 1 year and a corresponding fine.
2. The penalty of imprisonment of up to 2 years is applicable to all those who, exercising any profession that requires a qualification, and being by reason thereof depositaries of secrets entrusted to them, reveal those that come to their knowledge in the exercise of their activity.
3. The preceding provisions apply without prejudice to the penalties for insult or defamation, if applicable.

#### CHAPTER XI

Crimes Against the Family

##### SECTION I

Bigamy, usurpation of civil status and supposed and illegal marriages

###### ARTICLE 259

(Bigamy)
1. Whoever, being married, contracts another marriage without the first having been annulled or dissolved, commits the crime of bigamy, punished with a penalty of imprisonment of up to 2 years and a fine.
2. The same penalty is applied to whoever contracts marriage with a married person.

###### ARTICLE 260

(Usurpation of another person's civil status)
Whoever wilfully usurps the civil status of another person, or who, in order to prejudice the rights of someone, usurps conjugal rights by means of a false marriage, or who for the same purpose pretends to be married, or usurps any family rights, is punished with a penalty of imprisonment from 1 to 2 years.

##### SECTION II

Supposed births

###### ARTICLE 261

(Supposed birth and substitution of newborn)
1. A woman who, without having given birth, presents it as her own, or who, having given birth to a child alive or dead, substitutes it for another, is punished with a penalty of imprisonment from 1 to 3 years.
2. The same penalty shall be imposed on the husband who is aware and consents.
3. Those who participate in this crime are punished as accomplices, according to the general rules.

###### ARTICLE 262

(False declarations relating to birth or death of newborn)
1. False declarations by the parents of a newborn, made with or without their consent, before the competent authority and with the purpose of prejudicing the rights of someone, as well as false declarations made before the same authority and with the same purpose, of the birth and death of a child who never existed, are punished with a penalty of imprisonment from 1 to 3 years.
2. The false declarations referred to in the preceding paragraph, made without intent to prejudice the rights of someone, are punished with a penalty of imprisonment of up to 6 months.

##### SECTION III

Abduction and concealment of minors

###### ARTICLE 263

(Violent or fraudulent abduction of minor under twelve years)
Whoever, by means of violence or fraud, removes or takes, or causes to be removed or taken, a minor under twelve years from the house or place where, with the authorisation of the persons in charge of their custody or direction, they are located, is punished with a penalty of imprisonment from 1 to 5 years.

###### ARTICLE 264

(Compelling a minor to abandon the home of parents or guardians)
1. Whoever compels, by violence or induces by fraud, a minor under eighteen years to abandon the home of their parents or guardians, or of those in charge of their person, or to abandon the place where by their order they are located, or removes or takes them, is punished with a penalty of imprisonment of up to 2 years, without prejudice to the penalty for kidnapping, if applicable.
2. If the minor is under sixteen years, the penalty is from 18 months to 3 years of imprisonment.

###### ARTICLE 265

(Concealment, exchange and leading astray of minors)
1. Whoever conceals or causes to be concealed, or exchanges or causes to be exchanged for another, or leads astray or causes to be led astray a minor under twelve years, is punished with a penalty of imprisonment from 1 to 5 years.
2. If the minor is over twelve and under eighteen years, they are punished with a penalty of imprisonment from 18 months to 3 years, without prejudice to the penalties for kidnapping, if applicable.
3. The same penalty is applied to whoever, being in charge of the person of a minor, does not present them to those who have the right to claim them, nor justifies their disappearance.
4. In all cases enumerated so far in this section, whoever does not show where the minor is located is punished with a penalty of imprisonment from 16 to 20 years.

##### SECTION IV

Exposure or abandonment of minor

###### ARTICLE 266

(Exposure or abandonment of minor)
1. Whoever exposes or abandons a minor under twelve years in any place other than a public establishment intended for the reception of foundlings, is sentenced to a penalty of imprisonment of up to 2 years and a corresponding fine.
2. If the exposure or abandonment is in an isolated place, they are punished with a penalty of imprisonment from 1 to 5 years.

3. If this crime is committed by the ascendant or adopter, or guardian or person in charge of the custody or education of the minor, the penalty is aggravated with the maximum fine.
4. If the exposure or abandonment endangers the life of the minor, or if injury or death results, the penalty is from 2 to 10 years of imprisonment.

###### ARTICLE 267

(Omission of presentation to authority of exposed minor)
Whoever, finding a newborn exposed in any place, or who, finding in an isolated place a minor under twelve years, abandoned, does not present them to the nearest administrative authority, is punished with a penalty of imprisonment from 1 month to 2 years.

###### ARTICLE 268

(Illegal delivery of minor under twelve years)
Whoever, having in their charge the upbringing or education of a minor under twelve years, delivers them to a public establishment, or to another person, without the consent of the one who entrusted them or of the competent authority, is punished with a penalty of imprisonment from 1 month to 1 year and a corresponding fine.

##### SECTION V

Use of minor in begging

###### ARTICLE 269

(Use of minor in begging)
Whoever uses a minor under eighteen years or a psychologically incapable person in begging, is punished with a penalty of imprisonment of up to 2 years and a fine.

### TITLE II

Crimes Against Property In General

#### CHAPTER I

Crimes Against Property

##### SECTION I

Theft

###### ARTICLE 270

(Theft)
1. Whoever, with illegal intention of appropriation, for themselves or for another, subtracts another person's movable property, is punished with a penalty of:
a) imprisonment of up to 6 months and a fine of up to 1 month, if the value of the stolen property does not exceed 10 minimum wages;
b) imprisonment of up to 1 year and a fine of up to 2 months, if it exceeds 10 minimum wages and is not greater than 40 minimum wages;
c) imprisonment of up to 2 years and a fine of up to 6 months, if it exceeds 40 minimum wages and is not greater than 125 minimum wages;
d) imprisonment from 2 to 8 years, with a fine of up to 1 year, if it exceeds 125 minimum wages and is not greater than 500 minimum wages; and
e) Imprisonment from 8 to 12 years, if it exceeds 500 minimum wages.
2. The total of the various portions subtracted by the same agent from the same person, even at different times, is considered as a single theft.

###### ARTICLE 271

(Theft, destruction or misappropriation of one's own deposited property)
The penalties for theft are imposed on anyone who, with unlawful intent of appropriation for themselves or for another, removes a thing belonging to them, when it is pledged or deposited in the possession of someone, or destroys or misappropriates it, when it is seized or deposited in their possession by court order.

###### ARTICLE 272

(Unlawful appropriation in case of accession or found property)
1.	Anyone who unlawfully appropriates another's property that has come into their possession or detention by effect of natural force, error, fortuitous event or by any manner independent of their will, is punished with the penalties for theft, but mitigated under general terms.
2.	The same penalties are incurred by anyone who unlawfully appropriates another's property that they have found.

###### ARTICLE 273

(Aggravation)
Theft is punished with penalties immediately superior to those of article 270, according to value, when there is a concurrence of any one or more of the following circumstances:
1.	ª The offender or any of the offenders carrying, at the time of the crime, visible or concealed weapons;
2.	ª In an inhabited house or one intended for habitation, in a public building or one intended for religious worship, during a religious act or in a cemetery;
3.	ª The object removed being by nature highly dangerous, or of high cultural or religious value or of important scientific, artistic, or historical value and found in a public collection or exhibition or accessible to the public;
4.	ª On the road or public way, being objects transported along it;
5.	ª With breaking and entering, scaling or false keys, in a house not inhabited nor intended for habitation;
6.	ª The agent exploiting the situation of special weakness of the victim, of disaster, accident or public calamity;
7.	ª Any salaried servant or any individual habitually working in the dwelling, workshop, establishment, office, institution or service in which they commit the theft;
8.	ª Innkeepers or any persons who receive and shelter others for money or their agents, boatmen, transporters, or any carriers or their agents, who steal all or part of what was entrusted to them by this title;
9.	ª Involving vehicles, parts or accessories belonging to them or objects or valuables left in them.

###### ARTICLE 274

(Theft, destruction or misappropriation of proceedings, registry books, documents or deposited objects)
1.	Anyone who removes any proceedings or part thereof, registry book or part thereof, or any document, is punished with imprisonment up to 2 years.
2.	The same provision applies to anyone who removes a title, document or piece of proceedings that has been produced or delivered in court.

3.	If the proceedings are criminal and concern a crime for which the law imposes any of the penalties of imprisonment exceeding 2 years, the theft is punished with imprisonment from 2 to 8 years and, if the penalty is less than 2 years imprisonment, the theft is punished with imprisonment from 1 to 2 years.
4.	If the theft of papers or any objects deposited in public depositories or establishments charged by law with guarding these objects, the penalty is aggravated according to general rules.
5.	The provisions of this article apply to anyone who misappropriates or destroys the said papers or objects.

###### ARTICLE 275

(Theft of use of vehicle)
1.	The use, without authorization from the rightful person, of an automobile or other motorized vehicle, aircraft, railway vehicle, boat, bicycle or animal-drawn vehicle, is punished with imprisonment from 1 month to 1 year and corresponding fine.
2.	The penalty shall be aggravated by half in its maximum limit, if with the use of the vehicle damage is caused to it.
3.	Attempt is punishable.

###### ARTICLE 276

(Theft of fluids)
1.	Anyone who, by any means, removes, for personal or third-party consumption, telephone signal, radio, television, internet, voice data, image, video or other intangible goods with economic value, is punished with imprisonment up to 1 year, if no more serious penalty applies.
2.	Attempt is punishable.

###### ARTICLE 277

(Standing for criminal action)
1.	In all cases declared in this section, if the theft does not exceed the amount of twenty minimum wages, and not being habitual, criminal proceedings only take place upon complaint by the injured party.
2.	Criminal action does not take place without complaint by the injured party, when the theft is committed by the agent against their siblings, brothers-in-law or sisters-in-law, parents-in-law or sons-in-law or daughters-in-law, stepfathers, stepmothers or stepchildren, guardians or masters and the proceedings cease as soon as the injured parties so request.

###### ARTICLE 278

(Non-punishment of the crime of theft)
Criminal action does not take place in thefts committed:
a)	By the spouse or person with whom one lives as such, except where there is judicial separation of persons and property;
b)	By the ascendant against the descendant and vice versa; and
c)	By the adopter against the adopted and vice versa.

##### SECTION II

Robbery

###### ARTICLE 279

(Robbery)
1.	Anyone who, with unlawful intent of appropriation for themselves or for another person, removes, or compels the delivery to them of, movable property belonging to another, by means of violence against a person, or threat with imminent danger to life or physical integrity, is punished with the penalty immediately superior to that corresponding to the crime of theft.

2.	Entry into an inhabited house with breaking and entering, scaling or false keys is considered as violence against persons, if they were actually inside at that time.

###### ARTICLE 280

(Aggravation)
1.	The penalty of imprisonment from 12 to 16 years is applied when the robbery:
a)	occurs at night or in an isolated place;
b)	is committed with weapons;
c)	is committed by two or more persons;
d)	concurs with the crime of kidnapping or the agent produces danger to the victim's life or inflicts upon them, at least through negligence, serious injury to physical integrity;
e)	concurs with usurpation of title, or uniform, or insignia of any public servant, civil or military, or alleging false order from any public authority.
2.	The penalty of imprisonment from 16 to 20 years is applied, when the robbery is committed, concurring with the crime of rape.
3.	When the robbery is committed or attempted, concurring with the crime of homicide, it is punished with the penalty of 20 to 24 years imprisonment.

###### ARTICLE 281

(Punishment of robbery in special cases)
If, by the circumstances described in paragraphs a) and c) of number 1 of the preceding article, there is no record of use of weapons and the violence or threat is of little gravity and, further, the value of the thing removed does not exceed ten minimum wages, the penalty of imprisonment from 1 to 3 years and fine up to 1 year may be applied to the agent.

###### ARTICLE 282

(Theft or robbery by creditor from debtor for payment of debt)
If the creditor steals or robs something belonging to their debtor to pay themselves for the debt, this circumstance does not justify the criminal act, but may be considered as a mitigating circumstance.

###### ARTICLE 283

(Breaking and entering, scaling and false keys)
1.	Breaking and entering is the breaking, fracture or destruction, in whole or in part, of any construction that serves to close or prevent entry, externally or internally, to a house or enclosed place dependent on it, or of furniture intended to keep any objects.
2.	Scaling is the introduction into a house or enclosed place dependent on it, over roofs, doors, walls, or any constructions that serve to close the entry or passage, and also through underground opening not intended for entry.
3.	The following are considered false keys:
a)	those imitated, counterfeited or altered;
b)	the genuine ones, existing fortuitously or surreptitiously outside the power of the person who has the right to use them;
c)	lock picks or any instruments that can serve to open locks.
4.	The removal of closed furniture, which serves for the security of the objects it contains, and committed inside the house or building, is considered done with the circumstance of breaking and entering, even if the furniture is opened or broken into in another place.

##### SECTION III

Usurpation of immovable property and removal of boundary markers

###### ARTICLE 284

(Usurpation of immovable property)
1.	If any person, by means of violence or threat against persons, invades or occupies immovable property, arrogating to themselves the ownership or possession, or the use thereof, without it belonging to them, is punished with a penalty of imprisonment up to 2 years and corresponding fine.
2.	The penalty provided for in the preceding number is applicable to whoever, by the means indicated in the preceding number, diverts or dams waters, without having the right to do so, with the intention of obtaining, for themselves or for another person, illegitimate benefit.

###### ARTICLE 285

(Removal of boundary markers)
1.	Any person who, without authority of the competent entity, or without consent of the parties to whom the right of use and exploitation of the land belongs, removes a marker placed in any demarcation, or in any way suppresses or alters it, is punished with a penalty of imprisonment of 1 month.
2.	Markers are considered to be any constructions, beacons or signs intended to establish the boundaries between different parcels, as well as trees planted for the same purpose or recognized as such.

#### CHAPTER II

Crimes Against Patrimonial Rights

##### SECTION I

Harmful administration

###### ARTICLE 286

(Harmful administration)
Whoever is entrusted with administering or managing interests or patrimonial assets of a third party, and in violation of the duties inherent to the function, causes patrimonial damage to the latter, is punished with a penalty of imprisonment from 1 to 5 years, if a more serious penalty is not applicable.

##### SECTION II

Fraud

###### ARTICLE 287

(Fraud)
Whoever, with the intention of obtaining for themselves or for a third party illegitimate enrichment, by means of error or deception about facts that they cunningly provoked, causes another person to perform acts that cause them, or cause another person, patrimonial damage, is punished with imprisonment from 1 to 2 years.

###### ARTICLE 288

(Aggravation)
Fraud is punished with penalties from 1 to 3 years of imprisonment when one or more of the following circumstances occur:
a)	the agent uses a fraudulent artifice fundamental to obtaining financing or credit intended for personal use or that of a company or establishment;
b)	the agent uses a cunning or deceptive means, and, in this way, induces, deceives or leads another person to participate in false financial investments, with the purpose of obtaining for themselves or for a third party illegitimate enrichment, with patrimonial damage to another person;

c)	the agent uses fraudulent means essential to obtaining funds, subsidies or pensions;
d)	the agent causes themselves or another person, fraudulently, injury to physical integrity or damage or aggravates its consequences caused by an accident whose risk is covered in order to receive, or cause another person to receive, insurance;
e)	the agent carries out a sale or in any way alienates, mortgages or pledges land.

###### ARTICLE 289

(Computer and communications fraud)
1.	Whoever, with the intention of obtaining for themselves or for a third party illegitimate enrichment, causes another person patrimonial damage, by interfering with the result of data processing or by means of incorrect structuring of a computer program, incorrect or incomplete use of data, use of data without authorization or intervention in any other unauthorized manner in the processing, is punished with a penalty of imprisonment up to 3 years or with a fine.
2.	The same penalty is applicable to whoever, with the intention of obtaining for themselves or for a third party an illegitimate benefit, causes another person patrimonial damage, by using programs, electronic devices or other means that, separately or together, are intended to diminish, alter or prevent, totally or partially, the normal functioning or operation of telecommunications services.

###### ARTICLE 290

(Fraud relating to work or employment)
Whoever, with the intention of obtaining for themselves or for a third party illegitimate enrichment, causes another person patrimonial damage, through enticement or promise of work or employment in the country or abroad, is punished with a penalty of imprisonment up to 1 year and corresponding fine.

###### ARTICLE 291

(Common provisions)
1.	Attempt is punishable.
2.	The provisions of article 278 are correspondingly applicable.
3.	Criminal proceedings depend on a complaint by the victim.

##### SECTION III

Issuance of cheque without provision

###### ARTICLE 292

(Issuance of cheque without provision and equivalent crime)
1.	Whoever issues a cheque that, when presented for payment within the terms and time period legally established, is not fully paid for lack of provision, is punished with a penalty of imprisonment from 1 to 5 years and fine up to 1 year.
2.	Without prejudice to what is provided for in specific legislation, whoever incurs the penalties for the crime of issuance of cheque without provision:
a)	prohibits the drawee bank, within the time period for presentation for payment under the terms postulated in the Uniform Law Relating to Cheques, from paying a cheque issued and delivered, without there being a relevant legal reason;
b)	endorses a cheque that they received, knowing of the lack of provision.
3.	Criminal proceedings depend on a complaint by the victim.

###### ARTICLE 293

(Extinction of criminal liability)
1.	Liability for the commission of the crime of issuance of cheque without cover or equivalent is extinguished with payment, made until the beginning of the trial hearing at first instance, directly to the bearer of the cheque, of the amount thereof plus interest calculated at the maximum rate practiced at the time of payment by the drawee bank for credit operations, further increased by 10 percentage points, and a deposit may be made to their order if the bearer of the cheque refuses to receive or give receipt.
2.	After the time period provided for in the preceding number has elapsed, the payment or deposits provided for therein, made until the closure of the discussion of the case, determine the suspension of the penalty that may be applicable.
3.	In any case, court costs and the justice fee are fixed at the expense of the agent.

##### SECTION IV

Crimes relating to other payment instruments

###### ARTICLE 294

(Fraud relating to electronic payment instruments and channels)
1.	Punished with a penalty of imprisonment from 1 to 3 years and fine up to 1 year is whoever:
a)	falsifies an electronic payment instrument or channel;
b)	illegally accesses an electronic payment system, by means of improper violation of security mechanisms;
c)	installs objects that affect the functioning of the electronic payment channel or system, aiming to obtain, adulterate or destroy data or information;
d)	illegally appropriates an electronic payment instrument of another person, including the corresponding secret code;
e)	possesses, holds, imports, exports, receives, transports, sells or transfers to third parties electronic payment instruments obtained improperly or that have been the object of counterfeiting or falsification; and
f)	creates computer programs, instruments, objects and other means deliberately prepared for the commission of offences related to electronic payment instruments.
2.	An electronic payment instrument is considered to be the device or electronic record that allows the user to transfer funds or pay a beneficiary.
3.	When the actions described in the preceding numbers relate to data recorded or incorporated in a bank payment card or in any other device that allows access to a payment system or means, to a communications system or to a conditional access service, the penalty is imprisonment up to 5 years and fine up to 1 year.
4.	Whoever imports, distributes, sells or holds for commercial purposes any device that allows access to a payment system or means on which any of the actions provided for in number 2 has been carried out, is punished with a penalty of imprisonment up to 5 years.

###### ARTICLE 295

(Abuse of electronic means of payment)
Whoever, by abusing the possibility conferred by the possession of electronic means of payment, causes the issuer to make a payment or causes damage to the issuer or to a third party, is punished with the penalties for theft.

##### SECTION V

Breach of trust, simulations and other types of fraud

###### ARTICLE 296

(Breach of trust)
1.	Whoever, unlawfully, appropriates movable property that has been delivered to him under a title not transferring ownership, is punished with the penalties for theft.
2.	The same penalty applies to whoever, under the terms of this article, encumbers or pledges the said property, when thereby prejudicing or potentially prejudicing the owner, possessor or holder.

###### ARTICLE 297

(Simulated transaction)
1.	Whoever engages in a simulated transaction, to the prejudice of a third party or the State, is punished with a penalty of imprisonment from 1 to 2 years and corresponding fine.
2.	Criminal proceedings, when the State is not the injured party, depend on a complaint.

###### ARTICLE 298

(Extortion and blackmail)
1.	Whoever, by means of verbal or written threat to make revelations or injurious or defamatory imputations, or under the pretext of not making them, extorts from another person valuables, or coerces to write, sign, deliver, destroy and falsify, or in any way render useless a writing or title that constitutes, produces or proves an obligation or discharge, is punished with imprisonment up to 1 year and corresponding fine, without prejudice to that proclaimed for the penalties for theft, if there is material damage.
2.	If the valuables are not extorted, nor the title or writing signed, delivered, written, destroyed, falsified, or in any way rendered useless, the penalty is imprisonment up to 3 months and fine up to 1 month.

###### ARTICLE 299

(Usury)
Whoever, with the intention of obtaining a patrimonial benefit for himself or for another person, exploits the situation of need, dependence, psychic anomaly, incapacity, ineptitude, inexperience or weakness of character of the contracting party and causes him to oblige himself to grant or promise, in any form, in his favour or in favour of another person, a pecuniary advantage that is, according to the circumstances of the case, manifestly disproportionate to the counter-performance, is punished with a penalty of imprisonment up to 2 years and corresponding fine.

###### ARTICLE 300

(Loan sharking)
1.	Whoever, without authorization from the competent authority, engages in granting loans of money to third parties, with a character of habituality and with charging of interest, is punished with a penalty of imprisonment from 2 to 8 years and fine up to 1 year.

2.	The penalty of imprisonment up to 2 years and fine up to 1 year is incurred by whoever carries out collection of debts on behalf of the loan shark.

###### ARTICLE 301

(Fraud in sales)
1.	The following is punished with a penalty of imprisonment up to 2 years and fine up to 1 year:
a)	whoever deceives the buyer about the nature of the thing sold;
b)	whoever deceives the buyer, selling him falsified merchandise, or goods altered with some substance, even if not harmful to health, to increase the weight or volume;
c)	whoever, using false weights or false measures, deceives the buyer.
2.	The mere possession of false weights or false measures in warehouses, factories, commercial establishments or in any place where merchandise is exposed for sale, is punished with a fine up to 1 year.
3.	Weights and measures that the law does not authorize are considered false.
4.	The objects of the crime, if they still belong to the seller, are forfeited in favour of the State, and likewise the false weights and measures are forfeited and rendered useless.

###### ARTICLE 302

(Common provisions)
1.	With the exception of that provided in article 300, the provisions of articles 277 and 278 are correspondingly applicable to the crimes of this section.
2.	The attempt is punishable.

#### CHAPTER III

Crime of Receiving and Material Assistance

###### ARTICLE 303

(Receiving)
1.	Whoever, with the intention of obtaining for himself or for another person patrimonial advantage, conceals a thing that was obtained by another person through an unlawful act typical against patrimony, receives it in pledge, acquires it under any title, holds it, preserves, transmits or contributes to transmitting it, or in any way secures, for himself or for another person, its possession, is punished with a penalty of imprisonment from 1 to 8 years and fine up to 1 year.
2.	Whoever, without having previously ascertained its legitimate provenance, acquires or receives, under any title, a thing that, by its quality or by the condition of the person who offers it, or by the amount of the proposed price, reasonably gives rise to suspicion that it comes from an unlawful act typical against patrimony, is punished with a penalty of imprisonment up to 2 years and corresponding fine.
3.	If the agent makes receiving a way of life, he is punished with the penalty of paragraph 1, but aggravated by one third in its maximum limit.
4.	Values or products directly obtained with them are equated to things.

###### ARTICLE 305

(Legitimacy for criminal action)
Criminal proceedings depend on notification or complaint by the injured party, except if the receiver is a spouse or person with whom he lives in an analogous situation, de facto union, ascendant, descendant, adopter, adoptee, relative or in-law up to the 2nd degree of the agent, in which case criminal action shall depend on private prosecution.

### TITLE III

Crimes of Common Danger

#### CHAPTER I

Fire and Damage

##### SECTION I

Fire

###### ARTICLE 306

(Fire, flood and other especially dangerous conduct)
1.	Whoever causes a fire, namely by setting fire to a building, construction, vehicle or means of transport, bush or grove, and thereby creates actual danger to life, to the physical integrity of another person or actual danger to the patrimonial property of others, is punished with a penalty of imprisonment from 1 to 5 years and fine up to 1 year.
2.	The same penalty is incurred by whoever causes an explosion, releases toxic or asphyxiating gases, emits radiation or releases radioactive substances, causes a flood or landslide or collapse of a construction, and thereby creates the danger described in the preceding paragraph.
3.	If danger to the life or physical integrity of persons does not occur, the penalty to be applied is imprisonment up to 2 years.
4.	If the object of the crime is a field, plantation, bush, grove, forest, crop and it consists of small burnings for purposes of cultivating the land, with no harmful results occurring beyond the area intended for cultivation, the agent may be exempted from penalty.

###### ARTICLE 307

(Aggravation by result)
Whoever commits the crime of fire, in any of the cases enumerated in the preceding article, causing the death of any person who, at the moment when the fire was set, was in the place set on fire, is punished with a penalty of imprisonment from 8 to 12 years.

###### ARTICLE 308

(Fire in unspecified objects)
Fire in objects not included in this section is punished by applying the provisions relating to damage, with an aggravating circumstance, according to the general rules.

###### ARTICLE 309

###### ARTICLE 304

(Material assistance)
Whoever assists another person to take advantage of the benefit of a thing obtained by means of an unlawful act typical against patrimony, is punished with the penalties of paragraph 2 of the preceding article, but attenuated under the general terms.

(Common provisions)
1.	The attempt is punishable in the crimes of this section.
2.	Negligence is punishable with imprisonment up to 1 year and corresponding fine, without prejudice to conviction for the contravention, if applicable.
3.	Criminal proceedings depend on a complaint by the injured party, except when the State or another public entity is injured.

##### SECTION II

Damage

###### ARTICLE 310

(Damage)
Whoever destroys, renders useless, causes to disappear, or in any way damages property totally or partially belonging to another, is punished with imprisonment up to 2 years and corresponding fine, taking into account the value of the damage caused.

###### ARTIGO 311

(Aggravation)
A penalty of imprisonment from 1 to 5 years and a fine of up to 1 year shall apply if:
a) the act results in the destruction, derailment or disruption, in whole or in part, of any railway, or the placing upon it of any object that obstructs circulation, or that is intended to cause the train to leave the rails;
b) the act results in the destruction of pipelines for the transport of water, hydrocarbons, fuels, solid or liquid bulk goods and other goods;
c) the act results in the destruction of means of transmission of electrical energy and communications, or opposition with violence or threat to their restoration;
d) the act is committed in an archive, registry, museum, library or scientific institution or on goods of important historical, artistic, cultural or scientific value, or, in any way, intended for public use and utility;
e) poisonous, flammable or corrosive substances are used; or
f) the damage is caused with the purpose of preventing the free and legitimate exercise of public authority, or out of revenge against those who have contributed to the execution of the laws.

###### ARTIGO 312

(Involuntary damage)
If, by violation or failure to observe police and administrative measures contained in laws and regulations, and without malicious intent, someone causes fire or any damage to the movable or immovable property of another, they shall be punished with a fine of up to 3 months, without prejudice to the penalties decreed in the same laws or regulations for the contravention.

###### ARTIGO 313

(Legitimacy for criminal proceedings)
1. Criminal proceedings for the crimes of damage provided for in this section depend on a complaint, except in relation to the contravention or when the State or any legal person under public law is the victim.
2. Criminal proceedings depend on a complaint and also on private prosecution when, in the same crimes, the perpetrator is the spouse, ascendant or descendant, adopter or adoptee or relative up to the third degree of the collateral line of the victim or person living with them in conditions analogous to those of spouses.
3. Compensation for damages up to the holding of the trial hearing at first instance extinguishes the criminal proceedings, without prejudice to the contravention, if applicable.

#### CAPÍTULO II

Crimes Against the Environment

##### SECÇÃO I

Typical acts

###### ARTIGO 314

(Illegal prospecting and exploitation of mineral resources)
Whoever, without authorization from the competent bodies or in violation of the license granted, prospects, exploits or mines mineral resources, or fails to carry out natural recovery of the exploited area, shall be punished with imprisonment from 1 to 5 years and a fine of up to 2 years.

###### ARTIGO 315

(Toxic substances)
Whoever produces, processes, packages, imports, exports, markets, supplies, transports, keeps, stores, abandons or uses toxic, dangerous or harmful substances to human health, or operates enterprises with a potential degree of pollution, without an environmental license or in non-observance thereof, shall be punished with imprisonment of up to 3 years and a fine of up to 2 years.

###### ARTIGO 316

(Dissemination of diseases)
Whoever disseminates diseases, pests or other species that may affect or cause damage to agriculture, livestock, fauna, flora and the ecosystem, shall be punished with imprisonment from 1 to 2 years and a corresponding fine.

###### ARTIGO 317

(Pollution)
1. Whoever, not observing legal or regulatory provisions or obligations imposed by the competent authority, produces, deposits in the soil or subsoil, discharges into water or into the atmosphere, any toxic or polluting substances or in any other way degrades the environment, shall be punished with imprisonment of up to 2 years and a corresponding fine.
2. The same penalty applies to whoever, in a serious manner, causes noise pollution through the use of technical devices or installations, in particular machines or land, river, sea or air vehicles of any nature.

##### SECÇÃO II

Common provisions

###### ARTIGO 318

(Complicity)
The accomplice shall receive the penalty applicable to the perpetrator.

###### ARTIGO 319

(Attempt)
Attempt is punishable.

###### ARTIGO 320

(Negligence)
Negligence in crimes against the environment is always punished, without prejudice to the penalties decreed in laws or regulations for the contravention.

###### ARTIGO 321

(Desistance from execution of crime)
Whoever, being involved in the preparation of an environmental crime, reveals it to the authorities before the commencement of its execution, or in time to avoid its consequences, shall be exempt from penalty.

### TÍTULO IV

Crimes Against Public Faith

#### CAPÍTULO I

Crimes of Falsification

##### SECÇÃO I

Preliminary provision

###### ARTIGO 322

(Falsification of documents)
Whoever, with intent to cause harm to another person or to the State, to obtain for themselves or for another person an illegitimate benefit, or to prepare, facilitate, execute or conceal another crime, shall be punished with imprisonment from 1 to 2 years and a corresponding fine:
a) manufactures a document, totally or partially, false;
b) imitates, feigns or abuses the handwriting, signature, firm, initial or sign of another;
c) makes it appear in an act that persons intervened who did not participate in it or attributes to those who intervened declarations they did not make, or different from those they actually made;
d) fails to tell the truth in the narration or declaration of facts essential to the validity of a document, or in that of those which it is intended to certify;
e) alters the true dates;
f) makes in a true document any alteration or insertion that changes its meaning or value;
g) certifies or recognizes as true false facts;
h) issues a transcript, certificate, copy that must be authentic, or public form of a supposed document, or in which something different from what is found in the original is declared;
i) inserts any act in a protocol, book or official register, or registering, without legal existence, any act of the nature of those for which the law establishes registration, or canceling what should subsist;
j) manufactures provisions, obligations, or releases in any deed, title, diploma, record or writing, which by law must have the same faith as public deeds; or
k) adds, changes or diminishes in any part of the document, after its completion, so as to alter the substance or intention thereof by the addition, diminution or change of provisions, obligations or releases, or of the facts that such document is intended to certify or authenticate.

###### ARTIGO 323

(Aggravation)
A penalty of 1 to 8 years of imprisonment shall apply if the falsification:
a) concerns an authentic or authenticated document; or
b) the act is committed by a public servant in the exercise of their functions.

###### ARTIGO 324

(Use of false document)
Whoever makes use of the false documents declared in the preceding articles, or fraudulently has any act registered or any registration canceled, shall be convicted as if they were the author of the falsification.

###### ARTIGO 325

(Use of another person's identification or travel document)
1. Outside the cases declared in the preceding article, whoever, with intent to cause harm to another person or to the State, to obtain for themselves or for another person an illegitimate benefit, or to prepare, facilitate, execute or conceal another crime, uses an identification or travel document issued in favor of another person, shall be punished with imprisonment of up to 2 years and a fine.
2. The same penalty shall be incurred by whoever, with intent to make possible the act described in the preceding number, provides an identification or travel document to a person in whose favor it was not issued.

##### SECÇÃO II

Falsification of currency, credit instruments and stamped values

###### ARTIGO 326

(Counterfeiting of currency)
1. Whoever commits counterfeiting of currency, with intent to put it into circulation as legitimate, shall be punished with imprisonment from 8 to 12 years.
2. Whoever, with intent to put it into circulation, falsifies or alters the face value of legitimate currency to a higher value shall be punished with imprisonment from 2 to 8 years.

###### ARTIGO 327

(Depreciation of the value of metallic currency)
1. Whoever, with the intention of putting it into circulation as whole, depreciates legitimate metallic currency, diminishing its value by any means, is punished with imprisonment up to 2 years.
2. With the same penalty is punished whoever, without legal authorization and with the intention of passing it or putting it into circulation, manufactures metallic currency with the same or greater value than that of the legitimate one.
3. The attempt is punishable.

###### ARTIGO 328

(Passing of counterfeit currency in concert with the counterfeiter)
1. The penalties established in articles 326 and 327 are correspondingly applicable to whoever, in concert with the agent of the acts described therein, passes or puts into circulation by any means, including exposure for sale, the said currencies.
2. The attempt is punishable.

###### ARTIGO 329

(Passing of counterfeit currency)
1. Is punished with imprisonment from 8 to 12 years, whoever, by any means, including exposure for sale, passes or puts into circulation, as legitimate or intact, counterfeit or falsified currency.
2. Is punished with imprisonment from 2 to 8 years, whoever, by any means, including exposure for sale, passes or puts into circulation, as legitimate or intact, depreciated metallic currency, at its full value, or metallic currency with the same or greater value than that of the legitimate one, but manufactured without legal authorization.

3. If the agent only became aware that the currency is counterfeit or falsified after having received it, he is punished:
a) in the case of number 1, with imprisonment up to 2 years;
b) in the case of number 2, with imprisonment up to 1 year.

###### ARTIGO 330

(Acquisition of counterfeit currency to be put into circulation)
1. Is punished with imprisonment from 8 to 12 years, whoever acquires, receives on deposit, imports or otherwise introduces into Mozambique, for himself or for another person, with the intention of, by any means, including exposure for sale, passing it or putting it into circulation, as legitimate or intact, counterfeit or falsified currency.
3. Whoever, in the circumstances described in the previous number, acquires, receives on deposit, imports or otherwise introduces into Mozambique, for himself or for another person, with the intention of, by any means, including exposure for sale, passing it or putting it into circulation, as legitimate or intact, depreciated metallic currency, at its full value, or metallic currency with the same or greater value than that of the legitimate one, but manufactured without legal authorization, is punished with imprisonment from 2 to 8 years.

###### ARTIGO 331

(Instruments equivalent to currency)
1. For the purposes of the provisions of articles 326 to 329, the following are equivalent to currency:
a) credit instruments consisting, by force of law, of a type of paper and printing specially intended to guarantee them against the danger of imitations and which, by their nature and purpose, cannot, by themselves, fail to incorporate a patrimonial value; and
b) debit and credit cards.
2. The provisions of the previous number do not cover the falsification of elements for whose guarantee and identification the use of paper or printing is not specially intended.

###### ARTIGO 332

(Counterfeiting of stamped values)
1. Whoever commits counterfeiting or falsification of stamped or sealed values whose supply is exclusive to the State, namely fiscal or postal stamps, with the intention of using them or of, by any means, including exposure for sale, putting them into circulation as legitimate or intact, is punished with imprisonment from 2 to 8 years.
2. Is punished with imprisonment from 1 to 3 years or with a fine, whoever:
a) uses as legitimate or intact the said stamped or sealed values, when counterfeit or falsified, or
b) with the intention referred to in the previous number, acquires, receives on deposit, imports or otherwise introduces into Mozambique, for himself or for another person, the said stamped or sealed values, when counterfeit or falsified,
3. If, in the case of paragraph a) of the previous number, the agent only became aware that the stamped or sealed values were counterfeit or falsified after having received them, he is punished with imprisonment up to 2 years.
4. If the falsification consists of making disappear from the said stamped or sealed values the sign that they have already been used, the agent is punished with imprisonment up to 1 year.

##### SECÇÃO III

Falsification of seals, stamps and marks

###### ARTIGO 333

(Falsification of seal, stamp, mark or seal of authority)
1. Whoever falsifies a seal, stamp, mark or seal of any authority or public office, introduces them into the country, or makes use of them, which is not specifically incriminated in another article, is punished with imprisonment from 2 to 8 years.
2. The penalty of the previous number is applied to whoever falsifies stamped paper, seal or postal stamps, or other stamped objects, whose supply is exclusive to the State, and to those who fraudulently introduce them into the country, issue them, pass them, expose them for sale or make use of them.

###### ARTIGO 334

(Use of false marks, stamps or seals)
1. Whoever commits any falsification, using falsified marks, seals or stamps of assay or appraisers, whose certificates have legal force in court, is punished with imprisonment from 1 to 2 years, without prejudice to any other penalty, if applicable.
2. If the falsified marks, seals, or stamps are of any industrial or commercial establishment, the penalty is imprisonment up to 2 years, without prejudice to a more serious penalty, if applicable, and subject to reparation, according to the general rules.
3. The same penalty is imposed on whoever exposes for sale or puts into circulation objects marked with supposed or altered names, or who has placed or caused to appear in any way on manufactured objects the name or firm of a factory other than that in which the manufacture took place.
4. The penalty of no. 2 is also imposed on whoever makes disappear from seal or postal stamps, or from tickets for the transport of persons or things, the sign that they have already been used, or makes use of them in this state.
5. Whoever, in tickets or tokens for admission to a public establishment or place, or in lottery receipts or in the respective list, and with the fraudulent purpose of obtaining for himself or for another some profit, or of harming a third person, falsifies the numbering, date or value, or makes use of them, or sells them or exposes them for sale, is punished with imprisonment up to 1 year.

###### ARTIGO 335

(Falsification by illicit use of legitimate instruments)
The penalties declared in the previous articles of this chapter are applicable, according to the various cases designated therein, to whoever, in order to execute any falsification to the prejudice of the State or of any person, makes use of legitimate instruments that have been entrusted to him, or that he has had in his possession in any way.

##### SECÇÃO IV

Computer falsification and related crimes

###### ARTIGO 336

(Computer falsification)
1. Whoever introduces, modifies, deletes or suppresses in an intentional and illegitimate manner computer data, producing non-genuine data or documents, with the intention that these be considered or used for legal purposes as if they were, is punished with imprisonment from 1 to 5 years and a fine up to 1 year.

2. Whoever, acting with the intention of causing harm to another or of obtaining an illegitimate benefit, for himself or for a third party, uses a document produced from computer data that was the object of the acts referred to in no. 1 or a card or other device in which the data object of the acts referred to in the previous number are recorded or incorporated, is punished with the penalties provided for in one and the other number, respectively.
3. Whoever imports, distributes, sells or holds for commercial purposes any device that allows access to a communications system or to a conditional access service, on which any of the actions provided for in the previous numbers has been performed, is punished with imprisonment from 2 to 8 years.

###### ARTICLE 337

(Interference with data)
1. Whoever alters, deteriorates, renders useless, erases, suppresses, destroys or, in any way, alters computer data, is punished with a prison sentence of 1 to 2 years and a corresponding fine.
2. The same penalty applies to whoever, by introducing or transmitting computer data or, in any other way, installing vulnerabilities, interferes with the functioning of a computer system, intentionally causing damage to someone.

###### ARTICLE 338

(Interference with systems)
Whoever, without legal permission or without being authorised by the owner, by another holder of the right to the system or part thereof, hinders, prevents, interrupts or seriously disturbs the functioning of a computer system, through the introduction, transmission, deterioration, damaging, alteration, erasure, prevention of access or suppression of programs or other computer data or any other form of interference with a computer system, is punished with a prison sentence of up to 2 years and a fine of up to 1 year.

###### ARTICLE 339

(Abusive use of devices)
Incurs a prison sentence of 1 to 2 years, whoever illegitimately produces, sells, distributes, imports or in any other way disseminates or introduces into one or more computer systems devices, programs or other computer data intended to produce the unauthorised actions described in the preceding number.

##### SECTION V

Provision common to the preceding sections of this title

###### ARTICLE 340

(Mitigation and waiver of penalty)
1. In crimes of falsity it is always a mitigating circumstance that the public or private document, or falsified object, has not been used, or that the use thereof has not resulted in the damage or benefit that determined the falsity; including in the case where the presenter of a false document in court has declared to desist from it in accordance with civil law, after being accused of falsity.

2. The penalties determined in the articles of the preceding sections of this title, against the use of the false thing, do not apply when the person who used it did not know of the falsification.

###### ARTICLE 341

(Destruction of instruments and forfeiture of objects of the crime)
In all crimes of falsity, the destruction of instruments specially intended for committing them shall be ordered in the conviction sentence, as well as of the objects of the same crimes that have been seized, if they have been found.

#### CHAPTER II

Assumed or Usurped Names, Attire, Employments and Titles

###### ARTICLE 342

(Illegal change and use of false name)
1. Whoever changes name, without this change being legally authorised with the formalities determined by civil law, is sentenced to a prison sentence of up to 1 year and a corresponding fine, without prejudice to the reparation of any damages thereby caused.
2. Is punished with a prison sentence of 1 to 6 months and a fine of up to 1 month, whoever, by taking a false name, attempts to evade, in any way, the legal surveillance of the public authority, or causes any damage to the State or to private individuals.
3. The use of an assumed name may be temporarily authorised by competent authority for just cause.

###### ARTICLE 343

(Improper use of assumed titles, designation, insignia or uniform)
1. Whoever arrogates any decoration, honorary title or other established by law, which does not belong to them, is punished with a prison sentence of up to 3 months and a fine of up to 1 month.
2. Whoever, illegitimately and with the intention of making it believed that they belong to them, uses or wears a designation, insignia, uniform or attire proper to a public service function is punished with a prison sentence of up to 6 months and a corresponding fine.
3. If the designation, insignia, uniform or attire is exclusive to a person exercising public authority, the agent is punished with a prison sentence of up to 1 year and a corresponding fine.

###### ARTICLE 344

(Unlawful exercise of public functions or titled profession)
1. Whoever, without title or legitimate cause, exercises functions proper to a public servant or to a person belonging to the defence and security forces, paramilitary forces, police forces, arrogating such quality, is punished with a prison sentence of 2 to 8 years and a fine of up to 1 year, without prejudice to the penalties for falsity, if applicable.
2. If the functions are of a military command although the offender is not military, the provisions of military laws are observed, and is punished with the penalty of the preceding number aggravated, if a more serious penalty does not apply.
3. Whoever exercises, against law or regulation, acts proper to a profession without possessing the corresponding official title, diploma or authorisation that, legally, qualifies for such exercise, is punished with a prison sentence of 6 months to 2 years and a corresponding fine.

### TITLE V

Crimes Against Public Order and Tranquillity

#### CHAPTER I

Public Incitement and Criminal Association

###### ARTICLE 345

(Public incitement to a crime)
1. Whoever, in a public meeting, through a means of social communication, by dissemination of writing or any other means, provokes or incites the commission of a specific crime, is punished with a prison sentence of 1 to 3 years or with a fine of up to 2 years, if a more serious penalty does not apply by virtue of another legal provision.
2. Whoever, for religious motivations, incites another or participates in acts of violence and disturbance of public order, is punished with a prison sentence of 1 to 5 years.
3. The penalty cannot exceed that provided for the typical unlawful act committed.

###### ARTICLE 346

(Public apology for crime)
1. Whoever, in a public meeting, through a means of social communication, by dissemination of writing or any other means, rewards or praises another person for having committed a crime, in a manner adequate to create danger of the commission of another crime of the same kind, is punished with a prison sentence of up to 6 months and a corresponding fine, if a more serious penalty does not apply by virtue of another legal provision.
2. The provision of number 3 of the preceding article is correspondingly applicable.

###### ARTICLE 347

(Threat with commission of crime, abuse and simulation of danger signals)
1. Whoever, by means of threat with the commission of a crime, or by making it believed simulatedly that a crime is going to be committed, causes alarm or unrest among the population, is punished with a prison sentence of up to 2 years and a fine of up to 1 year.
2. With a prison sentence of up to 1 year and a fine of up to 6 months, is punished whoever abusively uses an alarm or distress signal or call, or simulatedly makes it believed that assistance from others is necessary by virtue of disaster, danger or situation of collective necessity.

###### ARTICLE 348

(Criminal association)
1. Whoever promotes or founds or participates in a group, organisation or association, being a set of two or more persons acting in concert over a certain period of time and whose purpose or activity is directed to the commission of one or more crimes is punished with a prison sentence of 1 to 8 years.
2. The same penalty is incurred by whoever supports or facilitates the activities of these groups, organisations or associations, namely by supplying weapons, ammunition or vehicles, receiving, keeping or acquiring the proceeds of crimes or making available places for keeping the referred proceeds or for meetings and hiding places of the group or any of its members.
3. Whoever directs or leads the groups, organisations or associations referred to in the preceding numbers is punished with a prison sentence of 8 to 12 years in prison.

4. The referred penalties may be specially mitigated or punishment may not take place if the agent prevents or seriously endeavours to prevent the continuation of the groups, organisations or associations, or communicates their existence to the authority so that it can prevent the commission of crimes.

#### CHAPTER II

Participation in Riot, Disobedience to Order of Dispersal and Others

##### SECTION I

Participation in riot

###### ARTICLE 349

(Participation in riot)
1. Whoever takes part in a riot, during which violence is collectively committed against person or property, is punished with imprisonment up to 1 year and a fine up to 6 months, if a more severe penalty is not applicable by virtue of another legal provision.
2. If the agent has provoked or directed the riot, he is punished with imprisonment up to 2 years and a corresponding fine.
3. The agent is not punished if he has withdrawn from the riot by order or admonition of the authority without having committed or provoked violence.

###### ARTICLE 350

(Participation in armed riot)
1. The minimum and maximum limits of the penalties provided for in numbers 1 and 2 of the preceding article are doubled if the riot is armed.
2. A riot is considered armed when one of the participants carries a firearm or objects capable of being used as such.
3. For the purposes of the provisions of the preceding number, a riot is not considered armed:
a) in which the weapons are brought accidentally and without intention of use; or
b) when the participants who bring weapons immediately withdraw or are expelled.
4. Whoever brings a weapon without the knowledge of the others is punished as if he actually participated in an armed riot.
5. The provisions of number 3 of the preceding article are correspondingly applicable.

###### ARTICLE 351

(Disobedience to order of dispersal of public meeting)
1. Whoever does not obey a lawful order to withdraw from a gathering or public meeting, given by a competent authority with warning that disobedience constitutes a crime, is punished with imprisonment up to 1 year and a fine up to 6 months.
2. If the disobedient person is the promoter of the meeting or gathering, he is punished with imprisonment up to 2 years and a fine up to 1 year.

##### SECTION II

Resistance

###### ARTICLE 352

(Illegal resistance and coercion of public servant)
1. Whoever employs violence, including threat or offense to physical integrity, against a public servant or member of the Defense and Security Forces, to oppose him from performing an act relating to the exercise of his functions, or to compel him to perform an act relating to the exercise of his functions, but contrary to his duties, is punished with imprisonment from 1 to 2 years and a fine up to 1 year.
2. The same penalty is applicable to whoever disobeys a stop signal and directs against a public servant or member of the Defense and Security Forces a vehicle, with or without motor, that he drives on a public road or equivalent, or a vessel, that he pilots in inland river or maritime waters, to oppose him from performing an act relating to the exercise of his functions, or to compel him to perform an act relating to the exercise of his functions, but contrary to his duties, if a more severe penalty is not applicable by virtue of another legal provision.
3. If the means employed for the resistance, or the object thereof constitute a crime, to which a more severe penalty than those established in this article is applicable, the general rules for the concurrence of crimes are observed.

##### SECTION III

Disobedience

###### ARTICLE 353

(Disobedience)
1. Whoever fails to obey lawful orders or mandates of the public authority or its agents, is punished with imprisonment up to 6 months and a corresponding fine, if a different penalty is not established by law or provision of equal force.
2. This provision includes those who infringe the determinations of edicts of the competent authority, which have been duly published.

###### ARTICLE 354

(Qualified disobedience)
1. Whoever refuses or fails to perform the services or provide the assistance that are required in case of flagrant offense or to prevent the escape of a prisoner, or in circumstances of tumult, shipwreck, flood, fire or other calamity, or of any accidents in which public tranquility may be endangered, is punished with imprisonment up to 1 year and a corresponding fine.
2. The same penalty is applied when the disobedience is committed in the capacity of witness, expert, interpreter, guardian or member of the family council.

#### CHAPTER III

Violation of Public Provisions

###### ARTICLE 355

(Embezzlement or destruction of objects placed under public power)
Whoever destroys, damages or renders useless, totally or partially, or, in any way, removes from the public power to which it is subject, a document or other movable object, as well as a thing that has been attached, seized or subject to a precautionary measure, is punished with imprisonment from 2 to 8 years, if a more severe penalty is not applicable by virtue of another legal provision.

###### ARTICLE 356

(Breaking of marks and seals)
Whoever opens, breaks or renders useless, totally or partially, marks or seals, lawfully affixed by a competent public servant, to identify or keep inviolable any thing, or to certify that it has been subject to attachment, seizure or precautionary measure, is punished with imprisonment up to 2 years and a corresponding fine.

###### ARTICLE 357

(Tearing down, destruction or alteration of edicts)
Whoever tears down, destroys, damages, alters or, in any way, prevents knowledge of an edict posted by a competent public servant is punished with imprisonment up to 1 year and a fine up to 3 months.

#### CHAPTER IV

Removal and Escape of Prisoners and of Those Who Do Not Comply with Their Sentences

##### SECTION I

Removal and evasion of prisoners

###### ARTICLE 358

(Removal and evasion of prisoners)
1. If someone removes or attempts to remove a prisoner, by means of violence or threats to the public authority, its subordinates or agents, or to any person of the people, in cases where the latter may arrest, he is sentenced to the penalties of the crime of illegal resistance.
2. If the removal of the prisoner is done by means of some fraudulent artifice, the imprisonment does not exceed 1 year.
3. The prisoner who, before the sentence becomes final, escapes, is punished with the disciplinary penalties of the regulations of the prison or house of custody or detention, without prejudice to liability for crimes committed to accomplish the escape, but if he is convicted, the evasion is taken into account as an aggravating circumstance.

###### ARTICLE 359

(Complicity of the person in charge of guarding the prisoner)
1. The public servant in charge of guarding a prisoner, who has intentionally sought or facilitated the escape of the same prisoner, if the latter was imprisoned for a crime to which the law imposes a penalty more severe than imprisonment exceeding 8 years, is punished with imprisonment from 2 to 8 years.
2. In the case of any other penalty, the sentence is imprisonment from 1 to 2 years.
3. If the escape takes place without the employees or agents mentioned in number 1 having the circumstances referred to therein and if the same agents do not prove fortuitous event or force majeure that excludes all imputation of negligence, they are punished with imprisonment from 1 month to 1 year.
4. The penalty of this article ceases once the escaped prisoner is captured, not having committed after the escape any crime, for which he should be imprisoned.

###### ARTICLE 360

(Violent escape)
1. If the escape from prison, or from the place of custody or detention, takes place with breaking in, scaling or false key, or any other violence, every employee or agent charged with the custody of the prisoner, who either is the author of the breaking in, scaling or violence, or supplies, or concurs, or wilfully fails to prevent the supply of instruments or weapons for that purpose, is punished with a penalty of imprisonment from 8 to 12 years.
2. If any other individuals carry out the breaking in, scaling, opening of door or window with false key or any other violence, to procure or facilitate the escape of the prisoner, they are punished with a penalty of imprisonment from 1 to 8 years.
3. The individuals declared in the previous number, who have only supplied the prisoner with weapons or other instruments to

escape, are punished with a penalty of imprisonment from 1 to 8 years, if the escape is carried out, and with a penalty of imprisonment up to 2 years in the contrary case; but if they are ascendants, descendants, adopter, adopted, spouse, brothers or sisters, or relatives by affinity, in the same degrees, of the prisoner, they only incur criminal liability, if the latter has made use of the weapons or other instruments against any person.

###### ARTICLE 361

(Mutiny of prisoners)
Prisoners, detainees or internees who mutiny and, in a concerted manner:
a) exercise violence or threat of violence against a public servant legally charged with their custody, treatment or surveillance;
b) coerce a public servant legally charged with their custody, treatment or surveillance, by means of violence or threat of violence, to perform an act or to refrain from performing it;
are punished with a penalty of imprisonment up to 6 months.

##### SECTION II

Non-compliance with convictions

###### ARTICLE 362

(Escape of convicted prisoner)
1. Whoever, being convicted by final judgment, escapes without having served the sentence, has the sentence penalty extended by double the time spent as a fugitive, subject to the provisions in the following numbers.
2. The increase in duration of the sentence penalty shall not in any case exceed half the time of the same penalty.
3. When the penalty is mixed, the increase referred to in the previous number is calculated only in relation to the type of penalty that the convicted person was serving when he escaped.

#### CHAPTER V

Harbouring of Malefactors

###### ARTICLE 363

(Harbouring of malefactors)
1. Whoever has, harbours, or conceals, or causes to have, harbour, or conceal in his house, or in another place, any individual convicted of the commission of a crime is punished with a penalty of imprisonment from 1 to 8 years.
2. Whoever, voluntarily and habitually, harbours or gives lodging to malefactors, knowing that they have committed crimes against public tranquillity and order, or against persons or property, whether by successively giving this harbouring, or by providing them with a meeting place, is punished as an accomplice to the crimes that those malefactors commit subsequent to his first act of harbouring.
3. Excepted from the provision of number 1 are the ascendants or descendants, adopter and the adopted of the person who was harboured or concealed, the spouse or person living as such, the siblings and relatives by affinity in the same degrees.

#### CHAPTER VI

Illegal Immigration

###### ARTICLE 364

(Entry and re-entry)
Whoever violates the prescription of prohibition of entry and re-entry into the Republic of Mozambique, is punished with a penalty of imprisonment up to 2 years and corresponding fine.

###### ARTICLE 365

(Enticement and instigation)
1. Whoever entices or instigates another to enter or remain illegally in the Republic of Mozambique, is punished with a penalty of imprisonment from 1 to 8 years and fine up to 1 year.
2. Whoever, with lucrative intent, transports, facilitates or favours, in any manner, the entry, stay, exit or illegal transit of a foreign citizen in the national territory, is punished with a penalty of imprisonment from 1 to 8 years and fine up to 1 year.
3. The attempt to aid illegal immigration is punished with a penalty of imprisonment up to 2 years and corresponding fine.

###### ARTICLE 366

(Transport)
1. Whoever, voluntarily, transports or keeps a foreign citizen in inhuman or degrading conditions, endangering his life or causing him serious injury to integrity or death, is punished with a penalty of imprisonment from 2 to 8 years, if a more serious penalty does not apply.
2. Until re-embarkation occurs, the carrier is obliged to pay for food, accommodation and assistance of the illegal immigrant.

###### ARTICLE 367

(Harbouring)
1. Whoever harbours, shelters, lodges or installs an illegal immigrant, knowing him as such, is punished with a penalty of imprisonment up to 2 years and corresponding fine.
2. Whoever voluntarily hires or intermediates the hiring, directly or indirectly, even if precarious, of an illegal immigrant, is punished with a penalty of imprisonment from 3 months to 1 year and corresponding fine.

###### ARTICLE 368

(Obligation to report)
Whoever does not report illegal immigration, and, on account of the silence, obtains, directly or through an intermediary, patrimonial advantage or any other benefit, for himself or for a third party, is punished with a penalty of imprisonment up to 2 years and corresponding fine, if a more serious penalty does not apply.

#### CHAPTER VII

Lotteries, Illicit Conventions on Public Funds and Abuses in Pawnbroking Houses

##### SECTION I

Games

###### ARTICLE 369

(Game of fortune or chance with minor)
1. Whoever participates in a game of fortune or chance with a minor under eighteen years is punished with a penalty of imprisonment from 1 to 6 months and fine of one month.
2. The same penalty is imposed on whoever instigates the minor to the game, or to the violation of obedience due to his parents or guardians for this purpose.

##### SECTION II

Lotteries and other games

###### ARTICLE 370

(Lotteries and other illicit games)
1. All lotteries and other games, which are not authorised by law, are prohibited.

2. Whoever violates the provision in the previous number is punished with a penalty of imprisonment up to 1 year and corresponding fine.

###### ARTICLE 371

(Falsification of lottery tickets or other games)
Whoever falsifies lottery tickets or other games is punished with a penalty of imprisonment up to 2 years and corresponding fine.

##### SECTION III

Illicit conventions on public funds

###### ARTICLE 372

(Illicit conventions on public funds)
1. Whoever agrees to the sale or delivery of State funds, or of foreign funds, or of public establishments, or of anonymous societies, if he does not prove that at the time of the agreement he had those funds at his disposal, or that he was to have them at the time of delivery, is punished with a penalty of imprisonment from 1 to 6 months and corresponding fine.
2. The buyer, if he is aware of the circumstances declared in this article, is punished with 1 month of imprisonment.

#### CHAPTER VIII

Frauds or Violence in Auctions and Tenders

###### ARTICLE 373

(Frauds or violence in auctions and tenders)
Whoever, in any judicial auction or public tender authorised or imposed by law, has obtained by gifts or promises, that someone not bid, as well as whoever obstructs or disturbs the freedom of the act, by means of violence or threats, is punished with a penalty of imprisonment from 2 months to 2 years and corresponding fine, without prejudice to the more serious penalty.

### TITLE VI

Crimes Against the State

#### CHAPTER I

Crimes Against the Security of the State

##### SECTION I

Crimes against national sovereignty
SUBSECTION I
Crimes against national independence and integrity

###### ARTICLE 374

(Treason to the homeland)
Whoever, by means of violence, threat of violence, usurpation or abuse of functions of sovereignty:
a) attempts to fragment the country, or deliver to a foreign country or submit to foreign sovereignty all the national territory or part of it; or
b) offends or endangers the independence of the Country;
is punished with a penalty of imprisonment from 16 to 20 years.

###### ARTIGO 375

(Military service in enemy armed forces)
1.	Any person who, being Mozambican, takes up arms under the flag of a foreign nation against Mozambique shall be punished with imprisonment of 12 to 16 years.

2.	If, before hostilities or the declaration of war, the agent was in the service of an enemy State with authorization from the Mozambican Government, the penalty may be specially mitigated.
3.	Any person who, being in the territory of an enemy State before the declaration of war or hostilities, is forced by the military laws of that State to take up arms under a foreign flag against Mozambique shall not be punishable.

###### ARTIGO 376

(Intelligence with foreigners to provoke war)
1.	Any person who has intelligence with the government of a foreign State, with a foreign party, association, institution or group, or with any agent thereof, with the intention of promoting or provoking war or armed action against Mozambique, shall be punished with imprisonment of 16 to 20 years.
2.	If the conduct described in the preceding number is not followed by the effect provided therein, the agent shall be punished with imprisonment of 8 to 12 years.

###### ARTIGO 377

(Performance of acts suitable to provoke war)
1.	Any person who, being Mozambican, foreign or stateless, residing or being in Mozambique, performs acts not authorized by the Mozambican Government and suitable to expose the Mozambican State to a declaration of war or to armed action shall be punished with imprisonment of 8 to 12 years.
2.	If the conduct described in the preceding number is not followed by the effect provided therein, the agent shall be punished with imprisonment of up to 2 years.

###### ARTIGO 378

(Campaign against war effort)
Any person who, being Mozambican, foreign or stateless, residing or being in Mozambique, makes or publicly reproduces, in time of war, statements that he knows to be false or grossly distorted, with the intention of preventing or disturbing the war effort of Mozambique or of assisting or fomenting enemy operations, shall be punished with imprisonment of 2 to 8 years.

###### ARTIGO 379

(Sabotage against national defence)
Any person who prejudices or endangers national defence by destroying, damaging or rendering unusable, in whole or in part, even if temporarily, military works or materials belonging to the Armed Forces, shall be punished with imprisonment of 12 to 16 years.

###### ARTIGO 380

(Violation of State secret)
1.	Any person who, endangering interests of the Mozambican State relating to national independence, the unity and integrity of the State or its internal and external security, transmits, makes accessible to an unauthorized person, or makes public any fact or document, plan or object that must, in the name of those interests, be kept secret, shall be punished with imprisonment of 2 to 8 years.
2.	Any person who destroys, removes or falsifies a document, plan or object referred to in the preceding number, endangering the interests indicated in the same number, shall be punished with imprisonment of 2 to 8 years.

3.	If the agent commits the act described in the preceding numbers in violation of a duty specifically imposed by the statute of his function or service, or of the mission conferred upon him by competent authority, he shall be punished with imprisonment of 8 to 12 years.
4.	If the agent commits by negligence the acts referred to in numbers 1 and 2, having access to the objects or State secrets by reason of his function or service, or of the mission conferred upon him by competent authority, he shall be punished with imprisonment of up to 2 years.

###### ARTIGO 381

(Espionage)
1.	Any person who shall be punished with imprisonment of 8 to 12 years:
a)	collaborates with a foreign government, association, organization or intelligence service, or with an agent thereof, with the intention of committing an act referred to in the preceding article; or
b)	recruits, harbours or receives an agent who commits an act referred to in the preceding article or in the preceding paragraph, or, in any way, favours the commission of such act.
2.	If the agent commits the act described in the preceding number in violation of a duty specifically imposed by the statute of his function or service, or of the mission conferred upon him by competent authority, he shall be punished with imprisonment of 12 to 16 years.

###### ARTIGO 382

(Means of proof of national interest)
1.	Any person who falsifies, removes, destroys, renders useless, causes to disappear or conceals a means of proof concerning a fact relating to relations between Mozambique and a foreign State or international organization, suitable to endanger national rights or interests, shall be punished with imprisonment of 2 to 8 years.
2.	If the action consists in uprooting, displacing, falsely placing, rendering unrecognizable or, in any way, suppressing landmarks, beacons or other signs indicating the limits of Mozambican territory, the agent shall be punished with the penalty of the preceding number, but mitigated.

###### ARTIGO 383

(Diplomatic infidelity)
1.	Any person who, officially representing the Mozambican State, with the intention of causing prejudice to national rights or interests, shall be punished with imprisonment of 1 to 8 years:
a)	conducts State business with a foreign government or international organization; or
b)	assumes commitments before them without being duly authorized to do so in the name of Mozambique.
2.	Imprisonment of up to 2 years and a fine of up to 1 year shall be applied to the diplomatic agent who fails to provide the protection that the laws require to be given to any Mozambican in the foreign country where he is in representation, without prejudice to the application of disciplinary measures.

###### ARTIGO 384

(Usurpation of Mozambican public authority)
Any person who, in Mozambican territory, with usurpation of functions, exercises, in favour of a foreign State or an agent thereof, an act exclusive to Mozambican public authority shall be punished with imprisonment of 2 to 8 years, if a more severe penalty is not applicable by virtue of another legal provision.

SUBSECÇÃO II
Crimes against State security

###### ARTIGO 385

(Conjuration or conspiracy for the commission of a crime against State security)
1.	Conjuration or conspiracy, consisting in the concerted action between two or more subjects for the commission of a crime against State security, shall be punished, if a more severe penalty is not established by law, with imprisonment of 2 to 8 years, when followed by some other preparatory act of execution, or with imprisonment of 1 to 2 years and a fine of up to 1 year, if no preparatory act has followed.
2.	If the conspiracy takes the form of an illicit association or secret organization with a view to inciting or executing any of those crimes, the penalty of imprisonment of 2 to 8 years shall be applicable, regardless of the perpetration of any other preparatory act; the leaders or promoters of the association or organization shall be punished with imprisonment of 8 to 12 years.
3.	When the association or organization or its members use or possess weapons to facilitate their criminal purposes, the penalties provided shall always be aggravated.

###### ARTICLE 386

(Piracy)
1. Commits the crime of piracy, punishable by a prison sentence of 16 to 20 years, anyone who crews or commands by violent means, a ship or aircraft, or appropriates it with fraud or violence, or diverts it from its normal route, with the intent to commit robberies, practice violence against the ship or aircraft or against persons or property on board the same, as well as to attack the security of the State or of a foreign State.
2. Also commits the crime of piracy, punishable under the terms of the preceding number, anyone who usurps the command of a national ship or aircraft or one chartered by a national company, followed by navigation in violation of fundamental norms of freedom and security of commerce or with injury to national interests.
3. The alteration of land, sea or air signals that constitute fraudulent shipwreck manoeuvres, the portage, water landing, or landing of ships or aircraft with the purpose of attacking them or the persons or property on board is punished as a crime of piracy.
4. To the penalty for the crime of piracy are added those of other crimes committed, proceeding to their aggravation whenever the crime of kidnapping, sexual crime or homicide concurs or, further, when the perpetrators of the crime have abandoned persons and means to save themselves or have caused the loss of the ship or have abandoned it while navigating.

###### ARTICLE 387

(Hostility against Mozambican ship or aircraft in peacetime)
1. Whoever, commanding any foreign armed ship or aircraft, with authorization from the Mozambican Government, commits in peacetime hostilities against any Mozambican ship or aircraft, is punished with a prison sentence of 2 to 8 years and a fine of up to 1 year.
2. If commanding it without authorization from the Mozambican Government and commits the hostilities, is punished with a prison sentence of 12 to 16 years, unless by those hostilities commits some crime for which deserves a more serious penalty.

###### ARTICLE 388

(Mercenarism)
1. Commits the crime of mercenarism, punished with a prison sentence of 16 to 20 years, anyone who attempts to overthrow by armed violence a legitimately constituted foreign government, creating for that purpose armed forces composed in whole or in part by foreigners.
2. Is punished with the penalty corresponding to the crime of mercenarism, whoever, voluntarily, recruits, organizes, finances, supplies, equips, trains and transports the individuals referred to in the preceding number, as well as whoever enlists in the forces referred to in the same number.

###### ARTICLE 389

(Unlawful delivery of person to foreign entity)
Whoever, in Mozambican territory, practices acts conducive to the unlawful delivery of a person, national or foreign, to a foreign State, to an agent thereof or to any public or private entity existing in that State, using for such purpose violence or fraud, is punished with a prison sentence of 2 to 8 years, if a more serious penalty does not apply by virtue of another legal provision.

SUBSECTION III
Crimes against foreign States and international organizations

###### ARTICLE 390

(Crimes against person enjoying international protection)
1. Whoever attacks the life, physical integrity or liberty of a person enjoying international protection, the victim being in Mozambique in the performance of official functions, is punished with a prison sentence of 20 to 24 years.
2. Enjoy international protection for the purpose of the provisions of the preceding number:
a) head of State, Head of Government, or whoever exercises, under constitutional terms, those functions, as well as family members who accompany them; and
b) representative or public servant of a foreign State or agent of an international organization who, at the time of the crime, enjoys special protection according to international law, as well as family members who live with them.

###### ARTICLE 391

(Outrage of foreign symbols)
Whoever, publicly, by words, gestures, dissemination of writing or other means of communication with the public, insults the official flag or other symbol of sovereignty of a foreign State or of an international organization of which Mozambique is a member, is punished with a fine of up to 2 years.

##### SECTION II

Crimes against the realization of the rule of law

###### ARTICLE 392

(Violent alteration of the rule of law)
1. Whoever, by means of violence or threat of violence, attempts to destroy, alter or subvert the rule of law constitutionally established is punished with a prison sentence of 12 to 16 years.

2. If the act described in the preceding number is committed by means of armed violence, the agent is punished with a prison sentence of 16 to 20 years.
3. In the case provided for in the preceding number, the penalty is specially mitigated if the agent, not having exercised command functions, surrenders without offering resistance, or delivers or abandons the weapons before or immediately after warning by the authority.

###### ARTICLE 393

(Attack against the President of the Republic)
1. Whoever attacks the life, physical integrity or liberty of the President of the Republic or of whoever constitutionally substitutes him, is punished with a prison sentence of 20 to 24 years.
2. The attack consists in the execution or its attempt.
3. The preparatory acts of the crime of attack against the life of the President of the Republic are punished with a prison sentence of 12 to 16 years, if a more serious penalty does not apply.

###### ARTICLE 394

(Attack against the life of certain entities)
The attack against the life of the holders and members of the organs of sovereignty, magistrates, Ombudsman and members of the Council of State, is punished with a prison sentence of 16 to 20 years.

###### ARTICLE 395

(Bodily harm or attack against the liberty of certain entities)
1. Any bodily harm or attack against the liberty of the entities mentioned in the preceding article shall be punished with the penalty correspondingly applicable to the crime of voluntary bodily harm, but aggravated by half in its minimum and maximum limits.
2. If the crimes are committed against other agents of public authority, expert or witness, in the exercise of their respective functions, the penalty is correspondingly applicable to the crime of voluntary bodily harm, but aggravated by one third in its minimum and maximum limits.

###### ARTICLE 396

(Incitement to collective disobedience)
1. Whoever, with the intention of destroying, altering or subverting by violence the rule of law constitutionally established, incites, in a public meeting or by any means of communication with the public, to collective disobedience of laws of public order, is punished with a prison sentence of 2 to 8 years.
2. In the same penalty incurs whoever, with the intention referred to in the preceding number, publicly or by any means of communication with the public:
a) disseminates false or tendentious news capable of provoking alarm or unrest in the population;
b) provokes or attempts to provoke, by the means referred to in the preceding paragraph, divisions within the Armed Forces, between these and the militarized or security forces, or between any of these and the organs of sovereignty; or
c) incites to political struggle by violence.
3. If the acts described in the preceding numbers are accompanied by distribution of weapons, the agent is punished with a prison sentence of 20 to 24 years.

###### ARTICLE 397

(Outrage of national symbols)
Whoever, publicly, by words, gestures or dissemination of writing, or by means of communication with the public, outrages the State, the Flag, the National Anthem, or other symbols of Mozambican sovereignty, or fails to show the respect that is due to them, is punished with a prison sentence of up to 1 year and corresponding fine.

###### ARTICLE 398

(Coercion against public bodies)
1. Whoever, by violence or threat of violence, prevents or constrains the free exercise of the functions of a sovereign body or minister of the Republic is punished with imprisonment from 5 to 10 years, if a more severe penalty is not applicable by virtue of another legal provision.
2. If the acts described in the preceding paragraph are committed against other public authorities or in an establishment, ceremony, examination or public meeting, the agent is punished with imprisonment from 1 to 5 years.

###### ARTICLE 399

(Disturbance of the functioning of a public body)
Whoever, with tumults, disorders or shouting, unlawfully disturbs:
a) the functioning of a body referred to in paragraph 1 of the preceding article, not being a member thereof, is punished with imprisonment up to 2 years and corresponding fine; and
b) in the case of paragraph 2, with imprisonment up to 6 months and corresponding fine.

###### ARTICLE 400

(Abandonment of execution)
Whoever, being involved in the preparation of a crime provided for in this Chapter, voluntarily reveals it to the authorities, before the commencement of execution or in time to avoid its consequences, is exempt from penalty.

#### CHAPTER II

Crimes Against the Administration of Justice

##### SECTION I

Simulation of crime, false accusation, prevarication, judicial secrecy and others

###### ARTICLE 401

(Simulation of crime)
1. Whoever, without imputing it to a specific person, reports a crime or causes suspicion of its commission to be created before the competent authority, knowing that it did not occur, is punished with imprisonment up to 1 year and fine up to 6 months.
2. If the act relates to a contravention, the agent is punished with a fine up to 2 months.

###### ARTICLE 402

(False accusation)
1. Whoever, by any means, before an authority or publicly, with awareness of the falsity of the imputation, reports or casts upon a specific person the suspicion of having committed a crime, with the intention that proceedings be instituted against them, is punished with imprisonment from 2 to 8 years and fine up to 1 year.

2. If the conduct consists of falsely imputing a contravention, the agent is punished with imprisonment up to 1 year and corresponding fine.
3. If the means used by the agent consists of presenting, altering or distorting evidence, the agent is punished with imprisonment up to 2 years.
5. If the act results in deprivation of liberty of the victim, the agent is punished with imprisonment from 4 to 8 years.

###### ARTICLE 403

(Failure to initiate criminal proceedings)
A public servant who, failing in the obligations of their office, intentionally fails to initiate proceedings or to employ measures within their competence to prevent or forestall the perpetration of any crime, is punished with a fine up to 2 years.

###### ARTICLE 404

(Intentional prosecution by the Public Prosecutor)
If a magistrate of the Public Prosecutor's Office proceeds criminally against a specific person, knowing that the evidence is false, they are punished with imprisonment from 1 to 8 years, if the falsity of the evidence necessarily results from the falsity of the instrument constituting it, and with imprisonment up to 2 years and corresponding fine, in any other case.

###### ARTICLE 405

(Violation of judicial secrecy)
Whoever unlawfully discloses, in whole or in part, the content of an act of criminal proceedings that is covered by judicial secrecy, or to whose course the general public is not permitted to attend, is punished with imprisonment from 1 to 2 years and corresponding fine, unless another penalty is prescribed for the case by procedural law.

###### ARTICLE 406

(False testimony or statement)
1. Whoever gives testimony as a party, making false statements regarding facts about which they must testify, after having taken an oath and having been warned of the criminal consequences to which they are exposed by giving false testimony, is punished with imprisonment from 2 to 8 years.
2. The same penalty is incurred by the assistant and civil parties regarding statements they make in criminal proceedings, as well as by the accused regarding statements about identity and criminal record.

###### ARTICLE 407

(False testimony, expert opinion, interpretation or translation)
1. Whoever, as a witness, expert, technician, translator or interpreter, before a court or public servant competent to receive as evidence, testimony, report, information or translation, gives testimony, presents a report, provides information or makes false translations, is punished with imprisonment from 2 to 8 years.
2. The same penalty is incurred by whoever, without just cause, refuses to testify or to present a report, information or translation.
3. If the act referred to in paragraph 1 is committed after the agent has taken an oath and been warned of the criminal consequences to which they are exposed, the penalty ranges from 5 to 8 years of imprisonment.

###### ARTICLE 408

(False advice or information)
1. A public servant who, being obliged by the nature of their functions to give advice or information to a superior authority, advises or intentionally provides false information about a fact, is punished with imprisonment up to 1 year.
2. The penalty established in the preceding paragraph is aggravated up to 2 years of imprisonment if the advice or information intentionally provided results in negative impact against the Mozambican State.

###### ARTICLE 409

(Bribery of false witness)
1. In all cases declared in the preceding articles, if the person who testified falsely was bribed with gifts or promises, the penalty applicable to them under the same articles is always aggravated.
2. What was received is forfeited to the State.
3. The briber is punished with the same penalties.
4. Attempted bribery is punishable.

###### ARTICLE 410

(Retraction of false testimony)
1. The penalty for false testimony ceases if the person who gave it retracts voluntarily, in time for the retraction to be taken into account in the decision and before any prejudice to a third party has resulted from the false testimony, report, information or translation.
2. The retraction may be made, as the case may be, before the court, the Public Prosecutor's Office or the criminal police body.

###### ARTICLE 411

(Prevarication of judge and others)
1. A judge who, by favoritism or hatred, renders a manifestly unjust final judgment, is punished if the judgment is:
a) condemnatory in a criminal case, with imprisonment from 1 to 8 years;
b) rendered in a non-criminal case, with imprisonment up to 2 years.
2. If the judgment is not final, the maximum penalty shall be reduced to half its duration.
3. A judge who advises one of the parties about the litigation pending before them is sentenced to imprisonment up to 2 years.
4. The provisions of paragraph 1(b) and paragraphs 2 and 3 of this article are applicable to all public authorities who, by virtue of their functions, decide or judge any contentious matter submitted to their knowledge.

###### ARTICLE 412

(Prevarication of defense counsel and Public Prosecutor)
1. The following incur imprisonment up to 2 years and fine up to 1 year:
a) the defense counsel who reveals the secrets of their client, having learned of them in the exercise of their function;
b) whoever, having received from one of the parties, money or any other thing, for advocating or pursuing their case and claim, or having accepted the power of attorney and learned the secrets of the case, advocates, pursues or advises, publicly or secretly, for the other party, in the same case;
c) whoever receives anything from the party against whom they are acting.

2. The same penalty is imposed on a magistrate of the Public Prosecutor's Office who incurs any of the crimes mentioned in this article, unless a more severe penalty should be imposed for corruption.

###### ARTICLE 413

(Violation of other professional secrets)
A public servant is punished with a penalty of imprisonment up to 2 years and a corresponding fine:
a) who reveals a secret of which they only have knowledge or are a depositary, by reason of the exercise of their employment;
b) who unduly delivers a document or copy of a document, which should not have publicity and is entrusted to them or exists in the respective department, or gives knowledge of it without due authorization.

###### ARTICLE 414

(Special mitigation and waiver of penalty)
The penalties provided for in the preceding articles are specially mitigated, and a waiver of penalty may take place when:
a) the falsity concerns circumstances that do not have essential significance for the proof to which the testimony, report, information or translation is intended; or
b) the act was committed to prevent the agent, the spouse or person with whom they live in an analogous situation, an adopter or adoptee, relatives or in-laws up to the 2nd degree from being exposed to the danger of being subjected to a penalty or security measure.

##### SECTION II

Abuse of authority

###### ARTICLE 415

(Illegal imprisonment)
1. A penalty of imprisonment up to 2 years is imposed, which may be aggravated with a fine up to 1 year, according to the circumstances:
a) any public servant who arrests or causes to be arrested by their order any person, without being competent;
b) who, having this power, exercises it outside the cases determined by law or against any person whose arrest is the exclusive attribution of another authority;
c) who detains in custody the person who should be released, by virtue of the law or of a final judgment, whose enforcement is their responsibility, or by order of the competent superior;
d) who orders or illegally prolongs the incommunicado detention of the prisoner, or who conceals a prisoner who should be presented.
2. By imprisonment is also understood any detention or custody.

###### ARTICLE 416

(Formally irregular imprisonment)
A penalty of imprisonment up to 1 year is imposed, which may be aggravated with a corresponding fine, according to the circumstances:
a) any public servant who orders or executes the arrest of any person, without observing the formalities prescribed by law;
b) who arbitrarily detains or orders the detention of any prisoner outside the public jail or the place determined by law or by the Government;

c) who, being competent to issue or order the issuance of a certificate of imprisonment, denies it or refuses to present the register of imprisonments, when competently requisitioned;
d) who, being in charge of police services and having knowledge of any arbitrary arrest, fails to report to the competent superior authority;
e) any agent of public authority, in charge of the custody of prisoners, who receives any prisoner without written order from the public authority.

###### ARTICLE 417

(Illegitimate rigour towards prisoners)
Any agent of public authority, in charge of the custody of any prisoner, who employs illegitimate rigour towards them, is punished with a penalty of imprisonment up to 2 years, and if the acts they commit have a more severe penalty under the laws, it shall be imposed.

###### ARTICLE 418

(Abusive entry into another's home)
Any public servant who, in this capacity, and abusing their functions, enters the dwelling of any person without their consent or against their will, outside the cases or without the formalities prescribed by law, is punished with a penalty of imprisonment up to 2 years and a fine corresponding to 1 month.

###### ARTICLE 419

(Concussion)
1. A public servant who, in the exercise of their functions or de facto powers arising therefrom, by themselves or through an intermediary with their consent or ratification, receives, for themselves, for the State or for a third party, by inducing error or taking advantage of the victim's error, a patrimonial advantage that is not due to them, or is greater than what is due, namely contribution, tax, fee, fine or administrative fine, is punished with a penalty of imprisonment up to 2 years and a fine up to 6 months, if a more severe penalty is not applicable by virtue of another legal provision.
2. If the act is committed by means of violence or threat of significant harm, the agent is punished with a penalty of imprisonment from 2 to 8 years, if a more severe penalty is not applicable by virtue of another legal provision.

###### ARTICLE 420

(Requisition of public force)
1. A public servant who, being competent, requisitions or orders the use of public force to prevent the execution of any law or regular warrant of justice or legal order of any public authority, is punished with a penalty of imprisonment up to 1 year and a corresponding fine.
2. If the impediment is not consummated, but the requisition or order has been followed by some effect, the penalty is imprisonment up to 2 years and a corresponding fine.
3. If the impediment is consummated, the penalty is imprisonment from 2 to 8 years, if that impediment does not constitute a crime to which a more severe penalty is applicable by law.

##### SECTION III

Excess of power and disobedience of public servant

###### ARTICLE 421

(Excess of power)
A penalty of imprisonment from 2 to 8 years, or a penalty of imprisonment, according to the gravity of the crime, is imposed:
a) on the public servant who interferes in the exercise of Legislative Power, suspending any laws or arrogating any of the attributions that exclusively belong to the legislative body;
b) on the judge who makes regulations in matters attributed to administrative authorities, or prohibits the execution of orders of the Administration;
c) on the public servant who commits the crime provided for in paragraph
a) of number 1 of article 415 against any deputy of the Assembly of the Republic, as well as the one who against that person executes the order, referred to in that paragraph a) the exemption established in article 449, paragraph a) not taking place in any case in this hypothesis;
d) on the administrative authority who, with any orders or prohibitions, attempts to prevent or disturb the exercise of Judicial Power.

###### ARTICLE 422

(Conflict between judicial and administrative authorities)
A penalty of fine up to 2 years is imposed:
a) on the judge who, after the decision that, under the terms of the law, raises a positive conflict between the administrative and judicial authority has been presented in court, does not suspend all terms of the case, or continues to issue orders in it, without the law expressly authorizing it, after articles of suspicion have been opposed to them; and
b) on the administrative authority who, after the claim of any of the interested parties, decides on a matter of the competence of Judicial Power, without the competent authority having judged the claim or after having judged it well-founded.

###### ARTICLE 423

(Disobedience to judicial decisions)
1. A judge or court officer who refuses to give due compliance to sentences, decisions or orders, clothed in legal forms and emanating from superior courts, within the limits of the jurisdiction they have in the hierarchical order, is punished with a penalty of fine up to 6 months.
2. Any other public servant who refuses to give due compliance to orders that the superior, to whom they owe direct obedience, gives them in legal form in matters of their competence, is punished with a penalty of imprisonment up to 3 months, according to the circumstances.
3. If it is a case in which, according to the law, the representation of the subordinate employee may take place, with suspension of the execution of the order, the penalty only takes place if, after the suspension is disapproved by the superior, and the order is repeated, there is a refusal of its execution.

###### ARTICLE 424

(Refusal of cooperation)
Any civil or military public servant who, having received a legal requisition from the competent authority to provide due

cooperation for the administration of justice or any public service, refuses to provide it, or without legitimate reason does not provide it, is punished with a penalty of imprisonment from 2 months to 1 year.

#### CHAPTER II

Corruption and Related Crimes

##### SECTION I

Public sector

###### ARTICLE 425

(Passive corruption for an unlawful act)
A public servant who, personally or through an intermediary, with their consent or ratification, solicits or accepts, for themselves or for a third party, without being entitled to it, a patrimonial or non-patrimonial advantage, or its promise, for any act or omission contrary to the duties of the office, even if prior to that solicitation or acceptance, is punished with a prison sentence of 1 to 8 years and a fine of up to 2 years.

###### ARTICLE 426

(Passive corruption for a lawful act)
1.	A public servant who, personally or through an intermediary, with their consent or ratification, solicits or accepts, for themselves or for a third party, without being entitled to it, a patrimonial or non-patrimonial advantage, or its promise, for any act or omission not contrary to the duties of the office, even if prior to that solicitation or acceptance, is punished with a prison sentence of 1 to 5 years and a fine of up to 1 year.
2.	The same penalty is incurred by a public servant who, personally or through an intermediary, with their consent or ratification, solicits or accepts, for themselves or for a third party, without being entitled to it, a patrimonial or non-patrimonial advantage from a person who has had, has or will have before them any claim dependent on the exercise of their public functions.

###### ARTICLE 427

(Active corruption)
1.	Whoever, personally or through an intermediary with their consent or ratification, gives or promises to a public servant, or to a third party with the knowledge of that servant, a patrimonial or non-patrimonial advantage to which the servant is not entitled, with the purpose indicated in article 425, is punished with a prison sentence of 6 months to 5 years.
2.	If the purpose is that indicated in article 426, the agent is punished with a prison sentence of up to 2 years and a corresponding fine.

###### ARTICLE 428

(Illicit enrichment)
1.	A public servant who, personally or through an intermediary, acquires, possesses or holds assets without a determined lawful origin, incompatible with their income or legitimate assets, is punished with a prison sentence of up to 1 year and a corresponding fine, if a more severe penalty is not applicable by virtue of another legal provision.
2.	In case of conviction, the part of the assets that exceeds the value proven by legitimate income is declared forfeited to the State.

###### ARTICLE 429

(Fraud)
A public servant who, with the purpose of obtaining a patrimonial or non-patrimonial advantage, intentionally misleads a hierarchical superior to decide on any claim, is punished with a prison sentence of up to 3 years and a fine of up to 1 year.

###### ARTICLE 430

(Simulation of competence)
The provisions of articles 425 and 426 apply in cases where the public servant, fraudulently arrogating or simulating competence to perform any act, accepts an offer or promise, or receives a gift or present, to perform, or not perform, that act.

###### ARTICLE 431

(Abuse of office or function)
1.	A public servant who makes abusive use of their office or function, performing acts or omitting or delaying acts in the exercise of their functions, in violation of the law, orders or superior instructions with the purpose of obtaining a patrimonial or non-patrimonial advantage for themselves or for a third party, shall be punished with a prison sentence of up to 2 years and a fine of up to 1 year, without prejudice to penalties for corruption if applicable.
2.	The same penalty applies if the public servant or entity vested with public authority, by any means, associates with other servants, agreeing among themselves on measures to prevent the execution of any law or lawful order.

###### ARTICLE 432

(Corruption of magistrates and criminal investigation agents)
1.	Magistrates and criminal investigation agents who, personally or through an intermediary, solicit or receive money or a promise of money or any patrimonial or non-patrimonial advantage, to, in criminal matters, investigate or refrain from investigating, accuse or refrain from accusing, arrest or refrain from arresting someone, indict or not indict, judge or refrain from judging, convict or refrain from convicting, are punished with a prison sentence of 1 to 8 years.
2.	If as a result of the corruption there has been a conviction to a more severe penalty than that declared in the preceding paragraph, the magistrate who allowed themselves to be corrupted is imposed that more severe penalty and the fine declared in the preceding paragraph.

###### ARTICLE 433

(Corruption of customs officials and other public servants)
A prison sentence of 1 to 8 years is applied to a public servant assigned to customs services, transportation, migration, civil and criminal identification who, personally or through an intermediary, with their consent or ratification, solicits or receives money or a promise of money or any patrimonial or non-patrimonial advantage, to, in matters of their office, perform or refrain from performing an act.

###### ARTICLE 434

(Embezzlement)
1.	A public servant who, by reason of their functions, has in their possession money, cheques, credit instruments, or movable or immovable property belonging to the State or local authorities or public entity or private legal person or private individuals, to guard, expend or administer, or to give them their legal destination,

and takes or appropriates any of these things, or allows them to be taken or appropriated or stolen by another, dissipates or applies them to their own or another's use, to the detriment of the State, those legal persons or private individuals, failing in the legal application or delivery, is punished with the penalties immediately superior to those corresponding to the crime of theft, taking into account the value of the thing, if more severe penalties are not applicable.
2.	The provisions of the preceding paragraph include persons constituted as depositaries, collectors, receivers, tax collectors, treasurers, operators or authorizing officers of the State Financial Administration System in relation to things of which they are depositaries, collectors, receivers, tax collectors or treasurers, or who act as such, in relation to things entrusted to them.

###### ARTICLE 435

(Embezzlement of use)
A public servant who uses or permits another person to use, for purposes other than those for which they are intended, vehicles or other movable things, public or private, that have been delivered to them, are in their possession or are accessible to them by reason of their functions, is punished with a prison sentence of up to 1 year and a corresponding fine.

###### ARTICLE 436

(Economic participation in business)
1.	A public servant is punished with a prison sentence of 1 to 5 years and a fine of up to 1 year, who:
a)	with the intention of obtaining for themselves or for a third party, economic participation, harms, in a legal transaction, the patrimonial interests that, in whole or in part, it is their duty, by reason of their functions, to administer, supervise, defend or realize;
b)	in any way, receives a patrimonial or non-patrimonial advantage as a result of an act relating to interests that they had, by virtue of their functions, at the time of the act, totally or partially, at their disposal, administration or supervision, even without harming them;
c)	takes or accepts, personally or through another, any interest by purchase or by any other title or means, in a thing or business of which they are in charge of the disposal, administration, inspection, supervision or custody, by reason of their functions or in which they are similarly in charge of making or ordering any collection, gathering, liquidation or payment.
2.	The provisions of the preceding paragraph apply:
a)	to those who, by commission or legal appointment of the public servant or the competent public authority, are entrusted with any of the objects referred to in the preceding paragraph;
b)	to expert appraisers, arbitrators, dividers, depositaries by public authority, as well as guardians, executors, with respect to things or business in which they must exercise their functions.

###### ARTICLE 437

(Violation of Plan and Budget rules)
A public servant who, by duty of their office, is responsible for compliance with the laws of the Plan and Budget and, voluntarily,

violates them, is punished with a prison sentence of 1 to 5 years, if a more serious penalty does not apply, when:
a) contracting or granting loans or authorizing State guarantees;
b) carrying out or ordering the carrying out of financial operations, active or passive, with direct or indirect implications for the Plan and Budget;
c) authorizing or promoting treasury operations that increase public debt or budgetary alterations.

###### ARTICLE 438

(Concept of public servant)
1. A public servant is considered to be a person who exercises a mandate, office, employment or function in a public entity, by virtue of election, appointment, contract or any other form of investiture or bond, even if temporarily or without remuneration.
2. The terms official, State agent, public employee, municipal agent or any other similar term used to refer to a person who performs functions in a public entity are understood as synonyms of public servant.
3. Also included in paragraph 1 are persons who exercise de facto any of the functions referred to therein.

###### ARTICLE 439

(Criminal liability of hierarchical superior)
1. If a public servant is accused of having committed any of the abusive acts of the preceding articles of this section qualified as crimes, and proves that the superior, to whom they owe direct obedience, had given them, in a matter within their competence, the order in legal form to perform that act, they are exempt from the penalty, which shall be imposed on the superior who gave the order.
2. The hierarchical superior is considered an accomplice and punished according to the general rules on complicity, in the case where, being aware of a crime committed by a subordinate who owes them direct obedience, they do not report it to the Public Prosecutor within a maximum period of five working days after becoming aware.

###### ARTICLE 440

(Accessory penalty)
A public servant sentenced to a prison sentence exceeding 2 years for a crime provided for in this Chapter is expelled from the exercise of their office or function, without prejudice to special rules governing the exercise of public office for certain categories.

##### SECTION II

International Trade

###### ARTICLE 441

(Active corruption with prejudice to international trade)
Whoever, by themselves or, with their consent or ratification, through an intermediary gives or promises to a national, foreign or international organization public servant, or to a third party with the knowledge of those persons, a patrimonial or non-patrimonial advantage, which is not due to them, to obtain or retain a business, a contract or other undue advantage in international trade, is punished with a prison sentence of 1 to 5 years.

###### ARTICLE 442

(Influence peddling in international transaction)
Whoever solicits, demands, collects or obtains, for themselves or for a third party, directly or indirectly, an advantage or promise of advantage

patrimonial or non-patrimonial, under the pretext of influencing, in an act related to an international commercial transaction, performed by a national or foreign public servant, in the exercise of their functions, is punished with a prison sentence of 1 to 5 years.

###### ARTICLE 443

(Concept of foreign public servant)
1. A foreign public servant is considered to be anyone who, in the service of a foreign country, even if temporarily, with remuneration or free of charge, voluntarily or compulsorily, exercises an office, mandate or employment in the public, administrative or jurisdictional function or, in the same circumstances, performs functions in public utility bodies, public companies, companies with public capital or with majority public capital participation, in companies holding public service concessions, and also in international public law organizations.
2. Also belonging to the category referred to in the preceding paragraph is anyone who, in the service of a foreign country, exercises an office within the legislative, judicial or executive function, at national, regional or local level, for which they have been elected or appointed.

##### SECTION III

Private sector

###### ARTICLE 444

(Passive corruption)
1. A private sector worker who, by themselves or with their consent or ratification, through an intermediary, solicits or accepts, for themselves or for a third party, without it being due to them, a patrimonial or non-patrimonial advantage, or its promise, for any act or omission that constitutes a violation of their functional duties is punished with a prison sentence of up to 2 years and a fine of up to 1 year.
2. If the act or omission provided for in the preceding paragraph is capable of causing a distortion of competition or patrimonial damage to the employer, the agent is punished with a prison sentence of 1 to 5 years and a fine of up to 1 year.

###### ARTICLE 445

(Active corruption)
1. Whoever by themselves or, with their consent or ratification, through an intermediary gives or promises to a person provided for in the preceding article, or to a third party with the knowledge of that person, a patrimonial or non-patrimonial advantage, which is not due to them, to pursue the purpose indicated therein is punished with a prison sentence of up to 2 years and a fine of up to 1 year.
2. If the conduct provided for in the preceding paragraph aims to obtain or is capable of causing a distortion of competition or patrimonial damage to third parties, the agent is punished with a prison sentence of up to 5 years and a fine of up to 1 year.
3. Attempt is punishable.

###### ARTICLE 446

(Obligation of auditor)
1. An auditor who finds evidence of the commission of crimes provided for in this section in an audit carried out

on a public entity or company in which the State holds a stake must report the fact, in writing, to the Public Prosecutor, within ten days from the date of preparation of the draft final audit report.
2. Whoever violates the provisions of the preceding paragraph is punished with a prison sentence of 2 to 5 years and is compulsorily subject to one of the accessory penalties provided for by law.

###### ARTICLE 447

(Influence peddling)
1. Whoever, by themselves or through an intermediary, with their consent or ratification, solicits or accepts, for themselves or for a third party, a patrimonial or non-patrimonial advantage, or its promise, to abuse their influence, real or supposed, with any public entity, is punished:
a) with a prison sentence of 6 months to 2 years, if a more serious penalty does not apply by virtue of another legal provision, if the purpose is to obtain any favorable unlawful decision;
b) with a prison sentence of up to 6 months and a corresponding fine, if a more serious penalty does not apply by virtue of another legal provision, if the purpose is to obtain any favorable lawful decision.
2. Whoever, by themselves or through an intermediary, with their consent or ratification, gives or promises a patrimonial or non-patrimonial advantage to the persons referred to in the preceding paragraph for the purposes provided for in paragraph a) is punished with a prison sentence of up to 3 years or with a fine.

##### SECTION IV

Common provisions

###### ARTICLE 448

(Publicity of conviction decision)
1. The conviction decision in crimes provided for in this Chapter must be given publicity in a media outlet to be determined by the court, as well as through the posting of a notice, containing the identification of the public servant, the elements of the offense, the sanctions applied and their duration.
2. The publicity of the conviction decision is carried out at the expense of the convicted person and at the place of exercise of the activity, for a period of not less than 30 days, in a manner clearly visible to the public.

###### ARTICLE 449

(Special mitigation and exemption from penalty)
In the crimes provided for in this chapter:
a) the penalty may be specially mitigated if, until the closing of the trial hearing at first instance, the agent concretely assists in the collection of decisive evidence for the identification or capture of other responsible parties or in some way contributes decisively to the discovery of the truth;
b) the agent may be exempted from penalty if, voluntarily, before the commission of the act, he repudiates the offer or promise that he had accepted, returns the advantage or gain or, in the case of a fungible thing, its value.
