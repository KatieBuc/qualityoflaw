Original title and preamble
Act of 9 July 2014, containing rules regarding municipal support in the
field of self-reliance, participation, sheltered living and shelter (Social Support
Act 2015)
We Willem-Alexander, by the grace of God, King of the Netherlands, Prince of Orange-Nassau,
etc. etc. etc.
All who shall see or hear these read, greetings! make known:
Whereas We have taken into consideration that citizens bear their own responsibility
for the manner in which they organise their lives and participate in social
life, and that citizens may be expected to assist each other to the best of their ability
in this regard; that it is also desirable to establish new rules to bring the rights and obligations
of citizens into better balance with each other; that citizens who, either themselves or
together with persons in their immediate environment, are insufficiently self-reliant or insufficiently
able to participate, must be able to rely on support organised by the government
; that the support of the self-reliance and participation
of persons with a disability, chronic mental or psychosocial problems,
must be aimed at enabling citizens to remain in their own living environment for as long as possible
; that it is reasonable to place government responsibility
for supporting self-reliance and participation as close as possible to the citizen; 
that it is therefore desirable to establish new rules regarding municipal responsibility
for the support of persons with a disability and persons with mental or
psychosocial problems; that it is furthermore desirable to ensure
good accessibility of provisions, services and spaces for people
with a disability;
So it is that We, having heard the Advisory Division of the Council of State, and in consultation
with the States General, have approved and decreed, as We hereby approve
and decree:

In this Act and the provisions based thereon, the following definitions apply:
–
provider:
natural person or legal entity who is obliged towards the executive board to deliver a general
provision or a customised provision;
–
general provision:
offer of services or activities that, without prior investigation into the needs,
personal characteristics and capabilities of the users, is accessible and that is aimed

–
AMHK:
advice and reporting centre for domestic violence and child abuse as referred to in

#### article 4.1.1

;
–
support:
activities aimed at promoting self-reliance and participation of the
client so that he can remain in his own living environment for as long as possible.
–
sheltered living:
living in accommodation of an institution with associated supervision and support,
aimed at promoting self-reliance and participation, psychological and psychosocial
functioning, stabilisation of a psychiatric condition, preventing neglect
or public nuisance or averting danger to the client or others,
intended for persons with psychological or psychosocial problems who are not able
to maintain themselves in society on their own;
–
professional:
natural person who is professionally active in person for a provider;
–
citizen service number:
citizen service number as referred to in

#### article 1, part b, of the Citizen Service Number (General Provisions) Act

;

#### article 6.1.1, first paragraph, of the Long-term Care Act

#### article 7.1.1, first paragraph, of the Long-term Care Act

;
–
client:

or personal budget has been granted or by or on behalf of whom a notification has been made
as referred to in

#### article 2.3.2, first paragraph

;
–

–

–

a provider;
–
domestic violence:
physical, mental or sexual violence or threat thereof by someone from
the domestic circle;
–
domestic circle:
a family member, a household member, the spouse or former spouse or a care provider;
–
aid:
movable property intended to reduce or eliminate limitations in self-reliance or participation;
–
child abuse:
any form of threatening or violent interaction of a physical,
psychological or sexual nature towards a minor, which the parents or other persons in relation to whom
the minor is in a relationship of dependence or lack of freedom, actively
or passively impose, whereby serious harm is caused or threatens to be caused to the minor in the form of physical or psychological injury;
–
social support:
1°.
promoting social cohesion, informal care and volunteer work, the accessibility
of provisions, services and spaces for people with disabilities, safety
and liveability in the municipality, as well as preventing and combating domestic violence,
2°.
supporting the self-reliance and participation of persons with a disability
or with chronic psychological or psychosocial problems as much as possible in their own
living environment,
3°.
providing sheltered living and shelter;
–
customised provision:
entirety of services, aids, housing adaptations and other measures tailored to the needs, personal characteristics and possibilities of a person:
1°.
for the benefit of self-reliance, including short-term stay in an institution
to relieve the informal caregiver, the necessary transport therefor, as well as aids,
housing adaptations and other measures,
2°.
for the benefit of participation, including the necessary transport therefor,
as well as aids and other measures,
3°.
for the benefit of sheltered living and shelter;

assistance for the benefit of self-reliance, participation, sheltered living, shelter, youth assistance,
raising and growing up of young people and care and other services as referred to in
the
Health Insurance Act

which is not provided in the context of a care profession;
–
Our Minister:
Our Minister of Health, Welfare and Sport;

accommodation and support for persons who have left the home situation, whether or not in

–
participation:
participating in social life;
–

amount from which payments are made on behalf of the executive board for services, aids,
housing adaptations and other measures that belong to a customised provision,

1
For the application of this Act and the provisions based thereon, the following shall be
equated with:
a.
spouse: registered partner;
b.
married: registered as partners.
2
For the application of this Act and of the decisions taken for its implementation:
a.
an unmarried adult who shares a joint household with another unmarried adult shall also
be regarded as married or as a spouse, unless it concerns a blood relative in the first
degree;
b.
a person who lives permanently separated from the person to whom he is married shall
also be regarded as unmarried.
3
A joint household exists if two persons have their principal residence in the same dwelling
and they demonstrate that they care for each other by making a contribution to the costs
of the household or otherwise.
4
A joint household shall in any event be deemed to exist if the persons concerned have
their principal residence in the same dwelling and:
a.
they have been married to each other or have previously been equated therewith for the
application of this Act,
b.
a child has been born from their relationship or recognition has taken place of a child
of one by the other,
c.
they have mutually obliged themselves to contribute to the household pursuant to a valid
cohabitation contract, or
d.
they are regarded on the basis of a registration as a joint household that corresponds
in nature and purport to the joint household referred to in the third paragraph.
5
By general administrative order it shall be determined which registrations, and for which
period, shall be taken into account for the application of the fourth paragraph, part
d.
6
By general administrative order, rules may be laid down regarding what is understood by
demonstrating that one cares for another, as referred to in the third paragraph.

A resident of the Netherlands shall be eligible in accordance with the provisions of this
Act for a tailored provision, consisting of:
a.
support for his self-reliance and participation to be provided by the executive board
of the municipality of which he is a resident, insofar as he is not sufficiently self-reliant
or able to participate on his own strength, with usual assistance, with informal care
or with assistance from other persons in his social network due to a disability, chronic
psychological or psychosocial problems,
b.
sheltered living, to be provided by the executive board of the municipality to which
he applies, insofar as he is not able to maintain himself in society on his own strength,
with usual assistance, with informal care or with assistance from other persons in his
social network due to psychological or psychosocial problems, or
c.
shelter, to be provided by the executive board of the municipality to which he applies,
if he has left the home situation, whether or not in connection with risks to his safety
as a result of domestic violence, and is not able to maintain himself in society on his
own strength, with usual assistance, with informal care or with assistance from other
persons in his social network.

1
The municipal council shall periodically adopt a plan concerning the policy to be pursued by the municipal executive
with regard to social support.
2
The plan describes the policy intentions regarding decisions to be taken by the executive board
or actions to be performed that are aimed at:
a.
promoting social cohesion, the accessibility of facilities, services and spaces for
people with disabilities, promoting safety and liveability in the municipality,
as well as preventing and combating domestic violence;
b.
enabling the various categories of informal carers and volunteers as much as possible
to perform their tasks as informal carer or volunteer;
c.
establishing at an early stage whether residents require social support;
d.
preventing residents from becoming dependent on social support;
e.
providing general facilities to residents who require social support;
f.
providing customised provisions to support the self-reliance and participation
of residents of the municipality who are unable or insufficiently able to do so on their own strength,
with usual help, with informal care or with help from other persons from their social network;
g.
providing customised provisions to persons who are unable to maintain themselves in society on their own
strength and require sheltered living or reception in connection
with psychological or psychosocial problems or because they have left the home situation,
whether or not in connection with risks to their safety as a result of domestic violence;
h.
implementing the Convention on the Rights of Persons with Disabilities concluded in New York
on 13 December 2006 (Treaty Series 2007, 169).
3
The plan is aimed at ensuring that:
a.
clients can remain in their own living environment for as long as possible;
b.
clients who receive sheltered living or reception have a safe living environment
and, if possible, are again able to maintain themselves in society on their own strength.
4
In the plan, special attention is given to:
a.
service provision that is as integrated as possible in the field of social support,
public health, prevention, care, youth assistance, education, welfare, housing, work and
income;
b.
cooperation with health insurers and healthcare providers as referred to in the
Health Insurance Act
with a view to service provision that is as integrated as possible;
c.
choice options between providers for those to whom a customised provision
is provided, taking into account the religious denomination,
life philosophy and cultural background of clients, in particular for
small target groups;
d.
the accessibility of facilities, services and spaces for people with disabilities;
e.
the manner in which the continuity of assistance is guaranteed, in particular with regard
to the person who can no longer receive youth assistance as
referred to in the
Youth Act
due to reaching a certain age;
f.
possibilities to enable people to remain in their own living environment for as long
as possible with the deployment of guidance, including day care;
g.
the manner in which residents are informed about the persons who can act
as representative of a client who cannot be considered capable of a reasonable
assessment of his interests in this regard.
5
The plan indicates in which manner the municipality

#### article 2.1.7

applies or the reason for not applying that article.
6
The plan indicates which results the municipal executive wishes to achieve in the period
covered by the plan, which criteria are used to measure how
these results have been achieved and which performance indicators are used with
regard to providers.

1
By ordinance it may be determined that a client owes a contribution to the costs:
a.
for the use of a general provision, not being client support;
b.
for a customised provision or a personal budget.
2
In the ordinance the amount of the contribution for the different types of
provisions may be determined differently and it may be determined that:
a.
for persons belonging to groups described therein, a discount indicated therein
on the contribution for a general provision applies;
b.
the contribution for a customised provision or a personal budget:
1°.
is owed as long as the client makes use of the customised provision or
during the period for which the personal budget is provided, and
2°.
is dependent on the income and assets of the client and his or her spouse.
3
The total of the contributions for a customised provision or a personal budget
does not exceed the cost price. The ordinance determines in what manner
the cost price is calculated.
4
By or pursuant to general administrative order, rules are established with regard
to the contributions for a customised provision or a personal budget,
including rules concerning:
a.
the amount,
b.
the manner in which income and assets are taken into account in the determination,
c.
the period within which the contribution owed must be paid,
d.
the method of collection, and
e.
the grounds for exception to owing a contribution.
5
The proposal for a general administrative order as referred to in the fourth paragraph,
which relates to the assets referred to in the second paragraph, shall not be made
until four weeks after the draft has been submitted to both Houses of the States General.
If one of the Houses of the States General decides not to agree with the draft,
no proposal shall be made and a new draft may not be submitted to both Houses of the States General
until six weeks after the decision of
that House of the States General.
6
The contribution for a customised provision or a personal budget is,
with the exception of that for shelter, determined and collected for the municipality by the
CAK.
7
The ordinance determines which body determines and collects the contribution for a customised provision
or a personal budget for shelter. The executive board
ensures that notification is given to the CAK of the contributions that have been determined by
the said body, insofar as not relating to persons
who have left the home situation in connection with risks to their safety as
a result of domestic violence.
