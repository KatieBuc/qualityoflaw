# Law on Protection from Domestic Violence

Law on Protection from Domestic Violence
The Law was published in the "Official Gazette of Montenegro", No. 46/2010.
See: Art. 152 of the Law - 40/2011-1.

I. BASIC PROVISIONS
Subject of the law

## Article 1

Protection from domestic violence (hereinafter: protection) is exercised in accordance with this law.
Domestic violence

## Article 2

Domestic violence (hereinafter: violence), within the meaning of this law, is an act or omission by a family member that endangers the physical, psychological, sexual or economic integrity, mental health and peace of another family member, regardless of the place where it was committed.
Family members

## Article 3

Family members, within the meaning of this law, are considered to be:
1) spouses or former spouses, their children and the children of each of them;
2) common-law spouses or former common-law spouses, regardless of the duration of the common-law union, their children and the children of each of them;
3) blood relatives and relatives from full adoption in direct line without limitation, and in collateral line up to and including the fourth degree;
4) relatives from incomplete adoption;
5) relatives by marriage up to and including the second degree in marital or common-law union;
6) persons living in the same family household, regardless of kinship;
7) persons who have a common child or the child has been conceived.
Right to assistance and protection

## Article 4

A victim of violence (hereinafter: victim) has the right to psychosocial and legal assistance and social and medical protection, in accordance with the law.
Protection of the victim is also ensured by imposing protective measures.
Special assistance and protection is enjoyed by a victim who is a child, elderly person, person with disability and a person who is not capable of taking care of themselves, in accordance with the law.
Institutions dealing with protection

## Article 5

The administrative body competent for police affairs (hereinafter: police), the body for misdemeanours, the State Prosecutor's Office, the social welfare centre or other institution of social and child protection, health institution, as well as other bodies and institutions dealing with protection are obliged to provide, within their powers, complete and coordinated protection that is necessary for the protection of the victim depending on the degree of their endangerment.
A non-governmental organization, other legal entity and natural person may provide protection in accordance with the law.
The bodies and institutions from paragraph 1 of this Article are obliged to give priority to resolving cases of violence in accordance with the law and to ensure mutual notification and assistance in order to prevent and detect violence, eliminate causes and provide assistance to the victim in establishing conditions for a safe life.
Urgency of procedure

## Article 6

In procedures relating to protection, the bodies and institutions from Article 5 paragraph 1 of this law are obliged to act urgently, taking into account that the interest and well-being of the victim, and especially if the victim is a child,

elderly person, person with disability and a person who is not capable of taking care of themselves, are a priority in those procedures.
Use of gender-sensitive language

## Article 7

Terms used in this law for natural persons in the masculine gender include the same terms in the feminine gender.
II. PROTECTION OF THE VICTIM
1. Forms and methods of providing assistance and protection
Forms of violence

## Article 8

Endangering the physical, psychological, sexual or economic integrity, mental health and peace of another family member is considered especially if a family member:
1) uses physical force, regardless of whether bodily injury to another family member has occurred;
2) threatens with attack or causes danger that may cause a feeling of personal insecurity or psychological pain to another family member;
3) verbally attacks, swears, calls by derogatory name and otherwise insults another family member;
4) restricts another family member's freedom to communicate with third parties;
5) exhausts by work, denies sleep and other type of rest, threatens with eviction from the apartment and taking away children;
6) sexually harasses another family member;
7) stalks and otherwise grossly harasses another family member;
8) damages or destroys common property or property of another family member or attempts to do so;
9) denies basic means of subsistence to another family member;
10) by rude behaviour endangers the family peace of a family member with whom they do not live in a family community.
Endangering the physical, psychological, sexual or economic integrity, mental health and peace of another family member is also considered if a family member does not take sufficient care of:
1) nutrition, hygiene, clothing, medical protection or regular school attendance or does not prevent the child from harmful association, vagrancy, begging or theft or otherwise to a greater extent neglects the upbringing and education of the child;
2) nutrition, hygiene, clothing or medical protection of another family member whom they are obliged to care for, and who needs special assistance due to illness, disability, old age or other personal characteristics due to which they are not capable of taking care of themselves.
A more serious form of domestic violence is considered to be the concealment of a family member with special needs.

Duty to report violence

## Article 9

State bodies, other bodies, health, educational and other institutions are obliged to report to the police committed violence of which they become aware in the performance of duties within their competence or activity.
A report on committed violence must be submitted to the police by the responsible person in the body or institution from paragraph 1 of this Article, as well as a health and social worker, teacher, educator and other person who becomes aware of committed violence in the performance of their duties.
The body for misdemeanours and the police are obliged to notify the social welfare centre of reported violence.
Emergency intervention

## Article 10

After becoming aware of violence, the police shall without delay undertake actions and measures aimed at protecting the victim, in accordance with this law and laws regulating the work and powers of the police, misdemeanour procedure, criminal procedure and witness protection.
The social welfare centre, or other institution of social and child protection, health institution, as well as other bodies and institutions dealing with protection are obliged to, without delay, provide protection and assistance to the victim in accordance with their competences.
The bodies and institutions from paragraph 2 of this Article are obliged to take care of all the needs of the victim and enable them access to all forms of assistance and protection.

Plan of assistance to the victim

## Article 11

The social welfare centre forms an expert team consisting of representatives of that institution, bodies and services of local administration, police, non-governmental organizations and experts dealing with family issues, in order to determine the plan of assistance to the victim and coordinate activities in the process of assisting the victim, in accordance with their needs and choice.
The plan of assistance to the victim specifically contains measures that need to be undertaken in accordance with the law regulating social and child protection. If the victim is a child, the plan of assistance to the victim also contains measures for the protection of the child in accordance with the law regulating family relations.
For the purpose of implementing activities from paragraph 1 of this Article, the expert team may also be formed by another body, institution or organization dealing with protection.

Social protection

## Article 12

Social protection of the victim includes material and non-material assistance, accommodation and social work services, in accordance with the law regulating social and child protection.
Social welfare centres are obliged to establish precise records of children and persons with special needs in their territory.
For the purpose of protection from violence against these persons, the centres are obliged to form special teams that will, at least once a month, visit families in which children and persons with special needs are accommodated or live, monitor the treatment of these persons and, based on the established situation, prepare a written report.
Social welfare centres are obliged to, at least once every six months, submit a written report to the ministry competent for social welfare affairs on the degree of realisation of social protection of children and persons with special needs.

Legal assistance

## Article 13

The victim has the right to free legal assistance, in accordance with a special law.

Security of the victim

## Article 14

A police officer is obliged to accompany the victim to the apartment or other residential space when taking personal belongings that are necessary for their daily life, unless the victim expressly objects to this.
The action from paragraph 1 of this article may also be carried out to secure temporary accommodation and care for the victim.
Confidentiality of proceedings

## Article 15

The bodies, institutions, organizations and other legal and natural persons from Article 5 of this law are obliged to
maintain confidentiality of information and ensure protection of personal data, in accordance with the law.
Information based on which the victim or a family member of the victim can be identified must not be publicly disclosed about the victim or the family member who committed the violence (hereinafter: perpetrator of violence), unless the adult victim expressly consents to this.

Confidential person

## Article 16

The victim may choose a person who will be present at all procedures and actions related to protection (hereinafter: confidential person).
The confidential person may be a family member, a person from a body, institution, non-governmental organization and other legal entity or another person in whom the victim has confidence.
The confidential person cannot be the perpetrator of violence.
The victim may choose a confidential person before or during the procedure and undertaking of actions related to protection.
Competent bodies are obliged to enable the presence of the confidential person in all procedures and actions in which the victim is involved, and which are related to family relations.

Multidisciplinary team and principle of cooperation

## Article 17

For the purpose of organizing, monitoring and improving coordinated and efficient protection, the body or institution from Article 5 paragraph 1 of this law may form a team of experts from the fields of social and child protection, health protection, judiciary, police protection, protection of human rights and freedoms, as well as representatives of non-governmental organizations dealing with protection.

Protection strategy

## Article 18

Protection is implemented in accordance with the strategy for protection from domestic violence (hereinafter: Strategy), which contains:
1)	assessment of the situation and identification of key problems in social and other protection;
2)	objectives and measures for improving social and other protection, particularly in relation to: raising the level of citizens' awareness about the problem of violence and forming attitudes about the unacceptability of violence; development of programs for prevention of violence; support to the family in prevention of violence; further development of the normative framework in the field of protection; strengthening cooperation of bodies, institutions, organizations and other legal and natural persons dealing with protection; acquisition of new knowledge and skills of all those dealing with protection; improvement of the system for collection and analysis of data and reporting on cases of violence.
Activities for implementation of the objectives and measures from paragraph 1 item 2 of this article are determined by the action plan for implementation of the Strategy.
The Strategy and the action plan for implementation of the Strategy are adopted by the Government of Montenegro.
2.	Protective measures
Purpose of protective measures

## Article 19

Protective measures are imposed for the purpose of preventing and suppressing violence, eliminating the consequences of committed violence and undertaking effective measures for re-education of the perpetrator of violence and eliminating circumstances that favor or encourage the commission of new violence.

Types of protective measures

## Article 20

One or more protective measures may be imposed on the perpetrator of violence:
1)	removal from the apartment or other residential space (hereinafter: removal from the apartment);
2)	prohibition of approach;
3)	prohibition of harassment and stalking;
4)	mandatory treatment for addiction;
5)	mandatory psychosocial treatment.
The bodies and institutions from Article 5 paragraph 1 of this law are obliged to provide the perpetrator of violence with appropriate information about their rights.

Removal from the apartment

## Article 21

Removal from the apartment may be imposed on the perpetrator of violence who lives with the victim in an apartment or other residential space, regardless of the ownership and other rights of the perpetrator of violence and the victim to the apartment or other residential space, if there is a danger that they will commit violence again.
The perpetrator of violence to whom removal from the apartment has been imposed is obliged to immediately leave the apartment or other residential space.
Removal from the apartment is imposed for a duration that cannot be shorter than thirty days nor longer than six months.
Prohibition of approach

## Article 22

Prohibition of approach to the victim may be imposed on the perpetrator of violence if there is a danger that they will commit violence again or if the presence of the perpetrator of violence near the victim would create a high degree of mental suffering in the victim which, for a shorter or longer time, prevents normal mental activities of the victim.
The body for misdemeanors is obliged to determine in the decision on prohibition of approach the place or area in which the perpetrator of violence must not approach the victim.
Prohibition of approach is imposed for a duration that cannot be shorter than thirty days nor longer than one year.
Prohibition of harassment and stalking

## Article 23

Prohibition of harassment and stalking of the victim may be imposed on the perpetrator of violence if there is a danger that they will commit violence again.
The protective measure from paragraph 1 of this article is imposed for a duration that cannot be shorter than thirty days nor longer than one year.

Mandatory treatment for addiction

## Article 24

Mandatory treatment for addiction may be imposed on the perpetrator of violence who commits violence as a result of addiction to alcohol, narcotic drugs or psychotropic substances, and there is a danger that they will commit violence again due to that addiction.
The protective measure from paragraph 1 of this article lasts as long as there is a need for treatment, but no longer than one year.
Mandatory psychosocial treatment

## Article 25

Mandatory psychosocial treatment may be imposed on the perpetrator of violence for the purpose of eliminating the causes of violent behavior and re-education, that is, with the aim of reducing and eliminating the danger that they will commit violence again.
The protective measure from paragraph 1 of this article lasts until the cessation of the reasons for which it was imposed, but no longer than six months.
The detailed manner of determining and implementing the protective measure from paragraph 1 of this article is prescribed by the ministry responsible for social welfare affairs.
3.	Procedure for determining protective measures
Imposition of protective measure

## Article 26

A protective measure may be imposed together with a penalty or as an independent sanction.
The body for misdemeanors may impose one or more protective measures on the perpetrator of violence when the conditions for their imposition prescribed by this law exist.
The body for misdemeanors may decide to extend the duration of the protective measures from Articles 21 to 25 of this law if the reasons for which they were determined have not ceased, but no longer than two years.

Applicants

## Article 27

A request for determination of a protective measure (hereinafter: request) may be submitted by the victim or their representative, the center for social work, that is, another institution of social and child protection, the police or the state prosecutor.
The body for misdemeanors may also determine a protective measure ex officio.

Order

## Article 28

A police officer may, in order to eliminate danger to the physical integrity of the victim, order the perpetrator of violence to be removed or prohibited from returning to an apartment or other residential premises, which may not last longer than three days.
A written order for removal or prohibition from returning to an apartment or other residential premises must be delivered by the police officer to the perpetrator of violence and the victim immediately, and at the latest within two hours, in the presence of an adult person who may be another police officer, but who is not a family member.
The written order from paragraph 2 of this article must contain: the day and hour of removal or prohibition from returning to an apartment or other residential premises; the boundaries of the area in which the perpetrator of violence may not move, stay or approach the victim and the address of residence of the perpetrator of violence for the duration of the removal or prohibition from returning.
When leaving the apartment or other residential premises, the perpetrator of violence has the right to take their personal belongings necessary for daily life and the obligation to hand over the keys to the apartment or other residential premises to the police officer.
The police officer attaches the written order from paragraph 2 of this article to the official report on the incident and immediately, and at the latest within 12 hours, notifies the misdemeanour authority and the social welfare centre.
The misdemeanour authority shall, within the period from paragraph 1 of this article, decide whether to impose a protective measure.
The detailed content and appearance of the form of the written order from paragraph 2 of this article and the manner of carrying out actions in applying the provisions of this article shall be prescribed by the ministry responsible for internal affairs.

Imposition of a protective measure before and during proceedings

## Article 29

If it assesses that it is necessary to protect the victim without delay, the misdemeanour authority may impose a protective measure before initiating and during proceedings, at the latest within 48 hours from the time of receipt of the request.
The misdemeanour authority may request from the social welfare centre or other social and child protection institution to provide assistance in obtaining necessary evidence and to present its opinion on the appropriateness of the requested protective measure.
If the request is submitted before initiating proceedings, and the applicant of that request does not submit a request to initiate proceedings within five days, the misdemeanour authority shall revoke the imposed protective measure.
The misdemeanour authority is obliged to warn the applicant of the consequences of failure to submit the request from paragraph 3 of this article.
Appeal

## Article 30

An appeal against the decision on determining a protective measure may be filed within three days from the day of delivery of the decision.
The appeal is decided by the second instance authority within three days from the day of receipt of the appeal. The appeal does not delay the execution of the decision on determining a protective measure.

Extension, termination and replacement of a protective measure imposed before and during proceedings

## Article 31

A protective measure imposed before and during proceedings may last until the reasons for which the measure was determined cease, and at the longest until the completion of proceedings.
Before the completion of proceedings, the misdemeanour authority may replace a protective measure imposed before and during proceedings with another protective measure.

Obligation to comply with a protective measure

## Article 32

The perpetrator of violence is obliged to act in accordance with the imposed protective measure.
A person who, in the scope of their work, learns that the perpetrator of violence is not acting in accordance with the imposed protective measure is obliged to notify the misdemeanour authority, the social welfare centre, or other social and child protection institution, the police or the state prosecutor.

Execution of the decision

## Article 33

The misdemeanour authority is obliged to deliver the decision on the imposed protective measure immediately to the authority or institution responsible for carrying out the execution, and at the latest within three days from the day of adoption of the decision.
The decision on the imposed protective measure from Articles 21, 22 and 23 of this law is delivered for execution to the police.
The decision on the imposed protective measure from Articles 24 and 25 of this law is delivered to the authority, or institution that executes the protective measure in accordance with the law governing the treatment and rehabilitation of persons addicted to psychoactive substances (alcoholics and drug addicts) and persons with other behavioural disorders.
The detailed manner of execution of the decision on the imposed protective measure from Articles 21, 22 and 23 of this law shall be prescribed by the ministry responsible for internal affairs, and the detailed manner of execution of the decision on the imposed protective measure from Articles 24 and 25 of this law shall be prescribed by the ministry responsible for health affairs.

Delivery of the decision and records

## Article 34

The misdemeanour authority is obliged to deliver the decision on the imposed protective measure to the social welfare centre in whose territory the victim and the perpetrator of violence have residence or domicile.
Records on reported cases of violence, victims, perpetrators of violence, imposed protective measures, as well as other protection and assistance measures are kept by the authorities and institutions from Article 5 paragraph 1 of this law, in accordance with their competences, and they deliver them to the ministry responsible for the protection of human and minority rights.
Corresponding application of the law

## Article 35

The provisions of laws governing the work and powers of the police, misdemeanour procedure, criminal procedure, criminal sanctions and their application and execution apply to the procedure for determining and implementing protective measures, unless otherwise regulated by this law.

Protection shall be realised in accordance with the strategy for protection against domestic violence (hereinafter: Strategy), which contains:
1)	assessment of the situation and identification of key problems in social and other protection;
2)	objectives and measures for improving social and other protection, particularly in relation to: raising the level of citizens' awareness of the problem of violence and forming attitudes about the unacceptability of violence; development of programmes for violence prevention; support to the family in violence prevention; further development of the normative framework in the field of protection; strengthening cooperation of bodies, institutions, organizations and other legal and natural persons dealing with protection; acquisition of new knowledge and skills of all those dealing with protection; improvement of the system for collection and analysis of data and reporting on cases of violence.
The activities for implementing the objectives and measures referred to in paragraph 1 item 2 of this Article shall be determined by an action plan for implementing the Strategy.
The Strategy and the action plan for implementing the Strategy shall be adopted by the Government of Montenegro.
2.	Protective measures
Purpose of protective measures

## Article 19

Protective measures shall be imposed for the purpose of preventing and combating violence, eliminating the consequences of committed violence and undertaking effective measures for re-education of the perpetrator of violence and eliminating circumstances that favour or encourage the commission of new violence.

Types of protective measures

## Article 20

One or more protective measures may be imposed on the perpetrator of violence:
1)	removal from the apartment or other residential premises (hereinafter: removal from the apartment);
2)	prohibition of approach;
3)	prohibition of harassment and stalking;
4)	mandatory treatment for addiction;
5)	mandatory psychosocial treatment.
The bodies and institutions referred to in Article 5 paragraph 1 of this Law are obliged to provide the perpetrator of violence with appropriate information about his rights.

Removal from the apartment

## Article 21

Removal from the apartment may be imposed on the perpetrator of violence who lives with the victim in an apartment or other residential premises, regardless of the ownership and other rights of the perpetrator of violence and the victim to the apartment or other residential premises, if there is a danger that he will commit violence again.
The perpetrator of violence to whom removal from the apartment has been imposed is obliged to immediately leave the apartment or other residential premises.
Removal from the apartment shall be imposed for a duration that cannot be shorter than thirty days nor longer than six months.
Prohibition of approach

## Article 22

Prohibition of approach to the victim may be imposed on the perpetrator of violence if there is a danger that he will commit violence again or if the presence of the perpetrator of violence in the vicinity of the victim would create a high degree of mental suffering in the victim which, for a shorter or longer time, prevents the victim's normal mental activities.
The misdemeanour authority is obliged to determine in the decision on prohibition of approach the place or area in which the perpetrator of violence may not approach the victim.
Prohibition of approach shall be imposed for a duration that cannot be shorter than thirty days nor longer than one year.
Prohibition of harassment and stalking

## Article 23

Prohibition of harassment and stalking of the victim may be imposed on the perpetrator of violence if there is a danger that he will commit violence again.
The protective measure referred to in paragraph 1 of this Article shall be imposed for a duration that cannot be shorter than thirty days nor longer than one year.

Mandatory treatment for addiction

## Article 24

Mandatory treatment for addiction may be imposed on the perpetrator of violence who commits violence as a result of addiction to alcohol, narcotic drugs or psychotropic substances, and there is a danger that he will commit violence again due to that addiction.
The protective measure referred to in paragraph 1 of this Article shall last as long as there is a need for treatment, but not longer than one year.
Mandatory psychosocial treatment

## Article 25

Mandatory psychosocial treatment may be imposed on the perpetrator of violence for the purpose of eliminating the causes of violent behaviour and re-education, that is, with the aim of reducing and eliminating the danger that he will commit violence again.
The protective measure referred to in paragraph 1 of this Article shall last until the cessation of the reasons for which it was imposed, but not longer than six months.
The detailed manner of determining and implementing the protective measure referred to in paragraph 1 of this Article shall be prescribed by the ministry responsible for social welfare affairs.
3.	Procedure for determining protective measures
Imposition of protective measure

## Article 26

A protective measure may be imposed together with a penalty or as an independent sanction.
The misdemeanour authority may impose one or more protective measures on the perpetrator of violence when the conditions for their imposition prescribed by this law exist.
The misdemeanour authority may decide to extend the duration of protective measures from Articles 21 to 25 of this law if the reasons for which they were determined have not ceased, but not longer than two years.

Applicants

## Article 27

A request for determination of a protective measure (hereinafter: request) may be submitted by the victim or their representative, the social welfare centre, or another social and child protection institution, the police or the state prosecutor.
The misdemeanour authority may also determine a protective measure ex officio.

Order

## Article 28

A police officer may, in order to eliminate danger to the physical integrity of the victim, order the perpetrator of violence to be removed or prohibited from returning to an apartment or other residential premises, which may not last longer than three days.
A written order on removal or prohibition from returning to an apartment or other residential premises must be delivered by the police officer to the perpetrator of violence and the victim immediately, and at the latest within two hours, in the presence of an adult person who may be another police officer, and who is not a family member.
The written order from paragraph 2 of this article must contain: the day and hour of removal or prohibition from returning to an apartment or other residential premises; the boundaries of the area in which the perpetrator of violence may not move, stay or approach the victim and the address of residence of the perpetrator of violence for the duration of the removal or prohibition from returning.
When leaving the apartment or other residential premises, the perpetrator of violence has the right to take their personal belongings necessary for daily life and the obligation to hand over the keys to the apartment or other residential premises to the police officer.
The police officer attaches the written order from paragraph 2 of this article to the official report on the event and immediately, and at the latest within 12 hours, notifies the misdemeanour authority and the social welfare centre thereof.
The misdemeanour authority shall, within the time limit from paragraph 1 of this article, decide whether to impose a protective measure.
The detailed content and appearance of the form of the written order from paragraph 2 of this article and the manner of carrying out actions in the application of the provisions of this article shall be prescribed by the ministry responsible for internal affairs.

Imposition of a protective measure before and during proceedings

## Article 29

If it assesses that it is necessary to protect the victim without delay, the misdemeanour authority may impose a protective measure before initiation and during proceedings, at the latest within 48 hours from the time of receipt of the request.
The misdemeanour authority may request the social welfare centre or another social and child protection institution to assist it in obtaining the necessary evidence and to present its opinion on the appropriateness of the requested protective measure.
If the request is submitted before initiation of proceedings, and the applicant of that request does not submit a request for initiation of proceedings within five days, the misdemeanour authority shall revoke the imposed protective measure.
The misdemeanour authority is obliged to warn the applicant of the consequences of failure to submit the request from paragraph 3 of this article.
Appeal

## Article 30

An appeal against a decision on determination of a protective measure may be filed within three days from the day of delivery of the decision.
The appeal is decided by the second instance authority within three days from the day of receipt of the appeal. The appeal does not delay the execution of the decision on determination of a protective measure.

Extension, cessation and replacement of a protective measure imposed before and during proceedings

## Article 31

A protective measure imposed before and during proceedings may last until the reasons for which the measure was determined cease, and at the longest until the conclusion of proceedings.
Before the conclusion of proceedings, the misdemeanour authority may replace a protective measure imposed before and during proceedings with another protective measure.

Obligation to comply with a protective measure

## Article 32

The perpetrator of violence is obliged to act in accordance with the imposed protective measure.
A person who in the course of their work learns that the perpetrator of violence is not acting in accordance with the imposed protective measure is obliged to notify the misdemeanour authority, the social welfare centre, or another social and child protection institution, the police or the state prosecutor thereof.

Execution of the decision

## Article 33

The misdemeanour authority is obliged to deliver the decision on the imposed protective measure immediately to the authority or institution competent for carrying out execution, and at the latest within three days from the day of adoption of the decision.
The decision on the imposed protective measure from Articles 21, 22 and 23 of this law is delivered for execution to the police.
The decision on the imposed protective measure from Articles 24 and 25 of this law is delivered to the authority, or institution that executes the protective measure in accordance with the law governing the treatment and rehabilitation of persons dependent on psychoactive substances (alcoholics and drug addicts) and persons with other behavioural disorders.
The detailed manner of execution of the decision on the imposed protective measure from Articles 21, 22 and 23 of this law shall be prescribed by the ministry responsible for internal affairs, and the detailed manner of execution of the decision on the imposed protective measure from Articles 24 and 25 of this law shall be prescribed by the ministry responsible for health affairs.

Delivery of the decision and records

## Article 34

The misdemeanour authority is obliged to deliver the decision on the imposed protective measure to the social welfare centre in whose territory the victim and the perpetrator of violence have residence or stay.
Records on reported cases of violence, victims, perpetrators of violence, imposed protective measures, as well as on other protection and assistance measures are kept by the authorities and institutions from Article 5 paragraph 1 of this law, in accordance with their competences, and they deliver them to the ministry responsible for the protection of human and minority rights.
Corresponding application of law

## Article 35

The provisions of laws governing the work and powers of the police, misdemeanour procedure, criminal procedure, criminal sanctions and their application and execution apply to the procedure for determining and implementing protective measures, unless otherwise regulated by this law.
III.	PENAL PROVISIONS
Misdemeanour liability of a family member

## Article 36

A fine of at least three times the amount of the minimum wage in Montenegro or imprisonment of at least ten days shall be imposed for a misdemeanour on a family member, if they:
1)	use physical force, regardless of whether bodily injury to another family member has occurred;
2)	threaten with an attack or cause danger that may produce a feeling of personal insecurity or mental pain in another family member;
3)	verbally attack, swear at, call by a derogatory name and otherwise insult another family member;
4)	restrict another family member's freedom to communicate with third parties;
5)	exhaust by work, deprive of sleep and other rest, threaten with eviction from the apartment and taking away of children;
6)	sexually harass another family member;
7)	stalk and otherwise grossly harass another family member;
8)	damage or destroy joint property or the property of another family member or attempt to do so;
9)	deny basic means of subsistence to another family member;
10)	by insolent behaviour endanger the family peace of a family member with whom they do not live in a family community (

A fine of at least five times the amount of the minimum wage in Montenegro or imprisonment of at least twenty days shall be imposed for a misdemeanour on an adult family member if they commit violence from paragraph 1 of this article in the presence of a child.
A fine of at least ten times the amount of the minimum wage in Montenegro or imprisonment of at least thirty days shall be imposed for a misdemeanour on a family member who commits violence from paragraph 1 of this article if the victim is a child.
