LAW TO PREVENT, PUNISH AND ERADICATE VIOLENCE AGAINST WOMEN AND MEMBERS OF THE FAMILY GROUP

## TITLE I

SUBSTANTIVE PROVISIONS FOR THE PREVENTION, PUNISHMENT AND ERADICATION OF VIOLENCE AGAINST WOMEN AND MEMBERS OF THE FAMILY GROUP

### CHAPTER I GENERAL PROVISIONS

#### Article 1. Object of the Law

The purpose of this Law is to prevent, eradicate and punish all forms of violence occurring in the public or private sphere against women on the basis of their status as such, and against members of the family group; especially when they are in a situation of vulnerability, due to age or physical condition such as girls, boys, adolescents, older adults and persons with disabilities.
To this end, it establishes mechanisms, measures and comprehensive policies for the prevention, care and protection of victims as well as reparation for the damage caused; and provides for the prosecution, punishment and re-education of convicted aggressors in order to guarantee women and the family group a life free of violence, ensuring the full exercise of their rights.

#### Article 2. Guiding principles

In the interpretation and application of this Law, and in general, in all measures adopted by the State through its public powers and institutions, as well as in the action of society, the following principles are given preferential consideration:
1.	Principle of equality and non-discrimination

Equality between women and men is guaranteed. All forms of discrimination are prohibited. Discrimination is understood as any type of distinction, exclusion or restriction, based on sex, that has the purpose or result of impairing or nullifying the recognition, enjoyment or exercise of the rights of persons.

2.	Principle of the best interests of the child
In all actions concerning girls and boys adopted by public or private social welfare institutions, courts, administrative authorities or legislative bodies, the best interests of the child must be a primary consideration.

3.	Principle of due diligence
The State adopts without delay all policies aimed at preventing, punishing and eradicating all forms of violence against women and members of the family group. Corresponding sanctions must be imposed on authorities who fail to comply with this principle.

4.	Principle of immediate and timely intervention
Justice operators and the National Police of Peru, in the event of an act or threat of violence, must act in a timely manner, without delay for procedural, formal or other reasons, ordering the exercise of the protection measures provided for in the law and other regulations, with the purpose of effectively assisting the victim.

5.	Principle of simplicity and orality
All proceedings for violence against women and members of the family group are conducted with minimal formality, in friendly spaces for the alleged victims, encouraging them to trust the system and cooperate with it for adequate punishment of the aggressor and the restitution of their violated rights.

6.	Principle of reasonableness and proportionality
The prosecutor or judge in charge of any violence proceeding must weigh the proportionality between the potential harm caused and the protection and rehabilitation measures to be adopted. To this end, they must make a reasonableness judgment according to the circumstances of the case, issuing decisions that effectively protect the life, health and dignity of the victims. The adoption of these measures is adapted to the phases of the cycle of violence and to the various typologies presented by violence against women and members of the family group.

#### Article 3. Approaches

Operators, when applying this Law, consider the following approaches:

1.	Gender approach
Recognizes the existence of asymmetric circumstances in the relationship between men and women, built on the basis of gender differences that constitute one of the main causes of violence against women. This approach should guide the design of intervention strategies aimed at achieving equal opportunities between men and women.

2.	Comprehensive approach
Recognizes that violence against women involves multiple causes and factors that are present in different spheres, at the individual, family, community and structural levels. Therefore, it is necessary to establish interventions at the different levels in which people operate and from different disciplines.

3.	Intercultural approach
Recognizes the need for dialogue among the different cultures that are integrated into Peruvian society, in a way that allows recovering, from the various cultural contexts, all those expressions that are based on respect for the other person. This approach does not admit accepting discriminatory cultural practices that tolerate violence or hinder the enjoyment of equal rights between persons of different genders.

4.	Human rights approach
Recognizes that the main objective of any intervention under this Law must be the realization of human rights, identifying the rights holders and what they are entitled to according to their particular needs; likewise identifying the duty bearers and the obligations that correspond to them. It seeks to strengthen the capacity of rights holders to claim these rights and of duty bearers to fulfill their obligations.

5.	Intersectionality approach
Recognizes that women's experience of violence is influenced by factors and identities such as their ethnicity, color, religion; political or other opinion; national or social origin, patrimony; marital status, sexual orientation, HIV-positive status, immigrant or refugee status, age or disability; and, where appropriate, includes measures aimed at specific groups of women.

6.	Generational approach
Recognizes that it is necessary to identify power relations between different ages of life and their connections to improve living conditions or common development. It considers that childhood, youth, adulthood and old age must have a connection, since together they are contributing to a common history and must be strengthened generationally. It presents long-term contributions considering the different generations and placing importance on building co-responsibilities among them.

#### Article 4. Scope of application of the Law

The provisions of this Law apply to all types of violence against women on the basis of their status as such and against members of the family group.

### CHAPTER II

DEFINITION AND TYPES OF VIOLENCE AGAINST WOMEN AND MEMBERS OF THE FAMILY GROUP

#### Article 5. Definition of violence against women

Violence against women is any action or conduct that causes them death, harm or physical, sexual or psychological suffering because of their condition as such, both in the public and private spheres.
Violence against women is understood as:

a.	That which takes place within the family or domestic unit or in any other interpersonal relationship, whether the aggressor shares or has shared the same domicile as the woman. It includes, among others, rape, physical or psychological abuse and sexual abuse.

b.	That which takes place in the community, is perpetrated by any person and includes, among others, rape, sexual abuse, torture, trafficking in persons, forced prostitution, kidnapping and sexual harassment in the workplace, as well as in educational institutions, health establishments or any other place.

c.	That which is perpetrated or tolerated by agents of the State, wherever it occurs.

CONCORDANCES:	D.S.N° 009-2016-MIMP (Regulations), Art. 8 (Modalities of violence)

#### Article 6. Definition of violence against members of the family group

Violence against any member of the family group is any action or conduct that causes them death, harm or physical, sexual or psychological suffering and that occurs in the context of a relationship of responsibility, trust or power, on the part of one member to another of the family group.
Special consideration is given to girls, boys, adolescents, older adults and persons with disabilities.

#### Article 7. Subjects of protection of the Law

The subjects of protection of the Law are:

a.	Women throughout their life cycle: girl, adolescent, young woman, adult and older adult.

b.	Members of the family group. Understood as such, spouses, former spouses, cohabitants, former cohabitants; stepfathers, stepmothers; ascendants and descendants; collateral relatives of spouses and cohabitants up to the fourth degree of consanguinity and second degree of affinity; and those who, without having any of the conditions indicated above, live in the same household, provided that there are no contractual or employment relationships; and those who have procreated children in common, regardless of whether they live together or not, at the time the violence occurs.(*)

#### Article 8. Types of violence

The types of violence against women and members of the family group are:

a) Physical violence. It is the action or conduct that causes harm to bodily integrity or health. It includes mistreatment by negligence, neglect or deprivation of basic needs, which have caused physical harm or may cause it, regardless of the time required for recovery.

b) Psychological violence. It is the action or conduct, tending to control or isolate the person against their will, to humiliate or shame them and which may cause psychological harm. Psychological harm is the affectation or alteration of some of the mental functions or capacities of the person, produced by an event or a set of situations of violence, which determines a temporary or permanent, reversible or irreversible impairment of previous integral functioning.

c) Sexual violence. These are actions of a sexual nature that are committed against a person without their consent or under coercion. They include acts that do not involve penetration or any physical contact. Likewise,

exposure to pornographic material is considered as such and which violate the right of persons to decide voluntarily about their sexual or reproductive life, through threats, coercion, use of force or intimidation.

d) Economic or patrimonial violence. It is the action or omission that is directed to cause impairment to the economic or patrimonial resources of any person, through:

1. the disturbance of the possession, holding or ownership of their property;

2. the loss, removal, destruction, retention or unlawful appropriation of objects, work instruments, personal documents, property, assets and patrimonial rights;

3. the limitation of economic resources intended to satisfy their needs or deprivation of the indispensable means to live a dignified life; as well as the evasion of compliance with their maintenance obligations;

4. the limitation or control of their income, as well as the receipt of a lower salary for equal work, within the same workplace. (*)

### CHAPTER III

RIGHTS OF WOMEN AND THE FAMILY GROUP

#### Article 9. Right to a life free of violence

Women and members of the family group have the right to a life free of violence, to be valued and educated, to be free from all forms of discrimination, stigmatization and stereotyped patterns of behavior, social and cultural practices based on concepts of inferiority and subordination.

#### Article 10. Right to comprehensive assistance and protection

The entities that make up the National System for the Prevention, Punishment and Eradication of Violence against Women and Members of the Family Group allocate specialized human, logistical and budgetary resources for the purpose of detecting violence, assisting victims, protecting them and restoring their rights.

The rights considered in this article are:

a.	Access to information
Victims of violence against women and members of the family group have the right to receive full information and adequate advice in relation to their personal situation, through the services, agencies or offices of the State at its three levels of government and according to their particular needs.

It is the duty of the National Police of Peru, the Public Prosecutor's Office, the Judiciary and all justice operators to inform, under responsibility, with professionalism, impartiality and in strict respect of the right to privacy and confidentiality of the victim, about their rights and complaint mechanisms. In all institutions of the justice system and in the National Police of Peru, information about the rights that assist victims of violence and the care services that the State provides free of charge for them must be displayed in a visible place, in Spanish or in the local language. For this purpose, the delivery of an information booklet to the victim in their own language is mandatory. The Ministry of the Interior verifies compliance with this obligation.(*)

#### Article 11. Labor rights

The male or female worker who is a victim of the violence referred to in this Law has the following rights:
a. Not to suffer dismissal for reasons related to said acts of violence.

b. To change workplace insofar as possible and without detriment to their remuneration and category rights. The same applies to work schedule, as pertinent.

c. To justification of absences and tardiness to the workplace derived from said acts of violence. These absences may not exceed five working days in a period of thirty calendar days or more than fifteen working days in a period of one hundred eighty calendar days. For this purpose, the complaint filed before the police department or before the Public Prosecutor's Office are considered justificatory documents.

d. To suspension of the employment relationship. The judge in charge of the process may, at the request of the victim and considering the seriousness of the situation, grant up to a maximum of five consecutive months of suspension of the employment relationship without remuneration.

The reincorporation of the male or female worker to their workplace must be carried out under the same conditions existing at the time of suspension of the employment relationship.

#### Article 12. Rights in the field of education

The person who is a victim of the violence referred to in this Law has, among others, the following rights:
a. To change place and schedule of studies without detriment to their rights.

b. To justification of absences and tardiness derived from acts of violence. These absences or tardiness may not exceed five days in a period of thirty calendar days or more than fifteen days in a period of one hundred eighty calendar days.
c. To specialized attention in the educational sphere for the consequences of violence, so that the educational service responds to their needs without detriment to its quality.
It is the obligation of the State to formulate specific measures to favor the permanence of victims in the educational sphere and, if applicable, favor their reinsertion therein.

Complaints for acts of violence against women and members of the family group are regulated by the rules provided in this Law and, in a supplementary manner, by the Criminal Procedure Code, promulgated by Legislative Decree 957, and Law 27337, Code of Children and Adolescents.(*)

Family courts or those that perform their functions are competent to hear complaints for acts of violence against women or against members of the family group.(*)

#### Article 15. Complaint

The complaint may be filed in writing or verbally. When it is a verbal complaint, a record is drawn up with no other requirement than to provide a succinct account of the facts.

The complaint may be filed by the injured person or by any other person on their behalf, without the need to have their representation. It may also be filed by the Ombudsman's Office. No lawyer's signature, fee or any other formality is required.

Notwithstanding the foregoing, health and education professionals must report cases of violence against women or members of the family group that they become aware of in the performance of their activity.

When the National Police of Peru becomes aware of cases of violence against women or members of the family group, in any of its police stations nationwide, it must bring the facts to the attention of the family courts or those that perform their functions within twenty-four hours of learning of the fact, sending the report summarizing the actions taken.(*)

#### Article 16. Process

Within a maximum period of seventy-two hours following the filing of the complaint, the family court or its equivalent proceeds to evaluate the case and resolves in oral hearing the issuance of the required protection measures that are necessary. Likewise, ex officio or at the request of the victim, in the oral hearing it rules on precautionary measures that safeguard claims for alimony, visitation regimes, custody, suspension or extinction of parental authority, liquidation of property regime and other related aspects that are necessary to guarantee the well-being of the victims.

Having analyzed the proceedings, the family court or its equivalent proceeds to refer the case to the criminal prosecutor's office for the initiation of criminal proceedings in accordance with the rules of the Criminal Procedure Code, promulgated by Legislative Decree 957.(*)

#### Article 22. Protection measures

Among the protection measures that may be issued in proceedings for acts of violence against women and members of the family group are, among others, the following:

1. Removal of the aggressor from the domicile.

2. Impediment of approach or proximity to the victim in any form, at the distance determined by the judicial authority.

3. Prohibition of communication with the victim via postal, telephone, electronic means; likewise, via chat, social networks, institutional network, intranet or other networks or forms of communication.

4. Prohibition of the right to possess and carry weapons for the aggressor, with notification to the National Superintendence of Control of Security Services, Weapons, Ammunition and Explosives for Civil Use to proceed to render void the license for possession and use, and to seize the weapons that are in possession of persons in respect of whom the protection measure has been issued.

5. Inventory of their assets.

6. Any other measure required for the protection of the personal integrity and life of the victims or family members. (*)

#### Article 24. Non-compliance with protection measures

Whoever disobeys, fails to comply with or resists a protection measure ordered in a proceeding arising from acts that constitute acts of violence against women or against members of the family group, commits the crime of resistance or disobedience to authority provided for in the Criminal Code.

#### Article 25. Protection of victims in investigative proceedings

In the processing of cases for violence against women and members of the family group, confrontation and conciliation between the victim and the aggressor are prohibited. The reconstruction of the facts must be carried out without the presence of the victim, unless the victim over fourteen years of age requests it, without prejudice to the provisions of article 194, subsection 3, of the Criminal Procedure Code, promulgated by Legislative Decree 957.

#### Article 26. Content of medical certificates and reports

The physical and mental health certificates issued by doctors of public health establishments of the different sectors and institutions of the State and levels of government, have probative value regarding the state of physical and mental health in proceedings for violence against women and members of the family group.

Certificates issued by parochial health centers and private establishments whose operation is authorized by the Ministry of Health have equal value.

The corresponding certificates of qualification of the physical and psychological harm of the victim must be in accordance with the medical-legal parameters of the Institute of Legal Medicine and Forensic Sciences of the Public Prosecutor's Office.

Medical certificates contain detailed information on the results of the physical and psychological evaluations to which the victim has been subjected. If applicable, certificates of physical evaluations must necessarily state the qualification of days of medical attention as well as the qualification of days of incapacity.

Within the framework of the care provided by all public and private health establishments, they must safeguard the adequate obtaining, preservation and documentation of evidence of acts of violence.

The psychological reports of the Women's Emergency Centers and other specialized state services have probative value of the state of mental health in proceedings for violence against women and members of the family group.

In the public sector, the issuance of certificates and the medical consultation that originates them, as well as complementary examinations or tests to issue diagnoses are free of charge.

For the purposes of this Law, it is not necessary to hold the special expert ratification hearing; therefore, the presence of professionals is not required to ratify the certificates and evaluations they have issued in order to grant them probative value. (*)

#### Article 27

Services for promotion, prevention and recovery of victims of violence
The protection of women and members of the family group against acts of violence is of public interest. The State is responsible for promoting prevention against such acts and the recovery of victims.

It is State policy to create care and prevention services against violence.

#### Article 29. Implementation and Registry of Temporary Shelter Homes

The creation of temporary shelter homes is a permanent policy of the State.

The Ministry of Women and Vulnerable Populations implements and administers the registry of temporary shelter homes that meets quality standards in service provision. The information in this registry is confidential and will be used for coordination, protection and technical assistance processes.

Local, provincial and district governments, and regional governments and private institutions that manage and administer temporary shelter homes shall provide information and access to the Ministry of Women and Vulnerable Populations for the fulfillment of its monitoring, follow-up and evaluation functions.

The Ministry of Women and Vulnerable Populations approves the minimum requirements to create and operate temporary shelter homes, as well as the minimum quality standards for service provision.

### CHAPTER II

REEDUCATION OF AGGRESSORS

#### Article 30. Reeducation of Aggressors

It is State policy to create treatment services that contribute to the reeducation of aggressors who have committed acts of violence against women and members of the family group so that the aggressor stops all types of violence against them.

#### Article 31

Penitentiary Treatment for the Social Reintegration of Aggressors Deprived of Liberty
The National Penitentiary Institute incorporates the axis of prevention of violence against women and members of the family group within the various penitentiary treatment programs directed at the prison population.
The person sentenced to effective custodial sentence for crimes related to violence against women and members of the family group, following evaluation, must undergo multidisciplinary and differentiated reeducation treatment, taking into account the approaches set forth in this Law in order to facilitate their social reintegration. Completion of treatment is a mandatory requirement for the granting of penitentiary benefits, pardon and commutation of sentence as applicable, in accordance with the current legal framework, which cannot be granted without the corresponding psychological and social report that addresses the evolution of the differentiated treatment.

The Ministry of Women and Vulnerable Populations provides technical assistance for the design of the reeducation program.

#### Article 32. Treatment for Aggressors in the Community

In proceedings for crimes related to acts of violence against women and members of the family group, the judge may impose on the aggressor psychosocial, psychiatric or specialized self-help group treatment on violence through attendance at therapies on violence against women and members of the family group, using the various programs developed by family protection institutions. This measure may be applied from the beginning of the procedure.
It is the obligation of local governments to implement, in coordination with the Ministry of Women and Vulnerable Populations, care and intervention services for men and aggressors.

In proceedings for crimes related to acts of violence against women and members of the family group, criminal courts must rule in the conviction regarding specialized treatment for the aggressor who does not serve an effective custodial sentence.

Submission to a treatment service for the reeducation of aggressors in public or private institutions as ordered by the court is considered a rule of conduct, without prejudice to the applicable criminal sanction.

## TITLE IV

NATIONAL SYSTEM FOR THE PREVENTION, PUNISHMENT AND ERADICATION OF VIOLENCE AGAINST WOMEN AND MEMBERS OF THE FAMILY GROUP

#### Article 33. Creation, Purpose and Competence of the System

The National System for the Prevention, Punishment and Eradication of Violence against Women and Members of the Family Group is hereby created, in order to coordinate, plan, organize and execute coordinated, integrated and complementary actions for State action in the prevention, care, protection and reparation of the victim, the punishment and reeducation of the aggressor, in order to achieve the eradication of violence against women and members of the family group. It is a functional system.

#### Article 34. Members of the System

The National System for the Prevention, Punishment and Eradication of Violence against Women and Members of the Family Group is composed of the entities that make up the high-level multisectoral commission, which has a technical secretariat, and the regional, provincial and district coordination bodies to eradicate violence against women and members of the family group.

#### Article 35. High-Level Multisectoral Commission

The High-Level Multisectoral Commission is hereby established for the purpose of directing the National System for the Prevention, Punishment and Eradication of Violence against Women and Members of the Family Group and formulating the guidelines and evaluation of the provisions established in this regulation.
The Commission is chaired by the head or the representative of the senior management of the Ministry of Women and Vulnerable Populations and composed of the heads or representatives of the senior management of the institutions to be determined in the regulations of this Law.
The Ministry of Women and Vulnerable Populations is the governing body in matters of prevention, protection and care of violence against women and members of the family group and is responsible for the coordination, articulation and monitoring of the effective application and compliance with this Law.

The General Directorate against Gender Violence of the aforementioned ministry is constituted as the technical secretariat of the Commission, which convenes specialists from different sectors and representatives of civil society for the purpose of constituting a national working group.

The regulations of this Law regulate the functioning of the Commission.

The regional coordination instance has the responsibility to develop, implement, monitor and evaluate public policies responsible for combating violence against women and members of the family group at the regional level, and to promote compliance with this regulation. Its composition is determined in the regulations of this Law.

#### Article 38. Provincial coordination instance

The provincial coordination instance has the responsibility to develop, implement, monitor and evaluate public policies responsible for combating violence against women and members of the family group at the provincial level, and to promote compliance with this regulation. Its composition is determined in the regulations of this Law.

The district coordination instance has the responsibility to develop, implement, monitor and evaluate public policies responsible for combating violence against women and members of the family group at the district level, and to promote compliance with this regulation. Its composition is determined in the regulations of this Law.

b.	The Single Registry of Victims and Aggressors.

c.	The National Observatory on Violence against Women and Members of the Family

Group.

d.	The Center for Advanced Studies against Violence against Women and Members of the Family Group.

#### Article 41. Base Protocol for Joint Action

The Base Protocol for Joint Action in prevention, care, protection, early detection and continued intervention, punishment and re-education in response to violence against women and members of the family group contains the guidelines for intersectoral articulation and the procedures that ensure the global and comprehensive action of the different administrations and services involved. It constitutes an instrument of mandatory compliance under penalty of liability.
The Protocol must give special consideration to the situation of women who, due to their condition as such and in intersection with other variables, are more exposed to suffering violence or greater difficulties in accessing the services provided for in this Law, such as those belonging to indigenous, Andean and Amazonian populations, Afro-descendants, those in situations of social exclusion and women with disabilities, among others. Similar consideration must be contemplated by the protocol with respect to members of the family group from the human rights, generational and intercultural approach.

#### Article 46. General obligations of the media

The media, in the dissemination of information related to violence against women, guarantee, with the corresponding informative objectivity, the defense of human rights, the freedom and dignity of women victims of violence and their children. In particular, they take special care in the graphic treatment of information.
Public and private broadcasting services allow the use of the educational segment of 10% of their programming so that, during family protection hours, the public institutions articulated in the National System for the Prevention, Punishment and Eradication of Violence against Women and Members of the Family Group develop content related to awareness, prevention, care, protection, punishment and reeducation for the eradication of violence against women and members of the family group.

#### Article 47. Intervention of indigenous or native peoples

The intervention of indigenous or native peoples in cases of violence against women and members of the family group is subject to the provisions of article 149 of the Political Constitution.(*)

FINAL COMPLEMENTARY PROVISIONS

FIRST. Regulation
The regulations of this Law shall be issued by the Executive Branch within a period not exceeding ninety calendar days from its entry into force. For this purpose, a commission is convened consisting of the Ministry of Justice and Human Rights, the Ministry of Women and Vulnerable Populations, the Ministry of the Interior, the Judicial Branch and the Public Prosecutor's Office.

SECOND. Normative prevalence
The provisions of this Law prevail over other general or special rules that oppose them. The rights that this Law recognizes to victims of violence against women and against members of the family group are inalienable.

THIRD. Implementation of the National Observatory on Violence against Women and Members of the Family Group and the Center for Advanced Studies
The implementation of the National Observatory on Violence against Women and Members of the Family Group and the Center for Advanced Studies against Violence against Women and Members of the Family Group, referred to in articles 43 and 44 of this Law, shall be subject to the budgetary availability that the Ministry of Women and Vulnerable Populations provides for this purpose.

"Fourth. Reference to family courts, chambers and prosecutor's offices
When this law refers to family courts, chambers and prosecutor's offices, it should be understood to include the courts, chambers and prosecutor's offices that serve in their place."(*)

(*) Provision incorporated by Article 3 of Legislative Decree No. 1386, published on September 4, 2018.

"Fifth. Publication on compliance with deadlines
The Judicial Branch publishes annually on its institutional portal information on compliance with deadlines for the issuance of protection measures by family courts."(*)

(*) Provision incorporated by Article 3 of Legislative Decree No. 1386, published on September 4, 2018.

"SIXTH. Reports

TRANSITIONAL COMPLEMENTARY PROVISIONS

FIRST. Proceedings in process
Proceedings that are in process shall continue to be governed by the rules under which they were initiated until their conclusion.

SECOND. Special Commission
The Special Commission is created for the design, conduct, coordination, supervision and evaluation of the process of adaptation of the National System for the Prevention, Punishment and Eradication of Violence against Women and Members of the Family Group to this Law.

THIRD. Members of the Special Commission
The Commission referred to in the second transitional complementary provision is composed of six members:

-	The head of the Ministry of Women and Vulnerable Populations or his representative, who shall preside over it.

-	The head of the Ministry of Justice and Human Rights or his representative.

-	The head of the Ministry of Economy and Finance or his representative.

-	The head of the Ministry of the Interior or his representative.

-	The head of the Judicial Branch or his representative.

-	The head of the Public Prosecutor's Office or his representative.

The powers of the Special Commission are as follows:

1.	Formulate the policies and objectives for the progressive adaptation of the Law.

2.	Design the proposal for the Plan for Adaptation of the Justice System to the National System for the Prevention, Punishment and Eradication of Violence against Women and Members of the Family Group.

3.	Prepare the draft regulations that are necessary for the transfer of budgetary resources as appropriate.

4.	Establish, in coordination with the related entities, the annual adaptation programs, provision of material and human resources that allow the execution of the Plan for Adaptation of the Justice System to the Law.

5.	Harmonize, supervise and monitor and evaluate the execution of the plans and programs for adaptation to the Law.

6.	Prepare semi-annual reports, which are sent to the High-Level Multisectoral Commission.

FIFTH. Deadline
The deadline for the formulation of the Justice System Adaptation Plan by the Commission is sixty working days from the installation of the same. Likewise, the deadline for said commission to complete its functions is one hundred eighty working days from the installation of the same.

"SIXTH. Design of multisectoral budget program
The Ministry of Economy and Finance, through the General Directorate of Public Budget, accompanies the Ministry of Women and Vulnerable Populations, which presides over the National System for the Prevention, Punishment and Eradication of Violence against Women and Members of the Family Group, in the design of a multisectoral budget program for the implementation of said system, as well as the indicators for its respective performance monitoring, evaluations and management incentives as appropriate, within one hundred eighty working days from the entry into force of the Law.

In view of the nature of the National System, the budget program must involve at least the Ministry of Women and Vulnerable Populations, Ministry of Justice and Human Rights, Ministry of the Interior, Judicial Branch, Public Prosecutor's Office and the three levels of government."(*)

(*) Provision incorporated by Article 2 of Law No. 30862, published on October 25, 2018.

MODIFYING COMPLEMENTARY PROVISIONS

FIRST. Modification of articles 45, 121-A, 121-B, 122, 377 and 378 of the Penal Code
Articles 45, 121-A, 121-B, 122, 377 and 378 of the Penal Code are modified in the following terms:

"Article 45. Prerequisites for establishing and determining the penalty
The judge, when establishing and determining the penalty, takes into account:

a.	The social deprivations that the agent may have suffered or the abuse of his position, economic position, training, power, trade, profession or the function he holds in society.

b.	His culture and customs.

c.	The interests of the victim, of his family or of the persons who depend on him, as well as the violation of his rights and especially considering his situation of vulnerability.

#### Article 121

A. Aggravated forms. Serious injuries when the victim is a minor, elderly or person with disability
In the cases provided for in the first part of article 121, when the victim is a minor, over sixty-five years of age or suffers from physical or mental disability and the agent takes advantage of such condition, a custodial sentence of not less than six nor more than twelve years shall be imposed.
When the victim dies as a consequence of the injury and the agent could have foreseen that result, the sentence shall be not less than twelve nor more than fifteen years. (*)

(*) Compare with the Single Complementary Repealing Provision of Legislative Decree No. 1323, published on January 06, 2017.

#### Article 121

B.- Aggravated forms. Serious injuries due to violence against women and their family environment
In the cases provided for in the first part of article 121, a custodial sentence of not less than six nor more than twelve years shall be imposed when the victim:
1.	Is a woman and is injured because of her condition as such in any of the contexts provided for in the first paragraph of article 108-B.
2.	Is an ascendant, descendant, natural or adoptive, spouse or cohabitant of the agent.

3.	Is dependent or subordinate.

When the victim dies as a consequence of the injury and the agent could have foreseen that result, the sentence shall be not less than twelve nor more than fifteen years.(*)

(*) Compare with Article 1 of Legislative Decree No. 1323, published on January 06, 2017.

#### Article 122. Minor injuries

1.	Whoever causes another person injuries to the body or health that require more than ten and less than thirty days of care or rest, or a moderate level of psychological harm, according to medical prescription, shall be punished with a custodial sentence of not less than two nor more than five years.
2.	The sentence shall be a custodial sentence of not less than six nor more than twelve years if the victim dies as a consequence of the injury provided for in paragraph 1 and the agent could have foreseen that result.
3.	The sentence shall be a custodial sentence of not less than three nor more than six years if the victim:

a.	Is a member of the National Police of Peru or the Armed Forces, magistrate of the Judicial Branch, the Public Prosecutor's Office or the Constitutional Court or authority elected by popular mandate or public official or servant and is injured in the exercise of their official functions or as a consequence thereof.

b.	Is a minor, over sixty-five years of age or suffers from physical or mental disability and the agent takes advantage of such condition.

c.	Is a woman and is injured because of her condition as such, in any of the contexts provided for in the first paragraph of article 108-B.
d.	Is an ascendant, descendant, natural or adoptive, spouse or cohabitant of the agent.

e.	Is dependent or subordinate in any form to the agent.

4.	The custodial sentence shall be not less than eight nor more than fourteen years if the victim dies as a consequence of the injury referred to in paragraph 3 and the agent could have foreseen that result.

5.	The judge imposes the corresponding disqualification for the cases provided for in paragraph 3.(*) (*) Compare with Article 1 of Legislative Decree No. 1323, published on January 06, 2017.

#### Article 377. Omission, refusal or delay of official acts

The public official who, illegally, omits, refuses or delays any act of their office shall be punished with a custodial sentence of not more than two years and with thirty to sixty days-fine.
When the omission, refusal or delay of official acts refers to a request for personal guarantees or case of family violence, the sentence shall be a custodial sentence of not less than two nor more than five years.

#### Article 378. Denial or deficient police support

The police officer who refuses, omits or delays, without justified cause, the provision of assistance legally required by the competent civil authority, shall be punished with a custodial sentence of not more than two years.
If the provision of assistance is required by a private individual in a situation of danger, the sentence shall be not less than two nor more than four years.
The sentence provided for in the second paragraph shall be imposed, if the provision of assistance refers to a request for personal guarantees or a case of family violence".

SECOND. Incorporation of articles 46-E and 124-B into the Penal Code
Articles 46-E and 124-B are incorporated into the Penal Code in the following terms:

"Article 46-E. Aggravating circumstance qualified by abuse of kinship
The sentence is increased up to one third above the maximum legal limit established for the crime when the agent has taken advantage of their status as ascendant or descendant, natural or adoptive, stepfather or stepmother, spouse or cohabitant of the victim. In this case, the custodial sentence may not exceed thirty-five years, unless the crime is punishable with an indeterminate custodial sentence, in which case the latter shall apply.
The aggravating circumstance provided for in the first paragraph is inapplicable when it is established as such in criminal law.

#### Article 124

B. Determination of psychological injury
The level of psychological injury is determined through an assessment carried out in accordance with the official specialized technical instrument that guides the expert work, with the following equivalence:

a.	Minor injury offense: mild level of psychological damage.
b.	Minor injuries: moderate level of psychological damage.
c.	Serious injuries: severe or very severe level of psychological damage". (*)

(*) Compare with Article 1 of Legislative Decree No. 1323, published on January 6, 2017.

THIRD. Amendment of article 242 of the Criminal Procedure Code
Article 242 of the Criminal Procedure Code, promulgated by Legislative Decree 957, is amended, which is worded as follows:

"Article 242. Cases of advance evidence.-

1.	During the Preparatory Investigation, at the request of the Prosecutor or other procedural parties, the Judge of the Preparatory Investigation may be requested to take advance evidence, in the following cases:
a)	Testimony and examination of the expert, when it is required to examine them urgently in the presence of a founded reason to consider that it cannot be done at the oral trial due to illness or other serious impediment, or that they have been exposed to violence, threat, offers or promise of money or other benefit so that they do not testify or do so falsely. The interrogation of the expert may include expert debate when this is appropriate.

b)	Confrontation between persons who have testified, for the same reasons as the previous paragraph, provided that the requirements established in article 182 are met.
c)	Recognitions, inspections or reconstructions, which by their nature and characteristics must be considered definitive and irreproducible acts, and it is not possible to postpone their performance until the trial is held.

d)	Statement of girls, boys and adolescents in their capacity as victims of crimes included in articles 153 and 153-A of Chapter I: Violation of personal freedom, and those included in Chapter IX: Violation of sexual freedom, Chapter X: Procuring and Chapter XI: Offenses against public decency, corresponding to Title IV: Crimes against freedom, of the Criminal Code.

The statements of girls, boys and adolescents shall be made with the intervention of specialized psychologists in Gesell chambers or interview rooms implemented by the Public Prosecutor's Office.
The statements and interviews shall be filmed and recorded in order to avoid revictimization of the victims.

2.	The same evidentiary proceedings may be carried out during the intermediate stage". (*)

(*) Compare with Article 2 of Legislative Decree No. 1307, published on December 30, 2016, which entered into force nationwide ninety (90) days after its publication in the official gazette El Peruano.

FOURTH. Amendment of article 667 of the Civil Code
Article 667 of the Civil Code, approved by Legislative Decree 295, is amended, which is worded as follows:

"Exclusion from succession due to unworthiness

#### Article 667

The following are excluded from the succession of a certain person, by unworthiness, as heirs or legatees:
1. The perpetrators and accomplices of intentional homicide or its attempt, committed against the life of the decedent, his ascendants, descendants or spouse. This ground of unworthiness does not disappear by pardon nor by prescription of the penalty.

2. Those who have been convicted of an intentional crime committed against the decedent or any of the persons referred to in the preceding paragraph.
3. Those who have slanderously denounced the decedent for a crime for which the law sanctions with deprivation of liberty.
4. Those who have employed fraud or violence to prevent the decedent from granting a will or to compel him to do so, or to revoke in whole or in part the one granted.
5. Those who destroy, conceal, falsify or alter the will of the person whose succession is concerned and those who, knowingly, make use of a falsified will.
6. Those who have been sanctioned with a final judgment on more than one occasion in a family violence proceeding against the decedent. (*)

(*) Compare (numeral 6) with the Second Complementary Modifying Provision of Law No. 30490, published on July 21, 2016.
7. Unworthy to succeed the child is the parent who has not voluntarily recognized him during minority or who has not provided him with food and assistance according to his economic possibilities, even when he has reached the age of majority, if he is unable to procure his own economic resources. Also unworthy to succeed the decedent is the relative with hereditary vocation or the spouse who has not provided him with assistance and food when by law he was obliged to do so and it had been raised as such in the judicial process".
