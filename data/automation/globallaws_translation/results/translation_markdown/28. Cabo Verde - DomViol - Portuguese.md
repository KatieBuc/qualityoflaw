## TITLE I

General provisions

##### Article 1

Object

1.	This law regulates the measures for the implementation of the principle of gender equality.

2.	This law establishes, in particular, the measures intended to prevent and punish the crime of gender-based violence, hereinafter referred to as GBV.

##### Article 2

Scope

1.	This law is applicable to all situations of violence that undermine effective gender equality.
Special Law Against Gender-Based Violence	7

2.	This law is especially applicable to situations arising from the exercise of power between persons, in which gender-based violence is practiced, in an isolated or recurrent manner, by any of the manifestations provided for in this law.

3.	This law is also especially applicable when there exists, at the time of the aggression or at a previous time, a relationship of intimacy, affection, marriage or situation analogous to marriage, including in particular:

a)	The scope of the domestic unit, understood as the space of permanent cohabitation of persons, with or without family ties, including those sporadically aggregated;
b)	The scope of the family, understood as the community formed by individuals who are or consider themselves related, united by natural ties, by affinity or by express will;
c)	Any intimate relationship of affection, in which the aggressor cohabits or has cohabited with the victim, regardless of cohabitation.

4.	This law also applies to any situation of violence practiced by anyone who, having authority or influence over another person, sexually harasses them.

##### Article 3

Definitions

For the purposes of this law, the following are considered:

a)	"Gender": Social representation of biological sex, determined by the idea of the tasks, functions and roles attributed to women and men in society and in public and private life, as well as the relationship that develops between them;
b)	"Gender equality": Equality, in constitutionally enshrined terms, between men and women, recognizing equal rights and duties for both, implying equal visibility, empowerment and participation of both sexes in all spheres of public and private life;
c)	"Gender-based violence": All manifestations of physical or psychological violence, whether they result in offenses to physical integrity, sexual freedom, or in coercion, threat, deprivation of liberty or harassment, based on the construction of unequal power relations, namely through the economic, social, cultural or any other ascendancy of the aggressor in relation to the victim, considering for this purpose:
i)	Physical violence: any conduct that offends the body or health of the victim;
Special Law Against Gender-Based Violence	9

ii)	Psychological violence: any conduct that causes emotional harm, diminution of self-esteem, that prejudices and disturbs the full development of the victim, that aims to degrade or control their actions, beliefs and decisions, through threat, constraint, humiliation, manipulation, isolation, constant surveillance, persecution, insult, blackmail, ridicule, exploitation, dishonor, discredit, disregard for personal value and dignity as well as the limitation of the right to come and go;
iii)	Sexual violence: any conduct practiced for the release or satisfaction of sexual instinct, involving threat, intimidation, coercion, fraud,
 	deliberate placement of the victim in a situation of un-
consciousness or impossibility of resisting, physical aggression, blackmail, comprising not only the sexual act of penetration, but also any other forms of sexual contact, limiting or nullifying the exercise of their sexual and reproductive rights;
iv)	Patrimonial violence: any conduct that configures retention, subtraction, partial or total destruction of objects, work instruments, personal documents, goods, values and rights or economic resources of the victim, including those intended to satisfy their needs;

d)	"Sexual harassment": Any conduct practiced by any person who, having authority or influence over another, makes dependent, namely, the hiring, permanence at work, contract renewal, promotion or the acquisition of any other privileges, as well as scholarships, subsidies or other benefits relevant to themselves or those who depend on them, on obtaining sexual favors for themselves or for a third party.

##### Article 4

Fundamental objectives

This law has as fundamental objectives:

a)	To ensure the exercise of special rights to victims of GBV, particularly in the social, labor and criminal domains;
b)	To promote special obligations of the State and other public authorities in the adoption of public policies for the prevention, assistance and repression of gender-based violence;
c)	To create or strengthen the capacity of institutional structures to combat gender-based violence;
d)	To create conditions that guarantee swift, specialized and effective responses to victims, both in terms

of police and judicial assistance and social protection;
e)	To recognize that all rights contained in this law are equally guaranteed to foreigners who are in national territory, regardless of the situation in which they find themselves.

Victim assistance policy

##### Article 12

Labour rights

1.	The labour rights of all those who find themselves in a situation of gender-based violence are specially protected.

2.	Victims are guaranteed, under the terms of this law, the right to:

a)	Non-dismissal due to impossibility of providing work by virtue of situations of gender-based violence;
b)	Flexibility in working hours, regardless of the functions they perform;
c)	Facilitation of mobility within the possibilities of the employing entity;
d)	Granting of short, medium or long-term leave, without loss of place at work, regardless of the length of service provided;
e)	Termination of the employment contract in a unilateral and justified manner.

Access to justice

1.	The right of access to justice in an urgent manner is guaranteed in all proceedings that have as their direct or indirect cause GBV.

2.	Victims who demonstrate that they do not have economic means must be ensured the right to patronage, representation or assistance by a lawyer, in a priority and urgent manner.

##### Article 14

Social assistance, guidance and professional insertion

1.	Victims of GBV, as well as minors who are under their care, have the right to immediate social assistance, namely through Shelters.

2.	Support is ensured to the victim of GBV with regard to guidance and professional insertion, directly through Victim Support Centres and Shelters or through other existing programmes that should also be promoted by the State.

Social security

1.	Comprehensive social protection is ensured to victims of GBV, as well as to minors in their care, under the terms of the law.

2.	Absences or delays at work motivated by the situation arising from GBV are considered justified, their regime being subject to regulation.

3.	Workers who are unable to provide work by virtue of GBV resulting in incapacity for work for a maximum period of six months cannot be dismissed.

4.	Workers who, by virtue of GBV, are prevented from providing services for more than two months may resort to social promotion services for financial support.

5.	Workers who, by virtue of GBV, are unable to work are guaranteed a subsidy of not less than 80% of their salary or remuneration, by the social security service, and the processing of the case must be carried out within a maximum period of 30 days.

6.	The transfer of the amount of the family allowance may be requested by the victim of GBV, when applicable,

##### Article 16

Health

1.	Public health services must ensure that victims of GBV receive adequate, urgent care free from payment of fees.

2.	The completion of medical treatment forms must be done based on the assumptions of this law, taking special account of the purposes for which they are intended.

3.	When a medical report is requested by the judicial authorities, it must be prepared by a professional trained in GBV and must be sent urgently.

#### Section III Protection measures

##### Article 18

Support structures
 	The following must be created in favour of victims of GBV:

a)	Victim Support Centres;
b)	Shelters;
c)	Victim Support Fund.

##### Article 19

Victim support centres

1. The Government must create, in coordination with Municipal Councils and other entities with a vocation for this purpose, Victim Support Centres, as multidisciplinary service structures, particularly in the areas of providing information, psychological and legal assistance, social support, educational support to the family unit, guidance and job placement.

2. The public body responsible for promoting public policies relating to gender equality is responsible for the implementation and supervision of Victim Support Centres, as well as encouraging the formation of networks to combat GBV.

3. Victim Support Centres are structures endowed with administrative and financial autonomy, installed at least on all islands.

4. Victim Support Centres act in coordination with health services, bodies responsible for providing legal aid, police, judicial entities, Shelters, the public body responsible for implementing public policies relating to children and adolescents and, also, non-governmental organisations with a vocation for promoting gender equality and family.

##### Article 20

Shelters

1. The Government must create, in coordination with Municipal Councils and non-governmental entities with a vocation for this purpose, Shelters for victims and minors in their care, aimed at their temporary and confidential accommodation, in cases where remaining in their residence implies an imminent threat against their physical integrity or life.

2. Shelters must be implemented throughout the national territory, with at least one on each island and must have specifically trained and qualified staff to assist victims of GBV and their minor children, if any.

3. The public body responsible for promoting public policies relating to gender equality is responsible for the implementation and supervision of Shelters.

4. The organisation and operation of Shelters is subject to regulation.

##### Article 21

Support Fund

1. The Government must create an autonomous fund to support victims of GBV, designated Support Fund.

2. 50% of the amount of applicable court costs in proceedings processed under the terms of this law shall revert to the Support Fund.

3. The Support Fund is used so that, in the shortest possible time, it can guarantee a pecuniary amount that allows the victim to cover urgent expenses as a consequence of the assault, under the terms to be set out in regulations. The Fund is also financed through the annual inclusion of its own funds in the State Budget.

4. The revenues of the Support Fund are also intended for the maintenance of Offices and Shelters and for carrying out recovery programmes, psychological and psychiatric support, education and prevention of gender-based violence for aggressors.

##### Article 22

Other measures

Without prejudice to the rights provided for in other legal provisions, the following are especially guaranteed to victims of GBV:

a) Partial or full police protection for the time necessary to preserve their physical integrity;
b) Maintenance for minors and/or the victim;
c) Regulation of the exercise of parental authority;
d) Psychological monitoring and treatment.

## TITLE III

Crimes and special procedures

### CHAPTER I

Criminal protection

#### Section I

Gender-based violence

##### Article 23

Gender-based violence

1. Whoever, by reason of gender, in the circumstances and conditions referred to in numbers 2, 3 and 4 of article 2, commits, against another, acts of violence referred to in paragraph c) of article 3, in any of the forms defined therein, is punished with a prison sentence of 1 to 5 years.

2. If, from the agent's conduct, the damages provided for in articles 122, 129, of the Criminal Code result, the penalties provided for in articles 123 and 124 of that Code are applicable.

3. The agent who commits the acts described therein against a spouse, ex-spouse or person with whom they are or were in a de facto union or are linked by an affective relationship, whether or not there is cohabitation, shall incur the penalties provided for in articles 142 and 144 of the Criminal Code.

4. The provisions of article 8 of the Criminal Code are applicable to this crime.

##### Article 24

Aggravation

The penalty referred to in number 1 of the preceding article is aggravated by one third in its minimum and maximum limits, when:

a) There are minors who are, or have been, in the care of the victim or the agent;
b) The violence is committed in public places or in a manner especially humiliating to the victim;
c) The agent has, for the commission of the crime, resorted to any of the means provided for in paragraphs a) and b) of article 123 of the Criminal Code;
d) The victim is any of the persons indicated in paragraph e of article 124 of the Criminal Code;
e) The crime is committed during the validity of precautionary measures duly imposed;
f) A serious contagious disease results for the victim.

##### Article 25

Harassment

1. Whoever, having authority or influence over another, makes dependent, namely, hiring, remaining in work, contract renewal, promotion or the acquisition of any other privileges, as well as scholarships, subsidies or other benefits relevant to themselves or those who depend on them, on obtaining sexual favours for themselves or for a third party, shall be punished with a prison sentence of up to 1 year or with a fine of 100 to 250 days.

2. Whoever, in the face of the victim's refusal to grant said favours, hinders or reduces their work opportunities or any other opportunity, intimidates them or creates a hostile environment in their workplace or in any other place, incurs the same penalties referred to in the preceding number.

##### Article 26

Suspension of sentence

1. The penalty applicable for the commission of the crimes provided for in this law may only be suspended when it does not exceed two years of imprisonment and the agent is willing, at the trial hearing, to:

a) Follow a monitoring and reintegration programme;
b) Perform community service, under the terms established in the Criminal Code.

2. The obligation provided for in paragraph a) of the preceding number may also be imposed on the agent, in the case of commission of a crime provided for in this law, regardless of the specific penalty applied.

#### Section II Other crimes

##### Article 27

Privileging

In addition to the cases provided for in article 84 of the Criminal Code, the court may also reduce the penalties provided for in articles 122 and 129 of the same instrument, by half, if there is sufficient evidence that the agent had been a victim of the crimes provided for in this law, with the purpose of reacting to a continuous and permanent threat against their life, physical integrity or freedom, even outside the circumstances that exclude the unlawfulness of the act.

##### Article 29

Subsidiary rules

In everything that is not specially established in this chapter, the rules of the Criminal Code are subsidiarily applicable.

### CHAPTER II

Procedural protection

#### Section I Procedural provisions

##### Article 29

Nature of the proceedings

1. The crime provided for in Article 23 of this law is of a public nature, whose criminal proceedings take place independently of a complaint, which may be made by any person.

2. The following have a special duty to report the crime, even if the perpetrator is unknown to them:

a) Police entities and criminal police bodies;
b) Public officials, within the meaning of Article 362 of the Penal Code;
c) Doctors or health technicians who, in the exercise of their functions or by reason thereof, have become aware of the commission of the crime.

3. A declaration by the victim that they wish to withdraw the complaint may only be considered at the time of determining the specific penalty to be applied, when the requirements for suspension of the sentence are met, pursuant to Article 26.

##### Article 30

Urgency

1. Criminal proceedings instituted under this law are, for all purposes, of an urgent nature.

2. Police entities, criminal police bodies and the other professionals referred to in paragraph 2 of the preceding article are obliged to report to the Public Prosecutor's Office all GBV criminal acts of which they become aware, within the shortest possible time, and may not, in any case, exceed 48 hours.

#### Section II Measures

##### Article 31

Prior measures

1. In cases indicating the commission of GBV, when attending to the victim or participating in any operation involving said type of violence, the police authority must ensure adequate information and support to the victim and to minors who are under their care, always protecting their privacy.

2. When circumstances so determine, the police authority must refer the victim to the nearest health establishment or directly to the Shelter or other safe place, namely in case of danger to life or harm to physical integrity, always safeguarding their dignity and privacy.

3. If necessary, the police authority must accompany the victim to remove their personal and professional belongings, as well as those of persons dependent on them, from the family home.

4. The police authority must return the victim to the family home and ensure the departure of the aggressor, following a judicial decision so determining.

##### Article 32

Police and health measures

1. Health and police services that have attended any victim of GBV are obliged to proceed ex officio with the measures intended to obtain the initial report, which must contain:

a) A description of the immediate consequences of the crime, namely the injuries, the instrument used and the treatment to which the victim has been subjected;

c) The probable identification of the aggressor, as well as information relating to previous complaints made against them, for similar behaviour or in relation to the same victim, depending on whether or not there is a record in the respective service.

2. It is the responsibility of the police authorities to send to the Public Prosecutor's Office the report referred to in the preceding paragraph, within the time limit established in paragraph 2 of Article 30.

##### Article 33

Special duties of the Public Prosecutor's Office

1. The Public Prosecutor's Office must, within a maximum period of 48 hours after registration in the respective registry of knowledge of evidence of the crime of GBV, order the first measures, which must be carried out within a maximum of 48 hours thereafter, without prejudice to the possibility of delegation of powers pursuant to law.

2. Among the measures to be ordered by the Public Prosecutor's Office must always include:

a) Presentation of the accused to the Judge, for first interrogation and application of a coercive measure;
b) Determination of support for the victim by the support services referred to in this law, with the objective of providing them with information, protection, social, legal and psychological assistance and legal aid, and these must present a final report on the victim's situation, prior to the indictment, when this is filed;

Coercive measures

3. The judge may set aside the application of the measure referred to in the preceding paragraph, by means of a specially reasoned order.

#### Section III Form of proceedings

and other procedural rules

##### Article 35

Form of proceedings

1. The trial of the crimes referred to in this law follows the procedure of the Abbreviated Proceedings, even if the requirements provided for in paragraph 1 of Article 430 of the Code of Criminal Procedure are not met, with the specificities established in the following paragraphs.

2. The indictment is always preceded by preliminary investigation.
3. The judge's order is issued within 48 hours after the entry of the corresponding case file in court.
4. In cases of remittal of proceedings to the form of ordinary common proceedings, admissible only in the situations provided for in paragraphs 2 and 3 of Article 23, the time limit for trial may not exceed 90 days.

##### Article 36

Provisional suspension of proceedings

Provisional suspension of proceedings may be determined by means of injunctions, corresponding to the conditions for suspension of the prison sentence provided for in this law, pursuant to Article 318 of the Code of Criminal Procedure.

##### Article 37

Time limits

1. When the Public Prosecutor's Office considers that, for reasons relating to the state of health, physical or mental, of the victim, or for other reasons that make it difficult at that time to present all the evidence necessary for the progress of the proceedings, it may, by means of a reasoned order, file an indictment within a maximum period of seventy-five days, without prejudice to that established in paragraph 1 of Article 35.

2. The trial takes place within a maximum period of twenty days after notification to the accused that an indictment has been filed.

##### Article 38

Statements of victims and witnesses

1. To preserve the victim from greater distress, due to the presence of the accused at trial hearing and their emotional state, their statements may be given:

a) Through video-conference;
b) In advance, without the presence of the accused, upon request of the Public Prosecutor or the victim, without prejudice to the defence rights of the accused.

2. If the victim is too debilitated to present their statements or to appear at trial hearing, their statements may be taken at their home, under the terms of article 346 of the Code of Criminal Procedure.

3. In cases of threats, pressure or intimidation against the victim or witness, the authorities must ensure the application of witness protection mechanisms, under the terms of the law.

##### Article 39

Prison-level programmes

1. The prison administration, in coordination with the public body responsible for promoting public policies relating to gender equality, must carry out specific programmes for inmates convicted of GBV crimes, through qualified and specialised personnel.

2. The inmate's participation in the programmes is valued for the purposes of granting permissions and conditional release.

##### Article 40

Subsidiary rules

In everything not specially established in this chapter, the rules of the Code of Criminal Procedure are subsidiarily applicable.

## TITLE IV

Civil protection

##### Article 41

Civil proceedings

1. Civil proceedings that are directly or indirectly related to cases of violence provided for in this law are urgent in nature and must be concluded within a maximum period of 180 days, depending on their complexity.

2. Appeals lodged in the proceedings mentioned in the previous article also have an urgent character and must be decided within a maximum period of 90 days.

3. In the attribution of the right to inhabit the family home, the victim enjoys the right of preference, regardless of the ownership of the property or the grantor in the lease contract.

##### Article 42

Non-compliance

1. Officials or other professionals on whom this law imposes special reporting obligations are subject to disciplinary sanction:

a) In case of non-compliance or reporting outside the established deadlines;
b) When they provide vexatious treatment to the victims they attend.

2. The conducts referred to in the previous number are considered serious offences, for the purposes of disciplinary proceedings.

## TITLE V

Transitional and final provisions

##### Article 43

Implementation
and regulation of measures

1. Within a maximum period of one year, the Government shall create the conditions for the implementation of awareness-raising or assistance measures, the application of which depends on the development of this law and the allocation of corresponding financial resources.

2. Within a maximum period of 1 year all regulations of this law must be approved.

3. The Cape Verdean Institute for Gender Equality and Equity (ICIEG) is the public body responsible for promoting the installation of the structures created under this law.

##### Article 44

Entry into force

This law enters into force sixty days after its publication.
