BASIC LAW NO. 65-60 OF 21 JULY 1965 ESTABLISHING THE PENAL CODE
PRELIMINARY PROVISIONS

###### Article premier

The offense that the laws punish with police penalties is a contravention.
The offense that the laws punish with correctional penalties is a misdemeanor.
The offense that the laws punish with an afflictive or infamous penalty is a felony.

###### Article 2

Any attempt to commit a felony that has been manifested by a commencement of execution, if it has been suspended or if it has failed to achieve its effect only by circumstances independent of the will of its author, is considered as the felony itself.

###### Article 3

Attempts to commit misdemeanors are considered as misdemeanors only in cases determined by a special provision of the law.

###### Article 4

(Loi n° 99-05 du 29.1.1999)
No felony, no misdemeanor, no contravention may be punished with penalties that were not provided for by law or regulation before they were committed

###### Article 5

In case of commission of several felonies or misdemeanors, the

most severe penalty alone is pronounced.
When a principal penalty is the subject of a pardon, account must be taken, for the application of the merger of penalties, of the penalty resulting from the commutation and not of the penalty initially pronounced.

## LIVRE PREMIER

PENALTIES IN CRIMINAL
AND CORRECTIONAL MATTERS AND THEIR EFFECTS

###### Article 6

Penalties in criminal matters are either afflictive and infamous or only infamous.

###### Article 7

Afflictive and infamous penalties are:
1)	Death;
2)	Hard labor for life;
3)	Hard labor for a term;
4)	Criminal detention.

###### Article 8

The only infamous penalty is civic degradation.

###### Article 9

Penalties in correctional matters are;
1° Imprisonment for a term in a correctional facility;
2° Temporary prohibition of certain civic, civil or family rights;
3° Fine.

###### Article 10

The sentence to penalties established by law is always pronounced without prejudice to restitutions and damages that may be owed to the parties.

###### Article 11

Prohibition of residence, fine and special confiscation, either of the corpus delicti, when ownership thereof belongs to the convicted person, or of those which have been used or which have been intended to commit it, are penalties common to criminal	and
correctional matters.

#### CHAPITRE PREMIER DES PEINES EN MATIERE

CRIMINELLE

###### Article 12

Every person sentenced to death shall be shot.

###### Article 13

The bodies of executed persons shall be delivered to their families if they claim them, on condition that they have them buried without any ceremony.

###### Article 14

The	execution report shall, on pain of a civil fine of 2,000 to 10,000 francs, be drawn up immediately by the clerk. It shall be signed by the president of the assizes or his replacement, the representative of the public prosecutor's office and the clerk.
Immediately   after
the execution, a copy of this report shall, on the same penalty, be posted at the door of the penitentiary establishment where the execution took place and shall remain

posted there for twenty-four hours. In the event that the execution was carried out outside the enclosure of a penitentiary establishment, the report thereof shall be posted at the door of the town hall of the place of execution.
No indication, no document relating to the execution other than the report, may be published by the press, on pain of a fine of 40,000 to 50,000 francs. It is prohibited, on the same penalty, as long as the execution report has not been posted, or the pardon decree notified to the convicted person or mentioned in the minute of the judgment, to publish by the press, poster, leaflet, or by any other means of publicity, any information relating to the opinions issued by the Superior Council of the Magistracy or to the decision taken by the President of the Republic.
The report shall, on the penalty provided for in paragraph 1, be transcribed by the clerk within twenty-four hours at the foot of the minute of the judgment. The transcription shall be signed by him and he shall make mention of everything on the same penalty, in the margin of the report. This mention shall also be signed and the transcription shall constitute proof like the report itself.
If the conviction emanates from a jurisdiction other than the Court of Assizes, its president shall exercise the powers belonging to the president of the assizes for the application of this article.

###### Article 15

The execution shall take place within the enclosure of one of the penitentiary establishments appearing on a list drawn up

by order	of	the	keeper of the seals, Minister of Justice.
Only the persons indicated below shall be admitted to attend the execution:
1)	The president of the court of assizes or, failing that, a magistrate designated by the first president of the Court of Appeal;
2)	A representative of the public prosecutor's office designated by the Attorney General;
3)	A judge of the court of the place of execution;
4)	The clerk of the court of assizes or, failing that, a clerk of the court of the place of execution;
5)	The defenders of the convicted person;
6)	A minister of religion;
7)	The director of the penitentiary establishment;
8)	The police commissioner and, if applicable, the agents of the public force requisitioned by the Attorney General or by the Public Prosecutor;
9)	The prison doctor or, failing that, a doctor designated by the Attorney General or by the Public Prosecutor.
No sentence may be executed on national or religious holidays, nor on Sundays.

###### Article 16

If a woman sentenced to death declares and it is verified that she is pregnant, she shall not undergo the penalty until after her delivery.

###### Article 17

The duration of the penalty of hard labor for a term shall be, according to the cases specified by law, either ten to twenty years, or five to ten years.

###### Article 18

The duration of the penalty of criminal detention shall be, according to the cases specified by law, either ten to twenty years, or five to ten years.

###### Article 19

Men sentenced to hard labor shall be employed in the most arduous works of public interest.
Women sentenced to hard labor shall be employed therein only within the interior of the penal camp.

###### Article 20

Criminal detention shall be executed in a special quarter of the penal camp. The convicted person shall communicate with persons placed in the interior of the place of detention or with those outside, in accordance with the regulations of the penitentiary administration.

###### Article 21

The duration of any penalty depriving liberty counts from the day when the convicted person is detained by virtue of the conviction,	having become irrevocable, which pronounces the penalty.

###### Article 22

When there has been preventive detention, this detention shall be fully deducted from the duration of the penalty that the

judgment or the conviction order shall have pronounced, unless the judge has ordered, by special and reasoned provision, that this imputation shall not take place or that it shall take place only in part.
With regard to preventive detention comprised between the date of the judgment or order and the moment when the conviction	becomes irrevocable, it shall always be imputed in the following two cases:
1)	If the convicted person has not exercised any recourse against the judgment or order;
2)	If, having exercised a recourse, his penalty has been reduced on his appeal or following his appeal.

###### Article 23

The sentence to a criminal penalty shall entail civic degradation.
Civic degradation shall be incurred from the day when the conviction becomes irrevocable and, in case of conviction in absentia, from the day of the accomplishment	of the publicity measures provided for in article 360 of the Code of Criminal Procedure.

###### Article 24

Anyone who has been sentenced to an afflictive and infamous penalty shall, moreover, during the duration of their sentence, be in a state of legal interdiction; a guardian and a deputy guardian shall be appointed to manage and administer their property, in the forms prescribed for the appointment of guardians and deputy guardians for persons under interdiction.

Legal interdiction shall not take effect during the period of conditional release.

###### Article 25

The property of the convicted person shall be returned to them after they have served their sentence and the guardian shall render an account of their administration.

###### Article 26

During the duration of the sentence, no sum, no provision, no portion of their income may be remitted to them.

###### Article 27

(Law No. 77-33 of
22 February 1977)
Civic degradation consists of:
1) The dismissal and exclusion of convicted persons from all functions, employments or public offices;
2) The deprivation of the right to vote, of eligibility and in general of all civic and political rights, and of the right to wear any decoration;
3) The incapacity to serve as a juror or expert, to be a witness except to give simple information in court;
4) The incapacity to be part of a family council and to be a guardian, deputy guardian or curator;
5) The deprivation of the right to carry and possess weapons, to serve in the National Gendarmerie, in the Police, in Customs, in the Fire Brigade corps and in the Armed Forces and in general to participate in any public service

whatsoever, to keep a school or to teach and to be employed in any educational establishment as a professor, master or supervisor.

###### Article 28

Whenever civic degradation is pronounced as a principal penalty, it may be accompanied by imprisonment the duration of which, fixed by the judgment of conviction, shall not exceed five years.
If the guilty party is a foreigner or a Senegalese person who has lost the status of citizen, the penalty of imprisonment must always be pronounced.

###### Article 29

A person sentenced to hard labour for life may not dispose of their property, in whole or in part, either by gift inter vivos or by will, nor receive by such title, except for maintenance.
Any will made by them prior to their contradictory conviction, having become final, is null and void. The above provisions are applicable to a person convicted in absentia only five years after the completion of the publicity measures provided for in Article 360 of the Code of Criminal Procedure.
The indictment chamber ruling on petition may relieve the convicted person of all or part of the incapacities pronounced by the preceding paragraph. It may grant them the exercise, in the place of execution of the sentence, of civil rights or some of those rights, of which they have been deprived by their state of legal interdiction. Acts done by the convicted person

in the place of execution of the sentence cannot bind the property they possessed on the day of their conviction, or which has devolved to them gratuitously since that time.

###### Article 30

In all cases where a conviction is pronounced for an offence provided for in Articles 56, 57, 58, 59, 79,
80, 152, 153, 158, 160, 161 and
163 bis, the competent courts may order the confiscation for the benefit of the Nation of all present property of the convicted person of whatever nature, movable, immovable, divided or undivided, according to the following procedures.

###### Article 31

If the convicted person is married, the confiscation shall apply only to half of their property.
If they have descendants or ascendants, the confiscation shall apply only to one-fifth of their property. Partition or sale by auction shall be proceeded with, if necessary, according to the rules applicable in matters of succession.

###### Article 32

The alienation of confiscated property shall be pursued by the domain administration in the forms prescribed for the sale of State property.
Property devolved to the State by the effect of confiscation shall remain charged, up to the amount of their value, with legitimate debts prior to the conviction.
All acts inter vivos or testamentary, for valuable consideration or gratuitous, accomplished by the guilty party less than three years before the commencement of proceedings, either directly or through an interposed person or by any other indirect means, shall be declared null at the request of the domain administration or the public prosecutor, if they were done with the intention of concealing or diverting all or part of their fortune.
Except for proof to the contrary which may be made by all means, any act of disposition or administration is presumed to have been accomplished with this intention if it is not established that it is prior to the period provided for in the preceding paragraph.
Those who have knowingly assisted, either directly or indirectly or through the interposition of persons, in the concealment of property or assets belonging to the convicted person shall be punished by imprisonment of one to five years and a fine of
100,000 francs.

#### CHAPTER II

PENALTIES IN CORRECTIONAL MATTERS

###### Article 33

The duration of the penalty of imprisonment shall be greater than one month without exceeding ten years, except in cases of recidivism or those for which the law has determined other limits.
The penalty of one day's imprisonment is twenty-four hours.
That of one month is thirty days.
The fine is greater than
20,000 francs.

###### Article 34

(Law No. 77-33 of
22 February 1977)
Courts sitting in correctional matters may, in certain cases, prohibit, in whole or in part, the exercise of the following civic, civil and family rights:
1) to vote
2) of eligibility;
3) to be called or appointed to the functions of juror or other public functions, or to positions in the administration or to exercise these functions or positions;
4) to carry and possess weapons;
5) to vote and suffrage in family deliberations;
6) to be a guardian, deputy guardian or curator;
7) to be an expert or witness except to give simple information in court.
When the penalty of imprisonment incurred is greater than five years, the courts may pronounce for a period of ten years more, the total or partial prohibition of the rights enumerated above.
When the penalty of imprisonment pronounced is greater than five years, the definitive prohibition of all rights must be compulsorily pronounced.
The prohibition shall take effect from the day the conviction becomes final.

###### Article 35

Courts shall pronounce the prohibition mentioned in the preceding article only when it has been authorized or ordered by a particular provision of the law.

#### CHAPTER III PENALTIES AND OTHER

CONVICTIONS THAT MAY BE PRONOUNCED FOR CRIMES OR OFFENCES

###### Article 36

(Law No. 67-52 of 29 November 1967)
When the law so provides, courts may prohibit the convicted person for a period of two to ten years from residing in the localities they designate.
The prohibition from residing in the Cap-Vert Region and in the chief towns of other regions is compulsorily pronounced for a period of ten years against any person sentenced to a custodial penalty without suspension under Articles 241 to 247 inclusive and Articles 364 to 373 inclusive. However, courts may, by reasoned decision, reduce the duration and territorial scope of this penalty or even grant total exemption from it. In case of omission and before the exercise, or in default, of a useful remedy, they may rule ex officio or at the request of the Public Prosecutor, at any time until the request for expiration of the principal penalty.
The prohibition from residing takes effect from the date on which the decision that ordered it became final. It is executed following

the custodial penalty.
Any violation of this prohibition shall be punished by a penalty of imprisonment which may not be less than two years.

###### Article 37

The prohibition from residence may be remitted or reduced by way of pardon.
The prescription of the sentence does not relieve the convicted person from the prohibition from residence to which he is subject.

###### Article 38

When restitution is required, the guilty party may be condemned, in addition, towards the injured party, if the latter so requests, to compensation the determination of which is left to the justice of the Court or Tribunal, when the law has not regulated it, without the Court or Tribunal being able, even with the consent of said party, to order its allocation to any charitable work whatsoever.

###### Article 39

The execution of sentences to fine, restitutions, damages and costs may be pursued by way of imprisonment for debt.

###### Article 40

In case of concurrence of the fine with restitutions and damages on the insufficient property of the convicted person, the latter sentences shall obtain preference.

###### Article 41

Subject to the provisions of articles 340, paragraph 6, and 463 of the Code of Criminal Procedure, all individuals convicted for the same offence or for related offences shall be held jointly and severally liable for fines, restitutions, damages and costs.

#### CHAPITRE IV DES PEINES DE LA

RECIDIVE
POUR CRIMES ET DELITS

###### Article 42

Whoever, having already been sentenced to an afflictive and infamous penalty, or only infamous, commits a new crime, shall be liable to double the penalty incurred.
However, an individual convicted by a Tribunal of the armed forces shall, in case of subsequent crime or offence, be liable to the penalties for recidivism only insofar as the first conviction was pronounced for crimes or offences punishable under ordinary criminal laws.

###### Article 43

Whoever, having been convicted for a crime to a penalty exceeding one year of imprisonment, shall, within a period of five years after the expiration of that penalty or its prescription, have committed an offence or a crime which must be punished by imprisonment, shall be sentenced to the maximum penalty provided by law, and that penalty may be increased up to double.

In addition, the convicted person may be prohibited from appearing, for up to ten years, in the places designated by the jurisdiction which pronounced the sentence.
The same shall apply to those sentenced to imprisonment of more than one year for an offence who, within the same period of five years, are found guilty of the same offence or of a crime to be punished by imprisonment.
Those who, having been previously sentenced to a penalty of imprisonment of shorter duration, commit the same offence under the same conditions of time, shall be sentenced to a penalty of imprisonment which may not be less than double that previously pronounced, without however exceeding double the maximum penalty incurred.
The offences of theft, fraud, breach of trust, passive corruption and all acts of misappropriation of public credit or funds shall be considered as being, from the point of view of recidivism, the same offence.
The same shall apply to the offences of vagrancy and begging.
Receiving shall be considered, from the point of view of recidivism, as the offence which procured the things received.

LIVRE DEUXIEME DES PERSONNES PUNISSABLES, EXCUSABLES OU
RESPONSABLES POUR CRIMES OU POUR DELITS

###### Article 45

Accomplices to a crime or offence shall be punished with the same penalty as the actual perpetrators of that crime or offence, except in cases where the law has provided otherwise.

###### Article 46

Shall be punished as accomplices to an action qualified as a crime or offence those who, by gifts, promises, threats, abuse of authority or power, guilty machinations or artifices, have provoked that action or given instructions to commit it.
Those who have procured weapons, instruments, or any other means which served the action knowing that they were to serve it.
Those who have, with knowledge, aided or assisted the perpetrator or perpetrators of the action, in the acts which prepared or facilitated it, or in those which consummated it, without prejudice to the penalties which shall be specifically provided by this Code against the perpetrators of conspiracies or provocations threatening the security of the State, even in the case where the crime which was the object of the conspirators or provocateurs was not committed.

###### Article 47

Those who, knowing the criminal conduct of malefactors exercising brigandage or violence against the security of the State, public peace, persons or property, habitually provide lodging, place of retreat or meeting, shall be punished as their accomplices.
Those who, outside the cases provided above, have knowingly harboured a person whom they knew to have committed a crime or whom they knew to be sought for that fact by justice or who have removed or attempted to remove the criminal from arrest or searches, or have helped him to hide or take flight, shall be punished by imprisonment of two months to three years and a fine of 25,000 francs to 1 million francs or by only one of these two penalties, all without prejudice to heavier penalties if applicable.
Excepted from the provisions of the preceding paragraph are the relatives or in-laws of the criminal up to and including the fourth degree.

###### Article 48

Without prejudice to the application of articles 88 and 89 of this Code, shall be punished by imprisonment of two months to three years and a fine of 25,000 francs to 1 million francs or by only one of these two penalties, whoever, having knowledge of a crime already attempted or consummated, has not, when it was still possible to prevent or limit its effects or when one could think that the guilty parties or one of them would commit new crimes which a denunciation could prevent, immediately informed the administrative or judicial authorities.

Excepted from the provisions of this article are relatives or in-laws, up to and including the fourth degree, of the perpetrators or accomplices of the crime or attempt except with regard to crimes committed against minors under fifteen years.

###### Article 49

Without prejudice to the application, where applicable, of heavier penalties provided by this Code and special laws, shall be punished by imprisonment of three months to five years and a fine of 25,000 francs to 1 million francs, or by only one of these two penalties, whoever, being able to prevent by his immediate action, without risk to himself or to third parties, either an act qualified as a crime, or an offence against the bodily integrity of a person, voluntarily refrains from doing so.
Shall be punished by the same penalties whoever voluntarily refrains from providing to a person in danger the assistance which, without risk to himself or to third parties, he could provide, either by his personal action or by summoning help.
Shall be punished by the same penalties whoever, knowing the proof of the innocence of a person held in preventive detention or tried for a crime or offence, voluntarily refrains from immediately bringing the testimony to the authorities of justice or police. However, no penalty shall be pronounced against whoever brings his testimony belatedly, but spontaneously.
Exempted from the provision of the preceding paragraph are the guilty party of the act which motivated the prosecution, his co-perpetrators, his accomplices and the relatives or in-laws of these persons up to and including the fourth degree.

###### Article 50

There is neither crime nor offence when the accused was in a state of insanity at the time of the action, or when he was compelled by a force which he could not resist.

###### Article 51

No crime or offence may be excused, nor the penalty mitigated, except in the cases and circumstances where the law declares the act excusable, or permits the application of a less rigorous penalty.

###### Article 52

If, by reason of the circumstances and the personality of the offender, it is decided that a minor over the age of thirteen must be subject to a criminal conviction, the sentences shall be pronounced as follows:
If he has incurred the death penalty, hard labour for life, he shall be sentenced to a term of imprisonment of ten to twenty years.
If he has incurred the penalty of hard labour for a fixed term of ten to twenty years or five to ten years, criminal detention of ten to twenty years or five to ten years, he shall be sentenced to imprisonment for a period equal to at most half of that for which he could have been sentenced to one of these penalties.
If he has incurred the penalty of civic degradation he shall be sentenced to imprisonment for not more than two years.

###### Article 53

If the offence committed by a minor over the age of thirteen is a misdemeanour or a petty offence, the penalty that may be pronounced against him under the conditions of Article 52 may not, subject to the same reservation, exceed half of that to which he would have been sentenced if he had been eighteen years old.

###### Article 54

Innkeepers and hoteliers, convicted of having lodged, for more than twenty-four hours, someone who, during his stay, committed a crime or a misdemeanour, shall be civilly liable for the restitutions, compensation and costs awarded to those to whom this crime or misdemeanour caused any damage, for failure to have recorded in their register the name, profession and domicile of the offender without prejudice to their civil liability.

###### Article 55

In other cases of civil liability that may arise in criminal, correctional, or police matters, the Courts and Tribunals before which these matters are brought shall comply with the provisions of the Code of Civil and Commercial Obligations.

###### Article 56

Shall be guilty of treason and punished by death any Senegalese, any soldier or sailor in the service of Senegal who:
1)	Bears arms against Senegal;
2)	Maintains intelligence with a foreign power, with a view to inducing it to undertake hostilities against Senegal, or provides it with the means to do so, either by facilitating the penetration of foreign forces into Senegalese territory, or by undermining the loyalty of the land, sea or air forces, or in any other manner;
3)	Delivers to a foreign power or its agents, either Senegalese troops, or territories, cities, fortresses, works, posts, stores, arsenals, equipment, munitions, vessels, ships or aircraft belonging to Senegal or assigned to its defence;
4)	With a view to harming national defence, destroys or damages a ship, an aircraft, equipment, supplies, a construction or any installation whatsoever, or who, for the same purpose, introduces into them, either before or after their completion, defects of a nature to damage them or to cause an accident.

###### Article 57

Shall be guilty of treason and punished by death any Senegalese, any soldier or sailor in the service of Senegal who, in time of war:

If the conspiracy has not been followed by an act committed or begun to prepare its execution, the penalty shall be that of criminal detention for a term of five to ten years.

There is conspiracy as soon as the resolution to act is concerted and settled between two or more persons.
If there has been a proposal made and not agreed to form a conspiracy to achieve the crimes mentioned in article 72, the one who shall have made such a proposal shall be punished by imprisonment of one year to ten years and a fine of 1

The attack the purpose of which shall have been, either to incite civil war by arming or by inciting citizens or inhabitants to arm themselves against one another, or to bring devastation, massacre or pillage upon the territory, shall be punished by death.

There is conspiracy as soon as the resolution to act is concerted and agreed upon between two or more persons.
If there has been a proposal made and not agreed to form a conspiracy to achieve the crimes mentioned in Article 79, the person who has made such a proposal shall be punished by criminal detention of five to ten years.

The same penalty shall be applied to those who have directed the association, raised or caused to be raised, organized or caused to be organized bands or have knowingly and voluntarily provided or procured for them subsidies, weapons, ammunition and instruments of crime or sent provisions or who have in any other manner maintained intelligence with the directors or commanders of the bands.

##### SECTION IV

CRIMES COMMITTED BY PARTICIPATION IN AN INSURRECTIONARY MOVEMENT

###### Article 85

Individuals who, in an insurrectionary movement, shall be punished by fixed-term criminal detention of ten to twenty years:
1)	Have made or helped to make barricades, entrenchments, or any other works having the purpose of hindering or stopping the exercise of the public force;
2)	Have prevented, by means of violence or threats, the convening or meeting of the public force, or who have provoked or facilitated the gathering of insurgents, whether by the distribution of orders or proclamations, or by the carrying of flags or other rallying signs, or by any other means of appeal;
3)	Have, in order to make attack or resistance against the public force, invaded or occupied buildings, posts and other public establishments, inhabited or uninhabited houses. The penalty shall be the same with regard to the owner or tenant who, knowing the purpose of the insurgents, has procured for them without constraint the entry to said houses.

###### Article 86

Individuals who, in an insurrectionary movement, shall be punished by criminal detention of ten to twenty years:
1)	Have seized weapons, ammunition or materials of all kinds, whether by means of violence or threats, or by the pillage of shops or posts, warehouses, arsenals or other public establishments, or by the disarming of agents of the public force;
2)	Have carried either visible or concealed weapons, or ammunition, or a uniform or costume or other civil or military insignia.
If the individuals carrying visible or concealed weapons, or ammunition, were wearing a uniform, a costume or other civil or military insignia, they shall be punished by the maximum term of criminal detention.
Individuals who have made use of their weapons shall be punished by hard labor for life.

###### Article 87

Those who have directed or organized an insurrectionary movement or who have knowingly and voluntarily provided or procured for it weapons, ammunition or instruments of crime, or sent provisions or who have, in any manner, maintained intelligence with the directors or commanders of the movement, shall be punished by death.

##### SECTION V MISCELLANEOUS PROVISIONS

###### Article 88

Subject to obligations arising from professional secrecy, any person who, having knowledge of acts constituting offenses against the security of the State referred to in this chapter, fails to report them to the administrative, judicial or military authorities from the moment he or she became aware of them, shall be punished by imprisonment of five to ten years and a fine of 100,000 to 2 million francs.
In addition to the persons designated in Article 46, anyone other than the perpetrator or accomplice shall be punished as an accomplice:
1)	Who provides without constraint and with knowledge of their intentions, subsidies, means of subsistence, lodging, place of retreat or meeting to the perpetrators of crimes and offenses against the security of the State;
2)	Who knowingly carries the correspondence of the perpetrators of such crimes or such offenses, or knowingly facilitates for them, in any manner whatsoever, the search for, concealment, transport, or transmission of the object of the crime or offense.
In addition to the persons designated in Article 430, anyone other than the perpetrator or accomplice shall be punished as a receiver:
1)	Who knowingly conceals objects or instruments having served or intended to serve to commit the crime or offense or the material objects or documents obtained by the crime or offense;

2)	Who knowingly destroys, removes, conceals, dissimulates or alters a public or private document likely to facilitate the investigation of the crime or offense, the discovery of evidence or the punishment of its perpetrators.

###### Article 89

Any person who, before any execution or attempt of a crime or offence against the security of the State, gives first knowledge thereof to the administrative or judicial authorities shall be exempt from the penalty incurred.
The penalty shall only be reduced by one degree if the denunciation occurs after the commission or attempt of the crime or offence, but before the commencement of prosecution.
The penalty shall also be reduced by one degree with regard to the offender who, after the commencement of prosecution, secures the arrest of the perpetrators or accomplices of the same offence or of other offences of the same nature or equal gravity.
Except for particular crimes which they have personally committed, no penalty shall be pronounced against those who, having been part of an armed gang without exercising any command therein and without fulfilling any employment or function therein, withdrew at the first warning from the civil or military authorities or surrendered to these authorities.
Those who are exempt from penalty by application of this article may nevertheless be prohibited from residence as in correctional matters and deprived of the rights enumerated in Article 34.

###### Article 90

The remuneration received by the offender, or the amount of its value when the remuneration could not be seized, shall be declared forfeited to the Treasury by the judgment.
The confiscation of the object of the crime or offence and of the objects and instruments having served to commit it shall be pronounced.
Included in the word weapons are all machines, all cutting, piercing or blunt instruments or utensils.
Pocket knives and scissors, simple canes and all other objects whatsoever shall be deemed weapons only insofar as they have been used to kill, wound or strike.

###### Article 91

The Head of State may, by decree after opinion of the Supreme Court, extend either for time of war or for time of peace, all or part of the provisions relating to crimes or offences against the security of the State to acts concerning it which are committed against States or powers, allied or friendly to Senegal.
In the cases provided for in this article, the Court may exempt from the penalty incurred the relatives or in-laws of the criminal up to and including the fourth degree.

#### CHAPITRE Il UNLAWFUL ASSEMBLIES, MEETINGS AND GATHERINGS

##### SECTION PREMIERE UNLAWFUL ASSEMBLIES

###### Article 92

(Law No. 74-13 of 24 June 1974)
The following are prohibited on the public highway or in a public place:
1) any armed unlawful assembly;
2) any unarmed unlawful assembly which could disturb the public tranquillity.
The unlawful assembly is armed if one of the individuals composing it is carrying a visible weapon, or if several of them are carrying concealed weapons, or any visible or concealed objects whatsoever, having served as weapons or brought with a view to serving as weapons.
The representatives of the public force called upon to disperse an unlawful assembly or to ensure the execution of the law, of a judgment or warrant of justice, may use force, if violence or assault is exercised against them or against any person or if destruction or damage is caused to property or if they cannot otherwise defend the ground they occupy or the posts whose guard is entrusted to them.
In other cases, the unlawful assembly is dispersed by force after the governor, the prefect, the sub-prefect, the mayor or one of his deputies, a police commissioner or any other judicial police officer wearing the insignia of his function:
1) has announced his presence by a sound or light signal of a nature to effectively warn the individuals constituting the unlawful assembly;
2) has ordered the persons participating in the unlawful assembly to disperse, using a loudspeaker or by using a sound or light signal also of a nature to effectively warn the individuals constituting the unlawful assembly;
3) has proceeded in the same manner to a second order if the first has remained without result.
The nature of the signals which must be used is determined by decree.

###### Article 93

(Law No. 74-13 of 24 June 1974)
Any unarmed person who, being part of an armed or unarmed unlawful assembly, has not abandoned it after the first order shall be punished by imprisonment of two months to one year.
The imprisonment shall be six months to three years if the unarmed person has continued to be part of an armed unlawful assembly which dispersed only before the use of force.
Persons convicted by application of this article may be deprived for at least one year and at most five years of all or part of the rights mentioned in Article 34.

###### Article 94

(Law No. 74-13 of 24 June 1974)
Without prejudice, where applicable, to heavier penalties, any person who, in an unlawful assembly, during a demonstration or on the occasion of a demonstration, during a meeting or on the occasion of a meeting, has been found carrying a visible or concealed weapon or any visible or concealed objects whatsoever having served as weapons or brought with a view to serving as weapons shall be punished by imprisonment of six months to three years.
The imprisonment shall be one to five years in the case of an unlawful assembly dispersed by force.
Persons convicted under this article may be prohibited from residence and deprived for at least five years and at most ten years of the rights mentioned in Article 34.
Prohibition from the national territory may be pronounced against any foreigner having rendered himself guilty in fact of the offences provided for in this article.

###### Article 95

(Law No. 74-13 of 24 June 1974)
Any direct incitement to an unarmed unlawful assembly either by speeches publicly uttered, or by writings or printed matter, posters or distributed, shall be punished by imprisonment of one month to one year, if it has been followed by effect and, in the contrary case, by imprisonment of two months to six months and a fine of 25,000 to 100,000 francs or one of these two penalties only.
Any direct incitement by the same means to an armed unlawful assembly is punished by imprisonment of one year to five years, if it has been followed by effect and in the contrary case, by imprisonment of three months to one year and a fine of 25,000 to 100,000 francs or one of these two penalties only.

##### SECTION Il

MEETINGS ON THE PUBLIC HIGHWAY

###### Article 96

(Law No. 74-13 of 24 June 1974)
Meetings on the public highway are prohibited.
All processions, parades and gatherings of persons and, in general, any demonstration on the public highway are subject to the obligation of a prior declaration. However, exits on the public highway in conformity with local customs are exempted from this declaration.
The declaration shall be made to the administrative authority responsible for maintaining public order on the territory where the demonstration is to take place, at least three clear days and at most fifteen clear days before the date of the demonstration.

###### Article 97

(Law No. 74-13 of 24 June 1974)
Those who have made an incomplete or inaccurate declaration likely to mislead as to the conditions of the planned demonstration or who, either before the filing of the declaration prescribed in Article 96, or after the prohibition, have sent, by any means, a summons to take part in it, shall be punished by imprisonment of six months to one year and a fine of

100,000 to 500,000 francs.
Those who have participated in an undeclared demonstration or one that has been prohibited shall be punished by imprisonment of one to three years and a fine of
100,000 to 500,000 francs.
Those who have participated in the organization of an undeclared demonstration or one that has been prohibited shall be punished by imprisonment of not less than two years and not more than five years and a fine of 1
0,000 to 1,500,000 francs.
In the cases provided for in the two preceding paragraphs, the guilty parties may be sentenced to prohibition of residence under the conditions provided for in Article 36.

##### SECTION III

VARIOUS ACTIONS AND GATHERINGS CAUSING DAMAGE TO PERSONS AND PROPERTY

###### Article 98

(Law No. 74-13 of 24 June 1974)
When, during various actions and unlawful or lawful gatherings, violence or assault is committed against persons or destruction or damage is caused to property, the

instigators, organizers and participants shall be punished under the following conditions:
1)	in the case of action taken following a verbal or written instruction, the instigators and organizers and persons who have voluntarily participated in such action, whatever form their participation may have taken, shall be punished by imprisonment of one to five years;
2)	in the case of an unlawful gathering, the instigators and organizers who have not given the formal order to disperse and persons who have continued to participate in such gathering after the commencement of violence, assault or destruction or damage, shall be punished by imprisonment of one to five years;
3)	in the case of an unlawful gathering, persons who have entered it with a view to committing or causing other participants to commit violence, assault, destruction or damage, shall, whether or not their purpose is achieved, be punished by imprisonment of one to five years;
4)	the organizers of an unlawful gathering, present at the scene, who do not give the order to disperse from the commencement of violence, assault, destruction, or damage, after having been invited to do so by the representatives of the administrative authority, shall be punished by imprisonment of one month to one year, if the damage continues after such formal notice.

##### SECTION IV MISCELLANEOUS PROVISIONS

###### Article 99

(Law No. 74-13 of 24 June 1974)
The institution of prosecutions for the offences specified above does not prevent prosecution for particular crimes or offences that may have been committed at the scene of such offences.
The provisions of Articles 381 et seq. of the Code of Criminal Procedure are applicable to the offences provided for and punished by this chapter.

###### Article 100

(Law No. 74-13 of 24 June 1974)
Persons found guilty of the offences defined in this chapter shall be pecuniarily liable for bodily or material damage caused by the riot, meeting or gathering referred to in connection with such offences.
The judge may, however, limit the compensation to only part of such damage and fix the share attributable to each convicted person, whom he may exempt from the joint and several liability provided for in Article 41. This limitation of liability has no effect on any other action for compensation available to the victim.

#### CHAPTER III CRIMES AND OFFENCES AGAINST THE CONSTITUTION

##### SECTION I OFFENCES

RELATING TO THE EXERCISE OF CIVIC RIGHTS

###### Article 101

When, by riot, assault or threats, one or more citizens have been prevented from exercising their civic rights, each of the guilty parties shall be punished by imprisonment of six months to two years, and by prohibition of the right to vote and to be eligible for election for not less than five years and not more than ten years.

###### Article 102

If such offence has been committed pursuant to a concerted plan to be carried out either throughout the territory of the Republic, or in one or more administrative districts, the penalty shall be two to five years' imprisonment.

###### Article 103

Any citizen who, being responsible, in a ballot, for counting the papers containing the votes of citizens, is caught falsifying such papers, or removing them from the mass, or adding to them, or recording on the ballots of illiterate voters names other than those that have been declared to him, shall be punished by a penalty of six months to two years' imprisonment and prohibition of the right to vote and to be eligible for election for not less than five years and not more than ten years.

###### Article 104

All other persons guilty of the acts set out in the preceding article shall be punished by imprisonment of not less than two months and not more than six months, and prohibition of the right to vote and to be eligible for election for not less than two years and not more than five years.

###### Article 105

Those who, in any manner whatsoever, have undermined or attempted to undermine the sincerity of a ballot, violated or attempted to violate the secrecy of the vote, prevented or attempted to prevent ballot operations, or who have changed or attempted to change the results thereof, shall be punished by imprisonment of one month to one year and a fine of 20,000 to 1 00,000 francs or by only one of these two penalties.
The offender may, in addition, be deprived of his civic rights for not less than two years and not more than five years.
If the guilty party is an official of the administrative or judicial order, an agent or employee of the Government or of a public administration, or entrusted with a ministry of public service, the penalty shall be doubled.
No prosecution relating to acts punished by this section, against a candidate, may be instituted before the proclamation of the ballot.

##### SECTION Il ATTACKS ON LIBERTY

###### Article 106

When a public official, an agent, an employee or a member of the Government, has ordered or committed any arbitrary act, or an attack either on individual liberty, or on the civic rights of one or more citizens, or on the Constitution, he shall be sentenced to the penalty of civic degradation.
If, however, he proves that he acted by order of his superiors for matters within their jurisdiction, in respect of which hierarchical obedience was due to them, he shall be exempt from the penalty, which shall, in such case, be applied only to the superiors who gave the order.

###### Article 107

If the persons accused of having ordered or authorized the act contrary to the Constitution claim that the signature imputed to them was obtained by fraud, they shall be required, while causing the act to cease, to denounce the person whom they declare to be the author of the fraud; otherwise they shall be prosecuted personally.

###### Article 108

Damages that may be awarded in respect of the attacks set out in Article 106 shall be claimed, either in the criminal prosecution, or by civil action, and shall be determined, having regard to the persons, the circumstances and the harm suffered, without in any case, and whatever the injured individual, such damages being below 10,000 francs

for each day of unlawful and arbitrary detention and for each individual,

###### Article 109

If the act contrary to the constitution was committed using a forged signature of the name of a minister or public official, the perpetrators of the forgery and those who knowingly made use of it shall be punished by hard labour for a fixed term of ten to twenty years, the maximum of which shall always be applied in this case.

###### Article 110

Public officials responsible for administrative or judicial police, who have refused or neglected to comply with a legal complaint seeking to establish illegal and arbitrary detentions, whether in premises intended for the custody of detainees or anywhere else, and who do not prove that they reported them to the superior authority, shall be punished by imprisonment of five to ten years, and held liable for damages, which shall be determined as stated in Article 108.

###### Article 111

The guards and keepers of detention, arrest, justice or penal facilities, who have received a prisoner without a warrant or judgment, or when it concerns an expulsion or extradition, without a provisional order from the Head of State, those who have detained him, or have refused to present him to the magistrate, the police officer or the bearer of his orders, without proving the prohibition of the Public Prosecutor or the judge, those who have refused to exhibit their

registers to the police officer, shall, as guilty of arbitrary detention, be punished by six months to two years' imprisonment and a fine of 20,000 to 100,000 francs.

###### Article 112

Shall be guilty of malfeasance and punished by civic degradation, all judicial police officers, all Public Prosecutors General or Public Prosecutors, all deputies, all judges, who have provoked, given or signed a judgment, an order or a warrant tending to the personal prosecution or accusation, either of a minister or of a member of the National Assembly without the authorizations prescribed by the laws of the State, or who, outside cases of flagrant offence, have, without the same authorizations, given or signed the order or warrant to seize or arrest one or more ministers or members of the National Assembly.

###### Article 113

Shall also be punished by civic degradation all Public Prosecutors General or Public Prosecutors, all deputies, all judges or all public officials who have detained or caused to be detained an individual outside the places determined by the Government or by the public Administration, or who have brought a citizen before an Assize Court without his having been previously formally indicted.

##### SECTION III COALITION OF PUBLIC OFFICIALS

###### Article 114

Any concerted action of measures contrary to the laws, practiced either by the assembly of individuals or bodies holding any part of public authority, or by deputation or correspondence between them, shall be punished by imprisonment of at least six months and at most two years against each guilty party, who may furthermore be sentenced to deprivation of civil rights and of any public employment for up to ten years.

###### Article 115

If by one of the means expressed above, measures have been concerted against the execution of the laws or against the orders of the Government, the penalty shall be imprisonment of two years to five years.
If this concerted action took place between the civil authorities and the military bodies or their leaders, those who are the authors or instigators thereof shall be punished by imprisonment of five to ten years; the other guilty parties shall be sentenced to a penalty of imprisonment of at least two years to at most five years.

###### Article 116

In the case where this concerted action had as its object or result a plot against the security of the State, the guilty parties shall be punished by hard labour for life.

###### Article 117

Shall be guilty of malfeasance and punished by the penalty

of civic degradation public officials who have, by deliberation, resolved to give orders the object or effect of which would be to prevent or suspend either the administration of justice or the performance of any service whatsoever.

##### SECTION IV OBSTRUCTION OF ADMINISTRATIVE AND JUDICIAL AUTHORITIES

###### Article 118

Shall be guilty of malfeasance and punished by civic degradation:
1) Judges, Public Prosecutors General or Public Prosecutors or their deputies, police officers, who have interfered in the exercise of legislative power, either by regulations containing legislative provisions, or by stopping or suspending the execution of one or more promulgated laws;
2) Ministers, Governors, Prefects, Mayors, all heads of administrative districts and other administrators who have interfered in the exercise of legislative power, as stated in paragraph 1, or who have taken orders or decisions tending to issue orders or prohibitions of any kind to Courts or Tribunals.
When the authorities referred to in paragraph 2 above, outside the cases provided for by law, encroach upon judicial functions by undertaking to hear private rights and interests within the jurisdiction of the Tribunals, and after the claim of the parties or one of them they have nevertheless decided the matter before the judicial authority has definitively ruled, they shall be punished by a fine of at least 500,000 francs and at most 1,000,000 francs.

#### CHAPTER IV CRIMES AND OFFENCES AGAINST PUBLIC PEACE

##### SECTION I FORGERY

###### Paragraph 1 Counterfeit currency

(Law No. 84-11 of 4 January 1984)

###### Article 119

Whoever has counterfeited, falsified or altered monetary signs having legal tender on the national territory or abroad shall be punished by hard labour for life and a fine ten times the value of said signs and at least equal to 20,000,000 francs.
If the guilty party benefits from mitigating circumstances, the penalty may not be less than five years of hard labour and 1,000,000 francs fine.

###### Article 119 bis

Whoever has:
- either counterfeited or altered gold or silver coins having had legal tender on the national territory or abroad;
- or coloured coins having or having had legal tender on the national territory or abroad, with the intent to deceive as to

the nature of the metal, shall be punished by imprisonment of five to ten years and a fine of
4,000,000 to 10,000,000
francs or by one of these two penalties only.
The attempt shall be punished as the completed offence.

###### Article 119 tertio

Whoever has counterfeited, falsified or altered banknotes or coins other than gold or silver having had legal tender on the national territory or abroad shall be punished by imprisonment of one to five years and a fine of
2,000,000 to 10,000,000
francs or by one of these two penalties only.
The attempt shall be punished as the completed offence.

###### Article 120

Whoever has participated in the issuance, use, display, distribution, importation or exportation of counterfeited, falsified, altered or coloured monetary signs shall be punished by the penalties provided for in the above articles, according to the distinctions set forth therein.
The attempt shall be punished as the completed offence.

###### Article 121

He who, having received as genuine counterfeited, falsified, altered or coloured monetary signs, has used or attempted to use them after having known their defects, shall be punished by imprisonment of six months to one year and a fine at least quadruple and at most ten times the value of said signs, without this fine being less than 200,000 francs or

by one of these two penalties only.
If he has knowingly kept them or has refused to hand them over to the authorities, he shall be punished by a fine at least double and at most quadruple the value of said signs, which may not be less than
100,000 francs.

###### Article 122

Anyone who has manufactured, subscribed, issued, used, displayed, distributed, imported or exported:
- either means of payment intended to supplement or replace monetary signs having legal tender on the national territory or abroad;
- or printed matter, tokens or other objects which present with said monetary signs a resemblance likely to facilitate their acceptance or using in place of said signs, shall be punished by imprisonment of one to five years and a fine of 2,000,000 to 10,000,000 francs or by one of these two penalties only.
The attempt shall be punished as the completed offence.

###### Article 122 bis

Any reproduction, total or partial by any process whatsoever, of monetary signs having legal tender on the national territory or abroad is prohibited, except with the prior authorization of the Central Bank or, in the case of foreign monetary signs, of the authority which issued them.
Also prohibited, and subject to the same reservations, is any display, distribution,

importation or exportation of such reproductions, including by way of newspapers, books or prospectuses.
Any infringement of the provisions of this article shall be punished by imprisonment of one to six months and a fine of 50,000 to 200,000 francs or by one of these two penalties only.

###### Article 122 tertio

Anyone who has manufactured, offered, received, imported, exported or possessed, without being authorized to do so, marks, materials, apparatus or other objects intended by their nature for the manufacture, counterfeiting, falsification, alteration or coloring of monetary signs, shall be punished by imprisonment of two to five years and a fine of 4,000,000 to 10,000,000 francs or by one of these two penalties only.
The attempt shall be punished as the completed offence.

###### Article 123

The penalties provided for in the preceding articles apply: - to offences committed on the national territory;
- to offences committed abroad, according to the distinctions and under the conditions provided for in articles 664 and following of the Code of Criminal Procedure.

###### Article 124

Shall be confiscated, whatever the classification of the offence, the objects referred to in articles 119 to 122 tertio as well as the metals, papers and other materials found in the possession of the guilty parties

and intended for the commission of similar offences. Said confiscated objects, metals, papers and other materials shall be handed over to the Central Bank upon its request, subject to the necessities of the Administration of Justice.

Also confiscated are the instruments having served to commit the offence, except when they have been used without the knowledge of their owner.

###### Article 124 bis

Shall be exempt from penalty anyone who, guilty of one of the offences provided for in articles 119, 119 bis, 119 tertio, 120 and 122 tertio, has given knowledge of it and revealed the perpetrators to the authorities before any prosecution. He may nevertheless be prohibited from residence.
May be exempted from penalty, totally or partially, anyone who, guilty of one of the same offences, has, after prosecution commenced, procured the arrest of the other guilty parties. He may nevertheless be prohibited from residence.

###### Paragraphe Il

Counterfeiting of State seals, public securities, and punches, stamps and marks

###### Article 125

Those who have counterfeited the seal of the State or made use of the counterfeited seal:
Those who have counterfeited or falsified securities issued by the public treasury with its stamp or mark or who

have made use of these counterfeited or falsified securities or who have introduced them into Senegalese territory, shall be punished by the maximum imprisonment.
The counterfeited seals and the counterfeited or falsified securities shall be confiscated and destroyed.
The provisions of the preceding article are applicable to the facts mentioned above.

###### Article 126

Those who have counterfeited or falsified, either one or more national stamps, or the State hammers used for forestry marks, or the punch or punches used to mark gold and silver materials, or who have made use of the falsified or counterfeited papers, securities, stamps, hammers or punches, shall be punished by imprisonment of three years to seven years.
Shall be punished by the same penalty, anyone who having improperly obtained the true stamps, hammers or punches having one of the purposes expressed in the preceding paragraph, has made an application or use thereof prejudicial to the rights or interests of the State.

###### Article 127

Shall be punished by imprisonment of two to five years and a fine of 25,000 to 2,500,000 francs:
1) Those who have counterfeited the marks intended to be affixed in the name of the Government on the various kinds of foodstuffs or goods

or who have made use of these false marks;
2) Those who have counterfeited the seal, stamp or mark of any authority whatsoever, or who have made use of counterfeited seal, stamp or mark;
3) Those who have counterfeited the letterheads or official printed matter in use in the assemblies instituted by the Constitution, the public administrations or the different jurisdictions, who have sold, peddled or distributed them, or who have made use of the papers or printed matter thus counterfeited;
4) Those who have counterfeited or falsified the postage stamps, franking impressions or reply coupons issued by the Senegalese postal administration and the revenue stamps who have knowingly sold, peddled, distributed or used said stamps, impressions or counterfeited or falsified reply coupons. They may be prohibited from residence.
In all cases, the corpus delicti shall be confiscated and destroyed.
The provisions of articles 125, 126 and of this article shall be applicable to attempts of these same offences.

###### Article 128

Anyone who having improperly obtained true seals, marks, stamps or printed matter provided for in the preceding article, has made or attempted to make a fraudulent application or use thereof shall be punished by imprisonment of six months to three years and a fine

of 25,000 to 1,000,000 francs.
The guilty parties may in addition be deprived of the rights mentioned in article 34 of this code for five years at least and ten years at most, from the day they have served their sentence.
They may also be prohibited from residence.

###### Article 129

Shall be punished by imprisonment from six months to three years and a fine from 20,000 to 50,000 francs.
1) Those who have manufactured, sold, hawked or distributed any objects, printed matter or forms, obtained by any process whatsoever which, by their external form, present with coins or banknotes having legal tender in Senegal or abroad, with government bonds, vignettes and stamps of the postal and telecommunications service or of State monopolies, shares, bonds, interest shares, dividend or interest coupons relating thereto and generally with fiduciary securities issued by the State or any other public or semi-public bodies, as well as by private companies or enterprises, a resemblance of a nature to facilitate the acceptance of said objects, printed matter or forms, in place of the imitated securities;
2) Those who have manufactured, sold, hawked, distributed or used printed matter which, by their format, their colour, their text, their typographical layout or any other characteristic,

present, with letterheads or official printed matter in use in the assemblies established by the Constitution, public administrations and the various courts, a resemblance of a nature to cause confusion in the mind of the public;
3) Those who have knowingly made use of postage stamps or mobile stamps having already been used, as well as those who have by any means altered stamps with the aim of removing them from cancellation and thus permitting their subsequent reuse;
4) Those who have overprinted by printing, perforation or any other means postage stamps or other postal fiduciary securities whether expired or not, with the exception of operations prescribed by the ministry of posts, telegraphs and telephones, as well as those who have sold, hawked, offered, distributed, exported postage stamps thus overprinted;
5) Those who have counterfeited, imitated or altered the vignettes, stamps, franking impressions or reply coupons issued by the postal service of a foreign country, who have sold, hawked or distributed said vignettes, stamps, franking impressions or reply coupons or who have made use thereof;
6) Those who have counterfeited, imitated or altered Senegalese or foreign postal identity cards, poste restante subscription cards, who have sold, hawked or distributed said cards or have made use thereof.

In all cases provided for in this article, the corpus delicti shall be confiscated and destroyed.

###### Paragraphe III

Forgery in authentic public documents

###### Article 130

Any public official or officer who, in the exercise of his functions, has committed or attempted to commit a forgery,
Either by false signatures;
- Or by alteration of deeds, writings or signatures;
- Or by substitution of persons;
- Or by writings made or inserted in registers or other public deeds, after their preparation or closure, shall be punished by the maximum imprisonment.

###### Article 131

Shall be punished by the same penalty, any public official or officer who, in drafting deeds of his ministry, has fraudulently misrepresented the substance or circumstances thereof, either by writing agreements other than those which had been drawn up or dictated by the parties, or by recording as true false facts, or as acknowledged facts which were not.

###### Article 132

Shall be punished by imprisonment from five to ten years all other persons who have committed

or attempted to commit a forgery in authentic and public documents:
- Either by counterfeiting or alteration of writings or signatures;
- Either by fabrication of agreements, dispositions, obligations or discharges or by their subsequent insertion in these deeds;
- Either by addition or alteration of clauses, declarations or facts which these deeds were intended to receive and record.
Shall be punished by the same penalty all military administrators or accountants who knowingly enter on the rolls, situation or review statements, a number of men, equipment, horses or days of presence beyond the actual strength, who exaggerate the amount of consumption or commit any other forgery in their accounts.

###### Article 133

In all cases expressed in articles 130 to 132, whoever has made use or attempted to make use of the false deeds shall be punished by imprisonment from at least two years to at most ten years.

###### Article 134

Excepted from the above provisions are the forgeries provided for in articles 137 to 145 inclusive on which particular provision is made hereinafter.

###### Paragraphe IV

Forgery in private, commercial or banking documents

###### Article 135

Any individual who has, in one of the manners expressed in article 132, committed or attempted to commit a forgery in private, commercial or banking documents, shall be punished by imprisonment from three to seven years and a fine from
50,000 to 2,000,000 francs.
The offender may in addition be sentenced to prohibition of residence for up to ten years.

###### Article 136

Shall be punished by the same penalties whoever has made use or attempted to make use of the false document.

###### Paragraphe V

Forgeries committed in certain administrative documents, in travel warrants and certificates

###### Article 137

Whoever has counterfeited, falsified or altered permits, certificates, booklets, cards, bulletins, receipts, passports, passes or other documents issued by public administrations with a view to establishing a right, an identity or a quality, or to granting an authorization, shall be punished by imprisonment from six months to three years and a fine from 20,000 to 500,000 francs.
The offender may, in addition, be deprived of the rights mentioned in article 34 of this Code for at least five years and at most ten years from the day he has served his sentence.

The attempt shall be punished as the completed offence.
The same penalties shall be applied:
1) To whoever has made use of one of the counterfeited, falsified or altered documents;
2) To whoever has made use of one of the documents referred to in the first paragraph, when the particulars invoked by the person concerned have become incomplete or inaccurate.

###### Article 138

Whoever has obtained unduly or has attempted to obtain unduly one of the documents provided for in the preceding article, either by making false declarations, or by taking a false name or a false quality, or by providing false information, certificates or attestations shall be punished by imprisonment from three months to two years and a fine from 20,000 to 50,000 francs.
The same penalties shall be applied to whoever has made use of such a document, either obtained in the aforesaid conditions, or drawn up under a name other than his own.
The official who issues or causes to be issued one of the documents provided for in the preceding article to a person whom he knows not to be entitled thereto, shall be punished by imprisonment from one to four years and a fine from 20,000 to 500,000 francs, without prejudice to the more severe penalties he might incur by application of articles 159 and following. The offender may, in addition, be deprived of the rights

mentioned in article 34 of this code for at least five years and at most ten years from the day he has served his sentence.

###### Article 139

Lodging-house keepers and innkeepers who, knowingly, register in their registers, under false or assumed names, the persons lodged with them, or who, in collusion with them, have omitted to register them, shall be punished by imprisonment from one to three months and a fine from
20,000 to 50,000 francs.

###### Article 140

Whoever manufactures a false travel warrant, or falsifies a travel warrant originally genuine, or makes use of a manufactured or falsified travel warrant, shall be punished, namely:
- By imprisonment of at least six months and at most three years, if the false travel warrant had as its only object to deceive the surveillance of the public authority;
- By imprisonment of at least one year and at most four years, if the Public Treasury has paid to the bearer of the false warrant travel expenses that were not due to him or that exceeded those to which he could have been entitled, all however above 5,000 francs;
- And by imprisonment of at least two years and at most five years, if the sums unduly received by the bearer of the warrant amount to 5,000 francs or above.
In these latter two cases, the guilty parties may,

in addition, be deprived of the rights mentioned in Article 34 of this Code for at least five years and at most ten years, from the day they have served their sentence.

###### Article 141

The penalties provided for in the preceding article shall be applied, according to the distinctions established therein, to any person who has had a travel warrant issued to him by the public officer under a false name or who has made use of a travel warrant issued under a name other than his own.

###### Article 142

If the public officer was aware of the false name when he issued the travel warrant, he shall be punished namely:
- In the first case set out in Article 140, by imprisonment of at least one year and at most four years;
- In the second case of the same article, by imprisonment of at least two years and at most five years;
- In the third case, by imprisonment of five to ten years; - In the first two cases, he must, in addition, be deprived of the rights mentioned in Article 34 of this Code for at least five years and at most ten years from the day he has served his sentence.

###### Article 143

Whoever, to redeem himself or exempt another from any public service, manufactures under the name of a doctor, surgeon or any other

person exercising a medical or paramedical profession, a certificate of illness or infirmity, shall be punished by imprisonment of at least one year and at most three years.

###### Article 144

Apart from the case of corruption provided for in Article 159 below, any doctor, surgeon, dentist or midwife who, in the exercise of his functions and to favour someone, falsely certifies or conceals the existence of illnesses or infirmities or a state of pregnancy, or provides false information on the origin of an illness or infirmity or the cause of a death, shall be punished by imprisonment of one to three years.
The guilty party may, in addition, be deprived of the rights mentioned in Article 34 of this Code for at least five years and at most ten years from the day he has served his sentence.

###### Article 145

Whoever manufactures, under the name of a public official or officer, a certificate of good conduct, indigence or other circumstances likely to call the benevolence of the Government or of private individuals upon the person designated therein, and to procure for him positions, credits or assistance, shall be punished by imprisonment of six months to two years.
The same penalty shall be applied:
1) To whoever falsifies a certificate of this kind, originally genuine, to appropriate it to a person

other than the one to whom it was originally issued;
3) To any individual who has made use of the certificate thus manufactured or falsified.
If this certificate is manufactured under the name of a simple private individual, the manufacture and use shall be punished by one month to one year of imprisonment.
Shall be punished by imprisonment of six months to two years and a fine of 20,000 to 200,000 francs, or by only one of these two penalties, without prejudice to the application, where appropriate, of the more severe penalties provided for by this Code and special laws, whoever:
1) Has knowingly drawn up an attestation or certificate stating materially inaccurate facts:
2) Has falsified or modified in any manner an attestation or certificate originally sincere;
3) Has knowingly made use of an inaccurate or falsified attestation or certificate.

###### Article 146

The forgeries punished under this paragraph from which there could result either injury to third parties or prejudice to the Public Treasury, shall be punished by a penalty of imprisonment of at least two years and at most ten years.

##### SECTION II

MALFEASANCE, OFFENCES AGAINST PUBLIC FUNDS AND CRIMES AND OFFENCES OF PUBLIC OFFICIALS IN THE EXERCISE OF THEIR FUNCTIONS

###### Article 149

Any crime committed by a public official in the exercise of his functions is malfeasance.

###### Article 150

Any malfeasance for which the law does not impose more severe penalties is punished by civic degradation.

###### Article 151

Simple offences do not constitute officials in malfeasance.

###### Paragraph one Misappropriations,

thefts and frauds relating to public funds

###### Article 152

Any person who has misappropriated or stolen or attempted to misappropriate or steal, funds or

effects in lieu thereof, documents, payment instruments, securities, acts containing or effecting obligation or discharge, movable effects, commodities, works of art or any objects whatsoever to the prejudice of the State, a public collectivity, a public establishment, a national company, a mixed economy company subject by right to State control, a legal person under private law benefiting from the financial support of the public authority, a professional order, a private body charged with the execution of a public service, an association or foundation recognised as being of public utility, shall be punished:
- if it is a simple private individual, by imprisonment of one to five years;
- if it is a civil or military agent of the State or of a public collectivity, whether or not he is a public accountant, a person vested with a public mandate, a public depositary or a public or ministerial officer, a director or an agent of any nature of public establishments, national companies, mixed economy companies subject by right to State control, legal persons under private law benefiting from the financial support of the public authority, professional orders, private bodies charged with the execution of a public service, associations or foundations recognised as being of public utility, by imprisonment of five to ten years.
Shall also be punished by five to ten years of impri-

sonment any civil or military agent of the State or of a public collectivity, whether or not he is a public accountant, any person vested with a public mandate, any public depositary and any public or ministerial officer who has misappropriated or stolen or attempted to misappropriate or steal funds or documents to the prejudice of private persons, on the occasion of the exercise of his functions.

###### Article 153

Any person designated in the first paragraph of the preceding article who has obtained or attempted to obtain fraudulently from the State, a public authority, a national company or a mixed economy company subject by operation of law to State control, by means of false documents or any maneuvers, sums of money or undue material advantages, shall be punished with the same penalties following the same distinctions as in the preceding article.
Shall also be punished with five to ten years' imprisonment, any civil or military agent of the State or of a public authority, any person holding a public mandate, any public depositary or public or ministerial officer who has obtained or attempted to obtain fraudulently from a private person by means of false documents or any maneuvers, sums of money or undue material advantages in connection with the exercise of his functions.

###### Article 154

(Law No. 69-47 of 16 July 1969)

In the cases expressed in the two preceding articles, a fine of 20,000 to
5,000,000 francs shall always be imposed on the convicted person.
The confiscation of all the property of the convicted person shall be mandatorily imposed under the conditions provided for in articles 30 to 32 when the sums or objects misappropriated or removed have not been reimbursed or returned in full at the time of judgment.

###### Article 155

With regard to accused persons found guilty of the acts punished by articles 152 to 154, the application of mitigating circumstances shall be subject to the return or reimbursement, before judgment, of at least one-third of the value misappropriated or removed.
The benefit of suspended sentence may only be granted in case of return or reimbursement before judgment of at least three-quarters of said value.
The application or proposal for conditional release shall only be admissible after return or reimbursement of the entirety of said value.
Money, effects and objects of any kind which are not returned spontaneously by the perpetrator of the offense or upon his precise indications, shall not be included in the calculation of the reimbursement fractions allowing the application of mitigating circumstances or suspended sentence.
The examining magistrate and the president of the court shall bring the provisions of this

article to the knowledge of the accused or the defendant.
The administrative act establishing the amount of the sum owed to the State by the defendant is not prejudicial to the judgment of the offenses punished by articles 152 to 154.

###### Paragraphe Il Extortion committed by

public officials and their interference in affairs or businesses incompatible with their capacity

###### Article 156

All public officials or officers, all collectors of duties, contributions or public monies, their clerks or agents, who have received, demanded or ordered the collection of, for duties, taxes, contributions or monies, or for salaries or remuneration, what they knew was not due or exceeded what was due, shall be punished, namely: the officials, public officers or collectors, with imprisonment of two to ten years and their clerks or agents with imprisonment of one to five
years; a fine of 250,000 to 500,000 francs shall always be imposed.
The convicted clerks or agents may be prohibited for up to ten years, from the expiration of the sentence, from the rights enumerated in article 34 of this Code. In addition, they may be declared incapable of exercising any public employment for up to twenty years.
The foregoing provisions are applicable to court clerks and ministerial officers when the act was

committed in connection with the receipts with which they are charged by law.
Shall be punished with the same penalties all holders of public authority who order direct or indirect contributions other than those authorized by law, all officials, agents or employees who establish the rolls thereof or collect them.
The same penalties shall be applicable to holders of public authority who, in any form and for any reason whatsoever, have, without authorization of law, granted exemptions or franchises from duties, taxes or public charges, or have effected free delivery of products from State establishments.
The beneficiaries shall be punished as accomplices.
In all cases provided for in this article, the attempt of the offense shall be punished as the offense itself.

###### Article 157

Any official, any officer, any member or agent of the Government, who, either openly or by simulated acts or by interposition of persons, has taken or received any interest whatsoever in acts, awards, enterprises or administrations of which he has or had, at the time of the act, in whole or in part, the administration or supervision, shall be punished with imprisonment of at least one year and at most five years, and shall be sentenced to a fine which may not exceed one-quarter of the restitutions and indemnities, nor be less than one-twelfth.

He shall moreover be declared forever incapable of exercising any public function.

###### Article 158

Any official, any agent of the administrative or judicial order, any career officer or military person, who, openly or by simulated acts or by interposition of persons, has engaged in commercial activity, shall be punished with a fine of
100,000 francs to 2,500,000
francs and the confiscation of all property forming the object of this activity or enabling its exercise.
The spouse shall not be deemed an interposed person when the official, the agent of the administrative or judicial order, the career officer or military person, has completed the formality provided for in article 11 of law No. 61-33 of 15 June 1961 relating to the general status of civil servants.

###### Paragraphe III Corruption of

public officials and employees of private enterprises

###### Article 159

Shall be punished by imprisonment of two to ten years and a fine of double the value of the promises accepted or things received or requested, provided that said fine shall not be less than 150,000 francs, whoever shall have solicited or accepted offers or promises, solicited or received gifts or presents for:
1) Being invested with an elective mandate, public official of the administrative or judicial order, military or equivalent, agent or employee

of a public administration, citizen charged with a ministry of public service, director or agent of any nature of a public establishment, a professional order, a cooperative benefiting from State support or a public authority, a private body charged with a public service mission, an association or foundation recognized as being of public utility or a company in which a public authority holds at least half of the capital, doing or refraining from doing an act of his functions or employment, just or not, but not subject to salary;
2) Being an arbitrator, or expert appointed either by the Court or by the parties, rendering a decision or giving an opinion favorable or unfavorable to a party;
3) Being a doctor, surgeon, dentist, midwife, falsely certifying or concealing the existence of diseases or infirmities, a state of pregnancy or providing false information on the origin of a disease or infirmity or the cause of a death.
Shall be punished by imprisonment of one to three years and a fine of 25,000 to 100,000 francs or one of these two penalties only any clerk, employee or agent, salaried or remunerated in any form whatsoever, who, either directly or through an intermediary, shall have, without the knowledge and without the consent of his employer, either solicited or accepted offers or promises, or solicited or received gifts, presents, commissions, discounts or takings for doing or refraining from doing an act of his employment.

If the offers, promises, gifts or solicitations tended toward the accomplishment or abstention of an act which, although outside the personal attributions of the corrupted person, was or would have been facilitated by his function or by the service he provided, the penalty shall be, in the case of paragraph 1 of the first subparagraph, imprisonment of one to three years and a fine of 50,000 to 500,000 francs and, in the case of the second subparagraph, imprisonment of six months to two years and a fine of 25,000 to 75,000 francs or one of these two penalties only.

###### Article 160

Shall be punished by imprisonment of at least one year and at most five years and the fine provided for by the first subparagraph of Article 159, any person who shall have solicited or accepted offers or promises, solicited or received gifts or presents to obtain or attempt to obtain decorations, medals, distinctions or rewards, positions, functions or employments or any favors whatsoever granted by the public authority, contracts, enterprises or other benefits resulting from treaties concluded with the public authority or with an administration placed under the control of the public power, or, in general, a favorable decision from such an authority or administration and shall have thus abused a real or supposed influence.
However, when the offender is one of the persons referred to in paragraph 1 of the first subparagraph of Article 159 and he has abused the real or supposed influence given to him by his

mandate or his capacity, the penalty of imprisonment shall be at least two years and at most ten years.
However, persons who shall have, before any judicial prosecution under Articles 159 and 160, revealed to the competent authorities the acts committed by the corrupted person shall not be prosecuted.

###### Article 161

Whoever, to obtain either the accomplishment or abstention of an act, or one of the favors or advantages provided for in Articles 159 and 160, shall have used acts of violence or threats, promises, offers, gifts or presents, or yielded to solicitations tending toward corruption, even if he did not take the initiative, shall be, whether the constraint or corruption has or has not produced its effect, punished by the same penalties as those provided for in said articles against the corrupted person.

###### Article 162

In the case where the corruption or influence peddling would have as its object an act involving a more severe penalty, this more severe penalty shall be applied to the offenders.
In the cases provided for in subparagraphs 1 and 3 of Article 159 and in subparagraph 2 of Article 160, the offender, if he is an officer, shall, in addition, be punished by dismissal. If the offender is a military person or equivalent, the provisions of the Code of Military Justice shall be applied with regard to the fine penalty.
In the cases provided for in the three preceding articles,

the offenders may, in addition, be deprived of the rights mentioned in Article 34 for at least five years and at most ten years, from the day they shall have served their sentence.
The court may order the restitution to the person exempted from prosecution, of the things delivered by him or their value.
Otherwise, they shall be confiscated for the benefit of the Treasury.

###### Article 163

If it is a judge pronouncing in criminal matters, or a juror who has allowed himself to be corrupted, either in favor of or to the prejudice of the accused, he shall be punished by imprisonment of five years to ten years in addition to the fine ordered by Article 159.

###### Paragraphe III bis

Illicit enrichment
(Law No. 81-53 of July 10, 1981)

###### Article 163 bis

The illicit enrichment of any holder of an elective public mandate or governmental function, any magistrate, civil or military agent of the State, or of a public authority, a person vested with a public mandate, a public depositary or a public or ministerial officer, a director or agent of any nature of public establishments, national companies, mixed economy companies subject by right to State control, legal persons under private law benefiting from the financial support of the public power, professional orders, private bodies

charged with the execution of a public service, associations or foundations recognized as being of public utility, is punished by imprisonment of five to ten years and a fine at least equal to the amount of the enrichment and which may be increased to double that amount.
The offense of illicit enrichment is constituted when, upon simple formal notice, one of the persons designated above finds himself unable to justify the lawful origin of the resources which enable him to be in possession of assets or to maintain a lifestyle out of proportion to his lawful income.
The lawful origin of the elements of the assets may be proved by any means.
However, the sole proof of a gratuity is not sufficient to justify this lawful origin.
In the case where the illicit enrichment is realized through an intermediary or a natural person directing the legal person shall be prosecuted as accomplices of the principal perpetrator.

###### Paragraphe IV Abuses of authority

First class
Abuses of authority against individuals

###### Article 164

Any official of the administrative or judicial order, any officer of justice or police, any commander or agent of the public force who, acting in said capacity, shall have entered the dwelling of a

citizen against the latter's will, outside the cases provided for by law, and without the formalities it has prescribed, shall be punished by imprisonment of six months to two years, and a fine of 25,000 to 150,000 francs, without prejudice to the application of the second paragraph of Article 106.
Any individual who shall have entered by means of threats or violence into the dwelling of a citizen shall be punished by imprisonment of two months to one year and a fine of 25,000 to 100,000 francs.

###### Article 165

Any judge or tribunal, any administrator or administrative authority, who, under any pretext whatsoever, even the silence or obscurity of the law, shall have denied to render the justice owed to the parties, after having been requested to do so, and who shall have persisted in such denial after warning or injunction from superiors, may be prosecuted, and shall be punished by a fine of 25,000 to 150,000 francs and disqualification from the exercise of public functions from five years to twenty years.

###### Article 166

When a public official or officer, an administrator, an agent or employee of the Government or the police, an executor of judicial warrants or judgments, a commander in chief or subordinate commander of the public force, shall have, without legitimate reason, used or caused to be used violence against persons in the exercise or on the occasion of the exercise of his functions, he shall be punished according to the nature and gravity of such violence, and by increasing the

penalty according to the rule set forth in Article 178 below.

###### Article 167

Any suppression, any opening of letters entrusted to the post, committed or facilitated by an official or agent of the Government or the postal administration, shall be punished by a fine of
25,000 to 150,000 francs and
imprisonment of three months to five years. The offender shall moreover be disqualified from any public function or employment for at least five years and at most ten.
Outside the cases provided for in the preceding paragraph, any suppression, any opening of correspondence addressed to third parties, done in bad faith, shall be punished by imprisonment of six days to one year and a fine of
20,000 to 100,000 francs or
one of these two penalties only.

Second class
Abuses of authority against the public interest

###### Article 168

Any public official, member, agent or employee of the Government, of whatever status and rank, who shall have requisitioned or ordered, caused to be requisitioned or ordered the action or employment of the public force against the execution of a law or against the collection of a lawful tax, or against the execution either of an order or warrant of justice, or of any other order emanating from the legitimate authority, shall be punished by imprisonment of five to ten years.

###### Article 169

If such requisition or such order has been followed by its effect, the maximum penalty must be pronounced.

###### Article 170

The penalties pronounced in Articles 168 and 169 shall cease to be applicable to officials or employees who have acted by order of their superiors, only insofar as such order shall have been given by the latter for matters within their jurisdiction and on which hierarchical obedience was owed to them; in such case, the penalties set forth above shall be applied only to the superiors who first gave such order.
If, as a result of said orders or requisitions, other offences occur punishable by penalties stronger than those expressed in Articles 168 and 169, such stronger penalties shall be applied to the
officials, agents or
employees, guilty of having given said orders or made said requisitions.

###### Paragraphe V

Certain offences relating to the keeping of civil status records

###### Article 172

Civil status officers who shall have recorded their acts on simple loose sheets, shall be punished by imprisonment of at least one month and at most three months and a fine of
25,000 to 50,000 francs.

###### Article 173

When, for the validity of a marriage, the law prescribes the consent of the father, mother

or other persons, and the civil status officer shall not have ascertained the existence of such consent, he shall be punished by a fine of 25,000 to 60,000 francs and imprisonment of at least six months and at most one year.

###### Article 174

The civil status officer shall also be punished by a fine of 25,000 to 75,000 francs, when he shall have received before the time prescribed by the provisions of the Civil Code, the marriage certificate of a woman having already been married.

###### Article 175

The penalties set forth in the preceding articles against civil status officers shall be applied to them, even if the nullity of their acts had not been requested or had been cured: all without prejudice to the stronger penalties pronounced in case of collusion, and without prejudice also to the other penal provisions of the Code of Civil and Commercial Obligations.

###### Paragraphe VI

Exercise of public authority illegally anticipated or prolonged

###### Article 176

Any public official who shall have entered into the exercise of his functions without having taken an oath, may be prosecuted and shall be punished by a fine of
25,000 to 50,000 francs.

###### Article 177

Any public official revoked, dismissed, suspended or legally disqualified who, after having had official knowledge thereof, shall have continued the exercise of his

functions, or who being elective or temporary, shall have exercised them after having been replaced, shall be punished by imprisonment of at least six months and at most two years and a fine of
25,000 to 100,000 francs. He shall be disqualified from the exercise of any public function for at least five years and at most ten years, from the day he shall have served his sentence: all without prejudice to the stronger penalties against officers or military commanders under Article 76 of this Code.

##### SECTION III DISTURBANCES

###### Article 179

Ministers of religion or religious authorities

who shall pronounce in the exercise of their ministry and in public assembly a speech containing malicious criticism directed at the Government, a law, a decree or any other act of public authority, shall be punished by imprisonment of three months to two years.

###### Article 180

If the speech contains a direct provocation to disobedience to the laws or other acts of public authority, or if it tends to incite or arm one part of the citizens against the others, the minister of religion or religious authority who shall have pronounced it shall be punished by imprisonment of two to five years, if the provocation has not been followed by any effect, and by imprisonment of five to ten years if it has given rise to disobedience, other however than that which would have degenerated into sedition or revolt.

##### SECTION IV

RESISTANCE, DISOBEDIENCE AND OTHER OFFENCES AGAINST PUBLIC AUTHORITY

###### Paragraphe premier Rébellion

###### Article 185

Any attack, any resistance with violence and assault against ministerial officers, forest guards, the public force, officers responsible for the collection of taxes and contributions, bearers of writs of execution, customs officers, sequestrators, officers or agents of the administrative or

judicial police, acting for the execution of laws, orders or ordinances of the public authority, warrants of justice or judgments, is qualified as the offence of rebellion.

###### Article 186

If the rebellion has been committed by more than three persons, the guilty parties shall be punished by imprisonment of two to five years.
The guilty parties may in addition be deprived of the rights mentioned in article 34 for five years at least and ten years at most.

###### Article 187

If the rebellion has been committed by fewer than three persons, the guilty parties are punished by imprisonment of six months to two years and a fine of 20,000 to 50,000 francs, or by one of these penalties only.

###### Article 188

Those who are found carrying weapons shall be liable to double the penalties provided for in articles 186 and 187.

###### Article 189

In case of rebellion with a band or gathering, article 89 of this Code shall be applicable to rebels without functions or employment in the band, who have withdrawn at the first warning of the public authority, or even since, if they have been seized only outside the place of the rebellion and without new resistance and without weapons.

###### Article 190

The perpetrators of crimes and offences committed during the course and on the occasion of a rebellion shall be punished by the penalties pronounced against each of these crimes, if they are stronger than those for rebellion.

###### Article 191

Shall be punished as gatherings of rebels, those which have been formed with or without weapons, and accompanied by violence or threats against the administrative authority, officers and agents of the police, or against the public force:
1)	By workers or day labourers in public workshops or factories;
2)	By individuals admitted to hospices;
3)	By prisoners under investigation, accused or convicted.

###### Article 192

The penalty applied for rebellion to prisoners under investigation, accused or convicted in relation to other crimes or offences, shall be served by them, namely:
By those who, by reason of the crimes or offences which caused their detention, are or would be sentenced to a penalty neither capital nor perpetual, immediately after the expiration of that penalty;
And by the others, immediately after the judgment or decision in final resort which has acquitted them or discharged them from the act for which they were detained.

###### Article 193

The leaders of a rebellion and those who have provoked it may, in addition, be sentenced to prohibition of residence for a period of two to ten years and to deprivation of the rights mentioned in article 34.

###### Paragraphe Il Outrages et violences

envers les dépositaires de l'autorité et de la force publique

###### Article 194

When one or more magistrates of the administrative or judicial order, when one or more jurors have received in the exercise of their functions or on the occasion of that exercise any outrage by words, by writing or drawing not made public, tending, in these various cases, to impugn their honour or their integrity, the person who has addressed that outrage to them shall be punished by imprisonment of one month to two years.
If the outrage by words took place at the hearing of a Court or a Tribunal, the imprisonment shall be two to five years.

###### Article 195

The outrage committed by gestures or by threats or by sending any objects with the same intention, and targeting a magistrate or a juror, in the exercise or on the occasion of the exercise of his functions, shall be punished by one month to six months of imprisonment; and if the outrage took place at the hearing of a Court or a Tribunal, it shall be punished by imprisonment of one month to two years.

###### Article 196

The outrage committed by words, gestures, threats, writings or drawings not made public or also by sending any objects with the same intention, and targeting any ministerial officer or agent holding the public force or any citizen charged with a ministry of public service, in the exercise or on the occasion of the exercise of his functions, shall be punished by imprisonment of one month to three months and a fine of 20,000 to 50,000 francs or by one of these two penalties only.

###### Article 197

The outrage mentioned in the preceding article, when it has been directed against a commander of the public force, shall be punished by imprisonment of one to six months and may also be punished by a fine of 20,000 to
50,000 francs.

###### Article 198

Whoever has publicly by acts, words or writings, sought to bring discredit upon an act or a judicial decision, in conditions of a nature to undermine the authority of justice or its independence, shall be punished by one to six months of imprisonment and a fine of 20,000 to 100,000 francs or by one of these two penalties only.
The Tribunal may, in addition, order that its decision shall be posted and published under the conditions it shall determine at the expense of the convicted person without such expenses being able to exceed the maximum of the fine provided above.

The preceding provisions cannot, in any case, be applied to purely technical commentaries, nor to acts, words or writings tending to the revision of a conviction.
When the offence has been committed by any means of public dissemination, the provisions of article 270 of this Code shall be applicable.

###### Article 199

Shall be punished with the penalties provided for in Article 198 whoever shall have published, before the final judicial decision is rendered, comments tending to exert pressure on the statements of witnesses or on the decision of the investigating or trial courts.
The provisions of the last three paragraphs of Article 198 are furthermore applicable.
Any individual who, even without weapons and without resulting in injuries, shall have struck a magistrate in the exercise of his functions or on the occasion of such exercise, or committed any other violence or assault against him in the same circumstances, shall be punished with imprisonment of two to five years,
The maximum of this penalty shall always be pronounced if the assault took place at the hearing of a Court or Tribunal,
The offender may, in addition, in both cases, be deprived of the rights mentioned in Article 34 of this Code for five years at least and ten years at most from the day he shall have served his sentence and prohibited from residence.

###### Article 201

Violence or assault of the kind expressed in Article 200, directed against a ministerial officer, an agent of the public force or a citizen charged with a ministry of public service, if they took place while they were exercising their ministry or on that occasion, shall be punished with imprisonment of one month at least and three years at most and a fine of
20,000 to 50,000 francs.

###### Article 202

If the violence exercised against the officials and agents designated in Articles 200 and 201 has been the cause of bloodshed, wounds or illness, the penalty shall be imprisonment of three to ten years; if death has ensued, the offender shall be punished with hard labour for life.

###### Article 203

In the case where such violence has not caused bloodshed, wounds or illness, the blows shall be punished with imprisonment of five to ten years, if they were inflicted with premeditation or ambush,

###### Article 204

If the blows were inflicted or the wounds made to one of the officials or agents designated in Articles 200 and
201 in the exercise or on the occasion of the exercise of their functions with intent to cause death, the offender shall be punished with death.

###### Paragraphe III Refusal of a service legally due

Article 205
Any commander, any officer or non-commissioned officer of the public force who, after having been legally requisitioned by the administrative or judicial authority, shall have refused or abstained from deploying the force under his orders, shall be punished with dismissal and imprisonment of one year to two years, or with one of these penalties only.

###### Article 206

The penal laws and regulations relating to military conscription shall continue to be executed.

###### Article 207

Witnesses and jurors who shall have alleged an excuse recognized as false, shall be sentenced, in addition to the fines pronounced for non-appearance, to imprisonment of one month to three months.

###### Paragraphe IV Escape of detainees

###### Article 208

Whenever an escape of detainees takes place, the bailiffs, the commanders in chief or in subordinate command, whether of the gendarmerie or of the armed force serving as escort or manning the posts, the concierges, guards, jailers, and all other persons assigned to the conduct, transport or custody of detainees, shall be punished as provided in the following articles.

###### Article 209

If the escape is due to the negligence of those assigned to the custody or conduct of the detainee, the penalty incurred by them shall be two months to six months imprisonment.
In case of connivance, the penalty incurred shall be one year to five years imprisonment and a fine of 100,000 francs to 1 million francs; in addition the offender may be deprived of the rights mentioned in Article 34 for five years at least and ten years at most.
Those who, even not being charged with the custody or conduct of the detainee shall have procured, facilitated or attempted to procure or facilitate his escape or his flight, once the escape is accomplished, shall be punished with imprisonment of one month to two years and a fine of 50,000 to 200,000 francs.

###### Article 211

If the escape took place or was attempted with violence or prison break, those who shall have favoured it by providing instruments suitable for carrying it out shall be punished with imprisonment of six months to three years and a fine of 50,000 to
200,000 francs, all without
prejudice to the more severe penalties provided in the preceding articles.
The offenders may, in addition, be deprived of the rights mentioned in Article 34 for five years at least and ten years at most from the day they shall have served their sentence.

###### Article 212

When third parties who shall have procured or facilitated

the escape shall have succeeded by corrupting the guards or jailers, or in connivance with them, they shall be punished with the same penalties as said guards and jailers.

###### Article 213

If the escape with breaking or violence has been favoured by transmission of weapons, the guards and conductors who shall have participated therein shall be punished with imprisonment of five to ten years; other persons, with imprisonment of two to five years and, mandatorily, with definitive deprivation of all the rights mentioned in Article 34.

###### Article 214

All those who shall have conspired in the escape of a detainee shall be jointly and severally condemned, as damages, to all that the civil party of the detainee would have had the right to obtain against him.

###### Article 215

Detainees who shall have escaped or who shall have attempted to escape, by prison break or by violence, shall, for this fact alone, be punished with imprisonment of six months at least, which may be raised up to a penalty equal to that by reason of which they were detained, or, if they were detained
preventively, to that
attached by law to the charge which motivated the detention, without it being able, in either case, to exceed ten years imprisonment; all without prejudice to the more severe penalties they might have incurred for other crimes or offences they might have committed in their violence.

They shall serve this penalty immediately after the expiration of that which they shall have incurred for the crime or offence by reason of which they were detained or immediately after the judgment or decision which shall have acquitted them or discharged them from said crime or offence.
Shall be punished with the same penalty, which shall be served under the same conditions, any detainee transferred to a health or hospital establishment and who, by any means, shall have escaped or shall have attempted to escape therefrom.
Shall be punished with the same penalty, which shall be served under the same conditions, any convict who shall have escaped or shall have attempted to escape while he was outside a penitentiary establishment, or while he was subject to the regime of semi-liberty, or while he was benefiting from permission to leave a penitentiary establishment.

###### Article 216

The penalties established above against conductors or guards in case of negligence only, shall cease when the escapees are recaptured or surrender, provided that it is within four months of the escape and that they are not arrested for other crimes or offences committed subsequently.
No prosecution shall take place against those who shall have attempted to procure or facilitate an escape if, before it has been accomplished, they have given knowledge of the plan to the administrative or judicial authorities and have revealed its authors to them.

###### Article 217

Without prejudice to the application, where appropriate, of the heavier penalties provided for in the preceding articles, anyone who has, under irregular conditions, delivered or caused to be delivered or attempted to deliver or cause to be delivered to a detainee, in any place whatsoever, sums of money, correspondence, or any objects whatsoever, shall be punished by imprisonment of one month to six months.
The removal or attempted irregular removal of sums of money, correspondence or any objects whatsoever, shall be punished by the same penalties.
The acts referred to in the preceding paragraphs shall be considered as accomplished under irregular conditions if they were committed in violation of a regulation emanating from the prison administration or approved by it.
If the offender is one of the persons designated in Article 208 or a person authorized by their functions to approach detainees in any capacity whatsoever, the penalty for them shall be imprisonment of six months to two years.

###### Paragraphe V Breaking of seals and

removal of documents from public depositories

###### Article 218

When seals affixed, either by order of the Government or pursuant to a court order rendered in any matter whatsoever, have been broken, the guardians shall be punished, for simple negligence, by

six months to two years of imprisonment.

###### Article 219

Anyone who has deliberately broken or attempted to break affixed seals or participated in the breaking of seals or in the attempted breaking of seals shall be punished by imprisonment of one to three years.
If it is the guardian himself who has broken the seals or participated in the breaking of seals, he shall be punished by imprisonment of two to five years.
He may, in addition, be deprived of the rights mentioned in Article 34 for at least five years and at most ten years from the day he has served his sentence.

###### Article 220

In the cases provided for in the preceding article, the offender shall be sentenced to a fine of 20,000 to
250,000 francs.

###### Article 221

Any theft committed by means of breaking seals shall be punished as theft committed by means of breaking and entering.

###### Article 222

As for the misappropriations, destructions and removals of documents or criminal proceedings, or other papers, registers, deeds and effects, contained in archives, court registries or public depositories or entrusted to a public depositary in that capacity, the penalties shall be against negligent court clerks, archivists, notaries or other depositaries, of three months to one year of imprisonment and a

fine of 20,000 to 150,000 francs.

###### Article 223

Anyone who has been guilty of the misappropriations, removals or destructions mentioned in the preceding article, shall be punished by imprisonment of two years to five years.
If the offense is committed by the depositary himself, he shall be punished by imprisonment of five to ten years.
The attempt of the offense shall be punished as the offense itself.

###### Article 224

If the breaking of seals, the misappropriations, removals or destructions of documents have been committed with violence against persons, the penalty shall be, against any person, that of imprisonment of five to ten years, without prejudice to heavier penalties, if applicable, according to the nature of the other crimes that may be joined thereto.

###### Paragraphe VI

Degradation of property belonging to the State or affecting public matters
(Loi n° 74-54 du 4 novembre 1974)

###### Article 225

Without prejudice to the heavier penalties provided for by this code or by special laws, anyone who has voluntarily destroyed or degraded

immovable property belonging to the State or to any other public authority shall be punished by imprisonment of two to five years and a fine which may not exceed one quarter of the restitutions and compensation, nor in any case be less than 100,000 francs.
Anyone who has voluntarily destroyed or degraded pipes or installations of any nature serving the water supply, electrical or telephone installations, monuments and statues or other objects of any nature, intended for public utility or decoration when they have been erected by the public authority or with its authorization, shall be punished by the same penalties.
The penalties provided for in the preceding paragraphs are doubled when the damages resulting from the damage committed exceed 500,000 francs.

###### Paragraphe VII Usurpation of titles or functions

###### Article 226

Anyone who, without title, has interfered in public, civil or military functions, or has performed an act of one of these functions, shall be punished by imprisonment of two to five years, without prejudice to the penalty for forgery if the act bears the character of that offense.

###### Article 227

Any person who has publicly worn a costume, a uniform or a decoration that did not belong to them, shall be punished by imprisonment of six months to two years and a fine of 20,000 to 100,000
francs (Loi n° 77-33 du 22
février 1977).
Anyone who, without fulfilling the required conditions, has

made use of or claimed a legally regulated profession, either an official diploma, or a qualification whose conditions of attribution have been fixed by the authority, or a qualification whose attribution has been established by an act of the public authority, shall be punished by the same penalties.
Anyone who, without right and with a view to attributing an honorary distinction to themselves, has publicly taken a title, changed, altered or modified the name assigned to them by civil status records, shall be punished by a fine of 20,000 to 100,000 francs.
The Court shall order the mention of the judgment in the margin of authentic deeds or civil status records in which the title has been unduly taken and the name altered.
In all cases provided for by this article, the Court may order the full or partial insertion of the judgment in the newspapers it designates.
All at the expense of the convicted person.

###### Article 228

Anyone who has publicly worn a costume presenting a resemblance of a nature to cause confusion in the mind of the public with the uniform of a State body as defined by a regulatory text, shall be punished by a fine of 25,000 to 150,000 francs and may be punished by imprisonment of one month to one year.

###### Article 229

Without prejudice to the application of heavier penalties if appropriate, any person who, in a public or authentic act or in an administrative document intended for the public authority, and except in cases where the regulations in force authorize them to sign these acts or documents under a borrowed civil status, has not taken the patronymic name that is legally theirs, shall be punished by a fine of 20,000 to

50,000 francs.
The Court may order that its decision be published in full or in extracts in the newspapers it designates, and posted in the places it indicates, all at the expense of the convicted person.

###### Paragraphe VIII Obstruction of the free exercise

of worship Article 230
Any private individual who, by acts of violence or threats, has compelled or prevented one or more persons from exercising one of the authorized forms of worship, from attending the exercise of that worship, from celebrating certain festivals, from observing certain days of rest, and, consequently, from opening or closing workshops, shops or stores, and from doing or leaving certain work, shall be punished, for that fact alone, by a fine of 20,000 to 50,000 francs and imprisonment of one month to three months.

###### Article 231

Those who have prevented, delayed or interrupted the exercises of a form of worship by disturbances or disorders caused in the temple or other place intended for or currently serving these exercises, shall be punished by a fine of 20,000 to 75,000 francs, and

imprisonment of two to six months.

###### Article 232

Any person who has, in any manner whatsoever, desecrated:
1)	Places intended for or currently used for the practice of a religion;
2)	Objects of worship, in the places indicated above, shall be punished by a fine of 20,000 to 100,000 francs and imprisonment of three months to one year.

###### Article 233

Whoever has insulted a minister of religion in the exercise of his functions shall be punished by a fine of
20,000 to 50,000 francs and imprisonment of six months to two years.
Whoever has struck a minister of religion in the exercise of his functions shall be punished by imprisonment of two years to five years.

###### Article 233 bis

Whoever has, by one of the means provided for in Article 248, provoked or attempted to provoke acts of intolerance between persons of different religions or religious sects, shall be punished by a fine of
50,000 to 500,000 francs and
imprisonment of six months to two years.

###### Article 234

Shall be punished by imprisonment of one to five years and a fine of 20,000 to
100,000 francs or by one of these two penalties only, whoever has participated in a commercial transaction

having as its object the purchase or sale of human bones or has engaged in practices of sorcery, magic or charlatanism likely to disturb public order and cause harm to persons or property.

###### Paragraphe IX Irregular use of titles

###### Article 235

Shall be punished by imprisonment of one to six months and a fine of
200,000 to 1,000,000
francs or by one of these two penalties only, the founders, directors or managers of companies or establishments with a commercial, industrial or financial purpose, who have caused or allowed the name of a member of the Government or a parliamentarian or a member of the Economic and Social Council, with mention of his capacity, to appear in any publicity made in the interest of the enterprise which they manage or which they propose to found.
In case of recidivism, the penalties provided for above may be increased to one year of imprisonment and
2,000,000 francs fine.

###### Article 236

Shall be punished by the penalties provided for in the preceding article the founders, directors or managers of companies or establishments with a commercial, industrial or financial purpose who have caused or allowed the name of a former member of the
Government, an

The same penalties shall be applicable to all bankers or canvassers who have made use of the publicity provided for above.

###### Article 237

Shall be punished by a fine of 50,000 to 500,000 francs, persons exercising the profession of business agent or legal counsel who have caused or allowed their capacity as honorary magistrate, former magistrate, honorary lawyer, former lawyer, honorary public or ministerial officer, former honorary public or ministerial officer, former public or ministerial officer, former approved representative, to appear on all prospectuses, announcements, leaflets, advertisements, plaques, letterheads, mandates and in general on all documents or writings whatsoever used in the context of their activity.
It is prohibited under the same conditions and under the same penalties to claim professional diplomas allowing access to the functions of lawyer, public or ministerial officer.
In case of recidivism, the penalty provided for above may be doubled.

##### SECTION V CRIMINAL ASSOCIATION, VAGRANCY AND BEGGING

###### Paragraphe premier Criminal association

###### Article 238

Any association formed, whatever its duration or the number of its members, any agreement established for the purpose of preparing or committing one or more crimes against persons or property, constitute a crime against public peace.

###### Article 239

Shall be punished by the penalty of hard labour for a term of ten to twenty years whoever has joined an association formed or has participated in an agreement established for the purpose specified in the preceding article.
Persons who have made themselves guilty of the crime mentioned in this article shall be exempt from penalty if, before any prosecution, they have revealed to the constituted authorities the agreement established or made known the existence of the association.

###### Article 240

Shall be punished by hard labour for a term of ten to twenty years whoever has knowingly and voluntarily favoured the perpetrators of the crimes provided for in Article 238 by providing them with instruments of crime, means of correspondence, lodging or meeting place.
The guilty party may in addition be subject to prohibition of residence

for a period of five to ten years.
However, the provisions contained in paragraph 2 of Article 239 shall be applicable to the guilty party of the acts provided for by this article.

###### Paragraphe Il Vagrancy

###### Article 241

Vagrancy is an offence.

###### Article 242

Vagrants or persons of no fixed abode are those who have neither a fixed domicile, nor means of subsistence, and who do not habitually exercise any trade or profession.

###### Article 243

Vagrants or persons of no fixed abode who have been legally declared as such shall, for this fact alone, be punished by one month to three months of imprisonment.

###### Article 244

Individuals declared vagrants by judgment may, if they are foreigners, be conducted, by order of the Government, outside the territory of the Republic.
If they are claimed by their Government, this measure may be taken even before the expiration of their sentence.

###### Paragraphe III Begging

###### Article 245

Begging is prohibited. The act of soliciting alms on days, in places and under conditions sanctioned by religious traditions

does not constitute an act of begging.
(Law No. 75-77 of 9 July 1975)
Any act of begging is punishable by imprisonment of three months to six months.
Shall be punished by the same penalty those who allow minors under twenty-one years subject to their authority to beg.
All beggars who have used threats or have entered, without permission of the occupant or persons of his household, either into a dwelling, or into an enclosure or dependency;
Or who feign wounds or infirmities;
Or who beg in a group, unless they are husband and wife, father or mother and their young children, the blind person and his guide, shall be punished by imprisonment of six months to two years.
Provisions common to vagrants and beggars

###### Article 246

Any beggar or vagrant who has been seized disguised in any manner whatsoever,
Or carrying weapons, although he has not used them or threatened,
Or provided with files, hooks or other instruments suitable either to commit thefts or other offences, or to provide him with the means to enter houses, shall be punished by two to five years of imprisonment.

###### Article 247

Any beggar or vagrant who has exercised or attempted to exercise any act of violence whatsoever against persons shall be punished by imprisonment of two to five years, without prejudice to heavier penalties, if applicable, by reason of the type and circumstances of the violence.
If the beggar or vagrant who has exercised or attempted to exercise violence is found, in addition, in one of the circumstances expressed by Article 246, he shall be punished by imprisonment of five to ten years.

###### Article 247 bis

(Law No. 67-12 of 29 November 1967)
Any beggar or vagrant shall be mandatorily sentenced to prohibition of residence under the conditions and subject to the reservations set out in Article 36.
In case of recidivism, the application of the provisions of Article 433 of the Penal Code may not result in either the substitution of a fine for imprisonment or the reduction of imprisonment below the minimum penalty incurred under Articles 243, 245, 246 and 247.

##### SECTION VI OFFENCES

COMMITTED BY ANY MEANS OF PUBLIC DISSEMINATION

###### Article 248

The following are considered means of public dissemination: radio broadcasting, television, cinema, the press, posting, display, the

distribution of writings or images of all kinds, speeches, songs, cries or threats uttered in public places or meetings, and generally any technical process intended to reach the public.

###### Paragraphe premier Incitement to crimes and offences

###### Article 249

Those who, by one of the means referred to in Article 248, have directly incited the perpetrator or perpetrators to commit an action classified as a crime or offence shall be punished as accomplices to that action, if the incitement has been followed by effect.
This provision shall also apply when the incitement has been followed only by an attempt at a punishable offence.

###### Article 250

Those who, by one of the means set out in Article
248, have directly incited either to a crime or to an offence, shall be punished, in the event that such incitement has not been followed by effect, by imprisonment of one to five years and a fine of
25,000 to 500,000 francs.

###### Article 251

Any incitement by one of the means set out in Article 248 addressed to military personnel, gendarmes or republican guards, with the aim of diverting them from their military duties and from the obedience they owe to their superiors in everything they command them to do for the execution of military laws and regulations, shall be

punished by imprisonment of one to five years and a fine of 20,000 to 300,000 francs.

###### Article 252

Those who, by one of the means set out in Article 248, have made the apology of a crime or an offence shall be punished by imprisonment of one to three years and a fine of
20,000 to 200,000 francs.

###### Article 253

All religious cries and songs uttered in public places or meetings shall be punished by imprisonment of two months to six months and a fine of 20,000 to 60,000 francs, or by one of these two penalties only.

###### Paragraphe Il Offences against the public

interest Article 254
(Law No. 77-87 of 10 August 1977)
Offence to the President of the Republic by one of the means set out in Article
248 is punished by imprisonment of six months to two years and a fine of 100,000 to 1,500,000
francs or by one of these two penalties only.
The penalties provided for in the preceding paragraph are applicable to offence to the person exercising all or part of the prerogatives of the President of the Republic.

###### Article 255

The publication, dissemination, disclosure or reproduction, by any means whatsoever, of

false news, fabricated, falsified or falsely attributed documents to third parties, shall be punished by imprisonment of one to three years and a fine of
100,000 to 1,500,000 francs when the publication, dissemination, disclosure, reproduction, whether or not made in bad faith, has resulted in disobedience to the laws of the country or harmed the morale of the population, or brought discredit upon public institutions or their functioning.
The same penalties shall also be incurred when such publication, dissemination, disclosure or reproduction have been likely to result in the same consequences.
In all cases, the perpetrators may be subject to prohibition of residence for up to five years.
The attempt at the offence shall be punished as the completed offence; it is constituted in particular by the legal deposit at the office of the public prosecutor of copies of the newspaper or periodical publication containing the false news, fabricated, falsified or falsely attributed documents to third parties.

###### Article 256

Any person who has:
-	Manufactured or possessed with a view to trading, distributing, renting, posting or displaying;
-	Imported or caused to be imported, exported or caused to be exported, transported or caused to be transported knowingly for the same purposes;
-	Posted, displayed or projected to public view;
-	Sold, rented, offered for sale or rental, even non-publicly;
-	Offered, even free of charge, even non-publicly in any form whatsoever, directly or by indirect means;
-	Distributed or delivered with a view to their distribution by any means whatsoever.
All printed matter, all writings, drawings, posters, engravings, paintings, photographs, films or plates, matrices or photographic reproductions, emblems, all objects or images contrary to public morals
shall be punished by imprisonment of one month to two years and a fine of
25,000 to 300,000 francs.
The convicted person may furthermore be subject, for a period not exceeding six months, to a prohibition from exercising, directly or through an intermediary, in law or in fact, management functions of any printing, publishing or grouping and distribution enterprise of newspapers and periodical publications. Any person who contravenes the prohibition referred to above shall be punished by the penalties provided for in this article.

###### Article 257

Any person who has publicly caused to be heard songs, cries or speeches contrary to public morals;
Any person who has publicly drawn attention to

an occasion for debauchery or has published an announcement or correspondence of this kind, whatever the terms, shall be punished by the same penalties.

###### Paragraphe III

Offences against persons Article 258
Any allegation or imputation of a fact which harms the honour or reputation of the person or body to which the fact is imputed is defamation. When it has been committed by one of the means referred to in Article 248, it is punishable even if it is expressed in a dubitative form or if it concerns a person or body not expressly named, but whose identification is made possible by the terms of the speeches, cries, threats, writings or printed matter, placards or posters incriminated.
Any offensive expression, any term of contempt whether or not relating to a person's origin, any invective which does not contain the imputation of any fact is an insult.

###### Article 259

(Law No. 77-87 of 10 August 1977)
Defamation committed by one of the means set out in Article 248 against the Courts and Tribunals, the Army and public administrations shall be punished by imprisonment of four months to two years and a fine of 200,000 to
1,500,000 francs or by one
of these two penalties only.

###### Article 260

Defamation committed by the same means, by reason of their functions or capacity, against one or more members of the Government, one or more members of the National Assembly, a public official, a holder or agent of public authority, a citizen charged with a public service or mandate, temporary or permanent, a juror or a witness, by reason of his testimony, shall be punished by the same penalty.
Defamation against the same persons concerning private life falls under the following article.

###### Article 261

(Law No. 77-87 of 10 August 1977)
Defamation committed against private individuals by one of the means set out in Article 248 shall be punished by imprisonment from three months to two years and a fine from 100,000 to
1,000,000 francs or by one of these two penalties only.
Defamation committed by the same means against a group of persons not designated by the preceding article, but who belong, by their origin, to a particular race or religion, shall be punished by imprisonment from two months to two years and a fine from 50,000 to 500,000 francs, when it has had the purpose of inciting hatred among citizens or inhabitants.

###### Article 262

Insult committed by the same means against the bodies or persons designated by Articles 259 and 260 shall be punished by imprisonment from one to three months and a fine from
20,000 to 100,000 francs, or by one of these two penalties only.
Insult committed in the same manner against private individuals, when it has not been preceded by provocation, shall be punished by imprisonment of a maximum of two months and a fine from 20,000 to 100,000 francs, or by one of these two penalties only. The maximum imprisonment penalty shall be six months and the maximum fine
500,000 francs if the insult was
committed against a group of persons who belong, by their origin, to a particular race or religion, with the purpose of inciting hatred among citizens or inhabitants.

###### Article 263

Articles 260, 261 and 262 shall only be applicable to defamation or insults directed against the memory of the dead in cases where the authors of such defamation or insults intended to harm the honour or reputation of the living heirs, spouses or universal legatees.
Whether or not the authors of defamation or insults intended to harm the honour or reputation of the living heirs, spouses or universal legatees, the latter

may exercise, in both cases, the right of reply.

###### Article 264

Any reproduction of an imputation that has been judged defamatory shall be deemed to have been made in bad faith, unless proven otherwise by its author.

of State and foreign diplomatic agents

###### Article 265

(Law No. 77-87 of 10 August 1977)
Offence committed publicly against foreign Heads of State, foreign Heads of Government and ministers of a foreign government shall be punished by imprisonment from six months to two years and a fine from 100,000 to

of these two penalties only.

###### Article 266

(Law No. 77-87 of 10 August 1977)
Outrage committed publicly in connection with their functions against ambassadors and ministers plenipotentiary, envoys, chargés d'affaires or other diplomatic agents
accredited to the government of the Republic, shall be punished by imprisonment from three months to two years and a fine from 100,000 to
1,000,000 francs or by one of these two penalties only.

###### Paragraphe V Prohibited publications, immunities of the defence

###### Article 267

It is prohibited to publish any criminal or correctional procedural documents before they have been read in public hearing, on pain of a fine from
20,000 to 50,000 francs.

###### Article 268

It is prohibited to report on the proceedings of trials for defamation or insult when they concern the private life of persons, or facts going back more than ten years or subject to amnesty, as well as trials for declaration of paternity, divorce, legal separation, adultery and abortion.
This prohibition does not apply to judgments or rulings, which may be published.
In all civil and commercial matters, the Courts and Tribunals may prohibit the reporting of the trial.
Any infringement of these provisions shall be punished by a fine from 20,000 to 50,000 francs.

###### Article 269

Speeches made within the National Assembly and the Economic and Social Council as well as reports or any other document printed by order of one of these assemblies shall not give rise to any action.
The report of the public sessions of

the Assembly referred to in the above paragraph made in good faith in newspapers shall not give rise to any action.
Neither the faithful report made in good faith of judicial proceedings, nor speeches delivered or writings produced before the Courts shall give rise to any action for defamation, insult or outrage.
Nevertheless, the judges, seized of the case and ruling on the merits, may order the suppression of insulting, outrageous or defamatory speeches, and condemn the appropriate party to damages. The judges may also, in the same case, issue injunctions to lawyers and ministerial officers.
However, defamatory facts unrelated to the case may give rise either to public prosecution or to civil action by the parties, when these actions have been reserved to them by the Courts, and, in all cases, to civil action by third parties.

###### Paragraphe VI Persons responsible

###### Article 270

The following shall be liable, as principal authors, to the penalties constituting punishment for the offences provided for in this section:
1) Directors of publications, co-directors, producers, publishers or managers whatever their designation;
2) In their absence, the authors;
3) In the absence of the authors, the directors of printing, recording, reproduction, or distribution enterprises, of whatever nature;
4) In the absence of these, the sellers, bill-posters and distributors, whatever their designation.
Importers, exporters or forwarding agents who have knowingly participated in the said offences may be prosecuted directly as principal authors.

###### Article 271

When the directors of publications, co-directors, producers, publishers, or managers are involved, the authors shall be prosecuted as accomplices.
Article 46 may not be applied to directors of enterprises prosecuted for acts of printing, reproduction or distribution except in the cases and conditions provided for by Article 95 or in the absence of a co-director of the publication when the appointment of the latter is mandatory. However, they may be prosecuted as accomplices if the criminal irresponsibility of the director or co-director of the publication was pronounced by the Courts.

###### Article 272

The owners of newspapers, periodical writings and all distribution enterprises whatever their designation, are responsible for pecuniary convictions pronounced in favour of third parties against the authors, co-authors and accomplices of the offences provided for in this section.
In all cases, the recovery of fines

and damages may be pursued against the assets of the enterprise.

###### Article 273

The civil action resulting from the offences of defamation provided for and punished by Articles 259 and
260 may not, except in the case of death of the author of the incriminated act or amnesty, be pursued separately from the public prosecution.

###### Article 274

The penalties enacted above may be pronounced even when the various acts constituting the elements of the offences were accomplished in different countries.

###### Article 275

The convicted person may, in addition, be subject to a prohibition from exercising, directly or through an intermediary, in law or in fact, management functions of any printing, recording, reproduction, publishing, grouping, distribution, publication or broadcasting enterprise, of any nature whatsoever; however the Court may reduce this prohibition to a duration which shall not be less than six months. Whoever contravenes the prohibition referred to above shall be punished by the penalties provided for in Article 256.

###### Article 276

Where the person responsible for the offence is a legal person, proceedings shall be brought against the chairman and chief executive officer or the director, or the managing director, or the manager.

TITLE TWO CRIMES AND OFFENCES AGAINST PRIVATE INDIVIDUALS

#### CHAPTER ONE CRIMES AND OFFENCES AGAINST PERSONS

##### SECTION ONE ATTACKS AND THREATS OF ATTACK AGAINST PERSONS

###### Paragraphe premier Murder, assassination, parricide, infanticide, poisoning

###### Article 280

Homicide committed intentionally is classified as murder.

###### Article 281

Any murder committed with premeditation or lying in wait is classified as assassination.

###### Article 282

Premeditation consists in the design formed, before the action, to make an attempt on the person of a determined individual, or even of one who will be found or encountered, even if that design is dependent on some circumstance or some condition.

###### Article 283

Lying in wait consists in waiting for a longer or shorter time in one or various places, for an individual, either to put him to death, or to exercise acts of violence upon him.

###### Article 284

The murder of legitimate, natural or adoptive fathers or mothers, or of any other ascendant, is classified as parricide.

###### Article 285

Infanticide is the murder or assassination of a newborn child.

###### Article 286

Any attempt on the life of a person by the effect of substances which may cause death more or less promptly, in whatever manner those substances have been used or administered and whatever the consequences, is classified as poisoning.

###### Article 287

Any person guilty of assassination, parricide or poisoning shall be punished by death.

###### Article 288

(Law No. 76-02 of 25 March 1976)
The benefit of mitigating circumstances may not be granted to accused persons found guilty, where torture or acts of barbarity have resulted in the death of the victim.
Where torture or acts of barbarity have not resulted in the death of the victim and the benefit of mitigating circumstances has been granted to accused persons found guilty, the penalty of hard labour for life shall be obligatorily pronounced, notwithstanding the provisions of Article 432, paragraph 2.

###### Article 289

Murder shall carry the death penalty, where it has preceded, accompanied or followed another crime.

Murder shall also carry the death penalty, where its purpose was either to prepare, facilitate or execute an offence, or to favour the flight or ensure the impunity of the perpetrators or accomplices of that offence.
In any other case, the person guilty of murder shall be punished by hard labour for life.
In all cases provided for in this paragraph, the confiscation of weapons, objects and instruments used to commit the crime shall be ordered.

###### Paragraphe Il Threats

###### Article 290

Whoever has threatened, by anonymous or signed writing, image, symbol or emblem, with assassination, poisoning or any other attack against persons, which would be punishable by a criminal penalty, shall in the case where the threat was made with an order to deposit a sum of money in an indicated place or to fulfil any other condition, be punished by imprisonment of two years to five years and by a fine of 25,000 to 200,000 francs.
The guilty party may, in addition, be deprived of the rights mentioned in Article 34 of this Penal Code for five years at least and ten years at most from the day on which he has served his sentence.

###### Article 291

If that threat was not accompanied by any order or condition, the penalty shall be imprisonment of one year at least and three

years at most and a fine of 20,000 to 100,000 francs.

###### Article 292

If the threat made with an order or under a condition was verbal, the guilty party shall be punished by imprisonment of six months to two years and by a fine of 20,000 to 75,000 francs.
In this case, as in that of the preceding articles, the penalty of banishment from residence may be pronounced against the guilty party.

###### Article 293

Whoever has, by one of the means provided for in the preceding articles, threatened with assault or violence not provided for by Article 290, shall be punished by imprisonment of six days to three months and by a fine of 20,000 to 50,000 francs or by only one of these two penalties.

##### SECTION Il VOLUNTARY WOUNDING AND BLOWS NOT

CLASSIFIED AS MURDER, AND OTHER VOLUNTARY CRIMES AND OFFENCES.

###### Article 294

Any individual who voluntarily inflicts injuries or strikes blows or commits any other violence or assault, if such violence results in illness or total incapacity for personal work for more than twenty days, shall be punished by imprisonment of one to five years and a fine of 20,000 to 250,000 francs; the offender may in addition be deprived of the rights mentioned in Article 34 for at least five years and at most ten years.

When the violence specified above has been committed against a female person or a particularly vulnerable person by reason of pregnancy, advanced age or state of health resulting in physical deficiency, the offender shall be punished by imprisonment of one year and a fine of 30,000 to 150,000 francs. Suspension of execution of the sentence may be pronounced (Law No. 99-05 of 29 January 1999).

###### Article 295

When there has been premeditation or ambush, the penalty shall be, if death has ensued or if the violence has resulted in mutilation, amputation, or deprivation of the use of a limb, blindness, loss of an eye or other permanent infirmities, that of hard labour for a fixed term of ten to twenty years; in the case provided for in the first paragraph of Article 294, the penalty of imprisonment shall be five to ten years.

###### Article 295-1

(Law No. 96-15 of 28 August 1996)
Torture consists of injuries, blows, physical or mental violence or other assaults voluntarily exercised by a public official or any other person acting in an official capacity or at his instigation or with his express or tacit consent, either for the purpose of obtaining information or confessions, inflicting reprisals, or carrying out acts of intimidation, or for any discriminatory purpose.

The attempt is punished as the completed offence.
The persons referred to in the first paragraph guilty of torture or attempt shall be punished by imprisonment of five to ten years and a fine of 100,000 to 500,000 F
No exceptional circumstances whatsoever, whether a state of war or threat of war, internal political instability or any state of emergency, may be invoked to justify torture.
The order of a superior or a public authority may not be invoked to justify torture.

###### Article 296

When the injuries or blows or other violence or assault have not caused illness or incapacity for personal work mentioned in Article 294, the offender shall be punished by imprisonment of one month to two years and a fine of 20,000 to 100,000 francs or by one of these two penalties only.
If there has been premeditation or ambush, the imprisonment shall be two to five years and the fine 50,000 to 200,000 francs.

###### Article 297

Anyone who voluntarily inflicts injuries or strikes blows against his legitimate, natural or adoptive father or mother or other ascendants shall be punished by the maximum penalty provided for in the preceding articles.

###### Article 297 bis

(Law No. 99-05 of 29 January 1999)
Anyone who voluntarily inflicts injuries or strikes blows or commits any other violence or assault against his spouse shall be punished by a penalty of imprisonment of one to five years and a fine of 50,000 to 500,000 francs if such violence results in illness or incapacity for work of more than twenty days.
When the injuries or blows or other violence or assault have not caused illness or total incapacity for work of a duration equal to that mentioned in the preceding paragraph, the offender shall be liable to the penalties provided for in paragraph 2 of Article 294.
In the cases referred to in the first two paragraphs of this article, suspension of execution of the penalties shall not be pronounced.
If the different types of violence have resulted in mutilation, amputation or deprivation of the use of a limb, blindness or loss of an eye or other permanent infirmities, the penalty shall be that of hard labour for a fixed term of ten to twenty years.
If the habitually practiced blows or violence have caused death without intention to cause it, the penalty of hard labour for life shall always be pronounced.
If the habitually practiced blows or violence have caused death, the perpetrators shall be punished as guilty of murder.

###### Article 298

Anyone who voluntarily inflicts injuries or strikes blows against a child under the age of fifteen years, or who voluntarily deprives him of food or care to the point of compromising his health or who commits against him any other violence or assault, excluding minor violence, shall be punished by imprisonment of one to five years and a fine of 25,000 to 200,000 francs.
If the different types of violence or deprivation mentioned above have resulted in illness or total incapacity for work of more than twenty days or if there has been premeditation or ambush, the penalty shall be three to seven years' imprisonment and a fine of 50,000 to 250,000 francs.
If the offenders are the father and mother or other ascendants, or any other persons having authority over the child or having his custody, the penalty of imprisonment shall be five to ten years.
In the cases provided for by this article, the offender may, in addition, be deprived of the rights mentioned in Article 34 for at least five years and at most ten years from the day he has served his sentence.

###### Article 299

If the violence or deprivation provided for in the preceding article has been followed by mutilation, amputation or deprivation of the use of a limb, blindness, loss of an eye or other permanent infirmities, or if they have caused death without intention to cause it, the

penalty shall be that of hard labour for a fixed term of ten to twenty years.
If the offenders are the father and mother or other ascendants, or any other persons having authority over the child or having his custody, the penalty shall be that of hard labour for life.
If the violence or deprivation has been practiced with the intention of causing death, the perpetrators shall be punished as guilty of murder or attempt of this crime.
If the habitually practiced violence or deprivation has caused death even without intention to cause it, the penalty of hard labour for life shall always be pronounced.

###### Article 299 bis

(law no. 99 - 05 of
29 January 1999)
Anyone who has harmed or attempted to harm the integrity of the genital organ of a female person by total or partial ablation of one or more of its elements, by infibulation, by desensitization or by any other means shall be punished by imprisonment of six months to five years.
The maximum penalty shall be applied when such sexual mutilations have been performed or facilitated by a person belonging to the medical or paramedical profession.
When they have caused death, the penalty of hard labour for life shall always be pronounced.
Any person who, by gifts, promises, influence, threats, intimidation, abuse of authority or power, has provoked such sexual mutilations or given instructions to commit them shall be punished by the same penalties.

###### Article 300

Any person who, in the context of the consummation of a marriage celebrated according to custom, shall have performed or attempted to perform the sexual act upon the person of a child under 13 years of age, shall be punished by imprisonment of two to five years.
If serious injuries, an infirmity, even temporary, have resulted for the child, or if the intercourse has caused the death of the child or if it was accompanied by violence, the offender shall be punished by imprisonment of five to ten years.
In the case provided for in the first paragraph of this article, the offender may, in addition, be deprived of the rights mentioned in article 34 for at least five years and at most ten years from the day he shall have served his sentence.

###### Article 301

The crimes and offences provided for in this section and in the preceding section, if they are committed in a seditious assembly, with rebellion or pillage, are imputable to the leaders, authors, instigators and provokers of these assemblies, rebellions or pillages who shall be punished as guilty of these crimes or these offences and sentenced to the same penalties as those who shall have personally committed them.

###### Article 302

Any individual who shall have manufactured or sold any weapons, of whatever kind, prohibited by laws and regulations, shall be punished by imprisonment of three months to one year and a fine of 120,000 to
240,000 francs.
Any person who shall be found carrying said weapons shall be punished by imprisonment of one to six months and a fine of 20,000 to 50,000 francs.
In both cases, the weapons shall be confiscated. All without prejudice to heavier penalties, if applicable, in case of complicity in a crime or offence.

###### Article 303

In addition to the correctional penalties mentioned in the preceding articles, the Courts may pronounce a residence ban.

###### Article 304

Any person guilty of the crime of castration shall undergo the penalty of hard labour for a term of ten to twenty years.
If death has resulted, the offender shall be punished by death.

###### Article 305

Any person who, by food, beverages, medicines, manoeuvres, violence, or by any other means, shall have procured or attempted to procure the abortion of a pregnant woman, whether she consented or not, shall be punished by imprisonment of one to five years and a fine of
20,000 to 100,000 francs.

The imprisonment shall be five to ten years and the fine
50,000 to 500,000 francs if it is established that the offender has habitually engaged in the acts referred to in the preceding paragraph.
The woman who shall have procured an abortion for herself or attempted to procure it for herself, or who shall have consented to make use of the means indicated or administered to her for that purpose, shall be punished by imprisonment of six months to two years and a fine of
20,000 to 100,000 francs.
Doctors, pharmacists and any person exercising a medical or paramedical profession as well as medical students, pharmacy students or employees, herbalists, bandage makers, dealers in surgical instruments, who shall have indicated, facilitated or practised the means of procuring abortion, shall be sentenced to the penalties provided for in the first and second paragraphs of this article.
Suspension for at least five years or absolute incapacity to exercise their profession shall, in addition, be pronounced against the offenders.
Any person who contravenes the prohibition to exercise his profession pronounced pursuant to the preceding paragraph shall be punished by imprisonment of at least six months and at most two years and a fine of
100,000 to 500,000 francs or
by one of these two penalties only.
Suspension of execution of the sentence may not be granted when the offender

is one of the persons listed in paragraph 4.

###### Article 305 bis

(Law No. 80-49 of 24 December 1980)
Any person who:
-	either by speeches uttered in public places or meetings;
-	either by the sale, offering for sale or offer, even non-public, or by the display, posting or distribution on the public highway or in public places, or by distribution to homes, delivery under wrapper or under closed or unclosed envelope, to the post office or to any distribution or transfer agent, of books, writings, printed matter, announcements, posters, drawings, images and emblems;
-	either by the advertising of medical or supposedly medical offices, shall have incited to the offence of abortion, even if this incitement shall not have been followed by effect,
shall be punished by imprisonment of six months to three years and a fine of
50,000 francs to 1,000,000 francs, or by one of these two penalties only.
Any person who shall have sold, offered for sale or caused to be sold, distributed or caused to be distributed, in any manner whatsoever, remedies, substances, instruments or any objects, knowing that they were intended to commit the offence of abortion, even if this abortion had been neither consummated nor attempted, and even if these remedies, substances, instruments or any objects proposed as effective means

of abortion were in reality unfit to achieve it, shall be punished by the same penalties.

###### Article 306

Any person who shall have caused to another a disease or incapacity for personal work, by voluntarily administering to him, in any manner whatsoever, substances which, without being of a nature to cause death, are harmful to health, shall be punished by imprisonment of one month to five years, and a fine of 20,000 to 100,000 francs; he may in addition be subject to a residence ban.
If the disease or incapacity for personal work has lasted more than twenty days, the penalty shall be five to ten years' imprisonment.

##### SECTION III INVOLUNTARY HOMICIDE AND INJURIES

###### ARTICLE 307

Any person who, through clumsiness, imprudence, inattention, negligence or non-observance of regulations, shall have involuntarily committed a homicide or injuries, or shall have involuntarily been the cause thereof, shall be punished by imprisonment of six months to five years and a fine of
20,000 to 300,000 francs.
When there has been a hit-and-run offence, the penalties provided for in this article shall be doubled and may not be accompanied by the benefit of suspension of sentence.

###### Article 308

If an involuntarily caused fire results in the death or causes injuries to one or more persons, the penalties

provided for homicide or injuries through imprudence shall be applied.

##### SECTION IV EXCUSABLE OFFENCES AND CASES WHERE THEY CANNOT BE EXCUSED

###### Article 309

Murder as well as injuries and blows are excusable, if they have been provoked by serious blows or violence against persons.

###### Article 310

The crimes and offences mentioned in the preceding article are equally excusable, if they have been committed in repelling during the scaling or breaking of enclosures, walls or entry of a house or an inhabited apartment or their outbuildings.
If the act occurred during the night, this case is governed by article 317.

###### Article 311

Parricide is never excusable.

###### Article 312

Murder committed by the husband upon the wife, or by the latter upon her husband, is not excusable, if the life of the husband or wife who committed the murder was not endangered at the very moment when the murder took place.
Nevertheless in the case of adultery, provided for by article 330, murder committed by one of the spouses upon the other, as well as upon the accomplice, at the instant when he surprises them in flagrante delicto, is excusable.

###### Article 313

The crime of castration, if it has been immediately provoked by a violent outrage to decency, shall be considered as excusable murder or injuries.

###### Article 314

When the mitigating circumstance is proven:
If it concerns a crime carrying the death penalty or that of hard labor for life, the penalty shall be reduced to imprisonment of two to ten years;
If it concerns any other crime, it shall be reduced to imprisonment of two to five years.
In these first two cases, the guilty parties may furthermore be prohibited from residing in certain areas by the judgment or ruling for a period of five to ten years.
If it concerns an offense, the penalty shall be reduced to imprisonment of one month to one year.

###### Article 315

There is neither crime nor offense when the homicide, injuries and blows were ordered by law and commanded by legitimate authority.

###### Article 316

There is neither crime nor offense when the homicide, injuries and blows were commanded by the actual necessity of legitimate defense of oneself or others.

###### Article 317

Included in cases of actual necessity of defense are the following two cases:
1° If the homicide was committed, if the injuries were inflicted, or if the blows were struck in repelling, during the night, the scaling or breaking of enclosures, walls or entry of a house or an inhabited apartment or their outbuildings;
2° If the act took place in defending oneself against the perpetrators of thefts or pillaging carried out with violence.

Any person who commits a public outrage against decency shall be punished by imprisonment of three months to two years and a fine of 20,000 to 200,000 francs.

###### Article 319

Any indecent assault consummated or attempted without violence on the person of a child of either sex under the age of thirteen years shall be punished by imprisonment of two to five years.

If the act was committed with a minor under

###### Article 319 bis

(Law No. 99-05 of January 29, 1999)
The act of harassing another person by using orders, gestures, threats, words, writings or coercion with the aim of obtaining favors of a sexual nature, by a person abusing the authority conferred by their functions, shall be punished by imprisonment of six months to three years and a fine of 50,000 to 500,000 francs.
When the victim of the offense is under 16 years of age, the maximum imprisonment penalty shall be imposed.

If it has resulted in death, the perpetrators shall be punished as guilty of murder.
If the offense was committed on a child under 13 years of age or a particularly vulnerable person by reason of their state of pregnancy, advanced age or state of health having resulted in physical or psychological impairment, the guilty party shall receive the maximum penalty.

2) In any form whatsoever, shares the proceeds of the prostitution of another or receives subsidies from a person habitually engaging in prostitution;
3) Knowingly lives with a person habitually engaging in prostitution;
4) Being in habitual relations with one or more persons engaging in prostitution, cannot justify resources corresponding to their lifestyle;
5) Recruits, induces or maintains, even with their consent, a person even if of age for the purpose of prostitution or delivers them to prostitution or debauchery;

7) By threat, pressure, maneuver or by any other means, obstructs the action of prevention, control, assistance or rehabilitation undertaken by qualified organizations in favor of persons engaging in prostitution or in danger of prostitution.

###### Article 324

2) The offence was accompanied by threat, coercion, violence, assault, abuse of authority or fraud;
3) The perpetrator of the offence was carrying a visible or concealed weapon;
4) The perpetrator of the offence is the spouse, father, mother or guardian of the victim or belongs to one of the categories listed in Article 321;
5) The perpetrator of the offence is called upon to participate, by virtue of his functions, in the fight against prostitution, the protection of health or the maintenance of public order;
6) The offence was committed against several persons;
7) The victims of the offence were delivered or incited to engage in prostitution outside the national territory;

8) The victims of the offence were delivered or incited to engage in prostitution upon their arrival or within a short time of their arrival on the national territory;
9) The offence was committed by several perpetrators, co-perpetrators or accomplices;
Whoever shall have committed an offence against morals by habitually inciting, favouring or facilitating the debauchery or corruption of youth of either sex under the age of twenty-one years, or, even occasionally, of minors of sixteen years, shall be punished with the penalties provided for in this article.

Any individual shall be punished with the penalties provided for in the preceding article:

3) Who assists the individuals referred to in 1 0 and 20;
In case of a new offence within ten years, the penalties incurred shall be doubled.

###### Article 326

In all cases where the incriminated acts occurred in an establishment referred to in 1 0 and 20 of Article 325 and of which the holder, manager or agent is convicted by application of Articles 324 or 325, the judgment shall order withdrawal of the licence of which the convicted person is a beneficiary and shall further order the closure of the establishment or of the parts of the establishment used for the purpose of prostitution for a period which may not be less than three months nor more than five years.

The attempt of the offences referred to in this section shall be punished with the penalties provided for these offences.

###### Article 328

Notwithstanding the particular provisions regulating the trade, possession and use of poisonous substances, those who, without authorization, have cultivated, possessed, transported, distributed, offered for sale all products classified as narcotics and in particular Indian hemp known as "yamba" shall be punished with imprisonment from three months to five years and a fine from 180,000 to 1,800,000 francs.

###### Article 331

The accomplice to adultery shall be liable to the same penalty as the guilty party.
The only evidence that may be admitted against the accused of complicity shall be, in addition to being caught in the act, that resulting from confession or from letters or other documents written by the accused.

##### SECTION VI ILLEGAL ARRESTS AND SEQUESTRATIONS

###### Article 334

Those who, without order from the constituted authorities and outside the cases where the law orders the seizure of accused persons, have arrested, detained or sequestered any persons whatsoever shall be punished with the penalty of hard labour for a fixed term of ten to twenty years.
Whoever has provided a place to carry out the detention or sequestration shall suffer the same penalty.
Those who have concluded an agreement having as its object the alienation either gratuitously or for consideration of the freedom of a third person shall also be punished with the same penalty. The confiscation of the money, objects or valuables received in execution of the said agreement shall always be ordered. The maximum penalty shall always be imposed if the person who is the subject of the agreement is under fifteen years of age.
Whoever has placed or received a person as a pledge, whatever the reason, shall be punished with imprisonment from one month to two years and a fine from 2,000 to 150,000 francs or with one of these penalties only. The penalty

of imprisonment may be increased to five years if the person placed or received as a pledge is under fifteen years of age.
The guilty parties may further in all cases be deprived of the rights mentioned in Article 34 for at least five years and at most ten years.

###### Article 335

If the detention or sequestration has lasted more than one month, the penalty shall be that of hard labour for life.

###### Article 336

The penalty shall be reduced to imprisonment of one to five years, if the perpetrators of the offenses mentioned in Article 334, not yet actually prosecuted, have restored freedom to the person arrested, sequestered or detained, before the tenth day completed since that of the arrest, detention or sequestration.
The perpetrators may nevertheless be prohibited from residence for five to ten years.

###### Article 337

In each of the following two cases:
1) If the arrest was carried out with a false costume, under a false name, or on a false order of the public authority;
2) If the individual arrested, detained or sequestered, was threatened with death.
The perpetrators shall be punished by hard labor for life.
But the penalty shall be death, if the persons arrested, detained or

sequestered have been subjected to bodily torture.

###### Article 337 bis

(Law No. 76-02 of March 25, 1976)
In the case where the person, whatever their age, has been arrested, detained or sequestered as a hostage, either to prepare or facilitate the commission of a crime or an offense, or to favor the flight or ensure the impunity of the perpetrators or accomplices of a crime or an offense, or to answer for the payment of a ransom, the execution of an order or a condition, the perpetrator shall be punished by the death penalty.
However, the penalty shall be that of hard labor for a term of ten to twenty years, if the person arrested, detained or sequestered as a hostage is released voluntarily, without any order having been executed or any condition realized, before the fifth day completed since that of the arrest, detention or sequestration.
The benefit of mitigating circumstances may not be granted to the accused found guilty of the crime specified in the first paragraph when the taking of a hostage has resulted in the death of any person or that of the person taken hostage, whether the death occurred while that person was in the hands of their captors or as a result of injuries or violence suffered during their abduction.
When the taking of a hostage has not resulted in the death of any person and the benefit of mitigating circumstances has been granted

to the accused found guilty of the crime specified in paragraph 1, the penalty of hard labor for life shall be mandatorily pronounced, notwithstanding the provisions of Article 432, paragraph 2.

##### SECTION VII

OFFENSES RELATING TO THE CIVIL STATUS OF A CHILD, ABDUCTION OF MINORS, ABANDONMENT OF FAMILY, OFFENSES AGAINST LAWS ON BURIALS

###### Paragraphe premier Crimes and offenses against the child

###### Article 338

Those guilty of abduction, concealment, or suppression of a child, substitution of one child for another, or imputation of a child to a woman who has not given birth, shall be punished by imprisonment of five to ten years.
Those who, being in charge of a child, do not present them to the persons who have the right to claim them, shall be punished by the same penalty.

###### Article 339

Any person who, having assisted at a childbirth, has not made the declaration prescribed to them by the civil status regulations, shall be punished by imprisonment of one month to six months and a fine of
20,000 to 75,000 francs.

###### Article 340

Any person who, having found a newborn child, has not delivered them to the civil status officer, shall be punished by the

penalties provided in the preceding article.
This provision does not apply to one who has consented to take charge of the child and who has made their declaration in this regard before the administrative authority of the place where the child was found.

###### Article 341

Those who have exposed or caused to be exposed, abandoned or caused to be abandoned, in a solitary place, a child or an incapacitated person, unable to protect themselves by reason of their physical or mental state, shall, for this fact alone, be sentenced to imprisonment of one to three years and a fine of
20,000 to 200,000 francs.

###### Article 342

The penalty provided in the preceding article shall be two to five years and the fine 20,000 to 400,000 francs, against ascendants or any other persons having authority over the child or the incapacitated person, or having custody of them.

###### Article 343

If the exposure or abandonment has resulted in an illness or total incapacity of more than twenty days, the maximum penalty shall be applied.
If the child or incapacitated person has remained mutilated or crippled or if they have remained afflicted with a permanent infirmity, the perpetrators shall undergo the penalty of imprisonment of five to ten years.
If the perpetrators are the persons mentioned in Article 342, the penalty shall be ten years' imprisonment.

When the exposure or abandonment in a solitary place has caused death, the action shall be considered as murder.

###### Article 344

Those who have exposed or caused to be exposed, abandoned or caused to be abandoned, in a non-solitary place, a child or an incapacitated person unable to protect themselves by reason of their physical or mental state, shall, for this fact alone, be sentenced to imprisonment of three months to one year and a fine of 20,000 to 200,000 francs.
If the perpetrators are the persons mentioned in Article 342, the penalties shall be doubled.

###### Article 345

If the exposure or abandonment has resulted in an illness or total incapacity of more than twenty days, or one of the infirmities provided for by Article
294 paragraph 2, the perpetrators shall undergo imprisonment of one to five years and a fine of 20,000 to 200,000 francs.
If death has been caused without intention to cause it, the penalty shall be that of criminal detention of five to ten years.
If the perpetrators are the persons mentioned in Article 342, the penalty shall be, in the first case, that of imprisonment of five to ten years, and in the second, that of hard labor for a term of ten to twenty years.

###### Paragraphe Il Abduction of minors

###### Article 346

Whoever has, by fraud or violence, abducted or caused to be abducted minors, or has enticed, diverted, or displaced them or has caused them to be enticed, diverted or displaced, from the places where they were placed by those to whose authority or direction they were subject or entrusted, shall undergo the penalty of hard labor for a term of five to ten years.

###### Article 347

(Law No. 76-02 of March 25, 1976)
If the minor thus abducted or diverted is under fifteen years of age, the penalty shall be that of hard labor for life.
However, the penalty shall be that of hard labor of five to ten
years if the minor is found alive before the judgment of conviction has been rendered.
The abduction shall carry the death penalty if it has been followed by the death of the minor.

###### Article 348

One who, without fraud or violence, has abducted or diverted, or attempted to abduct or divert a minor of eighteen years, shall be punished by imprisonment of two to five years and a fine of
20,000 to 200,000 francs.
When a female minor thus abducted or diverted has married her abductor, the latter may be prosecuted only upon the complaint of the persons who have standing to request the annulment of the marriage and may be convicted

only after that annulment has been pronounced.

###### Article 349

When the custody of a minor has been determined by a court decision, provisional or final, the father, mother or any person who does not present this minor to those who have the right to claim them or who, even without fraud or violence, abducts or diverts them or causes them to be abducted or diverted from the hands of those to whom their custody has been entrusted, or from the places where the latter have placed them, shall be punished by imprisonment of two months to two years and a fine of 20,000 to 200,000 francs.
If the perpetrator has been declared deprived of parental authority, the imprisonment may be increased up to three years.

###### Paragraphe IlI Family abandonment

###### Article 350

(Law No. 77-33 of 22 February 1977)
Shall be punished by imprisonment of three months to one year and a fine of
20,000 to 250,000 francs:
1) the spouse who abandons without serious reason, for more than two months, the family residence and evades all or part of the moral or material obligations arising from marriage as well as from parental authority; the period of two months may only be interrupted by a return to the home implying the intention to resume family life definitively;

2) the husband who, without serious reason, abandons for more than two months his wife knowing her to be pregnant;
3) the father or mother, whether or not the forfeiture of parental authority has been pronounced against him or her, who seriously compromises by ill-treatment, by pernicious examples of habitual drunkenness or notorious misconduct, by lack of care, or by material abandonment, either the health, or the safety, or the morality of one or more of their children.
With regard to the offences provided for in paragraphs 1 and 2 of this article, the prosecution shall initially involve a summons, recorded by official report, of the person prosecuted, by a judicial police officer or a bailiff. A period of fifteen days shall be granted to him or her to fulfil his or her obligations. If the person prosecuted is in flight or if he or she has no known residence, the summons is replaced by sending a registered letter to the last known domicile, or by notice given to the village chief or neighbourhood delegate of that last domicile.
In the same cases, during the marriage, the prosecution shall only be brought upon complaint of the spouse who remained in the home, who has the power to stop the proceedings or the effect of the conviction.

###### Article 351

Shall be punished by the same penalties any person who, in contempt of an enforceable instrument or a court decision having ordered him or her to pay maintenance to his or her

spouse, to his or her ascendants, to his or her descendants, has voluntarily remained for more than two months without providing the totality of the support determined by the judgment or paying the full amount of the maintenance.
The failure to pay shall be presumed voluntary, unless proven otherwise. Insolvency resulting from habitual misconduct, laziness or drunkenness shall in no case be a valid ground of excuse for the debtor.
Any person convicted of one of the offences provided for in this article and in the preceding article may in addition be deprived, for five years at least and ten years at most, of the rights mentioned in Article 34 of the Penal Code.
The Court competent to hear the offences referred to in this article shall be that of the domicile or residence of the person who is to receive the maintenance or benefit from the support.

###### Paragraphe IV Offences against the laws on

burials Article 352
Those who, without the prior authorization of the civil registrar, in the case where it is prescribed, have had a deceased individual buried, shall be punished by two to six months' imprisonment and a fine of 50,000 to
100,000 francs without prejudice to the prosecution of crimes of which the perpetrators of this offence could be accused in this circumstance.
The same penalty shall be pronounced against those who have contravened in any

manner whatsoever the law and regulations relating to burials.

###### Article 353

Whoever has concealed or hidden the corpse of a person who has been murdered or died as a result of blows or injuries, shall be punished by imprisonment of six months to two years, and a fine of 50,000 to 150,000 francs, without prejudice to more severe penalties if he or she participated in the crime or offence.

###### Article 354

Shall be punished by imprisonment of three months to one year, and a fine of 50,000 to
180,000 francs, whoever has been guilty of violation of a tomb or burial place, without prejudice to the penalties for crimes or offences that may be joined to this one.
The same penalties shall be applicable to whoever has desecrated or mutilated a corpse, even if not buried.

##### SECTION VIII FALSE TESTIMONY, SLANDER, INSULTS,

DISCLOSURE OF SECRETS

###### Paragraphe premier False testimony

###### Article 355

Whoever is guilty of false testimony in criminal matters either against the accused or in his or her favour, shall be punished by the penalty of hard labour for a term of five to ten years.
If however the accused has been sentenced to a more severe penalty than that of hard labour for a term of five to ten years, the false witness

who testified against him or her shall undergo the same penalty.

###### Article 356

Whoever is guilty of false testimony in correctional matters, either against the defendant or in his or her favour, shall be punished by imprisonment of two years at least and five years at most, and a fine of
20,000 to 300,000 francs.
If however the defendant has been sentenced to more than five years' imprisonment, the false witness who testified against him or her shall undergo the same penalty.
Whoever is guilty of false testimony in police matters, either against the defendant or in his or her favour, shall be punished by imprisonment of one year at least and three years at most, and a fine of 20,000 to
100,000 francs.
In these two cases, the guilty parties may, in addition, be deprived of the rights mentioned in Article 34 of the Penal Code for five years at least and ten years at most, from the day they have served their sentence, and be prohibited from residing for the same duration.

###### Article 357

In all other matters, the person guilty of false testimony shall be punished by imprisonment of one year to three years and a fine of
50,000 to 500,000 francs. He or she may also be subject to the accessory penalties mentioned in the preceding article.

###### Article 358

The false witness in criminal matters, who has received money, any reward

whatsoever or promises, shall be punished by hard labour for a term of ten to twenty years.
The false witness, in any other matter, who has received money, any reward whatsoever or promises, shall be punished by imprisonment of one to three years and a fine of
20,000 to 200,000 francs.
He or she may also be subject to the accessory penalties mentioned in Article 356.
In all cases, what the false witness has received shall be confiscated.

###### Article 359

Whoever, either during proceedings and in any state of the case, or in any matter with a view to a claim or defence in court, has used promises, offers or gifts, pressure, threats, acts of violence, manoeuvres or artifices to induce another to make or deliver false testimony, a false statement or a false attestation, shall, whether or not this subornation has produced its effect, be punished by imprisonment of one to three years and a fine of
50,000 to 500,000 francs or
by one of these two penalties only, without prejudice to the more severe penalties provided for in the preceding articles, if he or she is an accomplice to false testimony qualified as a crime or offence.

###### Article 360

He to whom the oath has been deferred or referred in civil matters, and who has taken a false oath, shall be punished by imprisonment of one year at least and five

years at most and a fine of 20,000 to 500,000 francs.
He may in addition be deprived of the rights mentioned in Article 34 of this Code for five years at least and ten years at most and be prohibited from residing for the same number of years from the day he has served his sentence.

###### Article 361

The interpreter who, after having taken the oath, has in bad faith distorted the substance of words or documents orally translated, shall be punished by the penalties for false testimony according to the provisions contained in Articles 355, 356, 357 and
358.
The subornation of an interpreter shall be punished as subornation of a witness according to the provisions of Article 359.

###### Paragraph II Slander, insults, disclosure of secrets

###### Article 362

Whoever shall have, by any means whatsoever, made a slanderous denunciation against one or more individuals, to officers of justice or of administrative or judicial police or to any other authority having the power to act upon it or to refer the matter to the competent authority, or to the hierarchical superiors or to the employers of the person denounced, shall be punished by imprisonment from six months to five years and a fine from 50,000 to 500,000 francs.
The Court may in addition order the insertion of the

judgment, in whole or in part, in one or more newspapers, at the expense of the convicted person.
If the fact denounced is liable to penal or disciplinary sanction, proceedings may be instituted under this article either after judgment or decision of acquittal or discharge, or after order or decision of no case to answer, or after dismissal of the denunciation by the magistrate, officials superior authority or employer competent to give it the follow-up it was liable to entail.
The court seised under this article shall be required to stay proceedings if proceedings concerning the fact denounced are pending.
The law does not distinguish between the case where the discharge is based on the non-existence of the fact denounced and that where it is pronounced for the benefit of the doubt. It nonetheless follows in the latter case that the facts denounced are not established and that the denouncer has not provided the proof of which he assumed the burden by his denunciation. The assessment of the elements of proof on this point is beyond the control of the Supreme Court.

###### Article 363

Doctors, surgeons, as well as pharmacists, midwives and all other persons who are depositaries, by status or by profession or by temporary or permanent functions, of secrets confided to them, who, except in cases where the law obliges or authorises them to act as denouncers, shall have disclosed these secrets, shall be punished by imprisonment

from one to six months and a fine from 50,000 to 300,000 francs.
Professional secrecy can never be invoked against the judge who, for the purposes of the investigations he carries out or orders, may release from it those who are bound by it.
It is also not opposable to judicial police officers and agents of the General Directorate of Taxes and State Property acting in the context of preliminary investigations conducted on written instructions from the Special Prosecutor at the Court for the Repression of Illicit Enrichment, for the search for and establishment of offences provided for in article 163 bis
Paragraphs 2 and 3 above were instituted by law no. 81 53 of 10 July 1981 relating to illicit enrichment.

#### CHAPTER II CRIMES AND OFFENCES

AGAINST PROPERTY

##### SECTION ONE THEFTS

###### Article 364

Whoever has fraudulently taken a thing that does not belong to him is guilty of theft.

###### Article 365

Takings committed by husbands to the prejudice of their wives, by wives to the prejudice of their husbands, by a widower or a widow with regard to things that had belonged to the deceased spouse, may only give rise to civil remedies.

Takings committed:
1)	by children or other descendants to the prejudice of their fathers or mothers or other ascendants, by fathers or mothers or other ascendants to the prejudice of their children or other ascendants;
2)	by relatives by marriage in the same degrees provided that the takings are committed during the marriage and outside a period during which the spouses are authorised to live separately, may only be prosecuted upon complaint by the victim. Withdrawal of the complaint extinguishes the public prosecution.
With regard to all other individuals who shall have concealed or applied to their own benefit all or part of the stolen objects, they shall be punished as guilty of receiving in accordance with articles 430 and 431.

###### Article 366

Individuals guilty of theft committed with two of the following circumstances shall be punished by hard labour for a term of ten to twenty years:
1)	If the theft was committed by two or more persons;
2)	If the guilty party or parties were carrying real or fake weapons;
3)	If threats, violence or assault were used;
4)	If the guilty party or parties secured the use of a vehicle with a view to facilitating their enterprise or favouring their flight.

###### Article 367

(Law no. 76-02 of 25 March 1976)
Even if only violence was used and if such violence resulted in incapacity of more than fifteen days or permanent infirmity, the guilty parties shall be liable to hard labour for life.
If the violence resulted in death, the death penalty shall be pronounced.
If a weapon was used, even if no incapacity or infirmity was caused, the guilty parties shall be liable to hard labour for life.
In the cases provided for in the three preceding paragraphs, the benefit of mitigating circumstances may not be granted to accused persons found guilty.

###### Article 368

(Law no. 76-02 of 25 March 1976)
Any individual guilty of theft or attempted theft committed with one of the circumstances provided for in article 366 or with one of those set out below shall be punished by imprisonment from five years to ten years and a fine from
50,000 to 500,000 francs:
1)	If breaking and entering, climbing, undermining or false keys were used;
2)	If the theft was committed on public roads or in a means of public transport or within the precincts of a station, port or airport;

3)	If the theft was committed in a place intended for or used for the practice of worship;
4)	If the theft was committed by a domestic servant or an employee in the course of his service;
5)	If the theft was committed by an innkeeper, hotelier, carrier, boatman or one of their employees, when they shall have stolen all or part of the things entrusted to them in that capacity;

##### SECTION Il BANKRUPTCY, FRAUD AND OTHER TYPES OF FRAUD

###### Paragraphe premier Bankruptcy and fraud

###### Article 376

Those who shall be declared guilty of bankruptcy shall be punished:
Simple bankrupts by imprisonment of one month to two years.
Fraudulent bankrupts by
imprisonment of five to ten years.

###### Article 377

Accomplices to bankruptcy, simple or fraudulent, incur the penalties provided for in the preceding article, even if they do not have the status of merchant.

###### Article 378

Those who engage in currency exchange operations, brokerage in securities, or banking operations, when they are found guilty of bankruptcy, simple or fraudulent, shall be punished in all cases by the penalties for fraudulent bankruptcy.

###### Article 379

Whoever, either by making use of false names or false qualities, or by employing any fraudulent maneuvers whatsoever, shall have had delivered or issued to him, or shall have attempted to have delivered or issued to him funds, movables or obligations, dispositions, bills, promises, receipts or discharges, and shall have, by one of these means, defrauded or attempted to defraud all or part of another's fortune, shall be punished by imprisonment of at least one year and at most five years, and a fine of 100,000 to 1,000,000 francs.
If the offense was committed by a person having made an appeal to the public with a view to the issuance of shares, bonds, vouchers, parts or any titles whatsoever, either of a company or of a commercial or industrial enterprise, the above penalties may be doubled.
In all cases, the guilty parties may be, in addition, deprived for up to ten years

of the rights mentioned in article 34 of this code; they may also be prohibited from residence for the same number of years.
The penalties provided for in the 1st and 3rd paragraphs of this article shall also apply to whoever shall have, in the case of a marriage to be celebrated according to custom, given or promised in marriage a girl over whom, according to that custom, he could not or could no longer dispose and received or attempted to receive all or part of the dowry and gifts fixed by custom.

###### Article 379 bis

(Law No. 99-05 of 29-1-1991)

###### Paragraphe Il Breach of trust

###### Article 381

Whoever shall have abused the needs, weaknesses or passions of a minor to make him subscribe, to his prejudice, obligations, receipts or discharges, for a loan of money or movable things or commercial instruments or any other obligatory instruments, in whatever form this negotiation may have been made or

disguised shall be punished by imprisonment of at least two months and at most two years and a fine of
20,000 to 500,000 francs.
The fine may, however, be increased to one quarter of the restitutions and damages, if it is greater than the maximum provided for in the preceding paragraph.
The guilty party may furthermore be deprived of the rights mentioned in article 34 for ten years; he may also be prohibited from residence for the same number of years.

###### Article 382

Whoever, abusing a blank signature which shall have been entrusted to him, shall have fraudulently written above it an obligation or discharge, or any other act that may compromise the person or fortune of the signatory, shall be punished by imprisonment of one to five years and a fine of
20,000 to 500,000 francs.
In the case where the blank signature had not been entrusted to him, he shall be prosecuted as a forger and punished as such.

###### Article 383

Anyone who, having received from owners, possessors, or holders, effects, monies, goods, bills, receipts or writings containing or creating an obligation or discharge by way of lease, deposit, mandate, pledge, loan for use, or for paid or unpaid work, shall not, after simple notice, have fulfilled his undertaking to return or produce them or to make a specific use or employment thereof,

shall be punished by imprisonment of at least six months and at most four years and a fine of at least
20,000 francs and at most 3,000,000 francs.
There is no offence when the non-performance of the undertaking is caused by force majeure, the act of the transferor or of a third party or the involuntary fault of the perpetrator. The latter may establish the justifying fact by any means.
If the breach of trust has been committed by a person making an appeal to the public, in order to obtain, either for his own account or as director, administrator or agent of a company or of a commercial or industrial enterprise, the delivery of funds or securities by way of deposit, mandate or pledge, the duration of imprisonment may be increased to ten years and the fine to
12,000,000 francs.
In all cases, the guilty parties may further be deprived for up to ten years of the rights mentioned in Article 34 of this Code; they may also be prohibited from residing in certain areas for the same number of years.

###### Article 384

Anyone who, after having produced, in a judicial dispute, any title, document or memorandum, shall have removed it in any manner whatsoever, shall be punished by a fine of 20,000 to 100,000 francs. This penalty shall be pronounced by the Court hearing the dispute.

###### Paragraphe III Misappropriation of loans granted or guaranteed by the State

###### Article 385

Anyone who, having benefited from an advance, a loan, an endorsement or a guarantee in any form whatsoever, either from the State or from a credit institution, a marketing organization, or a fund having the form of a public establishment or operating under the supervision of the State, shall have used all or part of the sums of money lent or advanced to him for purposes or under conditions other than those provided for in the loan or advance contract, shall be punished by imprisonment of at least one year and at most five years, and a fine of
100,000 to 1,000,000
francs.
The guilty party may, in addition, be deprived for up to ten years of the rights mentioned in Article 34 of this Code as well as prohibited from residing in certain areas for the same number of years.
The beneficiary of one of the operations provided for above who gives to all or part of the goods purchased a destination other than that provided for in the contract shall be punished by the same penalties.
He must at any time, at the request of the creditor institution, justify the use of the sums received or produce them. Failing his ability to do so, he shall be punished by the penalties provided for in paragraph 1 of this article.

###### Article 386

Anyone who shall have obtained or attempted to obtain a loan, an advance, an endorsement or a guarantee from the State or from one of the institutions referred to in the preceding article, either by making a false declaration, or by assuming a false identity or a false capacity, or by providing false information, a false certificate or a false attestation, shall be punished by the penalties provided for in Article 385 paragraphs 1 and 2.
When the beneficiary of the loan, advance, guarantee or endorsement is a legal person, its directors, managers, administrators or agents are criminally liable for the offences referred to in this paragraph.
In the event of a pecuniary conviction, the legal person shall be jointly and severally liable with them for payment of the conviction.

###### Article 387

The provisions of Articles 385 and 386 are applicable to advances, credits, loans, endorsements or guarantees granted by companies, consortia or private marketing organizations approved by the State under the conditions laid down by decree.

###### Paragraphe IV

Contravention of regulations on gaming houses, lotteries and pawnshops

###### Article 388

Those who, without authorization given by

The guilty parties may further be, from the day they shall have served their sentence, deprived for at least five years and at most ten years of the rights mentioned in Article 34 of this Code.
In all cases, all funds or effects that shall be found exposed to gaming or put in the lottery, the furniture, instruments, utensils, apparatus used or intended for the service of games or lotteries, the furniture and movable effects with which the premises shall be furnished or decorated shall be confiscated.

###### Article 389

Those who shall have established or kept pawnshops or pledge houses without legal authorization, or who, having an authorization, shall not have kept a register in conformity with the regulations, containing consecutively, without any blank or interlineation, the sums or objects lent, the names, domicile and profession of the borrowers, the nature, quality, value of the objects placed in pledge, shall be punished by imprisonment of one month to six months and a fine of 20,000 to 250,000 francs.

Any violation of the laws and regulations relating to Senegalese products that shall be exported abroad, and which have the purpose of guaranteeing the good quality, the

###### Article 395

All those:
1) who, by false or slanderous facts knowingly spread among the public, by offers thrown on the market with the intention of disrupting prices, by overbids made at the prices demanded by the sellers themselves, by any fraudulent ways or means;

The Court may, in addition, pronounce against the guilty parties, the penalty of prohibition from residing for at least two years and at most five.

The imprisonment may be increased to five years if it concerns foodstuffs or goods that do not fall within the habitual exercise of the offender's profession.
In the cases provided for in this article, the prohibition from residing, which may be pronounced, shall be at least five years and at most ten years.

###### Article 397

Any edition of writings, musical composition, film, drawing, painting or any other production printed, recorded or engraved in whole or in part, in defiance of the laws and regulations relating to the property of authors, is a counterfeiting; and all counterfeiting is an offense.

The sale, export and import of counterfeit works shall be punished by the same penalties.

###### Article 398

###### Article 399

In case of recidivism, after conviction pronounced pursuant to the preceding paragraph, the temporary or permanent closure of establishments operated by the habitual counterfeiter and his accomplices may be pronounced.
When this closure measure shall have been pronounced, the staff must receive compensation equal to their salary, increased by all benefits in kind, for the duration of the closure and at most for six months.
If collective or individual agreements provide, after dismissal, for a higher compensation, it is the latter that shall be due.
Any violation of the provisions of the two preceding paragraphs shall be punished by imprisonment from one to six months and a fine from
25,000 to 100,000 francs.
In case of recidivism, the penalties shall be doubled.

###### Article 400

In all cases provided for by Articles 397, 398 and 399, the guilty parties shall, in addition, be sentenced to the confiscation of sums equal to the amount of shares of receipts produced by the reproduction, representation or unlawful distribution as well as to the confiscation of all equipment specially installed for the purpose of unlawful reproduction and of all counterfeit copies and objects.
The Court may order, at the request of the civil party, the publication of judgments of conviction, in full or in extract, in the newspapers it shall designate and the posting of said judgments in the places it shall indicate, in particular at the doors of the domicile, of all establishments, performance halls, of the convicted persons, all at their expense, without however the costs of this publication being able to exceed the maximum fine incurred.
When posting is ordered, the Court shall fix the dimensions of the poster and the typographical characters that must be used for its printing.
The Court must fix the time during which this posting must be maintained, without the duration being able to exceed fifteen days.
The removal, concealment or total or partial tearing of posters shall be punished by a fine that may not exceed 50,000 francs. In case of recidivism, imprisonment of at most three months may be pronounced.
When the removal, concealment or total or partial tearing of posters shall have been carried out voluntarily by the convicted person, at his instigation or on his orders, the provisions of the judgment relating to posting shall be fully executed again, at the expense of the convicted person.

###### Article 401

In the cases provided for by Articles 397 to 400, the equipment or counterfeit copies, as well as the receipts or shares of receipts having given rise to confiscation, shall be handed over to the author or his successors in title

to compensate them to that extent for the prejudice they shall have suffered; the remainder of their compensation or the entire compensation if there has been no confiscation of equipment, counterfeit objects or receipts, shall be settled by ordinary means.

###### Paragraphe VII Offences by suppliers

###### Article 402

All individuals charged, whether as members of a company or individually, with supplies, enterprises or administrations on behalf of the armed forces, who without having been compelled by force majeure, shall have caused the service with which they are charged to fail, shall be punished by imprisonment of two years to five years and a fine which may not exceed one quarter of the damages; nor be less than 100,000 francs; all without prejudice to more severe penalties in case of collusion with the enemy.

###### Article 403

When the cessation of service arises from the act of the agents of the suppliers, the agents shall be sentenced to the penalties provided by the preceding article.

The suppliers and their agents shall be equally sentenced, when both have participated in the offence.

###### Article 404

If public officials or agents, representatives or employees of the Government, have aided the guilty parties in causing the service to fail, they shall be punished by imprisonment of five to ten

years, without prejudice to more severe penalties in case of collusion with the enemy.

###### Article 405

Although the service has not failed, if, through negligence, deliveries and works have been delayed, or if there has been fraud as to the nature, quality or quantity of works or labour or things supplied, the guilty parties shall be punished by imprisonment of at least six months and at most five years and a fine which may not exceed one quarter of the damages, nor be less than 50,000 francs.
In the various cases provided for by the articles composing this paragraph, prosecution may only be brought upon denunciation by the Government.

##### SECTION III DESTRUCTION, DAMAGE, INJURY

###### Article 406

Whoever shall have wilfully set fire to buildings, ships, aircraft, boats, warehouses, shipyards, when they are inhabited or used for habitation, and generally to places inhabited or used for habitation, whether they belong or do not belong to the perpetrator of the crime, shall be punished by death.
Shall be punished by the same penalty whoever shall have wilfully set fire, either to carriages or wagons containing persons, or to carriages or wagons not containing persons, but forming part of a convoy which contains them.

Whoever shall have wilfully set fire to buildings, ships, aircraft, boats, warehouses, shipyards, when they are neither inhabited, nor used for habitation, or to forests, woods, coppices, or standing crops, when these objects do not belong to him, shall be punished by hard labour for life.
He who, by setting or causing to be set fire to one of the objects enumerated in the preceding paragraph and belonging to himself shall have wilfully caused prejudice to another, shall be punished by hard labour for a term of five to ten years.
Shall be punished by the same penalty he who shall have set fire on the order of the owner.
Whoever shall have wilfully set or attempted to set fire, either to huts or straw huts when they are neither inhabited nor used for habitation, or to straw or crops in stacks or ricks, or to wood arranged in piles or stacks, or to carriages or wagons loaded or not loaded with goods, or other movable objects not forming part of a convoy containing persons, if these objects do not belong to him, shall be punished by imprisonment of five to ten years.
He who, by setting or causing to be set fire to one of the objects enumerated in the preceding paragraph, and belonging to himself shall have wilfully caused any prejudice whatsoever to another, shall be punished by the same penalty.
Shall be punished by the same penalty he who shall have set or

attempted to set fire on the order of the owner.
He who shall have communicated the fire to one of the objects enumerated in the preceding paragraphs, by wilfully setting fire to any objects whatsoever belonging either to himself or to another, and placed so as to communicate the said fire shall be punished by the same penalty as if he had directly set fire to one of the said objects.
In all cases, if the fire has caused the death or permanent disability of one or more persons present at the places set on fire, the penalty shall be death.

###### Article 407

The penalty shall be the same, according to the distinctions made in the preceding article, against those who shall have wilfully destroyed in whole or in part or attempted to destroy by the effect of a mine or any explosive substance the buildings, dwellings, dikes, causeways, ships, aircraft, boats, vehicles of all kinds, warehouses or shipyards, or their dependencies, bridges, public or private roads and generally all movable or immovable objects of whatever nature they may be.
The depositing, with criminal intent, on a public or private road, of an explosive device, shall be assimilated to attempted premeditated murder.
Persons guilty of the offences mentioned in this article shall be exempt from penalty, if, before the consummation of these offences and before any prosecution, they have given

knowledge thereof and revealed the perpetrators to the constituted authorities, or if, even after prosecution has commenced, they have procured the arrest of the other guilty parties.

###### Article 408

The threat of fire or of destroying, by the effect of a mine or any explosive substance, the objects included in the enumeration of the preceding article, shall be punished by the penalty provided against the threat of assassination, and according to the distinctions established by articles 290, 291 and 292.

###### Article 409

Whoever, wilfully, shall have destroyed or overthrown, by whatever means, in whole or in part, buildings, bridges, dikes or causeways or other constructions which he knew to belong to another, or caused the explosion of any energy installation, shall be punished by imprisonment of five to ten years and a fine which may not exceed one quarter of the restitutions and indemnities, nor be less than 100,000 francs.
If there has been homicide or injury, the guilty party shall, in the first case, be punished by death, and, in the second, punished by hard labour for life.

###### Article 410

Whoever, by acts of violence, shall have opposed the execution of works authorized by the Government or the execution of a judgment rendered in land or immovable matters, shall be punished by imprisonment of

three months to two years, and a fine which may not exceed one quarter of the damages, nor be less than 50,000 francs.
The instigators shall undergo the maximum penalty.

###### Article 411

Whoever shall have wilfully burned or destroyed, in any manner whatsoever, registers, minutes or original acts of public authority, titles, notes, bills of exchange, commercial or banking instruments, containing or effecting obligation, disposition or discharge;
Whoever shall have knowingly destroyed, removed, concealed, hidden or altered a public or private document of a nature to facilitate the investigation of crimes and offences, the discovery of evidence or the punishment of their perpetrator shall, without prejudice to more severe penalties provided by law, be punished as follows:
If the destroyed documents are acts of public authority or commercial or banking instruments, the penalty shall be imprisonment of five years to ten years;
If it concerns any other document, the guilty party shall be punished by imprisonment of two years and a fine of
50,000 to 100,000 francs.

###### Article 412

Any pillage, any destruction of foodstuffs or goods, effects, movable property, committed in assembly or gang and by open force, shall be punished by hard labour for a term of ten to twenty years; each of the guilty parties shall moreover be

sentenced to a fine of
25,000 to 500,000 francs.

###### Article 413

Nevertheless, those who prove that they were led by provocations or solicitations to take part in such violence may be punished only by the penalty of criminal detention of five to ten years.

###### Article 414

If the looted or destroyed foodstuffs are grains, seeds or flour, farinaceous substances, bread, milk, wine or other beverage, the penalty to be imposed on the leaders, instigators or provocateurs only shall be the maximum term of hard labour and that of the fine pronounced by Article 412.

###### Article 415

Whoever, by means of a corrosive liquid or by any other means, has wilfully damaged any goods, materials or instruments used in manufacturing, shall be punished by imprisonment of one month to two years, and by a fine which may not exceed one quarter of the damages, nor be less than 50,000 francs.
If the offence has been committed by a worker of the factory or by an employee of the commercial establishment, the imprisonment shall be from two to five years, without prejudice to the fine, as just stated.

###### Article 416

Whoever has devastated standing crops or plants grown naturally or planted by human hand shall be punished by imprisonment of at least

two years and at most five years.
The guilty parties may furthermore be prohibited from residing in the area for a period of five to ten years.

###### Article 417

Whoever has felled one or more trees which he knew belonged to another person shall be punished by imprisonment which shall not be less than one month nor more than six months for each tree, without the total being able to exceed five years.

###### Article 418

The penalties shall be the same for each tree mutilated, cut or stripped of bark in such a way as to cause it to perish.

###### Article 419

If there has been destruction of one or more grafts, the imprisonment shall be from one to three months for each graft, without the total being able to exceed two years.

###### Article 420

Whoever has cut grain or fodder which he knew belonged to another person shall be punished by imprisonment of one to three months.

###### Article 421

The imprisonment shall be from two to six months if green grain has been cut.
In the cases provided for by this article and the five preceding ones, if the act has been committed in hatred of a public official by reason of his functions, the guilty party shall be punished by the maximum penalty established

by the article to which the case refers.
The same shall apply, even if this circumstance does not exist, if the act has been committed during the night.

###### Article 422

Any breaking, any destruction of agricultural instruments, livestock pens, or guardians' huts shall be punished by imprisonment of two months to two years.

###### Article 423

Whoever has cultivated or occupied in any manner whatsoever land which another person could dispose of, either by virtue of a land title or by virtue of an administrative or judicial decision, shall be punished by imprisonment of six months to three years and by a fine which may not be less than
50,000 francs.
(Law No. 66-16 of 1 February 1966)
Whoever has unlawfully occupied land forming part of the national domain or registered in the name of the State or of a public authority or has concluded or attempted to conclude an agreement relating to such land shall be punished by the same penalties.

###### Article 424

Whoever has poisoned horses or other beasts of carriage, riding or burden, horned cattle, sheep, goats or pigs, or fish in ponds, fish ponds or reservoirs, shall be punished by imprisonment of one year to

five years, and by a fine of
20,000 to 100,000 francs.
He may, in addition, be prohibited from residing in the area for a period of at least two years and at most five years.

###### Article 425

Those who, without necessity, have killed or mutilated one of the animals mentioned in the preceding article shall be punished as follows:
If the offence has been committed in the buildings, enclosures and outbuildings or on the lands of which the owner of the killed or mutilated animal was proprietor, lessee, settler or tenant, the penalty shall be imprisonment of one month to one year;
If it has been committed in any other place, the imprisonment shall be from one to six months.
The maximum penalty shall always be pronounced in case of violation of an enclosure.

###### Article 426

Whoever has, without necessity, killed or mutilated a domestic animal in a place of which the person to whom this animal belongs is proprietor, lessee, settler or tenant, shall be punished by imprisonment of one month to six months.
If there has been violation of an enclosure, the maximum penalty shall be pronounced.

###### Article 427

In the cases provided for by Articles 416 and following up to Article 426
inclusive, a fine shall be pronounced which may not exceed one quarter of the restitution and damages

and interest, nor be less than 20,000 francs.

###### Article 428

Whoever has, in whole or in part, filled in ditches, destroyed enclosures, of whatever materials they are made, cut or torn up live or dry hedges; whoever has removed boundary markers or corner posts, or other trees planted or recognized to establish the boundaries between different properties, shall be punished by imprisonment which may not be less than one month nor exceed one year, and by a fine equal to one quarter of the restitution and damages and interest which, in no case, may be less than 50,000 francs.

###### Article 429

If the correctional police offences referred to in this chapter have been committed by forest guards or police officers in any capacity whatsoever, the penalty of imprisonment shall be at least one month, and at most one third in addition to the highest penalty which would be applied to another guilty party of the same offence.

##### SECTION IV RECEIVING STOLEN GOODS

###### Article 430

Those who, knowingly, have received, in whole or in part, things taken, misappropriated or obtained by means of a crime or an offence, shall be punished by the penalties provided for by Article 370.
The fine may even be increased beyond
200,000 francs up to half the value of the received objects.

All without prejudice to heavier penalties, if applicable in case of complicity.

###### Article 431

In the case where an afflictive and infamous penalty is applicable to the act which procured the received things, the receiver shall be punished by the penalty attached by law to the crime and to the circumstances of the crime of which he had knowledge at the time of receiving. Nevertheless, the death penalty shall be replaced with respect to receivers by that of hard labour for life.
GENERAL PROVISIONS

###### Article 432

Except for contrary and express provisions of the law, the penalties pronounced against the accused or those of the accused found guilty in whose favour the Court of Assizes has declared mitigating circumstances shall be modified as follows:
If the penalty provided for is death, the Court shall apply the penalty of hard labour for life or that of hard labour for a term of ten to twenty years.
If the penalty provided for is that of hard labour for life, the Court shall apply the penalty of hard labour for a term of ten to twenty years or that of hard labour for a term of five to ten years.
If the penalty is that of criminal detention or civic degradation, the Court shall apply the penalty of imprisonment of five to ten years or that of imprisonment of two to five years.

In the case where the law specifies that the penalty of hard labour for life shall be obligatorily pronounced, the Court shall apply the penalty of twenty years of hard labour.
In the case where the law pronounces the maximum of an afflictive or infamous penalty, if mitigating circumstances exist, the Court shall apply the maximum of that penalty or even the immediately lower penalty.

###### Article 433

Except where otherwise expressly provided by law, the penalties imposed on the accused person or persons found guilty, in whose favour mitigating circumstances have been declared to exist, shall be modified as follows:
(Law No. 77-33 of 22 February 1977)

They may also impose either of these penalties separately and even substitute a fine for imprisonment.

PRELIMINARY TITLE SCOPE OF APPLICATION

###### Article 1

The provisions of this law apply to the following organisations:
-	Banks within the meaning of article 3 of the law on Banking Regulation'
-	Postal Cheque Services subject to the specificities related to their status;

- the Public Treasury and any other body duly authorised by law.
For the purposes of this law, the term banker means the organisations referred to in the preceding paragraph without prejudice to the particular provisions applicable to them.

### TITLE I CHEQUES

#### CHAPTER I OPENING AND OPERATION OF

ACCOUNTS

###### Article 2

Prior to opening a cheque account, the banker must verify the identity and address of the applicant upon presentation of an official document in force bearing his or her photograph.
He is bound by the same diligence with regard to the co-holder of a joint account. The banker must inform clients to whom a chequebook is issued of the penalties incurred in case of refusal to pay made in violation of article 43 paragraph 3 of this law.
He is also required to send his client a statement of account at least once per quarter.

###### Article 3

Cheque forms are subject to standardisation defined by Instructions of the Central Bank of West African States hereinafter referred to as the Central Bank, or, where appropriate, by ministerial order.

Cheque forms mention the address and telephone number of the bank branch at which the cheque is payable.
They also mention the name and address of the account holder.

###### Article 4

Before any delivery of cheque forms, the banker must inquire into the situation of the applicant by consulting the payment incident file provided for in article 93 of this law.
Pre-crossed cheque forms may be issued that are not endorsable except in favour of a banker.
Other cheque forms are subject to a stamp duty the amount of which shall be fixed by ministerial order. This duty is borne by the person requesting the delivery of such forms.

###### Article 5

When they are issued, cheque forms are free of charge.

###### Article 6

The banker may, by duly reasoned decision, refuse to deliver to the account holder cheque forms other than those issued for a withdrawal of funds by the drawer from the drawee or for certification. He may, at any time, request the return of forms previously delivered.
The return must be requested upon closure of the account.

#### CHAPTER Il

CREATION AND FORM OF CHEQUES

###### Article 7

The cheque contains:
1°- the denomination cheque, inserted in the text itself of the instrument and expressed in the language used for the drafting of that instrument;

2°- The unconditional mandate to pay a specified sum;
3°- The name of the person who is to pay (drawee);
4°- The indication of the place where payment is to be made;
5°- The indication of the date and place where the cheque is created;
6°- The handwritten signature of the person who issues the cheque (drawer).

###### Article 8

The instrument in which one of the particulars indicated in article 7 of this law is missing does not have validity as a cheque, except in the cases determined by the following paragraphs.
In the absence of special indication, the place designated next to the name of the drawee is deemed to be the place of payment. If several places are indicated next to the name of the drawee, the cheque is payable at the first place indicated.
In the absence of these indications or any other indication, the cheque is payable at the place where the drawee has its principal establishment.
The cheque without indication of the place of its creation is

considered as drawn in the place designated next to the name of the drawer.

###### Article 9

A cheque may only be drawn on a banker who has, at the time of the creation of the instrument, sufficient funds at the disposal of the drawer and in accordance with an agreement, express or tacit, under which the drawer has the right to dispose of those funds by cheque.
Instruments drawn and payable in Senegal in the form of cheques on any person other than those referred to in the first paragraph of this article are not valid as cheques.
The provision must be made by the drawer or by the person on whose account the cheque is to be drawn, without the drawer on behalf of another ceasing to be personally liable to the endorsers and the holder only.
The drawer alone is required to prove, in case of denial, that the drawee had provision at the time of the creation of the instrument; otherwise he is required to guarantee it even if the protest is made after the time limits set.

###### Article 10

A cheque may not be accepted. A mention of acceptance placed on the cheque is deemed unwritten.
However, the drawee has the option of certifying the cheque in accordance with the provisions of Article 36 of this law.

###### Article 11

A cheque may be stipulated payable:

-	to a named person, with or without the express clause "to order";
-	to a named person, with the clause "not to order" or an equivalent clause; to bearer.
A cheque in favour of a named person, with the mention "to bearer", or an equivalent term is valid as a cheque to bearer.
A cheque without indication of the beneficiary is valid as a cheque to bearer.

###### Article 12

A cheque may be to the order of the drawer himself. A cheque may be drawn on behalf of a third party.
A cheque may not be drawn on the drawer himself, except in cases where it is a cheque drawn between different establishments of the same drawer and provided that this cheque is not to bearer.

###### Article 13

Any stipulation of interest inserted in the cheque is deemed unwritten.

###### Article 14

A cheque may be payable at the domicile of a third party, either in the locality where the drawee has his domicile, or in another locality, provided however that the third party is a banker within the meaning of Article 1, paragraph 2 of this law.
This domiciliation may not be made against the will of the holder.

###### Article 15

A cheque whose amount is written both in words and in figures is valid, in case of difference, for the sum written in words.
A cheque whose amount is written several times, either in words or in figures, is valid, in case of difference, only for the lesser sum.

###### Article 16

If a cheque bears signatures of persons incapable of binding themselves by cheque, forged signatures or signatures of fictitious persons, or signatures which, for any other reason, could not bind the persons who signed the cheque, or in whose name it was signed, the obligations of the other signatories are nonetheless valid.

###### Article 17

Whoever affixes his signature on a cheque, as representative of a person for whom he had no power to act, is himself bound by virtue of the cheque and, if he has paid, has the same rights as the purported represented party would have had. The same applies to a representative who has exceeded his powers.

###### Article 18

The drawer is guarantor of payment. Any clause by which the drawer exempts himself from this guarantee is deemed unwritten.
The delivery of a cheque in payment accepted by a creditor does not entail novation. Consequently, the original debt subsists

with all the guarantees attached thereto until the said cheque is paid.

###### Article 19

Any cheque for which the corresponding provision exists at the disposal of the drawer must be certified by the drawee if the drawer or the holder with the drawer's agreement so requests, subject to the option for the drawee to replace this cheque with a cheque issued under the conditions provided for in Article 12, paragraph 3 of this law.

###### Article 20

Any person who delivers a cheque in payment must prove his identity by means of an official document in force bearing his photograph.

#### CHAPTER III

ON TRANSFER

###### Article 21

A cheque stipulated payable in favour of a named person with or without the express clause "to order" is transferable by way of endorsement.
A cheque stipulated payable in favour of a named person with the clause "not to order" or an equivalent clause is transferable only in the form and with the effects of an ordinary assignment.

###### Article 22

An endorsement may be made even in favour of the drawer or any other obligor. These persons may endorse the cheque again.

###### Article 23

An endorsement must be pure and simple. Any condition to which it is subject is deemed unwritten.
A partial endorsement is null and void.
An endorsement to the drawee is also null and void.
An endorsement to bearer is valid as a blank endorsement.
An endorsement to the drawee is valid only as a receipt, except in the case where the drawee has several establishments and where the endorsement is made in favour of an establishment other than that on which the cheque was drawn.

###### Article 24

An endorsement must be inscribed on the cheque or on a sheet attached thereto (allonge). It must be signed by the endorser. The signature of the latter is affixed, either by hand or by any non-handwritten process.
An endorsement may not designate the beneficiary or consist simply of the signature of the endorser (blank endorsement). In the latter case, the endorsement, to be valid, must be inscribed on the back of the cheque or on the allonge.

###### Article 25

An endorsement transfers all the rights resulting from the cheque and in particular the ownership of the provision.
If the endorsement is in blank, the holder may:

1°- Fill in the blank, either with his name or with the name of another person;
2°- Endorse the cheque again in blank, or to another person;
3°- Deliver the cheque to a third party, without filling in the blank and without endorsing it.

###### Article 26

The endorser is, unless otherwise stipulated, guarantor of payment. He may prohibit a further endorsement; in that case, he is not liable as guarantor to the persons to whom the cheque is subsequently endorsed.

###### Article 27

The holder of an endorsable cheque is considered a legitimate holder if he justifies his right by an uninterrupted series of endorsements, even if the last endorsement is in blank. Crossed-out endorsements are, in this respect, deemed unwritten. When a blank endorsement is followed by another endorsement, the signatory of the latter is deemed to have acquired the cheque by the blank endorsement.

###### Article 28

An endorsement appearing on a cheque to bearer makes the endorser liable under the terms of the provisions governing recourse; it does not, moreover, convert the instrument into an order cheque.

###### Article 29

When a person has been dispossessed of an order cheque by any event whatsoever, the beneficiary who justifies his right in the manner indicated in Article 27

of this law is required to part with the cheque only if he acquired it in bad faith or if, in acquiring it, he committed gross negligence.

###### Article 30

Persons sued by virtue of the cheque may not set up against the holder defences based on their personal relations with the drawer or with previous holders, unless the holder, in acquiring the cheque, acted knowingly to the detriment of the debtor.

###### Article 31

When the endorsement contains the mention "value for collection", "for collection", "by procuration" or any other mention implying a simple mandate, the holder may exercise all rights arising from the cheque, but he may only endorse it by way of procuration.
The obligors may not, in this case, invoke against the holder any defences other than those which would be opposable to the endorser.
The mandate contained in a procuration endorsement does not terminate upon the death of the mandator or the occurrence of his incapacity.

###### Article 32

An endorsement made after protest or after expiry of the time limit for presentment produces only the effects of an ordinary assignment.
In the absence of proof to the contrary, an undated endorsement is presumed to have been made before

the protest or before the expiry of the time limit referred to in the preceding paragraph.
It is prohibited to backdate orders on pain of forgery.

#### CHAPTER IV GUARANTEES OF THE

CHEQUE

##### SECTION ONE AVAL

###### Article 33

Payment of a cheque may be guaranteed for all or part of its amount by an aval.
This guarantee is provided by a third party, except the drawee, or even by a signatory of the cheque.

###### Article 34

The aval is given either on the cheque or on an allonge, or by a separate instrument indicating the date and place where it was given.
It is expressed by the words
"good for aval" or by any other equivalent formula; it is signed by the giver of the aval with indication of his name and address.
It is considered as resulting from the sole signature of the giver of the aval, affixed on the face of the cheque, except when it is the signature of the drawer.
The aval must indicate for whose account it is given. In the absence of this indication, it is deemed to be given for the drawer.

###### Article 35

The giver of the aval is bound in the same manner as the person for whom he has stood guarantor.

His undertaking is valid even if the obligation which he has guaranteed is null for any cause other than a defect of form.
When he pays the cheque, the giver of the aval acquires the rights arising from the cheque against the guaranteed party and against those who are bound towards the latter by virtue of the cheque.

##### SECTION II VISA

###### Article 36

The visa is a guarantee of the existence of the provision at the time it is affixed on the cheque.
The drawee bank may not refuse to affix the visa if there is provision.
However, the affixing of the visa does not imply for the bank the obligation to block the provision.

##### SECTION III

CERTIFICATION

###### Article 37

The drawer or the holder of a cheque may request its certification from the drawee bank, if there is provision in the account.
However, the cheque may only be certified with the written agreement of the drawer.
When the cheque is certified, the provision is then blocked under the responsibility of the drawee for the benefit of the holder until the expiry of the time limit for presentment referred to in Article 40 of this law.
Certification results from the affixing on the cheque by the drawee of a formula

comprising, in addition to his signature, the mentions relating to the certification and the date thereof, the amount for which the cheque has been drawn and the designation of the drawee institution. These mentions must be affixed by means of a mechanical process of indelible marking or printing offering every guarantee of security.
In all cases where the delivery of a certified cheque is required, this requirement may be validly satisfied by the delivery of a cheque issued under the conditions provided for in Article 19 of this law.

#### CHAPTER V

PRESENTMENT AND PAYMENT

###### Article 39

The cheque is payable at sight. Any mention to the contrary is deemed unwritten.
A cheque presented for payment before the day indicated as the date of issue is payable on the day of presentment;

###### Article 40

A cheque issued and payable in Senegal must be presented for payment within eight days if payment is to be made
at the place of issue, and, in other cases, within 20 days.
A cheque issued in a member State of the West African Monetary Union (UMOA) and payable in Senegal must be presented within 45 days.
A cheque issued outside the UMOA and payable in

Senegal must be presented within 70 days.
The starting point of these time limits is the day shown on the cheque as the date of issue.
For the rest, the rules laid down in Articles 70 and 71 of this law apply to the presentment of the cheque.

###### Article 41

When a cheque payable in Senegal is issued in a country where a calendar other than the Gregorian calendar is in use, the day of issue shall be converted to the corresponding day in the Gregorian calendar.

###### Article 42

Presentment to a clearing house is equivalent to presentment for payment.

###### Article 43

When the provision exists, the drawee must pay even after expiry of the time limit for presentment.
He must also pay even if the cheque has been issued in disregard of the injunction prescribed by Article 74 or in violation of the prohibition provided for in Article 85 (first paragraph) of this law.
Opposition to payment of the cheque by the drawer is only admitted in case of loss, theft, fraudulent use of the cheque or opening of collective proceedings against the holder. The drawer must immediately confirm his opposition and indicate the reason therefor in writing, whatever the medium of that writing. This prohibition to pay only terminates by withdrawal or by prescription.

In case of dispute by the holder, with regard to an opposition by the drawer, the judge in summary proceedings, even in the case where proceedings on the merits are initiated, may order the withdrawal of the opposition.

###### Article 44

In case of loss of the cheque, the person to whom it belongs may pursue payment on a second, third, fourth, etc.
The owner of the mislaid cheque must, in order to obtain a second one, apply to his immediate endorser who is bound to lend him his name and his services to act towards his own endorser, and so on going back from endorser to endorser up to the drawer of the cheque. The owner of the mislaid cheque shall bear the costs.
If the person who has lost the cheque cannot produce the second one, he may request payment of the lost cheque and obtain it by order of the judge by proving his ownership by his books and by giving security. The undertaking of the security is extinguished after six months, if during that time, there have been no claims or legal proceedings.
In case of refusal of payment, upon the application made pursuant to the preceding paragraph, the owner of the lost cheque retains all his rights by an act of protest. This act must be made at the latest on the first working day following the expiry of the time limit for presentment. The notices prescribed by Article 54 must be given to the drawer and to the endorsers within the time limits fixed by that article.

###### Article 45

Neither the death of the drawer, nor his incapacity occurring after issue, affect the effects of the cheque.

###### Article 46

The person who presents a cheque for payment must prove his identity by means of an official document bearing his photograph.
The drawee may require, on paying the cheque, that it be delivered to him receipted by the holder.
If the provision is less than the amount of the cheque, the holder has the right to demand payment up to the amount of the provision.
In case of partial payment, the drawee may require that mention of this payment be made on the cheque and that a receipt be given to him therefor.
This receipt, issued on a separate document, enjoys with regard to stamp duty the same exemption as the receipt given on the cheque itself.
Partial payments on the amount of a cheque discharge the drawers and endorsers.
The holder may have the cheque protested for the difference.

###### Article 47

Anyone who pays a cheque without opposition is presumed to be validly discharged.
The drawee who pays an endorsable cheque is obliged to verify the regularity of the series of endorsements, but not the signature of the endorsers.

###### Article 48

When a cheque is stipulated payable in a currency not having legal tender in Senegal, the amount may be paid, within the period for presentation of the cheque, according to its value in CFA francs on the day of payment. If payment has not been made upon presentation, the holder may, at his option, request that the amount of the cheque be paid in CFA francs according to the rate either of the day of presentation or of the day of payment.
The practices in force for the quotation of currencies in which cheques are denominated must be followed to determine the value of these currencies in CFA francs. However, the drawer may stipulate that the sum to be paid shall be calculated according to a rate specified in the cheque.
The rules set out above do not apply to the case where, in accordance with the provisions of exchange control regulations, the drawer has stipulated that payment must be made in a certain specified currency (clause of effective payment in a foreign currency)
If the amount of the cheque is indicated in a currency having the same denomination but a different value in the country of issue and in that of payment, reference is presumed to be made to the currency of the place of payment.

#### CHAPITRE VI

DU CHEQUE BARRE

###### Article 49

The drawer or the holder of a cheque may cross it.

The crossing is effected by means of two parallel lines affixed on the front. It may be general or special.
The crossing is general if it bears between the two lines no designation or the mention "banker" or an equivalent term; it is special if the name of a banker is inscribed between the two lines.
A general crossing may be transformed into a special crossing, but a special crossing may not be transformed into a general payment.
The striking out of the crossing or of the name of the designated banker is deemed to be of no effect.

###### Article 50

A cheque with a general crossing may be paid by the drawee only to a banker.
A cheque with a special crossing may be paid by the drawee only to the designated banker or, if the latter is the drawee, only to his customer. However, the designated banker may have recourse for collection to another banker.
A banker may acquire a crossed cheque only from one of his customers or from another banker. He may collect it only for the account of such persons.
A cheque bearing several special crossings may be paid by the drawee only in the case where there are two crossings, one of which is for collection through a clearing house.
The drawee or the banker who does not observe the above provisions is liable for the

damage up to the amount of the cheque.

###### Article 51

Cheques to be credited to account issued abroad and payable on Senegalese territory shall be treated as crossed cheques.

#### CHAPITRE VII

DES RECOURS FAUTE DE PAIEMENT

###### Article 52

The holder may exercise his recourse against the endorsers, the drawer and the other parties liable, if the cheque, presented within the period provided for in Article 40, is not paid and if the refusal of payment is established by an authentic act (protest).

###### Article 53

The protest must be made before the expiration of the period for presentation.
If the presentation takes place on the last day of the period, the protest may be drawn up on the first following business day.

###### Article 54

The holder must give notice of non-payment to his endorser and to the drawer within the four business days following the day of the protest or, in case of a return without costs clause, the day of presentation.
Notaries and bailiffs are required, on pain of damages, to notify the drawer within forty-eight hours following registration, by post and by registered letter, of the reasons for refusal to pay. This letter

gives rise, for the benefit of the notary or the bailiff, to the correspondence fee fixed by the tariff applicable to him.
Each endorser must, within the two business days following the day on which he received the notice, make known to his endorser the notice he has received, indicating the names and addresses of those who gave the preceding notices, and so on, going back to the drawer. The periods indicated above run from the receipt of the preceding notice.
When, in accordance with the preceding paragraph, a notice is given to a signatory of the cheque, the same notice must be given within the same period to his guarantor.
In the case where an endorser has not indicated his address or has indicated it in an illegible manner, it is sufficient that the notice be given to the endorser who precedes him.
Anyone who has to give a notice may do so in any form whatsoever, even by a simple sending of the cheque.
He must prove that he gave the notice within the allotted period. This period shall be considered as observed if a letter giving the notice has been posted within the said period.
Anyone who does not give the notice within the period indicated above does not incur forfeiture; he is liable, if appropriate, for the damage caused by his negligence, without the damages being able to exceed the amount of the cheque.

###### Article 55

The drawer, an endorser or a guarantor may, by the

clause "return without costs",
"without protest", or any other equivalent clause, inscribed on the instrument and signed, dispense the holder, for exercising his recourse, from having a protest drawn up.
This clause does not dispense the holder from the presentation of the cheque within the prescribed period, nor from the notices to be given. The proof of non-observance of the period is incumbent upon the person who relies on it against the holder.
If the clause is inscribed by the drawer, it produces its effects with regard to all the signatories; if it is inscribed by an endorser or a guarantor, it produces its effects only with regard to the latter. If, despite the clause inscribed by the drawer, the holder has a protest drawn up, the costs thereof remain at his charge. When the clause emanates from an endorser or a guarantor, the costs of the protest, if one is drawn up, may be recovered against all the signatories.

###### Article 56

All persons liable by virtue of a cheque are jointly and severally liable towards the holder.
The holder has the right to take action against all these persons, individually or collectively, without being required to observe the order in which they became liable;
The same right belongs to any signatory of a cheque who has reimbursed it.
An action brought against one of the parties liable does not prevent action being taken against the others, even those subsequent to the one who was first sued.

###### Article 57

The holder may claim from the person against whom he exercises his recourse:
1°- The amount of the unpaid cheque;
2°- Interest from the day of presentation, due at the legal rate for cheques issued and payable in Senegal and at the rate of 6% for other cheques;
3°- The costs of protest, those of the notices given, as well as other costs.

###### Article 58

Anyone who has reimbursed the cheque may claim from his guarantors:
1°- The full sum he has paid
2°- Interest on the said sum, from the day on which he disbursed it, calculated at the legal rate for cheques issued and payable in Senegal and at the rate of 6% for other cheques;
3°- The costs he has incurred.

###### Article 59

Any party liable against whom recourse is exercised or who is exposed to recourse may require, against
reimbursement, the delivery of the cheque with the protest and a receipted account.
Any endorser who has reimbursed the cheque may strike out his endorsement and those of subsequent endorsers.

###### Article 60

When the presentation of the cheque or the drawing up of the

protest within the prescribed time limits is prevented by an insurmountable obstacle (legal prescription or other case of force majeure), these time limits are extended.
The holder is required to give, without delay, notice of the case of force majeure to his endorser and to mention this notice, dated and signed by him, on the cheque or on an allonge; for the rest, the provisions of Article 54 of this law are applicable.
After the cessation of the force majeure, the holder must, without delay, present the cheque for payment and, if necessary, have the protest drawn up.
If the force majeure persists beyond fifteen days from the date on which the holder has, even before the expiration of the time limit for presentation, given notice of the force majeure to his endorser, recourse may be exercised, without either presentation or protest being necessary.
Facts purely personal to the holder or to the person he has charged with the presentation of the cheque or the drawing up of the protest are not considered as constituting cases of force majeure.

#### CHAPTER VIII PROTESTS

###### Article 61

The protest must be made, by a notary or by a bailiff, at the domicile of the person on whom the cheque was payable or at his last known domicile.

###### Article 62

The deed of protest contains the literal transcription of the

cheque and the endorsements as well as the demand to pay the amount of the cheque. It states the presence or absence of the person who must pay, the reasons for refusal to pay and the inability or refusal to sign and, in case of partial payment, the amount of the sum that has been paid.
Notaries and bailiffs are required, under penalty of damages, to make, under their signature, mention on the cheque of the protest with its date.

###### Article 63

No act on the part of the holder of the cheque can replace the deed of protest, except in the case provided for by Article 44 of this law concerning the loss of the cheque.

###### Article 64

Notaries and bailiffs are required, under penalty of costs, damages to the parties, to leave an exact copy of the protests.
The service of the protest on the drawer by ministry of bailiff or notary is equivalent to a demand for payment. In the absence of payment of the amount of the cheque and the costs at the expiration of a period of fifteen days, the notary or bailiff must, under the aforementioned sanctions, deliver to the registry of the Court against receipt two exact copies of the protests, one of which is intended for the public prosecutor's office.

#### CHAPTER IX PLURALITY OF COPIES

###### Article 65

Any cheque issued in one country and payable in another country may be drawn in

several identical copies.
In this case, these copies must be numbered in the very text of the instrument, failing which, each of them is considered as a separate cheque.
However, a cheque to bearer cannot be drawn in several copies.

###### Article 66

Payment made on one of the copies is liberating, even if it is not stipulated that this payment cancels the effect of the other copies.
The endorser who has transmitted the copies to different persons, as well as subsequent endorsers, are liable for all the copies bearing their signature which have not been returned.

#### CHAPTER X

ALTERATIONS AND PRESCRIPTION

##### SECTION ONE ALTERATIONS

###### Article 67

In case of alteration of the text of a cheque, signatories subsequent to this alteration are bound by the terms of the altered text; prior signatories are bound by the terms of the original text.

##### SECTION Il

PRESCRIPTION

###### Article 68

Actions for recourse of the holder against the endorsers, the drawer and the other obligors are prescribed after six months from

the expiration of the time limit for presentation.
Actions for recourse of the various persons obligated to pay a cheque against each other are prescribed after six months from the day on which the obligor reimbursed the cheque or from the day on which he was himself sued.
However, in case of forfeiture or prescription, there remains an action against the drawer who has not provided funds or the other obligors who would have been unjustly enriched.
The action of the holder of the cheque against the drawee is prescribed after three years from the expiration of the time limit for presentation referred to in Article 40 of this law.

###### Article 69

Prescriptions in case of action brought in court only run from the day of the last procedural act. They do not apply if there has been a conviction or if the debt has been acknowledged by separate deed.
The interruption of prescription has effect only against the person with regard to whom the interruptive act was made.
Nevertheless, the alleged debtors shall be required, if so requested, to affirm under oath that they are no longer indebted and their surviving spouses, heirs or successors in title, that they believe in good faith that nothing is owed any longer.

#### CHAPTER XI GENERAL AND PENAL PROVISIONS

##### SECTION ONE COMPUTATION OF

TIME LIMITS

###### Article 70

The presentation and protest of a cheque can only be made on a business day.
When the last day of the time limit granted by law for the performance of acts relating to the cheque, and in particular for presentation or for the drawing up of the protest is a legal holiday, this time limit is extended until the first business day following its expiration. Intervening holidays are included in the computation of the time limit.
Days on which, under the terms of the laws in force, no payment can be demanded nor any protest drawn up are assimilated to legal holidays.

###### Article 71

The time limits provided for by this law do not include the day which serves as their starting point.
No period of grace, neither legal nor judicial, is admitted except in the cases provided for by the legislation relating to the extension of protest and to that of the maturity dates of negotiable instruments.

##### SECTION Il

WARNING, BANKING PROHIBITION AND REGULARIZATION

###### Article 72

Cheque forms other than those which are delivered for a withdrawal of funds by the drawer from the drawee or for a certification

or bank cheques cannot, subject to the provisions of Article 76 of this law, be issued to the account holder or his agent for 5 years from a payment incident recorded in the name of the account holder for lack of funds and declared to the Central Bank.
The provisions of this article must be observed by the banker who has refused payment of a cheque for lack of funds and by any banker who has been informed of the payment incident, by the Central Bank, in application of Articles 93 and 95 of this law.

###### Article 73

The drawee banker who has refused payment of a cheque for lack or insufficiency of funds must:
1°- Issue a rejection certificate to the beneficiary, specifying the reason for refusal of payment
2°- Record in his books the payment incident at the latest on the second business day following the refusal of payment.
3°- Send to the account holder, at the latter's expense, a warning letter specifying the reason for refusal of payment and the sanctions incurred in the absence of regularization.
The warning letter is only sent to the account holder if the account has not recorded any payment incident in the six months preceding the recording referred to in 2°.

###### Article 74

The drawee bank must, in the absence of regularization within one month from the date of sending the warning letter:
1°- Notify the Central Bank of the incident on the 4th business day following the expiration date of the period;
2°- Notify the account holder that he is prohibited, for a period of 5 years, from issuing cheques other than those which exclusively permit the withdrawal of funds by the drawer from the drawee or those which are certified.
At the same time, the drawee bank must order the account holder to return to all banks of which he is a client the cheque forms in his possession and in that of his agents. The latter are also informed by the drawee bank.
When the warning letter has not been sent pursuant to article 73 paragraph 2 of this law, the drawee bank must notify the Central Bank no later than the 2nd business day following the recording of the incident.
The drawee bank is also bound by the other steps referred to in article 74 paragraph 1- 20 of this law, relating to the notification of the banking prohibition on issuing cheques and the order to return cheque forms to the account holder.

###### Article 75

When the payment incident is the act of the holder of a joint account with or without    solidarity,    the

provisions of articles 72 and 76 of this law are automatically applicable to the other account holders with respect to said account.

###### Article 76

The account holder recovers the ability to issue cheques when, from the aforementioned order, he proves having:
1°- Paid the amount of the unpaid cheque or constituted sufficient and available provision intended for its payment through the drawee;
2° Paid a discharge penalty under the conditions and subject to the reservations set by articles 77 to 79 of this law.
In these cases, the prohibition pronounced pursuant to article 74 of this law is lifted under the conditions set by instructions of the Central Bank, and the drawee bank issues, upon request, a payment certificate to the drawer.
The discharge penalty due is acquired by the Public Treasury under the conditions and procedures set by ministerial order.

###### Article 77

The discharge penalty is not due when the account holder who issued the cheque or his agent proves, within 30 days from the order provided for by article 74, having paid the amount of the cheque or constituted sufficient and available provision intended for its payment through the drawee.
In this case, the exemption from  penalty  applies  to

all cheques rejected subsequently for lack of provision on the same account and regularized within the aforementioned period.
The discharge penalty is not due when the drawer was unable to regularize within the required time periods. This impossibility must be justified before the Public Treasury which assesses its legitimacy.

###### Article 78

The amount of the discharge penalty provided for by article 76 of this law is doubled when the account holder or his agent has already proceeded with two regularizations which allowed him to recover the ability to issue cheques pursuant to the aforementioned article during the twelve months preceding the payment incident.
The amount of the discharge penalty is determined in relation to the fraction of the sum remaining unpaid.

###### Article 79

Disputes relating to the prohibition on issuing cheques and to the discharge penalty referred to in articles 76 and 78 of this law are referred to the civil jurisdiction within the ordinary appeal time limits.
Legal action before this jurisdiction has no suspensive effect. However, the jurisdiction seized may, even in summary proceedings, order the suspension of the prohibition on issuing cheques in case of serious dispute.

###### Article 80

The banking prohibition may also be lifted when it has been pronounced as a result of circumstances not attributable to the drawer, notably following errors committed by the bank.

##### SECTION III

CERTIFICATE OF NON-PAYMENT

###### Article 81

In the absence of payment of the cheque within thirty days from the first presentation or the constitution of provision within the same period, the drawee issues a certificate of non-payment to the bearer of the cheque under the conditions determined by ministerial order.
The effective notification or service of the certificate of non-payment to the drawer by bailiff constitutes a demand for payment.
The bailiff who has not received proof of payment of the amount of the cheque and costs within fifteen days from receipt of the notification or service records the non-payment. The deed drawn up is then delivered by the bailiff to the chief clerk of the competent Court which issues without further procedure, an enforceable title.
In any event, costs of any nature occasioned by the rejection of a cheque without provision are borne by the drawer.

###### Article 82

Except in the case provided for in article 92 of this law, the bank which has paid a

cheque despite the absence, insufficiency or unavailability of provision is, with respect to the account holder, subrogated to the rights of the bearer up to the amount of the sum it has advanced.
In the absence of automatic deduction from the account and without prejudice to any other legal remedy, it may:
1°- Have the ab- sence or insufficiency of available provision recorded;
2°- Issue a formal demand by bailiff to the account holder to pay the sum owed to it.
If there is no payment within 20 days from the formal demand, the procedure is as stated in article 81 of this law.

##### SECTION IV CRIMINAL AND

CIVIL SANCTIONS

###### Article 83

Is punished by imprisonment of one year to three years and a fine of 100,000 F to 2,500,000 F or one of these two penalties only:
1°- The account holder or agent who, in bad faith, has issued a cheque without provision, or has, after issuing a cheque, withdrawn by any means whatsoever, all or part of the provision;
2°- The drawer or agent who, in bad faith, has issued a cheque domiciled on a closed account;
3°- The drawer who, in defiance of the order addressed to him pursuant to article 74 of this law, has issued one or more cheques;
4°- The agent who, in bad faith, has issued one or more cheques the issuance of which was prohibited to his principal pursuant to article 74 of this law
5°- Any person who has forbidden the drawee to pay outside the cases provided for by this law.
6°- Any person who accepts in bad faith a cheque without provision.
The aforementioned fine may   be   increased   to
3,000,000 F if the drawer is a merchant.

###### Article 84

Is	punished	by imprisonment of one year to five years and a fine of
100,000 F to 5,000,000 F or
one of these two penalties only:
1°- Any person who has counterfeited or falsified a cheque;
2°- Any person who, in bad faith, has used or attempted to use a counterfeited or falsified cheque;
3°- Any person who, in bad faith, has accepted to receive a counterfeited or falsified cheque.

###### Article 85

In all cases provided for in Articles 83 and 84 of this law, the Court must prohibit the convicted person, for a period of one to five years, from issuing cheques other than those which exclusively permit the withdrawal of

funds by the drawer from the drawee or those which are certified. This prohibition may be declared provisionally enforceable. It is accompanied by an injunction addressed to the convicted person to return to the bankers who had issued them the forms in his possession and in that of his agents. The Court may order, at the expense of the convicted person, the publication by extracts of the decision imposing the prohibition in the newspapers it designates and according to the procedures it determines;
As a consequence of the aforementioned prohibition, any banker informed thereof by the Central Bank in accordance with Articles 93 and 95 of this law, must refrain from issuing to the convicted person and his agents cheque forms other than those mentioned in the preceding paragraph.
When the conviction is pronounced following a payment incident noted on a joint account with or without joint and several liability, the prohibition provided for in the first paragraph is automatically applicable to the other holders with regard to said account.

###### Article 86

Is punished by imprisonment of one to five years and a fine of
100,000 F to 2,500,000 F, or
by only one of these two penalties, the drawer who has issued one or more cheques in violation of the prohibition pronounced in application of Article 85 first paragraph of this law.
Shall be punished by the same penalties the agent who, in bad faith, has issued one or more cheques whose

issuance was prohibited to his principal in application of Article 85 first paragraph.

###### Article 87

All the acts punished by Articles 83 and 84 of this law are considered, for the application of the provisions concerning recidivism, as constituting the same offence.
In case of recidivism, the maximum penalty is incurred.

###### Article 88

On the occasion of criminal proceedings brought against the drawer, the holder who has constituted himself a civil party is entitled to claim before the judges of the public action a sum equal to the amount of the cheque, without prejudice, where applicable, to any damages. He may, nevertheless, if he prefers, bring an action for payment of his claim before the civil court.
In the absence of constitution as a civil party and if proof of payment of the cheque does not result from the elements of the proceedings, the judge of the public action may, even of his own motion, order the drawer to pay to the beneficiary, in addition to the costs of execution of the decision, a sum equal to the amount of the cheque, increased, where applicable, by interest from the day of presentment in accordance with Article 54 of this law and costs resulting from non-payment, when the cheque has not been endorsed except for collection purposes.
When the provisions of the preceding paragraph are applied, the judge issues to the beneficiary a copy of the decision in enforceable

form under the same conditions as a civil party duly constituted;

###### Article 89

Is liable to a fine of 100,000 F to
2,000,000 F the drawee who, without having complied with the provisions of Article 43 paragraph 3 of this law refuses payment of a cheque on the ground that the drawer has stopped payment on it.

###### Article 90

Is liable to a fine of 100,000 F to
3,000,000 F:
1°- The drawee who indicates a provision lower than the existing and available provision;
2°- The drawee who rejects a cheque for insufficiency or unavailability of the provision without indicating, when such is the case, that the cheque was issued in disregard of an injunction addressed in application of Article 74 or in violation of a prohibition pronounced in application of Article 85 first paragraph of this law;
3°- The drawee who has not declared, under the conditions provided for, payment incidents as well as the offences provided for by Articles 83 (1 0 to 60), 84 and 86 of this law;
4°- The drawee who contravenes the provisions of Articles 72, 74, 81 and 85 paragraph 2;
5°- The drawee who contravenes the provisions of Articles 2 and 4.

###### Article 91

When it has refused payment of a cheque, the drawee

must be in a position to justify that it has complied with the legal and regulatory requirements relating to the opening of the account and the issuance of cheque forms as well as the legal and regulatory obligations resulting from payment incidents, in particular with regard to the injunction to return the cheque forms.

###### Article 92

The drawee must pay, notwithstanding the absence,
insufficiency or unavailability of the provision, any cheque:
1°- Issued by means of a form of which it has not obtained the return under the conditions provided for in Article 74 of this law, unless it justifies that it has implemented the diligence provided for by that article;
2°- Issued by means of a form which it has issued in violation of the provisions of Articles 72 and 85 paragraphs 1 and 2 of this law.
The drawee who refuses payment of a cheque issued by means of one of the aforementioned forms is jointly and severally liable to pay, in addition to a sum equal to the amount of the cheque, the damages awarded to the beneficiary by reason of non-payment.

PRELIMINARY PROVISIONS

###### Article premier

Police penalties are - imprisonment;
- fine;
- and confiscation of certain seized objects.

###### Article 2

Imprisonment for a police petty offence may not be less than one day, nor exceed one month.
The month of imprisonment is thirty days.
The days of imprisonment are complete days of twenty-four hours.

###### Article 3

Fines for petty offences may be imposed from 200 francs up to 20,000 francs inclusive.

###### Article 4

Imprisonment for debt takes place for payment of the fine.

###### Article 5

In case of insufficiency of assets, restitutions and compensation due to the injured party take precedence over the fine.

###### Article 6

In the cases provided for by this Code or by particular laws and regulations, there shall or may be confiscated either the things seized in contravention, or the things produced by the contravention, or the materials or instruments which served or were intended to commit it.

###### Article 7

In specially provided cases, the courts may order that their decision be posted in very conspicuous characters, in the places they indicate, at the expense of the convicted person.
Unless otherwise provided, this posting shall be ordered for a period which may not exceed 15 days.
The removal, concealment and total or partial tearing of posters affixed in accordance with this article, carried out voluntarily, shall be punished by a fine of 500 francs to 18,000 francs and imprisonment of 1 day to

15 days at most or by only one of these two penalties; the posting shall be carried out again in full at the expense of the convicted person.

### TITLE ONE CONTRAVENTIONS AGAINST THE PUBLIC INTEREST

#### CHAPTER ONE PUBLIC SAFETY AND TRANQUILITY

###### Article 8

Shall be punished by the penalties provided for in Articles 2 and 3 or by only one of these two penalties:
1)	Those who have contravened decrees and orders legally made by the administrative authority or orders published by the municipal authority;
2)	Those who have neglected to maintain, repair or clean ovens, chimneys or factories where fire is used;
3)	Those who, being obliged to provide lighting, have neglected it and those who have neglected to clean streets or passages, in localities where this responsibility is left to the inhabitants;
4)	Those who have left in fields or public places instruments or weapons which may be misused by wrongdoers;
5)	Those who have thrown or exposed on the public highway things likely to cause harm by their fall or by unhealthy exhalations; those who have thrown hard objects or filth at persons, against the buildings

and enclosures of others, or into gardens or enclosures;
Innkeepers, hoteliers, lodging-house keepers or lessors of furnished houses who have neglected to record upon arrival, without any blank space, in a register kept regularly, the first names, names, occupations, usual domicile and date of entry of any person sleeping or spending all or part of the night in their houses, as well as, upon departure, the date of exit; those among them who have failed to produce this register at the times determined by the regulations, or when required to do so by mayors, deputies, commissioners or police officers, or by citizens appointed for this purpose; all this, without prejudice to the cases of liability mentioned in Article 54 of the Penal Code, relating to crimes or offences of those who, having lodged or stayed with them, have not been regularly recorded;
7)	Those who have established or held in streets, roads, squares or public places, lottery games or other games of chance;
8)	Those who have allowed to wander insane or furious persons under their care, or harmful or ferocious animals;
9)	Those who have accepted, held or used means of payment intended to supplement or replace monetary signs having legal tender;
10)	Those who have refused to receive national coins and currency, not counterfeit nor altered, according to the value for which they are legal tender;

11 ) Those who, being able to do so, have refused or neglected to perform work, service, or to provide assistance with which they have been required, in circumstances of accidents, tumults, shipwreck, flood, fire, or other calamities, as well as in cases of brigandage, pillage, flagrant offence, public outcry or judicial execution, subject to application where appropriate of the penalties provided for by Article 49 of the Penal Code and by the laws and regulations in force;
12)	Those who use weights and measures different from those established by the laws in force;
13)	The authors or accomplices of noises, disturbances or gatherings that are injurious or nocturnal, disturbing the tranquility of the inhabitants,
14)	Those who, without being duly authorized, have removed from the public domain turf, earth or stones, or who, in the national domain have removed earth or materials subject to rights of use;
15)	Those who have worn in public insignia, ribbons or rosettes presenting with those of decorations conferred by the State, a resemblance likely to cause confusion in the mind of the public;
16)	Those who, outside the cases provided for in Articles 185 and following of the Penal Code, have opposed, by acts, words, gestures, any maneuvers, or by any voluntary abstentions premeditated, repeated or concerted, the exercise of the legitimate authority of an agent depositary of the public force or of any citizen charged with a ministry of

public service, and have thereby undermined public order or hindered the proper functioning of administrative or judicial services;
17)	Those who, without authorization from
the administration, have by any process whatsoever, made inscriptions, traced signs or drawings on movable or immovable property of the domain of the State, territorial collectivities, or on property located on this domain, either with a view to enabling the execution of a public service, or because it is made available to the public;
18)	Those who have voluntarily diverted or unduly used water intended for irrigation by law or by regulatory provisions emanating from the administration or distribution organizations.

#### CHAPTER Il PUBLIC HYGIENE AND HEALTH

###### Article 9

Shall be punished by the penalties provided for in Articles 2 and 3 or by only one of these two penalties:
1)	Those who, without authorization or regular declaration, offer, put up for sale or expose for sale goods in public places in contravention of regulatory provisions on the policing of these places;
2)	Those who have exposed or caused to be exposed on the public highway or in public places posters or images contrary to decency. The judgment of conviction shall order,

notwithstanding all means of appeal, the suppression of the incriminated object or objects which, if not voluntary, shall be carried out ex officio and without delay at the expense of the convicted person;
3)	Those who, by gestures, words, writings or by any other means, publicly solicit persons, of either sex, with a view to provoking them to debauchery.

#### CHAPTER III ROADS AND TRAFFIC

###### Article 10

Shall be punished by the penalties provided for in Articles 2 and 3 or by only one of these two penalties:
1)	Those who have obstructed the public highway, by depositing or leaving there without necessity materials or any things which prevent or diminish the freedom or safety of passage;
2)	Those who have neglected to light materials stored by them or excavations made by them in streets and squares;

4)	Those who contravene the provisions of laws and regulations having as their object:
-	The solidity of public vehicles;
-	Their weight;
-	The mode of their loading;

-	The number and safety of passengers,
-	The indication, inside the vehicles, of the places they contain and the price of places;
-	The indication, on the outside, of the name of the owner.
5)	Those who have damaged, or deteriorated, in any manner whatsoever, public roads or encroached upon their width.

#### CHAPTER II DAMAGES TO ANIMALS

###### Article 12

Shall be punished by the penalties provided for in Articles 2 and 3 or by only one of these two penalties:
