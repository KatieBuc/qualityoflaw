To establish a set of measures aimed at preventing and combating domestic violence in Puerto Rico; to classify the crimes of Abuse, Aggravated Abuse, Abuse by Threat, Abuse by Restriction of Liberty and Spousal Sexual Assault and set penalties; to empower the courts to issue Protection Orders for victims of domestic violence and establish an easy and expeditious procedure for the processing and adjudication of said Orders; to establish measures aimed at the prevention of domestic violence and order the Commission for Women's Affairs [Note: Replaced by the Office of the Women's Advocate] to disseminate and educate the community about the scope of this law and to allocate funds.

STATEMENT OF MOTIVES

Domestic violence is an antisocial behavior that constitutes a serious problem for the Puerto Rican family. It involves the physical and emotional abuse suffered by a person at the hands of their spouse or ex-spouse, or at the hands of a person with whom they maintain or have maintained an intimate relationship. Although both men and women are usually the victims of the aggressive and violent conduct that we call spousal abuse. The National Crime Survey, (National Crime Survey Bureau of Justice Statistics, Special Report, August 1986, pages 1-5), conducted a study between the years 1978 to 1982 on this matter and found that in the United States an estimated 2.1 million women were victims of spousal abuse at least once in an average period of twelve (12) months. Researchers estimate that in Puerto Rico 60% of married women are victims of spousal abuse.
Although the majority of domestic violence incidents are not reported to law enforcement agencies, Police statistics reflect alarming trends. In 1983, 81% of murders or homicides against women involved a family or friendship relationship, in 1984 that percentage decreased to 58%, but in 1985 it rose to 64%. The police do not have this data available for subsequent years, but it is a reality that incidents both in frequency and intensity and that are often transmitted from one generation to another.
Researchers maintain that children who suffer from domestic violence or who come from homes where domestic violence incidents occur carry with them throughout their lives the mark and patterns of violence. Tolerating domestic violence today contributes to the disintegration of the family, to fostering criminality and to the weakening of the values of human coexistence.
Domestic violence is one of the most complex criminal acts facing our society. Aware of its difficulties and effects, in the last six years the Legislative Assembly has been evaluating different legislative initiatives to address this problem and seek viable alternatives to eradicate this conduct, which are not necessarily limited to

establishing certain punishments for the offender. Throughout these years, various reports, statistics, true cases, psychological evaluations of people affected by domestic violence and the recommendations of professionals have been studied, which guided us to the adoption of comprehensive legislation that also includes aspects of prevention through education, guidance and protection.
There is no doubt that to confront this problem effectively, the will to unite efforts and purposes among the public sector, the private sector, the police, the courts, helping professionals and the community in general is required. It is essential that we confront this problem by focusing our attention on its violent and criminal nature and that we design measures aimed at aggressors and protection measures for victims. The novel aspect of this law rests on the power granted to the Judges of the Court of First Instance and Municipal Judges to issue affirmative protection measures for victims through the issuance of orders directed at the aggressor to refrain from engaging in certain conduct with respect to the victim. Therefore, this law specifies and establishes a simple and agile process for obtaining civil remedies through Protection Orders. This procedure has its root in the interest of the Legislative Assembly to address the needs of victims of domestic violence. Its purpose is to expedite the process for the attention and immediate solution of controversies generated in the home where violence prevails and to address the protection claims that victims of domestic violence present to the State. Self-help elements are also incorporated aimed at encouraging the victim of domestic violence to seek provisional and immediate legal remedies on their own, which will contribute to the development of feelings of self-esteem and self-affirmation in people who find themselves in situations of abuse.
This law, in addition, classifies the crime of abuse in various modalities and imposes penalties for its commission, which are greater in case of recidivism and when aggravating circumstances exist. It also establishes measures for police intervention, such as mandatory arrest, the responsibility to provide assistance to the victim and to collect information on domestic violence.
This law comprehensively includes different measures to address the fundamental areas that require immediate solution to execute the public policy of combating criminality and providing alternatives of hope to the Puerto Rican family.

### CHAPTER I. — GENERAL PROVISIONS

#### Article 1

1 — Title. (8 L.P.R.A.

#### § 601 note)

This law shall be known as the "Law for the Prevention and Intervention with Domestic Violence".

#### Article 1

2 — Public Policy. (8 L.P.R.A.

#### § 601)

The Government of the Commonwealth of Puerto Rico recognizes that domestic violence is one of the most serious and complex problems confronting our society. Domestic violence lacerates the integrity and dignity of every victim, regardless of sex, marital status, sexual orientation, gender identity or immigration status of any of the persons involved in the relationship. In developing policy on this matter, we must emphasize addressing the difficulties that situations of domestic violence present for every victim, particularly women and minors, to preserve their physical and emotional integrity, ensure their safety and save their lives.
Domestic violence is one of the most critical manifestations of the effects of inequity in relationships between men and women. The inequity that motivates domestic violence manifests itself in consensual couple relationships, regardless of sex, marital status, sexual orientation, gender identity or immigration status of any of the persons involved in the relationship. Discriminatory ideas, attitudes and conduct also permeate the social institutions called upon to resolve and prevent the problem of domestic violence and its consequences. The efforts of these institutions toward the identification, understanding and attention to the same have been limited and at times inadequate.
The Government of the Commonwealth of Puerto Rico reaffirms its constitutional commitment to protect the life, safety and dignity of men and women, regardless of their sex, marital status, sexual orientation, gender identity or immigration status. Furthermore, it recognizes that domestic violence threatens the very integrity of the person, of their family and of the members thereof and constitutes a serious threat to the stability and preservation of the civilized coexistence of our people.
As public policy, the Government of the Commonwealth of Puerto Rico energetically repudiates domestic violence as being contrary to the values of peace, dignity and respect that this people wishes to maintain for individuals, families and the community in general. Through this public policy, the development, establishment and strengthening of effective remedies is promoted to offer protection and assistance to victims, alternatives for the rehabilitation of offenders and strategies for the prevention of domestic violence.

#### Artículo 1

3. — Definitions. (8 L.P.R.A.

#### § 602)

For the purposes of this law, the following terms shall have the meaning expressed below:
(a) Law enforcement agent. — Means any member or officer of the Puerto Rico Police Corps or a municipal police officer duly trained and accredited by the State Police Department.
(b) Shelter. — Means any institution whose primary function is to provide protection, security, support services and temporary lodging to the surviving victim of domestic violence and their daughters and sons. This definition shall not apply to the term "sheltered," as used in subsection (a) of Article 3.2 of this law. For purposes of said subsection, the term "sheltered" shall be understood in its common and ordinary meaning.
(c) Sheltered. — Means that person who is a surviving victim of domestic violence who resides temporarily in a shelter as defined in this law.
(d) Cohabit. — Means to maintain a consensual couple relationship similar to that of spouses with respect to the aspect of cohabitation, regardless of sex, marital status, sexual orientation, gender identity or immigration status of any of the persons involved in the couple relationship.
(e) Employee. — Means any person who provides service to any person, partnership or corporation that employs one or more persons under any express or implied, oral or written contract for services, expressly including among these those whose labor is of an incidental nature.
(f) Serious emotional harm. — Means and arises when, as a result of domestic violence, there is evidence that the person manifests recurrently one or more of the following characteristics: paralyzing fear, feelings of helplessness or hopelessness, feelings of frustration and failure, feelings of insecurity, defenselessness, isolation, weakened self-esteem or other similar conduct, when it is the product of repeated acts or omissions.
(g) Advocate. — Means any person who has accredited training or studies in the area of counseling, guidance, psychology, social work or legal advocacy, who is certified by the Office of the Women's Advocate or by a private non-profit entity authorized to issue such certifications according to the regulations approved by the Office of the Women's Advocate for those purposes.
(h) Intimidation. — Means any action or word that, manifested recurrently, has the effect of exercising moral pressure on the mind of a person, who, for fear of suffering some physical or emotional harm to their person, their property or to the person of another, or mistreatment of companion animals or pets of the victim or of the children of the victim or of the perpetrator, is forced to carry out an act contrary to their will. When intimidation is committed that causes the person to fear suffering mistreatment of an animal or pet, the animal mistreatment referred to in this subsection shall be defined in accordance with the definition of animal mistreatment provided in Article 2(n) of Law 154-2008, as amended.
(i) Protection order. — Means any mandate issued in writing under the seal of a court, in which measures are ordered to an aggressor to refrain from engaging in or carrying out certain acts or conduct constituting domestic violence.

(j) Employer. — Means any natural or juridical person who employs one or more employees, workers; and the chief, official, manager, officer, agent, administrator, superintendent, foreman, steward, agent or representative of said natural or juridical person.
(k) Stalking. — Means to keep a person under constant or frequent surveillance with one's presence in places immediately or relatively close to the home, residence, school, work or vehicle in which the person is located, in order to instill fear or dread in the mind of a prudent and reasonable person.
(l) Respondent. — Means any person against whom a protection order is requested.
(m) Petitioner. — Means any person eighteen (18) years of age or older who requests that a court issue a protection order.
(n) Couple relationship. — Means the relationship between spouses, ex-spouses, persons who cohabit or have cohabited, those who maintain or have maintained a consensual relationship and those who have procreated a son or daughter with each other, regardless of sex, marital status, sexual orientation, gender identity or immigration status of any of the persons involved in the relationship.
(o) Sexual relation. — Means any sexual penetration, whether vaginal, anal, orogenital, digital or instrumental.
(p) Court. — Means the Court of First Instance of the General Court of Justice and the offices of the municipal judges.
(q) Cyber or digital violence. — Means that psychological violence as defined in subsection (s), where any type of electronic or digital communication is used, through text messages, voice mail, emails or social networks, or any other digital means, including satellite tracking systems, that has the effect of harassing, stalking, intimidating, threatening or distressing a person with whom one maintains or has maintained a couple relationship.
(r) Domestic violence. — Means the use of physical force or psychological violence, intimidation or stalking or economic violence against a person by their spouse, ex-spouse, a person with whom they cohabit or have cohabited, with whom they maintain or have maintained a consensual relationship or a person with whom a daughter or son has been procreated, regardless of sex, marital status, sexual orientation, gender identity or immigration status of any of the persons involved in the relationship, to cause physical harm to their person, their property or to the person of another or to cause serious emotional harm.
(s) Psychological violence. — Means that conduct exercised in dishonor, discredit or contempt of personal worth, unreasonable limitation of access to and management of common property, blackmail, surveillance, stalking, isolation, deprivation of access to adequate food or rest, threats to deprive of custody of sons or daughters, or destruction of objects cherished by the person, except those that belong privately to the offender.
(s) Economic violence. — Means that conduct exercised with the purpose of undermining present or future financial capacity, economic stability or housing and dwelling security through threats, coercion, fraud with restriction or deprivation of access to or use of accounts, assets, financial information, identification or credit cards, money or government assistance, concealment of information related to payment of rent or mortgages, or forced evictions; exercise of undue influence on the decisions or behavior or financial and economic decisions of a person, or interference in the work relationship or performance of a person or in their own business. It also includes the improper use of the person's economic resources, including money, assets and credit for one's own benefit, and preventing access to formal courses of study or impairing the academic performance of the victim.

### CAPITULO II. — ORDENES DE PROTECCION Y ASPECTOS PROCESALES

#### Artículo 2

1 — Ordenes de Protección. (8 L.P.R.A.

#### § 621)

Any person, eighteen (18) years of age or older, who has been a victim of domestic violence or conduct constituting a crime, as defined in this Law or in the Penal Code of the Commonwealth of Puerto Rico [sic] or in any other special law, in the context of a couple relationship, as defined by subsection (m) of Article 1.3 of this Law, may file on their own behalf, through their legal representative or through a law enforcement officer a petition in the Court and request a protection order, without the prior filing of a complaint or accusation being necessary.
When the court so determines or issues a protection or stalking order, the court shall immediately order the respondent party to surrender to the Puerto Rico Police for custody, any firearm belonging to the respondent and for which a license to have or possess, or to carry, or for target shooting, hunting or of any type has been issued, as the case may be. The order to surrender any firearm as well as the suspension of any type of firearm license shall be enforced compulsorily. Likewise, upon the issuance of such order by a court, said ruling shall have the effect of suspending the license to possess or carry any firearm including of any type, such as but not limited to, target shooting, hunting or of any type even when it forms part of the professional performance of the accused. Said restriction shall apply for a minimum of the same period of time for which the order extends. Any violation of the terms of the protection order, resulting in a conviction, shall entail the permanent revocation of any type of weapons license that the respondent possessed, and the confiscation of the weapons belonging to them shall proceed. The objective of this statute is to eliminate the possibility that the accused may use any firearm to cause bodily harm, threat or intimidation to the petitioner or to the members of their family unit.
(a)	Award provisional custody of the minor children of the petitioning party.
(b)	Suspend all filial relations with respect to the minor children of the respondent party, when the petitioning party is sheltered. To make such determination the court must consider the following elements:
(1)	The capacity of the shelter to provide security for the persons involved in the filial relations process.
(2)	That the shelter has the necessary resources for the transportation of the minors to the filial relations.
(3)	The distance between the shelter and the place where the filial relations will take place.

(4)	The dangerousness that the respondent party represents, if any, for the persons involved in the filial relations process: children, shelter personnel and the mother.
(5)	The presence of a resource approved by the petitioning party as intermediary in the filial relations.
(6)	That the respondent party has not engaged in conduct constituting domestic violence in the presence of the minors as established in Article 3 of this law.
(7)	That there is no protection order in favor of the minors against the respondent party.
(8)	The duration of the pattern of domestic violence.
(9)	The time elapsed since the last contact with the minors and the person requesting the parental-filial relations.
(10)	The quality of the relationship of the minors with the respondent party.
(11)	Whether the respondent party has failed to comply with any protection order.
(12)	Whether the respondent party has engaged in threatening conduct against the shelter personnel.
(13)	Whether the respondent party has verbally, physically or emotionally assaulted the minors.
(14)	Whether the respondent party has affected the emotional health of the minors.
Should any of the elements described in this subsection not be present, the court, based on the best welfare of the minor, shall make any other determination based on Articles 50, 51 and 52 of the Law for the Welfare and Comprehensive Protection of Children [Note: Repealed by Law 246-2011; repealed and replaced by Law 57-2023]
(c)	Order the respondent party to vacate the residence shared with the petitioning party, regardless of the right claimed over it.
(d)	Order the respondent party to refrain from bothering, harassing, pursuing, intimidating, threatening or in any other way interfering with the exercise of provisional custody over the minors that has been awarded to one of them.
(e)	Order the respondent party to refrain from entering any place where the petitioning party is located, when at the court's discretion such limitation is necessary to prevent the respondent party from bothering, intimidating, threatening or in any other way interfering with the petitioning party or with the minors whose provisional custody has been awarded to them.
(f)	Order the respondent party to pay support for the minors when custody of them has been awarded to the petitioning party, or for the minors and the petitioning party when there exists a legal obligation to do so.
(g)	Prohibit the respondent party from hiding or removing from the jurisdiction the minor children of the parties.
(h)	Prohibit the respondent party from disposing in any manner of the separate property of the petitioning party or the property of the community property or the community of property, when they exist. Provided, that when it concerns acts of administration of business, commerce or industry, the party against whom the order is issued must submit a monthly financial report to the court of their administrative actions.
(i)	Order any provisional measures regarding the possession and use of the parties' residence and regarding those movable property enumerated and included in subsections (1), (2), (3),

(4), (4)(a), (5) and (6) of Article 249 of the Code of Civil Procedure, as amended (32 L.P.R.A. sec. 1130) which establishes properties exempt from execution.
(j)	Order the respondent party to pay economic compensation from their separate estate for damages caused by conduct constituting domestic violence. Said compensation may include, but shall not be limited to compensation for moving expenses, expenses for property repairs, legal expenses, medical, psychiatric, psychological, counseling, guidance, lodging, shelter and other similar expenses, without prejudice to other civil actions to which the petitioning party is entitled.
(k)	Order that payments of rent or mortgage installments for the principal residence continue during the validity of the Order.
(l)	Order the respondent party to refrain from bothering, harassing, pursuing, intimidating, threatening or in any other way interfering with the work activity of the petitioning party, including those actions or expressions directed at damaging the reputation and professional stability of the petitioning party.
(m)	Order that all financial information be shared from those accounts or finances in which the petitioning party or their dependents may have an interest, including keeping them accurately informed about communications, actions and claims related to accounts receivable, mortgages, rents, or about administrative or judicial actions in execution of any type of debt; notifications about government assistance, or any other related information.
(n)	Order the respondent party to refrain from improperly using the economic resources of the petitioner, including their money, property and credit information to the detriment of the petitioner.
(o)	Issue any order necessary to fulfill the purposes and public policy of this law.

#### Article 2

1-A. — Prohibition of Reciprocal Protection Orders. (8 L.P.R.A.

#### § 621a)

The court may not issue reciprocal protection orders to the parties, unless each:
(a) Has filed an independent petition requesting a protection order against the other party;
(b) has been notified of the petition filed by the other party;
(c) demonstrates in an evidentiary hearing that the other party engaged in conduct constituting domestic violence, and
(d) demonstrates that the domestic violence did not occur in self-defense.

#### Article 2

1-B. — Automatic Issuance. (8 L.P.R.A.

#### § 6

21b) [Note: Law 95-2023 added this new Article]
When a court determines that there is cause for arrest based on a complaint in a criminal proceeding under Rule 6 of the Rules of Criminal Procedure, as amended, for any offense typified within this Law, and the accused is a repeat offender for violations of any of the offenses typified in this Law, it shall, without any additional proceeding, issue a protection order in favor of the victim for a period of validity that shall not be less than one (1) year and that may be extended at the discretion of a court and with the consent of the victim. In cases where it is the accused's first offense under any of the offenses typified in this law, the court shall issue a protection order in favor of the victim for a period of validity that shall not be less than six (6) months and that may be extended at the discretion of a court and with the consent of the victim, provided that the victim so requests or that the court deems it necessary with the consent of the victim.
Notwithstanding, before issuing the protection order established in this Article, in either of the two foregoing instances, the court shall explain to the victim their right to be issued a protection order in that same criminal proceeding, and their right to refuse the same, which must be expressed in open court and under oath. The court, before accepting a waiver of the protection order, shall ascertain that the victim is capable of making that decision, in a free, conscious and voluntary manner. The court shall have discretion to reject the waiver of the issuance of the protection order and consequently shall issue the same in accordance with the provisions of this Article.

#### Article 2

2 — Jurisdiction. (8 L.P.R.A.

#### § 622)

Any judge of the Court of First Instance or municipal judge may issue a protection order pursuant to this law. Any protection order may be reviewed, in appropriate cases, in any court of superior hierarchy and in those pertinent instances in the Family Relations Courts.

#### Article 2

3 — Procedure. (8 L.P.R.A.

#### § 623)

Any person of legal age, eighteen (18) years of age or older, may request the civil remedies established by this Article for themselves, or on behalf of any other person when the latter suffers from physical or mental incapacity, in case of emergency or when the person is prevented from requesting it for themselves.
The civil remedies established by this Article may also be requested by parents, mothers and sons or daughters of legal age, on behalf of their sons or daughters and mothers or fathers who are or have been victims of domestic violence or of conduct constituting an offense as typified in this Law. In these cases, the parents, mothers and the sons or daughters of legal age, must have witnessed the acts of domestic violence; or that the victim has confided or revealed to them that they have been a victim of acts constituting domestic violence. Such request must be under oath and must include that the petitioner informed the victim, prior to the commencement of the petition process, of their intention to request the protection order on their behalf.
An employer may request a protection order on behalf of employees, visitors and any other person who is in their workplace if one of their employees is or has been a victim of domestic violence or of conduct constituting domestic violence has occurred in the workplace.

Before initiating this proceeding, the employer must notify of their intention to request the protection order to the employee who is or has been a victim of domestic violence or of conduct constituting an offense as typified in this law.
The right to request the remedies established herein shall not be affected because the petitioning party has abandoned their residence to avoid domestic violence.
(a) Commencement of action. — A proceeding to obtain a protection order may be commenced:
(1) By filing a verbal or written petition; or
(2) within any pending case between the parties, or
(3) at the request of the Prosecuting Attorney in a criminal proceeding, or as a condition for probation or parole.
To facilitate for interested persons the process of obtaining a protection order under this law, the Court Administration shall have available in the Clerk's Office of the Courts of Puerto Rico and in the offices of municipal judges simple forms, to request and process said order. Likewise, it shall provide them with the necessary assistance and guidance to complete and file them.

#### Article 2

4 — Notice. (8 L.P.R.A.

#### § 624)

(a) Once a petition for a protection order is filed in accordance with the provisions of this law, the court shall issue a summons to the parties under penalty of contempt, for an appearance within a term that shall not exceed five (5) days.
(b) Notice of the summonses and copy of the petition shall be made in accordance with the Rules of Civil Procedure of Puerto Rico (32 L.P.R.A. App. III), and shall be served by a court marshal or by any other public law enforcement officer as soon as possible and shall take preference over other types of summons, except those of similar nature. The court shall maintain a file for each case in which every summons issued under this law shall be recorded.
(c) The failure to appear of a person duly summoned under this law shall be punishable as contempt of the court that issued the summons.
(d) When the petition is filed, notice thereof shall be effected in accordance with the provisions of the Rules of Civil Procedure of Puerto Rico (32 L.P.R.A. App. III).
(e) At the request of the petitioning party the court may order that delivery of the summons be effected by any person over 18 years of age who is not a party to the case.

#### Article 2

5 — Ex Parte Orders. (8 L.P.R.A.

#### § 625)

Notwithstanding the provisions of other legal provisions, the court may issue a protection order on an ex parte basis if it determines that:
(a) Diligent efforts have been made to notify the respondent party with a copy of the summons issued by the court and of the petition that has been filed with the court and have been unsuccessful; or
(b) there is a likelihood that giving prior notice to the respondent party will cause the irreparable harm that is intended to be prevented by requesting the protection order, or

(c) when the petitioning party demonstrates that there is a substantial likelihood of immediate risk of abuse.
Whenever the court issues a protection order on an ex parte basis, it shall do so on a provisional basis, shall immediately notify, and within a term that may not exceed forty-eight (48) hours, the respondent party, with a copy thereof or in any other manner, and shall provide an opportunity to oppose it. For these purposes, it shall schedule a hearing to be held within the next twenty (20) days after said ex parte order has been issued, unless the respondent party requests an extension for such purpose. During this hearing the court may vacate the order or extend the effects thereof for the term it deems necessary. Failure to serve the order within the forty-eight (48) hour term established herein shall not have the consequence of rendering said order without effect.

#### Artículo 2

6 — Content of Protection Orders. (8 L.P.R.A.

#### § 626)

(a) Every protection order must specifically state the court's determinations, the remedies ordered, and the period of validity.
(b) Every protection order must state the date and time it was issued and expressly notify the parties that any violation thereof shall constitute contempt of court, which could result in a penalty of imprisonment, fine, or both.
(c) Any protection order of an ex parte nature must include the date and time it was issued, as well as the term of validity thereof. In addition, it must indicate the date, time, and place at which the hearing for the extension or annulment thereof will be held and the reasons why it was necessary to issue said ex parte order.
(d) The Court shall deliver the number of copies of the Protection Order requested by the victim, up to a maximum of five (5).
(e) Together with every protection order, the Court shall include a guide of recommendations on precautionary measures that the victim of domestic violence should take to achieve greater effectiveness thereof. This guide must include, among others, the following recommendations:
(1) An orientation to the victim to notify and provide a copy of the Protection Order, as well as a photo of the aggressor or the person against whom the protection order is issued, in the following places:
A. at the State and Municipal Police station nearest to his or her residence.
B. at the access-controlled entrances of his or her community or urbanization, so that they can identify the aggressor or the person against whom a protection order is issued;
C. to his or her immediate neighbors;
D. at his or her place of employment, so that the security guards in the work area are aware of the order issued;
E. at his or her children's school, so that they do not summon the defendant or parent against whom the order was issued simultaneously with the victim.
(2) In addition, the petitioning party shall be advised that he or she must at all times:
A. Keep a copy of the Protection Order with him or her;

B. Immediately report to the Police any violation of the Protection Order;
C. Never allow the aggressor or the person against whom the protection order is issued to enter his or her residence.
D. Not accept summons made by the aggressor or the person against whom a protection order is issued, or any other person whom the victim knows to be related to his or her aggressor, to any place, whether private or public.
E. Not accept telephone calls, nor answer messages through instant messaging programs, nor through social networks via the Internet, or by any other method of communication from the aggressor or the person against whom a protection order is issued, or from any other person whom the victim knows to be related to his or her aggressor.
F. Walk with caution and always try to be accompanied in public places and in parking lots when returning to his or her motor vehicle.
G. If he or she becomes aware that the party or the person against whom a protection order is issued is following him or her, he or she should go to the nearest Police station or any safe place and report to the Police.
(f) The Court shall have discretion, after having heard the evidence presented to it or at the request of the Public Prosecutor, to impose as an additional condition to the request for the Protection Order, that the respondent participate on a compulsory basis in a program or workshop of education, whether public or private, on the scope of this Law. This, to prevent conduct constituting a crime of domestic violence from being committed and to raise awareness about the harmful effect thereof on the family. The Court shall order and establish the same as part of the provisions to be complied with when it grants the Protection Order. Said program or workshop must be taken within the period of validity of the Order. The term of the program shall not be less than thirty (30) hours. In addition, the respondent party must evidence to the Court, within a term of three (3) working days from the date on which he or she was notified of the issuance of the Protection Order against him or her, that he or she enrolled in a program or workshop for this purpose. Upon expiration of the Order, the respondent party must present evidence to the Court of his or her compliance with said program or workshop.
Provided, that having elapsed the period of validity of the Protection Order, without the respondent party having notified and evidenced to the Court compliance with this provision, the respondent party may be found to be in contempt for noncompliance with the provisions of the protection order. In cases where the respondent has been subject to more than one (1) Protection Order against him or her, with the same or any petitioner, and that fact is known or brought to the attention of the Court, the Court shall order enrollment in the program or workshop on domestic violence on a mandatory basis.
The Court shall impose on the respondent party payment of the costs of the program or workshop, if any. When the respondent party demonstrates his or her inability to pay the cost of the program or workshop, the respondent party shall be subject to hours of community service as payment for the cost of the program or workshop.

The programs or workshops of education on the scope of the protection order, as well as on all conduct constituting domestic violence and the harmful effect on the family, among other topics, must be reviewed and developed in coordination with the Office of the Women's Advocate and the Regulatory Board of Reeducation and Retraining Programs for Aggressors.

#### Artículo 2

7 — Notification to the Parties and the Public Order and Child Welfare Agencies. (8 L.P.R.A.

#### § 627)

(a) A copy of every protection order shall be filed in the clerk's office of the court that issues it. The clerk of the court shall provide a copy thereof, upon request of the parties or any interested persons.
(b) Any order issued under this law shall be personally served on the respondent party, either through a court bailiff, a law enforcement officer, any person over 18 years of age who is not a party to the case, or in accordance with the procedure established in the Rules of Civil Procedure, (32 L.P.R.A. App. III).
(c) After the order has been served on the respondent party, a bailiff of the Court from which it is granted shall have a time period of no more than twenty-four (24) hours to inform, personally, the petitioning party that such service has been effected.
(d) The clerk of the court shall send a copy of the orders issued under this Law to the Police unit responsible for maintaining a file of protection orders so issued. Likewise, the clerk shall send a copy of said orders issued so that they may be entered into the Electronic Archive of Protection Orders, in accordance with the procedures established in Law No. 420-2000, known as the "Protection Orders Archive Law." Pursuant to Law No. 420-2000, the Police shall include all information contained in the protection order, as well as procedural incidents in the notification of the parties and agencies involved.
(e) The Puerto Rico Police shall offer adequate protection to the party for whose benefit a protection order is issued.
(f) The clerk of the court shall send to the Child Support Administration of the Department of the Family a copy of protection orders that provide for the payment of child support for a minor, in accordance with the provisions of subsection (e) of Article 2.1 of this law.
(g) The Clerk of the Court shall send a copy of the protection orders issued under this Law to the Domestic Violence Divisions of the Police of the jurisdiction where the petitioning party resides, to the employer of the petitioner as informed by the latter; and to the security company in charge of access control at the petitioner's residence, if applicable. These shall inform the Puerto Rico Police of any violation of the order issued.
Any natural or legal person who, by itself or through its agents, representatives or employees, fails to comply with the provisions of this subsection, shall be punished with a fine of two hundred fifty dollars ($250.00).

(h) The Clerk of the Court shall send a copy of the protection orders issued under this Law to the Parole Board, when the aggressor party is under the jurisdiction of said Board.
(i) The Clerk of the Court shall send a copy of the protection orders issued under this Law to the relatives and/or persons, natural or legal, that the victim, after orientation, determines freely and voluntarily to be notified. These relatives and/or persons, natural or legal, shall be notified by mail, email or any other means, to the address provided by the victim.

#### Article 2

8 — Noncompliance with Protection Orders. (8 L.P.R.A.

#### § 628)

Any knowing violation of a protection order issued in accordance with this Law shall be punished as a third degree felony in its lower half, providing that the courts shall be obligated to impose electronic monitoring if any type of suspended sentence is granted.
Notwithstanding the provisions of Rule 11 of the Rules of Criminal Procedure, as amended, even if there is no order to that effect, every law enforcement officer shall make an arrest if presented with a protection order issued under this Law or a similar law against the person to be arrested; or if he or she determines that such an order exists through communication with the pertinent authorities, the petitioner's employer, or the security company in charge of access control where the petitioner resides and has founded reasons to believe that the provisions thereof have been violated.

#### Article 2

9 — Social Work Evaluation. (8 L.P.R.A.

#### § 629)

In every case in which a protection order is issued, and from the evidence presented at the hearing, it appears that any or all of the children of the parties witnessed and/or perceived the act of abuse, the court may refer the case to the Department of the Family, so that the person charged with abuse may be referred and attend a social work evaluation, to determine if any type of psychological assistance is required that tends toward the protection of the sons or daughters.
The court may summon the charged party to a follow-up hearing to corroborate that he or she attended the Department of the Family and submitted to the social work evaluation. The Department of the Family shall issue a report on the social work evaluation, in which any type of psychological assistance to the charged party may be recommended.
If the charged party does not comply with the referral, it shall be considered that he or she has violated the protection order.

### CHAPTER III

CRIMINAL CONDUCT; PENALTIES AND OTHER MEASURES

#### Article 3

1 — Abuse. (8 L.P.R.A.

#### § 631)

Any person who employs physical force or psychological or economic violence, intimidation or persecution upon the person of his or her spouse, ex-spouse, or the person with whom he or she cohabits or has cohabited, or the person with whom he or she maintains or has maintained a consensual relationship, or the person with whom he or she has procreated a son or daughter, regardless of the sex, marital status, sexual orientation, gender identity or immigration status of any of the persons involved in the relationship, to cause physical harm to his or her person, to the companion animal or pet of the victim, of the children or of the victimizer, to the property valued by the latter, except those that belong privately to the offender, or to the person of another or to cause serious emotional harm, shall incur a fourth degree felony in its upper half. Proof of a pattern of conduct shall not be necessary for the crime of abuse to be constituted. The court may impose the penalty of restitution, in addition to the established penalty of imprisonment.
Psychological violence shall also occur when any type of electronic or digital communication is used, through text messages, voice mail, emails, or social networks, or any other digital means, including satellite tracking systems, that has the effect of harassing, persecuting, intimidating, or distressing a person with whom a partner relationship is or has been maintained, or the person with whom one cohabits or has cohabited. For psychological violence through digital or cyber violence to be constituted, proof of a pattern of conduct shall not be necessary.

#### Article 3

2 — Aggravated Abuse. (8 L.P.R.A.

#### § 632)

A penalty corresponding to a third-degree felony in its lower half shall be imposed when, against the person of a spouse, ex-spouse, or person with whom one cohabits or has cohabited, or with whom one maintains or has maintained a consensual relationship, or with whom one has procreated a son or daughter, regardless of the sex, marital status, sexual orientation, gender identity, or immigration status of any of the persons involved in the relationship, abuse as defined in this Law is committed, with one or more of the following circumstances:
(a)	Entry is made into the person's dwelling or the place where they are sheltered and abuse is committed there, in the case of spouses or cohabitants, regardless of the sex, marital status, sexual orientation, gender identity, or immigration status of any of the persons involved in the relationship, when they are separated or there is a protection order ordering the eviction from the residence of one of the parties; or
(b)	when serious bodily harm is inflicted on the person; or
(c)	when committed with a deadly weapon in circumstances that do not involve the intent to kill or mutilate; or
(d)	when committed in the presence of minors; or

(e)	when committed after a protection order or resolution against the accused person has been issued in aid of the victim of abuse; or
(f)	the person is induced, incited, or compelled to take controlled substances, or any other substance or means that alters the person's will, or to become intoxicated with intoxicating beverages;
(g)	when committed and simultaneously abuse of a minor as defined in Law No. 177 of August 1, 2003 [Note: Repealed by Law 246-2011; repealed and replaced by Law 57-2023] is committed, or
(h)	if the victim is compelled or induced through abuse, physical or psychological violence to participate or become involved in an unwanted sexual relationship with third parties.
(i)	When committed against a pregnant woman.
(j)	When committed against a person under sixteen (16) years of age and the aggressor is eighteen (18) years of age or older.
(k)	When committed and the person purposely, knowingly, willfully, or recklessly engages in torture or kills a companion animal or pet of the victim or of the victim's or victimizer's children.
The court may impose the penalty of restitution in addition to the established penalty of imprisonment.

#### Artículo 3

3 — Abuse by Threat. (8 L.P.R.A.

#### § 633)

Any person who threatens to cause harm to their spouse, ex-spouse, the person with whom they cohabit or have cohabited, or with whom they maintain or have maintained a consensual relationship, or the person with whom they have procreated a son or daughter, regardless of the sex, marital status, sexual orientation, gender identity, or immigration status of any of the persons involved in the relationship, to property valued by them, except that which belongs privately to the offender, or to the person of another, shall commit a fourth-degree felony in its upper half. The court may impose the penalty of restitution in addition to the established penalty of imprisonment.
The threat shall also occur when any type of electronic or digital communication is used, through text messages, voice mail, emails or social networks, or any digital means.

#### Artículo 3

4 — Abuse by Restriction of Liberty. (8 L.P.R.A.

#### § 634)

Any person who uses violence or intimidation against the person of their spouse, ex-spouse, the person with whom they cohabit or have cohabited, or with whom they maintain or have maintained a consensual relationship, or the person with whom they have procreated a son or daughter, regardless of the sex, marital status, sexual orientation, gender identity, or immigration status of any of the persons involved in the relationship, or who uses the pretext that they suffer or that one of the aforementioned persons suffers from mental illness or defect, to restrict their liberty with the victim's knowledge, shall commit a third-degree felony in its lower half.
The court may establish the penalty of restitution in addition to the established penalty of imprisonment.

#### Artículo 3

5 — Spousal Sexual Assault. (8 L.P.R..

#### § 635)

A penalty of imprisonment shall be imposed, as provided below, on any person who engages in non-consensual sexual relations with their spouse or ex-spouse, or with the person with whom they cohabit or have cohabited, or with whom they maintain or have maintained a consensual relationship, or the person with whom they have procreated a son or daughter, regardless of the sex, marital status, sexual orientation, gender identity, or immigration status of any of the persons involved in the relationship, in any of the following circumstances:
(a)	If they have been compelled to engage in sexual relations through the use of force, violence, intimidation, or threat of serious and imminent bodily harm; or
(b)	if their capacity to consent has been annulled or substantially diminished, without their knowledge or without their consent, through hypnotic, narcotic, depressant or stimulant means or substances or similar means; or
(c)	if due to mental illness or incapacity, temporary or permanent, the victim is incapacitated to understand the nature of the act at the time of its commission; or
(d)	if they are compelled or induced through abuse, physical or psychological violence to participate or become involved in an unwanted sexual relationship with third parties.
The crime of spousal sexual assault does not prescribe when the victim is under 18 years of age, and the accused is over 18 years of age at the time of commission of the crime. The penalty to be imposed for this crime, in all its forms, shall be that corresponding to a severe second-degree felony.
The court may impose the penalty of restitution in addition to the penalty of imprisonment established in any of the aforementioned forms.

#### Artículo 3

6 — Diversion of Procedure. (8 L.P.R.A.

#### § 636)

Once the trial has been held and the accused has been convicted or has entered a plea of guilty to any of the offenses defined in this Law, the Court may motu proprio or upon request of the Prosecutor or the defense, suspend all proceedings and place the convicted person on probation, subject to their participation in a reeducation and retraining program for persons who engage in abusive conduct in intimate partner relationships, as defined by subsection (m) of Article 1.3 of this Law. Before making any determination in this regard, the Court shall hear the Prosecutor.
This diversion alternative shall only be available when the following circumstances exist:
(a)	The person has not been convicted and incarcerated as a result of a final and firm sentence or is not currently enjoying the benefit of a diversion program under this Law or a suspended sentence, for the commission of offenses established in this Law or similar offenses established in the Laws of the Commonwealth of Puerto Rico or the United States against the person of their spouse, ex-spouse, person with whom they cohabit or have cohabited, person with whom they maintain or have maintained a consensual relationship, or person with whom they have procreated a son or daughter, regardless of

sex, marital status, sexual orientation, gender identity or immigration status of any of the persons involved in the relationship.
(b)	The person has not violated a protection order issued by any court under this law or any similar legal provision.
(c)	An agreement is executed among the Prosecutor, the accused and the agency, organization, public or private institution to which the accused will be referred.
(d)	As part of the agreement and participation in the reeducation program, the person submits a statement accepting responsibility for the commission of the charged offense and acknowledging their conduct.
The Court shall take into consideration the victim's opinion as to whether or not this benefit should be granted and shall impose such terms and conditions as it deems reasonable and the period of duration of probation that it sees fit to require, upon agreement with the entity that will provide the services, which term shall never be less than one (1) year nor more than three (3).
If the person granted probation under this Article fails to comply with the conditions thereof, the Court, after holding a hearing, may revoke the probation and proceed to impose sentence.
If the person granted probation under this Article does not violate any of the conditions thereof, the Court, after holding a hearing, may revoke the probation and proceed to impose sentence.
If the person granted probation under this Article does not violate any of the conditions thereof, the Court, upon recommendation of the competent personnel in charge of the program to which the accused was referred, in the exercise of its discretion and after holding a hearing, may dismiss the case against them.
The sentence dismissed under this Article shall be carried out without pronouncement of sentence by the Court, but the case file shall be preserved in the Court, with confidential character, not accessible to the public and separate from other records, for the purposes of being used by the courts in determining, in subsequent proceedings, whether the person qualifies to avail themselves of the benefits of this Article and to be considered for purposes of recidivism, if the person subsequently commits any of the offenses defined in this Law. In these cases, it shall be the responsibility of the prosecutor to always present the allegation of recidivism.
The dismissed sentence of the case shall not be considered as a conviction for purposes of disqualifications or incapacities imposed by law on those convicted of the commission of any offense, and the exonerated person shall have the right, after dismissal of the case, to have the Superintendent of the Puerto Rico Police return to them any fingerprint and photograph records in the possession of the Puerto Rico Police, taken in connection with the violation of the offenses that gave rise to the accusation.
The dismissal provided for in this section may only be granted on one occasion to any person.

#### Artículo 3

7 — Special Provisions. (8 L.P.R.A.

#### § 637)

(a) Bail. — When a person is accused of violating the provisions of this law or when at the time of the alleged violation was subject to the terms of a protection order issued in accordance with this law or any other similar law, or had been previously convicted of or had entered a plea of guilty to violating the provisions of this law or violating any other similar legal provision, before setting bail; in addition to the provisions of the Rules of Criminal Procedure, (34 L.P.R.A. App. II), the court must consider when imposing bail whether the person has a history of having violated orders of a court or of a governmental agency.
(b) Conditions for release on bail.— The court shall impose conditions on the accused's bail and must take into consideration whether the person has a history of domestic violence or a history of committing violent acts and whether the person represents a potential threat to the victim of the crime or to any person. In addition to the conditions established in the Rules of Criminal Procedure, the court shall impose the following conditions:
(1) Avoid all direct or indirect contact with the victim of the alleged acts constituting the crimes typified in this law, with the victim's relatives, except for the children that the accused and the victim have procreated, unless the court determines that for the best interests of the minors it is necessary to prevent paternal or maternal-filial contact. In making the determination to regulate or prohibit the accused's contact with his or her children, the court shall take into consideration the following factors:
(A) Whether the accused represents a danger to the welfare of the minors
(B) Whether the accused's history demonstrates dangerous conduct that may be detrimental to the welfare of the minors.
(C) Whether in the accused's history there is evidence of physical and emotional abuse of the minors.
(D) The opinion expressed by the minors when they have so requested directly or through an adult or helping professional; Provided, that the judge may hear the minors in private to protect their physical and/or emotional integrity.
(2) Avoid all contact with persons who provide shelter to the victim.
(3) Vacate the residence shared with the victim of the alleged crime.
(4) Refrain from intimidating or pressuring personally, or through telephone communication, or of another type or through the intervention of third parties, the victim or witnesses so that they do not testify or so that they withdraw the criminal charges filed against him or her.(5) Comply with orders regarding custody, alimony, parental relations, marital property, and any others related thereto, issued under this
law or another similar statute.
(6) Remain in a program that helps him or her manage domestic violence situations.
(c) Permits for confined persons to leave institutions and parole. — In addition to what is established in Law No. 116 of July 22, 1974, as amended [Note: Repealed and replaced by Plan 2-2011, as amended, "Reorganization Plan of the Department of Correction and Rehabilitation of 2011"], and in any other law or regulation to that effect, the Corrections Administrator or the Parole Board when making determinations regarding the granting of permits to leave penal institutions or public or private treatment centers, or when granting parole to confined persons convicted of violating the provisions of this law, must take into consideration the following circumstances:
(1) Whether the person has a history of domestic violence, or a history of committing other violent acts.
(2) Whether the person has a history of having violated orders of a court or of a governmental agency.
(3) Whether the person represents a potential threat to any other person.
(4) The opinion of the injured party, or of the persons who testified in the case and any other circumstance deemed pertinent.
(d) Executive clemency or pardon. — When considering the petition for executive clemency or pardon of a person convicted of any crime constituting domestic violence, the Parole Board must notify the injured party and the persons who testified to provide them the opportunity to be heard.
(e) Before any person may be released under the provisions of this section, the court, the Parole Board, the Corrections Administration and/or the Executive must notify the victim or injured party with sufficient advance notice so that the victim may take the necessary measures to guarantee his or her safety.
(f) Communication to the victim. — The Department of Correction and Rehabilitation shall have the obligation to notify the victim, through written or electronic communication, regarding the proximity of the release process of the aggressor who is deprived of his or her liberty for violating the provisions of this Law, and the treatment received to comply with the constitutional mandate for rehabilitation provided in our Magna Carta.
Said notification must be on or before one hundred twenty (120) days prior to the completion of the sentence. The personnel of this agency shall perform the affirmative actions that are necessary to guarantee that the victim has received the communication in reference, action that shall be recorded as part of the release file prepared by the agency.
Information that may reasonably identify the victim, including without limitation, his or her residential and postal address, telephone number or place of work, shall be kept protected under the strictest standards of confidentiality, so that it may not be provided to a third party without the authorization of a Court.
The Department of Correction and Rehabilitation shall have the responsibility to approve the corresponding regulations, or adapt the existing regulations, to guarantee that there exists a uniform administrative procedure that complies with the safeguards provided in this subsection.

#### Artículo 3

8 — Arrest. (8 L.P.R.A.

#### § 638)

Notwithstanding the provisions of Rule 11 of the Rules of Criminal Procedure, as amended (34 L.P.R.A. App. II) every public order officer must effect an arrest, even if an order to that effect is not present, if he or she has founded reasons to believe that the person to be arrested has committed, even if not in his or her presence, or is committing in his or her presence a violation of the criminal provisions of this law.

#### Artículo 3

9 — Signature and Oath of the Complaint. (8 L.P.R.A.

#### § 639)

Notwithstanding the provisions of Rule 5 of the Rules of Criminal Procedure, as amended (34 L.P.R.A. App. II) prosecutors and members of the Police of Puerto Rico must sign and swear to every complaint for violation of the provisions of this law when the facts constituting a crime are known to them by information and belief.
In no case in which the circumstances indicated above are present, shall the person who has been a victim of the alleged facts constituting a crime be required to sign the complaint.

#### Artículo 3

10 — Assistance to the Victim of Abuse. (8 L.P.R.A.

#### § 640)

Whenever a law enforcement officer intervenes with a person who alleges to be a victim of abuse, regardless of the sex, marital status, sexual orientation, gender identity or immigration status of said person, the officer shall take all measures deemed necessary to prevent said person from being abused again. Among others, the officer shall perform the following actions:
(a) If the person indicates that they have suffered damages, blows or injuries that require medical attention, even if not visible, the officer shall administer the necessary first aid to the person, offer to make arrangements for them to receive adequate medical treatment and provide transportation to a medical services center where they can be attended.
(b) If the person expresses concern for their safety, the officer shall make the necessary arrangements to transport them to a safe place.
(c) When the victim of abuse so requests, the officer shall provide protection by accompanying and assisting them at all times while they remove their personal belongings from their residence or from any other place where these are located.
(d) The officer shall advise the victim of abuse about the importance of preserving evidence.
(e) The officer shall provide the victim with information about their rights and about the governmental and private services available for victims of abuse, including, but not limited to, the remedies provided under the Law for the Protection and Assistance of Victims and Witnesses, Law No. 77 of July 9, 1986 and Law No. 91 of July 13, 1988. Likewise, the officer shall deliver a copy of an orientation sheet for victims of domestic violence.
The Public Prosecutor's Office shall have the duty to appear at every probable cause hearing for arrest in cases of a criminal nature filed under this Law, without any discretion, including violations of protection orders as established in Article 2.8 of this Law.
The Office of the Women's Advocate shall have to provide an advocate to appear at said judicial proceeding.

#### Artículo 3

10A. – Asistencia Servicio Telefónico Celular. (8 L.P.R.A.

#### § 6

40a) [Note: Art. 1 of Law 44-2016 added this Article]
Every company providing cellular telephone service shall transfer without any additional cost the responsibility, control and change of the telephone number, as well as the cellular telephone number or numbers of any minor under the custody of the person to whom a Protection Order has been issued in their favor. The petitioning parties to whom the Protection Order is issued shall have to voluntarily request that the Court of First Instance also provide them with an Order for Change in Control Over Telephone Number. This Order for Change in Control Over Telephone Number shall have a validity of thirty (30) days for its execution, and shall be delivered by the Court with full knowledge of the petitioners about the possible short and medium term consequences of requesting the change in control over telephone number.
The Order for Change in Control Over Telephone Number must contain:
a. The name and telephone number of the perpetrator, owner of the account under which the telephone number or numbers of the victim is assigned.
b. The name and contact information of the victim who will assume responsibility over the telephone number or telephone numbers and changes.
c. The number or numbers of each telephone to be transferred in favor of the victim.
d. The victim's request to obtain a new telephone number for each telephone to be transferred in favor of the victim, if so desired.
The cellular telephone service provider companies shall change the responsibility, control and change over the telephone number or numbers within a period of seventy-two (72) hours from the submission of the request and in accordance with their internal policies for the activation of services and the terms and conditions applicable to the requested service. These companies shall transfer to the petitioning parties control over billing and all duties and responsibilities for the services and mobile telephone equipment that are in their immediate possession, as well as the cellular telephone number or numbers of any minor under the custody of the petitioner for their use.
The change in control over telephone number as a precautionary measure must be available for the number used by the victim petitioner, as well as the cellular telephone number or numbers of any minor under the custody of the petitioner, at the time of the request for the Order and shall be completed, only in favor of the same, including the telephone number or numbers of any minor under the custody of the petitioner, and not of a third party. The cellular telephone service provider companies shall not be responsible at any time for any damage that the victim and their family members may suffer as a consequence of the change in control over telephone number voluntarily requested and correctly transferred.
The Order for Change in Control Over Telephone Number shall not apply to numbers classified as commercial, to lines that are in the name of a third party other than the perpetrator, since the purpose of the legislation is exclusively the protection of the victim of domestic violence or stalking. Furthermore, any change in control over telephone number shall be final and irrevocable. When the telephone service provider cannot, for operational or technical reasons, complete the transfer order, due to certain circumstances, including but not limited

to the following to be mentioned, the telephone service provider shall notify the petitioner within a period of seventy-two (72) hours from receipt of the order:
a. When the account owner has cancelled the account or terminated the services prior to the transfer of these.
b. When differences in technology limit the functionality of the equipment on the provider's network.
c. When there are geographical situations or other limitations of the network or service availability.
Every cellular telephone service provider company, upon request of the client, and accompanied by a copy of the Order for Change in Control Over Telephone Number issued by the Court of First Instance, shall remove all personal information in any directory or internal listing of telephone numbers of the telephone service provider companies or affiliated companies, without any additional cost. In the case of telephone directories, the information shall be removed in the next publication thereof.

#### Artículo 3

11 — Preparación de Informes. (8 L.P.R.A.

#### § 641)

Whenever a law enforcement officer intervenes in a domestic violence incident, he or she shall prepare a written report on the same. Said report shall contain the allegations of the persons involved and the witnesses, the type of investigation conducted, and the manner in which the incident was disposed of.
In said report, the law enforcement officer shall include any statement by the victim regarding the frequency and severity of prior domestic violence incidents and regarding the number of times he or she has gone to the Police or to any private or public entity or private individual to seek help.
This report shall be prepared for every intervention even if criminal charges are not filed against the alleged aggressor. The same shall be kept separate from reports on incidents of another nature.
The Superintendent of Police shall establish an information collection system that allows maintaining a copy of each intervention report at the precinct where it is generated and that facilitates the centralized compilation of the same at the Statistics Division of the Puerto Rico Police.
The Statistics Division of the Puerto Rico Police shall receive monthly a copy of every intervention report prepared under this section, shall compile the information contained therein, and shall prepare annually a public statistical report on domestic violence incidents in Puerto Rico. A copy of this report shall be sent to the Office of the Women's Advocate, as well as to the Legislative Assembly, which shall distribute it to all the offices of the various Committees.
The Court Administration shall provide to the Statistics Division of the Police information on protection orders requested and issued, as well as such information as may be useful so that the report contains, among other, the following information:
(1)	Population group most affected by domestic violence.
(2)	Ages of said groups, divided by number of incidents.

(3)	Number of persons who requested protection orders.
(4)	Number of persons who withdrew said requests for protection orders.
(5)	Number of persons who obtained protection orders.
(6)	Number of persons who did not obtain protection orders.
(7)	Number of situations in which dual or reciprocal protection orders were issued.
(8)	Number of situations in which minors are present and support orders were issued.
(9)	Number of protection orders sent by the Clerk of each Court to the Police Commands of the jurisdiction where the petitioning party resides.
(10)	Number of protection orders sent by the Clerk of each Court to the employers of the petitioning party.
(11)	Number of protection orders sent by the Clerk of each Court to the security company in charge of access controls at the residence of the petitioning party.
The Superintendent of Police shall establish rules to guarantee confidentiality regarding the identity of the persons involved in domestic violence incidents.

#### Artículo 3

12. — Alegaciones Preacordadas. (8 L.P.R.A.

#### § 6

42) [Nota: La Ley 136-2018 añadió este Artículo]
All those cases filed under this Law in which there are pre-agreed allegations between the defense of the accused and the representative of the Public Prosecutor, shall be governed by the procedure set forth in Rule 72 of the Rules of Criminal Procedure, providing, furthermore, that in every case in which a violation of any of the provisions of this Law is charged, said pre-agreed allegations shall be for offenses contained solely under the provisions of this Law.

### CAPITULO IV. — MEDIDAS PARA PREVENIR LA VIOLENCIA DOMÉSTICA

#### Artículo 4

1 — Funciones. (8 L.P.R.A.

#### § 651)

The Office of the Women's Advocate created by Law 20-2001, and in harmony with the public policy enunciated in this law, shall be responsible for:
(a)	Promoting and developing educational programs for the prevention of domestic violence.
(b)	Studying, investigating, and publishing reports on the problem of domestic violence in Puerto Rico, its manifestations, magnitude, consequences, and the alternatives to confront and eradicate it.
(c)	Identifying groups and sectors in which domestic violence manifests itself, educating them and raising their awareness in skills to combat it.
(d)	Raising awareness among helping professionals about the needs of persons who are victims of abuse and those of their families.

(e)	Developing strategies to foster changes in policies and procedures in government agencies in order to improve their responses to the needs of persons who are victims of abuse.
(f)	Establishing and fostering the establishment of programs for information services, support, and counseling to victims of abuse.
(g)	Fostering the establishment of shelters for persons who are victims of abuse.
(h)	Fostering, in coordination with the Department of the Family, programs of services to children who come from homes where abuse manifests itself.
(i)	Providing training and orientation services to helping professionals on treatment and counseling to persons who are victims of abuse.
(j)	Evaluating progress in the implementation of this law and submitting annual reports to the Governor of the Commonwealth of Puerto Rico and to the Legislative Assembly.
(k)	Analyzing and conducting needs studies on intervention, education, and retraining programs for persons who engage in conduct constituting abuse for the rehabilitation of the same.
(l)	Formulating guidelines on minimum requirements that the diversion services contemplated in Article 3.6 of this law must meet, which shall be considered by the courts in determinations on diversion.
(m)	Ensuring that protection orders issued by the Courts are immediately sent by the Clerk to the Police Command of the jurisdiction where the petitioning party resides, to the employer of the petitioning party, and to the security company in charge of access control at the residence of the petitioning party.
(n)	Supervising and ensuring faithful compliance with the functions assigned to the monitoring center for the surveillance of persons charged with violating this Law who are under electronic supervision. This includes the power to have a representative at said monitoring center to guarantee compliance with the purposes of electronic supervision.
(o)	Granting approval to every bidder for the purchase of equipment and devices used for electronic supervision of persons charged with violating this Law who are under electronic supervision, prior to the award of a bid.

#### Artículo 4

2 — Confidencialidad de Comunicaciones. (8 L.P.R.A.

#### § 652)

The Office of the Women's Advocate shall take measures to guarantee the confidentiality of communications and information it receives from its clients in the course of providing services to prevent and intervene with victims of domestic violence. Every communication between persons served at the Office of the Women's Advocate and its staff shall be privileged and shall be protected by the privilege of confidentiality established in the Rules of Evidence of Puerto Rico. Likewise, every communication between a victim of domestic violence and any other public entity or organization that provides services to victims of domestic violence shall enjoy the same character of privileged and confidential, in harmony with Rule 26-A of the Rules of Evidence of Puerto Rico and the Bill of Rights for Crime Victims and Witnesses.

#### Artículo 4

3 — Registro de Direcciones Sustitutas. (8 L.P.R.A.

#### § 652a)

The Criminal Justice Information System shall integrate the creation of a Substitute Address Registry for Victims of Domestic Violence, with the purpose of establishing strategies and protecting these victims, enabling governmental agencies and instrumentalities to respond to all requests for public documents without revealing the location or address of a victim of violence, in order to protect her from her aggressor.
This substitute address shall be used as a residential, work or school address, as the case may be for the participant in the Registry or her children, for the receipt of correspondence that has been established by maximum size and weight, according to the regulation established for those purposes. The substitute address that is assigned to a participant of this Registry shall have no relation whatsoever to the actual residential address of the victim of domestic violence.
The organization of the Substitute Address Registry for Victims of Domestic Violence shall provide this protection to any person who resides in Puerto Rico, regardless of the address of origin and the migratory status of said person. Likewise, it shall extend such protection on a reciprocal basis for any victim of domestic violence who has established her residence in Puerto Rico or who for such reason has moved to another jurisdiction. For purposes of this protection, the words "residence," "residential" and "resides" shall be understood in their common and ordinary meaning.
In addition to the substitute address, all other addresses of the participating victim shall be subject to the confidentiality of communications established by this law.

#### Artículo 4

4 — Collaboration of Governmental Agencies. (8 L.P.R.A.

#### § 653)

The departments, offices, bureaus, commissions, boards, administrations, councils, public corporations and subsidiaries thereof and other agencies and instrumentalities of the Commonwealth of Puerto Rico are authorized to provide the Criminal Justice Information System with the support services and resources necessary to perform and comply with the duties and functions assigned to it in this law. Likewise, these governmental entities are authorized to provide the necessary support to the Criminal Justice Information System. Such power shall be exercised subject to the provisions of law governing said public agencies.

### CAPITULO V. — SUPPLEMENTARY PROVISIONS

#### Artículo 5

1 — Independence of Civil Actions. (8 L.P.R.A.

#### § 661)

It shall not be required nor shall it be necessary for persons protected by this law to file criminal charges in order to request and have a protection order issued.

#### Artículo 5

2 — Constitutional Saving Clause. (8 L.P.R.A§ 662).

If any provision contained in this law is declared unconstitutional, such declaration of unconstitutionality shall not affect the other provisions thereof.

#### Artículo 5

3 — Rules for Civil and Criminal Actions. (8 L.P.R.A.

#### § 663)

Unless otherwise provided in this Law, the civil provisions established herein shall be governed by the Rules of Civil Procedure of 2009, as amended.
Likewise, criminal actions brought under the provisions thereof that define crimes shall be governed by the Rules of Criminal Procedure of 1963, as amended, unless otherwise provided in this Law.
In any civil action brought under this Law, the petitioning party shall have the right, if so desired, to be accompanied by an Advocate, who shall provide assistance or support to the petitioner in the different stages of the process, including help with the necessary forms to initiate the same.
The Court shall authorize the Advocate to remain at the side of the petitioning party while the latter testifies. The Advocate may not address the petitioning party without authorization from the Court.
Neither may the Advocate interfere in the hearing held before the Court. The participation of Advocates shall consist of accompanying the petitioning party to the hearings and providing emotional support, as well as the guidance and assistance that are necessary during the judicial process, without including providing legal advice or representation.
In those instances in which it is not possible to have the assistance of an Advocate, or the petitioning party does not wish the services they offer, the Court may authorize the presence of a support person to remain at the side of the petitioner while testifying. This support person shall refrain from interfering in the hearing held before the Court and their sole function shall be to accompany the petitioning party. Prior to authorizing the presence of the support person, the Court shall interview the petitioning party and ascertain that the presence of the support person is in the best interest of the petitioning party.
The request for accompaniment may be requested at the initiative of the Public Prosecutor or the petitioning party. Once the request is made, the Court shall resolve it immediately.
In criminal actions arising under this Law, the victim shall have the right, if so desired, to be accompanied by a victim and witness assistance technician assigned by the Department

of Justice, during the hearing for determination of cause for the arrest of the aggressor and the preliminary hearing. If this personnel is not available, or if the victim so desires, the Court shall authorize that the victim may be accompanied by an Advocate.
The Court shall authorize the victim and witness assistance technician, or Advocate, to remain at the side of the victim while testifying. The victim and witness assistance technician or Advocate may not address the victim without authorization from the Court and may not interfere in the hearing held before the Court.
In those instances in which it is not possible to have the assistance of a victim and witness assistance technician or Advocate, or the victim does not wish the services they offer, the Court may authorize the presence of a support person to remain at the victim's side while testifying. This person shall refrain from interfering in the hearing held before the Court and their sole function shall be to accompany the victim. Prior to authorizing the presence of the support person, the Court shall interview the petitioning party and ascertain that the presence of the support person is in the best interest of the petitioning party.
During the trial stage, this personnel shall remain in the courtroom in those cases where they do not serve as a witness. If necessary, the Court may grant time for the victim to be assisted by this personnel. In jury trial cases, the Court must give special instructions to clarify the functions of the victim and witness assistance technician, Advocate, or support person, emphasizing the fact that their presence has the purpose of facilitating the victim's statement and not of influencing in favor of their credibility.
Any adult person chosen by the victim, whether a family member or not, may serve as support personnel. Any person who has accredited training or studies in the area of counseling, guidance, psychology, social work or legal advocacy and who is certified by the Office of the Women's Advocate or by a private non-profit entity authorized to issue such certifications according to the regulations approved by the Office of the Women's Advocate for those purposes may serve as an Advocate.
It shall be the responsibility of the Office of the Women's Advocate to provide specialized technical advice for the certification of Advocates and the responsibility to ensure faithful compliance therewith.

FORM IV.

ORIENTATION TO VICTIMS OF DOMESTIC VIOLENCE

If your husband or wife, ex-husband or ex-wife, the person with whom you cohabit or have cohabited, or the person with whom you maintain or have maintained a consensual relationship, or the person with whom you have procreated a daughter or a son, has beaten you, threatened you, intimidated you, or deprived you of your liberty, or has exposed you to suffering serious physical or emotional harm, or has forced you to engage in unwanted sexual conduct, you may go to the Police station and request that a complaint be filed against the person who assaulted or abused you. You may also go without assistance of a male lawyer or of a female lawyer to any judge and request an order that provides you with the following remedies:
1.	That the aggressor or the female aggressor be ordered to refrain from abusing you, intimidating you or threatening you again.
2.	That the aggressor or the female aggressor be ordered to vacate the dwelling that he/she shares with you.
3.	That the aggressor or the female aggressor be prohibited from entering your residence, school, business or workplace, and its surroundings.
4.	That custody of your minor children be granted to you.
5.	That you be permitted to enter your home to retrieve your personal belongings or to the place where they are located and that the Police be ordered to accompany you at all times.
6.	That the male/female aggressor be prohibited from bothering, intimidating or intervening in any other way with your minor children, or other member of your family nucleus.
7.	That the male/female aggressor be ordered to pay alimony for your minor children and/or for you, when he/she has the legal obligation to do so.
8.	That the male/female aggressor be ordered to refrain from loitering around the surroundings of your home, workplace or place of study.
NOTE: A copy of the Protection Order issued by a Judge must be delivered to the station of your jurisdiction. You may also, if you are married to the male aggressor or the female aggressor, file a divorce complaint in the Family Relations Chamber of the Court of First Instance and request the same measures indicated above.
