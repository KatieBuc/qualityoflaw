# LAW No. 348

LAW No. 348
LAW OF MARCH 9, 2013
EVO MORALES AYMA
CONSTITUTIONAL PRESIDENT OF THE PLURINATIONAL STATE OF BOLIVIA
Whereas, the Plurinational Legislative Assembly has enacted the following Law
THE PLURINATIONAL LEGISLATIVE ASSEMBLY,
D E C R E E S :
COMPREHENSIVE LAW TO GUARANTEE
WOMEN A LIFE FREE OF VIOLENCE

## TITLE I

GENERAL PROVISIONS
SOLE CHAPTER
CONSTITUTIONAL FRAMEWORK, OBJECT, PURPOSE,
SCOPE AND APPLICATION

#### ARTICLE 1. (CONSTITUTIONAL FRAMEWORK)

This Law is based on the constitutional mandate and on the International Human Rights Instruments, Treaties and Conventions ratified by Bolivia, which guarantee all persons, particularly women, the right not to suffer physical, sexual and/or psychological violence both in the family and in society.

#### ARTICLE 2. (OBJECT AND PURPOSE)

The object of this Law is to establish comprehensive mechanisms, measures and policies for the prevention, care, protection and reparation of women in situations of violence, as well as the prosecution and punishment of aggressors, in order to guarantee women a dignified life and the full exercise of their rights to Live Well.

#### ARTICLE 3. (NATIONAL PRIORITY)

I.                 The Plurinational State of Bolivia assumes as a priority the eradication of violence against women, as it is one of the most extreme forms of discrimination based on gender.
II.                The Organs of the State and all public institutions shall adopt the necessary measures and policies, allocating sufficient economic and human resources on a mandatory basis.
III.               The Autonomous Territorial Entities, within the framework of their constitutional competencies and responsibilities, shall allocate the human and economic resources intended for the implementation of policies, programs and projects aimed at eradicating all forms of violence against women.

#### ARTICLE 4. (PRINCIPLES AND VALUES). This Law is governed by the following principles and values

1.      Living Well. It is the condition and development of a complete material, spiritual and physical life, in harmony with oneself, the family and social environment and nature.
2.      Equality. The State guarantees real and effective equality between women and men, respect for and protection of rights, especially of women, within the framework of diversity as a value, eliminating all forms of distinction or discrimination based on differences of sex, cultural, economic, physical, social or any other nature.
3.      Inclusion. Taking into account the culture and origin of women, to adopt, implement and apply appropriate mechanisms to safeguard their rights, ensure respect for them and guarantee the provision of effective and timely means for their protection.
4.      Dignified Treatment. Women in situations of violence receive priority, dignified and preferential treatment, with respect, quality and warmth.
5.      Complementarity. The communion between women and men of equal, similar or different ways of life and cultural identity who coexist in friendly and peaceful harmony.
6.      Harmony. Peaceful coexistence and living together between women and men, and with Mother Earth.
7.      Equality of Opportunities. Women, regardless of their personal, social or economic circumstances, their age, marital status, belonging to a native indigenous peasant people, sexual orientation, rural or urban origin, belief or religion, political opinion or any other; shall have access to the protection and actions that this Law establishes, throughout the national territory.
8.      Social Equity. It is the common welfare of women and men, with full and effective participation in all spheres, to achieve a fair distribution and redistribution of social products and goods.
9.      Gender Equity. Eliminating inequality gaps for the full exercise of the freedoms and rights of women and men.
10.    Culture of Peace. Women and men reject violence against women and resolve conflicts through dialogue and respect among persons.
11.    Informality. At all levels of public administration intended to prevent, attend to, detect, process and punish any form of violence against women, compliance with formal or material requirements that hinder the process of restoring violated rights and punishing those responsible shall not be required.
12.    Depatriarchalization. For the purposes of this Law, depatriarchalization consists of the development of public policies from the plurinational identity, for the visibility, denunciation and eradication of patriarchy, through the transformation of unequal structures, relationships, traditions, customs and behaviors of power, domination, exclusion, oppression and exploitation of women by men.
13.    Differentiated Care. Women must receive the care that their specific needs and circumstances demand, with differentiated criteria that ensure the full exercise of their rights.
14.    Specialization. At all levels of public administration and especially those of care, protection and punishment in cases of violence against women, public servants must have the necessary knowledge to guarantee women respectful, dignified and effective treatment.

#### ARTICLE 5. (SCOPE OF APPLICATION)

I.                 This Law applies throughout the territory of the Plurinational State of Bolivia and in places subject to its jurisdiction.
II.                The authorities and public servants of all Organs, Public Institutions, Autonomous Territorial Entities and civil society have the obligation to enforce it, under criminal, civil and administrative liability.
III.               It does not recognize jurisdiction or privilege of any kind, its application is preferential with respect to any other norm for the crimes established in this Law.
IV.               The provisions of this Law shall be applicable to any person who, due to their situation of vulnerability, suffers any of the forms of violence that this Law punishes, regardless of their gender.

#### ARTICLE 6. (DEFINITIONS)

For purposes of the application and interpretation of this Law, the following definitions are adopted:
1.      Violence. Constitutes any action or omission, open or covert, that causes death, suffering or physical, sexual or psychological harm to a woman or other person, generates damage to her patrimony, her economy, her employment source or in any other sphere whatsoever, for the sole fact of being a woman.
2.      Situation of Violence. Is the set of circumstances and conditions of aggression in which a woman finds herself, at a given moment in her life.
3.      Non-Sexist Language. Is the use of words and written, visual, symbolic and verbal messages that are non-discriminatory on the basis of sex.
4.      Gender-Sensitive Budgets. Are those that are oriented with priority character to the allocation and redistribution of resources toward public policies and take into account the different needs and interests of women and men, for the reduction of gaps, the social and economic inclusion of women, especially those who are in a situation of violence and those who are most discriminated against by reason of origin, background, nation, people, social position, sexual orientation, economic condition, disability, marital status, pregnancy, language and political position.
5.      Cultural Identity. Is the set of values, visions, traditions, uses and customs, symbols, beliefs and behaviors that gives people a sense of belonging.
6.      Aggressor. Whoever commits an action or omission that implies any form of violence toward a woman or other person.
7.      Sexual Integrity. Is the right to security and sexual control of one's own body in the concept of sexual self-determination.

#### ARTICLE 7. (TYPES OF VIOLENCE AGAINST WOMEN)

Within the framework of the forms of physical, psychological, sexual and economic violence, in an enunciative, non-limiting manner, the following are considered forms of violence:
1.      Physical Violence. Is any action that causes injuries and/or bodily harm, internal, external or both, temporary or permanent, that manifests immediately or in the long term, employing or not physical force, weapons or any other means.
2.      Feminicidal Violence. Is the action of extreme violence that violates the fundamental right to life and causes the death of a woman for the fact of being one.
3.      Psychological Violence. Is the set of systematic actions of devaluation, intimidation and control of the behavior and decisions of women, which have as a consequence the diminution of their self-esteem, depression, psychological instability, disorientation and even suicide.
4.      Media Violence. Is that produced by mass media through publications, dissemination of messages and stereotyped images that promote the submission and/or exploitation of women, that insult, defame, discriminate, dishonor, humiliate them or that violate their dignity, their name and their image.
5.      Symbolic and/or Covert Violence. Are the messages, values, symbols, icons, signs and social, economic, political, cultural and religious belief impositions that transmit, reproduce and consolidate relations of domination, exclusion, inequality and discrimination, naturalizing the subordination of women.
6.      Violence Against Dignity, Honor and Name. Is any verbal or written expression of offense, insult, defamation, slander, threat or other, tendentious or public, that discredits, disqualifies, devalues, degrades or affects the name, dignity, honor and reputation of a woman.
7.      Sexual Violence. Is any conduct that puts at risk sexual self-determination, both in the sexual act and in any form of contact or carnal access, genital or non-genital, that threatens, violates or restricts the right to the exercise of a free, safe, effective and full sexual life, with autonomy and sexual freedom of the woman.
8.      Violence Against Reproductive Rights. Is the action or omission that impedes, limits or violates the right of women to information, guidance, comprehensive care and treatment during pregnancy or loss, childbirth, postpartum and lactation; to decide freely and responsibly the number and spacing of daughters and sons; to exercise their safe maternity, and to choose safe contraceptive methods.
9.      Violence in Health Services. Is any discriminatory, humiliating and dehumanized action that omits, denies or restricts access to effective and immediate care and timely information by health personnel, putting at risk the life and health of women.
10.    Patrimonial and Economic Violence. Is any action or omission that by affecting the woman's own and/or community property, causes damage or impairment of her patrimony, values or resources; controls or limits her economic income and the disposition thereof, or deprives her of the indispensable means to live.
11.    Labor Violence. Is any action that occurs in any work environment by any person of superior, equal or inferior hierarchy that discriminates, humiliates, threatens or intimidates women; that obstructs or makes conditional their access to employment, permanence or promotion and that violates the exercise of their rights.
12.    Violence in the Plurinational Educational System. Is any act of physical, psychological or sexual aggression committed against women in the regular, alternative, special and higher educational system.
13.    Violence in the Political Exercise and Leadership of Women. Understood as established in Article 7 of Law No. 243, Against Harassment and Political Violence toward Women.
14.    Institutional Violence. Is any action or omission by public servants or personnel of private institutions, that implies a discriminatory, prejudiced, humiliating and dehumanized action that delays, obstructs, impairs or denies women access and attention to the required service.
15.    Violence in the Family. Is any physical, psychological or sexual aggression committed toward a woman by the spouse or ex-spouse, cohabitant or ex-cohabitant, or their family, ascendants, descendants, sisters, brothers, civil or affine relatives in direct and collateral line, guardians or those in charge of custody or care.
16.    Violence Against Sexual Rights and Freedom. Is any action or omission that impedes or restricts the exercise of the rights of women to enjoy a free, safe, affective and full sexual life or that violates their freedom of sexual choice.
17.    Any other form of violence that harms the dignity, integrity, freedom or that violates the rights of women.

## TITLE II

PUBLIC POLICIES AND INSTITUTIONALITY

### CHAPTER I

PUBLIC POLICIES

#### ARTICLE 8. (PUBLIC POLICIES)

It is the responsibility of the State, with intersectoral character and presided over by the Governing Entity, to adopt and coordinate the execution of the mandates of this Law, in all public policy and National Plan that involves the prevention of violence toward women, their care and protection.

#### ARTICLE 9. (APPLICATION)

For the application of this Law, the State Bodies, Autonomous Territorial Entities and Public Institutions, within the framework of their respective competencies and responsibilities, shall:
1.      Adopt, implement and supervise specialized care protocols, in the different care instances, for the restoration of the rights of women in situations of violence.
2.      Create, strengthen and sustain care and protection services for women in situations of violence.
3.      Create and sustain specialized comprehensive care and re-education services for aggressors, as well as other measures aimed at modifying their behavior.
4.      Adopt concrete action measures and clear and specific responsibilities, with the level of attention and priority required for the preservation of the life, safety and integrity of women.
5.      Articulate the instruments, policies, services and linked inter-institutional actions, for the prevention, care, sanction and eradication of violence against women.

#### ARTICLE 10. (PLANNING)

The State Bodies, Public Institutions and Autonomous Territorial Entities, within the framework of their respective competencies, attributions, functions and capacities, shall incorporate in their strategies, annual operational and budgetary planning, the sufficient and necessary actions and resources for the application of this Law, and shall have suitable personnel for its implementation. They shall focus their actions and resources on special attention to women in situations of violence and those who are in conditions of vulnerability, as a priority in rural areas.

#### ARTICLE 11

(PLURINATIONAL COMPREHENSIVE SYSTEM FOR THE PREVENTION, CARE, SANCTION AND ERADICATION OF GENDER-BASED VIOLENCE – SIPPASE).
I.                 The Governing Entity shall be in charge of the Plurinational Comprehensive System for the Prevention, Care, Sanction and Eradication of Gender-based Violence – SIPPASE, which reorganizes the entire system of comprehensive care for women in situations of violence and the information of public and private services, which shall be registered in a single registry on gender-based violence. The data information that this system generates shall be of a confidential nature.
II.                The entity responsible for this registry may issue certifications on the background of aggressors, complaints, actions of public servants, and on all information that may serve for the prevention, care, protection and sanction of individual cases, only upon prosecutorial request or judicial order.
III.               All information registered in this system shall be forwarded to the National Statistics Institute for processing and dissemination, according to jointly developed indicators, with a human rights and women's rights approach, disaggregated at least by sex, age and municipality.

#### ARTICLE 12. (TRAINING)

The State Bodies, the Public Prosecutor's Office and Forensic Investigations Institute, the Bolivian Police, the Ombudsman's Office, the Armed Forces, the Attorney General's Office, the Plurinational School of Public Management, the State Judges School, the Autonomous Territorial Entities and any other public entity or entity that provides public services, within the scope of their respective competencies, shall adopt and develop specific training programs relating to the culture against violence, equality and non-discrimination based on sex and on gender equity, among others, which must be permanently updated and shall be applied to all personnel, regardless of their hierarchy, without exception and on a mandatory basis.

#### ARTICLE 13. (ACCESS TO PUBLIC POSITIONS)

I.                 For access to a public position in any State Body or level of administration, whether by election, designation, appointment or hiring, in addition to those provided by Law, it shall be considered an inexcusable requirement not to have a background of violence exercised against a woman or any member of their family, that has an enforceable sentence with the quality of res judicata. The Judiciary Council, through the Judicial Registry of Criminal Records – REJAP, shall certify the background referred to in this Article, also reporting the Resolutions declaring default or conditional suspension of the process.
II.                For designation to public positions that are related to the care, protection, investigation and sanction of cases of women in situations of violence, training or proven experience in matters of gender and/or women's rights shall also be required.

#### ARTICLE 14. (SECTORAL POLICIES)

The Governing Entity of the central level of the State and the Autonomous Territorial Entities, according to their respective competencies, shall adopt specific measures aimed at eradicating all forms of violence against women and establishing a new social framework to guarantee respect and a dignified life free of violence, for which purpose the following programs are established on an indicative basis, not excluding others that could be adopted:
1.      Prevention at the structural, individual and collective levels that consolidates a new culture of respect for women, their dignity and rights.
2.      Training, specialization, awareness-raising and capacity-building of all those persons who provide care to women in situations of violence.
3.      Guidance and information for women for their revaluation as subjects of rights and access to care and protection instances.
4.      Care and protection for women in situations of violence, and for members of their family in situations of risk.
5.      Communication to deconstruct sexist stereotypes and the roles socially assigned to women, promoting self-regulation of the media regarding the advertising they broadcast, the disrespectful and commercial use of the image of women.
6.      Guidance, care and rehabilitation for aggressors to promote the values adopted by this Law and achieve behavioral changes for the effective respect of women's rights and avoid recidivism.

#### ARTICLE 15. (PARTICIPATION AND SOCIAL CONTROL)

Social organizations and women's organizations of civil society shall exercise participation and social control within the framework of the corresponding Law, participating in the design, evaluation and management of public policies for the prevention, care and protection of women and the quality of specialized services, public and private that provide basic services or that administer fiscal resources at all levels of the State.

### CHAPTER II

INSTITUTIONALITY

#### ARTICLE 16. (GOVERNING BODY)

The Ministry of Justice and Institutional Transparency, within the framework of its competencies and attributions, is the Governing Body responsible for coordination, articulation and oversight of the effective application and compliance with this Law. The Governing Body shall be in charge of the Plurinational Comprehensive System for Prevention, Attention, Sanction and Eradication of Gender-Based Violence – SIPPASE; likewise, said Governing Body shall coordinate the implementation of comprehensive policies for prevention, attention, sanction and eradication of violence against women, both at the central level and in the Autonomous Territorial Entities. The necessary human and economic resources shall be allocated for the fulfillment of its attributions, within the framework of this Law. All instances of the Executive Branch, with competencies linked to women's rights and the problem of violence and the services for prevention, attention and sanction of violence against women, shall coordinate their actions with the Ministry of Justice and Institutional Transparency, through the most appropriate mechanism and with sufficient hierarchy defined by the Executive Branch. The Ministry of Justice and Institutional Transparency shall submit an annual report to the Plurinational Legislative Assembly on the progress and compliance with this Law.

## TITLE III

PREVENTION, ATTENTION AND PROTECTION

### CHAPTER I

PREVENTION OF VIOLENCE AGAINST WOMEN

#### ARTICLE 17. (PREVENTION CRITERIA)

I.                 For the purposes of application of this Law, the central level of the State and the Autonomous Territorial Entities shall create and adopt the prevention measures that are necessary to modify violent individual and social behaviors and those that tolerate, naturalize and reproduce violence, under three action criteria:
1.      Structural Prevention. Comprises all those comprehensive measures intended to modify attitudes, practices, reactions, actions and omissions that have as effect and consequence violence against women, as well as their replacement by attitudes in individual, couple, family, community, social and state behavior, through awareness-raising and education within the family, at school and other academic levels, at work, health care centers, indigenous native peasant and Afro-Bolivian communities, political and union organizations, social organizations and any other sphere of social interaction.
2.      Individual Prevention. Refers to measures intended to strengthen and empower each woman and promote her skills to identify any possible manifestation of violence or aggression against her and confront it assertively, with the purpose of anticipating its expression or materialization and preventing it from occurring or continuing.
3.      Collective Prevention. Are measures intended to prevent violence and protect women through their organizations, institutions or any collectivity to which they belong by affinity (unions, neighborhood councils, guilds, communities, nations, indigenous native peasant, intercultural and Afro-Bolivian peoples).
II.                Prevention in the family, community, educational, health, labor and communication spheres must be prioritized.
III.               The Autonomous Territorial Entities, within the framework of their respective competencies and the exercise of legislative, regulatory and executive powers in the matter, shall incorporate mechanisms for the prevention of violence, as well as attention and protection for women in situations of violence.

#### ARTICLE 18. (COMMUNITY PREVENTION)

The indigenous native peasant and Afro-Bolivian authorities shall adopt in the communities in which they exercise their functions, the prevention measures they consider most appropriate under the three action criteria established to prevent any act of violence against women, with the participation of women in their planning, execution and monitoring, respecting their rights. No norm or procedure specific to the indigenous native peasant nations and peoples may violate the rights recognized in the Political Constitution of the State and the constitutional block.

#### ARTICLE 19. (MEASURES IN THE EDUCATIONAL SPHERE)

I.                 The Ministry of Education has the obligation and responsibility to adopt the following measures:
1.      Incorporate strategies and programs for comprehensive prevention and intervention against violence against women in public education policies.
2.      Incorporate the gender approach, the principles and values established in this Law, full respect for human rights and training in peaceful conflict resolution in the educational curriculum at all levels, including teacher training colleges and universities, to contribute to a culture of respect in the family, community, school, labor and social spheres, as a daily practice.
3.      Create in educational units a psychological care center, with mandatory specialization in violence, in agreement with public or private universities for the psychological care of students who live in families in situations of violence.
4.      Guarantee the immediate transfer to the corresponding educational units of the daughters and sons of women in situations of violence, if a change of domicile occurs.
5.      Formulate and implement a policy for the prevention of sexual harassment in the educational system.
6.      Develop regulations and a single protocol for the treatment of complaints of all forms of school violence and sexual harassment, protection mechanisms and specialized attention for child and adolescent victims.
7.      Prohibit as study texts, educational materials with sexist content, violent and discriminatory messages against women, and promote the preparation and dissemination of educational material with a gender equity approach, particularly equality of rights between women and men.
8.      Other actions necessary for the eradication of violence and the generation of mutual respect.
II.                The policies adopted by the Ministry of Education regarding prevention, protection and treatment of violence in the educational system shall be coordinated with the Governing Body.
III.               Teaching, administrative or professional support staff who, having detected a situation of violence, had not reported it, shall be subject to the corresponding legal sanctions.

#### ARTICLE 20. (MEASURES IN THE HEALTH SECTOR)

I.                 The Ministry of Health and Sports has the responsibility to adopt the following measures, aimed at guaranteeing women at risk or in situations of violence access to health services, their treatment and protection, as a public health problem:
1.      Incorporate strategies and programs for promotion, prevention and comprehensive intervention within the framework of the Intercultural Community Family Health Policy, with the purpose of guaranteeing the fight against violence towards women in Public Health Policies.
2.      Include and implement the National Clinical Care Standard, the single protocol for detection, care and referral of violence and its effects, including all forms of physical violence, violence in health services, psychological and sexual violence contemplated in this Law, with an intercultural and gender approach.
3.      Design and execute training, information and awareness plans on promotion, prevention and comprehensive treatment for women who suffer violence; for professional, auxiliary and administrative personnel of public health services, short-term social security management entities and private services, to guarantee their timely and adequate action in the detection, prevention, care and protection of women.
4.      Guarantee that the Public Health System, short-term social security and private sector, respond with emergency medical and psychological care, immediate treatment for the restoration of physical and emotional health of women who are at risk and/or in situations of violence; in the provision of free health care for the attention of women victims of violence at the time of implementation of the Universal Health Insurance, with denial of care being prohibited.
5.      Develop and implement mechanisms for the detection and reporting of potential cases of violence that women who resort to public health services, short-term social security and private services might suffer.
6.      Refer or transfer women in situations of violence or imminent risk to specialized medical, psychological and protection care services.
7.      Respect the decisions that women in situations of violence make in the exercise of their sexual rights and their reproductive rights, within the framework of current regulations.
8.      Generate and disseminate permanent and updated information on sexual rights and reproductive rights, prevention and treatment of sexually transmitted infections, HIV/AIDS, hemorrhages during the first trimester of pregnancy, unplanned pregnancies and all forms of sexual violence.
9.      Medical personnel of the Public Health System, short-term social security and private services, must issue ex officio, on a mandatory, free basis and on plain paper, a medical certificate to women who require care for physical or sexual harm arising from acts of violence, with the respective assessment of psychological harm to be referred to the competent authorities. The forensic physician must as a priority homologate the medical certificates issued in cases of violence against women and establish the degree of impairment.
10.    Adopt norms, policies and programs aimed at preventing and sanctioning violence in health services and any other form of violence against women in health services, exercised by any official of public health services, short-term social security and private services.
11.    Promote scientific research for the adoption of less invasive, painful or aggressive medical examinations and treatments.
12.    Promote active community participation of women and men in all public health establishments, short-term social security and private establishments that provide basic services or administer fiscal resources at all levels of the State, to exercise social control in compliance with the measures indicated in this Law.
13.    Expansion of care for victims of physical or sexual violence against women as a benefit of the short-term social security regime.
14.    Other necessary actions in the field of health care, which contribute to the eradication of all forms of violence towards women.
II.                Health services at all levels, public, social security and private services, have the obligation to attend, under responsibility, to any woman who requests medical and psychological care, as well as to report probable or confirmed cases of violence against women that they attend to, sending a signed copy of the case record to the Governing Entity, for inclusion in the Plurinational Comprehensive System for Prevention, Care, Sanction and Eradication of Gender-Based Violence – SIPPASE, within 48 hours of the facts becoming known.

#### ARTICLE 21. (MEASURES IN THE LABOR SPHERE)

I.                 The Ministry of Labor, Employment and Social Security must adopt the following measures aimed at guaranteeing respect for women:
1.      Legal and administrative mechanisms, and public policies that guarantee the exercise of women's labor rights and access to decent work, free from any form of violence, ensuring the same remuneration as men for work of equal value, both in the public and private sectors.
2.      Adopt measures to avoid the imposition of requirements for access to a job position, promotion, salary or employment stability, that generate discrimination based on sex, age, physical appearance, marital status or maternity condition. The presentation of laboratory tests, HIV/AIDS tests, pregnancy tests, interviews about personal decisions or situations or others of any other nature that affect a decision beyond suitability must be expressly prohibited.
3.      Regulation and sanction of unjustified dismissal of women due to their marital status, pregnancy, situation of violence, age, physical conditions, number of daughters or sons or any form that implies labor discrimination; guaranteeing labor stability according to current regulations.
4.      Protection against all forms of sexual harassment or workplace harassment, and adoption of internal and administrative procedures for its reporting, investigation, attention, prosecution and sanction.
5.      Adoption of a policy of permanent training, awareness-raising, strengthening and capacity-building for labor conciliation and inspection personnel, for the adequate attention of complaints filed by women, especially if they are in a situation of violence.
6.      In coordination with the Ministry of Health and Sports, a policy for specialized, timely and free medical and psychological care in the social security system for all women who have been subjected to any form of violence in the workplace.
7.      In coordination with care and protection services to prioritize access, permanence and promotions of women in situations of violence, to decent employment, including specific mechanisms in the national employment policy, special employment programs and the job bank, specific training, capacity-building and updating programs, guaranteeing remuneration without discrimination gaps.
8.      Adoption of a system of flexibility and tolerance in workplaces for women who are in a situation of violence, guaranteeing their labor rights, upon mere presentation of the resolution of any protection measure, within the framework of Article 35 of this Law.
9.      Adoption of norms that allow the reconciliation of work and family life of people who work, in order to allow greater balance between women and men in both spheres.
10.    All necessary actions for the eradication of violence against women.
II.                In case of violation of these rights, the woman in a situation of labor violence may resort to the corresponding administrative or judicial instances so that her rights are restored, the damage is repaired, sanctions are applied to the aggressor, and if applicable, to those responsible for care and protection who failed to fulfill their functions.

#### ARTICLE 22. (MEASURES IN THE COMMUNICATION SPHERE)

The Ministry of Communication, within the scope of its competencies, shall adopt the following measure:
Design and implementation of a national communication strategy, which includes mass media campaigns, aimed at informing and raising awareness about the causes, forms and consequences of violence against women, as well as deconstructing patriarchal stereotypes of subordination and devaluation of women, considering cultural diversity and allocating for this purpose the same resources it assigns to advertising on strategic issues for national development.

#### ARTICLE 23. (OBLIGATION OF THE MEDIA). The media shall adopt the following measures

1.      Adopt Codes of Ethics and other self-regulation measures, in relation to the dissemination of discriminatory content linked to violence against women or that reinforces or justifies tolerance, or that violates the rights of women.
2.      Allocate, within the framework of social responsibility, a minimum free space for the dissemination of messages that promote the values established in this Law.
3.      Disseminate information relating to violence against women in an objective manner, protecting the defense of their autonomy, freedom, dignity, privacy and rights, of their daughters and sons, restricting all graphic exposure that constitutes humiliation, public exposure and/or degradation.

### CHAPTER II

CARE FOR WOMEN IN SITUATIONS OF VIOLENCE

#### ARTICLE 24. (COMPREHENSIVE CARE SERVICES)

I.                 Public universities and higher education centers shall create free programs and services aimed at the prevention of violence against women, the care and rehabilitation of women in situations of violence, specialized and comprehensive professional advice. Universities and training centers shall include appropriate academic programs to achieve these purposes.
II.                Care programs and services shall be organized, coordinated and strengthened in each municipality charged to its annual budget, as instances of permanent support to the Municipal Comprehensive Legal Services and the Shelters and Temporary Refuge Houses. The care provided by said services must be priority, permanent, specialized and multidisciplinary. They shall act in a coordinated manner with all state guarantee instances, especially with the Bolivian Police, the Judicial Branch and health institutions.
III.               All care services must be extended to the daughters and sons of the woman in a situation of violence and to other dependent persons in conditions of risk.
IV.               The Comprehensive Care Services must promote, advise and support the permanent training and updating of their personnel, with the objective of ensuring that from their area and specialty, they work together from the vision, approach and language that the Law establishes regarding violence.
V.                The Comprehensive Care Services shall adopt the necessary measures regarding infrastructure, equipment and human resources, that guarantee that women in situations of violence will not be subjected to revictimization.

#### ARTICLE 25. (SHELTERS AND TEMPORARY REFUGE HOUSES)

The Autonomous Territorial Entities, within the framework of their competencies and financial sustainability, have the responsibility to create, equip, maintain and operate Shelters and Temporary Refuge Houses for women in situations of violence in urban and rural areas. They must have multidisciplinary personnel duly trained and specialized in care for women in situations of violence; the administration must design and implement a sustainability strategy. For compliance with the provisions of this Article, intergovernmental and interinstitutional agreements and conventions may be established.

#### ARTICLE 26. (SERVICES)

I.                 Shelters and Temporary Refuges shall provide women with the following services according to their needs and ongoing evaluation:
1.      To shelter, protect and provide free care to women in situations of violence, their daughters and sons and any family member who is under their care and is at risk.
2.      To stimulate and promote the empowerment of women in situations of violence, facilitating their access to education, job training and work.
3.      To coordinate with care services and public and private health centers, the medical care of women and their family members in situations of violence.
4.      To implement the national policy and the local policy that may have been adopted by the corresponding autonomous territorial entity, in coordination with the Governing Body and women's organizations and institutions.
5.      To provide women with the necessary interdisciplinary care for their physical and psychological recovery, enabling them to participate, gradually, in public, social and private life.
6.      To provide women with information about legal procedures, institutions that provide the free interdisciplinary services they require for their recovery and any matter of interest to them, related to their situation.
II.                Likewise, these Shelters and Temporary Refuges shall provide women and, where applicable, their daughters and sons with the following specialized and free services:
1.      Lodging and food.
2.      Comprehensive re-educational programs to promote changes in attitudes and values for their gradual integration and full participation in social and private life, enabling them to achieve independence from the aggressor.
3.      Training in the development of skills, techniques and knowledge for the performance of a work or productive activity.
4.      Priority access to the employment placement system, should they request it.
III.               The authority in charge of each shelter may coordinate private care for any of the mentioned services.

#### ARTICLE 27. (CONFIDENTIALITY)

Shelters and Temporary Refuges constitute safe refuge for women in situations of violence, therefore their location may not be revealed, except to persons authorized to go to them. The anonymity and privacy of the sheltered women shall be guaranteed.

#### ARTICLE 28. (STAY)

Women who resort to Shelters and Temporary Refuges may not remain in them for more than three months, unless due to the severity of the violence suffered or due to special conditions that justify it because of persistent physical or psychological instability or a risk situation, it is necessary to extend this time. In this exceptional case, following joint evaluation by the interdisciplinary staff comprised of at least the medical, psychological and legal personnel assigned by the care services to the Shelter, the woman's stay may be determined until her complete recovery.

#### ARTICLE 29. (COMMUNITY PROMOTERS)

Women who have overcome their situation of violence, or those who wish to assume this commitment, may voluntarily constitute themselves into support promoter networks for women who are still in such situation, generating solidarity and protection groups linked to public care services. The Autonomous Territorial Entity shall provide promoters with training in peaceful conflict resolution, notions of psychology, counseling and any other topic of interest for this purpose.

#### ARTICLE 30. (COMMUNITY WOMEN'S HOUSE)

In rural areas, organized women may define the creation of Community Women's Houses, for which purpose the Municipal Autonomous Government shall provide the necessary infrastructure. Those that are linked to the network of community promoters in the different communities that will attend and carry out the tasks of guidance, prevention and detection of cases of violence, may enter into agreements with public authorities and private institutions.

#### ARTICLE 31. (REHABILITATION OF AGGRESSORS)

I.                 The rehabilitation of aggressors, by order of the competent jurisdictional authority, shall be ordered by express order, with the objective of promoting changes in their aggressive conduct. Therapy shall not replace the sanction imposed for acts of violence.
II.                Rehabilitation services may be organized through intergovernmental agreements, both in urban and rural areas, in existing centers or in the place where the aggressor serves a criminal sanction. In no case shall therapy be provided together with the assaulted woman.
III.               Those responsible for these services must report the initiation, compliance or non-compliance with the program or therapy by the aggressor to the competent jurisdictional authority and to the Plurinational Comprehensive System for Prevention, Care, Sanction and Eradication of Gender-Based Violence – SIPPASE.

### CHAPTER III

PROTECTION MEASURES

#### ARTICLE 32. (PURPOSE)

I.                 Protection measures have the purpose of interrupting and preventing an act of violence against women, or guaranteeing, in the event that it has been consummated, that the corresponding investigation, prosecution and sanction is carried out.
II.                Protection measures are of immediate application, imposed by the competent authority to safeguard the life, physical, psychological, sexual integrity, patrimonial, economic and labor rights of women in situations of violence and those of their dependents.

#### ARTICLE 33. (REVICTIMIZATION)

Judicial or administrative procedures for the protection of women in situations of violence must apply the principle of dignified treatment contained in this Law, under liability in cases of non-observance.

#### ARTICLE 34. (COMPLAINT IN JUDICIAL PROCESS)

If during the processing of a proceeding the female or male judge becomes aware of acts of violence against a woman, he or she has the obligation, under liability, to refer the background of the act to the Public Prosecutor's Office for processing through criminal proceedings. Judges in family matters shall adopt the protection measures they consider appropriate to guarantee the life and integrity of the woman, her daughters and sons who may be at risk.

#### ARTICLE 35. (PROTECTION MEASURES)

The protection measures that the competent authority may order are the following:
1.      Order the departure, vacation, restriction of the aggressor from the marital home or where the woman in a situation of violence lives, regardless of proof of ownership or possession of the property, and order that the aggressor undergo psychological therapy in a rehabilitation service.
2.      Prohibit the aggressor from alienating, mortgaging, pledging, disposing of or changing the ownership title of common movable or immovable property.
3.      Arrange family assistance in favor of daughters, sons and the woman.
4.      Prohibit the aggressor from approaching, attending or entering the home, place of work or study, home of the ascendants or descendants, or any other space frequented by the woman who is in a situation of violence.
5.      Return the woman to the home from which she had been removed with violence, when she requests it, with sufficient guarantees to protect her life and integrity.
6.      Prohibit the aggressor from communicating, intimidating or harassing by any means or through third parties, the woman who is in a situation of violence, as well as any member of her family.
7.      Prohibit actions of intimidation, threats or coercion against witnesses to acts of violence.
8.      Temporarily suspend the aggressor from the visitation and cohabitation regime with his daughters and sons.
9.      Conduct an inventory of movable and immovable property of common ownership or legitimate possession.
10.    Order the immediate delivery of personal objects and documents of the woman and her daughters and sons or dependents.
11.    Retain ownership documents of movable or immovable property, while the reparation of damage is decided.
12.    Arrange the tolerance or reduction of working hours of the woman who is in a situation of violence, without affecting her labor and salary rights.
13.    Order the preventive notation of the aggressor's property subject to registration, as well as the freezing of bank accounts to guarantee family assistance obligations.
14.    Ensure the succession rights of women.
15.    Order the removal of the aggressor from sexual harassment in the workplace.
16.    Arrange measures to prevent discrimination in selection, qualification, permanence and promotion in her employment source.
17.    Restrict, in case of sexual harassment, all contact of the aggressor with the woman, without affecting the woman's labor rights.
18.    Order any precautionary measure of protection for women who are in a situation of violence indicated in the Code of Criminal Procedure and the Code of Civil Procedure.
19.    All those that guarantee the integrity of women who are in a situation of violence.

#### ARTICLE 36. (PROTECTION OF GIRLS AND BOYS)

If as a consequence of a crime of feminicide committed by the spouse or cohabitant, minor daughters and sons are left orphaned, they shall be immediately placed under the custody of the grandparents or another close relative through the maternal line, with the accompaniment of the Office for the Defense of Children and Adolescents until legal guardianship is established, and the entire family must have access to the victim and witness protection system of the Public Prosecutor's Office and the care system provided by this Law.

#### ARTICLE 37. (ALERT AGAINST VIOLENCE TOWARD WOMEN)

I.                 The Executive Branch, through the Governing Entity, shall declare an alert against violence in a determined area or sector at the national level, as the case may be, in relation to specific areas in which an alarming rate of cases of violence toward women is detected, expressed in any of its forms. In this case, all instances with responsibility and competence must activate emergency measures, actions and resources to address the problem efficiently and resolve it, preserving the rights of women.
II.                The declaration of alert against violence toward women shall be issued when:
1.      A high rate of crimes against the life, liberty and physical, psychological or sexual integrity of women is registered in a determined territory.
2.      A special area is detected in which cases of violence against women are reported and which as a consequence prevents the full exercise of their human rights.
III.               The Autonomous Territorial Entities may also declare a violence alert in all or part of their respective jurisdictions.

#### ARTICLE 38. (ATTENTION IN CASE OF ALERT)

When the alert against violence toward women is declared, the Governing Entity shall adopt the following immediate and mandatory measures:
1.      Establish a commission formed by a specialized inter-institutional and multidisciplinary technical team that carries out the respective follow-up, chaired and financed by the responsible entity.
2.      Implement with intensive character the prevention, attention and protection actions, to address and reduce cases of violence in the area or zone subject to the alert, and the Highest Executive Authorities of public entities and institutions and Autonomous Territorial Entities must reallocate the economic resources required to execute actions demanded by the attention to the alert, applying for this purpose the same procedure as that determined for the declaration of emergency situations.
3.      Prepare special reports on the progress achieved, through permanent monitoring that allows determining the conditions of women with respect to violence and evaluating the attention and protection mechanisms, as well as women's access to them, which includes recommendations for their strengthening.
4.      Disseminate for public knowledge the reason for the alert against violence toward women and the territorial zone or area covered by the measures to be implemented.

#### ARTICLE 39. (DURATION)

The alert against violence toward women shall subsist as long as the causes that gave rise to its declaration prevail, but it may not be extended for more than one (1) year.

#### ARTICLE 40. (RESPONSIBILITY)

In the event that at the end of this time the risk conditions for women have not changed, the actions of the entities responsible for the application of the determined emergency measures shall be evaluated in order to establish responsibilities for omission and non-compliance with functions within the framework of current regulations, which determine administrative, civil and criminal responsibilities.

#### ARTICLE 41. (ATTENTION IN INDIGENOUS NATIVE PEASANT COMMUNITIES)

I.                 The authorities of indigenous native peasant and Afro-Bolivian communities shall adopt measures of attention and protection for women in situations of violence, within the framework of their competencies and their own norms and procedures, with participation of women who hold positions of authority, and with community participation and social control.
II.                All cases of sexual violence, feminicide and analogous crimes shall be referred to the ordinary jurisdiction, in accordance with the Law of Jurisdictional Demarcation.
III.               The indigenous native peasant authorities may refer known cases to the Community Houses for Women, so that the woman in a situation of violence receives appropriate attention.
IV.               Cases that are attended to and resolved shall be reported to the Plurinational Comprehensive System for Prevention, Attention, Sanction and Eradication of Gender-Based Violence – SIPPASE, for its corresponding registration.

## TITLE IV

CRIMINAL PROSECUTION AND SANCTION

### CHAPTER I

COMPLAINT

#### ARTICLE 42. (COMPLAINT)

I.                 Any act of violence against women may be reported by the victim or any other person who has knowledge of a crime, to the following instances:
1.      Bolivian Police.
2.      Public Prosecutor's Office.
II.                In order to promote the complaint, the following institutions may be approached:
1.      Municipal Comprehensive Legal Services.
2.      Children and Adolescent Ombudsman's Offices, when the assaulted person is under 18 years of age.
3.      Integrated Services of Plurinational Justice.
4.      Plurinational Victim Defense Service.
5.      Indigenous native peasant authorities, when applicable.
III.               Once the complaint is known, it must be immediately referred to the Public Prosecutor's Office when it constitutes a crime, except in the case of paragraph II numeral 5, and consequently, reported to the Plurinational Comprehensive System for Prevention, Attention, Sanction and Eradication of Gender-Based Violence – SIPPASE.

#### ARTICLE 43. (OBLIGATIONS)

The instances for receiving, investigating and processing complaints must provide women with support and dignified and respectful treatment, in accordance with their situation, facilitating to the maximum extent the procedures they must carry out. Consequently, in addition to the obligations conferred by Law, they must:
1.      Advise them on the importance and manner of preserving evidence.
2.      Provide them with information about the rights they have and those special rights that the Law recognizes them and about the governmental and non-governmental services available for their care and treatment.
3.      Request the care that the woman requires, from the Comprehensive Care Services.
4.      Prepare a report containing all the elements that it has known, detected or determined, that serve to clarify the facts, to attach to the complaint.
5.      Resolve any consultation, doubt or request for information that the woman or her relatives need or demand, as well as provide that which it additionally considers necessary to guarantee her protection.

#### ARTICLE 44. (SPECIALIZED INTERDISCIPLINARY PERSONNEL)

The personnel responsible for receiving, investigating and processing complaints must be specialized or have work experience in human rights, women's rights or in addressing situations of gender-based violence.

#### ARTICLE 45. (GUARANTEES)

To ensure the exercise of all her rights and her effective protection, the State shall guarantee to every woman in a situation of violence:
1.      Access to justice in a free, real, timely and effective manner, through due process in which she is heard with due guarantees and within a reasonable time.
2.      The adoption of fair and independent judicial decisions, without gender biases or subjective criteria that affect or hinder the assessment of evidence and the consequent sanction of the aggressor.
3.      Access to immediate, timely and specialized protection services, from the moment the act constituting violence is brought to the attention of ordinary or indigenous native peasant and Afro-Bolivian authorities.
4.      Immediate, free and specialized legal guidance and assistance.
5.      Quality and warm care, support and reception to achieve her comprehensive recovery through multidisciplinary and specialized services.
6.      Access to clear, complete, truthful and timely information about judicial, police and other actions carried out in relation to her case, as well as about the mechanisms and procedures contemplated in this Law and other concordant regulations.
7.      The protection of her dignity and integrity, avoiding revictimization and mistreatment that she could receive from any person responsible for her care, treatment or investigation of the act.
8.      The investigation of the truth, reparation of the damage and prevention of the repetition of acts of violence.
9.      Access to the care they require for their physical and psychological recovery, in public services, short-term social insurance and private services, especially prophylactic treatment to prevent sexually transmitted infections, HIV/AIDS and emergency contraception, immediately and in a timely manner.
10.    Access to immediate, timely and specialized care and protection services by judicial, police, Public Prosecutor's Office, administrative, indigenous native peasant authorities, as well as health personnel.

#### ARTICLE 46. (PROHIBITION OF CONCILIATION)

I.                 Conciliation is prohibited in any act of violence against women that compromises their life and sexual integrity. No institution receiving complaints nor its personnel may promote conciliation or the signing of any type of agreement between the woman and her aggressor, under liability.
II.                In cases not provided for in the preceding paragraph, the Public Prosecutor's Office must impose the security measures that are necessary in order to preserve the physical, psychological and sexual integrity of women.
III.               Conciliation under pressure on the victim or to avoid procedural burden is not recognized, under official liability.
IV.               Exceptionally, conciliation may be promoted only by the victim, only once and not being possible in cases of recidivism.

#### ARTICLE 47. (PREFERENTIAL APPLICATION OF LAW)

In case of conflict or collision between individual and collective rights, preference shall be given to the rights for the dignity of women, recognized in international Human Rights treaties, in the Political Constitution of the State and in this Law.

#### ARTICLE 48. (INTEGRATED SERVICES OF PLURINATIONAL JUSTICE)

I.                 The Integrated Services of Plurinational Justice dependent on the Ministry of Justice and Institutional Transparency, receive complaints and provide guidance and free legal representation, must apply a human rights approach to women in situations of violence.
II.                The Ministry of Justice and Institutional Transparency must progressively create and implement these services throughout the country.

#### ARTICLE 49. (PLURINATIONAL VICTIM DEFENSE SERVICE)

The Plurinational Victim Defense Service, as a decentralized institution under the supervision of the Ministry of Justice and Institutional Transparency, shall support women in situations of violence lacking economic resources, through free legal representation and psychological support to guarantee their access to the administration of justice and the sanction of aggressors. For the fulfillment of this purpose, this service shall exercise its functions in attention to achieving the most favorable solution for the victim.

#### ARTICLE 50. (MUNICIPAL COMPREHENSIVE LEGAL SERVICES)

I.                 Autonomous Municipal Governments have the obligation to organize these services or strengthen them if they already exist, with a permanent and free character, for the psychological, social and legal protection and defense of women in situations of violence, to guarantee the validity and full exercise of their rights. For their operation, they shall allocate the necessary and sufficient budget, infrastructure and personnel to provide adequate, effective and specialized attention to the entire population, especially those living in the rural area of their respective jurisdiction.
II.                Within the framework of their competencies, Autonomous Municipal Governments, through the Municipal Comprehensive Legal Services, shall have the following responsibilities with respect to women in situations of violence:
1.      Organize, coordinate and strengthen Comprehensive Care Services, charged to their annual budget, as permanent support instances.
2.      Provide psychological, social and legal support services.
3.      Provide specialized individual and group psychological therapy with a gender approach.
4.      Provide guidance regarding procedures to file complaints before administrative, police or judicial instances in criminal, family, labor, civil or any other matter in which their rights are diminished as a consequence of acts of violence.
5.      Intervene immediately upon the complaint of an act of violence against a woman.
6.      Provide free legal representation in administrative, police and judicial instances for the prosecution of proceedings until obtaining a final judgment.
7.      Promote the dissemination, defense and exercise of women's rights with the active participation of citizens.
8.      Develop prevention actions, in coordination with public, private institutions and women's organizations.
9.      Request, through the competent authority, the judicial adoption of provisional measures, precautionary measures and immediate protection measures, coordinating their compliance with Shelters, support institutions and health care institutions.
10.    Conduct home visits for support and follow-up and social reports.
11.    Refer to the Public Prosecutor's Office, immediately, cases that constitute a crime, with the corresponding reports.
12.    Promote the execution of family assistance agreements and their approval by the competent authority.
13.    Prepare medical, psychological, social and legal reports ex officio or at the request of the interested party, the Public Prosecutor's Office or the judicial authority hearing the act of violence.
14.    Report all complaints received, the procedure applied and the final result of the case, to the Plurinational Comprehensive System for Prevention, Care, Punishment and Eradication of Gender-Based Violence –SIPPASE.
15.    Whatever action is necessary for the defense and protection of women in situations of violence.

#### ARTICLE 51. (TRAINING)

Autonomous Municipal Governments shall adopt a system of permanent training for their personnel on Human Rights, especially women's rights, gender approach and affirmative action measures and, for those who are directly responsible for their care, shall also include basic training in the psychology of battered women.

#### ARTICLE 52. (INDIGENOUS NATIVE PEASANT AUTHORITIES)

I.                 For the purposes of this Law, the scopes of validity established in the Law of Jurisdictional Demarcation shall be applicable, in cases of conflicts of interest the case shall be referred to the ordinary jurisdiction.
II.                In case of conflict of competencies between the indigenous native peasant jurisdiction and the ordinary jurisdiction, it shall be resolved according to the provisions of the Constitutional Procedural Code.
III.               Conciliation may be carried out within the framework of what is established in Article 46 of this Law.

### CHAPTER II

INVESTIGATION

#### ARTICLE 53. (SPECIAL FORCE TO COMBAT VIOLENCE)

I.                 The Special Force to Combat Violence is created as a specialized body of the Bolivian Police responsible for the prevention, assistance and investigation, identification and apprehension of those allegedly responsible for acts of violence against women and the family, under the functional direction of the Public Prosecutor's Office, in coordination with public and private entities. Its structure, organization and procedures shall be established according to regulations and shall have four levels of action.
II.                The permanence of special investigators is guaranteed, in accordance with Article 80 of the Law of the Public Prosecutor's Office.

#### ARTICLE 54. (PLATFORM FOR CARE AND RECEPTION OF COMPLAINTS)

The Special Force to Combat Violence, at its level of care and reception of complaints, shall have the following functions:
1.      Receive complaints from women in situations of violence or from third parties who are aware of the act.
2.      Carry out proceedings aimed at the individualization of the authors and participants, ensure their appearance, apprehend them immediately in case of flagrant crime and place them at the disposal of the Public Prosecutor's Office, within a maximum period of eight (8) hours.
3.      In case of flagrancy, assist the assaulted persons and their daughters and sons or other dependents, even when they are inside a home, without the need for a warrant or limitation of time and day, with the sole purpose of providing them protection and preventing further assaults.
4.      Draw up a record of the events that occurred, for which they must collect information from family members, neighbors or other persons present.
5.      Gather and secure all evidence.
6.      Confiscate weapons and objects used to threaten and assault, placing them at the disposal of the Public Prosecutor's Office.
7.      Guide victims about the remedies that the Law confers on them and the care and protection services available to them.
8.      Take the assaulted person to health services, promoting their immediate care.
9.      Draw up an inventory and inform the Judge or Public Prosecutor's Office.
10.    If the woman in a situation of violence requests it, accompany and assist her while she removes her personal belongings from her home or another place, preventing the retention of any personal effects or documents and take her where she indicates or to a shelter or temporary refuge.
11.    Follow up with the woman for seventy-two (72) hours, in order to guarantee the effectiveness of the protection provided to the woman in a situation of violence and other persons who may be at risk.

#### ARTICLE 55. (MOBILE UNITS AGAINST VIOLENCE)

The Special Force to Combat Violence, in its mobile care, shall have specialized equipment and personnel for the reception of complaints and immediate assistance, in coordination with the Prosecutors of the Matter, at the place where the act occurs, prioritizing its action in rural areas.

#### ARTICLE 56. (DECONCENTRATED SERVICES)

I.                 The Special Force to Combat Violence, in the Comprehensive Police Stations or places where the Bolivian Police provides services, shall have specialized personnel to handle complaints of violence, investigative proceedings and others under the direction of the Public Prosecutor's Office.
II.                All proceedings carried out by these services shall be referred to the investigation level and shall have evidentiary value.

#### ARTICLE 57. (DIVISION OF VIOLENCE CRIMES)

In addition to the general functions assigned to it by current regulations and the Bolivian Police for the investigation of crimes, this division has the following specific functions:
1.      Coordinate and execute legal operational procedures in force and the investigation of crimes against life, against bodily integrity and health, against sexual freedom, of economic and patrimonial violence, and others that constitute violence against women.
2.      Receive the proceedings carried out in preventive police intervention, complaints and criminal complaints, through the platform for attention and reception of complaints, acts that shall have the quality of evidence.

#### ARTICLE 58. (MEASURES OF ACTION)

I.                 The Special Force to Combat Violence shall adapt its actions to the protocols adopted for the reception of complaints, immediate attention and referral of cases of violence against women. Mandatorily, it shall adopt the following measures of action:
1.      Assist, guide and evaluate the attention and protection that must be provided to women in situations of violence through a multidisciplinary team.
2.      Respect and protect the dignity, privacy and rights of women in situations of violence.
3.      Avoid contact, confrontation or any type of proximity of the woman with her aggressor.
4.      Carry out coordination actions with all instances of the comprehensive system of attention to women in situations of violence.
5.      Avoid any action that implies revictimization, under liability.
6.      Organize and design prevention and guidance campaigns for citizens, through civil society organizations, in order to reduce the rates of violence against women.
II.                No female or male police officer shall deny assistance and support to women in situations of violence alleging lack of jurisdiction, even if they are not part of the Special Force to Combat Violence.

#### ARTICLE 59. (EX OFFICIO INVESTIGATION)

I.                 The investigation shall proceed ex officio, independently of the complainant's initiative. Every complaint must be mandatorily referred to the Public Prosecutor's Office and reported to the Plurinational Comprehensive System for Prevention, Attention, Sanction and Eradication of Gender-Based Violence – SIPPASE, indicating the course it has followed.
II.                When there is imminent danger to the physical integrity of persons protected by the Law, compliance with formalities shall not become an impediment to the timely intervention of the police.

#### ARTICLE 60. (INFRASTRUCTURE AND EQUIPMENT)

The Special Force to Combat Violence, at all its levels of action, shall be provided, with priority, with specialized and multidisciplinary personnel, adequate infrastructure and equipment, within the framework of the Citizen Security Law "For a Safe Life".

### CHAPTER III

CRIMINAL PROSECUTION

#### ARTICLE 61. (PUBLIC PROSECUTOR'S OFFICE)

In addition to the common powers established by the Organic Law of the Public Prosecutor's Office, the Subject Matter Prosecutors who exercise public criminal action in cases of violence against women must adopt the following measures in the exercise of their functions:
1.      Adoption of the protection measures that are necessary, in order to guarantee the woman in a situation of violence maximum protection and security, as well as her daughters and sons, request the jurisdictional authority for their approval and the precautionary measures provided by Law, when the act constitutes a crime.
2.      Collection of the necessary evidence, as responsible for the investigation of crimes of gender-based violence, without subjecting the woman to medical tests, interrogations, reconstructions or expert examinations that are not essential, and must resort to alternative, scientific investigation methods with the support of technology, in order to ascertain the truth.
3.      In case technical expert examinations are required, they shall not be demanded of the woman. In case of flagrant crime, the accused shall be responsible for paying for these, as well as for the medical and psychological treatment that the woman requires; if provably insolvent, recourse shall be made to the services of the Comprehensive Care System of their jurisdiction.
4.      Direct the investigation of the police instances responsible for the investigation of crimes linked to violence against women, defining common protocols and criteria for action, in order to standardize procedures, preserve evidence and achieve registration and follow-up of cases until their conclusion, generating statistics at the municipal, departmental and national level.
5.      Coordination of the action criteria of the various instances for receiving complaints of cases of violence against women, for which the Attorney General of the State shall issue the corresponding instructions.
6.      Preparation and biannual presentation to the Attorney General of the State, for consolidation at the departmental and national level, a report on the procedures applied and the actions carried out by the Public Prosecutor's Office in matters of violence against women and cases that compromise their rights.
7.      Request the assignment of state legal representation to the woman in a situation of violence lacking economic resources.
8.      Request interpretation or translation when necessary and arrange for specialized assistance, avoiding all forms of revictimization.
9.      When appropriate, arrange for the admission of direct and indirect victims of crimes against their life, their bodily integrity or their sexual freedom to the Unit for Attention and Protection of Victims and Witnesses of Crimes.
10.    Send a copy of the rejection resolutions and conclusive requirements to the Departmental Prosecutor in ex officio investigations and submit to the Plurinational Comprehensive System for Prevention, Attention, Sanction and Eradication of Gender-Based Violence – SIPPASE, their biannual reports, with details of all cases handled, disaggregated at least by sex, age and type of crime.

#### ARTICLE 62. (MOBILE SUBJECT MATTER PROSECUTORS)

In rural areas, the Subject Matter Prosecutors specialized against violence against women must travel regularly and permanently.

#### ARTICLE 63. (SPECIALIZED ADVICE)

The Subject Matter Prosecutors against violence against women shall have specialized support personnel, to provide each woman in a situation of violence with effective and adequate attention. In each Department the Public Prosecutor's Office shall have at least one team of specialized professional advisors for the investigation of cases of violence against women, for which they may also request the collaboration of human rights and women's organizations and institutions.

#### ARTICLE 64. (FORENSIC DOCTORS)

Forensic doctors with specialty in gender violence shall be appointed, who must attend to women in situations of violence with maximum respect, care, warmth and understanding. The reports they issue, under liability, must be expeditious and timely, and must avoid as much as possible repeated and unnecessary medical examinations.

#### ARTICLE 66. (ACCESS TO DOCUMENTATION)

Any assaulted woman may request simple or certified copies of all proceedings contained in the investigation, from the moment of the complaint, which must be granted expeditiously, without prior notification and without additional cost beyond that of photocopies.

#### ARTICLE 67. (SPECIALIZED FORENSIC DIRECTORATE)

The Attorney General of the State, within the framework of their powers, shall create and regulate within the Forensic Investigations Institute, a directorate specialized in cases of violence against women, with the necessary personnel to guarantee its effective operation.

### CHAPTER IV

ORDINARY JURISDICTION

#### ARTICLE 68. (COURTS OF SUBJECT MATTER JURISDICTION AGAINST VIOLENCE TOWARDS WOMEN)

Articles 57, 58, 68 and 72 of Law No. 025, Law of the Judicial Branch, are modified with the following text:
"Article 57. (POWERS OF THE CHAMBERS IN MATTERS OF FAMILY, CHILDREN AND ADOLESCENTS). The powers of the chambers in matters of family, children and adolescents are:
1.      To hear on appeal, the resolutions issued by judges in matters of family, children and adolescents;
2.      To resolve in consultation or review, the resolutions when the Law so determines;
3.      To resolve excuses presented by their justices and chamber secretaries;
4.      To resolve recusations filed against their justices;
5.      To resolve excuses and recusations against judges in matters of family, children and adolescents and;
6.      Others established by Law.

#### Article 58. (POWERS OF THE CHAMBERS IN CRIMINAL MATTERS). The powers of the chambers in criminal matters are

1.      To process and resolve according to Law the appeals of orders and judgments of courts in criminal matters and against violence towards women;
2.      To resolve excuses presented by their justices and chamber secretaries;
3.      To resolve recusations filed against their justices and;
4.      Others established by Law.

#### Article 68. (SUBSTITUTIONS)

In cases of excuse and recusation or any other impediment of the judge, the case shall pass to the knowledge of the next in number of the same subject matter and, by impediment of all those corresponding to the same subject matter, the order of substitutions shall be the following:
1.      From civil and commercial, it shall pass to those of family and criminal, in that order;
2.      From family, it shall pass to those of civil and commercial matters, and against violence towards women, in that order;
3.      From children and adolescents, it shall pass to those of family matters and against violence towards women, in that order;
4.      From violence towards women, it shall pass to those of criminal and family matters, in that order;
5.      From labor and social security, it shall pass to those of civil and commercial matters, and criminal, in that order;
6.      From administrative, fiscal coercive and tax, it shall pass to those of labor matters and criminal, in that order;
7.      From criminal, it shall pass to those of matters against violence towards women and civil and commercial, in that order;
8.      From anti-corruption, it shall pass to those of criminal matters;
9.      From criminal execution, it shall pass to those of criminal matters;
10.    Others established by Law.

#### Article 72. (JURISDICTION OF PRELIMINARY INVESTIGATION COURTS OF SUBJECT MATTER JURISDICTION AGAINST VIOLENCE TOWARDS WOMEN)

The preliminary investigation judges against violence towards women have jurisdiction to:
1.      Control of the investigation, in accordance with the powers and duties provided in the Law;
2.      Issue the jurisdictional and protection resolutions that correspond during the preparatory stage and the application of opportunity criteria;
3.      The processing and resolution of the abbreviated procedure;
4.      Resolve the application of the immediate procedure for flagrant crimes;
5.      Conduct the trial preparation hearing and resolve the issues and incidents raised therein;
6.      Decide the suspension of the trial on probation;
7.      Decide on requests for international judicial cooperation;
8.      Hear and resolve on the seizure of assets and its incidents; and
9.      Others established by Law.

#### Article 72

bis (JURISDICTION OF TRIAL COURTS IN MATTERS OF VIOLENCE AGAINST WOMEN). The trial judges in matters of violence against women have jurisdiction to:
1.      Hear and resolve trials for public action crimes that constitute violence against women, punishable by deprivation of liberty whose legal maximum is four years or less;
2.      Apply restriction and provisional measures to the aggressor, and assistance and protection to the woman in a situation of violence, when the act does not constitute a crime;
3.      The procedure for reparation of damages, when a conviction has been issued;
4.      Impose ex officio the application of protection measures, that allow women in situations of violence their access to shelters, temporary separation of spouses and/or cohabitants and prevention of new aggressions and any other intended to safeguard their rights;
5.      Sanction non-compliance with judicial orders or resolutions, issued by their court;
6.             Sanction judicial support staff who engage in mistreatment or revictimization of women in situations of violence and;
7.      Others established by Law.

#### Article 72

ter. (JURISDICTION OF TRIAL TRIBUNALS IN MATTERS OF VIOLENCE AGAINST WOMEN). The Trial Tribunals against violence towards women have jurisdiction to:
1.      Hear the processing and resolution of criminal trials in all public action crimes that constitute violence against women, punishable by deprivation of liberty greater than four (4) years, with the exceptions established in the Law and;
2.      Others established by Law."

#### ARTICLE 69. (APPOINTMENT)

To be a judge and auxiliary officials of these courts, in addition to the requirements indicated by Law, the following shall be required:
1.      Specialty in criminal matters and knowledge of gender or, alternatively, of human rights; progressively, the requirement must reach an academic level that denotes specialty in these subjects.
2.      Certificate of sufficiency issued by the Council of the Magistracy, of psychotechnical aptitude for the performance of the position, that allows measuring personality traits, interests and personal values.

#### ARTICLE 70. (SPECIALIZED TRAINING)

The State Judges School shall enter into agreements with universities that can organize postgraduate courses in the specialties required for the exercise of the judicial function, to require that those who aspire to be judges of subject matter jurisdiction against violence towards women have a specialization in criminal matters with a gender and human rights approach.

#### ARTICLE 71. (INTERDISCIPLINARY TEAM)

The Public Courts and Tribunals of Subject Matter Jurisdiction against Violence towards Women shall have an interdisciplinary team from the social and psychological areas specialized in human rights and women's rights or with work experience in the care of violence, issuing technical expert reports of a comprehensive nature. These services may be provided, with equal legal value, by professionals assigned by the Comprehensive Care Services.

#### ARTICLE 72. (FUNCTIONS). The functions of the interdisciplinary team are

1.      To intervene as independent and impartial specialists in judicial proceedings, performing comprehensive technical expert assessments.
2.      To implement the protocol for attention to testimonies and statements of children and adolescents according to their age and degree of maturity, of young and adult women in situations of violence in order not to alter their recovery process through the repetition of interrogations, and to avoid revictimization.
3.      To monitor compliance with therapies for victims, aggressors and/or family members ordered by the judicial authority, reporting the progress, abandonment or completion thereof.

#### ARTICLE 73. (AUXILIARY SERVICES)

When the case requires it, the female judge, male judge or tribunal may order expert assessments and other technical assistance services in other matters and professions that support their work.

#### ARTICLE 74. (REPORTING OF CASES)

The Council of the Magistracy must report to the Plurinational Comprehensive System for Prevention, Attention, Sanction and Eradication of Gender-Based Violence – SIPPASE, the movement of cases of violence against women, on a quarterly basis, breaking down all information, in addition to regular indicators, by gender and age of the parties, crime, status of the proceeding.

#### ARTICLE 75. (DEFENSE ACTION)

Constitutional defense actions may be filed by women, or other persons on their behalf when appropriate, in order to guarantee the full exercise of their rights.

## TITLE V

CRIMINAL LEGISLATION

### CHAPTER I

ALTERNATIVE SANCTIONS

#### ARTICLE 76. (APPLICATION OF ALTERNATIVE SANCTIONS)

I.                 In crimes of violence against women, provided that the perpetrator is not a repeat offender, alternative sanctions to deprivation of liberty may be applied, when:
1.      The imposed sentence is not greater than three years, in which case it shall be replaced by an alternative sanction from those indicated in this Law.
2.      At the request of the person sentenced to deprivation of liberty exceeding three years who has served at least half of it, the alternative sanctions may not exceed the time of the principal sentence imposed.
II.                The judicial authority shall apply one alternative sanction together with others, when necessary to protect the woman, sons and daughters or the family unit in a situation of violence.

#### ARTICLE 77. (FINE)

The imposition of a fine as an alternative or accessory sanction does not replace reparation to the woman for the damage caused as a result of the violence; it may not be greater than three hundred sixty-five days nor comprise for the calculation more than fifty percent (50%) of the sanctioned person's salary and when the salary is indeterminate, it shall be calculated on fifty percent (50%) of the national minimum wage. The day of deprivation is equivalent to one day-fine and is revocable upon non-compliance.
The fines shall be allocated to the Comprehensive Care Services in charge of the Municipal Autonomous Governments, which shall establish a fund and open an exclusive fiscal account for this purpose, and must allocate the resources with preferential character to the Shelters and Temporary Refuge and cost for health services. The funds may not be allocated to any other purpose.

#### ARTICLE 78. (WEEKEND DETENTION)

It is a limitation of liberty that is applied from Friday at 19:00 hours until Monday at 6:00 hours. For purposes of equivalence, the day of deprivation of liberty corresponds to one day of weekend detention. It may also be applied to holidays, under the same conditions.

#### ARTICLE 79. (COMMUNITY WORK)

Community work consists of the provision of work in favor of the Municipal Autonomous Government, which shall be performed on weekends, holidays and working days at times different from the usual ones. This sanction shall be applied for a minimum of one (1) year which is equivalent to work of fifty-two (52) weeks, with their respective holidays and working days, and a maximum of up to one hundred four (104) weeks.
The Municipal Autonomous Government must supervise and report compliance with the sanction to the competent court and to the Plurinational Comprehensive System for Prevention, Attention, Sanction and Eradication of Gender-Based Violence – SIPPASE.

#### ARTICLE 80. (SECURITY MEASURES)

The judicial authority in execution of sentence, when alternative sanctions have been ordered, shall apply the necessary security measures to protect the woman who is in a situation of violence, and her daughters and sons or her family unit.

#### ARTICLE 81. (DISQUALIFICATION)

The sanction of disqualification may be applied when a person sanctioned for crimes of violence against women exercises a profession or occupation related to education, care and attention of persons, regardless of their age or situation, medical, psychological care, counseling or advising, administrative position in universities or educational units, sports, military, police institutions; temporary suspension of parental authority for the duration of the sanction, closure of premises and loss of licenses. It has a time limit of twelve years and not all restrictions of those rights may be imposed in a single sentence. After half of the imposed term has elapsed, or a minimum of five years, rehabilitation may be granted.

#### ARTICLE 82. (COMPLIANCE WITH INSTRUCTIONS)

The judicial authority may apply a conduct plan to the convicted person when alternative sanctions that imply total or partial liberty are applied, by virtue of which the person must comply with instructions that may not be vexatious or susceptible to offending dignity or self-esteem. They may be modified during the execution of sentence and may not extend beyond the time that the principal sentence lasts. The instructions that may be imposed shall be:
1.      Prohibition from carrying any type of weapon, especially firearms;
2.      Refrain from attending public places where alcoholic beverages are sold and brothels;
3.      Refrain from consuming drugs or alcohol;
4.      Join groups or programs to modify behaviors that have influenced the commission of the act;
5.      Attend an educational center or learn a trade.

### CHAPTER II

CRIMES OF VIOLENCE AGAINST WOMEN

#### ARTICLE 83. (AMENDMENTS TO THE CRIMINAL CODE)

Articles 246, 254, 256, 267 bis, 270, 271, 272, 308, 308 bis, 310, 312 and 313 of the Criminal Code are amended, which shall be worded with the following text:
"Article 246. (ABDUCTION OF A MINOR OR INCAPACITATED PERSON). Whoever abducts a minor under sixteen years (16) of age or an incapacitated person, from the authority of their parents, adopters, guardians or curators, and whoever retains the minor against their will, shall be sanctioned with deprivation of liberty from one (1) to three (3) years.
The same penalty shall apply if the minor is over sixteen years (16) of age and there is no consent on their part.
The penalty shall be aggravated by double if the crime is committed by one of the parents with the purpose of exercising any type of coercion against the other.

#### Article 254. (HOMICIDE BY VIOLENT EMOTION)

Whoever kills another person in a state of excusable violent emotion, shall be sanctioned with imprisonment from two (2) to eight (8) years.
This criminal type shall not apply in case of feminicide.

#### Article 256. (HOMICIDE-SUICIDE)

A person who instigates another to suicide or assists in committing it, if death has been attempted or consummated, shall incur imprisonment of two (2) to six (6) years.
If injuries are produced as a result of the attempt, the imprisonment sanction shall be one (1) to five (5) years.
Even if the victim's consent has been given in a double suicide, the survivor shall be imposed a penalty of imprisonment of two (2) to six (6) years.
When a person commits suicide as a consequence of a situation of violence, the female or male aggressor shall be sanctioned with deprivation of liberty of ten (10) years.
If the victim of the crime in any of the cases of this Article turns out to be a girl, boy or adolescent, the penalty shall be aggravated by two thirds.

#### Article 267

bis. (FORCED ABORTION). Whoever through physical, psychological or sexual violence against a woman causes her an abortion, shall be sanctioned with imprisonment of four (4) to eight (8) years.

#### Article 270. (VERY SERIOUS INJURIES)

Whoever in any manner causes another person an injury from which any of the following consequences result shall be sanctioned with deprivation of liberty of five (5) to twelve (12) years:
1.      Psychic, intellectual, physical, sensory or multiple illness or disability.
2.      Permanent psychological or psychiatric damage.
3.      Permanent weakening of health or total or partial loss of a sense, a limb, an organ or a function.
4.      Permanent incapacity for work or exceeding ninety days.
5.      Indelible mark or permanent deformation in any part of the body.
6.      Imminent danger of losing life.
When the victim is a girl, boy or adolescent the penalty shall be aggravated by two thirds in both the minimum and the maximum.

#### Article 271. (SERIOUS AND MINOR INJURIES)

Whoever in any manner causes another person physical or psychological harm, not included in the cases of the preceding Article, from which derives incapacity for work of fifteen (15) to ninety (90) days, shall be sanctioned with deprivation of liberty of three (3) to six (6) years.
If the incapacity is up to fourteen (14) days, the perpetrator shall be imposed a sanction of community work of one (1) to three (3) years and compliance with instructions that the female or male judge determines.
When the victim is a girl, boy or adolescent the penalty shall be aggravated by two thirds in both the minimum and the maximum.

#### Article 272. (AGGRAVATING CIRCUMSTANCE)

In the cases of Articles 267 bis, 270 and 271, the sanction shall be aggravated by one third of the maximum or minimum, when the circumstances enumerated in Article 252 are present, except that provided in numeral 1.

#### Article 308. (RAPE)

Whoever through intimidation, physical or psychological violence performs with a person of either sex, non-consensual sexual acts that involve carnal access, through penetration of the male member, or of any other part of the body, or of any object, by vaginal, anal or oral route, with libidinous purposes, shall be sanctioned with deprivation of liberty of fifteen (15) to twenty (20) years; and whoever, under the same circumstances, even if physical violence or intimidation did not occur, taking advantage of the serious mental illness or insufficiency of intelligence of the victim or that the victim was incapacitated by any other cause to resist.

#### Article 308

bis. (RAPE OF INFANT, GIRL, BOY OR ADOLESCENT). If the crime of rape is committed against a person of either sex under fourteen (14) years of age, it shall be sanctioned with deprivation of liberty of twenty (20) to twenty-five (25) years, even if there is no use of force or intimidation and consent is alleged.
In case any of the aggravating circumstances provided in Article 310 of the Penal Code is evidenced, and the penalty reaches thirty (30) years, the penalty shall be without right to pardon.
Consensual relations between adolescents over twelve (12) years of age are exempt from this sanction, provided that there is no age difference greater than three (3) years between both and no violence or intimidation has been committed.

#### Article 310. (AGGRAVATING CIRCUMSTANCE)

The penalty shall be aggravated in the cases of the preceding crimes, by five (5) years when:
a)      As a product of the rape any of the circumstances provided in Articles 270 and 271 of this Code occurred;
b)      The act occurs in front of girls, boys or adolescents;
c)      Two or more persons concurred in the execution of the act;
d)      The act occurs while the victim is in a state of unconsciousness;
e)      In the commission of the act weapons or other dangerous means capable of producing the death of the victim were used;
f)       The perpetrator is a spouse, cohabitant, or with whom the victim maintains or had maintained an analogous relationship of intimacy;
g)      The perpetrator is in charge of the education of the victim, or if the victim is in a situation of dependence with respect to the perpetrator;
h)      The perpetrator has subjected the victim to humiliating or degrading conditions.
i)        The victim has some degree of disability;
j)        If the victim is over 60 years of age;
k)      If the victim is pregnant or if pregnancy results as a consequence of the act;
If the death of the victim occurs as a consequence of the act, the penalty corresponding to feminicide or murder shall be applied.

#### Article 312. (SEXUAL ABUSE)

When under the same circumstances and by the means indicated in Articles 308 and 308 bis sexual acts not constituting penetration or carnal access are performed, the penalty shall be six (6) to ten (10) years of deprivation of liberty. The aggravating circumstances provided in Article 310 shall apply, and if the victim is a girl, boy or adolescent the custodial penalty shall be ten (10) to fifteen (15) years.

#### Article 313. (ABDUCTION)

Whoever with lascivious purposes and through violence, serious threats or deception, abducts or detains a person, shall be sanctioned with deprivation of liberty of four (4) to eight (8) years. The penalty shall be aggravated by one third in both the minimum and the maximum, when the victim is a girl, boy or adolescent."

#### ARTICLE 84. (NEW CRIMINAL OFFENSES)

Articles 154 bis, 252 bis, 271 bis, 272 bis, 312 bis, 312 ter, 312 quater are incorporated into the Criminal Code, under the following text:
"Article 154 bis. (FAILURE TO FULFILL DUTIES OF PROTECTION FOR WOMEN IN SITUATIONS OF VIOLENCE). The public servant who through action or omission in the exercise of a public function fosters impunity or obstructs the investigation of crimes of violence against women, shall receive an alternative sanction of community work from ninety (90) days to one hundred twenty (120) days and disqualification from one (1) to four (4) years for the exercise of public function."
"Article 252 bis. (FEMINICIDE). The penalty of thirty (30) years imprisonment without right to pardon shall be imposed on whoever kills a woman, in any of the following circumstances:
1.      The perpetrator is or has been the spouse or cohabitant of the victim, is or has been linked to her by an analogous relationship of affection or intimacy, even without cohabitation;
2.      Because the victim refused to establish with the perpetrator, a relationship of partnership, courtship, affection or intimacy;
3.      Because the victim is pregnant;
4.      The victim is in a situation or relationship of subordination or dependence with respect to the perpetrator, or has with him a relationship of friendship, work or companionship;
5.      The victim is in a situation of vulnerability;
6.             When prior to the fact of death, the woman has been a victim of physical, psychological, sexual or economic violence, committed by the same aggressor;
7.      When the act has been preceded by a crime against individual freedom or sexual freedom;
8.      When the death is connected to the crime of trafficking or smuggling of persons;
9.      When the death is the result of rituals, group challenges or cultural practices."
"Article 271 bis. (FORCED STERILIZATION). The person who deprives another of their reproductive function temporarily or permanently without their express, voluntary, free and informed consent, or that of their legal representative in the case of a person with severe intellectual disability, shall be sanctioned with a custodial sentence of five (5) to twelve (12) years.
The penalty shall be aggravated by one third when the crime is committed against a minor woman or taking advantage of her condition of disability, or when the circumstances provided in Article 252 concur.
If the crime were committed as part of the crime of genocide perpetrated with the intention of destroying totally or partially a national, social, indigenous native peasant group or religious group as such, adopting measures intended to prevent births within the group, the penalty of thirty (30) years of deprivation of liberty without right to pardon shall apply."
"Article 272 bis. (FAMILY OR DOMESTIC VIOLENCE). Whoever physically, psychologically or sexually assaults within the cases included in numerals 1 to 4 of this Article shall incur a penalty of imprisonment of two (2) to four (4) years, provided it does not constitute another crime.
1.      The spouse or cohabitant or whoever maintains or had maintained with the victim an analogous relationship of affection or intimacy, even without cohabitation.
2.      The person who has procreated children with the victim, even without cohabitation.
3.      Ascendants or descendants, brothers, sisters, blood relatives or relatives by affinity in direct line and collateral up to the fourth degree.
4.      The person who is in charge of the care or custody of the victim, or if the victim is in the home, under a situation of dependence or authority.
In other cases the party may assert their claim before the corresponding venue."
"Article 312 bis. (ABUSIVE SEXUAL ACTS). The person who during consensual sexual relations, forces their partner or spouse to endure acts of physical violence and humiliation shall be sanctioned with deprivation of liberty from four (4) to six (6) years.
The penalty shall be aggravated by one third when the perpetrator forces their spouse, cohabitant or sexual partner to have sexual relations with third parties."
"Article 312 ter. (SEXUAL SUFFERING). Whoever, in the context of a widespread or systematic attack against a population or human group, engages in the following actions shall be sanctioned with a custodial sentence of fifteen (15) to thirty (30) years:
1.      Subjects one or more persons to rape or any form of sexual abuse, sexual humiliation and outrage.
2.      Subjects one or more persons to forced prostitution.
3.      Keeps confined a woman who has been forcibly impregnated with the intention of influencing the ethnic composition of a population."
"Article 312 quater. (SEXUAL HARASSMENT).
I.       The person who, taking advantage of a hierarchical position or power of any kind, harasses, pursues, demands, pressures, threatens to cause harm or prejudice of any kind, conditions the obtaining of a benefit or compels by any means another person to maintain a relationship or perform acts or have behaviors of sexual content that would otherwise not be consented to, for their benefit or that of a third party, shall be sanctioned with deprivation of liberty from four (4) to eight (8) years.
II.      If the demand, request or imposition is exercised by a public servant within the scope of the hierarchical relationship they hold, they shall be removed from their position and the penalty shall be aggravated by one third."

#### ARTICLE 85. (CRIMES AGAINST THE FAMILY)

Title VII of the Criminal Code "Crimes against the family" is modified, incorporating Chapter III called "Crimes of economic and patrimonial violence".
"Article 250 bis. (ECONOMIC VIOLENCE). The person who engages in any of the following conducts shall be sanctioned with a custodial sentence of two (2) to four (4) years:
a)      Diminishes, limits or restricts the free disposal of the woman's economic income.
b)      Destroys or conceals documents justifying ownership, personal identification, professional titles or goods, personal objects, work instruments of the woman that are indispensable for her to carry out her habitual activities.
c)      Restricts or suppresses the fulfillment of their family economic obligations that put at risk the well-being of their spouse, daughters and sons, as a means to subjugate the woman's will.
d)      Controls income or flow of monetary resources entering the home through psychological, sexual or physical violence.
e)      Prevents the woman from carrying out a work or productive activity that generates income for her.

#### Article 250

ter. (PATRIMONIAL VIOLENCE). Whoever by any means prevents, limits or prohibits the use, enjoyment, administration, transformation, or disposition of one or more assets belonging to the woman with whom they maintain a relationship of marriage or free union, shall be sanctioned with a fine of one hundred (100) up to three hundred sixty-five (365) days.

#### Article 250

quater. (MISAPPROPRIATION OF PROFITS FROM FAMILY ECONOMIC ACTIVITIES). The person who unilaterally disposes of profits derived from a family economic activity or disposes of them for their exclusive personal benefit, to the detriment of the rights of their spouse or cohabitant, shall be sanctioned with a penalty of deprivation of liberty from six (6) months to one (1) year plus a fine of up to fifty percent (50%) of the sanctioned person's salary up to three hundred sixty-five (365) days."

### CHAPTER III

SIMPLIFICATION OF CRIMINAL PROCEDURE
FOR CRIMES OF VIOLENCE AGAINST WOMEN

#### ARTICLE 86. (PROCEDURAL PRINCIPLES)

In cases involving acts of violence against women, female and male judges in all matters, prosecutors, police and other justice operators, in addition to the principles established in the Criminal Code, must be governed by the following procedural principles and guarantees:
1.      Free of charge. Women in situations of violence shall be exempt from payment of fees, legalizations, notifications, forms, testimonies, certifications, writs, remittance costs, letters rogatory, instructed orders, expert reports and others, in all public offices.
2.      Promptness. All female and male operators of the administration of justice, under liability, must strictly comply with the procedural time limits provided, without any delay under warning.
3.      Orality. All proceedings concerning acts of violence against women must be oral.
4.      Legitimacy of evidence. All means of evidence and elements of conviction legally obtained that may lead to knowledge of the truth shall be legitimate.
5.      Publicity. All proceedings relating to violence against women shall be of public knowledge, safeguarding the identity, address and other data of the victim.
6.      Immediacy and continuity. Once the hearing has begun, it must conclude on the same day. If not possible, it shall continue for the fewest number of consecutive days.
7.      Protection. Female and male judges, immediately upon learning of the case, shall issue protection measures to safeguard the life, physical, psychological, sexual integrity, and the patrimonial, economic and labor rights of women in situations of violence.
8.      Procedural economy. The female or male judge may carry out one or more proceedings in a single judicial action and shall not request evidence, statements or expert reports that could constitute revictimization.
9.      Accessibility. The lack of formal or material requirements in the procedure must not delay, hinder or prevent the restitution of violated rights and the sanction of those responsible.
10.    Recusal. The recusal of a judge, panel member or magistrate who has a history of violence may be requested, and the case must be immediately referred to the competent court or tribunal.
11.    Material truth. Administrative or judicial decisions adopted regarding cases of violence against women must consider the truth of the proven facts, above pure and simple formality.
12.    Burden of proof. In all criminal proceedings for acts that threaten the life, security or physical, psychological and/or sexual integrity of women, the burden of proof shall correspond to the Public Prosecutor's Office.
13.    Imposition of precautionary measures. Once the complaint has been filed, the judicial authority shall immediately issue the precautionary measures provided for in the Code of Criminal Procedure, prioritizing the protection and safety of the woman during the investigation, until the formal accusation is made. At this stage, it shall ratify or expand the measures adopted.
14.    Confidentiality. The bodies receiving the complaint, officials of the care and treatment units, competent courts and others must maintain the confidentiality of matters submitted for their consideration, unless the woman herself requests total or partial publicity. The woman must be informed in advance and in a timely manner about the possibility of exercising this right.
15.    Reparation. It is compensation for material and immaterial damage caused, to which every woman who has suffered violence is entitled.

#### ARTICLE 87 (PROCEDURAL GUIDELINES)

In all administrative, judicial and indigenous native peasant proceedings, the following guidelines shall apply:
1.      Conciliation procedures, which shall be subject to the provisions of Article 46 of this Law.
2.      Application of a single protocol for receiving, registering and processing complaints.
3.      Provision of protection measures to safeguard women in situations of violence.
4.      Obligation to investigate, pursue and prosecute until achieving the sanction of all acts that constitute violence against women.
5.      Provision of rehabilitation therapies for the aggressor, which shall in no case substitute the sanction.
6.      Provision of strengthening therapies for women who are leaving the cycle of violence.
7.      Monitoring and verification of compliance with sanctions and therapies ordered by the competent authority.

#### ARTICLE 88. (PERMANENT ATTENTION)

Female and male Investigating Judges in matters of violence against women, on duty, must be available twenty-four (24) hours a day to adopt the necessary protection and restriction measures.

#### ARTICLE 89. (CONFIDENTIALITY)

Proceedings for acts of violence are confidential, unless the woman herself, after prior, free and timely information, requests total or partial publicity. In all cases, the name of the victim shall be kept in strict confidence.

#### ARTICLE 90. (CRIMES OF PUBLIC ORDER)

All crimes contemplated in this Law are crimes of public action.

#### ARTICLE 91. (DECLARATION OF DEFAULT)

In cases of crimes provided for in this Law, the accused shall be declared in default when he does not appear at the first hearing scheduled by the jurisdictional authority, after having been legally notified.

#### ARTICLE 92. (EVIDENCE)

All elements of conviction obtained that may lead to knowledge of the reported facts shall be admitted as means of evidence. The evidence shall be assessed by the female or male judge, setting forth the reasoning on which their legal evaluation is based.

#### ARTICLE 93. (ALTERNATIVE MEANS)

For the presentation of evidence, the woman in a situation of violence may decide whether to avail herself of the regular procedure or the following optional means:
1.      Give testimony or present evidence by alternative means, without appearing before the court.
2.      Provide evidence in judicial proceedings without being obliged to encounter the aggressor.

#### ARTICLE 94. (RESPONSIBILITY OF THE PUBLIC PROSECUTOR'S OFFICE)

No woman should have the responsibility of judicially proving those actions, acts, situations or facts related to her situation of violence; it shall be the Public Prosecutor's Office that, as responsible for the investigation of crimes, gathers the necessary evidence, within a maximum period of eight (8) days under liability, seeking not to subject the assaulted woman to medical tests, interrogations, reconstructions or expert reports, confrontations that constitute revictimization.
In case technical expert reports are required, they must not be demanded of the woman. If it were a flagrant crime, the accused shall be responsible for paying for these; if proven to be insolvent, recourse shall be had to the free services of the Comprehensive Care Services.
The female or male Prosecutor must shorten all time limits of the preparatory stage up to the accusation in cases of violence against women due to their situation of risk.

#### ARTICLE 95. (DOCUMENTARY EVIDENCE)

In addition to others established by Law, any of the following shall be admitted as documentary evidence:
1.      Medical certificate issued by any public or private health institution, certified by a forensic physician.
2.      Psychological and/or social work report, issued by professionals working in public or private institutions specialized in the subject matter and legally recognized.
3.      Property documents for movable or immovable property in originals or photocopies.
4.      Minutes or private documents.
5.      Letters, text messages, emails or others lawfully obtained.
6.      Any other document that leads to knowledge of the truth.

#### ARTICLE 96. (ASSESSMENT OF EVIDENCE FROM DECEASED PERSONS)

The records of victims or witnesses who cannot appear at the oral trial hearing due to their death, provided they are recorded in written documents and received according to procedure, shall be assessed as evidence by the judicial authority.

#### ARTICLE 97. (ASSESSMENT OF EVIDENCE)

Evidence may be presented together with the complaint, answer, hearing or before the resolution is issued; the lack of evidence at the time of filing the complaint or claim shall not prevent its admission.

#### ARTICLE 98. (CIVIL LIABILITY)

Once the judgment is final, the judicial authority shall proceed to the assessment and reparation of civil damages.

#### ARTICLE 99. (THIRD PARTY COADJUVANT)

I.                 In any case of violence against women, a natural or legal person, not party to the case, who has recognized experience or competence on the matter under debate, may intervene in order to offer specialized arguments of significance for the decision of the matter. Their participation may be proposed ex officio, by the prosecutor, by the woman in a situation of violence or requested by the male or female expert.
II.                Expert opinions are limited to an opinion that will guide the understanding of the fact, may be presented at any time during the process, before the judgment is issued and shall not have party status nor the right to collect fees.

#### ARTICLE 100. (FALSE COMPLAINT OR ACCUSATION)

Whoever has been falsely reported or accused as author and/or participant in the commission of a crime contemplated in this Law, may initiate the corresponding action, with the prosecutorial resolution rejecting the complaint or dismissing the case, or after the process has concluded with a final acquittal judgment.
TRANSITORY PROVISIONS
FIRST. For the implementation of this Law, all public institutions involved must:
I.                 Make the necessary adjustments in their institutional budgets for the 2013 fiscal year; additionally, the General Treasury of the Nation shall allocate resources to central level entities, according to financial availability and according to established deadlines.
II.                For subsequent fiscal years, the General Treasury of the Nation shall allocate necessary and sufficient resources to central level entities, according to financial availability for the comprehensive application of this Law.
III.               Autonomous Territorial Entities must allocate the necessary and sufficient resources in their respective institutional budgets.
SECOND. Public and private institutions, when applicable; responsible for care, protection and punishment in cases of violence against women, must integrate their respective specific protocols into a single form for detection and health care, receipt of complaints, registration, processing and follow-up, of which each shall use the part that corresponds to it and shall constitute a valid legal document during the judicial process. This instrument must be implemented within a period not exceeding three (3) months.
THIRD. The Council of the Magistracy shall create and implement public courts of jurisdiction against violence against women, according to an order of priorities and defined needs according to the procedural caseload.
FOURTH.
I.                 For the gradual and progressive implementation of courts of jurisdiction against violence against women, the Council of the Magistracy must, within a period not exceeding ninety (90) days:
1.      Design, organize and initiate, in the State School of Judges, the implementation of specialization courses in matters of gender, Human Rights and violence, in order to have trained judicial personnel, for the implementation of courts against violence against women, for which purpose it shall immediately allocate sufficient economic resources. Additionally, it may enter into agreements with public or private universities, national or foreign, to implement a master's degree for judges, in this specialty.
2.      Prepare the necessary instruments for the qualification, appointment and evaluation of female judges, male judges and female and male judicial officials, in order to ensure the appointment of those who meet the necessary requirements for compliance with this Law.
II.                While the Third Transitory Provision of the Law of the Judicial Branch becomes effective, through the gradual and progressive implementation of courts against violence against women, from the promulgation of this Law, criminal jurisdiction judges and rural area judges and mixed courts, shall hear and process with priority the proceedings for crimes of violence, applying the provisions of this Law.
FIFTH. The General Prosecutor's Office shall adopt the necessary measures for the creation of specialized Prosecutor's Offices of Jurisdiction against violence against women and the Specialized Forensic Directorate, to operate within three (3) months from the promulgation of this Law, within which they must approve all instruments and protocols for the fulfillment of their functions.
SIXTH. The Bolivian Police shall modify its function manuals and implement the Special Force to Combat Violence, within a period of three (3) months, designating the necessary personnel for its operation throughout the country.
All human, economic and infrastructure resources of the Family Protection Brigades shall integrate the Special Force to Combat Violence, in accordance with the provisions of Article 53 of this Law.
SEVENTH. The central level of the State shall adopt the necessary measures for the creation, adaptation and operation of the institutionality provided for by this Law, within three (3) months from its promulgation. Autonomous Territorial Entities may adopt intergovernmental agreements for compliance with the provisions of this Law. Type A Municipalities may apply gradually the obligations assigned to them by the Law.

ABROGATING AND DEROGATING PROVISIONS
FIRST. Articles 308 Ter (Rape in a State of Unconsciousness), 314 (Improper Abduction), 315 (With Matrimonial Intent), 316 (Mitigation), and 317 (Common Provision), of the Penal Code are repealed.
SECOND. All provisions contrary to this Law are Abrogated and Repealed.
