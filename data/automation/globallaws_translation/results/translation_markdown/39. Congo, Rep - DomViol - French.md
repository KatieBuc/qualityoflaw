OFFICIAL PART
- LAW -

Law MOUEBARA No. 19-2022 of 4 May 2022
on combating violence against women in the Republic of Congo

The National Assembly and the Senate have deliberated and adopted;
The President of the Republic promulgates the law the content of which follows:

### TITLE I: GENERAL PROVISIONS

###### Article 1

Without prejudice to the provisions of the penal code, the code of criminal procedure and law No. 4-2010 of 14 June 2010 on the protection of children in the Republic of Congo, the purpose of this law is to combat all forms of violence against women and girls.

It aims to provide a multidisciplinary response to violence against women and girls.

###### Article 2: For the purposes of this law, the following are understood as

-	woman: any person of the female sex of any age;
-	girl: any person of the female sex under 18 years of age.

###### Article 3

Violence against women constitutes any physical, moral, sexual or economic harm against her which results in prejudice, suffering or bodily, psychological, sexual or economic damage to her, both in public and private life.

###### Article 4

Physical violence constitutes any harmful act or abuse affecting the integrity or physical security of the woman or her life, such as assault and battery, pushing, disfigurement, burns, mutilations of certain parts of the body, sequestration, torture and homicide.

###### Article 5

Moral or psychological violence constitutes any verbal aggression, such as coercion, threat, abandonment, deprivation of rights and freedoms, humiliation, neglect, mockery, belittlement and other acts or words affecting the human dignity of the woman or aimed at intimidating or dominating her.

###### Article 6

Sexual violence constitutes any act or word by which the perpetrator aims to subject the woman to his sexual desires or to the sexual desires of another, by means of coercion, fraud, pressure or other means likely to weaken or harm her will, regardless of the perpetrator's relationship with the victim.

###### Article 7

Domestic violence constitutes any act of physical, sexual, psychological or economic violence perpetrated against a woman by a member of her family, a former or current intimate partner, whether this violence is perpetrated inside or beyond the limits of the home, even though the perpetrator of the offence shares or has shared the same domicile as the victim.

###### Article 8

Conjugal violence constitutes any behaviour within an intimate relationship that causes physical, psychological or sexual harm or suffering to the persons who are parties to that relationship.

The intimate partner may be a current or former cohabitant or the victim's husband.

###### Article 9

Political violence constitutes any act or practice, based on discrimination between the sexes, aimed at depriving women of the exercise of any political, partisan, associative activity or any fundamental right or freedom, or preventing them from doing so.

###### Article 10

Economic violence constitutes any act or omission likely to exploit women or deprive them of economic resources, whatever their origin, such as deprivation of funds, wages or income, control of wages or income and prohibition from working or coercion to work.

###### Article 11

Discrimination against women constitutes any distinction, exclusion or restriction which has the effect or purpose of impairing the recognition of her fundamental human rights and freedoms, on the basis of complete and effective equality, in the civil, political, economic, social and cultural fields, or of compromising this recognition or the enjoyment or exercise of these rights by women, regardless of colour, race, religion, thought, age, nationality, economic and social conditions, marital status, state of health, language or disability.

Positive procedures and measures aimed at accelerating the establishment of equality between the two sexes are not considered discriminatory.

###### Article 12

A situation of vulnerability constitutes a situation of fragility linked to young or advanced age, serious illness, pregnancy, or mental or physical deficiency affecting the victim's capacity to resist the perpetrator of the acts.

###### Article 13

A victim is a woman who has suffered sexual, physical, moral, psychological, economic harm or has been deprived of the enjoyment of her rights and freedoms by acts, words or cases of abandonment.

###### Article 14

Sexual harassment constitutes any repeated and unreciprocated sexual advance, unsolicited sexual attention, a request for access or sexual favours, any sexual allusion or

other verbal or physical attitude of a sexual nature, any exhibition of pornographic materials when this interferes with work or is presented as a condition of employment or creates an intimidating, hostile or shocking work environment.

###### Article 15

Violence in the professional environment constitutes any action, incident and behaviour that deviates from a reasonable attitude by which a woman is attacked, threatened, violated, injured or hurt in the context of or as a direct result of her work.

###### Article 16

Violence in a religious environment constitutes any act that results in individual or collective deadly behaviour justified by a religion.

###### Article 17

Violence in the school and academic environment constitutes any act or threat of sexual, physical, psychological and economic violence that occurs in and around schools, universities or training institutes, perpetrated by reason of gender-related norms and stereotypes and enforced by unequal power dynamics.

###### Article 18

Violence related to widowhood and inheritance constitutes any illegal eviction of the widow from the marital home, or any act aimed at excluding her from succession.

###### Article 19

Cultural violence against women constitutes harmful and degrading traditional practices in the context of widowhood:

-	forced levirate: a custom that consists of imposing on a widow to marry a member of her deceased husband's family;
-	forced sororate: a custom that consists of imposing on the sister of a deceased wife to marry the widower of her deceased sister;
-	the practice that consists of forcing the widow to have unprotected sexual relations with a stranger or a member of the deceased's family in order to chase away the ghost of the deceased husband;
-	any other form of degrading practices aimed at humiliating the widow such as forcing her not to wash until the funeral, to wear underwear soaked in spicy plants, to marry the remains of the deceased husband, to submit to complete hair removal.

###### Article 20

Social violence constitutes the expression of codified and institutional relations within the social space that exerts social pressure or constraint on women.

###### Article 21

Sexual assault constitutes any act with sexual connotation committed with violence, coercion, threat or surprise against women.

### TITRE II : DE LA PREVENTION DES VIOLENCES FAITES AUX FEMMES

###### Article 22

The economic exploitation of women, their employment in degrading working conditions or conditions detrimental to their health, safety and dignity are prohibited.

In case of contravention of these provisions, the competent judge in matters of employment contracts, seized for these purposes, may enjoin the employer to cease the discrimination by any appropriate measure.

In case of refusal, the employer may be ordered to repair the harm suffered as a result of the discrimination or employment in degrading conditions or conditions detrimental to the health, safety or dignity of the woman.

###### Article 23

The advertising and dissemination, by all media means and supports, of materials containing stereotyped images, scenes, words, or acts detrimental to the image of women, or concretizing violence exercised against them or attenuating the seriousness thereof, are prohibited.

Any person contravening these provisions is liable to the penalties provided for in Article 70 of this law.

### TITRE III

DE LA PROTECTION, DE L'ASSISTANCE AUX VICTIMES ET DE LA REINSERTION DES AUTEURS DE VIOLENCES FAITES AUX FEMMES

#### Chapitre 1 : De la protection des victimes

###### Article 24

Any woman victim of acts of violence in the terms defined in this law benefits from:

-	access to counselling spaces, temporary shelters and clinical, psychological, social and legal assistance centres;
-	priority care for obtaining evidence by the competent authorities;
-	free institutional, public or private care;
-	the issuance of a declaration of victim of violence status.

###### Article 25

The State ensures adequate protection for the victim, her family, as well as persons in a similar situation, when there is a real and serious threat of acts of revenge or strong indications that her private life may be seriously disrupted.

###### Article 26

The Public Prosecutor or the competent judge may, without prejudice to the measures and rules of criminal procedure and any other complementary legislation, apply within a maximum period of 72 hours protection measures for the victim, including:

-	temporarily accommodating the victim in a temporary reception space;
-	prohibiting any contact between the victim and the alleged perpetrator of the acts.

###### Article 27

The State must create all conditions to prevent secondary victimization and to prevent the victim from being subjected to any pressure, of any nature whatsoever.

To this end, the victim has the right to be heard in a protected environment.

###### Article 28

Apart from the lawyer to whom the case has been entrusted, the woman victim of violence who cannot appear personally due to her condition or her residence abroad, may give mandate to any legal representative, by all legal means, to represent her.

###### Article 29

The status of victim of violence ceases by judicial decision not subject to appeal. The cessation of victim of violence status does not preclude the obligations that the competent authorities have to continue offering assistance to the woman who claims to be a victim and to her family, notwithstanding the assessment of the beneficiary's situation.

#### Chapitre 2 : De l'assistance aux victimes

###### Article 30

The State must promote the creation of counselling spaces as well as clinical, psychological, social and legal assistance centres responsible for preventing violence and providing assistance to victims.

###### Article. 31

The centres referred to in Article 30 above must provide free services to victims of violence until the victim status ends.

#### Chapitre 3 : De la réinsertion des auteurs de violences faites aux femmes

###### Article 32

The State encourages the establishment of adequate and necessary mechanisms for psychological and psychiatric support for perpetrators of acts of violence.

###### Article 33

Reintegration programs for perpetrators of offences and crimes of violence must be developed and implemented by the competent authorities.

###### Article 34

Competent public or private services must refer to mediation techniques favouring reconciliation, in order to find solutions to conflicts of conjugal and domestic violence.

###### Article 35

In case of persistence of conflicts and conjugal and domestic violence, mediation meetings may be organized, without prejudice to procedures and measures adopted between the perpetrator of violence and the victim, if necessary under the auspices of an approved mediator.

###### Article 36

The mediation meetings mentioned in Article 35 above aim to restore family and social harmony, the protection of the legitimate interests of conjugal life.

###### Article 37

In case of illness, the treating physician establishes, at least every three months, a report on the evolution of the condition of the convicted person which he sends to the sentence enforcement judge.

When the treating physician is of the opinion to terminate this measure before the fixed date, he must inform the sentence enforcement judge by means of a separate report justifying this opinion.

The victim must be notified of the result of the treating physician's report pursuant to a decision of the sentence enforcement judge.

### TITRE IV : DES DISPOSITIONS RELATIVES AUX PROCEDURES JUDICIAIRES ET

AUX SERVICES ADMINISTRATIFS

#### Chapitre 1 : Des procédures judiciaires

###### Article 38

Complaints, denunciations and direct summons relating to violence against women are addressed to the Public Prosecutor or to any competent authority as provided for by the legislation in force.

###### Article 39

As soon as they are notified or referred by the public prosecutor of a flagrant offence of violence against women, police or gendarmerie officers shall proceed without delay to the scene of the offence to carry out the usual findings and investigations.

In all cases, they shall inform the public prosecutor.

###### Article 40: Any police or gendarmerie officer responsible for an investigation into violence against women who voluntarily or on instruction exerts pressure or any type of coercion on the victim with a view to causing her to renounce her rights, to modify her statement or to retract, shall be punished by a sentence of one (1) month to six

(6) months' imprisonment.

The sentence shall be double for the hierarchical superior who instructed the offending officer.

###### Article 41

Police or gendarmerie services are required to inform the victim of all her rights provided for by this law, including the claim of her right to protection before a judge.

Police or gendarmerie services may, with the authorization of the public prosecutor, and before the protection order is issued by the competent judge, take one of the following protective measures:

-	the transfer of the victim, where necessary, to secure locations or child protection services;
-	the transfer of the victim to receive first

aid when she has suffered bodily harm;
-	the removal of the alleged perpetrator of violence from the victim's home or the prohibition from approaching the victim or being near her home or place of work, in case of danger threatening the victim.

The above protective measures remain in force until the protection order is issued by the judge.

###### Article 42

Police or gendarmerie services shall prepare every six (6) months for the attention of the minister responsible for security and the minister responsible for justice a report on violence against women and their consequences.

###### Article 43

Confrontation between the alleged perpetrator and the victim of the offence of violence may only take place with the latter's consent, except where it constitutes the only means to exonerate the alleged perpetrator.

The victim of sexual violence must be heard in the presence of a psychologist or social worker when she so requests.

###### Article 44

A minor female victim of sexual violence may be heard more than once in the presence of a psychologist or social worker.

Her hearing must be received, where appropriate, in such a way as to preserve voice and image.

The psychologist or social worker who attended the hearing shall prepare a report for the attention of the competent court.

###### Article 45

The withdrawal of a complaint resulting from acts of domestic violence does not result in the extinction of the public prosecution.

###### Article 46

The public prosecution for the prosecution of crimes provided for by this law is prescribed after twenty
(20) full years from the day the offence was committed.

The public prosecution for the prosecution of offences provided for by this law is prescribed after five (5) full years from the day the offence was committed.

The public prosecution for the prosecution of contraventions provided for by this law is prescribed after two (2) full years from the day the offence was committed.

The limitation periods for public prosecution referred to in the above paragraphs, when the offences are committed against a minor female, run from the latter's majority.

By way of derogation from the preceding paragraphs, the limitation period for public prosecution for the prosecution

of a concealed or dissimulated offence runs from the day the offence was discovered and could be established under conditions allowing the initiation or exercise of public prosecution, without however the limitation period being able to exceed five (5) full years for offences and twenty full years for crimes from the day the offence was discovered.

An offence is concealed when, by reason of its constituent elements, it cannot be known either by the victim or by the judicial authority.

An offence is dissimulated when its perpetrator deliberately accomplishes any characterized manoeuvre tending to prevent its discovery.

#### Chapter 2: Application for protection of women victims of violence

###### Article 47

The judge is referred the application for protection by a written request emanating from:

-	the victim herself or her representative;
-	the public prosecutor;
-	the child protection delegate if the victim is a minor.

The judge may also refer himself.

The referral to the judge of an application for protection does not preclude the victim's right to bring proceedings before the competent civil or criminal courts.

###### Article 48

In addition to the necessary supporting documents attached to the application for protection, it shall include the statement of reasons, the measures sought, their duration and, where appropriate, the amount of maintenance and/or housing allowance sought.

###### Article 49

The judge shall rule on the application for protection according to the hour-by-hour summary procedure. He shall receive the parties' statements and hear any person whose hearing is deemed useful. He may be assisted in his work by officers of the public social services.

###### Article 50: The judge may, under the terms of the protection order

-	prohibit the defendant from contacting the victim at the family home, at the place of work or place of study, at the accommodation centre or at any place where they may be;
-	require the defendant, in case of danger threatening the victim, to leave the family home where the victim resides, while allowing him to recover his personal effects, by virtue of a report drawn up for this purpose, at his expense, by a bailiff;
-	require the defendant not to harm the private property of the victim concerned by the protection order, or the common property, and not to dispose of it;

-	designate the victim's housing and, where appropriate, require the defendant to pay the housing allowance, unless the competent court has been referred the matter or a judgment has been pronounced to that effect;
-	allow the victim in person or her representative, in case of departure from the family home, to recover her personal effects, by virtue of a report drawn up for this purpose by a bailiff, at the expense of the defendant;
-	deprive the defendant of custody, guardianship or curatorship and set the procedures for visitation rights while giving priority to the best interests of the minor female or the protected adult female;
-	determine the amount of maintenance for the wife victim of violence and, where appropriate, the contribution of each of the spouses to the maintenance, unless the competent court has been referred the examination of the maintenance or a judgment has been pronounced to that effect.

###### Article 51

The protection order must indicate the duration of the protection; this must not exceed, in all cases, six (6) months.

The judge may extend the duration of the protection only once for a period that cannot exceed six (6) months.

The decision to extend must be reasoned.

An appeal against the protection order does not have suspensive effect.

###### Article 52: The public prosecutor ensures the enforcement of protection orders

###### Article 53: Any person who resists or prevents, or attempts to resist or prevent the enforcement of orders and protective measures is punished by a maximum term of imprisonment of six (6) to twelve

(12) months or a fine of 50,000 to 500,000 CFA francs or both penalties.

###### Article 54

Any person who wilfully violates or attempts to wilfully violate the protective measures ordered by the judge is punished by imprisonment of one (1) to three (3) years and a fine of 100,000 to 1,000,000 CFA francs.

###### Article 55

The court hearing a case of violence or sexual assault against a woman may, at the request of the victim or the public prosecutor, sit in camera.

###### Article 56

When violence against a woman or a minor girl is reported by an association working in the fight against violence against women, the association may, in accordance with its statutes, constitute itself as a civil party, after written authorization from the victim.

#### Chapter 3: Administrative services

###### Article 57

Legal entities responsible for protecting women against violence, including judicial police officers, child protection services, health personnel, women's advancement services, family services, social affairs services, education services and others, must:

-	respond without delay to any request for assistance and protection presented directly by the victim;
-	respond immediately to any emergency call from the free emergency short number made available to women victims of violence;
-	give priority to alerts concerning the commission of violence threatening the physical, sexual and psychological security of the victim;
-	provide listening and examination when receiving complaints, by meeting the parties and witnesses, including children, in separate rooms while ensuring their integrity;
-	inform the victim of all her rights;
-	intervene, in case of loss of housing due to violence, to ensure accommodation in shelters for women victims of violence.

### TITLE V: OFFENCES AND PENALTIES

#### Chapter 1: Conjugal or domestic violence

###### Article 58

Any perpetrator of domestic or conjugal violence as defined in Articles 6 and 7 of this law is punished by imprisonment.

The penalty is that of hard labour for a fixed term when the offence was accompanied by any of the following circumstances:

-	the offence was committed against a particularly vulnerable victim, in particular a minor girl, a pregnant woman, an elderly woman, a woman suffering from a physical or mental deficiency, an indigenous woman, a woman with a disability, a woman living with HIV/AIDS;
-	the offence was committed against several victims;
-	the offence resulted in serious injury or permanent disability of the victim or a third party;
-	the offence was committed using weapons or under the influence of drugs, medication, alcohol or narcotics;
-	the offence was committed by a repeat offender;
-	the offence was committed on the victim by reason of her nationality, ethnicity, skin colour, religion, beliefs, or political opinions;
-	the offence was committed within the framework of

the activities of a criminal association;
-	the offence was committed by an ascendant, a collateral relative or any other person having authority of law or fact over the victim;
-	the offence was committed in flagrante delicto by a person who exercises a profession or function that confers immunity or jurisdictional privilege;
-	the offence was committed by several persons acting as perpetrators or accomplices.

The penalty is that of hard labour for life when the offence was accompanied by any of the following circumstances:

-	the offence resulted in the death of the victim or a third party, including by incitement to suicide, or the victim contracting a fatal or incurable disease including HIV/AIDS;
-	the offence was committed by resorting to acts of torture or barbarity;
-	the offence was committed within the framework of the activities of a criminal association.

###### Article 59

Any conviction for conjugal or domestic violence may be accompanied by the prohibition from exercising all or part of the following civic, civil and family rights:

-	to vote;
-	to stand for election;
-	to be called or appointed to the functions of juror, to other public functions, to positions in the administration or to exercise them;
-	to carry weapons;
-	to vote and suffrage in family deliberations;
-	to be a guardian, curator;
-	to be an expert or employed as a witness in acts;
-	to testify in court, other than to make simple declarations.

The offender, if of foreign nationality, may be sentenced to a prohibition from entering and staying in national territory for twenty (20) years from the prescription of the sentence incurred.

#### Chapter 2: Violence related to widowhood and inheritance

###### Article 60

Any perpetrator of violence related to widowhood and inheritance in any form whatsoever against the widow, orphans or any other heir is punished by imprisonment of two (2) to five (5) years and a fine of 500,000 to 5,000,000 CFA francs.

The penalty is that of hard labour for a fixed term when the offence was accompanied by one of the following circumstances:

-	the offence was committed against a particularly vulnerable victim, in particular

a pregnant widow, an elderly widow, a widow suffering from a physical or mental deficiency, an indigenous widow, a widow with a disability or living with HIV/AIDS;
-	the offence was committed against several widows;
-	the offence resulted in serious injury or permanent disability to the widow;
-	the offence was committed by several persons;
-	the offence was committed using weapons or under the influence of drugs, medication, alcohol or narcotics;
-	the offence was committed by a repeat offender;
-	the offence was committed on a person of the victim by reason of her nationality, ethnicity, skin colour, religion, beliefs, or political opinions;
-	the offence was committed by a person having authority of law or fact over the victim;
-	the offence was committed within the framework of the activities of a criminal association;
-	the offence was committed by a person who abuses the authority conferred by their functions;
-	the offence was committed by several persons acting as perpetrators or accomplices.

The penalty is that of hard labour for life when the offence was accompanied by any of the following circumstances:

-	the offence resulted in the death of the widow, including by incitement to suicide, or the victim contracting a fatal or incurable disease including HIV/AIDS;
-	the offence was committed by resorting to acts of torture or acts of barbarity;
-	the offence was committed within the framework of the activities of a criminal association having caused or occasioned the death of the widow.

#### Chapter 3: Violence in the workplace

###### Article 61

Any perpetrator of sexual assault and/or sexual harassment as defined in Article 16 of this law, in the workplace, religious, school or academic environment is punished by five (5) to ten (10) years' imprisonment and a fine of 500,000 to 5,000,000 CFA francs.

Also punished by the penalties for sexual assault and/or sexual harassment is anyone acting as perpetrator or accomplice, even in a non-repeated manner, who exercises upon a minor, a woman, a work colleague, whose particular vulnerability is apparent or known to the perpetrator, any form of serious pressure with the real or apparent aim of obtaining an act of a sexual nature, whether sought for himself or for a third party.

###### Article 62

Is guilty of sexual assault and/or sexual harassment and punished by imprisonment of two (2) to five (5) years and a fine of 500,000 to 5,000,000 CFA francs or one of these penalties, anyone who persists in assaulting or harassing a minor or a woman:

-	in public spaces, educational establishments, religious environments or others, through actions, abuse, words, gestures of a sexual nature or for sexual purposes;
-	through written, telephone or electronic messages, recordings or images of a sexual or pornographic nature or for sexual purposes.

The penalty is doubled if the perpetrator is the victim's hierarchical superior, teacher, religious leader, or a person responsible for maintaining order and security in public spaces or others.

###### Article 63: The penalty is imprisonment of five

(5) to ten (10) years and a fine of 1,000,000 to 10,000,000 CFA francs, if the sexual assault and/or sexual harassment is committed by an ascendant, a relative having with the victim an impediment to marriage, a guardian, a person having authority over the victim or having the victim in their charge or if the victim is a minor.

#### Chapter 4: Violence in the prison environment

###### Article 64

Is punished by imprisonment, anyone, prison administrator, prison officer, member of the public force or fellow inmate, who has committed within a penitentiary establishment, physical, sexual or psychological violence as defined in Articles 3, 4 and 10 of this law upon a female detainee:

The penalty is that of hard labour for a fixed term when the offence has been accompanied by any of the following circumstances:

-	the offence was committed against a particularly vulnerable victim, notably a minor, a pregnant or elderly woman, a woman suffering from a physical or mental deficiency, an indigenous woman, a woman with a disability or living with HIV/AIDS;
-	the offence was committed against several victims;
-	the offence resulted in serious injury or permanent disability of the victim or a third party;
-	the offence was committed by several persons;
-	the offence was committed using weapons or under the influence of drugs, medication, alcohol or narcotics;
-	the offence was committed by a repeat offender;
-	the offence was committed upon the person

of the victim by reason of their nationality, ethnicity, skin colour, religion, beliefs or political opinions;
-	the offence was committed by a person having authority of law or fact over the victim;
-	the offence was committed in the context of the activities of a criminal association;
-	the offence was committed by a person who abuses the authority conferred upon them by their functions;
-	the offence was committed by several persons acting as perpetrators or accomplices.

The penalty is that of hard labour for life, when the offence has been accompanied by any of the following circumstances:

-	the offence resulted in the death of the victim or a third party, including by suicide, or the contraction by the victim of a fatal or incurable disease, including HIV/AIDS;
-	the offence was committed by resorting to acts of torture or barbarity;
-	the offence was committed in the context of the activities of a criminal association having caused or occasioned the death of the victim or a third party.

#### Chapter 5: Violence in the context of armed conflicts

###### Article 65

Anyone, being an agent of the public force or of any militias or armed groups whatsoever, who has committed, during armed conflicts, physical, sexual or psychological violence as defined in Articles 3, 5 and 10 of this law shall be punished by imprisonment and a fine of 10,000,000 to 100,000,000 CFA francs.

The penalty is that of hard labour for a fixed term, if the offence has been accompanied by any of the following circumstances:

-	the offence was committed against a particularly vulnerable victim, notably a minor, a pregnant or elderly woman, a woman suffering from a physical deficiency, an indigenous woman or a woman with a disability or living with HIV/AIDS;
-	the offence was committed against several victims;
-	the offence resulted in serious injury or permanent disability of the victim or a third party;
-	the offence was committed by several persons acting as perpetrators or accomplices;
-	the offence was committed using weapons or under the influence of drugs, medication, alcohol or narcotics;
-	the offence was committed by a repeat offender;
-	the offence was committed upon the person

of the victim by reason of their nationality, ethnicity, skin colour, religion, beliefs or political opinions;
-	the offence was committed by a public official in the exercise of their functions;
-	the offence was committed by the spouse or cohabitant of the victim;
-	the offence was committed upon the victim by a person who has authority, or who has a relationship of responsibility or trust;
-	the offence was committed by a person who abuses the authority conferred upon them by their functions.

The penalty is that of hard labour for life, if the offence has been accompanied by any of the following circumstances:

-	the offence resulted in the death of the victim or a third party, including by suicide, or the contraction by the victim of a fatal or incurable disease, including HIV/AIDS;
-	the offence was committed by resorting to acts of repeated or collective rape, to acts of torture, barbarity or ill-treatment;
-	the offence was committed in the context of the activities of a criminal association;
-	the offence was committed by a perpetrator who proceeded to recruit into the fighting forces and to the forced enlistment of the victim;
-	the offence was committed by an actor who proceeded to the summary execution of the victim, or to their arbitrary detention, without charge or trial, or without a fair trial.

#### Chapter 6: Penalties for other specific violence against women

###### Article 66

Any abduction or sequestration committed by a spouse, a divorced spouse, an ex-spouse, a fiancé, an ascendant, a descendant, a collateral:, a brother, a guardian or a person having authority over the victim or having the victim in their charge or if the victim has been subjected to any other violence of any nature whatsoever, is punished by imprisonment of twenty (20) to thirty (30) years.

###### Article 67 : Is punished by imprisonment of six

(6) months to three (3) years and a fine of 200,000 to 2,000,000 CFA francs, anyone who knowingly proceeds, by any means, including computer systems, to intercept, record, disseminate or distribute words, images or information transmitted in a private or confidential setting, without the consent of their authors, including anyone found guilty of the acts referred to in Article 23 of this law.

Is liable to the same penalty, anyone who knowingly proceeds, by any means, to capture, record, disseminate or distribute the photograph of a woman located in a private place, without her consent.

###### Article 68

Is punished by imprisonment of one (1) year to three (3) years and a fine of 200,000 to 2,000,000 CFA francs, anyone who proceeds, by any means, including computer systems, to disseminate or distribute a montage composed of words or photographs of a person, without their consent, or proceeds to disseminate or distribute false allegations or mendacious facts, with a view to violating the private life of a woman or defaming her.

###### Article 69

The penalty is imprisonment of one (1) year to five (5) years and a fine of 500,000 to 5,000,000 CFA francs, if the acts provided for in Articles 67 and 68 of this law were committed in a state of recidivism or if the offense is against a minor or a woman by reason of her sex.

###### Article 70

Is punished by imprisonment of one (1) year to three (3) years and a fine of 200,000 to 2,000,000 CFA francs, anyone who, without legitimate reason, has expelled his spouse or concubine from the marital home or has refused to bring her back there.

The penalty is doubled in case of recidivism.

Aticle 71 : Without prejudice to more severe criminal provisions, is punished by imprisonment of six
(6) months to one (1) year and a fine of 3,000,000 to 30,000,000 CFA francs or only one of these two penalties, anyone who compels a minor or a woman to marriage by resorting to violence, threats, deceit or any other means.

The penalty is doubled when the victim has a disability or is known for her weak mental capacities.

###### Article 72

Is punished by imprisonment of one (1) month to six (6) months and a fine of 200,000 to 2,000,000 CFA francs or one of these two penalties, the spouse or concubine who organizes the dissipation or transfer of his assets, in bad faith, with the intention of harming the wife or concubine; or of circumventing the provisions of the family code concerning alimony, housing or rights resulting from the breakdown of the conjugal relationship or the distribution of assets.

Prosecution can only be initiated upon joint complaint or from the injured concubine.

Withdrawal of the complaint ends the prosecution.

###### Article 73

In case of conviction for harassment, assault, sexual exploitation, mistreatment, female genital mutilation or violence committed against women or minors, whatever the nature of the act or its perpetrator, the court may decide:

- to prohibit the convicted person from contacting the victim or approaching the place where she is located or communicating with her by any means,

for a period not exceeding five years from the date of expiration of the sentence to which he was sentenced or from the date of pronouncement of the judicial decision when the custodial sentence was pronounced with suspension or if he was sentenced only to a fine or an alternative sentence;
- to subject the convicted person, during the period provided for above or during the execution of the custodial sentence, to appropriate psychological treatment.

Reconciliation between spouses ends the prohibition from contacting the victim.

The above support measures, in the event they are pronounced, have no suspensive effect on the execution of the principal sentence.

###### Article 74

Anyone having knowledge of deprivations, mistreatment, assaults or sexual violations inflicted on a minor or a woman who is not able to protect herself due to her age, illness, infirmity, physical or mental deficiency or a state of pregnancy and who does not inform the judicial or administrative authorities or who continues not to inform these authorities while these offenses have not ceased, is punished by one (1) year to three (3) years of imprisonment and 200,000 to 2,000,000 CFA francs fine.

When the failure to inform concerns an offense committed against a minor under fifteen (15) years of age, the penalties are increased to five (5) years of imprisonment and 5,000,000 CFA francs fine.

Except when the law provides otherwise, persons bound to secrecy under the conditions provided for by the texts in force are excepted from the preceding provisions.

#### Chapitre 1

General provisions Section 1: Scope of application

###### Article premier

This decree regulates the technical control of the construction of building and infrastructure works, as well as equipment elements intended for their operation, undertaken or executed by public or private persons.
It determines the professional status, mission, domain, procedures for intervention and the principle of responsibility of technical controllers.

##### Section 2: Fundamental principles

###### Article 2

Technical control is an essential element of construction operations for works or building and infrastructure projects, considered in their different phases, from design to completion of works. Its purpose is to guarantee the quality of works, the viability and durability of the different types of works to be carried out and, through this, both to reassure the project owner and to limit the engagement of the liability of builders and construction insurance.

###### Article 3

Technical control is defined by its nature and its domain of intervention as specified in chapters 3 and 4 below.

The nature is characterized by the choice of technical hazards whose prevention is sought.

The domain of intervention of technical control consists of all works and equipment elements on which the mission focuses.

###### Article 4

Technical control constitutes a regulated activity, exercised by natural or legal persons called technical controllers.

The exercise of this activity is subject to prior obtaining of approval and the professional card issued by the minister responsible for construction for building works and the minister responsible for infrastructure for infrastructure.

###### Article 5

The activity of technical control is incompatible with the exercise of any activity of design, execution or real estate expertise.

The technical controller must be totally independent of the work contractors that he controls and must not be able to exercise the activities of those who are subject to his control. He must act with impartiality and must not infringe upon the independence of persons exercising the activities mentioned above.

Approved persons and holders of the professional card, administrators or managers and the management personnel of these bodies, as well as the personnel called upon for controls, must act with impartiality and have no link likely to infringe upon their independence with persons, bodies, companies or enterprises that exercise an activity of design, execution or real estate expertise in the field of construction works.

###### Article 6

Every project owner or builder of a real estate program, public or private, is obliged to secure the services of an approved private technical controller or a public body authorized for this purpose.

Any contract for studies and execution of works or a real estate program, to be valid, must bear the mention that the documents and works relating thereto are subject to examination or verification by a technical controller approved or authorized for this purpose.

##### Section 3: Definitions and qualifications

###### Article 7

For the purposes of this decree, the following terms are defined as follows:

-	infrastructure means all public and private facilities, installations and equipment, terrestrial, maritime or river, fixed to the ground or built underground, and necessary for the circulation of goods and persons, participating in or serving as support for the operation of services, human, economic and social activities,

which are exercised or have a vocation to be exercised across space, for territorial and local planning and development;
-	work means both the construction work, whether it be building work or civil engineering, and the result of construction work, such as real estate work adhering to the ground;
-	real estate or real estate construction work that which has as its object the realization of a building, that is to say the transformation of the ground, such as the construction of a building or a civil engineering work, the creation of roads and various networks;
-	real estate construction work may be immovable property by nature, according to the physical criterion of attachment to the ground or subsoil, of material connection with the ground, or immovable by destination, or by the object to which they apply.

###### Article 8: All property is movable or immovable

Property is immovable, either by its nature, or by its destination, or by the object to which it applies.

###### Article 9

Immovable property by nature is not capable of moving or being moved.

Land, buildings or constructions adhering to the ground, and accessories incorporated into the construction are immovable by their nature.

A property, structure or construction is immovable by nature when the connection, anchoring or foundation system reveals that it does not simply rest on the ground and is not maintained there by its weight alone, even if it consists of light and temporary constructions.
The following are also regarded as immovable by nature:
-	poles and pylons intended to support overhead electricity transmission lines, electric power transmission cables;
-	poles and pylons supporting telephone and telegraph lines and installations, relay antennas or macrocellular base stations for mobile telephony or electronic communications, radio and television transmitting or receiving antennas;
-	accessories intimately and specially incorporated into a structure, a building or forming an indivisible whole with a building, from which they cannot be separated or detached without affecting its integrity, without any damage to them or to the building.

Therefore, the ground and the property incorporated into it are considered immovable by nature.

###### Article10

Pipes used for conducting water in a house or other property are immovable by nature and form part of the land to which they are attached.

###### Article 11: Are immovable by destination

-	movable objects that the owner of a property has placed there for the service and operation of that property or as being absolutely indispensable and directly assigned to the operation of the property.

Thus a set of property movable by nature is considered immovable by destination. These are equipment and production assets assigned to the industrial, commercial and civil operation of an immovable property.

-	all movable effects that the owner has attached to the property in perpetuity, that is to say by a material connection between the effects and the immovable property by nature.

The owner is deemed to have attached movable effects to his property in perpetuity when they are sealed with plaster, lime or cement, or when they cannot be detached without being broken and damaged, or without breaking or damaging the part of the property to which they are attached.

A central heating installation and sanitary appliances, an installation of a central air conditioning system, must be declared immovable by destination, provided that the various elements that compose such installations, by their arrangement with the different parts of the building, the manner in which they are fixed, manifest the owner's intention to make them accessories of the immovable property and to fix them there in perpetuity.

###### Article 12

The equipment elements of a structure mentioned in Article 25 of Law No. 13-2004 of 31 March 2004 referred to above are immovable by destination, movable property, effects or equipment, and may be considered as accessories to construction operations or constitute the necessary complement to these operations.

They include both inseparable equipment elements incorporated into the construction and which cannot be detached from it without serious damage, and separable equipment elements, whether functioning or inert, of structural works within the meaning of Article 27 of Law No. 13-2004 of 31 March 2004 referred to above.

A separable equipment element of a structure is subject to a proper functioning guarantee and is thus understood at the same time, by associated criteria, as:
-	a separable equipment element on a new structure and not on an existing one;
-	an equipment element that does not cause unsuitability for purpose of the entire structure;
-	an equipment element intended to function.

Other inert equipment elements fall under ordinary contractual liability.

###### Article 13

The following are considered as movable equipment constituting the necessary complement of premises for office or residential use and residences for elderly persons:

a)	for premises for office use, these are partitions by removable elements, the distribution of computer networks and special arrangements for the use of computer equipment, alarm equipment against theft and fire, the power supply and cabling of telex and fax networks as well as the installations and equipment necessary for restaurants, canteens and laundries of educational, health or social establishments;

b)	for residential premises, these are central air conditioning equipment, thermal and sound insulation equipment, lighting installations for common areas, courtyards, gardens and private roads of buildings, installations of lifting devices, the complete installation of sanitary appliances, including taps and installation accessories, as well as collective or individual hot water production appliances, sinks, sink units, including taps and installation accessories, cupboards permanently incorporated in spaces specially provided for this purpose, collective antennas and radio-television cabling, complete garbage chute installations, letterboxes sealed in the masonry, direct coverings of bare floors, excluding carpets, and wall coverings;

c)	for residences for the elderly, these are, in particular, movable equipment necessary for the use of common areas, such as restaurants, kitchens, infirmaries, administrative offices and reception rooms.

###### Article 14

The following are considered as movable equipment accessories to construction operations: functional equipment which, without constituting the obligatory accessory of construction operations or the necessary complement of residential premises, has a connection with the construction, even if this is materialized by the simple connection of an appliance to existing pipes or by sealing reduced to a few fixed points.

This is the case, in particular, for household appliances, cookers, stoves, heating plates, electric hoods, clothes dryers, refrigerators, washing machines for laundry or dishes, as well as storage elements not incorporated in spaces that would have been specially reserved for them.

###### Article 15

The following are considered in particular as movable equipment having no physical connection with the construction, which are neither fixed nor even connected: carpets on parquet flooring, paintings, mirrors other than those installed in bathrooms, tapestries, statues, curtains, net curtains and other ornaments, audiovisual equipment.

###### Article 16

Pursuant to Articles 25 to 27 of Law No. 13-2004 of 31 March 2004 referred to above, the equipment elements of structures are of two categories: separable equipment elements and inseparable equipment elements.

###### Article 17

Inseparable equipment elements, within the meaning of Article 26, paragraph 2 of Law No. 13-2004 of 31 March 2004 referred to above, follow the regime of the works in which they are installed and their defects may give rise, depending on their nature and severity, to ten-year or two-year liability. This qualification of equipment element extends, in particular, to works or elements external to the main work, such as parking areas, provided that they were carried out as part of an overall construction operation.

Inseparable equipment elements may, however, also be considered as works in themselves, whether they are installed in a work under construction or in an existing work. Thus, they are subject to ten-year liability, on the basis of Article 25 of Law No. 13-2004 of 31 March 2004 referred to above, provided that the damage observed affects their own purpose, without it being necessary for the purpose of the work in which they are inserted to be affected.

The replacement of certain major equipment elements, such as that of a central heating or central air conditioning installation, lift installations, collective antennas and radio-television cabling, may be regarded as the construction of a work.

###### Article 18

Separable equipment elements may be subject to Articles 25 and 26 of Law No. 13-2004 of 31 March 2004 referred to above provided that they are installed during the construction of the work and not when they are installed on existing structures. They only give rise to the ordinary law liability of constructors.

###### Article 19

Public works constitute immovable works or works accessory to immovable works and executed either on behalf of a public entity for a purpose of general utility, or on behalf of a private person and by means of private funds, by a public entity, within the framework of a public service mission.

Public works relate to immovables by nature, such as immovable structures, building works, civil engineering or infrastructure works. They also concern immovables by destination, in particular, animals and objects that an owner of a piece of land has placed there for the service and operation of that land.

#### Chapitre 2

Des conditions d'accès et d'exercice de la profession de contrôleur technique

###### Article 20

Technical control of the construction of building or infrastructure works may only be

exercised by natural or legal persons holding a professional card and an approval issued, for building works by the minister responsible for construction and, for infrastructure by the minister responsible for infrastructure.

###### Article 21

The natural or legal persons referred to in the preceding article must demonstrate that they have personnel with the following professional qualifications:

a)	for operational management personnel and engineers:

-	either hold a post-secondary level diploma in building or civil engineering representing at least four years of higher education, and practical experience of at least three years in the design, construction, technical control or expert assessment of construction;
-	or have five years of practical experience in the field.

b)	for mission execution personnel:

-	either hold a secondary education certificate, and a practice of at least five years in the design, construction, technical control or expert assessment of construction;
-	or have six years of practical experience in the field.

###### Article 22

Any natural or legal person who applies for the professional card and technical controller approval from the ministers responsible for construction or infrastructure must also satisfy the following conditions:

a)	for the natural person:

1.	be of Congolese nationality or a national of ECCAS, CEMAC or a foreign State having concluded reciprocity agreements with Congo or being associated with a person of Congolese nationality or with a national technical control office;

2.	be at least 25 years of age;

3.	have a domicile in Congo;

4.	not be subject to any of the incompatibilities, incapacities or prohibitions provided for by the texts in force;

5.	enjoy civil rights and not have been convicted of acts contrary to professional integrity;

6.	have the status of trader.

b)	For the legal person under Congolese law: its legal or statutory representatives and senior management personnel must satisfy conditions 1, 2, 3, 4 and 5 of the preceding paragraph.

c)	For the legal person under foreign law: its legal or statutory representatives and senior management personnel must satisfy conditions 2, 3, 4 and 5 of the preceding paragraph of point a).

A list of branches, agencies or offices, which depend on the same declarant is, if applicable, attached to the application.

For legal entities, the following must be declared: the corporate name, the legal form, the share capital, the corporate purpose, the address of the registered office, the duration of the company, the civil status of the directors.

Inaccuracies or omissions in the information provided, of a nature to distort the assessment made by the administration on the applicant, may result in the rejection of the file or the cancellation of the authorization and the withdrawal of the professional card.

##### Section 2: Processing of the application

###### Article 29

From the filing of the file, the territorially competent departmental directorate proceeds with the transmission, within a period of ten working days, of a copy of the file to the directorate general of construction for technical inspectors of buildings or to the directorate general of infrastructures for technical inspectors of infrastructures.

###### Article 30

Each file received by the services indicated in the preceding article is assigned a registration number.

The petitioner receives a receipt indicating this number.

###### Article 31

From the filing of the application and the file, the administration has a period of ten working days to notify the petitioner, by registered letter or electronic mail, if he has made this choice, of the list of missing documents.

Silence at the end of this period implies that the file is deemed complete.

The applicant has a period of ten working days to transmit the missing documents of the file. Failure to comply with this period constitutes rejection of the application.

###### Article 32

The file is submitted to the approval commission which ensures the conformity of the documents provided. When it gives a favourable opinion, it informs the minister who issues the approval.

The ministry in charge of construction or the one in charge of infrastructures has a maximum period of forty-five working days, from the date of receipt of the file at the competent departmental directorate, to issue the approval and the professional card to the applicant. Any refusal to grant the approval and the professional card must be justified.

The ministerial commission provided for in the preceding paragraph may call upon any resource person.

##### Section 3: Issuance of approval and professional card

###### Article 33

The approval issued by the minister responsible for construction or the minister responsible for infrastructure, after the technical opinion of the ministerial commission established for this purpose, is valid for one calendar year.

The professional card is issued for a renewable period of five years from the date of signature, after the technical opinion of the ministerial commission established for this purpose.

###### Article 34

The renewal of the documents referred to in the preceding article takes place upon presentation of a written request in accordance with the provisions of this decree.

-	two color identity-size photographs of the manager(s) of the company;

#### Chapter 3

Nomenclature of building or infrastructure projects and works

###### Article 37

The building or land, maritime or river infrastructure projects and works referred to in article one of this decree are, in particular:

-	public buildings and edifices;
-	communication route infrastructure works;
-	port or airport infrastructure works;
-	energy, hydraulic and environmental infrastructure works;
-	postal and electronic communications infrastructure works.

Infrastructures include immovable property of the artificial public domain, circulation, defense and monuments.

###### Article 38

Public buildings and edifices include, in particular, civil, residential and institutional constructions, socio-educational, sports, cultural and religious constructions, housing complexes, commercial and industrial constructions, hotel constructions as well as buildings receiving the public built by private parties and warehouses.

###### Article 39

Road or pedestrian, railway, river, public or private communication route infrastructure works consist, in particular, of highways, national and departmental roads, local interest roads, municipal roads with a roadway width of at least 6 m, traffic routes established on the dependencies of a river or maritime public domain, railway lines, stations, platforms, roads and various networks, engineering structures and strategic or hydraulic works carried out therein.

###### Article 40

Port or airport infrastructure works include, in particular, maritime and river ports with necessary annexes, dikes, jetties, basins, moles, lighthouses, lighting and marking works, landing stages, quays, pontoons, buoys, navigation channels and their towpaths, quay railway tracks, aqueducts, bank and watercourse protection works.

Under the terms of the civil aviation code of the Economic and Monetary Community of Central Africa of July 21, 2000 referred to above, airport infrastructure works include

aerodromes, airports, air terminals and their annexes, civil engineering infrastructure, technical and commercial installations, buildings and various works, automobile parking lots.

###### Article 41

Energy, hydraulic and environmental infrastructure works concern, in particular: production, transmission, distribution, import and export installations for electricity, such as dams, hydroelectric developments, dikes and retention lakes, electrical lines and pipes, transformation stations or busbars.

Also concerned are gas pipelines, oil pipelines, storage depots and tanks for liquid or gaseous flammable products and special or strategic infrastructure, water production, treatment, storage and distribution works, sanitation and treatment works for rainwater and wastewater and drainage, class 1 installations referred to in article 41 of law no. 003-91 of April 23, 1991 referred to above.

###### Article 42

Postal and electronic communications infrastructure works concern in particular: technical works, poles and pylons serving as support for mobile radiotelephone relay antennas or base stations and accessory buildings, electronic communications networks, as defined in article 5 of law no. 9-2009 of November 25, 2009 referred to above.

#### Chapter 4: Mission and methods of intervention of the technical controller

##### Section 1: Mission of the technical controller

###### Article 43

The technical controller's mission is to contribute to the standardization of risks, in particular, to the prevention of different technical hazards relating to the solidity of constituent elements and inseparable equipment elements likely to be encountered in the construction of structures. This mission aims to verify whether the structure is built in accordance with the rules of the art, whether the technical provisions adopted for the structure, as well as the conditions of their execution, comply with these rules, and whether or not one is in the presence of a normal and insurable risk.

The technical controller intervenes at the request of the project owner and gives written opinions to the latter on technical matters, within the framework of the contract binding him to the project owner.

This opinion relates, in particular, to problems or risks related to construction, in particular those concerning the solidity of structures and the safety of persons in buildings, as well as compliance with construction rules in force.

The project owner must inform the technical controller of the action taken on the opinions he has sent him.

###### Article 44

The solidity of structures relates to major works and to equipment elements inseparably linked to the works of viability, foundation, framework, enclosure or roofing, within the meaning of articles 25 and 26 of law no. 13-2004 of 31 March
2004 referred to above.

The safety of persons relates to the devices of buildings, structures and equipment referred to in the preceding paragraph from the point of view of the safety of persons by the legislation or regulations applicable to the structure by reason of its intended use as it results from the building permit.

###### Article 45

For the exercise of the mission, the project owner must establish and transmit to the technical controller the information elements relating to the building or civil engineering structure, according to the framework set out in the annex to the contract.

He must also take the necessary measures so that the technical controller is informed in due time of the technical provisions envisaged as well as their possible modifications.

##### Section 2: Categories of acts

and the method of technical control

###### Article 46

The technical controller, to fulfill his mission, performs acts that fall into two categories, namely, technical acts and information acts.

Technical acts relate to design documents, graphic and written, to the arrangements made by the constructors so that the technical verifications incumbent upon them are properly conducted, and to the structures and equipment constructed. They also consist of verifying that the constructors take into account the certificates or test reports relating to materials, components and equipment of the construction.

Information acts correspond to four phases of intervention of the technical controller: the phase of examination of design documents, the phase of examination of execution documents, the phase of examination on the construction site of structures and equipment elements subject to control and the phase of examination of work carried out during the period of guarantee of perfect completion.

###### Article 47

The technical controller uses control methods that are specific to him and necessary for the accomplishment of technical acts and information of the project owner. He exercises his control, provides his opinion and carries out verifications based on the state of scientific and technical knowledge in the discipline concerned and on the comparison of the controlled object to recognized reference standards.

The reference standards consist, in particular, of:

-	legislative and regulatory texts on construction;

-	general technical clauses specifications applicable to technical control contracts;
-	texts and technical documents of a normative nature relating to rules, characteristics, recommendations or examples of good practices relating to products, services, methods, processes or organizations.

##### Section 3

Technical acts corresponding to the phases of intervention of the technical controller

###### Article 48

The technical control mission begins from the design of structures, namely, at the time of the establishment of preliminary project studies where the general technical provisions that may be envisaged are proposed, for new construction operations of building or infrastructure structures and, for reuse or rehabilitation operations of building or infrastructure structures, based in the latter case on diagnostic studies. It continues during the work execution phase. It may also intervene upon completion of the work.

###### Article 49

The technical controller must be appointed in due time, at the initial stage of the design of the structure or the consultation of the project manager, to be able to perform his task effectively. The technical controller must express reservations if he is appointed too late.

During the design phase, it is his responsibility to verify the quality of the design documents. The technical controller proceeds with the critical examination of all the technical provisions of the project, in particular, the conditions under which the technical verifications to which the other constructors are bound, for their own services, are carried out.
He establishes an initial report which he sends to the project owner before the signing of the work contract or contracts, with, at least:
-	the examination of the safety notice provided for by the regulations relating to establishments receiving the public;
-	the examination of the results of diagnostic studies for reuse or rehabilitation operations;
-	the examination of soil study reports;
-	the examination of preliminary and final draft projects;
-	the examination of the technical documents of the project with a view to establishing the initial technical control report.

The technical controller also participates in technical development meetings.

###### Article 50

During the phase of examination of work execution documents, the technical controller then proceeds to a second reading of the project and carries out a complete and critical examination of all the technical provisions, in particular:

-	the examination of documents relating to structures subject to control, in particular plans, estimates and calculation notes defining the structures;
-	the examination of documents relating to equipment elements subject to control. He also participates in technical development meetings.

###### Article 51

During the work execution phase, the technical controller, on the occasion of occasional site visits, ensures that the technical verifications incumbent upon each of the other constructors are carried out satisfactorily. He must, in addition, verify himself, physically on the construction site, that the work is executed according to the planned drawings and in accordance with the rules of the art. He must ensure in particular that the contractors actually carry out the necessary controls after execution and that the supervision of the project manager is indeed real.
The technical controller also participates in site meetings.

###### Article 52

During the completion phase, the control exercised over the works executed includes final verifications with a view to acceptance and may extend, at the request of the project owner, to the period of the guarantee of perfect completion. The technical controller then proceeds by visual examination or by auscultation of the visible or accessible parts, of the works and of the equipment elements subject to control.

The final control is prior to acceptance, but it must enable the project owner to make reservations.

In this phase, the technical controller prepares the final technical control report. If the technical control contract so provides, the technical controller provides assistance during the period of perfect completion by examining the works and equipment elements that have been subject to control and that are the subject of works carried out during the period of guarantee of perfect completion.

###### Article 53

The parties to the contract may agree to entrust the technical controller with other tasks or complementary missions, of a particular nature, such as those of controlling and certifying the completion of works or verifying that an existing or neighbouring work does not present any risk. They must then specify in the technical control contract the missions that are by definition not mandatory, which the controller agrees to take on.

###### Article 54

In addition to the obligations to verify, consisting of accomplishing the technical acts falling within his basic mission, the technical controller is required to inform the person who has contracted with him and also to advise him.
The information is given in the form of opinions contained in progress reports and in two main reports

prepared, the first, as stated in Article 32, paragraph 3 of this decree, at the initial stage of design, the second at the final stage, which depends on the scope of the mission.

The opinions are addressed to the project owner. With the authorization of the latter, the technical controller sends copies of his opinions to the insurers of the project owner and to the contractors concerned.

###### Article 55

If the project owner calls upon several technical controllers, he designates one of them to coordinate all the control missions.

#### Chapter 5: Means and remuneration of the technical controller

###### Article 56

The technical controller proposes the means necessary for the accomplishment of the technical acts and information acts corresponding to the mission requested by the project owner.

These means are specified in the offer. They include at a minimum a breakdown of the estimated intervention time and the overall price according to the mission phases and the qualifications of the technical personnel.

###### Article 57

The technical control mission gives rise to remuneration fixed contractually. The amount of this remuneration takes into account the scope of the mission requested.

#### Chapter 6: Scope of intervention of technical control

##### Section 1

Technical control of construction projects for building or infrastructure works

###### Article 58

Technical control covers, depending on the type of work, the solidity of utility works, foundations, framework, enclosure and roofing and equipment elements that form an inseparable part of said works, the quality of materials and construction elements used both for buildings or premises and other infrastructure works and for interior fittings, and prices at the technical level as well as the safety conditions for persons.
Technical control also covers compliance with rules relating to accessibility for disabled persons.

At the request of the project owner or his representative, technical control may, in addition, cover all other elements of the construction whose execution is likely to present particular technical uncertainties against which the project owner considers it useful to protect himself.

###### Article 59

Technical control relating to the solidity of the work requires a technical verification of the structural work, as defined according to the provisions of Article 66 of this law.

The technical controller must ensure the adaptation of the foundations of the work and its stability, without his mission extending, except by contrary agreement, to an assessment of its purpose.

###### Article 60

Technical control with regard to the safety of persons, this being closely linked to the solidity of the work, covers all elements likely to present a danger to persons in constructions, such as equipment elements forming an inseparable part of the works or elements that ensure enclosure, roofing and waterproofing, and likely to have repercussions in terms of fire risks, nuisances or hygiene.

###### Article 61

Constructions, works or building and infrastructure projects that, by reason of their nature, importance or location in areas exposed to natural or technological risks, present particular risks for the safety of persons and property, or whose operation is essential for civil security, defence or the maintenance of public order, are subject to mandatory technical control.

Mandatory technical control is exercised in particular on documents and construction, reconstruction, demolition, repair, rehabilitation or renovation works relating to the following types of works or building and infrastructure projects:

-	individual dwelling when it comprises several levels;
-	sports and leisure complex;
-	commercial complex;
-	industrial complex
-	hospitals;
-	school and university buildings;
-	administrative buildings;
-	water extraction, treatment, storage works, water towers and various reservoirs, and water distribution;
-	sanitation, drainage, treatment and purification works for wastewater;
-	roads and various networks, roads, expressways, interchanges;
-	bridges, viaducts, culverts, ferries;
-	aerodromes and air terminals;
-	parking lots, parking areas;
-	storage and handling areas;
-	earthworks and railway works, stations and railway platforms;
-	seaports, river ports;
-	earthworks, dikes;
-	hydroelectric dams and other electrical installations;
-	works for the transport of liquid or liquefied hydrocarbons, chemical products, gas;
-	works, transport or broadcasting installations and terminal equipment for electronic communications or telecommunications.

In all cases, technical control is mandatory for establishments receiving the public, all buildings comprising at least one floor or one basement or one terrace, all technical infrastructures for storage, transformation, conservation and packaging of agro-pastoral and fishery products and, for scientific, technical and research and development establishments.

##### Section 2

Technical control of technical equipment or equipment elements of works

Sub-section 1: Periodic technical control, maintenance and safety of elevators

###### Article 62

Elevators are subject to periodic technical inspection concerning their operating condition and the safety of persons.

The technical inspection is entrusted to a qualified or competent person in this field. The activities of this person must be covered by insurance against the consequences of their professional liability attached to the inspection of elevators. They must have no link likely to affect their impartiality and independence, either with the owner who calls upon them, or with a company likely to carry out work on an elevator or its maintenance. Where it is a legal person, its capital must not be held, even partially, by such a company.

However, it is not prohibited for the technical inspector to advise the owner, particularly regarding a prescription of works.

###### Article 63

Elevators are subject to maintenance appropriate to keep them in good working order and to ensure the safety of persons.

This obligation falls upon the owner of the elevator. The owner entrusts the maintenance of the elevator, by concluding a standard contract, or delegates the maintenance of the elevator, within the framework of a complete contract including the replacement of elements and parts, to a service provider, to a maintenance company. However, if they have the necessary technical capabilities, they may provide for it by their own means.

###### Article 64

The elevators to which the provisions of the articles of this section apply are devices that serve permanently, the levels of buildings and constructions by means of a car that moves along rigid guides whose inclination to the horizontal is greater than 15 degrees and which is intended for the transport either of persons, or of persons and objects, or only of objects provided that it is accessible, without difficulty to a person and that it is equipped with control elements located inside or within reach of the person located therein.

Also regarded as elevators are devices that move along a course

perfectly fixed in space, even if they do not move along rigid guides, notably elevators guided by scissors.

###### Article 65: The safety of an elevator consists in ensuring

1.	the closing of landing doors;
2.	safe access of persons to the car;
3.	the protection of users against shocks caused by the closing of doors;
4.	the prevention of risks of falling and crushing of the car;
5.	protection against malfunctions of the speed of the car;
6.	the provision to users of means of alert and communication with an intervention service;
7.	the protection of electrical circuits of the installation;
8.	safe access of intervention personnel to machine rooms, associated equipment and spaces traveled by the car.

###### Article 66

The maintenance of an elevator has the purpose of ensuring its proper functioning and maintaining the level of safety resulting from the application of Article 48 of this decree.

To this end, the owner of an elevator installation takes the following minimum measures:

1-	periodic operations and verifications:

a)	a visit every six weeks to monitor the operation of the installation and carry out necessary adjustments;
b)	verification every six weeks of the effectiveness of landing door locks and, where applicable, devices preventing or limiting acts affecting the locking of landing doors;
c)	semi-annual examination of the good condition of cables and annual verification of safety gears;
d)	annual cleaning of the pit of the installation, the car roof and the machine room;
e)	lubrication and cleaning of parts.

2-	occasional operations:

a)	repair or replacement, if they cannot be repaired, of small parts of the installation showing signs of excessive wear;
b)	specific maintenance measures intended to eliminate or mitigate defects presenting a danger to the safety of persons or affecting the proper functioning of the device that the technical inspection mentioned in Article 37 of this decree will have identified;
c)	in case of incident, interventions to release persons trapped in the car as well as troubleshooting and restoration to normal operation of the elevator;
d)	in addition, when important parts of the installation, other than those mentioned in point
2.a) are worn, the owner has them repaired or replaced if they cannot be repaired.

###### Article 67

The owner enters into a written maintenance contract with a company whose personnel responsible for maintenance must have received appropriate training.

The contract includes the following minimum clauses:

a)	the performance of the obligations prescribed in Article 49 of this decree, with the exception of its last paragraph;
b)	the duration of the contract, which cannot be less than one year, the terms of its renewal or termination;
c)	the conditions of availability and supply of spare parts, and the indication of the guaranteed time limit for the replacement of parts mentioned in point 2.a) of Article 49 of this decree;
d)	the description, established jointly, of the initial condition of the installation;
e)	the updating of the maintenance logbook;
f)	the guarantees provided by the insurance contracts of the maintenance company;
g)	the penalties incurred in case of non-performance or poor performance of contractual obligations as well as the procedures for settling disputes;
h)	the conditions and procedures for possible recourse to subcontractors;
i)	the conditions under which amendments may be made;
j)	the detailed formula for price revision.

###### Article 68

The owner of an elevator is required to have a technical inspection of their installation carried out every five years.

The technical inspection has the purpose of:

a)	verifying that the devices are equipped with safety devices and that these devices are in good condition;
b)	identifying any defect presenting a danger to the safety of persons or affecting the proper functioning of the device.

###### Article 69

To carry out the technical inspection provided for in Article 48 of this decree, the owner calls upon, of their own accord:

a)	a technical inspector, within the meaning of Article 4 of this decree, who benefits from prior authorization enabling them to intervene on elevators;
b)	a natural or legal person presenting guarantees of competence of persons carrying out technical inspections in elevator installations based on criteria of technical knowledge, professional experience and aptitude for technical inspection in the field of elevators, defined by order of the minister responsible for construction.

The person responsible for the technical inspection provides the owner with a document by which they attest on their honor that they are in a regular situation with regard to the provisions of the second paragraph of Article 45 of this decree.

The elevator owner makes available to the person responsible for the technical inspection a maintenance logbook and an annual report.

###### Article 70

The person who performs the technical inspection shall prepare a report indicating the operations carried out and, where applicable, the defects identified. Within one month following the end of the intervention, that person shall deliver this report to the owner.
The owner shall transmit the report to the company or person responsible for the maintenance of the elevator and, if works are made necessary, to the persons responsible for their design and execution.

A joint order of the ministers responsible for construction and industry shall specify, as necessary, the nature of the inspection measures to be performed and the procedures for preparing the inspection report.

###### Article 71

Any person holding a title of occupancy in a building containing an elevator has the right to information concerning the safety of the elevator, in particular, the right to consult, in the premises of the registered office or domicile of the owner or in those of his representative, the technical inspection report or its conclusions.

Upon request and at his expense, he may obtain from the owner of the elevator a written copy of these documents.

###### Article 72

In case of failure to comply with the requirements relating to the safety of elevators, the summary proceedings judge of the tribunal de grande instance in whose jurisdiction the building is located may be seized in order to order compliance with the obligations of maintenance, technical inspection and information of occupants or individuals provided for in articles 49 to 54 of this decree.

#### Chapitre 7

De la responsabilité du contrôleur technique et de l'assurance des risques
de la construction

###### Article 74

In the context of his mission, the technical inspector must take out ten-year liability insurance incurred in respect of construction risks or ten-year losses relating to building works. He shall be held liable for damage that would compromise the solidity of the building structure or render it unfit for its purpose.

The technical inspector is subject, within the limits of the mission entrusted to him by the project owner, to the presumption of contractual liability of structure constructors enacted by articles 5, 25 and 26 of law no. 13-2004 of 31 March 2004 referred to above and which is prescribed under the conditions provided for in article 32 of the same law. He is to that extent subject to the ten-year guarantee and his liability is extended to consequential damage.

He is not subject to the two-year or proper functioning guarantee by virtue of the fact that it does not fall within his mission to give his opinion on equipment elements separable from the building structure.

The technical inspector is liable towards other structure constructors to bear the repair of damage only up to the share of liability that may be attributed to him within the limits of the missions defined by the contract binding him to the project owner.

By virtue of his preventive role, he may not only incur his ten-year liability and, apart from that, he remains subject to a duty of advice which would lead to making him contractually liable for any breach of this obligation.

###### Article 75

The presumption of liability applies only within the framework of the mission entrusted to the technical inspector by the project owner.

#### Chapter 1: General provisions

###### Article 1

This decree establishes, in application of Article 13 of Law No. 29-2021 of 12 May 2021 referred to above, the procedures for classification, declassification and reclassification of tourist accommodation establishments.

###### Article 2

For the purposes of this decree, tourist accommodation establishment means any commercial establishment offering for rent, rooms, apartments or equipped and furnished suites, either to transient clientele or to clientele making a stay characterized by rental by the day, week or month.

#### Chapter 2

Compliance with classification standards in the construction, transformation
or extension of a tourist accommodation establishment

###### Article 3

Without prejudice to the provisions in force regarding urban planning and construction, any promoter carrying a tourist accommodation establishment project is required to determine the classification of his establishment when filing the application for a building permit.

###### Article 4

Without prejudice to the provisions in force regarding urban planning and construction, the issuance of the certificate of conformity for a construction, transformation or extension project of a tourist accommodation establishment is contingent upon compliance with the classification standards in force.

#### Chapter 3: Application for classification or reclassification

###### Article 5: Classification operations are mandatory

Any promoter of a tourist accommodation establishment is required to file a classification or reclassification application at the time of filing the application for authorization or approval.

###### Article 6

The application for classification or reclassification includes the following documents:

- the application addressed to the minister in charge of tourism, indicating the identity of the operator, the sign, the address and/or exact location of the establishment;
- the detailed plan of the establishment;
- the building permit duly issued by the competent services;
- the certificate of conformity duly issued by the competent services;
- the description of services to be provided specifying the accommodation capacity and ancillary activities if applicable;
- the self-assessment report of the tourist accommodation establishment according to the classification standards in force.

The fees relating to the application for classification or reclassification are set by joint order of the ministers in charge of tourism and finance.

#### Chapter 4: Classification of tourist accommodation establishments

###### Article 7

The classification of a tourist accommodation establishment is pronounced by the tourism administration on the basis of the classification standards established by decree in the Council of Ministers, after opinion of the national classification commission.

The composition of this commission shall be determined by order of the minister in charge of tourism.

###### Article 8

In tourist accommodation establishments of 4-star or 5-star category, the visit of the classification commission is supplemented by a so-called "mystery visit" whose standards are annexed to the decree establishing the classification standards.

The mystery visit takes place without the knowledge of the operator and has the purpose of ensuring the quality of services provided.

Based on the results of the "mystery visit," the tourism administration may either maintain the classification proposed by the national classification commission, or place it in a lower category than that proposed by the national commission.

###### Article 9

The classification of new tourist accommodation establishments must be pronounced within a period varying between two and four months following the date of the start of its operation.

###### Article 10

No tourist accommodation establishment may display the sign or be marketed at a category other than that which was assigned to it upon its classification.

###### Article 11

The administration in charge of tourism reserves the right to carry out classification operations of any tourist accommodation establishment including those that refrain from filing the classification application.

###### Article 12

The classification of a tourist accommodation establishment is valid for a period of five
(5) years.

At the end of the aforementioned validity period, the tourist accommodation establishment must file a new classification application according to the procedures specified in Article 6 of this decree.

###### Article 13

Promoters of tourist accommodation establishments must facilitate classification operations conducted by the tourism administration as well as any organization accredited by it, in accordance with the procedures provided by regulation.

###### Article 14

The classification of a tourist accommodation establishment does not exempt it from any inspection, or administrative procedure required by the regulations in force.

#### Chapter 5: Declassification

###### Article 15

The declassification of a tourist accommodation establishment is pronounced in case of failure to maintain the characteristics of the initial category.

###### Article 16

Declassification is pronounced by order of the minister in charge of tourism, after opinion of the national classification commission.

#### Chapter 6: Reclassification

###### Article 17

Any promoter of a tourist accommodation establishment may request a reclassification in case of improvement of operating conditions.

###### Article 18

Reclassification is pronounced by order of the minister responsible for tourism, after opinion of the national classification commission.

#### Chapter 7: Control of tourist accommodation establishments

###### Article 19

Control operations of tourist accommodation establishments are carried out by the tourism administration.

###### Article 20

The control operations mentioned in Article 19 above may be unannounced or notified in advance to the tourist accommodation establishment. They aim to verify the compliance of the tourist accommodation establishment with the regulations in force.

Any control must be formalized by the preparation of a report.

###### Article 21

In the case where the control of a tourist accommodation establishment reveals deficiencies, the tourism administration grants it a deadline for compliance, under penalty of the sanctions provided for by the texts in force.

#### Chapter 8: Transitional, miscellaneous and final provisions

###### Article 22

Functional tourist accommodation establishments have the obligation to regularize their situation within twelve (12) months following the publication of this decree.

###### Article 23

When the decision of classification, declassification or reclassification is pronounced, the promoter of an establishment has the obligation to affix on the main facade thereof a sign indicating the level of its classification.
The sign is provided by the tourism administration and remains the property of the State.

###### Article 24

This decree, which repeals all previous contrary provisions, shall be registered and published in the Official Journal of the Republic of Congo.

#### Chapitre 1 : Des missions

###### Article 3 : The office for the promotion of the tourism industry has, as its missions, to

-	implement the Government's national policy for the promotion of the tourism industry;
-	develop and promote the tourism potential of Congo at national and international level;
-	ensure the expansion of the tourism industry in the Republic of Congo;
-	design, develop and market tourism products;
-	stimulate tourist flows from source markets through an active presence with tour operators;
-	provide legal and natural persons, public and private, working in the tourism sector, with all the multifaceted assistance necessary for the promotion of their activities, including technical assistance;
-	foster synergy between the various State partners involved in the development of sustainable and responsible tourism;
-	ensure the promotion of tourism events, in particular through the organisation of fairs, exhibitions, workshops, trips, with a view to promoting cultural, sustainable and responsible tourism;
-	support tourism development actions undertaken by local authorities, in particular:
-	the development and promotion of tourism potential at departmental or municipal level;
-	the promotion of tourism events at departmental or municipal level.

###### Article 5

CONSTITUTIONAL COURT

Decision No. 005/DCC/SVA/22 of 10 May 2022 on the appeal for unconstitutionality of articles 1 and 16 of Order No. 1651 of 9 June 2000 authorising the company Maisons Sans Frontière to develop and subdivide the land known as "Lagune de Tchikobo", in the commune of Pointe-Noire.

The Constitutional Court,

Seized pursuant to an application registered at the secretariat general of the Constitutional Court, under number CC-SG 005, by which Mrs. BIKINDOU, née NKOUKA ALELE Catherine, requests the Constitutional Court to declare unconstitutional and then annul articles 1 and 16 of Order No. 1651 of 9 June 2000 authorising the company Maisons sans Frontière to develop and subdivide the land known as "Lagune de Tchikobo", in the commune of Pointe-Noire, and to order the modification of said order by a legislative or regulatory text;
Having regard to the Constitution;
Having regard to organic law No. 28-2018 of 7 August 2018 on the organisation and functioning of the Constitutional Court, as amended and supplemented by organic law No. 57-2020 of 18 November 2020; Having regard to decree No. 2018-452 of 14 December 2018 on the

appointment of members of the Constitutional Court; Having regard to decree No. 2018-456 of 15 December 2018 on the appointment of the president of the Constitutional Court; Having regard to decree No. 2018-457 of 15 December 2018 on the appointment of the vice-president of the Constitutional Court;
Having regard to decree No. 2018-479 of 26 December 2018 on the attributions, organisation and functioning of the secretariat general of the Constitutional Court; Having regard to decree No. 2021-111 of 26 February 2021 on the appointment of the secretary general of the Constitutional Court;
Having regard to decree No. 2018-459 of 15 December 2018 on the appointment of the deputy secretary general of the Constitutional Court;
Having regard to the rules of procedure of the Constitutional Court; Having regard to Order No. 1651 of 9 June 2000 authorising the company Maisons sans frontière to develop and subdivide the land known as "Lagune de Tchikobo", located in the commune of Pointe-Noire;
Together with the documents in the file; The rapporteur having been heard;

I.	On the facts

Whereas Mrs. BIKINDOU, née NKOUKA ALELE Catherine, requests the Constitutional Court to declare unconstitutional and then annul articles 1 and 16 of Order No. 1651 of 9 June 2000 authorising the company Maisons sans Frontière to develop and subdivide the land known as
"Lagune de Tchikobo", in the commune of Pointe-Noire, and to order the modification of said order by a legislative or regulatory text;

That she recalls that article 125 of the Constitution provides that the law also establishes the rules concerning the regime of property, real rights and civil and commercial obligations as well as those concerning the state and land regime;

That, according to her, it is in accordance with the Constitution that the law provided for articles 41, 82, 83 and 173 of law No. 52-83 of 21 April 1983 on the state and land code in the Republic of Congo;

That she maintains, therefore, that said order causes her harm in that it does not demonstrate that the lands, which had been previously allocated to her, had not been developed;

That, according to her, if the State, within the framework of its urbanisation policy and for reasons of public interest, may proceed with expropriation, it is in return for fair and equitable compensation of the victims;

That she considers that it is in disregard of articles 41, 82, 83 and 173 of law No. 52/83 of 21 April 1983 aforementioned and, consequently, in violation of article 125 of the Constitution that Order No. 1651 of 9 June 2000 authorised the company Maisons sans Frontière to develop and subdivide the land known as "Lagune de Tchikobo", in the commune of Pointe-Noire.

II.	On jurisdiction

Whereas Mrs. BIKINDOU, née NKOUKA ALELE Catherine, requests the Constitutional Court to declare unconstitutional and then annul articles 1 and 16 of Order No. 1651 of 9 June 2000 authorising the company Maisons sans Frontière to develop and subdivide the land known as "Lagune de Tchikobo", in the commune of Pointe-Noire, and to order the modification of said order by a legislative or regulatory text;

Whereas under the terms of article 175, paragraph 2, of the Constitution: "The Constitutional Court is the judge of the constitutionality of laws, treaties and international agreements";

Whereas the requests formulated by the applicant do not fall within the jurisdiction attributed to the Constitutional Court as determined in article 175, paragraph 2, aforementioned of the Constitution;

That it follows that the Constitutional Court does not have jurisdiction.
