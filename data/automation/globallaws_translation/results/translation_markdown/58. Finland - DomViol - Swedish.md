# Act on Restraining Orders

Act on Restraining Orders
See the note on copyright in the terms of use In accordance with the decision of Parliament, it is prescribed:
Chapter 1

General provisions

Section 1

Restraining order

A restraining order may be issued to avert a crime against life, health, freedom or peace or a threat of such a crime or to avert other serious harassment.
If the person who feels threatened and the person against whom the order is intended to apply permanently reside in a common dwelling, a restraining order may be issued to avert a crime against life, health or freedom or a threat of such a crime (restraining order concerning common dwelling). (30.7.2004/711)

Section 2

Prerequisites for restraining order

A restraining order may be issued if there is reasonable cause to assume that the person against whom the order is intended to apply will commit a crime against the life, health, freedom or peace of the person who feels threatened or will otherwise seriously harass that person.
A restraining order concerning a common dwelling may be issued if the person against whom the order is intended to apply, through threats, previous crimes or other behaviour, gives cause to assume that he or she is likely to commit a crime against the life, health or freedom of the person who feels threatened and a prohibition is not unreasonable having regard to the seriousness of the threatening crime, the circumstances of the persons residing in the common dwelling and other circumstances in the matter. (30.7.2004/711)

Section 2 a (30.7.2004/711)

How the prerequisites for a restraining order are assessed

When assessing the prerequisites for a prohibition, account shall be taken of the circumstances of the persons involved in the matter, the nature of the crimes or harassment that have occurred and whether they have been repeated, as well as the probability that the person against whom the order is intended to apply will continue the harassment or commit a crime against the person who feels threatened.

Section 3 (30.7.2004/711)

Content of the prohibition

In the manner specified in more detail in the decision in the matter, a person who has been issued a restraining order may not meet the person whom the order is intended to protect or otherwise contact that person or attempt to do so (normal restraining order), unless otherwise follows from paragraph 4. It is likewise prohibited to follow and observe the person whom the order is intended to protect.
In addition to what is prescribed in paragraph 1, a person who has been issued a restraining order concerning a common dwelling shall remove himself or herself from the common dwelling and may not return to it.
If there is reason to assume that a restraining order under paragraph 1 is not sufficient to avert the threat of crime or the harassment or that a restraining order under paragraph 2 is not sufficient to avert the threat of crime, the restraining order may be extended to also apply to presence in the vicinity of the permanent dwelling, holiday dwelling, workplace or a comparable specifically designated place where the person whom the order is intended to protect stays (extended restraining order).
The restraining order does not apply to contacts for which there is a factual basis and which for special reasons are manifestly necessary.
(16.2.2023/202)

Section 3 a (16.2.2023/202)

Technically monitored restraining order

The court may, at the request of an official entitled to detain, determine that monitoring of compliance with an extended restraining order shall be carried out with the aid of technical devices that are given to the person who has been issued the prohibition or attached to him or her at the wrist, ankle or waist, or with the aid of a combination of such devices (technically monitored restraining order) if technical monitoring is necessary to avert a threat of crime against life, health or freedom and the monitoring is not unreasonable having regard to the seriousness of the threatening crime, the circumstances of the person placed under monitoring and other circumstances that have emerged in the matter.
A person who has been issued a technically monitored restraining order is obliged to cooperate in putting the technical monitoring into use, and in attaching the devices referred to in paragraph 1 that are to be used in the monitoring, by remaining at the court's meeting place so that the devices can be attached after the meeting or by appearing at a later time so that the devices can be attached and by allowing the devices to be attached. A person who has been issued a technically monitored restraining order is obliged to cooperate in the implementation of the technical monitoring by possessing or wearing the devices used for monitoring, ensuring that they are kept functional and handling them carefully and following the Criminal Sanctions Agency's instructions on how they are to be used.

Chapter 2

Issuing of restraining order

Section 4

Competent authority

Matters concerning restraining orders are decided by the district court. The district court has quorum even with the chairperson alone. The meeting may be held also at another time and place than what is determined for a general court of first instance's meeting.
The competent court is the district court in the locality where the person whom the order is intended to protect has domicile or where the order is mainly intended to be applied. If the person against whom the order is intended to apply is suspected of a crime that may be of significance in deciding the matter concerning the restraining order, the court competent in the criminal case is also competent to decide the matter.

Section 11 provides for temporary restraining order

Section 5

How matters concerning restraining orders are initiated and prepared at the district court

A restraining order may be requested by the person who feels threatened or harassed or by a prosecutorial, police or social authority. The request may be written or oral. The request may be supplemented if necessary.
In a matter concerning a restraining order, a police investigation may be conducted pursuant to Chapter 6 of the Police Act (872/2011) if this promotes the handling of the matter. An order for a police investigation is given by the authority handling the matter concerning the restraining order. (22.7.2011/892)
A request for a restraining order shall be served on the person against whom the order is intended to apply and the person whom the order is intended to protect, if the latter is not the applicant, in the manner determined in Chapter 11 of the Code of Judicial Procedure concerning service of summons.
At the same time, the parties to the matter shall be requested to submit their response to the request for a restraining order in writing, within a time determined by the court, or orally at the court's meeting. The matter shall be transferred directly to main hearing if the court considers it unnecessary to request a written response.

Section 6

Main hearing at the district court

What is determined concerning proceedings in criminal cases applies mutatis mutandis to the handling of the matter.
The matter may be examined and decided despite the absence of the person against whom the order is intended to apply, unless that person's presence is necessary for the investigation of the matter and that person has been summoned to the court under penalty of such sanction. The police, prosecutorial and social authorities have the right to be present and plead at the handling of the matter also when the authority in question is not the applicant.
The district court shall ensure that the matter is handled thoroughly. Matters concerning restraining orders shall be handled expeditiously.

## § (16.2.2023/202)

Validity of the restraining order

A restraining order may be issued for a maximum of one year. However, a restraining order concerning a shared dwelling may be issued for a maximum of three months and a technically monitored restraining order for a maximum of six months. The order enters into force when the district court makes a decision on issuing the restraining order. The decision shall be observed even if an appeal has been lodged, unless a higher court handling the matter determines otherwise.
An order may be renewed. A restraining order may be renewed for a maximum of two years. However, a restraining order concerning a shared dwelling and a technically monitored restraining order may be renewed for a maximum of three months. Renewal may be requested before the previous order has ceased to be valid.

Content of the decision

The decision in a matter concerning a restraining order shall be in writing and state
1)	the authority that has decided the matter,
2)	the date of the decision,
3)	the person who has applied for the order,
4)	the person against whom the order is intended to apply and the person whom the order is intended to protect,
5)	a brief account of the matter,
6)	the decision together with reasoning, and
7)	the provisions of law that have been applied.
If an order is issued, the decision shall specify the content of the order and any exceptions as well as state the threat of punishment for violation of the order. The decision shall state the day on which the order ceases.
If a restraining order concerning a shared dwelling is issued, the decision shall also specify the dwelling from which the person issued the order shall remove themselves. The decision shall also state how the person issued the order shall gain access to the personal belongings that he or she needs. If necessary, the decision shall be accompanied by instructions on how the person issued the order can obtain help with arranging their accommodation during the period the order is in force.  (30.7.2004/711)
If a restraining order is issued, the decision shall be accompanied by an account of the possibility to apply for a European protection order in the manner prescribed in Directive 2011/99/EU of the European Parliament and of the Council on the European protection order and in the Act on the national implementation of the provisions falling within the scope of legislation in the Directive of the European Parliament and of the Council on the European protection order and on the application of the directive  (226/2015). Notifications of the judgment

The applicant, the person whom the order is intended to protect, if this person is not the applicant, and the person against whom the order is intended to apply shall be notified by the district court of the judgment in a matter concerning a restraining order. However, when a request for a restraining order has been rejected, the person against whom the order was intended to apply need not be notified of this if that person has not pleaded in the matter.
The person who has been issued a restraining order shall be notified in observance of one of the verifiable service procedures in Chapter 11 of the Code of Judicial Procedure, unless the judgment has been pronounced or communicated in that person's presence.

## § ( 30.7.2004/711)

Decision on temporary restraining order

A restraining order may also be issued temporarily. However, a technically monitored restraining order may not be issued temporarily. A decision on a temporary restraining order is made by an official entitled to order detention. The decision may also be made by a court. ( 16.2.2023/202)
An official entitled to order detention shall on their own initiative issue a temporary restraining order if a person whose life or health is threatened has such an obvious need for protection that it requires the order to be issued immediately.  (i 6.2.2023/202)
Before a matter concerning a temporary restraining order is decided, both the person against whom the order is intended to apply and the person whom the order is intended to protect shall be given an opportunity to be heard. However, the matter may be decided without hearing the party to the case if it is evident that the request is unfounded or if the person against whom the order is intended to apply cannot be located.
A decision on a temporary restraining order may not be appealed separately.

11 a §  (16.2.2023/202)

Referral to services

The police or the prosecutor shall ask the person whom the temporary restraining order is intended to protect whether he or she consents to his or her contact information being given to an operator providing support services and, if the person whom the order is intended to protect gives consent, convey the information without undue delay.
The police or the prosecutor shall inform the person issued a temporary restraining order about available support services.

Submission of decision to the district court

An official entitled to order detention shall without delay and at the latest within three days of their decision on a temporary restraining order submit the matter to the competent district court. The official who has made the decision shall simultaneously provide the district court with their decision and the documents in the matter. The matter shall be taken up for consideration at the court at the latest seven days after the temporary restraining order was issued.
The decision by which a temporary restraining order is issued shall, in addition to what is provided in

## §

shall also be stated when and at which district court the matter will be dealt with. A temporary restraining order shall be immediately observed unless the court dealing with the matter determines otherwise.

12 a § ( 30.7.2004/711)

How temporary restraining order concerning shared residence is dealt with at the district court

The district court shall hold the main hearing within one week from the time an official entitled to detain has brought his or her decision on a temporary restraining order concerning shared residence to the district court. If the main hearing is cancelled, a new main hearing shall be held no later than two weeks after the day the main hearing was intended to be held.
The district court may extend the time limits in subsection 1 if some exceptionally important reason requires this.
When the main hearing is cancelled or postponed, the district court shall at the same time determine whether the temporary restraining order concerning shared residence shall continue to apply.

Supplementary provisions

With regard to temporary restraining orders, what is provided concerning restraining orders shall otherwise apply as applicable. Chapter 4
Special provisions

Costs in the matter

Those who are parties to a matter concerning a restraining order shall themselves be responsible for their costs in the matter. If weighty reasons exist, however, a party to the matter may be ordered by the court to pay in whole or in part the opposing party's reasonable litigation costs.

14 a §  (16.2.2023/202)

Counsel

The court may appoint counsel for the person applying for a restraining order or for the person whom the order is intended to protect if he or she is not the applicant, if the request is based on suspicion of an offence against the life, health or liberty of the person whom the order is intended to protect and this shall be considered justified having regard to the seriousness of the suspected offence, the personal circumstances concerning the person whom the order is intended to protect and other circumstances.
In a matter concerning a technically monitored restraining order, counsel shall be appointed for the person against whom the order is sought if he or she requests it.
With regard to counsel, what is prescribed in Chapter 2 of the Criminal Procedure Act  (689/1997) concerning defence counsel and concerning counsel for an injured party shall apply as applicable. However, what is prescribed in section 1

## § of that chapter

shall not apply to the liability to the State for a person appointed counsel.

The police shall supervise compliance with restraining orders.

Revocation or amendment of restraining orders

The person against whom the restraining order is intended to apply or the person whom the order is intended to protect may request that the order be revoked or the content of the order amended, if this is justified on the ground that the circumstances for the parties to the matter have changed or for some other special reason. The request shall be directed to the district court that has decided the matter or, if the matter is pending before a court, to the court dealing with the matter. The request may be oral or written. In dealing with the request, sections 5 and

16 a §  (20.3.2015/228)

European protection order and protection measures in civil matters

Provisions on the issuing of a restraining order on the basis of a European protection order and on the issuing of a European protection order are laid down in Directive 2011/99/EU of the European Parliament and of the Council on the European protection order and in the Act on the national implementation of the provisions falling within the scope of legislation in Directive 2011/99/EU of the European Parliament and of the Council on the European protection order and on the application of the Directive.
Provisions on mutual recognition of protection measures in civil matters are laid down in Regulation (EU) No 606/2013 of the European Parliament and of the Council on mutual recognition of protection measures in civil matters and in the Act on the application of Regulation (EU) No 606/2013 of the European Parliament and of the Council on mutual recognition of protection measures in civil matters  (227/2015).

Penal provision

Punishment for breach of a restraining order is provided in Chapter 16, section 9 a of the Criminal Code.
