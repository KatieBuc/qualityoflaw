# NUMBER 16 OF 2015

# CONCERNING

# INTEGRATED SERVICES FOR THE EMPOWERMENT OF WOMEN AND CHILDREN

# BY THE GRACE OF ALMIGHTY GOD

# THE REGENT OF LAMANDAU,

Considering:  
a. that acts of violence against women and children that can cause physical and psychological victims constitute violations of human rights, discrimination against women and children and crimes against human dignity thus hindering the creation of justice and gender equality;  
b. that protection for women and children victims of violence is an effort to provide a sense of security and freedom from all acts of violence guaranteed by laws and regulations;  
c. that the regional government is obligated to regulate and manage the handling and provision of services for women and children victims of violence which include law enforcement, legal protection, health services, and spiritual aspects;  
d. that to improve protection for women and children victims of violence, it is necessary to establish a facility that can provide integrated services;  
e. that based on the considerations as referred to in letter a, letter b, letter c and letter d, it is necessary to stipulate a Regional Regulation of Lamandau Regency concerning Integrated Services for the Empowerment of Women and Children.

# CHAPTER I

## GENERAL PROVISIONS

#### Article 1

In this Regional Regulation, what is meant by:

1. Region is Lamandau Regency.
2. Regional Government is the administration of Government affairs by the Regional Government and the Regional House of Representatives according to the principles of Autonomy and Co-Administration with the principle of the widest possible autonomy within the system and principles of the Unitary State of the Republic of Indonesia as referred to in the 1945 Constitution.
3. Regional Government is the Regent together with Regional Apparatus as Elements of Regional Government Administration.
4. Regent is the Regent of Lamandau.
5. Regional House of Representatives is the Regional House of Representatives of Lamandau Regency.
6. Regional Secretary is the Regional Secretary of Lamandau Regency. 7. Child is a person who is not yet 18 (eighteen) years old, and not yet married, including a child who is still in the womb. 8. Woman is every person who is physically born as a woman.
9. Victim is a woman and or child who experiences misery and or suffering either directly or indirectly as a result of an act of violence.
10. Prevention is all efforts carried out by government or non-government institutions aimed at providing a sense of security to women and children.
11. Protection is all efforts aimed at providing a sense of security to victims carried out by the government, family, advocates, social institutions or other community institutions, police, prosecutors, courts or other parties either temporarily or based on court decisions or determinations, in connection with acts of violence against children and women.
12. Child Protection is all activities carried out to guarantee and protect children and their rights so that they can live, grow, develop, and participate optimally in accordance with human rights and dignity, and receive protection from violence and discrimination.
13. Women's Protection is all activities carried out to guarantee and protect women and their rights so that they can live, grow, develop, and participate optimally in accordance with human rights and dignity, and receive protection from violence and discrimination.
14. Misery is any act that results in or may result in suffering whether psychological, physical, sexual, economic, social to the victim.
15. Physical violence is any act that causes pain, injury, wounds or disability to a person's body, miscarriage, fainting and or causes death.
16. Psychological violence is an act that results in fear, loss of self-confidence, loss of ability to act, feelings of helplessness and or severe psychological suffering to women and or children.
17. Sexual violence is any act in the form of sexual harassment, forced sexual intercourse with others for commercial purposes and or certain purposes.
18. Service is an action taken as soon as possible to the victim, when seeing, hearing and knowing before, during or after the occurrence of violence against the victim.
19. Companion is a person or institution that has the ability to accompany victims to provide counseling, therapy, legal services and advocacy, for strengthening victim recovery.
20. Safe House is a temporary residence used to provide protection to women and children victims in accordance with the standard operating procedures or abbreviated as SOP that are determined.
21. Community is individuals, families, groups, social organizations and or other community organizations.
22. Integrated Service Center for Women and Children Empowerment hereinafter abbreviated as P2TP2A is one form of service facility for women and children in an effort to fulfill information and needs in the fields of education, health, economy, politics, law, protection and prevention of acts of violence and trafficking against women and children.

# CHAPTER II

## PRINCIPLES AND OBJECTIVES

#### Article 2

Integrated services for women and children empowerment are carried out based on Pancasila, the 1945 Constitution of the Republic of Indonesia, as well as the principles and basic rights as regulated in the Convention on women and children, which include:
a. Respect for human rights.
b. Justice and gender equality.
c. Non-discrimination;
d. The best interests of women and or children;
e. The right to life, survival, and development; f. Respect for the opinions of women and children;
g. Justice and gender equality; andh. Protection of victims.

#### Article 3

The objectives of organizing integrated services for women and children empowerment are:
a. To guarantee the fulfillment of the rights of women and children in accordance with applicable laws and regulations.
b. To prevent and protect women and children from violence. c. To encourage the community to carry out their obligations in the prevention and protection of women and children in accordance with applicable laws and regulations.
d. To restore victims as a result of acts of violence experienced. e. To take action against perpetrators of domestic violence.
f. To provide disaggregated data by sex and information on issues of empowerment and protection of women and children for the community that needs it.
g. To encourage the provision of facilities, infrastructure and various types of services in various areas of life for women and children such as data and information centers, counseling, psychological and medical therapy, assistance, education and training, referral centers, skills training and so on as an effort to improve the quality of life of women, welfare and protection of children managed by the community independently.

# CHAPTER III

## RIGHTS AND OBLIGATIONS

#### Article 4

The rights of children are as follows:
a. Every child has the right to live, grow, develop and participate properly in accordance with human dignity and worth, and to receive protection from violence and discrimination; b. Every child has the right to a name as identity and citizenship status;  
c. Every child has the right to worship according to their religion, to think and express themselves in accordance with their level of intelligence and age, under the guidance of parents;  
d. Every child has the right to know their parents, to be raised, and to be cared for by their own parents;  
e. In the event that for any reason the parents cannot guarantee the child's growth and development, or the child is in a state of neglect, then the child has the right to be cared for and adopted as a foster child or adopted child by another person in accordance with the provisions of applicable laws and regulations;  
f. Every child has the right to obtain health services and social security in accordance with physical, mental, spiritual and social needs;  
g. Every child has the right to obtain education and teaching in the context of developing their personality and level of intelligence in accordance with their interests and talents;  
h. Especially for children with disabilities, they have the right to obtain special education, while children who have excellence have the right to receive special education;  
i. Every child has the right to express and be heard their opinion, to receive, seek, and provide information in accordance with their level of intelligence and age for their self-development in accordance with the values of decency and propriety;  
j. Every child has the right to rest and utilize leisure time, to associate with peers, to play, to recreate, and to create in accordance with their interests, talents, and level of intelligence for self-development;  
k. Every child with disabilities has the right to obtain rehabilitation, social assistance, and maintenance of social welfare standards; and l. Every child while in the care of parents, guardians, or any other party responsible for care, has the right to receive protection from treatment of: - Discrimination;  

Exploitation, both economic and sexual;  

Neglect;  

Cruelty, violence, and abuse;  

Injustice; and - Other mistreatment.  
m. Every child has the right to be cared for by their own parents, unless there are valid reasons and/or legal rules showing that the separation is in the best interests of the child and is a last consideration.  n. Every child has the right to obtain protection from: - Abuse in political activities;  

Involvement in armed conflict;  

Involvement in social unrest;  

Involvement in events containing elements of violence; and - Involvement in warfare.  
o. Every child has the right to obtain protection from being the target of persecution, torture, or imposition of inhuman punishment. p. Every child has the right to obtain freedom in accordance with the law. q. Arrest, detention, or criminal imprisonment of a child shall only be carried out if in accordance with applicable law and can only be carried out as a last resort.  r. Every child deprived of liberty has the right to: - Receive humane treatment and placement separated from adults;  

Obtain legal assistance or other assistance effectively at every stage of applicable legal remedies; and - Defend themselves and obtain justice before an objective and impartial juvenile court in a closed hearing for law. s. Every child who is a victim or perpetrator of sexual violence or who is in conflict with the law has the right to confidentiality.  
t. Every child who is a victim or perpetrator of a criminal act has the right to receive legal assistance and other assistance.

#### Article 5

Victims of domestic violence have the right to receive: a. Protection from family, police, prosecution, courts, legal aid, social institutions, or other parties either temporarily or based on a protection order from the court; b. Health services in accordance with medical needs;  
c. Special handling related to victim confidentiality; d. Assistance by social workers and legal aid at every level of the examination process in accordance with the provisions of laws and regulations; ande. Spiritual guidance services.

#### Article 6

Every child has the obligation to:
a. Respect parents, guardians, and teachers;  
b. Love family, community, and care for friends;  
c. Love the homeland, nation and state;  
d. Perform worship in accordance with the teachings of their religion; ande. Practice noble ethics and morals.

#### Article 7

Every person is prohibited from committing domestic violence against persons within their household scope, by means of:
a. Physical violence;  
b. Psychological violence;  
c. Sexual violence; andd. Household neglect.

#### Article 8

1. Physical violence is an act that results in pain, illness, or serious injury.  
2. Psychological violence is an act that results in fear, loss of self-confidence, loss of ability to act, feelings of helplessness, and/or severe psychological suffering in a person. (3) Sexual violence includes:a. Forced sexual relations committed against a person residing within the household scope.  b. Forced sexual relations against one person within the household scope with another person for commercial purposes and/or certain purposes.  
4. Household neglect is an act of not providing livelihood, not providing care, or not providing maintenance to persons within the household scope.  
5. Neglect also applies to any person who causes economic dependence by restricting and/or prohibiting decent work inside or outside the home so that the victim is under the control of that person.

#### Article 9

1. The state, government, community, family, and parents have the obligation and responsibility for the implementation of child protection. (2) The government is responsible for efforts to prevent domestic violence.

#### Article 10

1. The state, government, community, family, and parents have the obligation and responsibility for the implementation of child protection. (2) The government is responsible for efforts to prevent domestic violence.  
3. Every person who hears, sees or knows of the occurrence of domestic violence is obliged to make efforts in accordance with their capacity to:a. Prevent the continuation of criminal acts;  
b. Provide protection to victims;  
c. Provide emergency assistance; andd. Assist in the process of submitting an application for protection order.

# CHAPTER IV

IMPLEMENTATION OF CHILD PROTECTION AND VICTIMS OF DOMESTIC VIOLENCE

#### Article 11

1. Every child receives protection to worship according to their religion. (2) Before the child can determine their choice, the religion embraced by the child follows the religion of their parents.  
3. The state, government, community, family, parents, guardians, and social institutions guarantee the protection of children in embracing their religion. (4) Protection of children in embracing their religion includes fostering, guiding, and practicing religious teachings for children.

#### Article 12

1. The Government is obligated to provide facilities and organize comprehensive health efforts for children, so that every child obtains an optimal degree of health from the womb.
2. Comprehensive health efforts include promotive, preventive, curative and rehabilitative services for both basic health services and referrals. (3) Parents and families are responsible for maintaining the health of children and caring for children from the womb.
4. The State, government, families and parents are obligated to protect children from acts of:a. Removal of children's organs and/or children's body tissue without regard to the health of the child;
b. Sale and purchase of organs and/or children's body tissue; andc. Health research that uses children as research subjects without parental consent and does not prioritize the best interests of the child.

#### Article 13

1. Regional governments are obligated to organize basic education of at least 9 (nine) years for all children.
2. The State, government, families and parents are obligated to provide the widest possible opportunity for children to obtain education. (3) Education is directed towards:a. Development of attitudes and personality abilities of children, talents, mental and physical abilities to achieve their optimal potential;
b. Development of respect for human rights and fundamental freedoms;
c. Development of respect for parents, cultural identity, language and their own values, national values where the child resides, from where the child originates, and civilizations that differ from one's own civilization;
d. Preparation of children for a responsible life; and e. Development of respect and love for the environment. (4) Children with physical and/or mental disabilities are given equal opportunities and accessibility to obtain regular education and special education.
5. Children in and around schools must be protected from acts of violence committed by teachers, school administrators or their friends within the school concerned or other educational institutions.

#### Article 14

1. The Government is obligated to organize the maintenance and care of neglected children, both in institutions and outside social institutions.
2. The Government in organizing maintenance and care is obligated to endeavor and assist children, so that children can:a. Participate;
b. Freely express opinions and think in accordance with their conscience and religion;
c. Freely receive oral or written information in accordance with the age and development stage of the child;
d. Freely associate and assemble;
e. Freely rest, play, recreate, create and engage in cultural arts; andf. Obtain play facilities that meet health and safety requirements.

#### Article 15

The Government and other state institutions are obligated and responsible to provide special protection to children in emergency situations, children in conflict with the law, children from minority and isolated groups, children exploited economically and/or sexually, children who are trafficked, children who are victims of narcotics, alcohol, psychotropic and other addictive substance abuse (napza), children victims of kidnapping, sale and trafficking, children victims of violence both physical and/or mental, children with disabilities, and children victims of maltreatment and neglect.

#### Article 16

1. Within 1x24 (one times twenty-four) hours from knowing or receiving a report of domestic violence, the police must immediately provide temporary protection to the victim.
2. Temporary protection is provided for a maximum of 7 (seven) days from the time the victim is received or handled.
3. Within 1x24 (one times twenty-four) hours from the provision of protection, the police must request a protection order decree from the court.

#### Article 17

In providing temporary protection, the police may cooperate with health workers, social workers, volunteer companions, and/or spiritual counselors to accompany victims.

#### Article 18

The police must provide information to victims about the victim's right to receive services and assistance.

#### Article 19

The police must immediately conduct an investigation after knowing or receiving a report about the occurrence of domestic violence.

#### Article 20

The police immediately convey to the victim about:
a. The identity of officers for introduction to the victim;
b. Domestic violence is a crime against human dignity; andc. The obligation of the police to protect victims.

#### Article 21

In providing health services to victims, health workers must:
a. Examine the health of victims in accordance with their professional standards; b. Prepare a written report of the examination results of the victim and visum et repertum at the request of the police investigator or a medical certificate that has the same legal force as evidence.

#### Article 22

In providing services, social workers must:
a. Conduct counseling to strengthen and provide a sense of security for victims;
b. Provide information regarding the rights of victims to obtain protection from the police and protection order decrees from the court;
c. Escort victims to safe houses or alternative residences; andd. Conduct integrated coordination in providing services to victims with the police, social services, social institutions needed by victims.

#### Article 23

In providing services, volunteer companions may:
a. Inform victims of their right to obtain one or more companions;
b. Accompany victims at the investigation, prosecution or court examination level by guiding victims to objectively and completely describe the domestic violence they have experienced;
c. Listen empathetically to all victim's accounts so that victims feel safe being accompanied by companions; andd. Actively provide psychological and physical strengthening to victims.

#### Article 24

In providing services, spiritual counselors must provide explanations regarding rights, obligations and provide strengthening of faith and piety to victims.

#### Article 25

In providing protection and services, advocates are obligated to: a. Provide legal consultation that includes information regarding victims' rights and the judicial process;
b. Accompany victims at the investigation, prosecution, and examination levels in court hearings and assist victims to completely describe the domestic violence they have experienced; andc. Coordinate with fellow law enforcers, volunteer companions, and social workers so that the judicial process proceeds as it should.

#### Article 26

1. Victims have the right to directly report domestic violence to the police either at the place where the victim is located or at the scene of the incident.
2. Victims may grant power of attorney to family members or other persons to report domestic violence to the police either at the place where the victim is located or at the scene of the incident.
3. In the event the victim is a child, the report may be made by parents, guardians, caregivers, or the child concerned which is carried out in accordance with the provisions of applicable laws and regulations.

#### Article 27

The Chief Judge within a period of 7 (seven) days from receipt of the application must issue a decree containing a protection order for the victim and other family members, unless there are valid reasons.

#### Article 28

An application to obtain a protection order may be submitted by:
a. The victim or the victim's family;  
b. A friend of the victim;  
c. The Police;  
d. Support volunteers; and
e. A spiritual counselor.  

Article 29 (1) An application for a protection order shall be submitted in oral or written form.  

2. In the event that the application is submitted orally, the clerk of the local district court shall record such application.  
3. In the event that the application for a protection order is submitted by a family member, a friend of the victim, the police, a support volunteer, or a spiritual counselor, the victim must give consent. In certain circumstances, the application may be submitted without the victim's consent.

#### Article 30

Upon the application of the victim or his/her attorney, the court may consider to:
a. Establish a special condition;  
b. Amend or revoke a special condition of the protection order.

#### Article 31

1. A protection order may be granted for a maximum period of 1 (one) year.  
2. A protection order may be extended by court order. (3) An application for extension of a protection order shall be submitted 7 (seven) days before the expiry of its validity period.

#### Article 32

1. The court may declare one or more additional protection orders.  
2. In granting additional protection orders, the court shall consider statements from the victim, health workers, social workers, support volunteers, and/or spiritual counselors.

#### Article 33

1. Based on consideration of the danger that may arise, the court may declare one or more additional conditions in the protection order. (2) In granting additional conditions in the protection order, the court shall consider statements from the victim, health workers, social workers, support volunteers, and/or spiritual counselors.

#### Article 34

1. The Police may arrest and subsequently detain without a warrant a perpetrator who is believed to have violated a protection order, even though such violation was not committed at the place where the police officer is on duty.  
2. The arrest and detention as referred to in paragraph (1) shall be accompanied by a warrant of arrest and detention after 1x24 (one times twenty-four) hours.  
3. Suspension of detention shall not apply to detention as referred to in paragraph (1) and paragraph (2).

#### Article 35

1. To provide protection to the victim, the police may arrest the perpetrator with sufficient preliminary evidence for having violated a protection order.  
2. The arrest as referred to in paragraph (1) may be followed by detention accompanied by a detention order within 1x24 (one times twenty-four) hours.

#### Article 36

1. The victim, the police or a support volunteer may submit a written report regarding an alleged violation of a protection order.  
2. In the event that the court receives a written report as referred to in paragraph (1), the perpetrator shall be ordered to appear within 3x24 (three times twenty-four) hours for examination.  
3. The examination as referred to in paragraph (2) shall be conducted by the court at the place where the perpetrator previously lived with the victim at the time the violation is alleged to have occurred.

#### Article 37

1. If the court finds that the perpetrator has violated a protection order and is suspected of committing further violations, the court may require the perpetrator to make a written statement containing a commitment to comply with the protection order.  
2. If the perpetrator continues to disregard such written statement as referred to in paragraph (1), the court may detain the perpetrator for a maximum of 30 days.  
3. The detention as referred to in paragraph (2) shall be accompanied by a detention order.

# CHAPTER V

## VICTIM RECOVERY

#### Article 38

For the purpose of recovery, victims may obtain services from: a. Health workers;  
b. Social workers;  
c. Support volunteers; and
d. Spiritual counselors.

#### Article 39

1. Health workers shall examine victims in accordance with their professional standards. (2) In the event that the victim requires treatment, health workers shall restore and rehabilitate the victim's health.

#### Article 40

Social workers, support volunteers, and/or spiritual counselors shall provide services to victims in the form of counseling to strengthen and/or provide a sense of security for the victim.

# CHAPTER VI

## INSTITUTIONAL ARRANGEMENTS

#### Article 41

1. In the context of providing protection services to women and children who are victims of violence, the Regional Government shall establish P2TP2A.  
2. The membership of P2TP2A as referred to in (1) shall consist of: a. regional government elements;  
b. community elements; and
c. academic elements.  
3. The membership of P2TP2A as referred to in paragraph (2) shall be determined by Regent's Decree.

#### Article 42

1. The Integrated Service Center for the Empowerment of Women and Children (P2TP2A) shall facilitate the provision of various services for the community, both physical and non-physical, including information, referral, consultation/counseling, skills training and other activities.  
2. The Integrated Service Center for the Empowerment of Women and Children (P2TP2A) may also serve as a venue for conducting training for cadres who have a strong commitment and concern for women's and children's issues in all fields (health, education, economy, politics, law and protection of women and children from violence and trafficking in persons) so that they can work together and participate in providing services to the community in a framework for improving the quality of life and protection for women and children.

#### Article 43

Programs that may be carried out by the Integrated Service Center for the Empowerment of Women and Children (P2TP2A) include: a. Women's empowerment.  
b. Protection of women and children from violence and trafficking in persons.  c. Communication, information and education.  d. Increasing community member participation.  e. Capacity building for managers.

#### Article 44

The success indicators of the Integrated Service Center for the Empowerment of Women and Children (P2TP2A) are as follows:
a. The establishment and functioning of the Integrated Service Center for the Empowerment of Women and Children (P2TP2A) that provides services for women's empowerment, protection of women and children in the fields of law, politics, education, health, economy, violence and trafficking in persons.  b. An increase in the number of women and children utilizing the Integrated Service Center for the Empowerment of Women and Children (P2TP2A). c. An increase in the number of women and children receiving training. d. The functioning of dialogue and communication mechanisms among government, community and business sectors.  e. The existence of cooperation between the Integrated Service Center for the Empowerment of Women and Children (P2TP2A) with government and business sectors.

# CHAPTER VII

## REPORTING

#### Article 45

1. The Integrated Service Center for the Empowerment of Women and Children (P2TP2A) shall report the implementation of activities to the Regent.  
2. The submission of written reports as referred to in paragraph (1) shall be carried out at least once every 3 (three) months.

# CHAPTER VIII

## FINANCING

#### Article 46

Financing for the implementation of the Integrated Service for the Empowerment of Women and Children shall be sourced from:
a. The regional revenue and expenditure budget of Lamandau Regency. b. Other lawful and non-binding sources.

# CHAPTER IX

## CLOSING PROVISIONS

This Regional Regulation shall come into force on the date of promulgation.

INTEGRATED SERVICES FOR THE EMPOWERMENT OF WOMEN AND CHILDREN I. GENERAL ELUCIDATION All forms of violence in general and acts of violence against women and or children are a form of violation of human rights and constitute a form of crime against human dignity as well as discriminatory treatment, therefore it is the obligation of the Government and Regional Government to realize guarantees of security/protection for honor, dignity, property, freedom from torture, or treatment that degrades human dignity and discriminatory treatment.

Current developments show that there are still frequent acts of violence against women and or children within the family environment as well as within society which clearly result in suffering and distress for women and or children physically, psychologically, or sexually including threats of certain actions, coercion, or deprivation of rights..

Services and handling, guidance and empowerment of women and or children have not been optimal so far. Various causes mentioned above are that most women and or child victims of violence choose to remain silent and their problems end with women and children being plunged into powerlessness.

This regional regulation is intended as an effort by the Lamandau Regency Government to meet the demands of society by providing services for the rights of women and child victims of violence in a comprehensive, clear and firm manner to protect or side with victims.
