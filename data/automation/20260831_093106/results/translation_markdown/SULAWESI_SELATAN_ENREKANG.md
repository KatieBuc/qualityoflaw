# REGENT OF ENREKANG.

a. that in order to guarantee and protect the rights of women and children so that they can participate optimally in accordance with human dignity and honor, and receive protection from violence, it is necessary to undertake efforts to protect women and children;  
b. that violence against women and children in Enrekang Regency continues to increase and spread, causing community members to be unsafe in carrying out their lives, so that integrated protection efforts are needed;  
c. that based on the provisions in Article 12 of Law Number 23 of 2014 concerning Regional Government, women's empowerment and child protection are mandatory governmental affairs for regional governments;  
d. that based on the considerations as referred to in letter a, letter b, and letter c, it is necessary to establish a Regional Regulation concerning Protection of Women and Children;

# CHAPTER I

## GENERAL PROVISIONS

#### Article 1

In this Regional Regulation, what is meant by:

1. Region is Enrekang Regency.  
2. Regional Government is the Government of Enrekang Regency.  
3. Regent is the Regent of Enrekang.  
4. Regional Apparatus, hereinafter abbreviated as PD, is the Regional Apparatus Organization within the Regional Government.  
5. Woman is a person of female gender.  
6. Child is a person who has not yet reached 18 (eighteen) years of age including a child still in the womb.  
7. Protection of Women is all efforts made by the family, regional government and others aimed at women to ensure the fulfillment of women's rights as a form of respect for human rights.  
8. Protection of Children is all activities to guarantee and protect children and their rights so that they can live, grow, develop and participate optimally in accordance with human dignity and receive protection from violence and discrimination.  
9. Special Protection is protection given to Women and Children in conflict with the law, economic and sexual exploitation, being trafficked, becoming victims of narcotics abuse, alcohol, psychotropic substances and other addictive substances (Napza), victims of kidnapping, sale, victims of violence, disability, victims of maltreatment and/or neglect.  
10. Prevention is direct efforts carried out by the Regional Government together with the community and Parents and/or family as well as private parties to prevent violations of the rights of women and children.  
11. Women's Empowerment is every effort to improve physical, mental, spiritual, social abilities, knowledge, and skills so that women are ready to be utilized according to their respective abilities.  
12. Discrimination is any distinction, harassment or exclusion that is direct or indirect based on human differentiation on the basis of religion, ethnicity, race, ethnic group, class, social status, socio-economic status, gender, language, political beliefs that result in reduction, deviation or elimination of recognition, implementation or use of human rights and basic freedoms in life both individually and collectively in the political, economic, legal, social, cultural and other aspects of life.  
13. Violence is any act that results in or may result in misery or suffering whether physical, sexual, economic, social, psychological to the victim.  
14. Violence against women is any act based on gender differences that results in or may result in misery or suffering of women physically, sexually, economically, socially, psychologically including threats of certain actions, coercion or arbitrary deprivation of liberty, whether occurring in public or private life.  
15. Violence against children is any act that results in misery or suffering to children physically, sexually, economically, socially, psychologically including threats of certain actions, coercion or deprivation of liberty.  
16. Domestic Violence is any act against a person, especially women, which results in misery or suffering physically, sexually, psychologically, and/or household neglect including threats to commit acts, coercion, or unlawful deprivation of liberty within the household sphere.  
17. Trafficking of women and children, hereinafter referred to as trafficking, is the act of recruitment, transportation, harboring, sending, transfer, or receipt of women and/or children by threat of violence, use of violence, kidnapping, confinement, forgery, fraud, abuse of power or position of vulnerability, debt bondage or giving payments or benefits, so as to obtain consent from the person holding control over that other person, whether carried out within the country or between countries, for the purpose of exploitation or resulting in the person being exploited.  
18. Exploitation is an act with or without the victim's consent which includes but is not limited to prostitution, forced labor or service, slavery or practices similar to slavery, oppression, extortion, utilization of physical, sexual, reproductive organs and/or body tissues or utilizing the labor or ability of a person by another party to obtain benefits both material and immaterial.  
19. Person with Disability is every person who experiences physical, intellectual, mental and/or sensory limitations for a long period of time who in interacting with the environment may experience obstacles and difficulties to participate fully and effectively with other citizens based on equal rights.  
20. Service is an action taken as soon as possible to the victim when seeing, hearing and/or knowing that violence against the victim will, is or has occurred.  
21. Victim is a woman and child who experiences and/or suffers either directly or indirectly as a result of violence.  
22. Children's Forum is a Children's Participation Forum formed with the aim of facilitating children's involvement in the decision-making process and enjoying changes concerning their lives either directly or indirectly which is carried out with the consent and will of all children based on awareness and understanding, in accordance with age and level of thinking maturity.  
23. Child-Friendly Regency, Sub-district, and Village is a Regency, Sub-district, and Village that has a child rights-based development system through the integration of government commitment and resources, community and business world that is planned comprehensively and sustainably in policies, programs and activities to ensure the fulfillment of children's rights.  
24. Safe house is a temporary shelter that aims to provide protection and a sense of security to victims of violence.  
25. Shelter house is a forum for guidance or welfare services for street children that carries out social assistance/guidance activities, maintenance, care, education and health, skills guidance in order to ensure that children do not carry out activities on the streets so that they can grow and develop normally.  
26. Parents are biological father and/or mother, or adoptive father and/or mother.

# CHAPTER II PRINCIPLES, OBJECTIVES AND SCOPE

#### Article 2

The implementation of Protection of Women and Children is based on the principles of:
a. non-discrimination;  
b. the best interests of women and children;  
c. the right to life, survival, and development;  
d. respect for the views of the child;  
e. legal certainty;  
f. local wisdom;  
g. justice and gender equality; andh. sustainability.

#### Article 3

The implementation of Protection of Women and Children aims to:
a. provide guidance to the regional government in planning, policies and strategies for the protection of women and children;  
b. strengthen the protective environment for women and children from all forms of violence, exploitation, maltreatment, and neglect;  
c. prevent violations of the rights of women and children;  
d. increase empowerment of women and children; ande. increase the role of government institutions, business entities, media and/or Non-Governmental Organizations in the region.

#### Article 4

The scope of regulation of Protection of Women and Children includes:
a. prevention;  
b. implementation of protection of women and children;  
c. services for women and children victims of violence;  
d. institutions;  
e. cooperation and partnership; andf. guidance and supervision.

# CHAPTER III RIGHTS OF WOMEN AND CHILDREN

## Part One Rights of women

#### Article 5

Every woman has the right to:
a. obtain protection from discriminatory actions;  
b. obtain protection from economic exploitation and sexual exploitation;  
c. obtain protection from physical, psychological, sexual violence, neglect and Trafficking;  
d. obtain decent work according to her abilities;  
e. obtain work leave while still receiving wages/salary in accordance with statutory regulations;  
f. obtain education and teaching in all types, levels and pathways of education; andg. receive special protection in the implementation of work or profession against matters that may threaten safety and/or health in relation to women's reproductive function.

## Part Two Rights of children

#### Article 6

Every child has the right to:
a. live, grow, develop and participate reasonably in accordance with human dignity;  
b. obtain protection from discriminatory actions;  
c. obtain protection from economic exploitation and sexual exploitation;  
d. obtain protection from physical, psychological, sexual violence, neglect and Trafficking;  
e. a name as personal identity and citizenship status;  
f. obtain a birth certificate free of charge;  
g. embrace religion and worship according to the teachings of their religion with the guidance of parents/guardians;  
h. not be forced to change religion by foster parents, adoptive parents and by anyone;  
i. be recognized and know their parents, be raised and cared for by their own parents;  
j. obtain health services and social security in accordance with physical, mental, spiritual and social needs;  
k. obtain education and teaching in accordance with their interests and talents;  
1. obtain care, education and training, improve themselves and the ability to participate in community and state life for children with special needs;  
m. express and have their opinions heard; andn. rest and utilize leisure time for self-development.

# CHAPTER IV

OBLIGATIONS AND RESPONSIBILITIES OF REGIONAL GOVERNMENT, COMMUNITY, PARENTS/GUARDIANS AND/OR FAMILY

## Part one

Regional Government

#### Article 7

1. The Regional Government in efforts to protect women and children is obligated and responsible to:a. guarantee the rights of women and children without discrimination in accordance with statutory regulations;  
b. establish and implement policies, programs and activities for the protection of the rights of women and the rights of children;  
c. provide support for facilities and infrastructure in the implementation of protection of women and children;  
d. cooperate with the community and/or community organizations in the implementation of protection of women and children in accordance with statutory regulations;  
e. conduct rehabilitation of women and children who are victims of violence;  
f. provide special protection to women victims of violence and trafficking in women and children;  
g. provide special protection to children in emergency situations, children in conflict with the law, child victims of violence and trafficking in women and children, children from minority and isolated groups, children exploited economically and/or sexually, children who are victims of narcotics, alcohol, psychotropic abuse and children with HIV/AIDS;  
h. organize inclusive education for children with Disabilities; andi. take preventive action in order to prevent violations of the rights of women and children.  
2. The obligations and responsibilities of the Regional Government in efforts to protect women and children as referred to in paragraph (1) shall be implemented by PD in accordance with their duties and functions.

#### Article 8

1. In carrying out the obligations and responsibilities as referred to in Article 7 paragraph (1), the Regional Government establishes programs and activities for the protection of women and children with a Regional Action Plan as the basis for PD in implementing the protection of women and children in accordance with their duties and functions.  
2. The Regional Action Plan as referred to in paragraph (1) shall be regulated by Regent Regulation.

## Part Two

Community

#### Article 9

The obligations and responsibilities of the community in efforts to protect women and children:
a. participate in prevention and provide support for the development of prevention programs for violations of the rights of women and children;  
b. provide information and/or report violations of the rights of women and children to the competent authorities; andc. supervise the law enforcement process for cases of violations of the rights of women and children.

#### Article 10

1. Parents/guardians in efforts to protect the rights of children are obligated and responsible to:a. care for, maintain, educate and protect children;  
b. develop children according to their abilities, talents and interests;  
c. provide formal and non-formal education;  
d. guide children to behave in accordance with religion, ethics and morals as well as customs; ande. prevent marriage at child age.  
2. In the event that parents/guardians do not exist, or their whereabouts are unknown, or due to a reason, cannot carry out their obligations and responsibilities, then the obligations and responsibilities as referred to in paragraph (1) may be transferred to the family.

## Part Three

Parents/Guardians and/or Family

#### Article 11

The family in efforts to protect the rights of women is obligated and responsible to:
a. protect women from conflict and domestic violence that degrades the dignity of women;  
b. provide equal opportunities for women to develop themselves in the fields of education, social, economic, political and cultural; andc. prevent violations of the rights of women in the household.

# CHAPTER V PREVENTION

## Part one Regional Government

#### Article 12

The Regional Government conducts prevention of violations of the rights of women and children by:
a. organizing socialization regarding the prevention of violence against women and children;  
b. providing guidelines on handling acts of violence against women and children;  
c. developing child-friendly schools;  
d. developing child-friendly health centers;  
e. encouraging increased participation of children through the establishment of children's forums;  
f. developing an integrated database and information system on acts of violence against women and children;  
g. developing family empowerment programs;  
h. realizing the development of Child-Friendly Regencies, Sub-districts, and Villages; andi. creating Child-Friendly Open Public Spaces (RPTRA).

## Part two

Community

#### Article 13

The community conducts prevention of violations of the rights of women and children by:
a. monitoring various conditions that occur in their environment that may cause violations of the rights of women and children;  
b. strengthening the role of community, religious and family environment organizations;  
c. preventing unregistered marriages and siri marriages;  
d. preventing marriage at Child age; ande. strengthening community participation in recovery and social reintegration.

## Part three Parents/guardians and/or Family

#### Article 14

Parents/guardians, and/or family conduct prevention of violations of the rights of women and children by:
a. protecting women and children from various acts of violation of the rights of women and children in the household.  b. supervising women and children in activities inside or outside the home; andc. providing opportunities for women and children to engage in dialogue and express opinions democratically.

# CHAPTER VI IMPLEMENTATION OF PROTECTION OF WOMEN AND CHILDREN

## Part One Protection of Women

### Paragraph 1

Health

#### Article 15

1. The Regional Government provides facilities and organizes comprehensive health efforts for women to obtain optimal health status.  
2. The provision of facilities and organization of comprehensive health efforts as referred to in paragraph (1) is supported by community participation.

### Paragraph 2

Education

#### Article 16

1. The Regional Government guarantees the implementation of informal education for women who have dropped out of school.  
2. The implementation of informal education as referred to in paragraph (1) is supported by the participation of the community and business sector.

### Paragraph 3 Empowerment

1. The Regional Government guarantees the implementation of empowerment for women.  
2. The implementation of women's empowerment as referred to in paragraph (1) is supported by the participation of the community and business sector.

### Paragraph 4

Employment

#### Article 18

1. Protection for women workers includes:a. obtaining a space for breastfeeding; andb. obtaining facilities for child care.  
2. Early childhood care facilities as referred to in paragraph (1) letter b managed by the community must obtain a recommendation from the Regional Government.

### Paragraph 5

Micro Economy

#### Article 19

Protection of Women's Micro Economy includes:
a. obtaining opportunities to conduct business activities in the household environment; andb. obtaining economic empowerment facilities especially for women heads of households.

### Paragraph 6

Marriage

#### Article 20

1. A woman during the marriage bond has equal rights and responsibilities with her husband over all matters relating to their married life, relations with their children and ownership rights and management of joint property.  
2. After a court decision regarding divorce, a woman has equal rights and responsibilities with her former husband over all matters relating to their children, taking into account the best interests of the child.  
3. After a court decision regarding divorce, a woman has equal rights with her former husband over all matters relating to joint property without reducing the rights of the child, in accordance with the provisions of laws and regulations.

## Part Two Child Protection

### Paragraph 1

Health

#### Article 21

1. The Regional Government provides facilities and organizes comprehensive health efforts for children to obtain optimal health status starting from the womb.  
2. The provision of facilities and organization of comprehensive health efforts as referred to in paragraph (1) is supported by community participation.  
3. Comprehensive health efforts as referred to in paragraph (1) include promotive, preventive, curative, and rehabilitative efforts both for basic health and referral.  
4. The Regional Government facilitates and provides nutritional assistance or supplementary food for improving the health of children and poor families.  
5. Comprehensive health efforts as referred to in paragraph (1) are carried out gradually in accordance with regional financial capacity.

#### Article 22

Families and parents are responsible for maintaining and caring for children from the womb.

#### Article 23

The Regional Government, business sector, community, families and parents endeavor to ensure that children born are protected from disease.

### Paragraph 2

Education

#### Article 24

1. The Regional Government guarantees the implementation of a 9 (nine) year compulsory education program for all children.  
2. The implementation of the compulsory education program as referred to in paragraph (1) is supported by the participation of the community and business sector.  
3. Parents and families provide the widest opportunity to children according to the child's ability to obtain education.  
4. Every organizer of an educational unit is prohibited from expelling children from educational institutions without clear reasons and through procedures in accordance with laws and regulations.

#### Article 25

Children in conflict with the law, children who experience pregnancy outside of marriage and children victims of HIV/AIDS transmission are protected in their right to obtain education.

#### Article 26

For children aged 7 (seven) to less than 15 (fifteen) years who have not completed their formal education may pursue education through informal education units:
a. package A study group equivalent to Elementary School and Madrasah Ibtidaiyah; orb. package B study group equivalent to Junior High School and Madrasah Tsanawiyah;

#### Article 27

1. The Regional Government may provide scholarships and/or educational assistance for children from poor families.  
2. The implementation of scholarship programs and/or educational assistance as referred to in paragraph (1) is supported by the participation of the community and community organizations.

#### Article 28

The Government is obliged to protect children in the school environment from acts of violence committed by teachers, school administrators or schoolmates.

#### Article 29

1. The regional government is obliged to develop a child-friendly school model.  
2. Child-friendly schools as referred to in paragraph (1) constitute a joint effort of the Regional Government with school administrators to create a safe and comfortable environment for children at school.  
3. Children from poor families and/or persons with disabilities have the right to equal and fair treatment to obtain services and guidance at school.

#### Article 30

1. Education organizers equip the schools they lead with gender-responsive facilities.  
2. Gender-responsive facilities as referred to in paragraph (1) include:a. toilets/WCs for men and women are separated and between teachers and students;  
b. teacher desks and student desks are equipped with covers.  
3. Implementation of the provisions as referred to in paragraph (1) and paragraph (2) is carried out in accordance with regional financial capacity.

### Paragraph 3

Employment

#### Article 31

1. Every person is prohibited from employing and involving children in the worst forms of work.  
2. The worst forms of work as referred to in paragraph (1) include:a. work in the form of slavery or the like;  
b. work that exploits, provides or offers children for prostitution, pornography production, pornographic performances or gambling.  c. work that exploits, provides, or involves children for the production and trade of alcoholic beverages, narcotics, psychotropics, and other addictive substances; and/ord. work that endangers the health, safety and morals of children.

### Paragraph 4

Social

#### Article 32

1. The Regional Government organizes care for neglected children.  
2. Care as referred to in paragraph (1) may be carried out by Community Organizations in the social field.

#### Article 33

1. The Regional Government organizes skills training for street children and neglected children.  
2. Skills training as referred to in paragraph (1) is organized by Regional Apparatus in accordance with their respective duties and functions.

#### Article 34

1. The Regional Government may provide shelters to foster street children and social institutions for neglected children.  
2. Shelters as referred to in paragraph (1) may be organized in cooperation with community organizations.

#### Article 35

The implementation of protection for women and children is carried out by cross-sectoral Regional Apparatus whose duties and functions are related to the protection of women and children.

# CHAPTER VII

## SERVICES FOR WOMEN AND CHILDREN VICTIMS OF VIOLENCE

## Part One

General

#### Article 36

Forms of services provided to women and children victims of violence are as follows:
a. complaint services;  
b. health services;  
c. social rehabilitation;  
d. legal assistance, ande. repatriation and social reintegration.

## Part Two Complaint Services

#### Article 37

1. The implementation of complaint services as referred to in Article 36 letter a is carried out through integrated services.  
2. Integrated services for women and children victims of violence use the principles of:a. fast, responsive, safe, empathetic;  
b. gender responsive;  
c. non-discriminatory;  
d. easily accessible;  
e. guarantee of confidentiality, andf. without charging fees from victims.

#### Article 38

1. Complaints of acts of violence against women and children may be made by reporters consisting of victims, families, the community and/or community organizations in the field of protection of women and children.  
2. Reporters as referred to in paragraph (1) may make complaints;  
a. directly;  
b. by telephone/short message service; orc. by letter/electronic mail.

#### Article 39

Complaint handling services for women and children victims of violence are carried out by:
a. Hospitals;  
b. Community Health Centers;

##### P2TP2A,
d. Police;  
e. Office of Religious Affairs;  
f. Legal Aid Institutions;  
g. Child Protection Institutions;  
h. Family Welfare Consultation Institutions;  
i. PPA Task Force

#### Article 40

Provisions regarding the complaint procedure as referred to in Article 38 shall be regulated by Regent Regulation

## Part Three

Health Services

#### Article 41

1. Health services as referred to in Article 36 letter b include promotive, preventive, curative and rehabilitative aspects.  
2. Health services for women and children victims of violence are carried out comprehensively, accessible 24 (twenty-four) hours, of quality and carried out in accordance with health service standards.  
3. Health services specifically for child victims of violence must prioritize the best interests of the child.  
4. Women and children victims of violence have the right to health services, including:a. medical services:b. medicolegal services; andc. psychosocial counseling.  
5. Comprehensive health service efforts as referred to in paragraph (4) intended for victims of violence, abduction, neglect, economically and sexually exploited and trafficking of women and children are guaranteed by the Regional Government.  
6. Comprehensive health service efforts as referred to in paragraph (1) are carried out in stages adjusted to regional financial capacity.

## Part Four

Social Rehabilitation

#### Article 42

1. Social Rehabilitation Services for Women and Children Victims of Violence are aimed at restoring and developing the abilities of victims who experience social dysfunction to be able to carry out their social functions in society properly.  
2. Social Rehabilitation Services as referred to in paragraph (1) include counseling services and spiritual guidance.  
3. The implementation of services as referred to in paragraph (2) is carried out by:a. Regional Apparatus having duties and functions in the social field; andb. service handling institutions.

#### Article 43

1. Counseling services as referred to in Article 42 paragraph (2) aim to:a. help victims understand problems and find effective ways to overcome them themselves;  
b. empower victims to determine their future;  
c. provide moral support for victims in facing the process they are undergoing; andd. make victims feel accepted in the community environment.  
2. Counseling services are carried out by gender-responsive social rehabilitation officers, consisting of:a. Counselors;  
b. Psychologists; andc. Psychiatrists.  
3. Counseling services are carried out in a place that guarantees a sense of security, comfort and confidentiality of information from victims.  
4. Stages of counseling services include:a. initial counseling; andb. follow-up counseling.

#### Article 44

1. The Regional Government is responsible for facilitating the availability of safe houses for women and children victims of violence.  
2. Safe houses aim to protect women and children victims of violence from intimidation by perpetrators and other parties.  
3. The location of safe houses as referred to in paragraph (1) must be kept confidential.  
4. Criteria for women and children victims of violence who require safe houses include:a. their lives are threatened;  
b. receiving rejection from family or community;  
c. requiring intensive services but their residence is relatively far away; and/ord. abandoned if not placed in a safe house.

#### Article 45

1. Women and children victims of violence are provided with spiritual guidance aimed at psychological recovery.  
2. Spiritual guidance as referred to in paragraph (1) is carried out based on the principles of:a. not imposing any religion or belief; andb. leaving the final decision in the hands of the victim/self determination (3) Spiritual guidance is carried out by spiritual guidance officers (4) Spiritual guidance officers as referred to in paragraph (3) consist of Consultants of the Advisory, Development and Preservation of Marriage Agency (BP4), Officers of the Office of Religious Affairs (KUA), Ustads/Ulama, Priests, Pastors and other gender-responsive religious leaders.

## Part Five

Legal Aid

#### Article 46

1. Legal aid as referred to in Article 36 letter d is aimed at providing assistance and protection to women and children victims of violence and witnesses with a gender-responsive approach so that victims obtain justice and certainty of their legal rights.  
2. Types of legal aid services include:a. legal aid in the reporting process and preparation of Investigation Report;  
b. legal aid in the prosecution process at the Prosecutor's Office;  
c. legal aid in the examination process at the Court;  
d. legal aid in the court decision process until there is a decision with permanent legal force;  
e. legal aid in the mediation process;  
f. legal aid in the Diversion process.  
3. The Regional Government provides legal aid to children and women victims of violence.  
4. In providing legal aid as referred to in paragraph (3) the Regional Government may cooperate with Legal Aid Institutions, Advocates and/or other gender-responsive legal assistants.

## Part Six

Repatriation and Social Reintegration Services

#### Article 47

1. Repatriation as referred to in Article 36 letter e is aimed at returning women and children victims of violence to their area of origin.  
2. Repatriation of women and children victims of violence is accompanied by social reintegration efforts.  
3. The Regional Government carries out repatriation and social reintegration of women and children victims of violence in accordance with laws and regulations.

# CHAPTER VIII

## INSTITUTIONS

#### Article 48

1. The implementation of Protection of Women and Children is carried out by the Regional Government.  
2. The implementation of Protection of Women and Children by the Regional Government as referred to in paragraph (1) is carried out by:a. P2TP2A, UPTb. LK3; andc. PPA Task Force.

### P2TP2A UPT PPA

#### Article 49

3. The formation of P2TP2A units as referred to in paragraph (2) at the sub-district level is stipulated by the Sub-district Head.

## Part Two

#### Article 50

1. LK3 services as referred to in Article 48 paragraph (2) letter b, constitute services to families experiencing psychosocial problems.  
2. Types of LK3 services as referred to in paragraph (1), consist of:a. consultation;  
b. counseling;  
c. information;  
d. advocacy;  
e. referral; andf. outreach.  
3. The membership composition of LK3 is stipulated by Regent Decree.

#### Article 51

4. The Regional Government facilitates the establishment of Children's Forums at the regency, sub-district, and village/urban village levels.  
5. The Children's Forum as referred to in paragraph (1) represents children in the Region, both representing the geographical domicile of children, components of children's socio-cultural groups, and children's educational backgrounds.

7. The establishment of the Children's Forum as referred to in paragraph (1) at the regency level is stipulated by Regent's Decree.  
8. The establishment of the Children's Forum as referred to in paragraph (1) at the sub-district level is stipulated by the Sub-district Head.  
9. The establishment of the Children's Forum as referred to in paragraph (1) at the Village level is stipulated by Village Head's Decree and at the Urban Village level is stipulated by the Urban Village Head.

## Part Three

PPA Task Force

#### Article 52

1. In order to ensure the effectiveness of the implementation of Women and Children Protection, the Regional Government establishes a Task Force.  
3. The Task Force as referred to in paragraph (1) has the duty to assist the Regional Apparatus that handles women's empowerment and child protection in addressing women's and children's issues.  
4. In carrying out the duties as referred to in paragraph (1), the task force has the following functions:a. conducting outreach to women and children experiencing problems;  
b. identifying the conditions and services needed by women and children experiencing problems;  
c. protecting women and children at the incident location from things that may endanger them;  
d. placing and evacuating women and children experiencing problems;  
e. making recommendations to women's and children's service organizations established by the region to obtain further services.  
5. The task force as referred to in paragraph (1) is under the coordination of the Regional Apparatus that administers affairs in the field of women's empowerment and child protection.  
6. The establishment of the Task Force is stipulated by Regent's Decree.  
7. Further provisions regarding organizational structure, procedures for establishment and work mechanisms are regulated by Regent Regulation.

# CHAPTER IX COOPERATION AND PARTNERSHIP

## Part One

Cooperation

#### Article 53

1. In order to achieve the objectives of Women and Children Protection, cooperation may be carried out with:a. the central government;  
b. the provincial government;  
c. other regency/city governments; andd. non-governmental institutions.  
2. Cooperation as referred to in paragraph (1) includes:a. delivery of data and information;  
b. rehabilitation of victims of violence;  
c. repatriation and social reintegration; andd. provision of evidence and witnesses.

## Part Two

Partnership

#### Article 54

1. The Regional Government may establish partnerships with the business sector in protecting women and children from acts of violence.  
2. Partnership as referred to in paragraph (1) is carried out through:a. an employment opportunity information system for women victims of violence;  
b. education and training for women victims of violence;  
c. educational assistance for women and children victims of violence who have been withdrawn from their education; andd. fostering and increasing the economic independence of women victims of violence;  
3. Partnership as referred to in paragraph (1) and paragraph (2), is set forth in the form of a Cooperation Agreement.

# CHAPTER X GUIDANCE AND SUPERVISION

#### Article 55

1. The Regional Government conducts guidance and supervision of the implementation of Women and Children Protection carried out by the community and community institutions.  
2. Guidance as referred to in paragraph (1), includes:a. guidelines and fulfillment standards;  
b. technical guidance and training;  
c. provision of facilities;  
d. monitoring;  
e. evaluation; andf. reporting (3) Supervision as referred to in paragraph (1), is carried out with the principles of:a. professional;  
b. transparent; andc. accountable.

# CHAPTER XI

## COORDINATION AND EVALUATION

## Part One

Coordination

#### Article 56

1. Regional Apparatus coordination includes:a. health service referrals to victims;  
b. legal aid;  
c. repatriation of victims of trafficking in women and children;  
d. social reintegration; ande. development of forms of Women and Children Protection.  
2. Development of forms of protection as referred to in paragraph (1) letter e includes:a. mediation process for Domestic Violence cases;  
b. assistance for people with HIV/AIDS (ODHA) and people living with HIV/AIDS (OHIDHA);  
c. assistance for victims of human trafficking;  
d. children victims of sexual exploitation or other exploitation; ande. other issues directly related to efforts to protect women and children.

#### Article 57

The Regional Apparatus in charge of planning affairs coordinates programs for the protection of women and children from violence, exploitation and discrimination with related Regional Apparatus.

## Part Two

Evaluation

#### Article 58

1. Evaluation of the implementation of policies, programs and activities for Women and Children Protection is carried out at the end of each fiscal year or if necessary according to needs.  
2. The results of the evaluation of the implementation of policies, programs and activities for the protection of Women and Children are used as input for the formulation of policies, programs and activities for the protection of Women and Children victims of violence for the following year.

# CHAPTER XII

## COMMUNITY AND PRIVATE SECTOR PARTICIPATION

#### Article 59

1. The community and private sector participate in assisting efforts to protect women and children from all forms of violence, exploitation and discrimination.  
2. Forms of community and private sector participation as referred to in paragraph (1) may include:a. establishing family partners at the Urban Village/Village level by the community;  
b. conducting independent socialization of women's and children's rights;  
c. reporting or providing information on incidents of violence, exploitation and discrimination experienced by women and children;  
d. providing safe houses and shelters;  
e. providing legal aid for children in conflict with the law;  
f. providing educational scholarships;  
g. providing playgrounds for children;  
h. conducting supervision of the implementation of women's and children's protection; andi. other participation relevant to child protection efforts.

# CHAPTER XIII FINANCING

#### Article 60

Financing for the implementation of Women and Children Protection is sourced from:
a. regional revenue and expenditure budget; andb. other legitimate sources in accordance with the provisions of laws and regulations.

#### Article 61

1. The Regional Government may provide financial assistance to community organizations, social organizations or non-governmental organizations that implement the Protection of Women and Children from acts of violence.  
2. Financial assistance as referred to in paragraph (1), is in accordance with regional financial capacity, and is implemented in accordance with the provisions of laws and regulations.

# CHAPTER XIV ADMINISTRATIVE SANCTIONS

#### Article 62

1. Any person or Institution that carries out the task of providing complaint handling services for victims of violence against women and children who violates the service principles as referred to in Article 37 paragraph (2) shall be subject to administrative sanctions.  
2. Administrative sanctions as referred to in paragraph (1) are in the form of:a. verbal warning;  
b. written warning;  
c. temporary license revocation; ord. license revocation.  
3. Temporary license revocation as referred to in paragraph (2) letter c is carried out after 3 (three) consecutive written warnings with a grace period of 7 (seven) working days.  
4. License revocation as referred to in paragraph (2) letter c, is carried out after temporary revocation with a grace period of 1 (one) month.

#### Article 63

Heads of educational units who violate the provisions as referred to in Article 24 paragraph (4) shall be subject to employment sanctions in accordance with the provisions of laws and regulations.

# CHAPTER XV

## TRANSITIONAL PROVISIONS

#### Article 64

At the time this Regional Regulation comes into force, the existing P2TP2A shall continue to carry out its duties and functions of integrated services for women and children until the P2TP2A is established based on this Regional Regulation.
