# CHAPTER I

## GENERAL PROVISIONS

#### Article 1

In this Regional Regulation, the following terms shall mean:

1. Region is Sarolangun Regency.  
2. Regional Government is the administration of governmental affairs by the Regional Government and the Regional House of Representatives according to the principles of autonomy and assistance tasks with the principle of the widest possible autonomy within the system and principles of the Unitary State of the Republic of Indonesia as referred to in the 1945 Constitution of the Republic of Indonesia.  
3. Regional Government is the Regent as an element of the Regional Government administrator who leads the implementation of governmental affairs that are the authority of the autonomous region.  
4. Regent is the Regent of Sarolangun.  
5. Regional House of Representatives, hereinafter abbreviated as DPRD, is a regional people's representative institution that is positioned as an element of the Regional Government administrator.  
6. Women's Empowerment is every effort to improve physical, mental, spiritual, social capacity, knowledge, and skills so that women are ready to be utilized according to their respective abilities.  
7. Women's Protection is all efforts aimed at protecting women and providing a sense of security in the fulfillment of their rights by providing consistent and systematic attention aimed at achieving gender equality.  
8. Discrimination Against Women is any distinction, exclusion or restriction made on the basis of sex which has the effect or purpose of impairing or nullifying the recognition, enjoyment or exercise of human rights and fundamental freedoms in the political, economic, social, cultural, civil or any other field by women, regardless of their marital status, on the basis of equality between men and women. 9. Gender is a concept that refers to the differentiation of roles and responsibilities of men and women that occur as a result of social and cultural conditions of society.  
10. Gender Equality is the equality of conditions for men and women to obtain opportunities and their rights as human beings so that they are able to play a role and participate in political, economic, social, cultural, defense and security activities, and equality in enjoying the results of development.  
11. Gender mainstreaming is a strategy built to integrate gender into an integral dimension of planning, formulation, implementation, monitoring, and evaluation of national development program policies.  
12. Child is a person who has not reached the age of 18 (eighteen) years, including a child still in the womb.  
13. Parents are biological father and/or mother, or stepfather and/or stepmother, or adoptive father and/or mother.  
14. Guardian is a person or body who in fact exercises parental authority as a parent over a child.  
15. Family is the smallest unit in society consisting of husband, wife and their children, or father and his children, or mother and her children, or blood relatives in a straight line upward or downward up to the third degree.  
16. Community is individuals, families, communities, religious leaders, community leaders, traditional leaders, business world and corporations, community organizations, social organizations, women's and gender study centers, mass media or similar others.  
17. Child Protection is all activities to guarantee and protect children and their rights so that they can live, grow and develop and participate optimally in accordance with human dignity and receive protection from violence, exploitation, discrimination, and neglect.  
18. Special protection is protection given to children in emergency situations, children in conflict with the law, children from minority and isolated groups, children who are exploited economically and/or sexually, children who are trafficked, children who are victims of narcotics, alcohol, psychotropic, and other addictive substance abuse (drugs), children who are victims of kidnapping, sale, trafficking, children who are victims of violence both physical and/or mental, children with disabilities, and children who are victims of mistreatment and neglect.  
19. Safe house is a shelter or temporary protection house for victims during the assistance process to ensure the safety and comfort of victims from threats and dangers of perpetrators and is only known by interested parties for reasons of victim safety and security.  
20. Child-friendly regency is a regency that has a child rights-based development system through the integration of commitments and resources of government, community, business world, and media that are planned comprehensively and sustainably in policies, programs and activities to ensure the fulfillment of children's rights and special protection for children.

#### Article 2

The Empowerment and Protection of Women and Children is organized based on the principles of:
a. respect for human rights;  
b. gender equality;  
c. non-discrimination;  
d. justice and legal certainty;  
e. benefit;  
f. participatory;  
g. best interests of the child;  
h. accountability; andi. gender responsive.

#### Article 3

The regulation regarding the Empowerment and Protection of Women and Children aims to:
a. guarantee the fulfillment of the rights of every woman and child to survival, growth and development;  
b. improve the quality of life of women, children and family quality; c. increase the institutional capacity of women's empowerment and child protection in the region, including the development of gender and child data systems; andd. provide protection of women's rights and fulfillment of children's rights including special protection for children from various forms of violence and other discriminatory treatment.

#### Article 4

The scope of regulation of the Empowerment and Protection of Women and Children includes:
a. fulfillment of women's rights;  
b. women's empowerment;  
c. women's protection;  
d. child protection;  
e. fulfillment of children's rights;  
f. safe house;  
g. child-friendly regency;  
h. guidance and supervision;  
i. community participation;  
j. awards; andk. financing.

# CHAPTER II

## WOMEN'S PROTECTION

## Part One

Fulfillment of Women's Rights

#### Article 5

1. Every woman has the right to:a. live and maintain life and improve her standard of living;  
b. fulfill her basic needs to grow and develop properly, have a family and continue offspring;  
c. develop her personality, to obtain education, and improve her quality of life to become a faithful, pious, responsible, noble, happy, and prosperous human being in accordance with human rights;  
d. obtain justice, security, and freedom to express opinions without discrimination;  
e. be involved in every stage of the development process;  
f. be free from slavery or enslavement and threats;  
g. obtain protection of her person, family, honor, dignity and property rights;  
h. obtain welfare and a decent life;  
i. participate in politics and obtain equal opportunities in government;  
j. perform legal acts; andk. freely choose a partner in her marriage.  
2. In addition to the rights as referred to in paragraph (1), specifically for women with disabilities, they have the right to obtain facilities and special treatment in public services.

#### Article 6

1. Every wife during the marriage bond has the same rights and responsibilities as her husband over all matters relating to their married life, relationship with their children, and ownership and management of joint property in accordance with the applicable laws and regulations.  
2. After the dissolution of marriage, a woman has the same rights and responsibilities with her former husband over all matters relating to children with due regard to the best interests of the child.  
3. After the dissolution of marriage, a woman has the same rights as her former husband over all matters relating to joint property without prejudice to the rights of the child, in accordance with the provisions of laws and regulations.

#### Article 7

1. Regional Government has the obligation and responsibility for the empowerment and protection of women.  
2. The efforts for empowerment and protection of women as referred to in paragraph (1) include:a. determination of policies, programs, and activities for improving the quality of life of women related to the fields of development, education, health, economy, law, Human Rights and politics, environment and socio-culture;  
b. provision of services to women especially to victims of violence, female workers, elderly women, and women with disabilities, victims of conflict and victims affected by disasters; andc. implementation of gender mainstreaming in government institutions, women's study centers, research and development institutions, non-governmental institutions.  
3. The efforts for empowerment and protection of women as referred to in paragraph (2) shall be carried out in an integrated manner in accordance with the applicable laws and regulations.  
4. The empowerment and protection of women shall be coordinated by the Regional Apparatus that administers regional government affairs in the field of women's empowerment and child protection.

## Part Two

Women's Empowerment

#### Article 8

Women's empowerment is directed to obtain opportunities and rights as human beings to be able to play a role and participate in the fields of: a. economy;  
b. socio-culture;  
c. politics; andd. law.

#### Article 9

Women's empowerment in the economic field as referred to in Article 8 letter a shall be implemented through:
a. provision of skills and job training;  
b. facilitation of the formation of productive economic business groups; c. facilitation of strengthening and development of productive economic business groups; d. facilitation and capital assistance; ande. facilitation of marketing network development.

#### Article 10

Women's empowerment in the socio-cultural field as referred to in Article 8 letter b shall be implemented through:
a. improvement of knowledge, attitudes and skills to encourage the fulfillment of tiered education in accordance with potential to improve social status;  
b. improvement of awareness, willingness and ability to overcome health problems through quality promotional, preventive, curative, and rehabilitative efforts, especially in the field of reproductive health;  
c. improvement of awareness and knowledge about independent, healthy and prosperous family planning including access to consultation services and marriage registration; andd. facilitation and efforts to preserve customs and develop science, technology, arts and culture for the advancement of women.

#### Article 11

1. Women's empowerment in the political field as referred to in Article 8 letter c includes:a. involvement of women in decision-making at various levels; b. provision of opportunities for women to hold public office;  
c. participation in general elections; andd. self-development through organizations to associate, assemble and express opinions.  
2. Women's empowerment in the political field as referred to in paragraph (1) shall be implemented in accordance with the applicable laws and regulations.

#### Article 12

1. Women's empowerment in the legal field as referred to in Article 8 letter d includes:a. improvement of awareness and knowledge in the legal field through communication, information and education services; andb. facilitation of access and legal consultation services.  
2. Women's empowerment in the legal field as referred to in paragraph (1) shall be implemented in accordance with the applicable laws and regulations.

## Part Three

Protection of Women

#### Article 13

1. Protection of women includes:a. social protection;  
b. economic protection; andc. legal protection.  
2. Social protection as referred to in paragraph (1) letter a is to ensure the survival of women in the form of social protection in order to ensure the fulfillment of a decent living.  
3. Economic protection as referred to in paragraph (1) letter b is to prevent and address the risk of vulnerability due to shocks and social vulnerability of a person, family, group and/or community for the survival of women.  
4. Legal protection as referred to in paragraph (1) letter c shall be implemented in terms of providing protection to women who become witnesses and victims of acts of violence and discrimination.

# CHAPTER III

## CHILD PROTECTION

## Part One

General

#### Article 14

Every Child has basic rights which include:
a. civil rights and freedoms;  
b. family environment and alternative care;  
c. basic health and welfare;  
d. education, leisure time utilization and cultural activities; and e. special protection.

## Part Two

Civil Rights and Freedoms

#### Article 15

Every Child has the right to obtain Civil Rights and Freedoms as referred to in Article 14 letter a in the form of:
a. birth certificate;  
b. child identity card.  c. expressing opinions in accordance with their age and level of intelligence;  
d. obtaining healthy and safe information;  
e. freedom to assemble and organize that is appropriate for them; f. protection of reputation and not being exploited to the public without the permission of the child; and/org. participating in development through children's forums at the village/sub-district, sub-district and even Regency levels.

#### Article 16

In fulfilling the Civil Rights and Freedoms of Children, the Family:
a. takes care of the child's birth certificate after the child is born;  
b. endeavors to obtain a child identity card;  
c. provides space to assemble and organize and listens to the child to express their opinion;  
d. supervises the child in accessing various information and provides healthy and safe information; ande. protects the child's private life and does not expose without the child's permission.

#### Article 17

The Government provides facilities for the fulfillment of Civil Rights and Freedoms of Children, by:
a. organizing fast and easy services in making birth certificates;  
b. involving children through children's forums at the village/sub-district, sub-district and city levels in development planning deliberations or other similar forums;  
c. providing a children's call center as a means of interactive communication or complaints related to the interests of children;  
d. providing healthy and safe information facilities by supervising internet service providers;  
e. providing child identity cards; andf. providing reading rooms or reading corners in public spaces.

## Part Three

Rights in Family Environment and Alternative Care

#### Article 18

Every Child has the right to obtain rights in the family environment and alternative care as referred to in Article 14 letter b in the form of:
a. obtaining priority to be raised by their own parents;  
b. not being separated from their parents, except such separation is in the best interests of the child;  
c. obtaining good, polite and loving parenting; d. obtaining balanced parenting from both parents; e. obtaining welfare support even though their parents are unable;  
f. obtaining alternative care in the event that both parents have died or suffer from an illness that makes it impossible to care for the child; andg. obtaining family harmony.

#### Article 19

In fulfilling rights within the family environment and alternative care, the Family:
a. provides parenting patterns, affection, attention, protection, facilities, maintains family harmony, always considering what is best for the child, according to the conditions and capabilities of the parents;  
b. prevents marriage at child age and keeps children from being trapped in promiscuity, permissive culture, and avoids NAPZA, HIV and AIDS and is protected from pornography;  
c. provides religious education and instills noble values from an early age to children;  
d. provides national insight, heroism and state defense from an early age to children; ande. provides welfare support.

#### Article 20

The Regional Government provides facilities for the fulfillment of children's rights in the family environment and alternative care environment, by: a. providing or facilitating alternative care for children whose parents have died or who are sick so that it is not possible to care for the child;  
b. providing welfare support to all children; c. fulfilling the rights of child development and protecting children with social welfare problems and children in conflict with the law; d. protecting children from cruel, inhuman and degrading treatment;  
e. conducting training for parents on good parenting patterns or establishing consultation institutions for families;  
f. playing an active role in assisting families in carrying out their obligations;  
g. preventing marriage at child age; and h. providing child-friendly infrastructure, child-friendly play spaces and safe school routes.

## Part Four

Rights to Basic Health and Welfare

#### Article 21

Every Child has the right to obtain basic health and welfare rights, as referred to in Article 14 letter c, in the form of: a. not to be aborted unless endangering the safety of the mother; b. good nutrition from the womb;  
c. breast milk until the age of two Years;  
d. complete basic immunization;  
e. regular health examinations for toddlers;  
f. smoke-free environment;  
g. availability of clean water;  
h. access to social security; andi. protection and rehabilitation from NAPZA, HIV and AIDS.

#### Article 22

In fulfilling children's basic health and welfare rights, the family: a. provides good nutrition from the womb;  
b. provides breast milk until the age of two Years;  
c. provides complete basic immunization;  
d. conducts regular health examinations for toddlers; e. frees children from cigarette smoke;  
f. fulfills the need for clean water;  
g. seeks social security; andh. prevents children from NAPZA, HIV and AIDS.

#### Article 23

The Regional Government provides facilities to fulfill basic health and welfare rights, by:
a. provision of child-friendly health centers;  
b. provision of breastfeeding rooms in Government and private Offices; c. organizing and facilitating Posyandu facilities and infrastructure in every banjar dinas/neighborhood;  
d. provision of clean water;  
e. arrangement of green open spaces and a clean, healthy and comfortable environment;  
f. provision of smoke-free public spaces; andg. facilities aimed at reducing maternal mortality and child mortality rates and increasing life expectancy, nutritional standards and health standards.

## Part Five

Rights to Education, Leisure Time and Cultural Activities

#### Article 24

Every Child has the right to obtain rights to education, leisure time and cultural activities, as referred to in Article 14 letter d, in the form of:
a. participating in early childhood education;  
b. obtaining the widest possible opportunity to obtain education; c. the right to develop talents, interests and creative abilities; d. recreation; ande. having leisure time to rest and engage in various arts, cultural and sports activities.

#### Article 25

In fulfilling the rights to education, leisure time and cultural activities, the family:
a. ensures the continuity of children's education from an early age;  
b. provides the widest possible opportunity for children to obtain education and builds communication related to teaching and learning activities at school with the school;  
c. directs and provides opportunities for children to develop interests, talents and creativity;  
d. provides leisure time to rest and engage in various arts, cultural and sports activities; ande. takes time to recreate with children according to the situation and condition of the parents.

#### Article 26

The Regional Government provides facilities to fulfill the Rights to Education, Leisure Time and Cultural Activities, by:
a. providing the widest possible opportunity for children to obtain education by ensuring the implementation of a 12 (twelve) year compulsory education program for all children free of charge;  
b. providing opportunities and accessibility for children with disabilities to obtain education;  
c. organizing inclusive education for children with disabilities; d. optimizing skills education;  
e. providing city parks, playgrounds, art buildings and sports arenas as a forum to hone and develop children's talents, interests and creativity in the fields of arts, culture and sports;  
f. realizing child-friendly schools at every level of education; g. providing scholarships for students from underprivileged families;  
h. facilitating school dropouts at Open Schools or Community Learning Activity Centers;  
i. giving awards to students who excel in academic, arts, culture and sports fields;  
j. issuing policies for education providers not to expel students from educational institutions, by conducting guidance, unless involved in criminal acts;  
k. organizing student orientation periods that are educational and without violence; andl. establishing child creativity centers.

## Part Six

Special Protection

#### Article 27

1. The Regional Government is responsible for realizing Child Protection in the Region in the form of coordinating the implementation of Child Protection in the Region.  
2. Coordination of the implementation of Child Protection in the Region as referred to in paragraph (1) is in order to fulfill the Rights of Children who require Special Protection.  
3. Children who require Special Protection as referred to in paragraph (1) include:a. children in emergency situations;  
b. children in conflict with the law;  
c. children from minority and isolated groups;  
d. children who are exploited economically and/or sexually;  
e. children who are victims of narcotics, alcohol, psychotropic, and other addictive substance abuse;  
f. children who are victims of pornography;  
g. children who are perpetrators of pornography;  
h. children with human immunodeficiency virus/ acquired immuno deficiency syndrome;  
i. children victims of kidnapping, sale, and/or trafficking;  
j. children victims of physical and/or psychological violence;  
k. children victims of sexual crimes;  
l. children victims of terrorism networks;  
m. children perpetrators of terrorism networks:n. children with disabilities;  
o. children victims of abuse and neglect;  
p. children with deviant social behavior; andq. children who are victims of stigmatization from labeling related to their parents' condition.

#### Article 28

Every family carries out its functions and responsibilities in terms of optimal fulfillment of children's rights so that children can avoid special conditions as referred to in Article 27 paragraph (3).

#### Article 29

The Regional Government seeks to fulfill Special Protection Rights for children by:
a. preparing programs to prevent children from being involved in situations and conditions as referred to in Article 27 paragraph (3);  
b. providing access to public services and social security for children with disabilities; andc. providing 24 (twenty-four) hour complaint service facilities, Shelters, rehabilitation centers and orphanages.

# CHAPTER IV

## SAFE HOUSE

#### Article 30

1. Women and child victims of violence who require continuous handling have the right to stay in a safe house.  
2. The safe house as referred to in paragraph (1) provides a place or temporary safe shelter for victims of violence until their rights are restored.  
3. The operation and management of the safe house as referred to in paragraph (1) shall be carried out by regional apparatus that performs functions in the field of women and child empowerment.  
4. Further provisions regarding the operation of the safe house as referred to in paragraph (1) shall be regulated by Regent Regulation.

# CHAPTER V

## CHILD-FRIENDLY REGENCY

#### Article 31

1. In the framework of implementing the fulfillment of child-appropriate rights and various laws and regulations as well as policies related to the fulfillment of children's rights and special protection of children, the Regional Government, Private Sector and Community Institutions shall make efforts to realize a Child-Friendly Regency.  
2. To realize a Child-Friendly Regency as referred to in paragraph (1), the Regional Government, Private Sector, and Community Institutions shall pay attention to children's rights related to the right to life, growth and development, protection, and participation.  
3. The Regional Government, Private Sector, and Community Institutions in realizing a Child-Friendly Regency are incorporated in the Child-Friendly Regency task force. (4) The Child-Friendly Regency task force as referred to in paragraph (3) is responsible for the fulfillment of children's rights related to: a. civil rights and freedoms;  
b. family environment and alternative care;  
c. basic health and welfare;  
d. education, use of leisure time, and cultural arts activities; and e. special protection of children.  
5. Further provisions regarding the Child-Friendly Regency as referred to in paragraph (1) shall be regulated by Regent Regulation.

# CHAPTER VI

## GUIDANCE AND SUPERVISION

#### Article 32

1. The Regent shall carry out guidance and supervision of the implementation of women empowerment and child protection in the Region. (2) The guidance as referred to in paragraph (1) shall be carried out by the Regional Apparatus in charge of women empowerment and child protection affairs.  
3. The Regional Apparatus in charge of women empowerment and child protection affairs as referred to in paragraph (2) shall be the coordinator for the implementation of women empowerment and child protection in the Region.

#### Article 33

1. The form of guidance as referred to in Article 32 may be carried out through activities:a. providing socialization to the community and business sector regarding women empowerment and child protection policies; b. providing books, leaflets, brochures regarding women empowerment and child protection, and other related issues and disseminating them to the community;  
c. facilitating capacity building of women empowerment and child protection institutions;  
d. facilitating forums for women's participation and women's and children's organizations;  
e. facilitating rehabilitation and reintegration services for women and children victims of discrimination, violence and disasters;  
f. coordinating the participation of the business sector and other institutions, including international assistance for women empowerment and child protection; andg. providing awards to the community and business sector both individually and institutionally who have made efforts to empower women and protect children.  
2. The form of supervision as referred to in Article 32 may be in the form of monitoring and evaluation activities on the implementation of policies, programs and activities for women empowerment and child protection.

# CHAPTER VII

## COMMUNITY PARTICIPATION

#### Article 34

1. The Regional Government has an obligation to strengthen community participation in the development of women empowerment and Child Protection.  
2. Community participation as referred to in paragraph (1) may be carried out in the form of:a. socialization;  
b. advocacy;  
c. counseling;  
d. guidance;  
e. supervision;  
f. facilitation;  
g. services;  
h. studies;  
i. training;  
j. capital; andk. provision of infrastructure.  
3. Strengthening community participation as referred to in paragraph (1) may be carried out through:a. strengthening cooperation;  
b. strengthening institutional capacity for prevention and services from the community, Islamic boarding schools and the business sector; andc. financing support.

#### Article 35

1. The business sector has responsibility in the implementation of women empowerment and child protection.  
2. The form of business sector responsibility as referred to in paragraph (4) shall be carried out in the form of:a. financial assistance to service institutions;  
b. empowerment;  
c. capacity building;  
d. publication and information media;  
e. provision of infrastructure;  
f. establishment of special institutions for prevention and violence complaint services; andg. non-binding social assistance.

# CHAPTER VIII

## AWARDS

#### Article 36

The Regent may grant awards to educational institutions, religious institutions, health service provider institutions, professional institutions and the business sector, service institutions, Islamic boarding schools, youth organizations, legal aid organizations, communities, media that have contributed to the implementation of women empowerment and child protection.

# CHAPTER IX

## FINANCING

#### Article 37

1. Financing for the implementation of women empowerment and child protection shall be sourced from the Regional Revenue and Expenditure Budget. (2) In addition to being sourced from the Regional Revenue and Expenditure Budget, financing for the implementation of women empowerment and child protection may be sourced from other legitimate and non-binding sources of financing.

# CHAPTER X

## CLOSING PROVISIONS

#### Article 38

This Regional Regulation shall come into force on the date of promulgation. In order that everyone may know of it, the promulgation of this Regional Regulation is ordered by placing it in the Regional Gazette of Sarolangun Regency.

### A. GENERAL EXPLANATION

Pancasila and the 1945 Constitution of the Republic of Indonesia have firmly and clearly provided protection for Human Rights, including the rights of women and children. Every person has equal dignity and worth not only guaranteed by the 1945 Constitution of the Republic of Indonesia, but also internationally recognized through the Universal Declaration of Human Rights (UDHR) of 1948 which explicitly regulates the importance of protection of Human Rights including the protection of women and children.

To guarantee the fulfillment of the rights of women and children, regional governments have the obligation and responsibility to implement and support national policies in the administration of governmental affairs in the field of empowerment and protection of women and children in the regions. In addition, regional governments are also given the obligation to supervise the administration of empowerment and protection of women and children.

The administration of empowerment and protection of women and children is an effort to realize capable women as well as an effort to protect women and children from all acts of violence in order to achieve the goal of welfare. The empowerment and protection of women and children that has been implemented so far has not been optimal and comprehensive in reaching the problems faced by women and children. In addition, public awareness of efforts to empower and protect women and children is still very lacking. To achieve these objectives and to implement the authority of regional governments as outlined in Law Number 23 of 2014 concerning Regional Government in the sub-affairs of empowerment and protection of women and children, a regulation is needed that can serve as a legal umbrella for regional governments in the administration of governmental affairs in the field of women's empowerment and child protection in the regions.

In formulating policies related to women's empowerment and child protection, it is necessary to pay attention to the principles of good legislation formation as regulated in Law Number 12 of 2011 concerning the Formation of Legislation, including:
r. clarity of purpose;
s. appropriate institutions or forming officials;
t. conformity between type, hierarchy, and material content;
u. implementability;
v. effectiveness and efficiency;
w. clarity of formulation; andx. transparency.

In addition to the principles of legislation formation, it is also necessary to pay attention to the principles of material content which include:
a. protection;
b. humanity;
c. nationality;
d. kinship;
e. archipelago;
f. unity in diversity;
g. justice;
h. equality of position in law and government; i. order and legal certainty; and/orj. balance, harmony, and congruence.

The administration of governmental affairs in the field of Women's Empowerment and Child Protection aims to increase efforts to protect women's rights, fulfill children's rights and provide Special Protection for Children and increase the role of Regional Governments and the business world, media and society in efforts to empower women and protect children. The principal matters contained in the body of the Regional Regulation concerning the Administration of Empowerment and Protection of Women and Children include:
a. fulfillment of women's rights;
b. women's empowerment;
c. protection of women; d. child protection;
e. fulfillment of children's rights;
f. integrated service center; g. safe house;
h. child-friendly regency;
i. guidance and supervision; j. community participation;
k. awards; andl. financing.
