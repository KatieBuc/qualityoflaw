MAYOR OF SOLOK

PROVINCE OF WEST SUMATRA

REGIONAL REGULATION OF SOLOK CITY

NUMBER 2 OF 2020

CONCERNING

THE IMPLEMENTATION OF PROTECTION FOR WOMEN AND CHILDREN

BY THE GRACE OF GOD ALMIGHTY

MAYOR OF SOLOK,

Considering:
a. that every person including women and children has the right to obtain protection from violence, exploitation, and discriminatory treatment in order to provide security and comfort;
b. that the implementation of protection for women and children from acts of violence, discrimination, exploitation, and human rights violations in Solok City has not been optimal so that the role of the Regional Government and the community is required;
c. that to fill the legal vacuum related to the implementation of protection for women and children from acts of violence, discrimination, exploitation and human rights violations, it is necessary to establish a regional regulation;
d. that based on the considerations as referred to in letter a, letter b, and letter c, it is necessary to stipulate a Regional Regulation concerning the Implementation of Protection for Women and Children;

In View of:

CHAPTER I

GENERAL PROVISIONS

Article 1

In this Regional Regulation, the following terms shall mean:

1. Region is the City of Solok.
2. Regional Government is the Mayor as an element of the Regional Government administration who leads the implementation of governmental affairs that are the authority of the autonomous region.
3. Mayor is the Mayor of Solok.
4. Regional Apparatus is an auxiliary element of the Mayor and DPRD in the administration of governmental affairs that are the authority of the Region.
5. Protection of Women is all efforts to guarantee and protect the rights of women so that they can live healthily, develop themselves and participate optimally in accordance with human dignity and receive protection from neglect, violence, and discrimination in the form of preventive, repressive, and postremedial protection.
6. Child is a person who has not yet reached the age of 18 (eighteen) years, including a child still in the womb.
7. Rights of the Child are part of human rights that must be guaranteed, protected and fulfilled by parents, family, community, government and the state.
8. Child Protection is all activities to guarantee and protect children and their rights so that they can live, grow, develop, and participate optimally in accordance with human dignity, and receive protection from violence and discrimination.
9. Neglected Child is a child whose needs are not properly fulfilled, whether physical, mental, spiritual, or social.
10. Street Child is a child whose life is irregular by spending most of their time outside the home to earn a living on the streets or in public places.
11. Regional Children's Forum hereinafter referred to as Forda is a forum for children's participation formed with the aim of facilitating children's involvement in the decision-making process and enjoying changes relating to their lives either directly or indirectly, which is carried out with the consent and will of all children based on awareness and understanding, in accordance with age and level of maturity of thinking.
12. Regional Technical Implementation Unit for the Protection of Women and Children hereinafter abbreviated as UPTD PPA is a regional technical implementation unit established by the Regional Government in providing services for women and children who experience violence, discrimination, special protection, and other problems
13. Service is an action that must be immediately taken to victims when seeing, hearing and knowing that violence against victims will, is or has occurred.
14. Child Protection is all activities to guarantee and protect children and their rights so that they can live, grow, develop and participate, optimally in accordance with human dignity, and receive protection from violence and discrimination.
15. Assistance is all actions taken in the form of complaint services, health, advocacy and legal aid, social rehabilitation including spiritual guidance, counseling, psychological therapy and economic empowerment, repatriation and social rehabilitation for the purpose of strengthening and advocacy as well as recovery of victims of violence;
16. Violence is any act unlawfully with or without the use of means against physical and psychological that causes danger to life, body or results in the deprivation of a person's freedom.
17. Violence against Women is any action based on gender differences that results or may result in suffering or hardship for women, physically, sexually or psychologically, including threats of certain actions, coercion or arbitrary deprivation of freedom, whether occurring in the public sphere or in private life.
18. Complaint Handling is a series of actions carried out by integrated service providers to follow up on reports of acts of violence against women and children submitted by victims, families and the community.
19. Social Rehabilitation is a service aimed at restoring and developing the ability of a person experiencing social dysfunction so that they can carry out their social functions properly.
20. Legal Aid is legal services provided by legal assistants and advocates to carry out the process of assisting witnesses and/or victims of violence against women and children.
21. Social Rehabilitation is an effort to reunite victims with their families, substitute families, or communities that can provide protection and fulfillment of needs for victims.
22. Spiritual Guidance is an effort to restore the psyche through strengthening the religion adhered to by the victim.

Article 2

The scope of this Regional Regulation includes:
a. rights of women and children;
b. protection of women;
c. fulfillment of children's rights;d. special protection for children;
e. handling of women and children victims of violence;
f. establishment of UPTD PPA;
g. guidance and supervision;
h. community participation;andi. financing.

CHAPTER II

RIGHTS OF WOMEN AND CHILDREN

Part One

Rights of Women

Article 3

Every woman has the right to:
a. obtain protection from discriminatory actions;
b. obtain protection from economic and sexual exploitation;
c. obtain protection from physical, psychological, sexual violence, and neglect;
d. obtain work leave while receiving salary or wages in accordance with statutory regulations;
e. obtain decent work according to their abilities; 6f. obtain education and teaching in all types, levels and pathways of education;
g. ownership and management of joint assets in marriage in accordance with statutory regulations;
h. elect and be elected in general elections in accordance with statutory regulations;
i. obtain special protection in the implementation of work or profession against matters that may threaten safety and or health related to women's reproductive function;  andj. obtain health services in accordance with standards.

Part Two

Rights of the Child

Article 4

Every child has the right to:
a. play, create, participate, grow and develop;
b. freely assemble, associate, live with parents, relate to parents if separated and survival;
c. obtain a name, identity, citizenship, education, child-appropriate information;
d. obtain health standards in accordance with decent living standards;
e. obtain protection and affection from parents;
f. obtain protection from exploitation as child labor, minority groups/isolated indigenous groups and from views or circumstances that by their nature are not yet appropriate for children to see;  andg. obtain special protection in emergency/urgent situations, displacement/displaced persons/evicted persons, if experiencing legal conflicts and armed conflicts or social conflicts.

CHAPTER III

PROTECTION OF WOMEN

Part One

General

Article 5

1. The Regional Government is responsible for organizing the protection of women by involving the active role of parents, families, ninik mamak, cadiak pandai, alim ulama, bundo kanduang, traditional leaders, religious leaders, the community, and institutions in the field of women's protection.
2. The responsibility of the Regional Government in organizing the protection of women as referred to in paragraph (1) is carried out by regional apparatus in accordance with their duties and functions.

Part Two

Regional Government

Paragraph 1

Protection

Article 6

Regional Government in efforts to protect women is responsible for:
a. providing protection from discriminatory actions in accordance with laws and regulations;
b. providing protection from economic exploitation and social exploitation;
c. providing protection from physical, psychological, sexual violence and neglect;
d. providing equal opportunities with men to obtain employment in accordance with their abilities;
e. providing work leave while continuing to receive salary or wages in accordance with laws and regulations;
f. providing adequate health and maternity facilities for pregnant and nursing mothers;
g. providing comprehensive health services free of charge; 8h. guaranteeing the right of women to elect and be elected in general elections in accordance with laws and regulations;
i. providing special protection in the performance of work or profession against matters that may threaten safety and or health in relation to women's reproductive function;
j. providing special protection to women victims of violence and human trafficking;
k. taking preventive action in order to prevent violations against women; andl. making regulations to regulate employers who employ women to comply with employment laws and regulations.

Paragraph 2

Prevention

Article 7

Regional Government in order to prevent violence against women is responsible for:
a. conducting socialization regarding prevention of violence against women to regional apparatus, community leaders, and community organizations;
b. optimizing the role of customary, cultural, religious institutions and community organizations in preventing acts of violence;
c. providing guidelines on handling acts of violence against women;
d. involving community participation in recovery and reintegration of women victims of violence;
e. developing an integrated database and information system on acts of violence against women; andf. developing family resilience programs.

Part Three

Community

Paragraph 1

Protection

Article 8

1. The community which in this case also includes ninik mamak, alim ulama, cadiak pandai, bundo kanduang in the context of protection of women is responsible for:a. participating and providing support for the development of women's protection programs;
b. providing information and/or reporting violations of women's rights to authorized institutions or parties; and  c. supervising the legal protection process for cases of violations of women's rights.
2. The responsibility as referred to in paragraph (1) is based on laws and regulations and the values of local wisdom of the local community.

Paragraph 2

Prevention

Article 9

The community in order to prevent violence against women is responsible for:
a. monitoring various conditions occurring in their environment that may lead to violence against women;
b. strengthening the role of community, religious organizations and family environment; andc. preventing unregistered marriages.

Part Four

Parents and/or Family

Paragraph 1

Protection

Article 10

Parents and/or family in order to prevent violence against women are responsible for:  a. protecting women from conflict and domestic violence that degrades the dignity of women;
b. providing equal opportunities to women to develop themselves in the fields of education, social, economic, political and cultural; andc. preventing violations of women's rights in the household.

Paragraph 2

Prevention

Article 11

Parents and/or family in preventing violations of women's rights are responsible for:
a. protecting women from various violations of rights in the household; andb. encouraging the formation of harmonious, educative and democratic families.

CHAPTER IV

FULFILLMENT OF CHILDREN'S RIGHTS

Part One

General

Article 12

1. Regional Government in the context of fulfillment of children's rights is responsible for:a. establishing and implementing policies, programs, and activities for the fulfillment of children's rights;
b. providing facilities and infrastructure in the implementation of fulfillment of children's rights;
c. conducting guidance and supervision in the context of implementation of fulfillment of children's rights;
d. respecting and guaranteeing the human rights of every child without distinguishing ethnicity, religion, race, class, gender, ethnicity, culture and language, legal status of the child, birth order of the child, and physical and/or mental condition; ande. cooperating with the private sector and/or non-governmental organizations in the implementation of fulfillment of children's rights.  (2) Regional Government in the context of fulfillment of children's rights may cooperate with the community, business sector, and mass media.  (3) The responsibility of Regional Government in efforts to fulfill children's rights as referred to in paragraph (1) is implemented by regional apparatus in accordance with their duties and functions.

Article 13

Regional Government in efforts to guarantee, protect and fulfill children's rights is responsible for establishing and implementing policies for the protection and fulfillment of children's rights covering the fields of birth registration, health, education, employment and social.

Article 14

The community in efforts to fulfill children's rights is responsible for:  a. providing space to gather and organize as well as listening to children to express their opinions;
b. supervising children in accessing various information and providing healthy and safe information;
c. providing information through socialization and education regarding children's rights and laws and regulations concerning children; and  d. conducting supervision and guidance in the context of organizing the fulfillment of children's rights through child facilitators.

Article 15

The business sector in efforts to fulfill Children's Rights, is responsible for:  a. implementing child perspective policies;
b. products intended for children must be safe for children; and  c. contributing to the fulfillment of children's rights through corporate social responsibility.

Article 16

Media is responsible in the fulfillment of children's rights to disseminate information and educational materials that are beneficial by taking into account the best interests of children from social, cultural, educational, religious and health aspects.

Part Two

Birth Registration

Article 17

1. Regional Government is obliged to organize birth registration of children with the issuance of birth certificates.
2. Birth certificates as referred to in paragraph (1) are organized free of charge.

Part Three

Health

Article 18

Policies for the protection and fulfillment of children's rights in the health field are as follows:
a. providing free health services for children from poor families;
b. organizing comprehensive health efforts for children;
c. ensuring that infants receive exclusive breast milk from their mothers;
d. ensuring that children receive nutrition that meets standards;
e. protecting children from efforts to transplant their body organs to other parties; andf. ensuring the provision of monitoring of growth and development of toddlers and pre-school age children.

Article 19

1. Regional Government is obligated to provide comprehensive basic health services for children in the context of fulfilling children's rights in the health sector so that children obtain optimal health status from the time they are in the womb.
2. Comprehensive basic health services as referred to in paragraph (1) include:a. promotive efforts;
b. preventive efforts;
c. curative efforts; andd. rehabilitative efforts.
3. Comprehensive basic health services as referred to in paragraph (2) shall be provided free of charge to children with malnutrition, children with disabilities, children with special needs, children infected with human immunodeficiency virus/acquired immunodeficiency syndrome, child laborers, child victims of narcotics abuse, alcohol, psychotropic substances and other addictive substances, children who are victims of violence, and/or child victims of human trafficking.

Article 20

Promotive efforts for basic health services as referred to in Article 19 paragraph (2) letter a shall be carried out through socialization or counseling to the community regarding healthy lifestyle patterns which include:
a. the importance of exclusive breast milk for infants;
b. the importance of immunization for infants and toddlers; andc. the importance of a healthy lifestyle and balanced nutrition;

Article 21

Preventive efforts for basic health services as referred to in Article 19 paragraph (2) letter b shall be implemented in order to prevent the occurrence of disease or health problems which are carried out by:
a. requiring mothers to provide exclusive breast milk to infants up to the age of 6 (six) months;
b. providing free immunization and vitamins to infants and toddlers;
c. providing nutritious food to infants and toddlers from underprivileged families;
d. providing monitoring of growth and development of toddlers and pre-school age children;
e. organizing comprehensive health efforts for children; 14f. optimizing the role of integrated service posts in serving maternal and child health; andg. providing sanitation facilities and clean water in every school.

Article 22

Curative efforts for basic health services as referred to in Article 19 paragraph (2) letter c shall be carried out in the context of treatment for disease cure, reduction of suffering due to disease, disease control, or restoration of disability so that the quality of children's health is maintained optimally.

Article 23

Rehabilitative efforts for basic health services as referred to in Article 19 paragraph (2) letter d are aimed at restoring conditions after illness which are carried out through periodic health examinations.

Part Four

Education

Article 24

1. Every organizer of an education unit is obligated to provide opportunities for children to obtain education without discrimination.
2. Children who have abnormalities and children who have potential intelligence and/or special talents shall be given equal opportunities to obtain formal, non-formal, and informal education services.
3. Regional Government shall provide educators for children who have abnormalities and potential intelligence and/or special talents in every education unit.

Article 25

Every child who is in the environment of an education unit shall be protected from acts of violence.

Article 26

Children in conflict with the law, children who experience pregnancy outside of marriage, child victims of human immunodeficiency virus/acquired immunodeficiency syndrome transmission, and child victims of narcotics abuse, alcohol, psychotropic substances and other addictive substances shall have their rights protected in order to obtain education.

Article 27

1. Every organizer of an education unit is prohibited from expelling children from educational institutions without guarantees for the continuity of the child's education.
2. Every organizer of formal education who violates the provisions as referred to in paragraph (1) may be subject to administrative sanctions in the form of:a. oral warning;b. written warning;
c. temporary suspension from activities;
d. termination of cooperation;
e. revocation of operational license; andf. withdrawal of facilities.
3. Provisions regarding the procedures for implementing administrative sanctions as referred to in paragraph (2) shall be regulated by Mayor Regulation.

Part Five

Employment

Article 28

1. Employers are prohibited from employing children except for children aged 13 (thirteen) to 15 (fifteen) years who may perform light work as long as it does not interfere with their health, physical, mental, social development and school time.
2. Employers who employ children in light work as referred to in paragraph (1) must meet the requirements:
a. written permission from parents/guardians;
b. work agreement between the employer and parents/guardians;
c. maximum working time of 3 (three) hours per day;
d. performed during the day and does not interfere with school time; 16e. occupational safety and health;
f. clear employment relationship;
g. receive wages in accordance with applicable laws and regulations; andh. children may perform work for the development of their talents and interests.
3. The provisions as referred to in paragraph (2) letter a, letter b, letter f and letter g are exempted for children who work in family businesses.
4. In the event that a company employs children as referred to in paragraph (1) and paragraph (2), the workplace for children must be separated from the workplace for adult workers.
5. Children may perform work in a workplace that is part of an educational curriculum or training approved by the authorized official.
6. Children as referred to in paragraph (5) must be at least 14 (fourteen) years old.
7. Work as referred to in paragraph (6) may be performed subject to the conditions:a. given clear instructions on how to carry out the work as well as guidance and supervision in carrying out the work; and
b. given occupational safety and health protection.

Article 29

1. Every person or entity is prohibited from employing and involving children in the worst forms of work.
2. The worst forms of work as referred to in paragraph (1) are:
a. all work in the form of slavery or the like;
b. all work that exploits, provides or offers children for prostitution, pornography production, pornographic performances or gambling;
c. all work that exploits, provides, or involves children for the production and trade of alcoholic beverages, narcotics, psychotropic substances and other addictive substances; and/ord. all work that endangers the health, safety, or morals of children.

Part Six

Social

Article 30

1. Regional Government shall organize the care of neglected children.
(2) Care as referred to in paragraph (1) may be carried out by Regional Apparatus that implements authority affairs in the social sector.

Article 31

1. In the event that a child is neglected for any reason that the parents neglect their obligations, then the Regional Government, community organizations as referred to in Article 30 paragraph (2) may submit an application to the court to determine the child as a neglected child.
2. Court determination as referred to in paragraph (1) shall simultaneously determine the place of shelter and care.

Article 32

Regional Government shall guarantee the protection, care, and welfare of children by taking into account the rights and obligations of parents, guardians, or other persons who are legally responsible for the child.

Article 33

Regional Government shall provide a child service information center as an interactive communication facility.

Article 34

1. Regional Government shall organize skills training for street children and neglected children who come from the Region.
3. Skills training as referred to in paragraph (1) shall be organized by Regional Apparatus that implements authority affairs in the social sector and may cooperate with community organizations.

Article 35

1. The Regional Government provides shelters to foster street children and social institutions for abandoned children.
2. The shelter as referred to in paragraph (1) may be organized by Regional Apparatus whose duties and functions are in the field of social affairs and manpower and may cooperate with community organizations.

Part Seven

Regional Children's Forum

Article 36

1. The Regional Government facilitates the establishment of Forda in implementing the fulfillment of children's rights to express opinions and participate.
2. Forda as referred to in paragraph (1) represents children, both representation of children's geographical domicile, components of children's socio-cultural groups and children's educational background.
3. In every formulation of policies related to children, the Regional Government must consider and accommodate children's opinions submitted through Forda.
4. Further provisions regarding the establishment of Forda as referred to in paragraph (1), paragraph (2) and paragraph (3) shall be stipulated by Mayor's Decree.

CHAPTER V

SPECIAL PROTECTION OF CHILDREN

Part One

General

Article 37

The Regional Government is responsible for organizing special protection of children by involving the active role of the community, ninik mamak, cadiak pandai, alim ulama, bundo kanduang and parents/family.

Part Two

Regional Government

Article 38

1. The Regional Government in efforts for special protection of children is responsible for:a. providing facilities and infrastructure in organizing special protection of children;
b. providing access to public services and social services for children with disabilities;
c. establishing and implementing policies and programs/activities for special protection of children;
d. cooperating with the community and/or community organizations in organizing special protection of children in accordance with laws and regulations;
e. conducting rehabilitation and assistance for children who become victims of violence;
f. providing special protection to children in emergency situations, children in conflict with the law, children victims of violence and human trafficking, children from minority and isolated groups, children exploited economically and/or sexually, children who are victims of narcotics, alcohol, psychotropic drugs abuse, and HIV/AIDS;
g. providing inclusive education for children with special needs; andh. taking preventive actions in order to prevent violations of children's rights.
2. The responsibility of the Regional Government in efforts for special protection of children as referred to in paragraph (1) shall be implemented by regional apparatus that carries out authority affairs in the social field.

Part Three

Community

Article 39

1. The community, ninik mamak, alim ulama, cadiak pandai, bundo kanduang in the context of special protection of children are responsible for:a. providing support for the development of special protection programs for children;
b. playing an active role in addressing symptoms that occur in the community that have the potential to violate children's rights;
c. monitoring and supervising the implementation of special protection of children; andd. providing information and or reporting if violence against children occurs to the integrated service center for women and children empowerment or the authorized party.
2. The responsibility of the community, ninik mamak, alim ulama, cadiak pandai, bundo kanduang as referred to in paragraph (1) is based on laws and regulations and local wisdom values of the local community.

Part three

Parents and Family

Article 40

1. The responsibility of parents and family towards special protection of children is as follows:a. providing formal and non-formal education in accordance with their talents and interests;
b. caring for, maintaining, educating and protecting children from acts of violence, exploitation, pornography, emergency situations and the possibility of children in conflict with the law; andc. facilitating children with special needs and children in conflict with the law as well as children who are victims of violence, exploitation, pornography and emergency situations.
2. In the event that parents do not exist, or their whereabouts are unknown, or due to a reason cannot carry out their responsibilities, then the responsibility as referred to in paragraph (1) is transferred to the guardian.

CHAPTER VI

HANDLING OF WOMEN AND CHILDREN VICTIMS OF VIOLENCE

Part one

General

Article 41

1. The implementation of handling women and children victims of violence is carried out through integrated services.
2. Integrated services for women and children victims of violence use the principles of:a. gender responsive;
b. non-discrimination;
c. equal and respectful relationships;
d. maintaining confidentiality;
e. providing a sense of security and comfort;
f. respecting individual differences;
g. non-judgmental;
h. respecting the choices and decisions of victims;
i. sensitive to the background and condition of victims and the use of appropriate language understood by victims;
j. fast and simple;
k. empathy; andl. fulfillment of women's and children's rights.

Part two

Forms of Handling

Paragraph 1

General

Article 42

The implementation of handling women and children victims of violence includes services for:
a. complaint handling;
b. health services;
c. social rehabilitation;
d. law enforcement and legal assistance; and/ore. repatriation and social rehabilitation.

Paragraph 2

Complaint Handling

Article 43

1. Complaint handling services for victims of violence against women and children as referred to in Article 42 letter a shall be implemented by:a. integrated service center for women and children empowerment;
b. hospitals;
c. community health centers;
d. children's social protection houses/shelters;
e. advisory board for marriage guidance and preservation;
f. district prosecutor's office;
g. district court;
h. child welfare institutions;
i. social welfare coordination institutions;
j. elderly welfare institutions;
k. family welfare consultation institutions; andl. domestic violence prevention groups.  (2) Complaints of acts of violence against women and children may be made by reporters consisting of victims, families, the community and/or community organizations in the field of protection of women and children.
3. The reporter as referred to in paragraph (1) may file a complaint:a. directly;
b. by telephone; and/orc. by letter.
4. In addition to handling complaints submitted by reporters, officers at service institutions as referred to in paragraph (1), are also authorized to handle victims of violence against women and children obtained through other information.

Paragraph 3

Health Services

Article 44

1. Health services for women and children victims of violence as referred to in Article 42 letter b constitute efforts that include curative and rehabilitative aspects.
2. Health services for women and children victims of violence are carried out comprehensively, accessible 24 (twenty-four) hours, of quality and carried out in accordance with health service standards.
3. Health services specifically for child victims of violence, must prioritize the best interests of the child.
4. Women and children victims of violence have the right to health services, including:a. medical services; andb. psychosocial counseling.

Paragraph 4

Social Rehabilitation

Article 45

Social rehabilitation services for women and children victims of violence as referred to in Article 42 letter c are aimed at restoring and developing the abilities of victims who experience social dysfunction to be able to carry out their social functions in society properly.

Article 46

Social rehabilitation services as referred to in Article 45 are as follows:
a. counseling services;
b. provision of safe houses; andc. spiritual guidance.

Article 47

1. Counseling services as referred to in Article 46 letter a aim to:
a. assist women and children victims of violence in recognizing their problems and finding effective ways to overcome them themselves;
b. provide moral support to women and children victims of violence in facing the process they are undergoing;
c. help alleviate, protect and restore the physical, psychological, social and spiritual condition of victims of violence so that they are able to carry out their social functions normally again; and
d. make victims feel accepted in the community environment.
(2) Counseling services as referred to in paragraph (1) are carried out by gender-responsive social rehabilitation officers, consisting of:
a. counselors;
b. psychologists; and
c. psychiatrists.
3. Counseling services as referred to in paragraph (1) are carried out in a place that guarantees a sense of security, comfort, and confidentiality of information from victims.
4. The stages of counseling services as referred to in paragraph (1) include:
a. initial counseling;
b. follow-up counseling;
c. case conference; and
d. home visit.
5. Counseling providers are:
a. regional apparatus having duties and functions in the social field;
b. regional apparatus in charge of women's empowerment, and may cooperate with integrated service centers for women's and children's empowerment or existing social community organizations; and/or
c. religious institutions.

Article 48

1. Provision of safe houses as referred to in Article 46 letter b may be provided by the Regional Government.
2. Safe houses as referred to in paragraph (1) aim to protect women and children victims of violence from intimidation by perpetrators or other parties.
3. The location of safe houses as referred to in paragraph (1) must be kept confidential.

Article 49

Criteria for women and children victims of violence who require safe houses include:
a. their lives are threatened;
b. they are rejected by family or community;
c. they require intensive services but their residence is relatively far away; and/or
d. they are abandoned if not placed in a safe house.

Article 50

The provision of safe house services is carried out by social rehabilitation officers who have gender sensitivity or gender awareness consisting of:
a. social workers or volunteers;
b. security officers; and/or
c. community leaders.

Article 51

1. Spiritual guidance as referred to in Article 46 letter c is intended for psychological recovery of women and children victims of violence.
2. Spiritual guidance as referred to in paragraph (1) is implemented based on the principles of:
a. not imposing any religion or belief; and
b. leaving the final decision in the hands of the victim.
3. Spiritual guidance is carried out by spiritual guidance officers, consisting of advisory board consultants, marriage development and preservation boards, religious affairs office officers, ustads or ulamas and/or other religious leaders.

Paragraph 5

Law Enforcement and Legal Aid

Article 52

1. Law enforcement and legal aid services are actions by law enforcement officials to implement laws and regulations in handling cases of violence against women and children, especially to provide sanctions against perpetrators and provide protection for witnesses and/or victims.
2. Law enforcement and legal aid services as referred to in paragraph (1) are carried out by prioritizing the best interests of victims.
3. Law enforcement and legal aid services are intended to provide assistance and protection to women and children victims of violence and witnesses with a gender-responsive approach so that victims obtain justice and certainty of their legal rights.
4. Types of legal aid services include:
a. legal aid in the reporting process and preparation of examination reports;
b. legal aid in the prosecution process at the prosecutor's office;
c. legal aid in the examination process at court;
d. legal aid in the court hearing process until there is a final and binding decision; and
e. legal aid in the mediation process.

Article 53

1. The Regional Government provides legal aid to women and children victims of violence free of charge.
2. The Regional Government in providing legal aid may cooperate with legal aid institutions, advocates and/or other legal assistants.

Part Seven

Repatriation and Social Rehabilitation

Article 54

1. Repatriation of victims of violence is intended to return women and children victims of violence to their area of origin.
(2) Repatriation of women and children victims of violence is accompanied by social rehabilitation efforts.
3. The Regional Government implements repatriation and social rehabilitation of women and children victims of violence in accordance with laws and regulations.

CHAPTER VII

ESTABLISHMENT OF UPTD PPA

Article 55

1. The Regional Government in order to administer women's empowerment and child protection affairs may establish UPTD PPA.
2. UPTD PPA functions to provide services to women and children experiencing criminal and civil problems.
(3) The scope of UPTD PPA services includes complaint services, consultation, mediation, outreach, health, social rehabilitation, spiritual guidance, assistance, law enforcement and legal aid, and repatriation and social rehabilitation services.
4. In carrying out its functions as referred to in paragraph (2), UPTD PPA is obliged to:
a. provide services and handling as quickly as possible to women and children experiencing problems;
b. provide convenience, comfort, safety and free of charge to women and children experiencing problems;
c. maintain the confidentiality of women and children experiencing problems; and
d. guarantee justice and legal certainty for women and children experiencing problems.
5. Further provisions regarding the organization and work procedures of UPTD PPA as referred to in paragraph (1) are regulated by Mayor Regulation.

CHAPTER VIII

GUIDANCE AND SUPERVISION

Article 56

1. The Regional Government conducts guidance on the implementation of protection of women and children.
2. Guidance as referred to in paragraph (1) may be carried out in the form of:
a. conducting socialization of programs and activities for the protection of women and children; and
b. providing training for government officials, law enforcers, religious leaders, community leaders, traditional leaders, educators, journalists, and media managers on the prevention and handling of violence against women and children.

Article 57

1. The Regional Government conducts supervision of the implementation of protection of women and children.
2. Supervision as referred to in paragraph (1) is carried out 1 (one) time in 1 (one) year by the Regional Apparatus whose duties and functions are in the field of supervision.
3. The results of supervision as referred to in paragraph (1) constitute evaluation material for policies on the protection of women and children in the Region.

CHAPTER IX

COMMUNITY PARTICIPATION

Article 58

1. The community may participate in the implementation of the protection of women and children.
2. The community as referred to in paragraph (1) consists of, among others, parents, families, ninik mamak, cadiak pandai, alim ulama, bundo kanduang, customary leaders, religious leaders, mass media, and the business community.
3. Community participation is carried out through the following means:a. optimizing the function of community organizations in the Region; 29b. conducting socialization and education on laws and regulations related to the protection of women and children;
c. providing input in the process of formulating laws and regulations in the Region;
d. conducting socialization on the prevention of child marriage;
e. carrying out recovery and reintegration of women victims of violence;
f. providing information and/or reporting the occurrence of violence, discrimination, exploitation, and human rights violations against women and children to the competent authorities;
g. supervising the process of handling cases of violence, discrimination, exploitation, and human rights violations against women and children;
h. providing space for children to gather and organize to express themselves, be creative and voice their opinions;
i. supervising children in accessing various information;
j. providing products that are safe for children;
k. contributing to the fulfillment of children's rights through corporate social responsibility; and/orl. disseminating information and educational materials that are beneficial for women and children.

CHAPTER X

FINANCING

Article 59

Financing for the implementation of policies, programs and activities for the protection of women and children is sourced from:
a. Regional Revenue and Expenditure Budget; andb. other lawful sources of revenue in accordance with the provisions of laws and regulations.

CHAPTER X

CLOSING PROVISIONS

Article 60

Implementing regulations of this Regional Regulation shall be stipulated no later than 1 (one) year from the promulgation of this Regional Regulation.

Article 61

This Regional Regulation shall come into force on the date of promulgation.

CONCERNING

IMPLEMENTATION OF THE PROTECTION OF WOMEN AND CHILDREN

I. GENERAL

The 1945 Constitution of the Republic of Indonesia mandates Regional Governments to regulate and manage their own regions for the realization of community welfare through improved Services, empowerment and community participation. One of the authorities of regional governments is to make regional policies with the aim of improving the welfare of the people in their regions.

In addition, the 1945 Constitution has also mandated in Article 28D paragraph (1) that every person has the Right to recognition, guarantees, Protection and just legal certainty as well as equal treatment before the law. Article 28B Paragraph 2 also states that every Child has the Right to survival, growth and development and has the Right to Protection from Violence and discrimination. This shows that female Children should be protected by the state, especially against Violence because their Rights are already regulated in the constitution.

Therefore, efforts need to be made to prevent and eradicate all forms of discrimination and Violence against women and Children, especially for the Solok City region. Solok City is one of the parties most authorized to carry out women's empowerment and Child Protection by conducting and formulating regulations to provide guarantees for the implementation of the Protection of Women and Children.

One form of such regulation is the Regional Regulation of Solok City. The regulation is expected to be able to encourage efforts to improve the physical and mental condition of women and Child victims of Violence.
