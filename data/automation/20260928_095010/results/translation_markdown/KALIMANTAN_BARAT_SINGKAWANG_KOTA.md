BY THE GRACE OF ALMIGHTY GOD

MAYOR OF SINGKAWANG,

Considering:

a. that children and women as creations of Almighty God need to be protected and their honor, dignity, and self-esteem safeguarded from all forms of discrimination, acts of violence, and exploitation to improve the quality of life of children and women physically, spiritually, and socially in realizing an advanced, just, prosperous, welfare, and democratic society based on Pancasila and the 1945 Constitution of the Republic of Indonesia;
b. that the fate of children and women in Singkawang City needs attention from the Regional Government and the community because cases of violence against children and women, and children in conflict with the law still occur, thus requiring efforts to provide legal protection for children and women;
c. that to provide protection for children and women, also as implementation of higher Laws and Regulations and constituting one of the mandatory governmental affairs that is the authority of the Regional Government, the regulation of protection of children and women needs to be regulated in a Regional Regulation;
d. that based on the considerations as referred to in letter a, letter b, and letter c, it is necessary to establish a Regional Regulation concerning Protection of Children and Women;

# CHAPTER I

## GENERAL PROVISIONS

#### Article 1

In this Regional Regulation, what is meant by:  
1. Region is the City of Singkawang.  
2. Regional Government is the regional head as an element of the Regional Government administration who leads the implementation of government affairs that are the authority of the autonomous region.  
3. Mayor is the Mayor of Singkawang City.  
4. Child is a person who has not yet reached the age of 18 (eighteen) years, including those still in the womb.  
5. Woman is an adult human being of female sex and a person recognized by law as a woman.  
6. Victim is a child and woman who experiences misery and/or suffering either directly or indirectly as a result of violence.  
7. Protection of Women is all activities aimed at providing a sense of security carried out by the Regional Government, the community, and/or other parties who know or hear that violence against women will occur, is occurring, or has occurred.  
8. Child protection is all activities to guarantee and protect children and their rights so that they can live, grow, develop, and participate optimally in accordance with human dignity and receive protection from violence and discrimination.  
9. Special protection is a form of protection received by children in certain situations and conditions to obtain a guarantee of security against threats that endanger themselves and their lives in their growth and development.  
10. Violence against women and children, hereinafter referred to as violence, is any act based on gender differences that results in misery or suffering physically, sexually, mentally, psychologically, including intimidation, forced eviction, threats of certain actions, coercion or deprivation of freedom, neglect and hindering the ability of women and children to enjoy all their rights and freedoms.  
11. Family is the smallest unit in society consisting of husband and wife, husband and wife and their children, or father and children, or mother and children, or blood relatives in a straight line upward or downward up to the third degree.  
12. Parents are biological father and/or mother, or step father and/or mother, or adoptive father and/or mother.  
13. Guardian is a person or body who in fact exercises parental authority as a parent over a child.  
14. Every person is an individual or corporation, whether a legal entity or not a legal entity.  
15. Service is an action taken as soon as possible to the victim when seeing, hearing and/or knowing that violence against the victim will occur, is occurring, or has occurred.  
16. Assistance is all actions in the form of counseling, psychological therapy, advocacy, and spiritual guidance, to strengthen victims of violence to resolve the problems they face.  
17. Regional Apparatus Organization, hereinafter abbreviated as OPD, is a regional apparatus organization within the Singkawang City Government.  
18. Prevention is an activity carried out so that acts of violence against children and women do not occur.  
19. Repatriation is an effort to return women and child victims of violence to their families, substitute families or communities that can provide protection and fulfill their needs.  
20. Rehabilitation is recovery from disturbances to physical, psychological, and social conditions so that they can carry out their roles again properly both in the family and in society.  
21. Reintegration is an effort to reunite victims with families, communities, institutions or other social environments that can provide protection.  
22. Institution is an agency/office/body/office within the scope of the Regional Government and/or non-governmental organizations that provide assistance.  
23. Agency is a regional apparatus work unit whose duties and responsibilities are to coordinate the handling of affairs in the field of organizing child and women protection.  
24. Integrated Service Center, hereinafter abbreviated as PPT, is a functional work unit that organizes integrated services for victims of violence.  
25. Integrated Service Center for Women and Children Empowerment, hereinafter abbreviated as P2TP2A, is an integrated, one-stop, and networked technical service established by the government, Regional Government, community, non-governmental organizations, donor institutions, and other institutions in efforts to empower women and protect women and children comprehensively from various types of discrimination and acts of violence, including trafficking in persons.  
26. Community is individuals, families, groups, and social organizations, and/or community organizations.  
27. Regional Action Plan, hereinafter abbreviated as RAD, is the foundation and guideline for relevant agencies, vertical agencies, and the community, in carrying out activities for the implementation of prevention and protection of children and women victims of violence.  
28. Task Force is a coordinative institution formed by the Regional Government that coordinates policies, programs, and activities for the protection of children and women in the region.  
29. Child-Friendly City, hereinafter abbreviated as KLA, is a city that has a child rights-based development system through the integration of commitments and resources of the Regional Government, community, and business world that are planned comprehensively and sustainably in policies, programs, and activities to ensure the fulfillment of children's rights.

# CHAPTER II

## RIGHTS OF CHILDREN, WOMEN, AND VICTIMS

#### Article 2

( 1) Every child has the right:  
a. to live, grow, develop, and participate reasonably in accordance with human dignity and worth, and to receive protection from criminal acts, violence, exploitation, and neglect;  
b. to a name as personal identity and citizenship status;  
c. to worship according to their religion under parental guidance and to practice their religious teachings;  
d. to know their parents, be raised, and cared for by their own parents;  
e. to obtain health services and social security in accordance with physical, mental, spiritual, and social needs;  
f. to obtain education and teaching in accordance with age level, physical and mental condition, intelligence, and interests and talents;  
g. to express and have their opinion heard and to receive, seek, and provide information in accordance with the level of intelligence and age for self-development in accordance with moral values and propriety;  
h. to rest and utilize leisure time, play, express themselves for self-development;  
1. to receive proper care from parents/guardians or other parties responsible for their care;  
J. to obtain protection from abuse in politics, involvement in armed conflicts, involvement in social unrest, involvement in events containing elements of violence, involvement in warfare, targets of persecution, torture or imposition of inhumane punishment and involvement of children in the worst forms of work;  
k. to obtain protection from the dangers of drugs, pornography, and violent shows or other things that impact changes in child development;  
I. to have contact with parents if separated; and
m. to obtain other rights in accordance with Laws and Regulations.  
2. Every woman has the right:  
a. to live, survive, and improve her standard of living;  
b. to live peacefully, safely, in peace, happily, prosperously physically and mentally;  
c. to enjoy a good and healthy living environment;  
d. to live in a family within a legal marriage bond based on the provisions of Laws and Regulations;  
e. to obtain work according to ability, conditions and fair and just wages;  
f. specifically for women with disabilities, the right to receive facilities and special treatment in public services;  
g. to play an active role in the political and governmental fields in accordance with the potential and capabilities they possess;  
h. to obtain legal information and services; and  
i. to obtain other rights in accordance with human dignity and based on the provisions of Laws and Regulations.  
3. Every victim has the right to receive:  
a. protection;  
b. information;  
c. optimal services;  
d . continuous handling up to the rehabilitation stage;  
e. confidential handling;  
f. assistance in accordance with applicable provisions;  
g. guarantee of rights related to the victim's status as a family member and community member;  
h . to obtain restitution in accordance with the provisions of Laws and Regulations; and
i. other rights regulated in the provisions of Laws and Regulations.

#### Article 3

Child victims of violence in addition to receiving rights as referred to in Article 2 paragraph (1), and paragraph (3) also receive special rights as follows:  
a. the right to respect and full use for survival, growth, and development;  
b. the right to basic services;  
c. the right to equal protection;  
d. the right to be free from various stigma; and  
e. the right to obtain freedom.

#### Article 4

Every victim has the right to report and receive assistance and to receive guarantees of their rights related to their status as a wife, mother, child, family member and as a community member.

#### Article 5

The rights as referred to in Article 2 paragraph (3) letter c, are also given to the families of witnesses and/or victims up to the second degree, if the families of witnesses and/or victims receive threats both physical and psychological from other people in connection with the testimony of witnesses and/or victims.

# CHAPTER III

## OBLIGATIONS OF CHILDREN

#### Article 6

Every child must:  
a. respect parents, guardians, and teachers;  
b. love family, community, and care for friends;  
c. love the homeland, nation, and state;  
d. perform worship according to their religious teachings; and  
e. practice noble ethics and morals.

# CHAPTER IV

## OBLIGATIONS AND RESPONSIBILITIES

#### Article 7

The obligations and responsibilities of organizers of child and women protection are the joint responsibility of the Regional Government, community, family, and parents or guardians.

#### Article 8

1. The obligations and responsibilities of the Regional Government as referred to in Article 7 are implemented:  
a. to formulate and implement policies, programs, and activities for child and women protection;  
b. to ensure the establishment and implementation of cooperation between various parties in the Region, with and between various parties in districts/cities and all related elements in the implementation of child and women protection;  
c. to provide support for facilities, infrastructure, and availability of human resources in organizing child and women protection;  
d. to fulfill, protect, and respect children's rights and ensure children to exercise their rights in expressing opinions in accordance with the child's age and intelligence; and  
e. to foster and supervise the organization of child and women protection.  
2. The obligations and responsibilities of the Regional Government as referred to in paragraph (1) are coordinated by the Regional Apparatus Organization responsible for women's empowerment and child protection affairs.  
3. In order to carry out the obligations and responsibilities as referred to in paragraph (1), the Regional Government establishes programs and action activities for child and women protection in 1 (one) RAD as the basis for Regional Apparatus Organizations in implementing child and women protection in the region.  
4. The RAD as referred to in paragraph (3) is part of the Regional Medium-Term Development Plan regulated by Mayor Regulation.

#### Article 9

1. The obligations and responsibilities of the community as referred to in Article 7 are organized in the form of community participation in organizing child and women protection.  
2. Community participation as referred to in paragraph (1), may be carried out in the form of:  
a. preventing acts of violence against women and children;  
b. submitting reports to the authorities if acts of violence against women and children occur;  
c. providing assistance to victims; and  
d. playing an active role in efforts to increase the fulfillment of children's rights and women's empowerment.  
3. Community participation as referred to in paragraph (2) is carried out by individuals, social community institutions, community organizations, professional organizations, educational institutions, religious institutions, private sector, mass media, and the business world.  
4. The role of community organizations, professional organizations, and educational institutions is carried out by taking necessary steps in accordance with the respective duties, functions, authorities, and competencies to assist in organizing child and women protection.  
5. The role of mass media is carried out through the dissemination of information and educational materials related to organizing child and women protection with due regard to the best interests of children and/or victims.  
6. The role of the business world is carried out through:  
a. company policies with child and women perspectives;  
b. products intended must be safe for children; and  
c. contributing to the fulfillment of children's rights and women's empowerment through corporate social responsibility.  
7. Forms of community participation as referred to in paragraph (2), are implemented responsibly in accordance with the provisions of Laws and Regulations.

#### Article 10

The obligations and responsibilities of the family as referred to in Article 7, legally have full responsibility to prevent all forms of violence and protect women and children as family members.

#### Article 11

1. The obligations and responsibilities of parents as referred to in Article 7, include:  
a. caring for, maintaining, educating, providing a sense of security and not committing mistreatment and violence against children;  
b. developing children according to their abilities, talents, and interests;  
c. reporting every birth of a child to the authorized agency to register births;  
d. preventing marriage at child age; and  
e. providing character education and instilling moral values in children.  
2. In the event that parents do not exist, or their whereabouts are unknown, or due to a certain reason cannot carry out their obligations and responsibilities, then the obligations and responsibilities as referred to in paragraph (1) may be transferred to the family, which is implemented in accordance with the provisions of Laws and Regulations.  
3. In the event that the parents and family of the child cannot carry out their obligations and responsibilities, then the obligations and responsibilities as referred to in paragraph (1), and paragraph (2), are transferred to a person or legal entity that meets the requirements appointed as guardian of the child concerned.

##### CHAPTER V

##### STATUS OF CHILDREN

## Part One

Identity of Children

#### Article 12

1. The identity of every child must be given from birth.  
2. The identity as referred to in paragraph (1) is set forth in a birth certificate.  
3. The making of a birth certificate is based on a certificate from the person who witnessed and/or assisted the birth process.  
4. In the case of a child whose birth process is unknown, and whose parents' whereabouts are unknown, the making of a birth certificate for that child is based on the statement of the person who found them and completed with a police examination report.

#### Article 13

1. The organizer of birth certificate making is carried out by the Regional Apparatus Organization that administers population administration affairs.  
2. Provisions regarding procedures and requirements for making birth certificates as referred to in paragraph (1) are regulated by Mayor Regulation.

## Part Two

Children Born from Mixed Marriages

#### Article 14

1. If a mixed marriage occurs between a citizen of the Republic of Indonesia and a foreign citizen, the child born from that marriage is entitled to obtain the citizenship of his or her father or mother in accordance with the provisions of Laws and Regulations.  
2. In the event of divorce from the marriage as referred to in paragraph (1), the child has the right to choose or based on a court decision, to be in the custody of one of both parents.  
3. In the event of divorce as referred to in paragraph (2), while the child is not yet able to determine a choice, and his or her mother is a citizen of the Republic of Indonesia, for the best interests of the child or upon the request of his or her mother, the Regional Government is obliged to arrange the citizenship status of the Republic of Indonesia for the child concerned.

# CHAPTER VI

## CUSTODY

#### Article 15

1. In the event that parents as referred to in Article 11 paragraph (1) neglect their obligations, supervision measures may be taken against them or parental custody may be revoked.  
2. Supervision measures against parents or revocation of custody as referred to in paragraph (1) are carried out through court determination.

#### Article 16

1. One of the parents, biological siblings or family up to the third degree, may submit an application to the court to obtain a court determination regarding the revocation of parental custody or to take supervision measures if there are strong reasons for it as referred to in Article 15 paragraph (1).  
2. If one of the parents, biological siblings, or family up to the third degree, cannot carry out their function, then the revocation of parental custody as referred to in paragraph (1) may also be submitted by an authorized official or other institution that has the authority to do so.  
3. The court determination as referred to in paragraph (1) may appoint an individual or government/community institution to become a guardian for the child concerned.  
4. Individuals who carry out child care as referred to in paragraph (3) must be of the same religion as the religion adhered to by the child to be cared for.

# CHAPTER VII

## GUARDIANSHIP

#### Article 17

1. In the event that a child is not capable of performing legal acts, while the parents or family are not known where they reside or their whereabouts, then a person or legal entity that meets the requirements may be appointed as guardian of the child concerned.  
2. To become a child's guardian as referred to in paragraph (1) is carried out through court determination.  
3. The appointed guardian's religion must be the same as the religion adhered to by the child.  
4. The guardian appointed as referred to in paragraph (2) is responsible for the child and must manage the property of the child concerned for the best interests of the child.  
5. Provisions regarding the requirements and procedures for appointing a guardian are implemented in accordance with the provisions of Laws and Regulations.  

JDIH Kota Singkawang

# CHAPTER VIII

## CHILD ADOPTION

#### Article 18

1. Child adoption may only be carried out for the best interests of the child, and is carried out based on customs and in accordance with Laws and Regulations.  
2. Child adoption does not sever blood relations between the adopted child and his or her biological parents.  
3. Child adoption must be registered in the birth certificate without eliminating the child's initial identity.  
4. Prospective adoptive parents must be of the same religion as the religion adhered to by the prospective adopted child.  
5. Child adoption by foreign citizens may only be carried out as a last resort in accordance with Laws and Regulations.  
6. In the event that the child's origin is unknown, then the child's religion is adjusted to the religion of the majority of the local population.

#### Article 19

1. Adoptive parents are obliged to inform their adopted child about his or her origin and biological parents.  
2. Notification of origin and biological parents is carried out with due regard to the child's mental readiness.

# CHAPTER IX

## IMPLEMENTATION OF PROTECTION

## Part One

General

#### Article 20

The implementation of child and women protection is carried out by:  
a. planning and development of child and women information systems;  
b. prevention and protection;  
c. child protection;  
d. special protection;  
e. women protection;  
f. protection in the workplace;  
g. handling, and recovery of victims of violence;  
h. rehabilitation, reintegration, and empowerment;  
i. law enforcement services, and legal assistance; and  
j. facilitation of juvenile justice.

## Part Two

Planning and Development of Child and Women Information Systems

#### Article 21

1. Planning for child and women protection is implemented by providing data and information related to child and women protection in the region.  
2. Data and information as referred to in paragraph (1) concerns data on gender mainstreaming (PUG), data on child rights mainstreaming (PUHA), and evaluation of programs and data services.  
3. Data and information as referred to in paragraph (2) are provided by the Regional Apparatus Organization that administers child and women protection affairs.  
4. Information data as referred to in paragraph (3) is needed for policy planning and program intervention coordinated by the Agency.

## Part Three

Prevention and Protection

#### Article 22

1. Regional Government is obliged to undertake prevention and protection efforts against violent treatment, exploitation, discrimination, maltreatment, and neglect.
2. Implementation of prevention and protection as referred to in paragraph (1), may cooperate with educational institutions, religious institutions, mass media, professional organizations, business entities, and/or community institutions.
3. Prevention and protection efforts as referred to in paragraph (2) are implemented by:
a. forming work networks;
b. conducting coordination, integration, and synchronization of prevention, and protection based on partnership patterns;
c. forming a system of violence prevention and protection for children and women;
d. conducting socialization of Laws and Regulations related to the protection of women victims of violence;
e. providing education for victims of violence and vulnerable groups;
f. organizing family support in the form of family parenting education, child parenting education, family mediation, and economic support;
g. increasing the capacity of protection services for women and children including institutional capacity development and increasing the professionalism and competence of service providers;
h. increasing the ability of vulnerable individuals and/or groups to recognize risks and dangers from situations or actions that may cause violence, exploitation, maltreatment and neglect; and
I. increasing the utilization of communication, information, and education media.
4. Prevention and protection efforts as referred to in paragraph (3) are carried out in an integrated manner by the Regional Government coordinated by the Regional Apparatus Organization responsible for women's empowerment and child protection.

#### Article 23

1. Every Person and/or Institution is obliged to protect children and women from violence, exploitation, discrimination, maltreatment and neglect.
2. Protection by Institutions as referred to in paragraph (1) by not implementing policies and actions that violate the rights of children and women.

## Part Four

Child Protection

### Paragraph 1

Religion

#### Article 24

1. Every child receives protection to worship according to their religion.
2. Before the child can determine their choice, the religion embraced by the child follows the religion of their parents.

#### Article 25

1. Regional Government, community, family, parents, guardians, and community social institutions guarantee the protection of children in embracing their religion.
2. Protection of children in embracing their religion as referred to in paragraph (1), includes guidance, counseling, and practice of religious teachings for children.

### Paragraph 2

Health

#### Article 26

1. Regional Government is obliged to provide facilities and organize comprehensive health efforts for children, so that every child obtains optimal health status from the womb.
2. Provision of facilities and organization of comprehensive health efforts as referred to in paragraph (1), is supported by community participation.
3. Comprehensive health efforts as referred to in paragraph (1) include promotive, preventive, curative, and rehabilitative efforts, both for basic health services and referrals.
4. Comprehensive health efforts as referred to in paragraph (3) are organized free of charge for families who are unable.
(5) Implementation of provisions as referred to in paragraph (1) through paragraph (4) is carried out in accordance with the provisions of Laws and Regulations.

#### Article 27

1. Parents and family are obliged to be responsible for maintaining the health of children and caring for children from the womb.
2. In the event that parents and family are unable to carry out their responsibilities as referred to in paragraph (1), then the Regional Government is obliged to fulfill it.
3. Obligations as referred to in paragraph (2), are implemented in accordance with the provisions of Laws and Regulations.

#### Article 28

Every person is prohibited from performing abortion on a child still in the womb, except for reasons and procedures justified in accordance with the provisions of Laws and Regulations.

#### Article 29

Regional Government, community, family, and parents or guardians are obliged to protect children from actions that disturb the health and development of children.

JDIH Kota Singkawang

### Paragraph 3

Education

#### Article 30

1. Regional Government is obliged to organize basic education of at least 9 (nine) years for all children.
2. Parents are obliged to fulfill the child's right to obtain basic education of at least 9 (nine) years.

#### Article 31

Regional Government, family, and parents or guardians are obliged to provide the widest possible opportunity for children to obtain education.

#### Article 32

Education as referred to in article 31 is directed at:
a. development of attitudes and personality abilities of children, talents, mental abilities, and physical abilities to achieve optimal potential;
b. development of respect for human rights and fundamental freedoms;
c. development of respect for parents, cultural identity, language, and their own values, national values where the child resides, from where the child originates, and different customs and traditions from the child's own area of residence;
d. preparation of children for a responsible life; and
e. development of respect, and love for the environment.

#### Article 33

1. Children with disabilities are given opportunities and accessibility to obtain inclusive education and/or special education.
2. Children who have excellence are given opportunities and accessibility to obtain special education.
3. Children who receive special protection are given opportunities to obtain their right to education.

#### Article 34

1. Regional Government is responsible for providing education costs and/or free of charge or special services for children from underprivileged families, neglected children, and children residing in remote areas.
2. Regional Government responsibility as referred to in paragraph (1) also includes encouraging the community to play an active role.

#### Article 35

1. Children in the educational unit environment must receive protection from physical violence, psychological violence, sexual crimes, and other crimes committed by educators, educational institutions, fellow students and/or other parties.
2. Protection as referred to in paragraph (1) is carried out by educators, education personnel, government officials and/or the community.

#### Article 36

(1) Regional Government is obliged to develop a child-friendly school model.
(2) Child-friendly schools as referred to in paragraph (1) constitute a joint effort of school members to:
a. create a safe and comfortable environment for children at school;
b. develop child participation in accordance with their age and maturity; and
c. provide opportunities for children from poor families and/or persons with disabilities to receive equal and fair treatment to obtain services and guidance at school.

### Paragraph 4

Social

#### Article 37

1. Regional Government is obliged to organize maintenance and care for neglected children, both in institutions and outside institutions.
2. Organization of maintenance as referred to in paragraph (1) may be carried out by institutions or the community.
3. To organize maintenance and care for neglected children by institutions and the community, as referred to in paragraph (2), may cooperate with various related parties.
4. In the case of organizing maintenance and care as referred to in paragraph (3), supervision is carried out by the Regional Apparatus Organization that administers affairs in the social sector in accordance with the provisions of Laws and Regulations.

#### Article 38

1. Regional Government in carrying out maintenance and care must endeavor and assist children to be able to:  
a. participate;  
b. freely express opinions and think in accordance with their conscience and religion;  
c. freely receive oral or written information in accordance with the child's age stage and development;  
d. freely associate and assemble;  
e. freely rest, play, recreate, create, and engage in cultural arts; and  
f. obtain play facilities that meet health and safety requirements.  
2. Efforts as referred to in paragraph (1) are developed and adjusted to the age, ability level of the child and their environment so as not to hinder and disturb the child's development.

#### Article 39

In the event a child is neglected because their parents neglect their obligations, then institutions or communities as referred to in Article 37, family or authorized officials may submit an application to the court to determine the child as a neglected child.

#### Article 40

1. Court determination as referred to in Article 39 simultaneously determines the shelter, maintenance and care for the neglected child concerned.  
2. Institutions or communities granted authority must provide a place as referred to in paragraph (1).

## Part Seven

Protection in the Workplace

#### Article 63

1. Every company is prohibited from recruiting child labor under age or exploiting children.
2. Companies must position female workers as work partners to grow the company.

#### Article 64

1. The provisions as referred to in Article 63 paragraph (1), may be exempted by taking into account the following requirements:
a. the age for child workers in the informal sector work is above 15 (fifteen) years;
b. obtain written approval from the parent/guardian of the child worker in the informal sector work;
c. the implementation must be set forth in a written work agreement between the company/employer and the parent/guardian of the child worker in the informal sector work;
d. not employed at night;
e. working time of at most 3 (three) hours per day;
f. not employed in a place or environment that can interfere with the child's growth and development process, both physical, mental, moral and intellectual as well as the child's health;
g. provide opportunities to socialize with family and the surrounding environment;
h. provide opportunities to obtain education in accordance with their talents and interests;
i. employed for light types of work; and j. provide health protection for children and one day off per week.
2. Protection for child workers in the informal sector work as referred to in paragraph (1), aims to:
a. prevent all forms of exploitation, discrimination, harassment and violence against children; and
b. protect children from activities that can interfere with the child's growth and development process, both physical, mental, moral and intellectual as well as the child's health.
3. Protection efforts for child workers in the informal sector work as referred to in paragraph (2), include:
a. providing counseling to the community about children's rights;
b. providing assistance in the form of psychological, medical and legal services to child workers in the informal sector work who experience exploitation, discrimination, harassment and violence;
c. empowering families through the provision of skills training and expenditure reduction;
d. providing scholarships to child workers in the informal sector work who drop out of school to continue formal education to a higher level; and
e. providing non-formal education and skills training for child workers in the informal sector work who do not pursue formal education.

#### Article 65

1. Female workers who work beyond working hours, the company is obliged to provide overtime pay in accordance with the provisions of Laws and Regulations.
2. Companies are obliged to protect occupational health and safety, and provide maternity leave rights to female workers.
3. Companies are prohibited from making policies that cause discrimination against female workers.
4. Companies that violate the provisions as referred to in paragraph (1), paragraph (2), and paragraph (3), may be subject to administrative sanctions in the form of:
a. verbal warning;
b. written warning; and
c. other administrative sanctions in accordance with the provisions of Laws and Regulations.

i. children with deviant social behavior;

a. identification process and receipt of complaints/reports;  
b. rescue actions;  
c. placement of children and women in temporary shelters;

2\. psychosocial recovery services; and

#### Article 67

a. ensuring readiness for complaints regarding child and women protection issues;  
b. following up on information or complaints/reports received regarding child and women protection issues; and  
c. identifying the type of problem, needs and handling plan.  
2. The Regional Apparatus Organization responsible for women's empowerment and child protection affairs is obligated to identify problems and receive complaints/reports.

#### Article 68

1. Rescue actions as referred to in Article 66 paragraph (2) letter b are carried out if based on identification results it is known that the safety of the child or woman victim of violence is threatened.  
2. Actions as referred to in paragraph (1) are carried out by removing the child from the situation and environment that poses a threat.  
3. The Regional Apparatus Organization responsible for women's empowerment and child protection affairs is obligated to carry out rescue actions and coordinate with police officers or civil service police units.

#### Article 69

a. there is a threat to safety;

c. the child cannot be reunited with family/caregiver/guardian and/or the community.  
2. Placement as referred to in paragraph (1) is carried out for a certain period of time while the child or victim receives recovery services and/or until the family/caregiver/guardian is assessed to have readiness to care for and protect the child.  
3. Services provided to children and/or women while in temporary shelters include services for fulfilling basic needs and psychosocial support.  
4. The Regional Apparatus Organization responsible for social affairs is obligated to provide temporary shelters.  
5. The Regional Apparatus Organization responsible for social affairs may cooperate with civil service police or the police in efforts to maintain the security of children or women whose safety is threatened while in temporary shelters.

d. deoxyribose nucleic acid verification services;  
e. delivery services for pregnancies resulting from sexual violence and/or rape;  
f. provision of further referral services in accordance with the victim's circumstances and condition; and  
g. provision of special rooms separated from general patients.

#### Article 71

c. psychological therapy and social rehabilitation programs;  
d. mental and spiritual guidance;  
e. assistance; and
f. provision of conducive and representative places for implementing psychosocial recovery service activities.  
2. The Regional Apparatus Organization responsible for social affairs is obligated to provide psychosocial recovery services.

a. ensuring children are accompanied by legal counsel; and.

2. The Regional Apparatus Organization responsible for women's empowerment and child protection affairs is obligated to provide or coordinate legal assistance services with relevant agencies.

#### Article 73

a. tracing family members;  
b. reuniting children with family members/substitute families and/or the community;

i. carrying out promotive, preventive, curative and rehabilitative efforts in accordance with Laws and Regulations.

## PROHIBITIONS

#### Article 98

o. recruiting or exploiting children for military and/or other interests, and leaving children without protection of life;

r. intentionally placing, allowing, involving, ordering the involvement of children in the abuse, as well as production and distribution of alcohol and other addictive substances.

## PROVISIONS ON INVESTIGATION

#### Article 99

1. Certain Civil Servant Officials within the Regional Government are given special authority as investigators to conduct investigations into criminal acts regulated in this Regional Regulation as referred to in the Criminal Procedure Code.  
2. The investigative authority as referred to in paragraph (1), is as follows:  
a. receiving, seeking, collecting, and examining information or reports concerning criminal acts of child and women protection so that such information or reports become more complete and clear;  
b. examining, seeking, and collecting information regarding individuals or entities about the truth of acts committed in connection with criminal acts of child and women protection;  
c. requesting information and evidence from individuals or entities in connection with criminal acts of child and women protection;  
d. examining books, records, and other documents concerning criminal acts of child and women protection;  
e. conducting searches to obtain evidence of bookkeeping, recording, and other documents, as well as conducting seizures of such evidence;  
f. requesting assistance from experts in the framework of carrying out investigation duties for criminal acts of child and women protection;  
g. ordering to stop and/or prohibiting someone from leaving a room or place when an examination is in progress and examining the identity of persons, objects, and/or documents carried;  
h. photographing persons related to criminal acts of child and women protection;  
i. summoning persons to be heard for their statements and examined as suspects or witnesses;  
j. terminating the investigation; and/or  
k. taking other actions necessary for the smooth investigation of criminal acts of child and women protection in accordance with the provisions of Laws and Regulations.  
3. In the event that the investigation meets the elements as referred to in paragraph (1), the Investigator notifies the commencement of the investigation and submits the results of the investigation to the Public Prosecutor through the Investigator Official of the Police of the Republic of Indonesia, in accordance with the provisions stipulated in the Criminal Procedure Code.

# CHAPTER XVIII

## PROVISIONS ON SANCTIONS

#### Article 100

1. Institutions or bodies designated by the Mayor to carry out service duties that violate service principles may be subject to administrative sanctions.  
2. Perpetrators of acts of violence are subject to criminal sanctions in accordance with applicable Laws.  
3. Every person who intentionally does not provide protection to women and children resulting in the occurrence of violence, allows violence to occur, does not report and does not provide protection to victims, is subject to sanctions as stipulated in the applicable Laws and Regulations.

# ELUCIDATION OF REGIONAL REGULATION OF SINGKAWANG CITY

NUMBER 4 YEAR 2017 CONCERNING PROTECTION OF CHILDREN AND WOMEN

##### I. GENERAL

The implementation of protection for children and women is carried out based on the principles of humanity, integration and justice. Therefore, every person must receive their right to protection for a sense of security and freedom from torture or treatment that degrades human dignity which is contrary to what is aspired to in the Preamble of the 1945 Constitution of the Republic of Indonesia, namely the realization of a just and civilized humanity.

In Article 2 of Law Number 23 of 2002 concerning Child Protection, the implementation of Child Protection is based on Pancasila and grounded in the 1945 Constitution of the Republic of Indonesia as well as the basic principles of the Convention on the Rights of the Child including:
a. non-discrimination;
b. the best interests of the child;
c. the right to life, survival, and development; and d. respect for the views of the child.

In Article 3 of Law Number 23 of 2004 concerning the Elimination of Domestic Violence, the elimination of domestic violence is implemented based on the principles of:
a. respect for human rights;
b. justice and gender equality,
c. non-discrimination; and
d. protection of victims.

In Article 3 of Law Number 23 of 2002 concerning Child Protection, Child Protection aims to ensure the fulfillment of children's rights so that they can live, grow, develop, and participate optimally in accordance with human dignity and worth, and receive protection from JDIH Kota Singkawang violence and discrimination, for the realization of quality Indonesian children who are virtuous and prosperous.

In Article 4 of Law Number 23 of 2004 concerning the Elimination of Domestic Violence, the elimination of domestic violence aims to: a. prevent all forms of violence in the household;
b. protect victims of domestic violence;
c. prosecute perpetrators of domestic violence; and
d. maintain the integrity of a harmonious and prosperous household.
To realize law enforcement and the fulfillment of the basic rights of children and women, the Government of Singkawang City is obliged to provide protection from acts of violence and discrimination as well as the fulfillment of civil rights and freedoms as set forth in a policy at the regional level.

The establishment of a Regional Regulation on the Protection of Children and Women is an elaboration of higher legislation that regulates the rights of children and women. In the Regional Regulation on the Protection of Children and Women, the norms formulated are efforts to address the problems faced in the implementation of child and women protection in Singkawang City.
The implementation of child and women protection is carried out to realize the fulfillment of the rights of children and women in accordance with human dignity and worth, and to receive protection from violence, discrimination, exploitation, and neglect for the realization of human resource development in Singkawang City that is faithful, intelligent, quality, virtuous and prosperous.
