# CHAPTER I

### GENERAL PROVISIONS

## Part One

Definitions

#### Article 1

In this Regional Regulation, what is meant by:  
1. Regency Area is the Regency Area of Sumedang.  
2. Regency Regional Government is the Regent as an element of regional government administration who leads the implementation of government affairs that are the authority of the autonomous region.  
3. Regency Regional Apparatus is an auxiliary element of the Regent and the Regional House of Representatives in the administration of government affairs that are the authority of the Region.  
4. Regent is the Regent of Sumedang.  
5. Regional Secretary is the Regional Secretary of Sumedang Regency.
6. Gender is the values, roles, and responsibilities of women and men that are socially and culturally constructed by society.  
7. Gender Perspective is a point of view to understand and analyze the values, roles, and responsibilities of women and men that are socially and culturally constructed by society.  
8. Gender Responsive is consistent and systematic attention to respond to the needs, problems, aspirations, and experiences of women and men aimed at realizing Gender Equality.  
9. Gender Responsive Planning and Budgeting hereinafter abbreviated as PPRG is a systematic effort to integrate Gender into planning and budgeting.  
10. Gender Mainstreaming hereinafter abbreviated as PUG is a strategy to realize Gender Equality, through the integration of Gender Perspective into regional development.  
11. Gender Equality is an equal condition and position between women and men to obtain their rights as human beings through a fair process in accessing and controlling resources, actively participating, and obtaining benefits from regional development.  
12. Disaggregated Data is data by sex, status and condition of women and men in all fields of Development including health, education, economy and employment, political field, legal field, social field, cultural field, decision making and violence.  
13. Access is the capacity to use resources to fully participate actively and productively (socially, economically and politically) in society including Access to resources, services, labor and employment, information and benefits.  
14. Participation is the involvement of a person/group both physically, mentally and spiritually in an activity and or in decision making.  
15. Benefit is the usefulness of resources that can be enjoyed optimally!
16. Village is a legal community unit that has territorial boundaries authorized to regulate and manage government affairs, the interests of the local community based on community initiative, rights of origin and/or traditional rights that are recognized and respected in the Government System of the Unitary State of the Republic of Indonesia.  
17. Women's Empowerment is an effort to obtain access and control over economic, political, social, and cultural resources, so that women can organize themselves and increase self-confidence to be able to play a role and actively participate in solving problems, so as to be able to build capacity and self-concept.  
18. Women's Protection is all efforts aimed at protecting and providing a sense of security to women and fulfilling their rights through consistent, structured, and systematic attention aimed at achieving Gender Equality.  
19. Quality of Life of Women is an effort to improve the physical and mental condition of women in fulfilling their rights and life needs as part of human rights from various fields of development, especially education, health, economy, science and technology, socio-culture, politics, law, and the environment.  
20. Institutionalization of PUG is the process of institutionalizing PUG into a sustainable system to achieve equality between women and men at all levels of government.  
21. PUG Working Group hereinafter referred to as Pokja PUG is a consultation forum for implementers and movers of PUG from various agencies and institutions.  
22. Gender-Based Violence hereinafter abbreviated as KBG is a violation of human rights caused by roles and labeling based on sex, which denies human dignity and the right to oneself that has an impact on, or has an impact similar to physical, psychological, and sexual or brings suffering to women and children including all forms of actions, coercion, arbitrariness and deprivation of freedom, carried out in the public sphere or private life.  
23. Child is a person who is not yet 18 (eighteen) years old, including a child who is still in the womb.  
24. Child's Rights are part of human rights that must be guaranteed, protected, and fulfilled by Parents, Family, community, state, government, and regional government.  
25. Fulfillment of Child's Rights is an effort to fulfill the human rights of Children by taking legislative, administrative, budgetary, legal actions, and other actions to ensure that Children obtain civil rights and freedoms, family environment and alternative care rights, basic health and welfare rights, as well as education rights, leisure time utilization, and cultural activities.  
26. Child Protection is all activities to guarantee and protect Children and their rights so that they can live, grow, develop, and participate optimally in accordance with human dignity and dignity, and receive protection from violence and discrimination.  
27. Special Protection is a form of protection received by Children in certain situations and conditions to obtain a guarantee of security against threats that endanger themselves and their lives in their development.  
28. Child-Friendly Regency hereinafter abbreviated as KLA is a Regency Area with a development system that guarantees the Fulfillment of Child's Rights and Special Protection of Children carried out in a planned, comprehensive, and sustainable manner.  
29. Children Requiring Special Protection hereinafter abbreviated as AMPK are Children in emergency situations, Children in conflict with the law, Children from minority and isolated groups, Children who are exploited economically and/or sexually, Children who are victims of narcotics, alcohol, psychotropic, and other addictive substance abuse, Children who are victims of pornography, Children with HIV/AIDS, Children victims of kidnapping, sale, and/or trafficking, Children victims of physical and/or psychological violence, Children victims of sexual crimes, Children victims of terrorism networks, Children with disabilities, Children victims of abuse and neglect, Children with deviant social behavior, and Children who are victims of stigmatization from labeling related to their parents' condition, and Children with other problems.  
30. Gender Data is data regarding the relationship of relations in status, roles, and conditions between men and women.  
31. Gender and Child Data System is the institutionalization of the implementation of Gender and Child Data consisting of components of regulations, institutions, and mechanisms in ministries/agencies and regions in the context of planning, implementation, evaluation, and reporting of results of policies/programs/activities of Gender Responsive and Child-caring development.  
32. Family is the smallest unit in society consisting of husband and wife, or husband and wife and their children, or father and his children, or mother and her children, or blood relatives in a straight line up or down to the third degree.  
33. Family Quality is the condition of the Family determined by the dimensions of quality of legality and structure, quality of physical resilience, quality of economic resilience, quality of socio-psychological resilience, and quality of socio-cultural resilience in realizing Gender Equality and Child's Rights.  
34. Violence is any action that results in misery or suffering physically, sexually, mentally, psychologically, including intimidation, forced eviction, threats of certain actions, coercion or deprivation of freedom, neglect and preventing the ability to enjoy all rights and freedoms.  
35. Violence Against Women hereinafter abbreviated as KTP is any action based on gender differences that results in or may result in misery or suffering of women physically, sexually, and psychologically, including threats of certain actions, arbitrary deprivation of freedom, whether occurring in the private or public sphere.  
36. Violence Against Children hereinafter abbreviated as KTA is any act against a Child that results in misery or suffering physically, psychologically, sexually, and/or neglect, including threats to commit acts, coercion, or unlawful deprivation of freedom.  
37. Domestic Violence hereinafter abbreviated as KDRT is any act against a person, especially women, that results in misery or suffering physically, sexually, psychologically, and/or household neglect including threats to commit acts, coercion, or unlawful deprivation of freedom within the household sphere.  
38. Discrimination is any restriction, harassment, or exclusion that is directly or indirectly based on human differentiation on the basis of religion, ethnicity, race, ethnic group, group, class, social status, economic status, gender, language, political beliefs, which results in reduction, deviation or elimination of recognition, implementation or use of human rights and fundamental freedoms in life both individually and collectively in the political, economic, legal, social, cultural and other aspects of life.  
39. Exploitation is an action with or without the victim's consent which includes but is not limited to prostitution, forced labor or service, slavery or practices similar to slavery, oppression, extortion, utilization of physical, sexual, reproductive organs, or unlawfully transferring or transplanting organs and/or body tissues or utilizing the labor or ability of a person by another party to obtain benefits both material and immaterial.  
40. Witness and/or Victim is a witness who is also a victim who experiences psychological, mental, physical, sexual, economic, and/or social suffering, caused by the criminal act of human trafficking.  
41. Parents are biological father and/or mother, or stepfather and/or stepmother, or adoptive father and/or adoptive mother.  
42. Guardian is a person or body who in fact exercises custody as a Parent over a Child.  
43. Business World is micro enterprises, small enterprises, medium enterprises, and large enterprises that conduct economic activities in Indonesia and are domiciled in Indonesia.  
44. Community is individuals, families, groups, mass media, business world, and social organizations and/or community organizations.  
45. Community Participation is the participation of community members to channel their aspirations, thoughts, and interests in the administration of Regional Government.  
46. Community Organization hereinafter referred to as Ormas is an organization established and formed by the community voluntarily based on common aspirations, will, needs, interests, activities, and goals to participate in development for the achievement of the objectives of the Unitary State of the Republic of Indonesia based on Pancasila and the 1945 Constitution of the Republic of Indonesia.  
47. Social Conflict hereinafter referred to as Conflict is a feud and/or physical clash with Violence between two or more community groups that lasts for a certain time and has a broad impact resulting in insecurity and social disintegration thus disrupting national stability and hindering national development.  
48. Regional Technical Implementation Unit for Women and Child Protection hereinafter abbreviated as UPTD PPA is a regional technical implementation unit established by the Regional Government in providing services for women and Children who experience Violence, discrimination, special protection, and other problems.  
49. Children's Forum is a forum for Children's Participation where its members are representatives of children's groups or children's activity groups or individuals, and are fostered by the government, as a means of channeling the aspirations, voices, opinions, desires, and needs of Children in the development process.  
50. Family Learning Center hereinafter referred to as Puspaga is a place of learning to improve the quality of family life carried out by professional personnel through increasing the capacity of Parents/Families or persons responsible for children in carrying out their responsibilities to care for and protect children so as to create the need for affection, attachment, safety, and welfare that is stable and sustainable in the best interests of the child, including protection from Violence, exploitation, abuse and neglect.  
51. Women's Empowerment Service Provider Institution is a government-owned institution or legal community institution that makes efforts to improve the quality of life and active Participation of women in development, both in the implementation of policies, activities, and administration in service delivery.  
52. Service Provider Institution for Children Requiring Special Protection hereinafter referred to as Service Provider Institution for AMPK is a government-owned institution or legal community institution that provides services for AMPK and other problems both in the implementation of policies, activities, and administration.  
53. Service Provider Institution for Improving the Quality of Life of Children is a government-owned institution or legal community institution that makes efforts to improve the quality of life of Children both in the implementation of policies, activities, and administration in service delivery.  
54. Service Provider Institution for Improving Family Quality is a government-owned institution or legal community institution that makes efforts to improve Family Quality both in the implementation of policies, activities, and administration in service delivery.  
55. Service Standard is a benchmark and guideline for the implementation of public services as well as a reference for assessing service quality as an obligation and promise of the organizer to the community in the context of services quickly, accurately, comprehensively and integrated.  
56. Social Rehabilitation is the recovery of witnesses and/or victims from psychosocial condition disorders and the restoration of normal social functioning both in the family and in the community.  
57. Repatriation is the act of returning witnesses and/or victims to their area of origin or country of origin while prioritizing protection services and fulfillment of their needs
58. Social Reintegration is the process of preparing Children who require Special Protection to be able to return to the family and community environment.  
59. Regional Long-Term Development Plan hereinafter abbreviated as RPJPD is a regional development planning document with a period of 20 (twenty) years, which contains the vision, mission, and direction of regional development that refers to the National Long-Term Development Plan and the West Java Provincial Long-Term Development Plan.  
60. Regional Medium-Term Development Plan hereinafter abbreviated as RPJMD is a Regional planning document for a period of 5 (five) years starting from inauguration until the end of the Regent's term of office.  
61. Regional Annual Development Plan hereinafter referred to as Regional Government Work Plan hereinafter abbreviated as RKPD is a Regional planning document for a period of 1 (one) year.  
62. Regional Apparatus Strategic Plan hereinafter referred to as Renstra is a Regional Apparatus planning document for a period of 5 (five) years.  
63. Regional Apparatus Work Plan hereinafter referred to as Renja is a Regional Apparatus planning document for a period of 1 (one) year.

## Part Two Principles

#### Article 2

The Implementation of Women's Empowerment and Child Protection is carried out based on the principles of:  
a. respect for human rights;  
b. justice:c. substantive equality;  
d. Participatory:e. non-discrimination;  
f. best interests of the Child;  
g. good governance; and  
h. sustainability.

## Part Three 

Intent and Purpose

#### Article 3

This Regional Regulation is intended as a reference or guideline in governance, development, and community services in the implementation of Women's Empowerment and Child Protection in the Regency Area.

#### Article 4

This Regional Regulation aims to:  
a. realize Women's Empowerment and Child Protection in the Regency Area that is effective, efficient, optimal, transparent, and accountable, as well as administratively orderly;  
b. realize Gender Responsive development by integrating Gender Perspective into planning, budgeting, implementation, monitoring, evaluation, supervision, and reporting on policies, programs, activities and sub-activities of development in the Regency Area;  
c. realize Gender Equality in family, community, national and state life;  
d. realize Gender Responsive Regional budget management;  
e. support efforts to increase the capacity of forums, institutions and/or organizations for Women's Empowerment and Child Protection;  
f. increase the synergy of Regency Regional Government and the community in the development of Gender Responsive Women's Empowerment and Child Protection; and  
g. increase Community Participation in the implementation of Women's Empowerment and Child Protection in the Regency Area.

## Part Four 

Scope

#### Article 5

The scope of this Regional Regulation regulates:  
a. Quality of Life of Women;  
b. Protection of Women;  
c. Protection of Children;  
d. Improvement of Family Quality;  
e. Gender and Child Data System;  
f. cooperation;  
g. Community Participation;  
h. coordination;  
i. monitoring;  
j. reporting;  
k. awards;  
l. funding; and  
m. sanctions.

# CHAPTER II 

### QUALITY OF LIFE OF WOMEN

## Part One

General

#### Article 6

1. The Regency Regional Government has the authority to implement Quality of Life of Women in the Regency Area.  
2. The implementation of Quality of Life of Women in the Regency Area as referred to in paragraph (1), is carried out by the Regency Regional Apparatus that administers governmental affairs in the field of Women's Empowerment and Child Protection.

## Part Two 

Implementation

### Paragraph 1 General

#### Article 7

The implementation of Quality of Life of Women in the Regency Area as referred to in Article 6 paragraph (1), consists of:  
a. implementation of PUG in the Regency Area;  
b. Institutionalization of PUG within the Regency Regional Apparatus;  
c. Women's Empowerment in the political, legal, social, and economic fields in Mass Organizations in the Regency Area;  
d. strengthening and development of Women's Empowerment Service Provider Institutions in the Regency Area; and  
e. Capacity Building of Women in the Regency Area.

### Paragraph 2  

Implementation of PUG in the Regency Area

#### Article 8

1. The Regent is responsible for the implementation of PUG in the Regency Area as referred to in Article 7 letter a.  
2. The responsibility of the Regent as referred to in paragraph (1), may be delegated to the Vice Regent.  
3. In the implementation of PUG in the Regency Area as referred to in paragraph (1), the Regent appoints the Regency Regional Apparatus that administers governmental affairs in the field of Women's Empowerment and Child Protection as the coordinator of the implementation of PUG in the Regency Area.  
4. Further provisions regarding the implementation of PUG in the Regency Area as referred to in paragraph (1), are regulated by a separate Regional Regulation.

### Paragraph 3  

Institutionalization of PUG within the Regency Regional Apparatus

#### Article 9

1. Institutionalization of PUG within the Regency Regional Apparatus as referred to in Article 7 letter b, is carried out through:  
a. fulfillment of PUG Institutionalization standards;  
b. integration of Gender Perspective;  
c. preparation of planning and budgeting; and  
d. advocacy, facilitation, and provision of technical assistance.  
2. Integration of Gender Perspective as referred to in paragraph (1) letter b, is incorporated into the policies and development programs of the Regency Area.  
3. Policies and development programs of the Regency Area as referred to in paragraph (2), consist of:  
a. RPJPD;  
b. RPJMD;  
c. RKPD;  
d. Renstra; and  
e. Renja.  
4. Policies and development programs of the Regency Area as referred to in paragraph (2), include:  
a. planning;  
b. preparation;  
c. implementation;  
d. monitoring;  
e. control; and  
f. evaluation.  
5. Preparation of planning and budgeting as referred to in paragraph (1) letter c, by considering equal Access, Participation, control, and Benefits between women and men, which takes into account the aspirations, needs, and specific problems of women and men.  
6. Advocacy, facilitation and provision of technical assistance as referred to in paragraph (1) letter d, is directed to institutions that have legislative functions and vertical agencies in the Regency Area related to the implementation of PUG.

#### Article 10

1. Institutionalization of PUG within the Regency Regional Apparatus as referred to in Article 7 letter b, is carried out in accordance with standards and procedures.  
2. PUG Institutionalization standards as referred to in paragraph (1), consist of:  
a. commitment of the Regent;  
b. policies and programs;  
c. institutions;  
d. resources;  
e. Sex-Disaggregated Data and Gender statistics;  
f. implementation methods; and  
g. Community Participation.  
3. PUG Institutionalization procedures as referred to in paragraph (1) consist of:  
a. coordination and synchronization of policy formulation for PUG implementation in the Regency Area;  
b. coordination and synchronization of PUG implementation in the Regency Area;  
c. policy advocacy and assistance for PUG implementation in the Regency Area including PPRG under Regency Area authority; and  
d. socialization of policies for PUG implementation in the Regency Area including PPRG under Regency Area authority.  
4. Coordination and synchronization of policy formulation for PUG implementation in the Regency Area as referred to in paragraph (3) letter a, includes:  
a. harmonization and synchronization of laws and regulations, Regency Area legal products, and operational policies regarding PUG; and  
b. preparation of RPJPD, RPJMD, RKPD, Renstra, and Renja as well as Gender Responsive Regency Regional Apparatus budgets.  
5. Coordination and synchronization of PUG implementation in the Regency Area as referred to in paragraph (3) letter b, includes:  
a. establishment of PUG Working Group;  
b. preparation of annual and 5 (five) year PUG Working Group action plans;  
c. PUG Working Group coordination meetings; and  
d. integration of Sex-Disaggregated Data into the Gender and Child Data System.  
6. Policy advocacy and assistance for PUG implementation in the Regency Area as referred to in paragraph (3) letter c, includes:  
a. technical coordination meetings for PUG implementation;  
b. technical guidance and assistance for PPRG in the preparation of Gender Responsive programs, activities, and budgets; and  
c. facilitation in the preparation of Gender Responsive Regency Area policies.  
7. Socialization of policies for PUG implementation in the Regency Area as referred to in paragraph (3) letter d, includes:  
a. dissemination of policies for PUG and PPRG implementation to stakeholders; and  
b. strengthening of Community Participation in PUG implementation.

#### Article 11

1. The achievement of PUG Institutionalization within the Regency Regional Apparatus as referred to in Article 7 letter b, is measured by criteria.  
2. The criteria for achievement of PUG Institutionalization within the Regency Regional Apparatus as referred to in paragraph (1) is measured by the PUG index in accordance with the provisions of laws and regulations governing the PUG index.

#### Article 12

Further provisions regarding PUG Institutionalization within the Regency Regional Apparatus as referred to in Article 9 through Article 11, shall be regulated in a Regent Regulation.

### Paragraph 4

Women's Empowerment in the Political, Legal, Social, and Economic Fields in Community Organizations in the Regency

#### Article 13

Women's Empowerment in the political, legal, social, and economic fields in Community Organizations in the Regency as referred to in Article 7 letter c, shall be implemented through:  
a. improving the quality and quantity of women in the political, legal, social, and economic fields in Community Organizations; and  
b. involving women in decision-making in the political, legal, social, and economic fields in Community Organizations.

#### Article 14

1. Women's Empowerment in the political, legal, social, and economic fields in Community Organizations in the Regency as referred to in Article 7 letter c, shall be implemented in accordance with standards and procedures.  
2. The standards for Women's Empowerment in the political, legal, social, and economic fields in Community Organizations in the Regency as referred to in paragraph (1) include:  
a. institutional;  
b. resources;  
c. services and programs; and  
d. monitoring, evaluation, and reporting.  
3. The procedures for Women's Empowerment in the political, legal, social, and economic fields in Community Organizations in the Regency, as referred to in paragraph (1) consist of:  
a. socialization of increasing women's Participation in the political, legal, social, and economic fields in the Regency; and  
b. policy advocacy and assistance for increasing women's Participation in politics, law, social, and economic matters in the Regency.  
4. Socialization of increasing women's Participation in the political, legal, social, and economic fields in the Regency as referred to in paragraph (3) letter a, includes:  
a. the role of Community Organizations in Women's Empowerment;  
b. the role of women in politics;  
c. women's leadership;  
d. legal literacy for women;  
e. social literacy for women; and  
f. women's entrepreneurship.  
5. Advocacy … (5) Policy advocacy and assistance for increasing women's Participation in politics, law, social, and economic matters in the Regency, as referred to in paragraph (3) letter b, includes:  
a. strengthening policies on increasing the Participation of Community Organizations, as well as professional institutions and the Business World for Women's Empowerment;  
b. developing synergistic cooperation models between the government and Community Organizations as well as professional institutions and the Business World for solving Women's Empowerment issues;  
c. developing communication forums for Community Organizations as well as professional institutions and the Business World for Women's Empowerment;  
d. creating a database of Community Organizations and professional institutions as well as the Business World for Women's Empowerment; and  
e. conducting studies on the Participation of Community Organizations as well as professional institutions and the Business World for Women's Empowerment.

#### Article 15

1. The achievement of Women's Empowerment in the political, legal, social, and economic fields in Community Organizations in the Regency as referred to in Article 7 letter c is measured by criteria.  
2. The criteria for Women's Empowerment in the political, legal, social, and economic fields in Community Organizations in the Regency, as referred to in paragraph (1) consist of:a. the number of synergistic Community Organization activities for Women's Empowerment in the political, legal, social, and economic fields; and  
b. the amount of budget contribution from Community Organizations in the Regency for Women's Empowerment in the political, legal, social, and economic fields.

#### Article 16

Further provisions regarding Women's Empowerment in the political, legal, social, and economic fields in Community Organizations in the Regency as referred to in Article 13 through Article 15, shall be regulated in a Regent Regulation.

### Paragraph 5

Strengthening and Development of Women's Empowerment Service Provider Institutions in the Regency

#### Article 17

Strengthening and Development of Women's Empowerment Service Provider Institutions in the Regency as referred to in Article 7 letter d, shall be implemented through:  
a. increasing the capacity of Women's Empowerment Service Provider Institutions;  
b. increasing …b. increasing the capacity of human resources managing Women's Empowerment Service Provider Institutions;  
c. assisting the implementation of Women's Empowerment services;  
d. strengthening networks among Women's Empowerment Service Provider Institutions in the Regency;  
e. facilitating efforts to meet the standards of Women's Empowerment Service Provider Institutions; and  
f. developing communication, information, and education about Women's Empowerment services.

#### Article 18

1. Strengthening and development of Women's Empowerment Service Provider Institutions in the Regency as referred to in Article 7 letter d shall be implemented in accordance with standards and procedures.  
2. The standards for strengthening and development of Women's Empowerment Service Provider Institutions in the Regency as referred to in paragraph (1) include:  
a. institutional;  
b. resources;  
c. services and programs; and  
d. monitoring, evaluation, and reporting.  
3. The procedures for strengthening and development of Women's Empowerment Service Provider Institutions in the Regency as referred to in paragraph (1), consist of:  
a. policy advocacy and assistance to Women's Empowerment Service Provider Institutions in the Regency;  
b. capacity building of resources of Women's Empowerment Service Provider Institutions in the Regency; and  
c. development of communication, information, and education on Women's Empowerment in the Regency.

#### Article 19

# CHAPTER III PROTECTION OF WOMEN

## Part One General

#### Article 21

1. The Regency Regional Government has the authority to administer the protection of women's rights in the Regency.  
2. The administration of protection of women's rights in the Regency as referred to in paragraph (1), shall be implemented by the Regional Apparatus that administers governmental affairs in the field of Women's Empowerment and Child Protection.

## Part Two Fulfillment of Women's Rights

#### Article 22

1. Fulfillment of the rights of every Woman in the Regency Area, includes:  
a. the right to life;  
b. the right to form a family and continue descendants;  
c. the right to self-development;  
d. the right to obtain justice;  
e. the right to personal freedom;  
f. the right to security;  
g. the right to welfare; and  
h. the right to participate in government.  
2. The right to life as referred to in paragraph (1) letter a, includes:  
a. the right to maintain life and improve one's standard of living;  
b. the right to obtain peace of mind, security, tranquility, happiness, physical and spiritual welfare; and  
c. the right to a good and healthy environment.  
3. The right to form a family and continue descendants as referred to in paragraph (1) letter b, namely the right to form a Family and continue descendants through lawful marriage in accordance with religion and the provisions of laws and regulations governing marriage.  
4. The right to self-development as referred to in paragraph (1) letter c, includes:  
a. the right to fulfillment of basic needs to grow and develop properly;  
b. the right to protection for personal development, to obtain education, educate oneself and improve quality of life to become a faithful, pious, responsible, noble, happy, and prosperous human being in accordance with human rights;  
c. the right to develop and obtain Benefits from science and technology, arts and culture in accordance with human dignity for the welfare of oneself, the nation, and humanity;  
d. the right to communicate and obtain information necessary to develop oneself and one's social environment in accordance with religious, social, and legal norms;  
e. the right to seek, obtain, possess, store, process, and convey information using all types of available means in accordance with religious, social, and legal norms;  
f. the right to fight for the right to self-development, both individually and collectively, to build society, nation, and state in accordance with religious, social, and legal norms;  
g. the right to perform social work in accordance with religious, social, and legal norms;  
h. the right to participate in the planning and implementation of government policies in accordance with the provisions of laws and regulations;  
i. the right to organize and form organizations in accordance with the provisions of laws and regulations; and  
j. the right to organize education and teaching in accordance with religious, social, and legal norms.  
5. The right to obtain justice as referred to in paragraph (1) letter d, includes:  
a. the right to receive equal treatment without Discrimination;  
b. the right to obtain justice;  
c. the right to submit applications, complaints, and lawsuits, whether in criminal, civil, or administrative cases; and  
d. the right to be tried through a free and impartial judicial process, in accordance with procedural law that guarantees objective examination by honest and fair judges to obtain a just and correct decision.  
6. The right to personal freedom as referred to in paragraph (1) letter e, includes:  
a. the right not to be enslaved or subjugated, trafficked, and all acts with similar purposes;  
b. the right to personal integrity, both spiritual and physical;  
c. the right not to be the object of research without one's consent; and  
d. the right to freely express and convey opinions with due regard to religious values, morality, public order, public interest, and national integrity.  
7. The right to security as referred to in paragraph (1) letter f, includes:  
a. the right to protection of oneself, Family, honor, dignity, and property rights;  
b. the right to recognition before the law as a human person; and  
c. the right to be free from threats to do or not do something that violates religious, social, and legal norms.  
8. The right to welfare as referred to in paragraph (1) letter g, includes:  
a. the right to have welfare, either alone or together; and  
b. the right to perform work and receive fair wages in accordance with the provisions of laws and regulations.  
9. The right to participate in government as referred to in paragraph (1) letter h, includes:  
a. the right to be elected and to vote in general elections;  
b. the right to pursue a career in government; and  
c. the right to convey aspirations directly or through intermediaries in government.

## Part Four Protection of Women as Witnesses and/or Victims

#### Article 27

Protection of women as Witnesses and/or Victims is carried out for every Woman in the Regency Area including:  
a. women as witnesses and victims;  
b. women as victims of domestic violence; and  
c. women after divorce.

#### Article 28

1. Every woman in the Regency Area as a witness and victim as referred to in Article 27 letter a, has the right to:  
a. obtain protection for the security of herself, Family, and property, and be free from threats related to testimony that will be, is being, or has been given;

d. obtain an interpreter;

g. obtain information regarding court decisions;

j. obtain a new identity;  
k. obtain temporary residence;  
l. obtain a new residence;  
m. obtain reimbursement of transportation costs according to needs;  
n. obtain legal advice;  
o. obtain temporary living cost assistance up to the time limit when protection ends; and/or  
p. obtain assistance.  
2. Women in the Regency Area as victims of gross human rights violations, victims of terrorism crimes, victims of human trafficking crimes, victims of torture crimes, victims of sexual violence crimes, and victims of severe persecution, in addition to being entitled to the rights as referred to in paragraph (1), are also entitled to obtain:  
a. medical assistance;  
b. psychosocial and psychological rehabilitation assistance;  
c. compensation; and  
d. restitution.

a. compensation for loss of wealth or income;  
b. compensation arising from suffering directly related as a result of the crime; and/or  
c. reimbursement of medical and/or psychological treatment costs.

#### Article 29

a. protection from the Family, police, prosecutor's office, court, advocates, social institutions, or other parties either temporarily or based on a protection order from the court;  
b. health services according to medical needs;  
c. special handling related to victim confidentiality;  
d. assistance by social workers and legal aid at every level of the examination process in accordance with the provisions of laws and regulations; and  
e. spiritual guidance services.  
2. The granting of rights to women in the Regency Area as Victims of domestic violence as referred to in paragraph (1), is implemented in accordance with the provisions of laws and regulations governing the elimination of domestic Violence.

#### Article 30

Every woman in the Regency Area has the right to obtain protection after divorce in accordance with the provisions of laws and regulations.

## Part Three Fulfillment of Children's Rights

### Paragraph 1 General

#### Article 52

1. The Regency Regional Government has the obligation and responsibility in the implementation of the Fulfillment of Children's Rights.  
2. The Fulfillment of Children's Rights as referred to in paragraph (1), includes:  
a. the right to live, grow, develop, and participate reasonably in accordance with human dignity and dignity, and to receive protection from violence and Discrimination;  
b. the right to a name as self-identity and citizenship status;  
c. the right to worship according to their religion, think, and express themselves in accordance with their level of intelligence and age under the guidance of Parents or Guardians;  
d. the right to know their Parents, be raised, and be cared for by their own Parents;  
e. the right to be cared for or adopted as a foster Child or adopted Child by others in the event that for any reason the Parents cannot guarantee the growth and development of the Child, or the Child is in a state of neglect;  
f. the right to obtain health services and social security in accordance with physical, mental, spiritual, and social needs;  
g. the right to obtain education and teaching in the context of developing their personality and level of intelligence in accordance with their interests and talents;  
h. the right to receive protection in educational units from sexual crimes and Violence committed by educators, education personnel, fellow students, and/or other parties;  
i. the right to express and be heard their opinion, receive, seek, and provide information in accordance with their level of intelligence and age for their self-development in accordance with the values of decency and propriety;  
j. the right to rest and utilize leisure time, socialize with children of the same age, play, recreate, and create in accordance with their interests, talents, and level of intelligence for self-development;  
k. the right to receive protection from Discrimination, Exploitation, both economic and sexual, neglect, cruelty, violence, and abuse, injustice, and other mistreatment while in the care of Parents, Guardians, or any other party responsible for care;  
1. the right to be cared for by their own Parents, unless there are valid reasons and/or legal rules showing that the separation and/or divorce is in the best interests of the Child and is a last consideration;  
m. the right to meet directly and have regular personal contact with both Parents, receive care, maintenance, education and protection for the growth and development process from both Parents in accordance with their abilities, talents, and interests, obtain living expenses from both Parents, and obtain other Children's Rights, in the event of separation and/or divorce as referred to in letter 1;  
n. the right to obtain protection from abuse in political activities, involvement in armed conflicts, involvement in social unrest, involvement in events containing elements of Violence, involvement in warfare, and sexual crimes;  
o. the right to obtain protection from being the target of persecution, torture, or imposition of inhuman punishment, and to obtain freedom in accordance with the law;  
p. the right to be treated humanely and placed separately from adults, obtain legal assistance or other assistance effectively at every stage of applicable legal efforts, and defend themselves and obtain justice before an objective and impartial juvenile court in a closed session to the public, in the case of a Child who is deprived of liberty;  
q. the right to confidentiality in the case of a Child who is a victim or perpetrator of sexual Violence or who is in conflict with the law;  
r. the right to receive legal assistance and other assistance in the event that a child becomes a victim or perpetrator of a criminal act; and  
s. the right to receive protection and maintenance after divorce.  
2. The implementation of the Fulfillment of Children's Rights in the Regency as referred to in paragraph (1) and paragraph (2), is carried out in accordance with the provisions of laws and regulations governing the protection of Children.

## Part Six Special Protection of Children

### Paragraph 1 General

#### Article 61

Special Protection of Children in the Regency Area is the obligation and responsibility of the Regency Regional Government.

#### Article 62

1. Special Protection of Children in the Regency Area as referred to in Article 61, is provided to:  
a. Children in emergency situations;  
b. Children in conflict with the law;  
c. Children from minority and isolated groups;  
d. Children who are exploited economically and/or sexually;  
e. Children who are victims of narcotics, alcohol, psychotropic, and other addictive substance abuse;  
f. Children who are victims of pornography;  
g. Children with HIV and AIDS;  
h. Children victims of kidnapping, sale, and/or trafficking;  
i. Children victims of bullying;  
j. Children victims of sexual crimes;  
k. Children victims of terrorism networks;  
l. Children with disabilities;  
m. Children victims of abuse and neglect;  
n. Children with deviant social behavior; and  
o. Children who are victims of stigmatization from labeling related to their parents' condition.  
2. Special Protection of Children in the Regency Area as referred to in paragraph (1), is carried out through efforts to:  
a. rapid handling, including treatment and/or rehabilitation physically, psychologically, and socially, as well as prevention of diseases and other health disorders;  
b. psychosocial assistance during treatment until recovery;  
c. provision of social assistance for Children from underprivileged Families; and  
d. provision of protection and assistance in every judicial process.  
3. Special Protection of Children in the Regency Area as referred to in paragraph (1) is provided at UPTD/UPTD PPA and Puspaga.  
4. Special Protection of Children in the Regency Area as referred to in paragraph (1) is implemented quickly, comprehensively, and in an integrated manner.

#### Article 63

In addition to being obligated and responsible for implementing Special Protection of Children in the Regency Area as referred to in Article 61 and Article 62, the Regency Regional Government is obligated to organize:  
a. protection of Children in Conflict;  
b. protection of Children as Witnesses and/or Victims;  
c. Protection of Children from acts of KGB in disasters; and  
d. protection of Children with disabilities.

#### Article 64

The provisions regarding the protection of Women in Conflict as referred to in Article 23 through Article 25 apply mutatis mutandis to the protection of children in Conflict as referred to in Article 63 letter a.

#### Article 65

The provisions regarding the protection of Women as Witnesses and/or Victims as referred to in Article 27 through Article 29 apply mutatis mutandis to the protection of children as Witnesses and/or Victims as referred to in Article 63 letter b.

#### Article 66

The provisions regarding the protection of women from acts of KGB in Disasters as referred to in Article 31 and Article 32 apply mutatis mutandis to the protection of children from acts of KGB in Disasters as referred to in Article 63 letter c.

#### Article 67

The provisions regarding the rights of women with Disabilities as referred to in Article 34 paragraph (2), apply mutatis mutandis to the rights of children with disabilities.

#### Article 68

In addition to obtaining rights as persons with disabilities as referred to in Article 34 paragraph (2) and Article 67, Children with disabilities have the right to:  
a. obtain Special Protection from Discrimination, neglect, harassment, Exploitation, as well as violence and sexual crimes;  
b. obtain care and upbringing from Family or substitute Family to grow and develop optimally;  
c. have their interests protected in decision-making;  
d. treatment of Children in a humane manner in accordance with the dignity and Rights of Children;  
e. fulfillment of special needs;  
f. equal treatment with other Children to achieve social integration and individual development; and  
g. obtain social assistance.

#### Article 69

The Regency Regional Government in implementing the organization of Special Protection of Children in the Regency Area as referred to in Article 61, provides:  
a. social workers and social welfare personnel;  
b. competent and trained health personnel;  
c. spiritual/worship guidance officers;  
d. educators and education personnel; and/or e. legal aid personnel.

##### I. GENERAL

Women and children have an important and strategic role in efforts to support development and the administration of regional government, however in reality the position of women and children in social, national and state life is still positioned as a group vulnerable to various forms of violence and/or threats of violence as well as other discriminatory treatment.

Women's Empowerment and Child Protection constitute the main pillars in efforts to improve the quality of reliable human resources in the future, by instilling and implementing the values of gender equality, and the implementation of the fulfillment of the rights of women and children, as well as enhancing the role and position of women and children in efforts to support the administration of government and development in the Region.

In the context of administering regional government affairs, women's empowerment and child protection, and improvement of family quality in the Sumedang Regency Area, is essentially one of the implementations of the administration of concurrent government affairs which are the authority of the Sumedang Regency Area in the field of Women's Empowerment and Child Protection.

The Policy on Women's Empowerment and Protection, Child Protection, and Family Quality Improvement in the Sumedang Regency Area is implemented by involving and/or being supported by all components of society and stakeholders in the Sumedang Regency Area from various sectors of community life, and every program and activity is carried out comprehensively, in an integrated, sustainable, and continuous manner, as well as in synergy with national policies and programs in the field of women's empowerment and protection, child protection and family quality improvement.

To provide guarantees of legal certainty and legal protection as well as to realize effectiveness, efficiency, optimization, accountability, transparency, and administrative order as well as to serve as a guideline in the implementation of women's empowerment and child protection in the Sumedang Regency Area, it is necessary to regulate and stipulate by Regional Regulation.

#### Article 62

Paragraph (1)
Point a
What is meant by "Children in Emergency Situations" are Children who are in environmental situations that threaten and disrupt the life and livelihood of Children caused by natural, non-natural, and/or social factors.

Point b
What is meant by "Children in Conflict with the Law" are Children in conflict with the law, Children who are victims of criminal acts, and Children who are witnesses to criminal acts.

Point c
What is meant by "Children from Minority and Isolated Groups" are Children who are left behind, at the forefront, outermost in environments with different cultures, traditions, ethnicities, races, religions from other children whose numbers are far fewer than Children of other groups.

Point d
What is meant by "Children Who Are Economically Exploited" are Children who are victims of acts of prostitution, forced labor or services, slavery or practices similar to slavery, oppression, extortion, exploitation of physical, sexual, reproductive organs, or unlawfully transferring or transplanting organs and/or body tissues or exploiting the labor or abilities of Children by other parties or other similar actions to obtain material benefits.

What is meant by "Children Who Are Sexually Exploited" are Children who are exploited to obtain benefits through sexual organs or other body organs of Children, including but not limited to all prostitution and indecency activities.

Point e
What is meant by "Children Who Are Victims of Narcotics, Alcohol, Psychotropic, and Other Addictive Substances Abuse" are Children who are persuaded, deceived, tricked, forced, and/or threatened to use narcotics, alcohol, psychotropics, and other addictive substances.

Point f
What is meant by "Children Who Are Victims of Pornography" are Children who experience trauma or suffering as a result of pornography crimes.

Point g
What is meant by "Children with HIV and AIDS" are Children who are infected with HIV and/or AIDS either transmitted from parents or from other risk factors.

Point h
What is meant by "Child Victims of Kidnapping" are Children who are taken by someone unlawfully with the intention of placing the Child under their power or the power of others or to place the Child in a helpless state.

What is meant by "Child Victims of Sale" are Children who are transferred by a person or group of people to other parties for compensation or other reasons.

What is meant by "Child Victims of Trafficking" are Children who experience psychological, mental, physical, sexual, economic, and/or social suffering resulting from acts of recruitment, transportation, harboring, sending, transfer, or receipt with threats of violence, use of violence, kidnapping, confinement, forgery, fraud, abuse of power or vulnerable position, debt bondage or giving payment or benefits, so as to obtain consent from the person holding control over that other person, whether conducted within the country or internationally, for the purpose of exploitation or resulting in the Child being exploited.

Point i
What is meant by "Child Victims of Physical Violence" are Children who experience violence that causes pain, illness, or serious injury.

What is meant by "Child Victims of Psychological Violence" are Children who experience fear, loss of self-confidence, loss of ability to act, feelings of helplessness, and/or severe psychological suffering.

Point j
What is meant by "Child Victims of Sexual Crimes" are Children who experience forced sexual intercourse, forced sexual intercourse in an unnatural and/or unwanted manner, and forced sexual intercourse with others for commercial purposes and/or specific purposes.

Point k
What is meant by "Child Victims of Terrorism Networks" are Children who experience physical, mental suffering, and/or economic loss caused by terrorism crimes whether as child victims, child perpetrators, children of perpetrators, and child witnesses.

Point 1
What is meant by "Children with Disabilities" are Children who experience physical, intellectual, mental, and/or sensory limitations over a long period of time who in interacting with the environment may experience obstacles and difficulties to participate fully and effectively based on equal rights.

Point m
What is meant by "Child Victims of Maltreatment" are Children who are threatened physically and non-physically due to acts of violence, treated wrongly or improperly in the family environment or closest social environment, so that their basic needs are not properly fulfilled either physically, spiritually, or socially.

Point n
What is meant by "Child Victims of Maltreatment" are Children who are threatened physically and non-physically due to acts of violence, treated wrongly or improperly in the family environment or closest social environment, so that their basic needs are not properly fulfilled either physically, spiritually, or socially.

What is meant by Child Victims of Neglect are Children who do not receive fulfillment of needs such as but not limited to health, security, and welfare needs from parents or other persons who have legal responsibility to care for the Child resulting in harm to the Child's growth and development process.

Point o
What is meant by "Children Who Are Victims of Stigmatization from Labeling related to Their Parents' Condition" are Children who are given negative social labels based on prejudice and aimed at separating, differentiating, discrediting, and ostracizing Children with bad marks or views from labeling related to their parents' condition.
