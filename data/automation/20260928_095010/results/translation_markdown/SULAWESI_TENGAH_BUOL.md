BY THE GRACE OF ALMIGHTY GOD

REGENT OF BUOL,

Considering:
 
 
 
 
 
 
 
a. that every citizen has equal rights to live a dignified life in accordance with the principles of humanity, equality, and justice;
b. that to guarantee women's empowerment and child protection in accordance with human dignity and to receive protection from violence, discrimination and other violations, women's empowerment and child protection must be carried out in an integrated and sustainable manner through the acceleration of fulfillment and protection of the rights of women and children in their personal lives, family, community, nation and state;
c. that in accordance with the provisions of Article 12 paragraph (2) letter b of Law Number 23 of 2014 concerning Regional Government as amended several times most recently by Law Number 9 of 2015 concerning the Second Amendment to Law Number 2014 concerning Regional Government, women's empowerment and child protection is one of the mandatory government affairs that is the authority of the Regional Government;
d. that based on the considerations as referred to in letter a, letter b, and letter c, it is necessary to establish a Regional Regulation concerning Women's Empowerment and Child Protection;

# CHAPTER I GENERAL PROVISIONS

#### Article 1

In this Regional Regulation, what is meant by:  
1. Region is Buol Regency.  
2. Regional Government is the head of the Region as an element of the Regional Government administration who leads the implementation of Government Affairs that are the authority of the autonomous Region.  
3. Regent is the Regent of Buol Regency.  
4. Regional Apparatus hereinafter abbreviated as PD is the Regional Apparatus within the Government of Buol Regency.  
5. Women's empowerment is an effort to enable women to obtain access and control over resources, economic, political, social, and cultural, so that women can manage themselves and increase self-confidence to be able to play a role and actively participate in solving problems, so as to be able to build capacity and self-concept.  
6. Protection of women is all efforts to guarantee and protect the rights of women so that they can live healthily and participate optimally in accordance with human dignity and receive protection from neglect, violence, and discrimination, whether in the form of preventive, repressive or post-remedial protection (assistance and treatment).  
7. Child protection is all activities to guarantee and protect children and their rights so that they can live, grow, develop and participate optimally in accordance with human dignity, and receive protection from violence and discrimination.  
8. Child is a person who has not reached the age of 18 (eighteen) years, including children who are still in the womb and are not married.  
9. Prevention is a direct effort carried out by the Regional Government, community and parents and/or family to prevent violations of the rights of women and children.  
10. Violence is any act that results in misery or suffering physically, sexually, psychologically and/or domestic neglect including threats to commit acts, coercion, or deprivation of a person's freedom unlawfully.  
11. Children's forum is a forum for children's participation formed with the aim of facilitating children's involvement in the decision-making process and enjoying changes relating to their lives either directly or indirectly, which is carried out with the consent and will of all children based on awareness and understanding, in accordance with age and level of thinking maturity.  
12. Child-Friendly Regency hereinafter abbreviated as KLA is a Regency that has a child rights-based development system through the integration of government commitment and resources, community and business world that is planned comprehensively and sustainably in policies, programs and activities to ensure the fulfillment of children's rights.  
13. Neglected child is a child whose needs are not properly fulfilled, whether physical, mental, spiritual or social.  
14. Social welfare is a condition of fulfillment of material, spiritual, and social needs of citizens so that they can live properly and are able to develop themselves, so that they can carry out their social functions.  
15. Gender mainstreaming hereinafter abbreviated as PUG is a strategy built to integrate gender into an integral dimension of planning, formulation, implementation, monitoring and evaluation of national development program policies.  
16. Community is individuals, families, groups and social organizations and/or community organizations.  
17. Women victims of violence are women who experience actions that result in misery or suffering physically, sexually or psychologically, including threats of certain actions, coercion or deprivation of freedom arbitrarily, whether occurring in the public sphere or in private life.  
18. Child victims of violence are children who experience actions that result in misery or suffering physically, mentally, sexually, psychologically or suffering that threatens life, body and degrades human dignity.  
19. Human trafficking is the act of recruitment, transportation, harboring, sending, transfer, or receipt of a person by threat of violence, use of violence, kidnapping, confinement, forgery, fraud, abuse of power or vulnerable position, debt bondage or giving payment or benefits, so as to obtain consent from the person holding control over that other person, whether carried out within the Country or between Countries.  
20. Complaint handling is a series of actions carried out by integrated service providers to follow up on reports of acts of violence against women and children, submitted by victims, families or the community.  
21. Social rehabilitation is a service aimed at restoring and developing the ability of a person experiencing social dysfunction so that they can carry out their social functions properly.  
22. Law enforcement is the action of officials authorized by the state to implement statutory regulations.  
23. PUG Focal Point is an OPD apparatus who has the ability to carry out gender mainstreaming in their respective work units.  
24. Legal aid is legal services provided by legal assistants and advocates to carry out the process of assisting witnesses and/or victims of violence against women and children that is gender sensitive.  
25. Repatriation is an effort to return women and child victims of violence from abroad to the debarkation point/entry point, or from the receiving Region to the Region of origin.  
26. Social reintegration is an effort to reunite victims with family, substitute family, or community that can provide protection and fulfillment of needs for victims.  
27. Safe house is a temporary shelter that aims to provide protection and a sense of security to victims of violence.  
28. Shelter home is a forum for guidance and welfare services for street children that carries out skills assistance/guidance activities to ensure that children do not carry out activities on the streets so that they can grow and develop normally.  
29. Spiritual guidance is an effort to restore the soul and systematic towards differences between women and men in society accompanied by efforts to eliminate structural and cultural barriers in achieving gender equality.  
30. Gender responsive is consistent and systematic attention to the differences between women and men in society accompanied by efforts to eliminate structural and cultural barriers in achieving gender equality.  
31. Gender sensitivity or gender awareness is the ability to identify gaps in power relations between women and men in the family and in the community, the impact of gender-based division of labor on women and men, and the experiences, problems, needs, interests and aspirations of women and men are different.  
32. Public service provider is every state organizing institution, corporation, independent institution formed based on Law for public service activities, and other legal entities formed solely for public service activities.  
33. Integrated Service Center for Women's Empowerment and Children hereinafter abbreviated as P2TP2A is an integrated service center in efforts to empower women in various fields of development, as well as protection of women and children from various types of discrimination and acts of violence, including human trafficking, established by the government or community-based, which includes: referral center, business consultation center, reproductive health consultation center, integrated crisis center (PKT), trauma recovery center (trauma center), women's crisis handling center (women crisis center), training center, science and technology information center (PIPTEK), safe house (shelter), shelter home, children's friend telephone (TESSA) 129 or other forms.  
34. Community Organization is an organization established and formed by the community voluntarily based on common aspirations, will, needs, interests, activities and goals to participate in development for the achievement of the objectives of the Unitary State of the Republic of Indonesia based on Pancasila.  
35. Parents are biological father and/or mother, or step father and/or mother, or adoptive father and/or mother.  
36. Family is the smallest unit in society consisting of husband and wife or husband, wife and their children, or father and his children, or mother and her children, or blood relatives in a straight line upward or downward up to the third degree.  
37. Customs are local wisdom values that apply and are still applied from generation to generation in the community environment.  
38. Child-Friendly Regency Task Force is a coordinating institution at the regency level that coordinates policy efforts, programs and activities to realize a child-friendly regency.  
39. Regional Action Plan for Child-Friendly Regency Development is a document containing policies, programs and activities to realize a Child-Friendly Regency.

# CHAPTER II

## RIGHTS OF WOMEN AND CHILDREN

#### Article 2

Every woman and child has basic rights as a human being that must be protected, promoted, upheld and fulfilled in accordance with the provisions of laws and regulations.

#### Article 3

The rights of women as referred to in Article 2 include:  
a. living peacefully, safely, in peace, happily, prosperously physically and spiritually;  
b. enjoying a good and healthy living environment;  
c. living in a family within a legal marriage bond based on the provisions of laws and regulations;  
d. obtaining work according to ability, conditions and fair and just wages;  
e. specifically for women with disabilities, the right to obtain facilities and special treatment in public services;  
f. playing an active role in the political and governmental fields according to their potential;  
g. obtaining legal information and services;  
h. obtaining services to increase their income;  
i. obtaining other rights according to their humanitarian benefits based on the provisions of laws and regulations;

#### Article 4

The rights of children as referred to in Article 2 include:  
a. obtaining education and teaching according to age level, physical and mental condition, intelligence and interests and talents;  
b. living, growing, developing, and participating reasonably in accordance with human dignity and receiving protection from acts of violence, exploitation, discrimination and neglect, cruelty, violence and abuse, injustice and mistreatment;  
c. having needs fulfilled by the family;  
d. obtaining religious guidance;  
e. obtaining their identity;  
f. obtaining other rights in accordance with the provisions of laws and regulations;  
g. specifically for children with disabilities, the right to obtain facilities and special treatment in their services;

#### Article 5

In addition to the rights of women and children as referred to in Article 3 and Article 4, women and children victims of violence have the right to:  
a. have their dignity respected as human beings;  
b. the right to file complaints and receive fast and affordable services;  
c. obtain legal information and certainty;  
d. confidentiality of the case being handled;  
e. compensation for the case being handled;  
f. obtain facilities in the judicial process;  
g. obtain assistance;  
h. obtain physical and psychological health recovery from the suffering experienced by the victim;  
i. determine their own decisions;  
j. obtain rehabilitation and social reintegration;  
k. obtain respect and services for survival, growth and development;  
l. obtain basic services;  
m. be free from negative stigma; and  
n. obtain freedom to determine choices to decide for themselves after their parents' divorce;

#### Article 6

The implementation of women's empowerment and child protection is based on the principles of:  
a. the best interests of women and children;  
b. gender justice and gender equality;  
c. local wisdom;  
d. law enforcement and legal certainty;  
e. non-discrimination;  
f. the right to life, survival, and development;  
g. respect for the views of children;

# CHAPTER III

## IMPROVING THE QUALITY OF LIFE OF WOMEN

## Part One

General

#### Article 7

1. In order to improve the quality of life of women, the regional government is responsible for:  
a. respecting and guaranteeing the rights of women without discrimination in accordance with human dignity;  
b. implementing gender mainstreaming;  
c. establishing a gender mainstreaming working group;  
d. increasing the empowerment of women in roles, quality and capacity in the fields of:
1\. politics;  
2\. law;  
3\. social; and
4\. economy.  
e. strengthening and developing service provider institutions for women's empowerment in the Region;  
2. The responsibility of the Regional Government in efforts to improve the quality of life of women as referred to in paragraph (1) is carried out by the Regional Apparatus that administers governmental affairs in the field of women's empowerment and child protection.

## Part Two

Implementation of Gender Mainstreaming

#### Article 8

1. The Regent is responsible for the implementation of gender mainstreaming.  
2. The responsibility of the Regent as referred to in paragraph (1) may be delegated to the Vice Regent.

#### Article 9

In implementing gender mainstreaming as referred to in Article 7 paragraph (1) letter b, the Regent appoints the Regional Apparatus that administers governmental affairs in the field of women's and children's empowerment as the coordinator for the implementation of gender mainstreaming.

## Part Three

Establishment of Gender Mainstreaming Working Group

#### Article 10

1. In an effort to accelerate the institutionalization of gender mainstreaming, a gender mainstreaming working group is established in all Regional Apparatus.  
2. Members of the gender mainstreaming working group are all heads/leaders of Regional Apparatus.  
3. The Regent appoints the Head of the Regional Apparatus that administers Regional Development Planning Affairs as Chair of the gender mainstreaming Working Group and the Head of the Regional Apparatus that administers governmental affairs in the field of women's empowerment and child protection as Head of the Secretariat of the gender mainstreaming Working Group.  
4. The establishment of the gender mainstreaming working group as referred to in paragraph (1) is stipulated by Regent's Decree.

#### Article 11

The gender mainstreaming working group as referred to in Article 10 has the tasks of:  
a. promoting and facilitating gender mainstreaming to each Regional Apparatus;  
b. conducting socialization and advocacy of gender mainstreaming to Sub-district Heads and Village Heads;  
c. preparing annual programs;  
d. encouraging the realization of gender-responsive planning and budgeting;  
e. preparing the work plan of the gender mainstreaming working group every year;  
f. being accountable to the Regent through the Vice Regent;  
g. formulating policy recommendations to the Regent;  
h. preparing the Regional Gender Profile;  
i. monitoring gender mainstreaming in each agency;  
j. establishing a technical team to conduct analysis of the regional budget;  
k. preparing a regional action plan for gender mainstreaming; and  
l. encouraging the implementation of selection and appointment of gender mainstreaming focal points in each Regional Apparatus.

#### Article 12

1. The Technical Team as referred to in Article 11 letter j consists of apparatus who understand gender-responsive budget analysis.  
2. The Regional gender mainstreaming regional action plan as referred to in Article 11 letter k contains:  
a. gender mainstreaming in laws and regulations;  
b. gender mainstreaming in the development cycle;  
c. strengthening gender mainstreaming institutions; and  
d. strengthening community participation.

#### Article 13

1. The gender mainstreaming focal point in each Regional Apparatus as referred to in Article 11 letter 1 consists of officials and/or staff in charge of planning and/or program tasks.  
2. The gender mainstreaming focal point as referred to in paragraph (1), has the tasks of:  
a. promoting gender mainstreaming in the work unit;  
b. facilitating the preparation of work plans and budgeting of Regional Apparatus that are gender-responsive;  
c. conducting training, socialization, advocacy of gender mainstreaming to all officials and staff within the Regional Apparatus;  
d. reporting the implementation of gender mainstreaming to the leadership of the Regional Apparatus;  
e. encouraging the implementation of gender analysis of policies, programs, and activities in the work unit; and  
f. facilitating the preparation of gender data in each Regional Apparatus.  
3. The gender mainstreaming focal point as referred to in paragraph (1), is selected and appointed by the Regional Apparatus.

## Part Four

Increasing the Role, Quality, and Capacity of Women

### Paragraph 1

Political Field

#### Article 14

Efforts to increase the role, quality and capability of women in the political field as referred to in Article 7 paragraph (1) letter d number 1 include:  
a. conducting socialization to increase women's political participation in general elections;  
b. implementing programs that encourage the level of women's involvement in political processes and public positions;  
c. facilitating the self-development of women in organizing, associating and assembling;  
d. involving women in decision-making concerning women's interests;

### Paragraph 2

Legal Field

#### Article 15

Efforts to increase the role, quality and capability of women in the legal field as referred to in Article 7 paragraph (1) letter d number 2 include:  
a. conducting socialization to increase women's legal knowledge and awareness;  
b. mapping and reviewing gender-biased policies;  
c. involving women's organizations in the process of drafting and discussing regional legal products related to women's participation.

### Paragraph 3

Social Field

#### Article 16

Efforts to increase the role, quality and capability of women in the social field as referred to in Article 7 paragraph (1) letter d number 3 include:  
a. increasing knowledge and skills to encourage the fulfillment of education in accordance with existing potential;  
b. increasing and fulfilling health quality for women;  
c. increasing awareness and knowledge about independent, healthy and prosperous family planning including access to consultation services and marriage registration;  
d. increasing women's awareness of the impact of early marriage;  
e. facilitating and endeavoring to preserve customs and develop science, technology, arts and culture for the advancement of women;  
f. increasing women's awareness and knowledge of independent, healthy and prosperous family planning;  
g. implementing programs to reduce maternal mortality rates;  
h. encouraging the formation of women's organizations.

### Paragraph 4

Economic Field

#### Article 17

Efforts to increase the role, quality and capability of women in the economic field as referred to in Article 7 paragraph (1) letter d number 4 include:  
a. implementing programs, activities, training to improve women's skills and expertise in order to realize the improvement of women's welfare;  
b. facilitating business capital strengthening for women with weak economies;  
c. marketing products from businesses of women with weak economies;  
d. training to develop women's capabilities through economic empowerment of pre-prosperous families;  
e. comprehensive entrepreneurship training for women.

# CHAPTER IV

## PROTECTION OF WOMEN

## Part One

General

#### Article 18

1. The Regional Government in efforts to protect women is responsible for:  
a. establishing and implementing policies, programs and activities for the protection of women;  
b. making efforts to prevent acts of violence against women including criminal acts of trafficking in persons;  
c. increasing the effectiveness of services for women victims of violence;  
d. increasing efforts to protect female workers;  
e. strengthening and developing institutions providing women's protection services;  
f. cooperating with the Government, other Regional Governments, the private sector, business world and/or community non-governmental organizations in efforts to prevent acts of violence against women; and  
g. involving the active role of families, customary leaders, religious leaders, communities and community social institutions in the field of women's protection.  
2. The responsibility of the Regional Government in efforts to protect women as referred to in paragraph (1) is implemented by the Regional Apparatus implementing Government Affairs in the field of women's and children's empowerment and in its implementation may coordinate with relevant agencies.

## Part Two

Prevention of Acts of Violence Against Women

#### Article 19

In order to prevent acts of violence against women, the Regional Government is obliged to:  
a. conduct socialization regarding the prevention of violence against women by optimizing the role of customary, cultural, religious institutions and community organizations;  
b. provide guidelines on handling acts of violence against women;  
c. involve community participation in recovery and reintegration;  
d. develop an integrated database and information system on acts of violence against women;  
e. strengthen the function of community organizations engaged in women's and children's empowerment; and  
f. provide public facilities that are comfortable and safe for women.

## Part Three

Effectiveness of Services for Women Victims of Violence

### Paragraph 1

General

#### Article 20

1. Protection of women victims of violence is implemented through integrated services.  
2. The provision of integrated services for women victims of violence as referred to in paragraph (1) uses the principles of:  
a. gender responsive;  
b. non-discrimination;  
c. equal and respectful relationships;  
d. maintaining confidentiality;  
e. providing a sense of security and comfort;  
f. respecting individual differences;  
g. non-judgmental;  
h. respecting the choices and decisions of victims;  
i. sensitive to the background and condition of victims and the use of appropriate language understood by victims;  
j. fast and simple;  
k. empathy; and  
l. fulfillment of women's rights.

#### Article 21

Forms of integrated services for women victims of violence include:  
a. complaint handling;  
b. health rehabilitation;  
c. social rehabilitation;  
d. law enforcement and legal assistance; and  
e. repatriation and social reintegration.

### Paragraph 2

Complaint Handling

#### Article 22

Integrated services for complaint handling as referred to in Article 21 letter a are implemented by:  
a. hospitals;  
b. community health centers;  
c. women's service units;  
d. trauma center shelters;  
e. women crisis centers;  
f. marriage counseling, guidance and preservation advisory boards;  
g. police;  
h. prosecutor's office;  
i. courts;  
j. ministry of religious affairs; and/orak. legal aid institutions.  
1. Complaints of acts of violence against women may be made by reporters consisting of victims, families, communities and/or community organizations in the field of women's and children's protection.  
2. Reporters as referred to in paragraph (1) may file complaints:  
a. directly;  
b. by telephone; and/orc. by letter.  
3. In addition to handling complaints submitted by reporters, officers at service institutions as referred to in Article 22 are also authorized to handle victims of acts of violence against women obtained through other information.  
4. Further provisions regarding the procedures for complaint handling services as referred to in paragraph (1) and paragraph (2) are regulated in a Regent Regulation.

### Paragraph 3

Health Rehabilitation

#### Article 24

1. Health rehabilitation as referred to in Article 21 letter b constitutes efforts that include aspects of:  
a. promotive;  
b. preventive;  
c. curative; and  
d. rehabilitative.  
2. Health rehabilitation as referred to in paragraph (1) is conducted comprehensively, accessible 24 (twenty-four) hours at the Regional General Hospital, of quality and conducted in accordance with health service standards.  
3. Women victims of violence have the right to free health services, including:  
a. medical services;  
b. medicolegal services; and  
c. visum.  
4. Further provisions regarding the procedures for implementing health services as referred to in paragraph (3) are regulated in a Regent Regulation.

### Paragraph 4

Social Rehabilitation

#### Article 25

1. Social rehabilitation as referred to in Article 21 letter c is aimed at restoring and developing the capacity of women victims of violence who experience social dysfunction to be able to carry out their social functions in society properly.  
2. Social rehabilitation as referred to in paragraph (1) includes services:  
a. counseling;  
b. provision of safe houses; and  
c. spiritual guidance.  
3. The provision of services as referred to in paragraph (2) is organized by:  
a. Regional Apparatus that organizes governmental affairs in the social sector;  
b. Regional Apparatus that organizes governmental affairs in the sector of women's and children's empowerment, and may cooperate with integrated service centers, women crisis centers or existing social community organizations; orc. religious institutions and/or organizations.

#### Article 26

1. Counseling services as referred to in Article 25 paragraph (2) letter a aim to:  
a. assist women victims of violence in recognizing their problems and finding effective ways to overcome them themselves;  
b. empower women victims of violence to determine their future;  
c. provide moral support to women victims of violence in facing the process they are undergoing; and  
d. make women victims of violence feel accepted in the community environment.  
2. Counseling services are carried out by gender-responsive social rehabilitation officers, consisting of:  
a. counselors;  
b. psychologists; and  
c. psychiatrists.  
3. Counseling services are carried out in a place that guarantees a sense of security, comfort and confidentiality of information from women victims of violence.  
4. The stages of counseling services include:  
a. initial counseling;  
b. follow-up counseling;  
c. case discussion; and  
d. site visits.

#### Article 27

1. The Regional Government may provide safe houses for women victims of violence.  
2. The provision of safe house services as referred to in Article 25 paragraph (2) letter b aims to protect women victims of violence from intimidation by perpetrators or other parties.  
3. The location of the safe house as referred to in paragraph (1) must be kept confidential.  
4. The criteria for women victims of violence who require a safe house include:  
a. their life is threatened;  
b. receiving rejection from family or community;  
c. requiring intensive services but their residence is relatively far; and/or  
d. abandoned if not placed in a safe house.  
5. The provision of safe house services is carried out by social rehabilitation officers who have gender sensitivity or gender awareness, consisting of:  
a. social workers/volunteers;  
b. security officers; and/or  
c. community leaders.  
6. Further provisions regarding the provision of safe house services are regulated in a Regent Regulation.

#### Article 28

1. Spiritual guidance as referred to in Article 25 paragraph (2) letter c aims at psychological recovery.  
2. Spiritual guidance as referred to in paragraph (1) is implemented based on the principles of:  
a. not imposing any religion or belief; and  
b. leaving the final decision in the hands of the victim.  
3. Spiritual guidance is carried out by spiritual guidance officers.  
4. Spiritual guidance officers as referred to in paragraph (3) consist of consultants from the Marriage Advisory, Development and Preservation Agency, Officers of the Religious Affairs Office, religious leaders, who have gender sensitivity or gender awareness.

### Paragraph 5

Law Enforcement and Legal Aid

#### Article 29

1. Law enforcement and legal aid services as referred to in Article 21 letter d constitute actions by law enforcement officials to implement laws and regulations in handling cases of violence against women, especially to impose sanctions on perpetrators and provide protection for witnesses and/or victims.  
2. Law enforcement and legal aid services as referred to in paragraph (1) are carried out by prioritizing the best interests of the victim.

#### Article 30

1. Law enforcement and legal aid services are aimed at providing assistance and protection to women victims of violence and witnesses with a gender-responsive approach so that victims obtain justice and legal certainty.  
2. Types of legal aid services include:  
a. legal aid in the reporting process and preparation of examination reports;  
b. legal aid in the prosecution process at the prosecutor's office;  
c. legal aid in the examination process at court;  
d. legal aid in the court hearing decision process until there is a final legal decision; and  
e. legal aid in the mediation process.  
3. The Regional Government provides legal aid to women victims of violence based on the provisions of laws and regulations.  
4. In providing legal aid as referred to in paragraph (3), the Regional Government may cooperate with legal aid institutions, women crisis centers, advocates and/or other gender-sensitive legal assistants.

### Paragraph 6

Repatriation and Social Reintegration

#### Article 31

1. Repatriation is aimed at returning women victims of violence to their region of origin.  
2. Repatriation of women victims of violence is accompanied by social reintegration efforts.

#### Article 32

1. Social reintegration is carried out in order to reunite women victims of violence with their family, substitute family, or community that can provide protection and fulfillment of needs for the victim.  
2. Social reintegration is aimed so that:  
a. women victims of violence can resume their social life; and  
b. women victims of violence can have a decent life.  
3. Social reintegration as referred to in paragraph (1) is carried out after the victim receives social rehabilitation services.

#### Article 33

The Regional Government carries out repatriation and social reintegration of women victims of violence in accordance with laws and regulations.

## Part Four

Employment Protection

#### Article 34

1. Employers who employ women must maintain decency, safety and security while at the workplace.  
2. Employers must place female workers in places that are safe for women's reproductive health.  
3. Employers must fulfill the rights of female workers to receive wages in accordance with the provisions of laws and regulations.  
4. Employers must provide special breastfeeding facilities at the company.

#### Article 35

1. Female workers/laborers under the age of 18 (eighteen) years are prohibited from being employed between 23.00 WIB until 07.00 WIB.  
2. Employers are prohibited from employing pregnant female workers/laborers who according to a doctor's statement are dangerous to the health and safety of their pregnancy.  
3. Employers who employ female workers/laborers between 23.00 WIB until 07.00 WIB, must:  
a. provide nutritious food and drinks; and  
b. maintain decency and security while at the workplace.  
4. Employers must provide shuttle transportation for female workers/laborers who depart and return from work between 23.00 WIB until 05.00 WIB.

# CHAPTER V

## IMPROVING FAMILY QUALITY

#### Article 36

1. In order to improve family quality, the Regional Government is responsible for:  
a. making efforts to improve family quality to realize gender equality and children's rights;  
b. providing services for families in realizing gender equality and children's rights; and  
c. strengthening and developing institutions that provide family quality improvement services.  
2. The responsibility of the Regional Government in efforts to improve family quality as referred to in paragraph (1) is carried out by Regional Apparatus in accordance with their duties and functions.

#### Article 37

The Regional Government shall undertake efforts to improve family quality as referred to in Article 36 paragraph (1) letter a including:  
a. guidance;  
b. counseling;  
c. supervision;  
d. facilitation of family development implementation;  
e. improving the health quality of pregnant and breastfeeding mothers;

#### Article 38

Guidance as referred to in Article 37 letter a, includes:  
a. improving child quality by providing access to information, education, counseling and services regarding child care, parenting and child development through child protection programs and parenting pattern development;  
b. improving adolescent quality by providing access to information, education, counseling and services regarding family life through planned generation activities, adolescent information and counseling centers and adolescent family development;  
c. improving the quality of life of the elderly through elderly guidance programs, elderly family development, elderly guidance and counseling; and  
d. improving family economy.

#### Article 39

Counseling as referred to in Article 37 letter b, includes:  
a. providing protection and assistance to develop oneself through improving family capabilities and skills;  
b. conducting counseling on improving women's quality of life;  
c. conducting counseling on improving gender equality in family and community life;  
d. improving access and opportunities to receive information and economic resources through efforts to increase prosperous family income and increase women's economic productivity; and  
e. undertaking empowerment efforts for female heads of household.

#### Article 40

Supervision as referred to in Article 37 letter c, includes:  
a. conducting evaluation and monitoring of child protection programs and parenting pattern development;  
b. conducting evaluation and monitoring of adolescent quality improvement;  
c. conducting evaluation and monitoring of elderly quality of life improvement;  
d. conducting evaluation and monitoring of family economic improvement; and  
e. conducting evaluation and monitoring of family capability and skills improvement.

#### Article 41

Provision of services for families as referred to in Article 36 paragraph (1) letter b includes:  
a. communication, information and education;  
b. contraceptive devices and medicines;  
c. recording and reporting of family planning services;  
d. adequate health service facilities and infrastructure; and  
e. breastfeeding corner rooms in every public facility.

#### Article 42

Communication, information and education as referred to in Article 41 letter a constitutes communication activities to increase knowledge and improve the attitudes and behavior of families and communities in population and family planning programs.

#### Article 43

1. In addition to the Regional Government, private parties are obligated to provide breastfeeding corner rooms.  
2. Breastfeeding corner rooms as referred to in paragraph (1) constitute proper and adequate rooms.  
3. Private parties that do not provide breastfeeding corner rooms as referred to in paragraph (2) shall be subject to administrative sanctions in the form of:  
a. written warning;  
b. limitation of business activities;  
c. suspension of business activities;  
d. administrative fine; and/ore. revocation of permit.

# CHAPTER VII

## FULFILLMENT OF CHILDREN'S RIGHTS

## Part One

General

#### Article 47

1. The Regional Government in efforts to fulfill children's rights is responsible for:  
a. establishing and implementing policies and programs/activities for the fulfillment of children's rights;  
b. respecting and guaranteeing the human rights of every child without distinguishing ethnicity, religion, race, class, gender, ethnicity, culture and language, legal status of the child, birth order of the child, and physical and/or mental condition;  
c. strengthening and developing service provider institutions for improving family quality in realizing children's rights;  
d. guaranteeing the protection, care, and welfare of children with due regard to the rights and obligations of parents, guardians, or other persons legally responsible for the child;  
e. providing facilities and infrastructure in the implementation of fulfillment of children's rights;  
f. guaranteeing the right of children to express opinions in accordance with the age and level of intelligence of the child;  
g. conducting supervision and guidance in the context of implementing the fulfillment of children's rights;  
h. conducting cooperation with private parties and/or NGOs in the implementation of child protection;  
2. The Government's responsibility as referred to in the implementation of Child Protection Institutions and Government Protection in the field of Child Empowerment.

## Part Two

Birth Registration and Education

#### Article 48

1. The Regional Government is obligated to administer birth registration of children with the issuance of birth certificates.  
2. Birth certificates as referred to in paragraph (1) are administered free of charge.

#### Article 49

The Regional Government guarantees the implementation of a 9 (nine) year compulsory education program for children aged 7 (seven) to 15 (fifteen) years.

#### Article 50

1. Every organizer of educational units for children to obtain educational services without discrimination.  
2. Every organizer of educational units is prohibited from expelling children from educational institutions unless there is a guarantee for the continuity of the child's education.  
3. In the event that a child victim of violence experiences dropping out of school, the organizer of the educational unit must coordinate with related agencies.

#### Article 51

1. Children with disabilities and children with special needs are given equal opportunities to obtain formal, non-formal and informal educational services, including inclusive education programs.  
2. The regional government provides teaching staff for inclusive education at every educational unit.

#### Article 52

Children in conflict with the law, children who experience pregnancy outside of marriage and/or unwanted pregnancy and child victims of HIV/AIDS transmission are protected in their rights to obtain education.

## Part Three

Health and Social

#### Article 53

1. Regional Government is obliged to provide comprehensive health services for children, so that every child obtains optimal health status from the womb.  
2. Comprehensive health services as referred to in paragraph (1) include:  
a. promotive, preventive, curative and rehabilitative efforts for basic health services;  
b. exemption from cost burden for malnourished children, children with disabilities, children with special needs, children infected with HIV/AIDS, child workers, child victims of narcotics abuse, alcohol, psychotropic substances and other addictive substances, children who are victims of violence, and child victims of human trafficking.  
3. Regional Government, community, family and parents are obliged to ensure that children who are born are protected from diseases that threaten survival and/or cause disability.

#### Article 54

1. Promotive efforts of basic health services as referred to in Article 53 paragraph (2) letter a are aimed at improving health status in a more optimal manner.  
2. Promotive efforts as referred to in paragraph (1) are carried out by providing socialization or counseling to the community regarding healthy lifestyle patterns which include:  
a. the importance of exclusive breastfeeding for infants;  
b. the importance of immunization for toddlers;  
c. clean living patterns;  
d. the importance of nutritious food;  
e. the dangers of drugs and smoking; and  
3. In addition to promotive efforts as referred to in paragraph (2), Regional Government may carry out other activities in the context of health provision.

#### Article 55

1. Preventive efforts of basic health services as referred to in Article 53 paragraph (2) letter a are aimed at preventing the occurrence of disease or health problems.  
2. Efforts as referred to in paragraph (1) are carried out by:  
a. requiring mothers to provide exclusive breastfeeding to infants up to the age of 6 (six) months;  
b. providing free immunization and vitamins to infants and toddlers;  
c. providing nutritious food to toddlers from underprivileged families;  
d. optimizing the role of posyandu for health services for mothers and toddlers;  
e. conducting periodic health examinations; and  
f. providing sanitation facilities and clean water in every school.

#### Article 56

1. Curative efforts of basic health services as referred to in Article 53 paragraph (2) letter a are aimed at preventing disease from becoming more severe through treatment.  
2. Treatment as referred to in paragraph (1) is carried out free of charge for children from underprivileged families.

#### Article 57

1. Rehabilitative efforts of basic health services as referred to in Article 53 paragraph (2) letter a are aimed at maintaining and restoring condition after illness.  
2. Rehabilitative efforts as referred to in paragraph (1) are carried out through periodic health examinations.

#### Article 58

1. Regional Government is obliged to provide social welfare for:  
a. children in conflict with the law;  
b. child victims of violence who are exploited economically and sexually, neglected children, child victims of kidnapping and child victims of human trafficking;  
c. children who do not have parents and are cared for by parties or families who are unable;  
d. child victims of natural disasters or social disasters; and  
e. children with disabilities;  
2. Social welfare as referred to in paragraph (1) may be in the form of provision of services:  
a. health;  
b. education;  
c. legal aid;  
d. assistance;  
e. social, mental and spiritual guidance;  
f. social rehabilitation; and/or  
g. reintegration.

## Part Four

Employment

#### Article 59

Regional Government conducts periodic supervision of every person/entity that employs children in efforts to protect child workers.

#### Article 60

Every person/entity that employs children must pay attention to the requirements:  
a. minimum age of 15 (fifteen) years;  
b. obtain written consent from the parent/guardian of the child worker;  
c. have a written employment agreement between the employer and the parent/guardian of the child worker and obtain validation from the authorized agency;  
d. not employ children at night;  
e. working time of maximum 3 (three) hours per day;  
f. not employed in a place or environment that can disrupt the child's growth and development process, whether physically, mentally, morally and intellectually or the child's health;  
g. provide opportunities to obtain education in accordance with their talents and interests;  
h. employ for light types of work; and  
i. provide one day off per week.

#### Article 61

In the event that children are employed together with adult workers, then the workplace of children must be separated from the workplace of adult workers.

#### Article 62

1. Every person/entity is prohibited from employing and involving children in the worst forms of work.  
2. The worst forms of work referred to in paragraph (1) include:  
a. all work in the form of slavery or the like;  
b. all work that utilizes, provides, or offers children for prostitution, pornography production, pornographic performances, or gambling;  
c. all work that utilizes, provides, or involves children for the production and trade of alcoholic beverages, narcotics, psychotropic substances, and other addictive substances; and/or d. all work that endangers the health, safety, or morals of children.

## Part Five

Facilitation of Child Justice System

#### Article 63

1. Facilitation of child justice system includes criminal and civil justice.  
2. Facilitation of criminal justice system as referred to in paragraph (1) includes:  
a. provision of companion or child volunteer at the time of examination by investigators;  
b. provision of legal and psychological assistance services;  
c. involving members/investigators of local police in efforts to prevent criminal acts committed by Children, children as victims and children as witnesses;  
d. realizing restorative justice process for children in conflict with the law; and  
e. rehabilitation and social reintegration services;  
3. Fulfillment of children's rights in the civil justice system as referred to in paragraph (1) includes provision and delivery of consultation and legal aid services during the judicial process.  
4. In facilitating the child justice system as referred to in paragraph (1) and paragraph (2), the authorized PD is the PD that administers governmental affairs in the field of protection of women and children as well as social affairs.

## Part Six

Children's Forum

#### Article 64

1. In organizing the fulfillment of children's rights to express opinions and participate, Regional Government facilitates the formation of a children's forum.  
2. Children's forum as referred to in paragraph (1) is a representation of children in the region, both representation of children's geographical domicile, components of children's socio-cultural groups and children's educational background.  
3. In every formulation of policies related to children, Regional Government is obliged to consider and accommodate children's opinions submitted through the children's participation forum.  
4. Further provisions regarding the formation of children's forum as referred to in paragraph (1) through paragraph (3) are stipulated by Regent's Decree.

## Part Seven

Child-Friendly Regency

#### Article 65

1. In the context of fulfillment of children's rights in an integrated, systematic and sustainable manner, Regional Government establishes a Regional Action Plan for Child-Friendly Regency Development.  
2. Regional Action Plan for Child-Friendly Regency Development contains:  
a. the concept of Child-Friendly Regency concerning objectives, strategies and roles of related parties;  
b. children's rights; and  
c. approach to Child-Friendly Regency development.

#### Article 66

1. The Regional Action Plan for the Development of a Child-Friendly Regency is directed at the fulfillment of children's rights divided into 5 (five) target groups, comprising:  
a. civil rights and freedoms;  
b. family environment and alternative care;  
c. basic health and welfare;  
d. education, leisure time utilization, and cultural activities; and  
e. special protection.  
2. Further provisions regarding the Regional Action Plan for the Development of a Child-Friendly Regency as referred to in paragraph (1) shall be regulated in a Regent Regulation.  
3. In order to ensure the effectiveness of the implementation of the Regional Action Plan for the Development of a Child-Friendly Regency in the Region, a Child-Friendly Regency Task Force shall be established.  
4. The establishment and membership composition of the Child-Friendly Regency Task Force shall be stipulated by Regent Decree.  
5. The Child-Friendly Regency Task Force shall be domiciled at the OPD that administers governmental affairs in the field of women's empowerment and child protection.  
6. The Child-Friendly Regency Task Force as referred to in paragraph (1) shall have the following principal duties:  
a. coordinating the implementation of policies and development of a Child-Friendly Regency;  
b. conducting socialization, advocacy and communication, information and education on Child-Friendly Regency policies;  
c. collecting baseline data for the development of a Child-Friendly Regency;  
d. conducting needs analysis sourced from baseline data;  
e. disseminating baseline data;  
f. determining the focus and priority of programs in realizing a Child-Friendly Regency, adjusted to regional potential;  
g. preparing a 5 (five) year regional action plan for a Child-Friendly Regency and work mechanisms; and  
h. conducting monitoring, evaluation and reporting at least once (1) a year.

#### Article 68

In carrying out its duties, the Child-Friendly Regency Task Force may coordinate and cooperate with P2TP2A institutions and other institutions that provide child protection services.

##### I. GENERAL

The 1945 Constitution as a constitutional foundation has explicitly regulated the importance of protection of Human Rights, including the rights of women and children. In addition to the guarantee from the 1945 Constitution of the Republic of Indonesia, the guarantee that every person must have equal dignity and degree has also been internationally recognized. The Universal Declaration of Human Rights or UDHR of 1948 which underlies Human Rights in general has guaranteed that all people are born with freedom, having equal dignity and rights without exception.

The 1945 Constitution of the Republic of Indonesia mandates the Regional Government to regulate and manage regional government affairs aimed at realizing community welfare through improved services, protection and empowerment of the community. As a community component that plays an important role in realizing a quality generation and as successors to the nation's struggle, women and children must receive guarantees for the fulfillment of their rights.

To ensure the implementation of women's empowerment and child protection in accordance with human dignity and in order to carry out mandatory affairs, the regional government needs to provide legal certainty in the implementation of women's empowerment and child protection through the formulation of a policy. The formulation of such policy is a form of support and concrete effort from various parties, ranging from the Regional Government, community and other parties in ensuring the fulfillment of the rights of women and children.

The implementation of Women's Empowerment and Child Protection is carried out based on the principles of:
a. non-discrimination:b. the best interests of women and children;
c. the right to life, survival, and development;
d. respect for the views of the child;
e. Legal certainty;
f. local wisdom; and justice and gender equality.

The formulation of the Regional Regulation on Women's Empowerment and Child Protection aims to:
a. provide guidance in planning and implementing Women's Empowerment and Child Protection policies;
b. increase Women's Empowerment efforts and fulfillment of children's rights in order to realize improved quality of life for women and children in the Region;
c. increase protection efforts for women and children from actions and policies that violate the rights of women and children;
d. increase the value of local wisdom and the role of customs in Women's Empowerment and Child Protection; and
e. increase the role of the Regional Government and other community institutions in Women's Empowerment and Child Protection efforts.

This Regional Regulation on Women and Child Protection regulates:
a. Improving the quality of life of women which contains government responsibilities, implementation of gender mainstreaming, efforts to increase the role, quality and capacity of women in the political, legal, social and economic fields;
b. Protection of women which contains regional government responsibilities in protecting women, preventing violence against women, protection for women victims of violence, and handling complaints;
c. Child protection which contains the responsibilities of the regional government, parents/families and the community in protecting children and efforts made by the regional government in implementing child protection. In addition, the need to establish a children's forum and Child-Friendly Regency as a form of appreciation in realizing protection and fulfillment of children's rights.
