REGENT OF TANAH BUMBU SOUTH KALIMANTAN PROVINCE

# REGIONAL REGULATION OF TANAH BUMBU REGENCY

NUMBER 12 OF 2023

CONCERNING

IMPLEMENTATION OF WOMEN'S EMPOWERMENT AND CHILD PROTECTION

BY THE GRACE OF ALMIGHTY GOD

REGENT OF TANAH BUMBU,

Considering:
 
 
 
 
 
 
 
a. that Women and Children have the right to obtain protection from torture, threats, pressure, and have the right to receive equal treatment and opportunities to obtain justice and welfare in accordance with the mandate of the 1945 Constitution of the Republic of Indonesia;
b. that Women's Empowerment and protecting the rights of Children are the obligations of the Regional Government and the Community in the Region;
c. that based on the provisions of Article 12 paragraph (2) letter b and letter H Division of Government Affairs in the Field of Women's Empowerment and Child Protection Appendix to Law Number 23 of 2014 concerning Regional Government as amended several times most recently by Law Number 6 of 2023 concerning Stipulation of Government Regulation in Lieu of Law Number 2 of 2022 concerning Job Creation into Law, the Regional Government has the authority to implement Women's Empowerment and Child Protection in the Region;

# CHAPTER I

## GENERAL PROVISIONS

#### Article 1

In this Regional Regulation, what is meant by:  
1. Region is Tanah Bumbu Regency.  
2. Regional Government is the head of the Region as an element of the Regional Government organizer who leads the implementation of government affairs that are the authority of the autonomous Region.  
3. Head of Region hereinafter referred to as Regent is the Regent of Tanah Bumbu Regency.  
4. Regional House of Representatives hereinafter abbreviated as DPRD is the people's representative institution of Tanah Bumbu Regency Region.  
5. Agency is a Regional Apparatus that organizes government affairs in the field of Women's Empowerment and Child Protection.  
6. Regional Apparatus is an auxiliary element of the Regent and DPRD in organizing government affairs that are the authority of the Region.  
7. Women's Empowerment is an effort to obtain access and control over resources, economic, political, social, cultural, so that Women can organize themselves and increase self-confidence to be able to play a role and actively participate in solving problems, so as to be able to build capacity and self-concept.  
8. Gender Mainstreaming is a strategy built to integrate gender into men and Women. 9. Women are adult humans of female sex and persons recognized by law as Women.  
10. Child is a person who has not reached the age of 18 (eighteen) years including a Child who is still in the womb. 11. Child in conflict with the law (ABH) is a Child in conflict with the law, a Child who is a victim of a criminal act and a Child who is a witness to a criminal act. 12. Violence is any act that results in misery or suffering physically, psychologically, sexually, and/or neglect, including threats to commit acts, coercion or deprivation of liberty unlawfully.  
13. Protection of Women is all efforts aimed at protecting Women and providing a sense of security in the fulfillment of their rights by providing consistent and systematic attention aimed at achieving gender equality.  
14. Child Protection is all activities to guarantee and protect Children and their rights so that they can live, grow, develop, and participate optimally in accordance with human dignity and dignity, and receive protection from Violence and discrimination. 15. Special Protection is a form of protection received by Children in certain situations and conditions to obtain a guarantee of security.  
16. Toddler is a Child aged 0 (zero) to 5 (five) years, in the early stages of human development.  
17. School-Age Child is a Child aged 6 (six) to 18 (eighteen) years.  
18. Neglected Child is a Child whose needs for mental and religious guidance as well as clothing, food, shelter, health, education, physical, and social services are not properly met.  
19. Child who is a victim of an act of Violence is a Child who experiences mistreatment such as being abused, humiliated, which endangers the Child physically, mentally and socially. 20. Child maltreatment is all forms of violence against children committed by those who should be responsible and/or those who have power over the child, who should be trusted, namely parents, close family, teachers, supervisors, law enforcement officers, caregivers and companions (World Health Organization).  
21. Child in emergency situations is a Child in situations and conditions that endanger him/herself such as Children victims of riots, Children who become refugees, Children victims of natural disasters and Children in armed conflict.  
22. Child in conflict with the law is a Child in conflict with the law, a Child victim of a criminal act, and a Child who is a witness to a criminal act.  
23. Child of isolated indigenous community is a Child who lives in a situation of isolation where they cannot access basic needs.  
24. Economically exploited Child is a Child who is forced and/or deceived to be employed by parents or others without being paid or being paid.  
25. Sexually exploited Child is the use of Children for sexual purposes with cash or other forms of compensation between the Child, buyers of sexual services, intermediaries or agents and other parties who profit from the sexual trafficking of the Child.  
26. Child who is a victim of drug abuse is a Child who suffers from dependence on drugs caused by drug abuse, either by their own will or because of encouragement or coercion from others. 27. Child with special needs is a Child who experiences physical and/or mental disabilities that interfere with their growth and development in accordance with the stages of Child development.  
28. Child victim of maltreatment is a Child who receives treatment that is not in accordance with the rights of the Child.  
29. Village is a legal community unit that has territorial boundaries authorized to regulate and manage Government affairs, the interests of the local Community, based on Community initiative, rights of origin, and/or traditional rights that are recognized and respected in the Government system of the Unitary State of the Republic of Indonesia.  
30. Child in need of Special Protection is a Child in emergency situations, a Child in conflict with the law, a Child from an isolated indigenous community, a Child who is economically and/or sexually exploited, a Child victim of narcotics, alcohol, psychotropic and other active substance abuse (Napza), a Child victim of kidnapping, sale and trafficking, a Child victim of physical and/or psychological Violence, a Child with special needs, and a Child victim of maltreatment and neglect.  
31. Child Trafficking is a criminal act or action that meets one or more elements of recruitment, transfer, handover of a Child using Violence or threat of violence, fraud, kidnapping, confinement, abuse of power, exploitation of a position of vulnerability or debt bondage for the purpose of and/or resulting in the exploitation of the Child.  
32. Rights of the Child are part of human rights that must be guaranteed, protected, and fulfilled by parents, family, Community, government/Regional Government, and the state.  
33. Obligations of the Child are everything that must be carried out by the Child in accordance with the function and role of the Child.  
34. Social Organization hereinafter referred to as Orsos is an institution/foundation/social association formed by the Community whether legally incorporated or not legally incorporated that functions as a means of Community participation in carrying out Social Welfare Efforts (UKS).  
35. Non-Governmental Organization hereinafter abbreviated as LSM is an organization/institution formed by members of the Indonesian citizen Community, voluntarily of their own will and interested in and engaged in the field of social welfare efforts determined by the organization/institution, as a manifestation of Community participation in efforts to improve the standard of living and welfare of the Community that emphasizes voluntary service.  
36. Napza are chemical substances which when introduced into the body either orally (drunk, smoked, inhaled and sucked) or injected, can affect a person's thoughts, mood, feelings and behavior. This can cause disturbances in social conditions characterized by negative indications, long duration of use and excessive use.  
37. Regional Technical Implementation Unit for the Protection of Women and Children hereinafter referred to as UPTD PPA is a Regional technical implementation unit for the Protection of Women and Children at the Agency for Women's Empowerment and Child Protection that provides services for Women and Children who experience violence, discrimination, Special Protection, and other problems.  
38. Community is individuals, families, groups, social organizations and/or community organizations.

# CHAPTER II

## PRINCIPLES, PURPOSE AND OBJECTIVES

Empowerment

#### Article 2

Women and Child Protection shall be conducted based on the principles of:  
a. respect for human rights;  
b. gender equality;  
c. non-discrimination;  
d. justice and legal certainty;  
e. benefit;  
f. participatory;  
g. best interests of the Child;  
h. accountability; and  
i. gender responsiveness.

#### Article 3

The purpose of establishing this Regional Regulation is as a guideline in the implementation of Women's Empowerment and Child Protection in accordance with the provisions of laws and regulations.

#### Article 4

1. The Implementation and Protection Regulation regarding Women's Empowerment and Child Protection aims to:  
a. guarantee the fulfillment of the rights of every Woman and Child to survival, growth and development;  
b. improve the quality of life of Women, Children and family quality;  
c. increase the institutional capacity of Women's Empowerment and Child Protection in the Region, including the development of gender and Child data systems; and  
d. provide protection of Women's rights and fulfillment of Children's Rights including Special Protection for Children from various forms of Violence and other discriminatory treatment.

#### Article 5

1. Women's Empowerment and Child Protection regulated in this Regional Regulation as an inseparable part of Regional development planning in the Regional long-term development plan, Regional medium-term development plan, and Regional Government work plan whose content concerns Women's Empowerment and Child Protection in the Region.  
2. Regional development planning as referred to in paragraph (1), shall be set forth in Women's Empowerment and Child Protection programs and activities by adhering to the classification, codification, and nomenclature of development planning.

# CHAPTER III

## SCOPE

#### Article 6

The scope of regulation for the implementation of Women's Empowerment and Child Protection includes:  
a. Women's Empowerment;  
b. Women's Protection;  
c. Child Protection;  
d. safe house;  
e. Women's Empowerment and Child Protection by Village government;  
f. family quality;  
g. cooperation;  
h. awards;  
i. Community participation;  
j. financing;  
k. prohibitions;  
1. investigation provisions; and  
m. criminal provisions.

# CHAPTER IV

## WOMEN'S EMPOWERMENT

#### Article 7

1. The Regional Government is responsible for Women's Empowerment.  
2. The responsibility as referred to in paragraph (1) shall be implemented by the Agency.  
3. In implementing the administration of Women's Empowerment, the Regional Apparatus as referred to in paragraph (1) shall involve:  
a. Regional Apparatus that administers governmental affairs in the fields of:  
1\. population administration;  
2\. education;  
3\. Village government;  
4\. Regional development planning;  
b. national commission on Women.

#### Article 8

The Regional Government has the authority to:  
a. institutionalize Gender Mainstreaming;  
b. conduct Women's Empowerment in the political, legal, social and economic fields in community organizations; and  
c. strengthen and develop institutions providing Women's Empowerment services.

#### Article 9

1. The Regional Government in implementing Women's Empowerment shall establish a Gender Mainstreaming institution.  
2. The Gender Mainstreaming institution as referred to in paragraph (1) shall be the responsibility of the Regent.

#### Article 10

Further provisions regarding the Gender Mainstreaming institution as referred to in Article 9 shall be regulated by Regent Regulation.

# CHAPTER V

## WOMEN'S PROTECTION

## Part One General

#### Article 11

1. The Regional Government is responsible for providing Women's Protection including efforts to:  
a. establish and implement policies, programs and activities for Women's Protection;  
b. conduct prevention efforts against Violence against Women;  
c. conduct prevention efforts against trafficking in persons;  
d. increase the effectiveness of services for Women and Children victims of violence;  
e. increase protection efforts for Women workers;  
f. strengthen and develop institutions providing protection services for Women workers;  
g. strengthen and develop institutions providing Women's Protection services;  
h. conduct cooperation with the government, Regional Government, other Regional Governments, private parties, business world, and/or Community non-governmental organizations in efforts to prevent Violence against Women; and  
i. involve the active role of families, religious leaders, the Community and institutions in the field of Women's Protection.  
2. The responsibility as referred to in paragraph (1), shall be implemented by the Regional Apparatus that administers governmental affairs in accordance with its duties and functions.

#### Article 12

The Regional Government provides Women's Protection in the form of:  
a. prevention of Violence against Women;  
b. provision of services for Women victims of Violence requiring Regional level coordination; and  
c. strengthening and development of institutions providing Women's Protection services.

## Part Two 

Protection of Women's Rights

#### Article 13

1. The Regional Government guarantees protection of Women's rights.  
2. Women's rights as referred to in paragraph (1) include the fields of:  
a. education;  
b. health;  
c. employment;  
d. politics;  
e. law; and  
f. economic, cultural and social.

#### Article 14

Women's rights in the field of education as referred to in Article 13 paragraph (2) letter a include:  
a. making policies to facilitate Women's access and revoking discriminatory policies that hinder the fulfillment of Women's rights to education;  
b. obtaining equal opportunities with men to obtain education and teaching in all types and levels of education pathways.

#### Article 15

Women's rights in the field of health as referred to in Article 13 paragraph (2) letter b include:  
a. obtaining education regarding reproductive health;  
b. obtaining health services;  
c. obtaining family planning services;  
d. obtaining services for pregnant mothers; and  
e. childbirth and post-childbirth services.

#### Article 16

Women's rights in the field of employment as referred to in Article 13 paragraph (2) letter c include:  
a. empowerment of Women entrepreneurs;  
b. obtaining decent work; and  
c. obtaining protection from the crime of trafficking in persons.

#### Article 17

Women's rights in the field of politics as referred to in Article 13 paragraph (2) letter d include:  
a. facilitating increased political participation of Women in legislative institutions;  
b. providing facilities and infrastructure for Women's organizations whose vision and mission is to fight for the fulfillment of rights and protection of Women;  
c. conducting socialization in order to increase awareness of Women's rights in the political field; and  
d. encouraging the formation of Women's forum organizations.

#### Article 18

Women's rights in the field of law as referred to in Article 13 paragraph (2) letter e include:  
a. facilitating the improvement of legal knowledge and awareness for Women; and  
b. encouraging the formation of organizations that provide free legal services for Women.

#### Article 19

Women's rights in the field of economic, cultural and social as referred to in Article 13 paragraph (2) letter f include:  
a. facilitating Women in improving family economy;  
b. encouraging Women's activities as agents of change;  
c. facilitating Women in increasing social activities;  
d. obtaining capital assistance.

#### Article 20

Further provisions regarding the procedures for implementing the fulfillment of Women's rights as referred to in Article 13 shall be regulated by Regent Regulation.

# CHAPTER VI

## CHILD PROTECTION

## Part One 

General

#### Article 21

1. The Regional Government shall carry out Child Protection in the Region.  
2. Child Protection as referred to in paragraph (1) shall be through the Protection of Children's Rights.  
3. Children's Rights as referred to in paragraph (2) shall be guided by the provisions of Tanah Bumbu Regency Regional Regulation Number 11 of 2021 concerning the Implementation of Child-Friendly Regency (Tanah Bumbu Regency Regional Gazette of 2021 Number 11, Supplement to Tanah Bumbu Regency Regional Gazette Number 126).

## Part Two

Implementation of Child Protection

#### Article 22

1. The Regional Government, NGOs/Social Organizations, the Community and parents/families shall be obligated to provide Child Protection consisting of:  
a. Children in the womb;  
b. Toddlers;  
c. School-Age Children;  
d. Neglected and street children; and  
e. Children requiring Special Protection.  
2. The implementation of Child Protection as referred to in paragraph (1) shall be carried out through:
a. policies, programs, and mechanisms concerning:  
1\. prevention, supervision, complaints/reporting, and development of Child data information systems;  
2\. integrated handling for Children who are victims of violence, maltreatment, exploitation and neglect;
3\. guarantee of fulfillment of the rights of every Child who is a victim of crime, violence, maltreatment, exploitation and neglect including:  
a. recovery and health maintenance services;  
b. continuity of education services;  
c. psychosocial services; and  
d. legal aid services.  
4\. implementation of family support including:  
a. counseling;  
b. child-rearing education;  
c. family mediation; and  
d. economic support.
b. raising awareness and attitudes of the Community through socialization, education, and information regarding:
1\. Children's Rights, Child Protection, prevention of child marriage and Child rearing; and
2\. the impact of violence, adverse exploitation, and Child neglect.  
c. increasing the capacity of Child Protection services which includes the development of institutional capacity and service provider personnel.  
d. service provider personnel as referred to in letter c includes:  
1\. health;  
2\. education;  
3\. psychosocial;  
4\. care; and  
5\. legal aid.
e. increasing the ability of Children to recognize risks and dangers from situations or acts that may cause violence, exploitation, maltreatment, and neglect.  
3. The implementation of Child Protection as referred to in paragraph (2) letters a through e shall be carried out and/or coordinated by the SKPD responsible for Child Protection affairs.

## Part Three Handling

#### Article 23

1. The targets of Child handling include:  
a. Children outside the care of parents;  
b. Children in disaster emergency situations;  
c. Children in conflict with the law;  
d. Child victims of physical, mental and/or sexual violence;  
e. Children subjected to maltreatment and neglect;  
f. Child victims of economic exploitation;  
g. Children who are victims of the crime of trafficking in persons;  
h. Children who are victims of gambling, narcotics abuse, alcohol, psychotropic substances and other addictive substances (Napza); and  
i. Children who are in situations where the nature and condition of the workplace may endanger the health, safety, and morals of the Child.  
2. Handling as referred to in paragraph (1) must be carried out immediately including:  
a. identifying and receiving complaints/reports;  
b. rescue actions;  
c. placement of Children in temporary shelters;  
d. rehabilitation including:  
1\. health recovery services;  
2\. psychosocial recovery services; and
3\. legal assistance/expert witness support.  
e. social reintegration in the form of post-rehabilitation service support.

#### Article 24

1. Identification and receipt of complaints/reports as referred to in Article 23 paragraph (2) letter a includes:  
a. ensuring the readiness of complaint services for Child Protection issues;  
b. following up on information or complaints/reports received regarding Child Protection issues; and  
c. identifying the type of problem, needs, and handling plan.  
2. The SKPD responsible for Child Protection affairs must identify problems and receive complaints/reports.

#### Article 25

1. Rescue actions as referred to in Article 23 paragraph (2) letter b shall be carried out if based on the results of identification it is known that the Child's safety is threatened.  
2. Actions as referred to in paragraph (1) shall be carried out by removing the Child from the threatening situation and environment.
3. The SKPD responsible for Child Protection affairs shall carry out rescue actions and coordinate with the Police/Civil Service Police Unit.

#### Article 26

1. Placement of Children in temporary shelters as referred to in Article 23 paragraph (2) letter c shall be carried out if based on the results of identification it is known that:  
a. the Child's safety is threatened;  
b. the Child has no family/caregiver or guardian; and  
c. the Child cannot be reunited with family/caregiver/guardian and/or the Community.  
2. Placement as referred to in paragraph (1) shall be carried out for a certain period of time while the Child receives recovery services and/or until the family/caregiver/guardian is deemed to have the readiness to care for and protect the Child.  
3. In the placement as referred to in paragraph (2), the Child shall receive services for the fulfillment of basic needs and psychosocial assistance.  
4. The SKPD responsible for social affairs must provide temporary shelters.

#### Article 27

1. Health recovery services as referred to in Article 23 paragraph (2) letter d number 1 include:  
a. emergency services;  
b. providing visum et repertum or visum psikiatricum upon police request or statement;  
c. follow-up services in the form of outpatient and/or inpatient care in accordance with medical provisions; and  
d. providing further referrals in accordance with the victim's circumstances and condition.  
2. The SKPD responsible for health affairs must provide health recovery services.

#### Article 28

1. Psychosocial recovery services as referred to in Article 23 paragraph (2) letter d number 2 include:  
a. counseling;  
b. psychosocial therapy;  
c. mental and spiritual guidance; and  
d. assistance.  
2. The SKPD responsible for social affairs must provide psychosocial recovery services.

#### Article 29

Legal assistance/expert witness services as referred to in Article 23 paragraph (2) letter d number 3 include:  
a. ensuring the Child is accompanied by legal counsel; and  
b. facilitating assistance to Child victims of violence, both in the examination process in court proceedings and outside court proceedings.

#### Article 30

1. Social reintegration in the form of post-rehabilitation service support as referred to in Article 23 paragraph (2) letter e includes:  
a. tracing family members;  
b. reuniting the Child victim and family members/substitute family and/or the Community;  
c. facilitating the provision of psychosocial assistance for families;  
d. support for access to further education or health services; and  
e. monitoring and evaluation.  
2. The SKPD responsible for social affairs shall implement social reintegration.

#### Article 31

1. Handling as referred to in Articles 23 through 30 shall be implemented synergistically.  
2. The implementation of handling as referred to in paragraph (1) shall be coordinated by the SKPD responsible for Child Protection affairs.

## Part Four

Protection of Children in the Womb

#### Article 32

Protection of Children in the womb as referred to in Article 22 paragraph (1) letter a shall be carried out through:  
a. provision of facilities and facilities for examination of Children in the womb;  
b. provision of nutritious food and immunization for pregnant women;  
c. provision of abortion prevention services;  
d. provision of appropriate and prompt delivery process services;  
e. provision of the best health services for mothers and Children during the delivery process and post-delivery; and  
f. implementation of Protection of Children in the womb for pregnant women with problems.

## Part Five

Protection of Toddlers

#### Article 33

1. Protection of Toddlers as referred to in

#### Article 22 paragraph (1) letter b is carried out through:  
a. provision of complete breastfeeding;  
b. provision of nutritious food and complete basic immunization;  
c. provision of Child growth and development program services and Child parenting patterns;  
d. provision of Child care facilities;  
e. provision of playgrounds; and  
f. provision of special breastfeeding rooms in certain places.  
2. Protection of Toddlers may be implemented through the PSTPA model and playgroups.  
3. PSTPA and/or playgroups as referred to in paragraph (2) must meet the following requirements:  
a. registered and received recommendation from the relevant SKPD within the Regional Government;  
b. have adequate human resources and funding sources to manage PSTPA and/or playgroups; and  
c. have facilities and infrastructure in accordance with service guidelines at PSTPA and/or playgroups.  
4. Protection of Toddlers may be implemented if in special or urgent conditions.

## Part Six

Protection of School-Age Children

#### Article 34

1. Protection of School-Age Children as referred to in Article 22 paragraph (1) letter c includes:  
a. receiving attention and guidance from teachers and/or mentors without discrimination;  
b. receiving attention and affection from family;  
c. receiving religious guidance at school and in the Community environment;  
d. receiving prevention, treatment and health rehabilitation services;  
e. receiving compulsory education for at least 9 (nine) years and being able to complete senior high school supported by a friendly and conducive environment;  
f. receiving education guarantee;  
g. receiving guidance and counseling; and  
h. providing adequate playgrounds and sports facilities.  
2. Every person and/or any party is obliged to protect School-Age Children from acts of violence, sexual harassment and food containing hazardous substances in the family, school, Office/agency/institution and Community environment.  
3. Every person and/or any party is obliged to protect School-Age Children from acts of suspending and/or expelling Children from school and/or causing Children to drop out of school.

## Part Seven

Protection of Neglected Children

#### Article 35

1. Protection of Neglected Children as referred to in Article 22 paragraph (1) letter d, namely Children whose parents do not have the ability to care for Children, is implemented through institutional and non-institutional forms of service;  
2. The institutional form of service as referred to in paragraph (2) is implemented by Child Protection Houses or PSAA owned by both the Regional Government and the Community.  
3. The non-institutional form of service as referred to in paragraph (2) is implemented within the family or Community environment that is not in the form of an institution.  
4. Child Protection Houses or PSAA owned by the Community as referred to in paragraph (2) must meet the following requirements:  
a. registered and received recommendation from the relevant SKPD within the Regional Government and/or Regency/City government;  
b. have adequate human resources and funding sources to manage Child Protection Houses or PSAA; and  
c. have facilities and infrastructure as determined in the service guidelines for Child Protection Houses or PSAA.

## Part Eight

Protection of Children Requiring Special Protection

#### Article 36

Children requiring Special Protection as referred to in Article 22 paragraph (1) letter e consist of:  
a. Children in emergency situations and conflict areas;  
b. Children in conflict with the law;  
c. Children from isolated indigenous communities;  
d. Children exploited economically and/or sexually;  
e. Children victims of Narcotics abuse including scented glue that can cause hallucinogenic effects and the like;  
f. Children victims of abduction, sale, and trafficking;  
g. Children victims of physical and/or psychological Violence;  
h. Children with special needs; and  
i. Children victims of abuse and neglect.

### Paragraph 1

Children in Emergency Situations and Conflict Areas

#### Article 37

Special Protection for Children in emergency situations and conflict areas as referred to in Article 36 letter a is in the form of fulfillment of basic needs consisting of:  
a. basic social services;  
b. education;  
c. religious guidance;  
d. health services;  
e. psychological counseling and/or psychotherapy;  
f. legal assistance; and  
g. recreational and educational activities.

### Paragraph 2

Children in Conflict with the Law

#### Article 38

1. Special Protection for Children in conflict with the law as referred to in Article 36 letter b includes Children in conflict with the law and Children victims of criminal acts, which is the obligation and responsibility of the Regional Government, Police, Prosecutor's Office, Court, Parents, Family and Community.  
2. Handling of Children in conflict with the legal process and implementation of Judge's decisions must continue to prioritize the Rights of the Child.  
3. Special Protection for Children in conflict with the law as referred to in paragraph (1) is implemented through:  
a. treatment of Children in a humane manner in accordance with the dignity and Rights of the Child;  
b. provision of special accompanying officers for Children from an early stage;  
c. provision of special facilities and infrastructure;  
d. imposition of appropriate sanctions for the best interests of the Child;  
e. continuous monitoring and recording of the development of Children in conflict with the law;  
f. provision of guarantees to maintain relationships with parents or family; and  
g. protection from identity disclosure through mass media and to avoid labeling.  
4. In the case of a Child in conflict with the law, law enforcement officials shall not conduct justicia Child examinations without the knowledge of parents or guardians, and shall be conducted with:  
a. rehabilitation efforts through special institutions for the best interests of the Child;  
b. protection efforts from identity disclosure through mass media and to avoid labeling;  
c. provision of safety guarantees for victim witnesses and expert witnesses, both physical, mental and social; and  
d. provision of accessibility to obtain information regarding case developments.

### Paragraph 3

Children from Isolated Indigenous Communities

#### Article 39

1. Special Protection for Children from isolated indigenous communities as referred to in Article 36 letter c is carried out through the provision of facilities and infrastructure to be able to enjoy their own culture, acknowledge and practice their own religion, and use their own language.  
2. Every person and/or and/or any parties are prohibited from obstructing Children as referred to in paragraph (1) from enjoying their own culture, acknowledging and practicing their religion, and using their own language without neglecting access to Community and cultural development.

### Paragraph 4

Children Exploited Economically and/or Sexually

#### Article 40

1. Special Protection for Children exploited economically and/or sexually as referred to in Article 36 letter d is carried out through:  
a. prevention of Children from supervision utilized by certain parties as objects of exploitation;  
b. disseminating and/or socializing laws and regulations related to Protection of Children exploited economically and/or sexually;  
c. monitoring, reporting and imposing sanctions; and  
d. involving various government agencies, companies, trade unions, NGOs, and the Community in eliminating exploitation of Children.  
2. Every person and/or any party is prohibited from conducting economic and/or sexual exploitation of Children as referred to in paragraph (1).

### Paragraph 5

Children Victims of Narcotics Abuse Including Scented Glue and the Like that can Cause Hallucinogenic Effects and the Like

#### Article 41

1. Special Protection for Children who are victims of narcotics abuse including aromatic glue and the like that can cause hallucinatory effects and the like as referred to in Article 36 letter e, and involved in the use, production and distribution thereof, shall be carried out through supervision, prevention, treatment, and rehabilitation efforts by the Regional Government and the Community.  
2. Every person and/or any party is prohibited from deliberately placing, allowing, involving, ordering the involvement of Children in the abuse, production and distribution of narcotics, aromatic glue and the like as referred to in paragraph (1).

### Paragraph 6

Children Victims of Kidnapping, Sale and Trafficking

#### Article 42

1. Special Protection for Children who are victims of kidnapping, sale and trafficking as referred to in Article 36 letter f shall be carried out through supervision, protection, prevention, treatment, rehabilitation and reintegration efforts by the Regional Government and the Community.  
2. To implement prevention efforts as referred to in paragraph (1), the Regional Government together with the Police, Prosecutor's Office, Courts, Community, NGOs and Social Organizations shall take steps in the form of:  
a. conducting socialization and/or campaigns on the prevention, handling and elimination of Child Trafficking practices;  
b. conducting cooperation with other Regions as well as with other countries through bilateral or multilateral cooperation in accordance with laws and regulations.  
3. Protection for Children who are victims of Child Trafficking shall be implemented through Shelters and/or other Child Protection Institutions, through referrals from government institutions or the Community.  
4. Every Child who is a victim of kidnapping, sale and trafficking, has the right to obtain physical, psychological treatment and rehabilitation and reintegration in the family environment as well as the Community.

### Paragraph 7

Children Victims of Physical and/or Psychological Violence

#### Article 43

1. Every Child who is a victim of physical and/or psychological Violence as referred to in Article 36 letter g shall receive rehabilitation services both physical and psychosocial.  
2. Forms of protection for Children who are victims of acts of Violence include basic social services, education, religious guidance, health services, psychological counseling and/or psychotherapy, legal assistance, recreational and educational activities and empowerment of parents of Children who are victims of acts of violence.  
3. Every person and/or any party is prohibited from committing acts of Violence against Children.

### Paragraph 8

Children with Special Needs

#### Article 44

1. Special Protection for Children with special needs as referred to in Article 36 letter h shall be carried out through efforts to:  
a. treat Children humanely in accordance with the dignity and Rights of the Child;  
b. fulfill special needs; and  
c. obtain equal treatment with other Children to achieve full social integration and individual development as much as possible.  
2. Every person and/or any party is prohibited from treating Children with special needs in a discriminatory manner, by disregarding their views including labeling and equalization in education for Children with special needs.

### Paragraph 9

Children Victims of Mistreatment and Neglect

#### Article 45

1. Special Protection for Children who are victims of mistreatment and neglect as referred to in Article 36 letter i shall be carried out through supervision, prevention, treatment and rehabilitation.  
2. Every person and/or any party is prohibited from placing, allowing, involving, ordering the involvement of Children in situations of mistreatment and neglect as referred to in paragraph (1).

# CHAPTER VII SAFE HOUSE

#### Article 46

1. Women and Children who are victims of Violence who require ongoing treatment have the right to stay in a safe house.  
2. The safe house as referred to in paragraph (1) shall provide a temporary safe place or shelter for victims of Violence until their rights are restored.  
3. The operation and management of the safe house as referred to in paragraph (1) shall be carried out by the Regional Apparatus that performs functions in the field of Women's Empowerment and Child Protection.  
4. Further provisions regarding the operation of the safe house as referred to in paragraph (1) shall be regulated by Regent Regulation.

# CHAPTER VIII

## WOMEN'S EMPOWERMENT AND

## CHILD PROTECTION BY VILLAGE GOVERNMENT

#### Article 47

1. The Village Government shall implement Women's Empowerment and Child Protection in the Village.  
2. The implementation of Women's Empowerment and Child Protection as regulated in this Regional Regulation shall apply mutatis mutandis in the implementation of Women's Empowerment and Child Protection by the Regional Government in accordance with the provisions of laws and regulations.  
3. Funding for the implementation of Women's Empowerment and Child Protection by the Regional Government as referred to in paragraph (1) shall be charged to the Village revenue and expenditure budget.  
4. Further provisions regarding the implementation of Women's Empowerment and Child Protection by the Regional Government shall be regulated by Regent Regulation.

# CHAPTER IX

## FAMILY QUALITY

#### Article 48

1. The Regional Government is obliged to improve family quality in the form of:  
a. improving family quality in realizing gender equality and Child Rights.  
b. strengthening and developing service provider institutions for improving family quality in realizing gender equality and Child Rights whose working area is within the Region.  
c. providing services for families in realizing gender equality and Child Rights whose working area is within the Region.  
2. Provisions regarding procedures for improving family quality as referred to in paragraph (1) shall be regulated in a Regent Regulation.

# CHAPTER X COOPERATION

#### Article 49

1. In efforts to implement Women's Empowerment and Child Protection, the Regional Government may cooperate.  
2. Cooperation as referred to in paragraph (1) shall be carried out with:  
a. the government;  
b. the provincial government;  
c. other Regional Governments;  
d. vertical agencies;  
e. advocates;  
f. social workers;  
g. volunteer companions;  
h. spiritual counselors; and  
i. non-governmental organizations engaged in the field of Women's Empowerment and Child Protection.  
3. Forms of cooperation as referred to in paragraph (1) include:  
a. socialization;  
b. provision of legal assistance;  
c. health services;  
d. victim assistance;  
e. counseling;  
f. social rehabilitation; and  
g. spiritual guidance.  
4. Procedures for implementing cooperation as referred to in paragraph (1) shall be in accordance with the provisions of laws and regulations.

# CHAPTER XI 

AWARDS

#### Article 50

1. The Regent may grant awards for the participation of every person and/or entity that contributes to Women's Empowerment and Child Protection in the Region.  
2. Forms of awards as referred to in paragraph (1) include:  
a. plaques/certificates of appreciation;  
b. grants; and/or  
c. Regional incentives.  
3. Regional incentives as referred to in paragraph (2) letter c include:  
a. Regional fiscal incentives; and/or  
b. Regional non-fiscal incentives.  
4. Forms of Regional fiscal incentives as referred to in paragraph (3) letter a, include:  
a. reduction and/or exemption of Regional taxes; and/or  
b. reduction of Regional levies.  
5. Forms of Regional non-fiscal incentives as referred to in paragraph (3) letter b, include ease of business licensing in the Region.  
6. Procedures for granting awards as referred to in paragraph (1) shall be guided by the provisions of laws and regulations.

# CHAPTER XII

## COMMUNITY PARTICIPATION

#### Article 51

1. Regional Government has the obligation to strengthen Community participation in the development of Women's Empowerment and Child Protection.  
2. Community participation as referred to in paragraph (1) may be carried out in the form of:  
a. socialization;  
b. advocacy;  
c. counseling;  
d. guidance;  
e. supervision;  
f. facilitation;  
g. services;  
h. studies;  
i. training;  
j. capital; and  
k. provision of infrastructure and facilities.  
3. Strengthening of Community participation as referred to in paragraph (1) may be carried out through:  
a. strengthening cooperation;  
b. strengthening institutional capacity for prevention and services from the Community, Islamic boarding schools and the business sector; and  
c. financing support.

#### Article 52

1. The business sector has responsibility in the implementation of Women's Empowerment and Child Protection.  
2. The form of business sector responsibility as referred to in paragraph (1) is carried out in the form of:  
a. financial assistance to service institutions;  
b. empowerment;  
c. capacity building;  
d. publication and information media;  
e. provision of infrastructure and facilities;  
f. establishment of special institutions for prevention and violence complaint services; and  
g. non-binding social assistance.

# CHAPTER XIII

## FINANCING

#### Article 53

Financing for Women's Empowerment and Child Protection in this Regional Regulation is charged to the Regional revenue and expenditure budget and other lawful sources and legislation that are non-binding in accordance with statutory provisions taking into account the Regional fiscal capacity.

# CHAPTER XIV PROHIBITIONS

#### Article 54

Every person is prohibited from:  
a. treating Women and Children in a discriminatory manner that results in Women and Children suffering losses, both material and moral, thereby hindering their social function;  
b. treating persons with disabilities in a discriminatory manner;  
c. placing, allowing, involving, ordering the involvement of Children in situations of maltreatment and neglect;  
d. placing, allowing, committing, ordering or participating in committing Violence against Women and Children;  
e. committing Violence or threat of Violence to force Women and Children to have sexual intercourse with him or her or with another person;  
f. committing Violence or threat of violence, forcing, committing fraud, committing a series of lies, or persuading Women and Children to commit or allow obscene acts to be committed;  
g. placing, allowing, committing, ordering or participating in committing kidnapping, sale and/or trafficking of Women and Children;  
h. preventing Women and Children from enjoying their own culture, acknowledging and practicing their religious teachings and/or using their own language without neglecting access to Community and cultural development;  
i. placing, allowing, committing, ordering or participating in committing economic and/or sexual exploitation of Women and Children;  
j. intentionally placing, allowing, involving Women and Children in the abuse, as well as production and distribution of narcotics and/or psychotropic substances; and/or  
k. intentionally placing, allowing, involving, ordering the involvement of Women and Children in the abuse, as well as production and distribution of alcohol and other addictive substances.

# CHAPTER XV

## INVESTIGATION PROVISIONS

#### Article 55

1. Civil Servant Investigators within the Regional Government are given special authority to conduct investigations of criminal acts as referred to in the applicable Criminal Procedure Code. (2) The authority of investigators as referred to in paragraph (1) is:  
a. receiving, seeking, collecting and examining information or reports concerning criminal acts;  
b. examining, seeking and collecting information or reports concerning criminal acts;  
c. requesting information and evidence from individuals or entities in connection with criminal acts;  
d. examining books, records and other documents concerning criminal acts;  
e. conducting searches to obtain evidence of bookkeeping, records and other documents as well as conducting seizures of such evidence;  
f. requesting assistance from experts in the context of carrying out criminal investigation duties;  
g. ordering to stop, prohibiting someone from leaving a room or place when an examination is in progress and examining the identity of persons and or documents carried as referred to in letter e;  
h. photographing persons related to criminal acts;  
i. summoning persons to be heard for their statements and examined as suspects or witnesses;  
j. terminating investigations;  
k. taking other actions necessary for the smooth conduct of criminal investigations according to law and which can be accounted for.  
2. Investigators as referred to in paragraph (1), in accordance with applicable provisions, shall notify the commencement of the investigation and submit the results of their investigation to the public prosecutor through the Indonesian National Police Investigator in accordance with the provisions stipulated in the Criminal Procedure Code.

# CHAPTER XVI 

CRIMINAL PROVISIONS

#### Article 56

1. Any person who violates the provisions as referred to in Article 34 paragraph (2) and paragraph (3), Article 39 paragraph (2), Article 40 paragraph (2), Article 41 paragraph (2), Article 43 paragraph (3), Article 44 paragraph (2) and Article 54, shall be punished with imprisonment of a maximum of 6 months or a fine of a maximum of Rp50,000,000.00 (fifty million rupiah).  
2. The criminal act as referred to in paragraph (1) constitutes a violation.

# CHAPTER XVII

## TRANSITIONAL PROVISIONS

#### Article 57

At the time this Regional Regulation comes into force, Regional policies concerning Women's Empowerment and Child Protection in the Region that already exist shall remain valid, insofar as they do not conflict with this Regional Regulation.

#### Article 58

At the time the provisions of Article 624 of Law Number 1 of 2023 concerning the Criminal Code (State Gazette of the Republic of Indonesia Year 2023 Number 1, Supplement to the State Gazette of the Republic of Indonesia Number 6842) come into force, then:  
a. imprisonment as referred to in Article 56 paragraph (1) shall be replaced with a fine of a maximum of category I; and  
b. category I fine as referred to in letter a shall be Rp1,000,000.00 (one million rupiah).

# CHAPTER XVIII

## CLOSING PROVISIONS

#### Article 59

Regent Regulations as implementation of this Regional Regulation shall be stipulated no later than 1 (one) year from the promulgation of this Regional Regulation.

#### Article 60

This Regional Regulation shall come into force on the date of promulgation.  

In order that everyone may know of it, the promulgation of this Regional Regulation is ordered by its placement in the Regional Gazette of Tanah Bumbu Regency.

# ELUCIDATION OF REGIONAL REGULATION OF TANAH BUMBU REGENCY 

NUMBER 12 OF 2023 CONCERNING THE IMPLEMENTATION OF WOMEN'S EMPOWERMENT AND CHILD PROTECTION

##### 1. GENERAL

In order to realize Women who are capable physically, mentally, spiritually, socially, in knowledge, and skills so that they are ready to be utilized, as well as efforts to protect Women and Children from all acts of Violence in order to achieve the goal of welfare through a Regional regulation that can serve as a reference for all parties related to Women's Empowerment and Child Protection.

The formulation of Regional regulations concerning Women's Empowerment and Child Protection has the purpose and objective as the implementation of the broadest Regional autonomy, so the Women's Empowerment and Child Protection sector is one of the sectors that is quite important to be managed and regulated in order to realize Women who are capable physically, mentally, spiritually, socially, in knowledge, and skills so that they are ready to be utilized, as well as efforts to protect Women and Children from all acts of Violence in order to achieve the goal of welfare through a draft Regional regulation that can serve as a reference for all parties related to Women's Empowerment and Child Protection.

The establishment of this Regional regulation is based on the principles of good legislation formation and the principles of material content of legislation.

The implementation of governmental affairs in the field of Women's Empowerment and Child Protection aims to increase efforts to Protect Women's rights, fulfill Children's Rights and Special Protection for Children and increase the role of Regional Government and the business world, media and the Community in efforts for Women's Empowerment and Child Protection. The principal matters contained in the body of the Regional Regulation concerning the Implementation of Women's Empowerment and Protection and Children include:
a. Women's Empowerment;
b. Women's Protection;
c. Child Protection;
d. safe houses;
e. Women's Empowerment and Child Protection by Village government;
f. family quality;
g. cooperation;
h. awards;

j. financing;
k. prohibitions;
1. investigation provisions; and
m. criminal provisions.

##### II. ARTICLE BY ARTICLE

#### Article 1

Sufficiently clear.

#### Article 2

Point a
What is meant by "respect for human rights is that the protection and empowerment of women is carried out as a manifestation of respect for women as part of Human Rights.

Point b
What is meant by "gender equality" is one of the rights as a human being, the right to live with dignity, free from fear and free to determine life choices is not only intended for men, women also have the same rights in essence.

Point c
What is meant by "non-discrimination" is an attitude and treatment toward Children by not making distinctions based on age, gender, race, ethnicity, religion and between groups, and the condition of the Child.

Point d
What is meant by "justice and legal certainty is that in implementing the protection and empowerment of women it is based on justice and legal certainty of equality between men and women.

Point e
What is meant by "benefit" is providing the broadest benefit for children and women for the interests of society, nation and state.

Point f
What is meant by "participatory" is that every member of society is encouraged to play an active role in the decision-making process and implementation of child protection and women's empowerment, either directly or indirectly.

Point g
What is meant by "the best interests of the Child" is that in all actions concerning Children carried out by the Regional Government, parents/family, and the Community, the best interests of the Child must be the primary consideration.
Point h What is meant by "accountability" is a principle that requires that every activity and final result of state administration activities must be accountable to the community and the people as holders of the highest sovereignty of the state in accordance with applicable statutory provisions.

Point i What is meant by "gender responsive" is consistent and systematic attention to the differences between women and men in society accompanied by efforts to eliminate structural and cultural barriers to achieve gender equality.
