REGENT OF DHARMASRAYA.

WEST SUMATRA PROVINCE

# REGIONAL REGULATION OF DHARMASRAYA REGENCY

NUMBER 16 OF 2018

CONCERNING

WOMEN'S EMPOWERMENT AND CHILD PROTECTION

BY THE GRACE OF ALMIGHTY GOD

REGENT OF DHARMASRAYA,

Considering:
 
 
 
 
 
 
 
a. that women and children have the right to obtain a sense of security and freedom from all forms of torture or treatment that degrades human dignity;
b. that women and children are vulnerable groups who tend to experience violence, therefore they need to receive optimal protection;
c. that the fulfillment of the constitutional rights of women and children as well as the improvement of the quality of life of women and children, is one of the mandatory affairs that is the duty, authority and responsibility of the Regional Government; d. that based on the considerations as referred to in letter a, letter b and letter c, it is necessary to establish a Regional Regulation on Women's Empowerment and Child Protection;

In View of:
 
 
 
 
 
 
 
1. Article 18 paragraph (6) of the 1945 Constitution of the Republic of Indonesia;
2. Law Number 23 of 2002 concerning Child Protection (State Gazette of the Republic of Indonesia of 2002 Number 109, Supplement to the State Gazette of the Republic of Indonesia Number 4236) as has been amended several times most recently by Law Number 17 of 2016 concerning the Stipulation of Government Regulation in Lieu of Law Number 1 of 2016 concerning the Second Amendment to Law Number 23 of 2002 concerning Child Protection into Law (State Gazette of the Republic of Indonesia of 2016 Number 237, Supplement to the State Gazette of the Republic of Indonesia Number 5946);
3. Law Number 38 of 2003 concerning the Establishment of Dharmasraya Regency, South Solok Regency and West Pasaman Regency in West Sumatra Province (State Gazette of the Republic of Indonesia of 2003 Number 153, Supplement to the State Gazette of the Republic of Indonesia Number 4348;
4. Law Number 23 of 2004 concerning the Elimination of Domestic Violence (State Gazette of the Republic of Indonesia of 2004 Number 95, Supplement to the State Gazette of the Republic of Indonesia Number 4419;
5. Law Number 23 of 2014 concerning Regional Government (State Gazette of the Republic of Indonesia of 2014), as has been amended several times, most recently by Law Number 32 of 2004 concerning the Third Amendment to Law Number 23 of 2014 concerning Regional Government (State Gazette of the Republic of Indonesia of 2014);
6. Regional Regulation of West Sumatra Province Number 5 of 2013 concerning the Protection of Women and Children (Regional Gazette of West Sumatra Province of 2013 Number 5, Supplement to the Regional Gazette of West Sumatra Province Number 88);

# CHAPTER I

## GENERAL PROVISIONS

## Part One

Definitions

#### Article 1

In this Regional Regulation, what is meant by:  
1. Region is Dharmasraya Regency.  
2. Regional Government is the Regent and regional apparatus as elements of the Regional Government administration of Dharmasraya Regency.  
3. Regent is the Regent of Dharmasraya.  
4. Regional Apparatus is the Regional Apparatus within the Government of Dharmasraya Regency.  
5. Social Service, Women's Empowerment, Child Protection, Population Control and Family Planning, hereinafter referred to as the Service, is the Regional Apparatus that handles affairs in the Field of Implementation of Women's and Children's Protection.  
6. Women's Protection is all efforts to guarantee and protect women's rights so that they can live healthily and participate optimally in accordance with human dignity and obtain protection from neglect, violence and discrimination, whether in the form of preventive, repressive or post-remedial protection (assistance and treatment).  
7. Gender Mainstreaming is a strategy carried out rationally and systematically to achieve and realize gender equality and justice in a number of aspects of human life, through policies and programs that take into account the experiences, aspirations, needs and problems of women and men in the planning, implementation, monitoring and evaluation of all policies and programs in various fields of life and development.  
8. Gender Mainstreaming Working Group, hereinafter referred to as the Gender Mainstreaming Working Group, is a consultation forum for implementers and drivers of gender mainstreaming from various agencies/institutions in the region.  
9. Gender Mainstreaming Focal Point is an apparatus in the Regional Apparatus who has the ability to carry out gender mainstreaming in their respective work units.  
10. Child is someone who has not yet reached the age of 18 (eighteen) years, including children still in the womb.  
11. Child protection is all activities to guarantee and protect children and their rights so that they can live, grow, develop, and participate optimally in accordance with human dignity, and receive protection from violence and discrimination.  
12. Prevention is a direct effort carried out by the Regional Government, community and parents and/or family to prevent violations of women's and children's rights.  
13. Children's Forum is a forum for children's participation formed with the aim of facilitating children's involvement in the decision-making process and enjoying changes concerning their lives either directly or indirectly, which is carried out with the consent and will of all children based on awareness and understanding, in accordance with age and level of maturity of thinking.  
14. Child-Friendly Regency, hereinafter abbreviated as KLA, is a Regency that has a child rights-based development system through the integration of government, community and business commitments and resources that are planned comprehensively and sustainably in policies, programs and activities to ensure the fulfillment of children's rights.  
15. Women victims of violence are women who experience actions that result in misery or suffering physically, sexually or psychologically, including threats of certain actions, coercion or deprivation of freedom arbitrarily, whether occurring in the public sphere or in private life.  
16. Child victims of violence are children who experience actions that result in misery or suffering physically, mentally, sexually, psychologically or neglect that threatens life, body and degrades human dignity.  
17. Human trafficking is the act of recruitment, transportation, harboring, sending, transfer, or receipt of a person by threat of violence, use of violence, abduction, confinement, forgery, fraud, abuse of power or position of vulnerability, debt bondage or giving payment or benefits, so as to obtain consent from the person holding control over that other person, whether carried out within the country or between countries, for the purpose of exploitation or resulting in the person being exploited.  
18. Complaint handling is a series of actions carried out by integrated service providers to follow up on reports of acts of violence against women and children, submitted by victims, families or the community.  
19. Social rehabilitation is a service aimed at restoring and developing the ability of a person experiencing social dysfunction so that they can carry out their social functions properly.  
20. Law enforcement is the action of apparatus authorized by the state to implement statutory regulations.  
21. Legal aid is legal services provided by legal assistants and advocates to carry out the process of assisting witnesses and/or victims of violence against women and children that is gender-sensitive.  
22. Repatriation is an effort to return women and child victims of violence from abroad to the debarkation point/entry point, or from the receiving area to the area of origin.  
23. Social reintegration is an effort to reunite victims with family, substitute family, or community that can provide protection and fulfillment of needs for victims.  
24. Safe house is a temporary shelter that aims to provide protection and a sense of security to victims of violence.  
25. Spiritual guidance is an effort to restore the soul through strengthening the religion adhered to by the victim. 26. Gender responsive is consistent and systematic attention to the differences between women and men in society accompanied by efforts to eliminate structural and cultural barriers in achieving gender equality.  
27. Parents are biological father and/or mother, or stepfather and/or stepmother, or adoptive father and/or adoptive mother. 28. Family is the smallest unit in society consisting of husband and wife or husband, wife and their children, or father and his children, or mother and her children, or blood relatives in a straight line up or down to the third degree.  
29. Customs are eternal patterns of behavior passed down from generation to generation as heritage, so that they are strongly integrated with community behavior patterns.

## Part Two

Principles, Objectives and Scope

#### Article 2

1. The implementation of women's protection is based on the principles of:  
a. best interests of women;  
b. gender justice and gender equality;  
c. local wisdom; and  
d. legal certainty.  
2. The implementation of child protection is based on the principles of:  
a. non-discrimination;  
b. best interests of the child;  
c. right to life, survival, and development;  
d. respect for the views of the child; and
e. legal certainty.

#### Article 3

Women's empowerment and child protection aims to:  
a. serve as a guideline for regional government in planning, policies and strategies for women's and children's protection;  
b. protect women and children from actions, decisions and policies that violate women's and children's rights;  
c. prevent violations of women's and children's rights;  
d. increase empowerment of women and children;  
e. enhance the role and value of local wisdom and the role of customs in the protection of women and children; and  
f. enhance the role of government and non-government institutions in the region in efforts to protect women and children.

#### Article 4

The scope of regulation of women's and children's protection includes:  
a. rights and obligations;  
b. improvement of women's quality of life;  
c. protection of women and children;  
d. improvement of family quality;  
e. gender and child data system;  
f. fulfillment of children's rights;  
g. child-friendly regency; and  
h. guidance and supervision.

# CHAPTER II

## RIGHTS AND OBLIGATIONS

## Part One

Rights of Women and Children

### Paragraph 1

Rights of Women

#### Article 5

Every woman has the right to:  
a. obtain protection from discriminatory acts;  
b. obtain protection from economic exploitation and sexual exploitation;  
c. obtain protection from physical, psychological, sexual violence, and neglect;  
d. obtain decent work in accordance with her abilities;  
e. obtain work leave while receiving wages/salary in accordance with laws and regulations;  
f. obtain education and teaching in all types, levels and pathways of education;  
g. obtain special protection in the implementation of work or profession against matters that may threaten safety and or health in relation to women's reproductive function; and  
h. ownership and management of joint assets in marriage in accordance with laws and regulations.

### Paragraph 2

Rights of Children

#### Article 6

Every child has the right to:  
a. live, grow, develop and participate reasonably in accordance with human dignity;  
b. obtain protection from discriminatory acts;  
c. obtain protection from economic exploitation and sexual exploitation;  
d. obtain protection from physical, psychological, sexual violence, and neglect;  
e. a name as personal identity and citizenship status;  
f. obtain a birth certificate free of charge;  
g. embrace religion and worship according to the teachings of their religion with the guidance of parents/guardians;  
h. not be forced to change religion by foster parents, adoptive parents and by anyone;  
i. be recognized and know their parents, be raised and cared for by their own parents;  
j. obtain health services and social security in accordance with physical, mental, spiritual, and social needs. k. obtain education and teaching in accordance with their interests and talents;  
l. obtain care, education, training, self-improvement and ability to participate in community and state life for children with physical, mental disabilities and special needs;  
m. express and have their opinion heard; and  
n. rest and utilize leisure time for self-development.

## Part Two

Obligations of Regional Government

#### Article 7

The obligations of the Regional Government in efforts to protect women include:  
a. establish and implement policy programs and activities for the protection of women's rights;  
b. cooperate with the community and/or Community Organizations in the implementation of women's protection in accordance with laws and regulations;  
c. conduct rehabilitation and empowerment of women who are victims of violence;  
d. provide special protection to women victims of violence and human trafficking; and  
e. take preventive action in order to prevent violations of women's rights.

#### Article 8

The obligations of the Regional Government in efforts to protect children include:  
a. guarantee the human rights of every child without discrimination;  
b. provide support for facilities and infrastructure in the implementation of child protection;  
c. guarantee the right of children to express opinions in accordance with the age and level of intelligence of the child;  
d. establish and implement policies and programs/activities for the protection of children's rights;  
e. cooperate with the community and/or Community Organizations in the implementation of child protection in accordance with laws and regulations;  
f. conduct rehabilitation of children who are victims of violence;  
g. provide special protection to children in emergency situations, children in conflict with the law, children victims of violence and human trafficking, children from minority and isolated groups, children exploited economically and/or sexually, children who are victims of narcotics, alcohol, psychotropic abuse and children with HIV/AIDS;  
h. organize inclusive education for children with special needs; and  
i. take preventive action in order to prevent violations of children's rights.

## Part Three

Obligations of the Community

#### Article 9

1. The obligations of the community in efforts to protect women and children:  
a. participate in prevention and provide support for the development of prevention programs for violations of the rights of women and children;  
b. provide information and/or report violations of the rights of women and children to the competent authorities; and  
c. supervise the law enforcement process in cases of violations of the rights of women and children.  
2. Ninik mamak, alim ulama and cadiak pandai as well as bundo kanduang are responsible for protecting and preventing violations of the rights of women and children.  
3. The obligations of the community as referred to in paragraph (1) and paragraph (2) are based on laws and regulations and the values of local wisdom of the local community.

## Part Four

Obligations of Family and/or Parents

### Paragraph 1

Obligations Towards the Protection of Women

#### Article 10

The family in efforts to protect the rights of women is obliged to:  
a. protect women from conflict and violence in the household that degrades the dignity of women;  
b. provide equal opportunities for women to develop themselves in the fields of education, social, economic, political and cultural; and  
c. prevent violations of women's rights in the household.

### Paragraph 2

Obligations Towards the Protection of Children

#### Article 11

1. Parents in efforts to protect the rights of children are obliged to:  
a. care for, maintain, educate, and protect children;  
b. develop children in accordance with their abilities, talents, and interests;  
c. provide formal and non-formal education;  
d. guide children to behave in accordance with religion, ethics and morals as well as customs; and
e. prevent marriage at child age.  
2. In the event that parents do not exist, or their whereabouts are unknown, or due to a reason, cannot carry out their obligations and responsibilities, then the obligations and responsibilities as referred to in paragraph (1) may be transferred to the family.

# CHAPTER III

## IMPROVING THE QUALITY OF LIFE OF WOMEN

## Part One

General

#### Article 12

1. In order to improve the quality of life of women, the Regional Government is responsible for:  
a. respecting and guaranteeing the rights of women without discrimination in accordance with human dignity;  
b. implementing Gender Mainstreaming in the Region;  
c. forming a Gender Mainstreaming working group in the Region;  
d. increasing the empowerment of Women in roles, quality and capacity in the fields of:
1\. politics;  
2\. law;  
3\. social; and
4\. economy.  
e. strengthening and developing service provider institutions for women's empowerment in the region.  
2. The responsibility of the regional government in efforts to improve the quality of life of women as referred to in paragraph (1) is carried out by the relevant Regional Apparatus.

## Part Two

Implementation of Gender Mainstreaming in the Region

#### Article 13

1. The Regent is responsible for the implementation of Gender Mainstreaming in the Region.  
2. The responsibility of the Regent as referred to in paragraph (1) may be delegated to the Vice Regent.

#### Article 14

In implementing Gender Mainstreaming in the region, the Regent appoints the Regional Apparatus in charge of Women's Empowerment as coordinator of the implementation of Gender Mainstreaming.

## Part Three

Formation of Gender Mainstreaming Working Group

#### Article 15

1. In an effort to accelerate the institutionalization of Gender Mainstreaming in all Regional Apparatus, a Gender Mainstreaming Working Group is formed.  
2. The Gender Mainstreaming Working Group as referred to in paragraph (1) consists of:  
a. chairperson: Head of Bapppeda;  
b. secretary: Head of the Regional Apparatus in charge of Women's Empowerment; and c. members: all heads/leaders of Regional Apparatus.  
3. The formation of the Gender Mainstreaming working group as referred to in paragraph (1) is stipulated by Regent's Decree.

#### Article 16

The Regional Gender Mainstreaming Working Group as referred to in Article 15 paragraph (1) has the following duties:  
a. promoting and facilitating Gender Mainstreaming to each Regional Apparatus;  
b. conducting socialization and advocacy of Gender Mainstreaming to Sub-district Heads and Wali Nagari;  
c. preparing an annual work program;  
d. encouraging the realization of gender-responsive planning and budgeting;  
e. preparing the annual work plan of the Gender Mainstreaming working group;  
f. being accountable to the Regent through the Vice Regent;  
g. formulating Gender Mainstreaming policy recommendations to the Regent;  
h. preparing the Regional Gender profile;  
i. monitoring the implementation of Gender Mainstreaming in each Regional Apparatus.  
j. establishing a technical team to conduct analysis of the gender-responsive regional budget;  
k. preparing a regional action plan on Gender Mainstreaming in the region; and  
l. encouraging the implementation of selection and appointment of focal points in each Regional Apparatus.

#### Article 17

1. The technical team as referred to in Article 16 letter j consists of officials who understand gender-responsive budget analysis.  
2. The Regional Gender Mainstreaming regional action plan as referred to in Article 16 letter k contains:  
a. gender mainstreaming in regional legislation;  
b. gender mainstreaming in the regional development cycle;  
c. strengthening of Gender Mainstreaming institutions in the region; and  
d. strengthening community participation in the region

#### Article 18

1. The Gender Mainstreaming Focal Point in each Regional Apparatus as referred to in Article 16 letter l consists of officials and/or staff in charge of planning and/or programs.  
2. The Gender Mainstreaming Focal Point as referred to in paragraph (1), has the following duties:  
a. promoting Gender Mainstreaming in the work unit;  
b. facilitating the preparation of gender-responsive work plans and budgeting of Regional Apparatus;  
c. conducting training, socialization, advocacy of Gender Mainstreaming to all officials and staff within the Regional Apparatus;  
d. reporting the implementation of Gender Mainstreaming to the leadership of the Regional Apparatus;  
e. encouraging the implementation of gender analysis on policies, programs, and activities in the work unit; and  
f. facilitating the preparation of gender data in each Regional Apparatus.  
4. The Gender Mainstreaming Focal Point as referred to in paragraph (1), is selected and appointed by the leadership of the Regional Apparatus

## Part Four

Increasing the Role, Quality, and Capacity of Women in Politics

#### Article 19

Efforts to increase the role, quality and capacity of women in politics include:  
a. conducting socialization to increase women's political participation in general elections;  
b. implementing programs that encourage the level of women's involvement in political processes and public positions;  
c. facilitating women's self-development in organizing, associating and assembling;  
d. involving women in decision-making concerning women's interests;

## Part Five

Increasing the Role, Quality, and Capacity of Women in Law

#### Article 20

Efforts to increase the role, quality and capacity of women in law include:  
a. conducting socialization to increase women's legal knowledge and awareness;  
b. mapping and reviewing gender-biased policies;  
c. involving women's organizations in the process of drafting and discussing Regional legal products related to women's participation;

## Part Six

Increasing the Role, Quality, and Capacity of Women in Social Affairs

#### Article 21

Efforts to increase the role, quality and capacity of women in social affairs include:  
a. increasing knowledge and skills to encourage the fulfillment of education in accordance with existing potential;  
b. increasing and fulfilling the quality of health for women;  
c. increasing awareness and knowledge about independent, healthy and prosperous family planning including access to consultation services and marriage registration;  
d. increasing women's awareness of the impact of early marriage;  
e. facilitating and seeking the preservation of customs and the development of science, technology, arts and culture for the advancement of women;  
f. increasing women's awareness and knowledge of independent, healthy and prosperous family planning;  
g. implementing programs to reduce maternal mortality rates;  
h. encouraging the formation of women's organizations in the Region;

## Part Seven

Increasing the Role, Quality, and Capacity of Women in Economics

#### Article 22

Efforts to increase the role, quality and capacity of women in the field include:  
a. implementing programs, activities, training to improve women's skills and expertise in order to realize the improvement of women's welfare;  
b. facilitating business capital strengthening for women with weak economies;  
c. marketing products from businesses of women with weak economies;  
d. training to develop women's capacity through economic empowerment of pre-prosperous families;  
e. comprehensive entrepreneurship training for women.

# CHAPTER IV

PROTECTION OF WOMEN AND CHILDREN Part One Prevention of Violence against Women and Children

#### Article 23

1. The Regional Government's responsibility to prevent acts of violence against women and children includes:  
a. conducting socialization regarding the prevention of violence against women and children by optimizing the role of customary, cultural, religious and community organizations;  
b. providing guidelines on handling acts of violence against women and children;  
c. involving community participation in recovery and reintegration;  
d. developing an integrated database and information system on acts of violence against women and children;  
e. developing family empowerment programs;  
f. strengthening the function of community organizations engaged in women's empowerment and child protection; and  
g. providing public facilities that are comfortable and safe for women and children.  
2. Prevention of acts of violence against women and children as referred to in paragraph (1), is carried out by Regional Apparatus whose duties and functions are in the fields of:  
a. social affairs;  
b. health;  
c. education;  
d. manpower;  
e. women's empowerment and child protection;  
f. mental and spiritual;  
g. informatics and communication; and  
h. peace and order.  
3. Prevention as referred to in paragraph (1), is carried out by Regional Apparatus in accordance with their respective fields of duty in an integrated and sustainable manner based on the Regional Action Plan.

#### Article 24

The community's responsibility to prevent acts of violence against women and children includes:  
a. monitoring various conditions occurring in their environment that may cause violations of the rights of women and children;  
b. increasing awareness of violations of the rights of women and children;  
c. strengthening the role of community, religious and family organizations; and  
d. preventing unregistered marriages and marriages at child age.

#### Article 25

The responsibility of parents and families to prevent acts of violence against women and children includes:  
a. protecting women and children from various violations of rights within the household;  
b. encouraging the formation of harmonious, educative and democratic families;  
c. providing good examples to children in accordance with religious and customary teachings;  
d. supervising children in their activities inside or outside the home; and  
e. providing opportunities for children to engage in dialogue and express opinions democratically.

## Part Three

Effectiveness of Services for Women and Children

### Paragraph 1

General

#### Article 26

1. In order to implement the Protection of Women and Children in the Region, the Regional Government establishes UPTDPPA in accordance with the provisions of laws and regulations.  
2. UPTD PPA as referred to in paragraph (1) functions to provide services to women and Children who experience criminal and/or civil problems.  
3. The scope of services of UPTD PPA as referred to in paragraph (2) includes:  
a. complaint services;  
b. health rehabilitation;  
c. social rehabilitation;  
d. assistance;  
e. law enforcement and legal aid; and  
f. repatriation and social reintegration.  
4. In carrying out its functions, UPTD PPA is obliged to:  
a. provide services and handling as quickly as possible to women and children who experience problems;  
b. provide convenience, comfort, safety and free of charge to women and children who experience problems;  
c. maintain the confidentiality of women and children who experience problems;  
d. guarantee justice and legal certainty for women and children who experience problems.  
5. UPTD PPA is responsible to the Regional Apparatus that administers governmental affairs in the field of Women's Empowerment and Child Protection.

#### Article 27

1. The implementation of UPTD PPA services is integrative, which is carried out under one roof or networked to provide optimal services to women and children who experience problems.  
2. In the event that the implementation of UPTDPPA services as referred to in paragraph (1) is carried out under one roof, UPTD PPA is responsible for carrying out the entire process in one unit to provide the services needed by women and children who experience problems.  
3. In the event that the implementation of UPTDPPA services as referred to in paragraph (1) is carried out in a networked manner, UPTD PPA is responsible for the entire referral process of services needed by women and children who experience problems.  
4. Every element within the UPTDPPA environment in carrying out its duties must apply the principles of coordination, integration, synchronization, accountability and openness, both within the UPTDPPA environment, as well as in relations between Regional Apparatuses, related agencies, and community elements.

#### Article 28

Further provisions regarding the organization and working procedures of UPTDPPA as referred to in Article 26 and Article 27 shall be stipulated by Regent Regulation in accordance with the provisions of laws and regulations.

### Paragraph 2

Complaint Services

#### Article 29

Complaint services as referred to in Article 26 paragraph (3) letter a may be coordinated by UPTDPPA with related agencies consisting of:  
a. hospitals;  
b. community health centers;  
c. integrated service centers for women's empowerment and children;  
d. trauma center shelters;  
d. women crisis centres;  
e. advisory boards for marriage guidance and preservation;  
f. police;  
g. prosecutor's office;  
h. courts;  
i. ministry of religion; and/or  
j. legal aid organizations;

#### Article 30

1. Complaints of acts of violence against women and children may be made by reporters consisting of victims, victims' families, the community and/or community organizations in the field of protection of women and children.  
2. Reporters as referred to in paragraph (1) may make complaints:  
a. directly;  
b. by telephone; and/or c. by letter.  
3. Further provisions regarding the procedures for handling complaint services as referred to in paragraph (1) and paragraph (2) shall be regulated by Regent Regulation.

### Paragraph 3

Health Rehabilitation

#### Article 31

1. Health rehabilitation as referred to in Article 26 paragraph (3) letter b constitutes efforts that include aspects of:  
a. promotive, improving so that health status increases;  
b. preventive, preventing from contracting disease;  
c. curative, curing a person from illness physically and psychologically;  
d. rehabilitative, maintaining so that a person who is not yet fully recovered can return to being fit as before.  
2. Health rehabilitation as referred to in paragraph (1) is carried out comprehensively, accessible 24 (twenty-four) hours, of quality and carried out in accordance with health service standards.  
3. Women and Children victims of acts of violence have the right to health services free of charge, including:  
a. medical services;  
b. medicolegal services;  
c. psychosocial counseling; and  
d. Visum et Repertum.  
4. Further provisions regarding the procedures for implementing health services as referred to in paragraph (3) shall be regulated by Regent Regulation.

### Paragraph 4

Social Rehabilitation

#### Article 32

1. Social rehabilitation as referred to in Article 26 paragraph (3) letter c is aimed at restoring and developing the capacity of women and children who experience social dysfunction due to criminal and/or civil problems to be able to carry out their social functions in society properly.  
2. Social rehabilitation as referred to in paragraph (1) includes services for:  
a. counseling;  
b. provision of safe houses; and  
c. spiritual guidance.  
3. In implementing services as referred to in paragraph (2), UPTD PPA may cooperate with women crisis centres and/or existing social community organizations.

#### Article 33

1. Counseling services as referred to in Article 32 paragraph (2) letter a aim to:  
a. help women and Children victims of acts of violence recognize their problems and find effective ways to overcome them themselves;  
b. empower women and children who experience criminal and/or civil problems to determine their future;  
c. provide moral support to women and children who experience criminal and/or civil problems in facing the process they are undergoing; and  
d. make women and children who experience criminal and/or civil problems feel accepted in the community environment.  
2. Counseling services are carried out by gender-responsive social rehabilitation officers, consisting of:  
a. counselors;  
b. psychologists; and/or  
c. psychiatrists.  
3. Counseling services are carried out in a place that guarantees a sense of security, comfort and confidentiality of information from women victims of violence.  
4. The stages of counseling services as referred to in paragraph (3) include:  
a. initial counseling;  
b. follow-up counseling;  
c. case discussion; and  
d. site visits.

#### Article 34

1. The provision of safe houses as referred to in Article 32 paragraph (2) letter b may be provided by the Regional Government for women and children who experience criminal and/or civil problems.  
2. The provision of safe house services as referred to in paragraph (1) aims to protect women and children as victims of criminal and/or civil problems from intimidation by perpetrators or other parties.  
3. The location of safe houses as referred to in paragraph (1) must be kept confidential.  
4. The criteria for women and children as victims of criminal and/or civil problems who require safe houses include:  
a. their lives are threatened;  
b. receiving rejection from family or community;  
c. requiring intensive services but their residence is relatively far away; and/or  
d. abandoned if not placed in a safe house.

#### Article 35

1. Spiritual guidance as referred to in Article 32 paragraph (2) letter c aims at psychological recovery.  
2. Spiritual guidance as referred to in paragraph (1) is implemented based on the principles of:  
a. not imposing any religion or belief; and  
b. leaving the final decision in the hands of the victim (self determination).  
3. Spiritual guidance is carried out by spiritual guidance officers.

### Paragraph 5

Assistance

#### Article 36

1. Accompaniment services as referred to in Article 26 paragraph (3) letter d are carried out for the empowerment and recovery of victims of violence.  
2. In providing accompaniment services as referred to in paragraph (1), P2TP2A may cooperate with persons or institutions that have expertise to conduct counseling, therapy and advocacy.

### Paragraph 6

Law Enforcement and Legal Aid

#### Article 37

1. Law enforcement and legal aid services as referred to in Article 26 paragraph (3) letter e constitute actions by law enforcement officials to implement laws and regulations in order to handle criminal and/or civil matters concerning women and children, especially to impose sanctions on perpetrators and provide protection for witnesses and/or victims.  
2. Law enforcement and legal aid services as referred to in paragraph (1) are carried out by prioritizing the best interests of victims.  
3. Types of legal aid services as referred to in paragraph (1) include:a. legal aid in the reporting process and preparation of examination reports;  
b. legal aid in the prosecution process at the prosecutor's office;  
c. legal aid in the examination process at court;  
d. legal aid in the court hearing decision process until there is a final legal decision; and
e. legal aid in the mediation process.
4. The Regional Government provides legal aid to women and children as victims of criminal and/or civil matters based on the provisions of laws and regulations.  
5. In providing legal aid as referred to in paragraph (3), UPTD PPA may cooperate with legal aid institutions, women crisis centers, advocates and/or other legal assistants who are gender sensitive.

### Paragraph 7

Repatriation and Social Reintegration

#### Article 38

1. Repatriation as referred to in Article 26 paragraph (3) letter f is intended to return women and children as victims of criminal and/or civil matters to their place of origin.  
2. Repatriation of women and children as victims of criminal and/or civil matters is accompanied by social reintegration efforts.

#### Article 39

1. Social reintegration as referred to in Article 38 paragraph (2) is carried out in order to reunite women and children as victims of criminal and/or civil matters with family, substitute family, or community that can provide protection and fulfillment of needs for victims.  
2. Social reintegration as referred to in paragraph (1) aims to enable women and children as victims to resume their social life and have a decent life.  
3. Social reintegration as referred to in paragraph (1) is carried out after victims receive social rehabilitation services.

#### Article 40

Repatriation and social reintegration of women and children as victims of criminal and/or civil matters as referred to in Article 38 and Article 39 are implemented by UPTDPPA in accordance with laws and regulations.

# CHAPTER VII

## FULFILLMENT OF CHILDREN'S RIGHTS

## Part One

General

#### Article 52

1. Regional Government in the effort to fulfill the rights of Children is responsible for:  
a. establishing and implementing policies and programs/activities for the fulfillment of Children's rights;  
b. respecting and guaranteeing the human rights of every Child without distinguishing ethnicity, religion, race, class, gender, ethnicity, culture and language, legal status of the Child, birth order of the Child, and physical and/or mental condition;  
c. strengthening and developing service provider institutions for improving family quality in realizing Children's rights;  
d. guaranteeing the Protection, care, and welfare of Children with due regard to the rights and obligations of parents, guardians, or other persons legally responsible for the Child;  
e. providing facilities and infrastructure in the implementation of the fulfillment of Children's rights;  
f. guaranteeing the right of Children to express opinions in accordance with the age and level of intelligence of the Child;  
g. conducting supervision and guidance in the framework of implementing the fulfillment of Children's rights; and  
h. cooperating with the private sector and/or NGOs in the implementation of Child Protection;
2. Responsibilities as referred to in paragraph (1) are carried out by Regional Apparatus in accordance with their duties and functions.

## Part Two

Fulfillment of Children's Rights in the field of Birth Registration and Child Identity

#### Article 53

1. Regional Government is obliged to organize birth registration of Children with the issuance of birth certificates.  
2. Birth certificates as referred to in paragraph (1) are organized free of charge.

#### Article 54

1. To encourage increased data collection, protection and public services and to realize the best interests of children, every child under the age of 17 years must have a child identity card.  
2. Child identity cards are issued by the Regional Apparatus in charge of population and civil registration.

## Part Three

Fulfillment of Children's Rights in the field of education

#### Article 55

Regional Government guarantees the implementation of compulsory education programs for Children in accordance with the provisions of laws and regulations.

#### Article 56

1. Every organizer of an education unit is obliged to provide opportunities for Children to obtain educational services without discrimination.  
2. Every organizer of an education unit is prohibited from expelling Children from educational institutions unless there is a guarantee for the continuity of the Child's education.  
3. In the event that a Child victim of Violence experiences dropping out of school, then the child victim of violence is provided with formal or non-formal education, in this case carried out by the organizer of the education unit and coordinated with the Women and Child Empowerment Unit

#### Article 57

1. Every Child has the right to obtain education and teaching in the context of developing their personality and level of intelligence in accordance with their interests and talents.  
2. Every Child has the right to receive Protection in education units from sexual crimes and Violence committed by educators, education personnel, fellow students, and/or other parties.

#### Article 58

1. Children with disabilities and Children with special needs are given equal opportunities to obtain formal, non-formal and informal education services, including inclusive education programs.  
2. Regional government provides educators for inclusive education in every education unit.

## Part Four

Fulfillment of Children's Rights in the field of Health and Social

#### Article 59

1. Regional Government is obliged to organize comprehensive health services for Children, so that every Child obtains optimal health status from the womb.  
2. Comprehensive health services as referred to in paragraph (1) include:  
a. promotive, preventive, curative and rehabilitative efforts for basic health services;  
b. exemption from cost burdens for malnourished children, children with disabilities, children with special needs, children infected with HIV/AIDS, child workers, children victims of narcotics abuse, alcohol, psychotropics and other addictive substances, children who are victims of violence, and child victims of human trafficking;  
3. Regional Government, community, family and parents are obliged to ensure that Children born are protected from diseases that threaten survival and/or cause disability.

#### Article 60

1. Promotive efforts for basic health services as referred to in Article 59 paragraph (2) letter a are aimed at improving health status in a more optimal manner.  
2. Promotive efforts as referred to in paragraph (1) are carried out by providing socialization or counseling to the community regarding healthy lifestyles which include:  
a. the importance of exclusive breast milk for babies;  
b. the importance of immunization for toddlers;  
c. clean living patterns;  
d. the importance of nutritious food; and  
e. the dangers of drugs and smoking.  
3. In addition to promotive efforts as referred to in paragraph (2), Regional Government may carry out other activities in the framework of health implementation.

#### Article 61

1. Preventive efforts for basic health services as referred to in Article 59 paragraph (2) letter a are aimed at preventing the occurrence of disease or health problems.  
2. Efforts as referred to in paragraph (1) are carried out by:  
a. requiring mothers to provide exclusive breastfeeding to babies up to the age of 6 (six) months;  
b. providing free immunization and vitamins to babies and toddlers;  
c. providing nutritious food to toddlers from underprivileged families;  
d. optimizing the role of posyandu for health services for mothers and toddlers;  
e. conducting periodic health examinations;  
f. providing sanitation facilities and clean water in every school; and  
g. providing special breastfeeding facilities or breastfeeding corner rooms in government agencies or the business world.

#### Article 62

1. Curative efforts for basic health services as referred to in Article 59 paragraph (2) letter a are aimed at preventing disease from becoming more severe through treatment.  
2. Treatment as referred to in paragraph (1) is carried out free of charge for children from underprivileged families.

#### Article 63

1. Rehabilitative efforts for basic health services as referred to in Article 59 paragraph (2) letter a are aimed at maintaining and restoring conditions after illness.  
2. Rehabilitative efforts as referred to in paragraph (1) are carried out through periodic health examinations.

#### Article 64

Regional Government is obliged to organize Social Welfare for:  
a. children in conflict with the law;  
b. child victims of violence who are economically exploited;  
c. children who do not have parents and are cared for by parties or families who are unable;  
d. child victims of natural disasters or social disasters; and  
e. children with disabilities.

## Part Five

Fulfillment of Children's Rights in the field of Employment

#### Article 65

Regional Government conducts periodic supervision of every person/entity that employs Children in the effort to Protect child workers.

#### Article 66

1. Every person/entity is prohibited from employing and involving Children in the worst forms of work.  
2. The worst forms of work referred to in paragraph (1) include:  
a. all work in the form of slavery or the like;  
b. all work that utilizes, provides, or offers Children for prostitution, pornography production, pornographic performances, or gambling;  
c. all work that utilizes, provides, or involves Children for the production and trade of alcoholic beverages, narcotics, psychotropics, and other addictive substances; and/or  
d. all work that endangers the health, safety, or morals of Children.

## Part Six

Children's Forum

#### Article 67

1. In organizing the fulfillment of children's rights to express opinions and participate, the Regional Government facilitates the establishment of a Children's Forum.  
2. The Children's Forum as referred to in paragraph (1) constitutes the representation of Children in the Region.  
3. The representation as referred to in paragraph (2) consists of the geographical domicile of Children, components of Children's socio-cultural groups and Children's educational background.  
4. The Children's Forum as referred to in paragraph (1) may be established at the Regional, Sub-district and Nagari levels.  
5. In every formulation of policies related to Children, the Regional Government is obliged to consider and accommodate the opinions of Children conveyed through the Children's Forum.  
6. The establishment of the Children's Forum as referred to in paragraph (1) shall be stipulated by Regent's Decree.

#### Article 72

b. technical guidance and training, comprising:

NUMBER 16 OF 2018 CONCERNING WOMEN'S EMPOWERMENT AND CHILD PROTECTION

##### I. GENERAL

Children are a trust as well as a gift from Almighty God, whom we must always protect because inherent in them are dignity, honor, and rights as human beings that must be upheld. The human rights of children are part of human rights contained in the 1945 Constitution and the United Nations Convention on the Rights of the Child. From the perspective of national and state life, children are the future of the nation and the generation that will continue the ideals of the nation, so that every child has the right to survival, growth, and development, to participate and has the right to protection from acts of violence and discrimination as well as civil rights and freedoms.

Likewise with women, from women are expected to be born children who are healthy, strong, resilient, of noble character, potential and quality. Through the role of women in the family, society and state will be born resilient generations who will continue the ideals of the nation. As citizens of Indonesia, women also have the right to improve quality and develop themselves according to their abilities, dignity and honor.

Women and children need to be protected from acts of discrimination and acts of violence whether physical, psychological, mental, sexual and neglect. Because of their physical weakness compared to men and adults and because of the nature of women themselves, women and children often become objects of venting desires, anger, economic exploitation, violence (physical, psychological and sexual), cruelty, neglect, and so on. As assets and hope of the nation and state, children need to be given protection and safeguarded from all forms of acts of violence that will hinder their growth and development.

To realize the empowerment of women and protection of children, support from laws and regulations is needed that can guarantee their implementation.

Although the protection of the rights of women and children has been regulated in national level laws and regulations, the fulfillment of the constitutional rights of women and children and the improvement of the quality of life of women and children is one of the mandatory affairs that is the duty, authority and responsibility of the Regional Government, so in providing protection to women and children, the Regional Government needs to establish a Regional Regulation on Women's Empowerment and Child Protection.

This Regional Regulation on Women's Empowerment and Protection regulates the following scope:
a. rights of women and children;
b. obligations;
c. improvement of women's quality of life;
d. protection of women and children;
e. improvement of family quality;
f. gender and child data system;
g. fulfillment of children's rights;
h. child-friendly regency; and
i. guidance and supervision.
