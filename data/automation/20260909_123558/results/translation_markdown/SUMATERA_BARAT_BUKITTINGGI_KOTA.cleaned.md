# CHAPTER I

## GENERAL PROVISIONS

#### Article 1

In this Regional Regulation, what is meant by:  
1. Region is the City of Bukittinggi.  
2. Regional Government is the administration of governmental affairs by the regional government and the regional house of representatives according to the principles of autonomy and assistance tasks with the principle of the widest possible autonomy within the system and principles of the Unitary State of the Republic of Indonesia as referred to in the 1945 Constitution of the Republic of Indonesia.  
3. Regional Government is the regional head as an element of regional government administration who leads the implementation of governmental affairs that are the authority of the autonomous region.  
4. Mayor is the Mayor of Bukittinggi.  
5. Regional House of Representatives of the City of Bukittinggi, hereinafter abbreviated as DPRD, is a regional people's representative institution as an element of regional government administration.  
6. Regional Work Unit, hereinafter abbreviated as SKPD, is one that has authority in the field of women's empowerment and family planning.  
7. Community is individuals, families, groups and social organizations and/or community organizations.  
8. Customs are eternal patterns of behavior passed down from one generation to another generation as a heritage so that it is strongly integrated with community behavior patterns.  
9. Non-Governmental Organization, hereinafter abbreviated as LSM, is an organization/institution formed by citizens of the Republic of Indonesia voluntarily of their own will and interested in and engaged in the field of social welfare business as a manifestation of community participation in improving the standard of living and welfare of the community with emphasis on voluntary service.  
10. Social Organization is an institution/foundation/social association formed by the community whether legally incorporated or not legally incorporated that functions as a means of community participation in carrying out social welfare business.  
11. Empowerment is a process, method and act of providing power and strength so that a person is able to explore potential and positive energy and have an impact on a better quality of life.  
12. Woman is every person who is born, recognized by society and registered as a woman.  
13. Protection of Women is all efforts to guarantee and protect the rights of women so that they can live healthily, develop themselves and participate optimally in accordance with human dignity and receive protection from neglect, violence and discrimination whether in the form of preventive, repressive or post-remedial protection (assistance and treatment).  
14. Child is a person who has not reached the age of 18 (eighteen) years, including a child who is still in the womb.  
15. Child Protection is all activities to guarantee and protect children and their rights so that they can live, grow, develop and participate optimally in accordance with human dignity, and receive protection from violence and discrimination.  
16. Family is the smallest unit in society consisting of husband and wife, or husband and wife and their children or father and children, or mother and children.  
17. Household Scope is husband, wife and children, people who have family relations with the husband, wife and children, whether due to blood relations, marriage, breastfeeding, care, and guardianship who reside in the household, and/or people who work to help the household and reside in that household.  
18. Parents are biological father and/or mother, or stepfather and stepmother, or adoptive father and/or mother.  
19. Guardian is a person or body who in reality exercises custody as a parent over a child.  
20. Neglected Child is a child whose needs are not properly fulfilled, whether physical, mental, spiritual, or social.  
21. Child with Disabilities is a child who experiences physical and/or mental obstacles that interfere with their growth and development naturally.  
22. Toddler is a child aged 1 (one) to 5 (five) years, in the early stages of human development.  
23. School-Age Child is a child aged 6 (six) to 18 (eighteen) years.  
24. Delinquent Child is a child who behaves deviating from community norms, harming/endangering their own health/safety, disturbing the peace and order of society and family and community life, but still below the category that can be prosecuted by law/court.  
25. Child Who Is a Victim of Violence is a child who receives mistreatment such as being abused, humiliated physically, mentally and socially.  
26. Child in Conflict with the Law is a child who is in conflict with the law and a child who is a victim of a criminal act.  
27. Economically Exploited Child is a child who is forced and deceived to be employed by parents or others without being paid or being paid.  
28. Child Who Is a Victim of Narcotics Abuse is a child who suffers from dependence on narcotics caused by narcotics abuse either of their own will or because of encouragement or coercion by others.  
29. Child Trafficking is a criminal act or deed that meets one or more elements of recruitment, sending, transfer of a child using violence or threat of violence, fraud, kidnapping, confinement, abuse of power, exploitation of a position of vulnerability or debt bondage for the purpose of and/or resulting in exploiting the child.  
30. Social Work Profession is a profession based on a framework of knowledge, values and technical skills and can be used as a vehicle in the implementation of social welfare business.  
31. Child Care Center, hereinafter abbreviated as TPA, is a forum for guidance and welfare services for children aged 0-5 years whose parents do not have the ability and opportunity in child care, which is possible for playgroup services.  
32. Playgroup is a forum for child welfare business prioritizing play activities and organizing pre-school education for children aged 3 years until entering basic education.  
33. Marriage is an outward and inward bond between a man and a woman as husband and wife with the aim of forming a happy and eternal family (household) based on the One Supreme God.  
34. Child Protection is all activities to guarantee and protect children and their rights so that they can live, grow, develop and participate optimally in accordance with human dignity, and receive protection from violence and discrimination.  
35. Violence is any act unlawfully with or without using means against physical and psychological that causes danger to life, body or results in the deprivation of a person's freedom.  
36. Violence Against Women is any action based on gender differences that results in or may result in misery or suffering of women, physically, sexually or psychologically, including threats of certain actions, coercion or deprivation of freedom arbitrarily, whether occurring in the public sphere or in private life.  
37. Violence Against Children is acts of physical, sexual violence, emotional abuse and neglect of children as well as acts of exploitation and discrimination against children that make children sick physically and/or psychologically, depressed, emotionally underdeveloped, feel excluded and lose self-confidence.  
38. Acts of Domestic Violence are any acts against a person, especially women, that result in misery or suffering physically, sexually, psychologically and/or domestic neglect including threats to commit acts, coercion or deprivation of freedom unlawfully within the household scope.  
39. Victim of Domestic Violence is a person who experiences violence and/or threat of violence within the household scope.  
40. Divorce is the release of the marriage bond from the husband's side by pronouncing a certain phrase such as "I have divorced you".  
41. Integrated Service Center for Women and Children Empowerment Saayun Salangkah, hereinafter abbreviated as P2TP2A Saayun Salangkah, is one form of service facility for women and children in an effort to fulfill information and needs in the fields of education, health, economy, politics and law, protection and prevention of acts of violence and trafficking against women and children in the City of Bukittinggi.  
42. Child-Friendly City, hereinafter abbreviated as KLA, is a city that has a child rights-based development system through the integration of government, community and business commitments and resources that are planned comprehensively and sustainably in policies, programs and activities to ensure the fulfillment of children's rights.  
43. Child-Friendly City Task Force, hereinafter abbreviated as Gugus Tugas KLA, is a coordinative institution at the city level that coordinates policies, programs and activities to realize KLA.

# CHAPTER II

## PRINCIPLES AND OBJECTIVES

#### Article 2

The implementation of Protection of Women and Children is based on:  
1. Pancasila and the 1945 Constitution of the Republic of Indonesia.  
2. Basic principles of children's rights, including non-discrimination, the best interests of the child, the right to life, survival and development, and respect for the views of the child.  
3. Universal teachings which state that women and children are a very important part in the context of the sustainability of a nation, not only viewed in religious principles, but in their implementation constitute human resources in the development of a nation.  
4. The outlook on life and philosophy of Minangkabau customs as set forth in the teachings of Adat Basandi Syarak, Syarak Basandi Kitabullah; Syarak Mangato Adat Mamakai, Alam Takambang Jadi Guru, and Anak dipangku kamanakan dibimbiang, urang kampuang dipatenggangkan.

#### Article 3

The objectives of this Regional Regulation are:  
a. To protect women and children from actions, decisions and policies that violate human rights.  
b. To provide clear responsibility for the government, community, family, ninik mamak and parents towards the rights of children and women.  
c. To provide clear boundaries regarding the role of mamak towards kemenakan and parents towards children.  
d. To protect women and children from acts of violence, neglect, exploitation, and trafficking in persons as well as the rights of women in the event of divorce.  
e. To contribute to the realization of gender equality and justice in various activities as well as improving the welfare of women and children in the Region.  
f. To organize the structure and institutions of government and customary institutions in efforts to protect women and children.  
g. As a guideline in making policies for the Regional Government and social institutions in matters relating to the protection of women and children in the Region.  
h. To protect women and children domiciled in the Region from actions that violate their human rights.  
i. To provide guidance on the role of the Regional Government, community, parents and other parties concerned with the interests of women and children.

# CHAPTER III

## RIGHTS AND OBLIGATIONS OF WOMEN AND CHILDREN

## Part One

Rights and Obligations of Women

#### Article 4

Every woman has the right to:  
a. Obtain protection from discrimination, exploitation (economic and social), neglect, cruelty, violence, abuse (physical, psychological, and sexual), injustice and other wrongful treatment.  
b. Obtain equal opportunities and rights with men as human beings, in order to be able to play a role and participate in political, economic, social and cultural activities, defense and security and equality in enjoying the results of development.  
c. For working women:  
1\. obtain leave/rest for 1.5 (one and a half) months before the time of childbirth and 1.5 (one and a half) months after childbirth according to the calculation of an obstetrician or midwife, while still receiving wages or salary in accordance with applicable laws and regulations;  
2\. obtain leave/rest for 1.5 (one and a half) months in the event of a miscarriage while still receiving wages or salary in accordance with applicable laws and regulations;  
3\. obtain appropriate opportunities to breastfeed her child if this must be done during working hours, while still receiving wages or salary in accordance with applicable laws and regulations.  
d. Every woman who is married has the right to receive material and spiritual support from her husband.  
e. Every woman who is left by her husband due to a divorce process must be provided with material support by her husband until there is a court decision in accordance with statutory regulations.  
f. Every woman has the right to obtain decent work in accordance with her dignity, honor and nature.  
g. Obtain knowledge and skills that are useful for improving self-quality and family welfare.

#### Article 5

Every woman is obliged to:  
a. Respect each other, love each other and help each other with all family members and relatives.  
b. Manage the household especially as a good housewife in accordance with local customs and her abilities.  
c. Respect her husband and obey her husband as long as it does not conflict with religious values and customs.  
d. Care for and educate children in accordance with religious values and customary norms.  
e. Increase knowledge and skills that are beneficial for the family and community.  
f. Comply with religious values and customary norms.

## Part Two

Rights and Obligations of Children

#### Article 6

Every child has the right:  
a. To live, grow, develop and participate reasonably in accordance with human dignity and worth, and to receive protection from acts of violence, exploitation, discrimination and neglect.  
b. To have a name as personal identity and citizenship status.  
c. To worship according to their religion, think and express themselves in accordance with their level of intelligence and age, under parental guidance.  
d. To know their parents, be cared for and raised by their own parents.  
e. In the event that for any reason the parents cannot guarantee the child's growth and development, or the child is in a state of neglect, then the child has the right to be cared for or adopted as a foster child or adopted child by another person in accordance with the provisions of applicable laws and regulations.  
f. To obtain health services and social security in accordance with physical, mental, spiritual, and social needs.  
g. To obtain education and teaching in the context of developing their personality and level of intelligence in accordance with their interests and talents.  
h. To receive protection in educational units from sexual crimes and violence committed by educators, education personnel, fellow students, and/or other parties.  
i. To receive special and extraordinary education for children with disabilities and children who have excellence.  
j. To express and have their opinions heard, receive, seek, and provide information in accordance with their level of intelligence and age for their self-development in accordance with the values of decency and propriety.  
k. To rest and utilize leisure time, socialize with peers, play, recreate, and create in accordance with their interests, talents, and level of intelligence for self-development.  
l. To obtain rehabilitation, social assistance and restoration of social welfare standards for children with disabilities.  
m. To receive protection from treatment while in the care of parents, guardians, or any other party responsible for care, from discriminatory treatment, exploitation, whether economic or sexual, neglect, cruelty, violence, and abuse, injustice and other mistreatment.  
n. To be cared for by their own parents, unless there are reasons and/or valid legal rules showing that the separation is in the best interests of the child and is a last consideration.  
o. In the event of separation between the child and their parents, the child still has the right to:  
1\. meet directly and maintain personal contact regularly with both parents;  
2\. receive care, maintenance, education and protection for the growth and development process from both parents in accordance with their abilities, talents and interests;  
3\. obtain financial support for living from both parents; and
4\. obtain other children's rights.  
p. To obtain protection from abuse in political activities, involvement in armed conflicts, involvement in social unrest, involvement in events containing elements of violence, involvement in warfare and sexual crimes.  
q. To obtain protection from being targets of persecution, torture or imposition of punishment or inhuman sanctions.  
r. To obtain freedom in accordance with the law.  
s. Every child who is deprived of their freedom has the right to:  
1\. receive humane treatment and placement separated from adults;  
2\. obtain legal assistance or other assistance effectively at every stage of applicable legal remedies; and
3\. defend themselves and obtain justice before an objective and impartial juvenile court in a closed hearing to the public.  
t. Every child who becomes a victim or perpetrator of sexual violence or who is in conflict with the law has the right to confidentiality.  
u. To receive legal assistance and other assistance if they become a victim or perpetrator of a criminal act.

#### Article 7

Every child has the obligation to:  
a. Respect parents, guardians and teachers.  
b. Love family, community and care for friends.  
c. Love the Homeland, Nation, and State.  
d. Perform worship in accordance with the teachings of their respective religions.  
e. Practice noble ethics and morals.  
f. Comply with all provisions deemed capable of damaging themselves and their future.

# CHAPTER IV

## IMPLEMENTATION OF PROTECTION OF WOMEN AND CHILDREN

#### Article 8

The Regional Government is responsible for the implementation of protection of women and children actively.

#### Article 9

Protection of women includes:  
a. Conducting socialization, education and anti-violence against women campaigns by actively involving all Regional Government Work Units, work partners and the community.  
b. Ensuring gender mainstreaming programs in every Regional Government Work Unit, to guarantee equality between men and women, which is reflected in the Budget Plan of Regional Government Work Units.  
c. Conducting early detection of cases of violence against women.  
d. Providing service facilities for victims of violence against women, both services for physical victims and psychological services for victims, through improvement of special facilities and facilities at Hospitals and at P2TP2A.  
e. Requiring all Hospitals to be willing to receive women victims of violence for post-mortem examination and to undergo health care.  
f. Providing women's rights in accordance with their dignity and worth as women.  
g. Protecting women workers from acts of discrimination, exploitation and violence.  
h. Requiring the provision of special infrastructure for women according to criteria, in every government building, private office and other public facilities.  
i. Requiring the provision of lactation room facilities for government buildings that provide public services and other public facility places such as malls, supermarkets, doctor's practice places and the like.

#### Article 10

1. Protection of children starts from the womb until the age of 18 (#### Article 10

1. Protection of children starts from the womb until the age of 18 (eighteen) years.  
2. Protection of children in the womb, infants and toddlers includes:  
a. protection of children in the womb, including ensuring children are born in a healthy condition, to reduce maternal mortality rates and reduce child mortality rates;  
b. ensuring every child is born with medical procedures in accordance with SOPs from the Ministry of Health;  
c. ensuring every child after birth is conducted with religious guidance adhered to by their parents;  
d. provision of complete immunization facilities at posyandu, puskesmas or government hospitals;  
e. improvement of quality and competence of cadres at Posyandu, to ensure correct procedures in order to guarantee the health of mothers and children including the immunization process;  
f. provision of proper facilities for every posyandu and Bina Keluarga Balita (BKB) in every kelurahan;  
g. ensuring the quality of knowledge of posyandu cadres and bina keluarga balita cadres in accordance with government standards;  
h. providing education to the community about becoming great parents, to ensure child health, both at posyandu, bina keluarga balita together with government and community work partners;  
i. provision of playgrounds and childcare facilities managed by the regional government or organizations under the auspices of the Regional Government;  
j. provision of Breastfeeding Corners in offices that have female workers; and  
k. provision of attention and affection from the family.  
3. Protection for school-age children:  
a. prohibiting acts of bullying against children at school;  
b. ensuring there are no acts of bullying between children, teachers to children, parents to children and the community to children whether at school, at home or in the midst of the community;  
c. providing education to schools, families and the community about child-friendly schools;  
d. requiring every school at all levels to meet child-friendly school criteria;  
e. receiving full attention and affection from family and community;  
f. receiving religious guidance and local customs/culture including sumbang 12 (dua baleh);  
g. receiving twelve years of compulsory education;  
h. providing scholarship programs for underprivileged children and children who excel;  
i. providing supporting facilities for extracurricular activities such as for sports subjects, arts and other local content as well as activity supervising teachers; and  
j. prohibiting school children from being in internet cafes during school hours and after 18.00 WIB.  
4. Every person and/or any party is obliged to protect school-age children from acts of violence in the family, school and community environment.  
5. Protection of neglected children:  
a. regional government, NGOs/Social organizations and the community are obliged to provide protection for neglected children domiciled in the Region in accordance with laws and regulations;  
b. regional government ensures children do not live on the streets;  
c. protection for neglected children whose parents do not have the ability and/or commit neglect as well as children who do not have parents, which is implemented through institutional and non-institutional services by the regional government and the community;  
d. the form of institutional services as referred to in letter (c), is implemented in a family atmosphere and in the child's nearest environment managed by the community and in the form of an institution;  
e. the form of non-institutional services as referred to in letter (c) is implemented in a family atmosphere managed by the community and not in the form of an institution; and  
f. community-owned institutions as referred to in letter (d) must meet the requirements in accordance with applicable laws and regulations.

# CHAPTER V

## RESPONSIBILITY FOR THE PROTECTION OF WOMEN AND CHILDREN

#### Article 11

The Regional Government together with the community is obliged and responsible to provide support for facilities and infrastructure in the implementation of the protection of women and children.

#### Article 12

The Regional Government guarantees the protection, care, and welfare of children by taking into account their rights and obligations.

#### Article 13

The Regional Government together with the community and the business world are obliged to fulfill facilities and infrastructure in accordance with child-friendly city indicators.

#### Article 14

Families, caregivers, institutions, and customary institutions have the right to take action towards women and children in the context of implementing education, embracing religion, skills development, moral and ethical development, as well as entrepreneurship development for women and children in accordance with religious and customary norms.

#### Article 15

Families, caregivers, institutions and customary institutions are obliged to play an active role in providing the rights of women and children in accordance with religious and customary norms as well as applicable laws and regulations.

# CHAPTER VI

## SYSTEM FOR THE PROTECTION OF WOMEN AND CHILDREN

#### Article 16

1. The protection of women and children is implemented by upholding universal values stated in the Universal Declaration of Human Rights.  
2. Bureaucratic limitations must not hinder efforts to protect women and children.  
3. Every parent, adoptive parent, guardian, teaching/educational staff, expert, caregiver at orphanages, and leaders of customary law community members are obliged to carry out obligations regarding the rights of children they care for and supervise.  
4. Every head of family, head of clan, head of tribe, leader, or other designation of customary law community leader is obliged to fulfill their obligations towards women who are in their family, clan and tribe.

#### Article 17

1. The protection of women and children is carried out in the form of:  
a. preventive protection;  
b. repressive protection; and  
c. postremedial protection.  
2. Preventive protection as referred to in paragraph (1) letter a is implemented through socialization and guidance to parents, heads of families, housewives, guardians, teaching/educational staff, experts, caregivers (owners and officers of orphanages) and customary law communities about their obligations in the protection of women and children.  
3. Repressive protection as referred to in paragraph (1) letter b is implemented through law enforcement by law enforcers to establish and implement legal sanctions against people who violate certain rights of children and women.  
4. Postremedial protection as referred to in paragraph (1) letter c is implemented through assistance efforts in law enforcement by lawyers, psychological guidance by psychologists and or religious leaders, and placement of victims in safe houses.

#### Article 18

The protection of women and children must be implemented by parents, families, relatives, guardians, caregivers, teachers, the Regional Government, and customary law communities in the Region.

# CHAPTER VII

## IMPLEMENTATION OF SOCIALIZATION OF THE PROTECTION

## OF WOMEN AND CHILDREN

#### Article 19

1. The Regional Government is obliged to conduct socialization and education regarding the protection of women and children by involving all elements of customary stakeholders, as well as universities and other community organizations in the Region.  
2. Socialization and education as referred to in paragraph (1) is carried out through the implementation of legal counseling, both with lecture methods, discussions, pamphlets, films, plays, randai, saluang, rabab, and other traditional arts.  
3. Financing of programs and activities for socialization and education on the protection of women and children is budgeted in the APBD every year in accordance with regional financial capacity and laws and regulations.

# CHAPTER VIII

## IMPLEMENTATION OF PROTECTION AGAINST

## VIOLATIONS OF WOMEN'S AND CHILDREN'S RIGHTS

#### Article 20

1. Violations of women's rights, whether in physical, psychological forms, discriminatory treatment, and exploitation must receive legal protection and be prosecuted legally.
2. In addition to the application of state law as referred to in paragraph (1), perpetrators of violations of women's rights may be subject to sanctions according to customary law that lives and develops in the midst of society.

#### Article 21

1. If parents or guardians feel unable or neglect their obligations in child care, blood relatives, clan members or tribe members may submit an application to the court in their legal jurisdiction covering the area of the child's residence to revoke custody from the parents or former guardian and grant the application as a new guardian, either as foster parents or as adoptive parents.
2. If parents, relatives, clan, tribe, paruik or guardians neglect their obligations in child care and protection, upon report from the community, an orphanage may submit an application to the court whose legal jurisdiction covers the child's place of residence to revoke the custody of the former parents or guardian and appoint the orphanage concerned as the new foster guardian or adoptive parents.
3. The new guardian or parents, whether individuals or orphanages appointed must have a religion that corresponds with the religion of the child's parents.
4. If an abandoned child's parents' religion is not known, then the child shall be cared for by a person or social institution based on the religion of the majority of the local population.
5. The appointment of new foster parents or adoptive parents does not sever legal relations with biological parents and all their relatives.
6. Parents whose custody has been revoked of a child, still have the obligation to bear the costs of raising their child.

#### Article 22

1. Violations of children's rights, whether in physical, psychological forms, discriminatory treatment, and exploitation must receive legal protection and be prosecuted legally.
2. In addition to the application of state law as referred to in paragraph (1), perpetrators of violations of women's rights may be subject to sanctions according to customary law that lives and develops in the midst of society.

# CHAPTER IX

IMPLEMENTATION OF HANDLING VICTIMS OF VIOLATIONS OF WOMEN'S AND CHILDREN'S RIGHTS

#### Article 23

1. Complaint Handling Services for violations of women's and children's rights are carried out by P2TP2A, PPA Units at Polres, Community Health Centers, Government Hospitals, and Legal Aid Institutions.
2. P2TP2A is established by the Regional Government.
3. P2TP2A management is established by Mayor's Decree with a management period of 2 (two) years.
4. In carrying out its main duties and functions, P2TP2A is financed by the Regional Budget or other lawful sources.
5. Complaint Handling Services are conducted in the form of:
a. reporters (victims or family, community and/or institutions) come directly, by telephone and/or letter to report acts of violence;
b. reporters are referred by NGOs, social organizations, the community or mass media; and
c. in certain cases officers may visit victims (reporters) directly.

#### Article 24

1. Every woman or child who becomes a victim of violations of women's and children's rights is accompanied by a lawyer provided by the regional government in the context of case handling.
2. Assistance costs as referred to in paragraph (1) are budgeted in the Regional Budget.

#### Article 25

Every woman or child who becomes a victim of violations of women's and children's rights receives free medical services at Community Health Centers, government hospitals.

#### Article 26

Every woman or child who becomes a victim of violations of women's and children's rights in the context of rehabilitation is accompanied by a psychologist and or cleric at the expense of the government.

#### Article 27

1. Every person who knows that a violation of women's and children's rights has occurred must report to law enforcement authorities the occurrence of such violation.
2. Every person, blood relatives, clan members, saparuik, tribe members, village members, or nagari members must report to law enforcement authorities if they know that a violation of women's and children's rights has occurred.

# CHAPTER X

## CHILD-FRIENDLY CITY

#### Article 28

1. To realize the fulfillment of children's rights in an integrated and systematic manner from all sectors on an ongoing basis, it is carried out through KLA development policies.
2. The fulfillment of children's rights as referred to in paragraph (1) is further regulated by Mayor's Regulation.

#### Article 29

1. KLA development policy contains:
a. KLA concept;
b. children's rights; and
c. KLA development approach.
2. The KLA concept as referred to in paragraph (1) letter a, includes:
a. definition;
b. objectives;
c. strategy; and
d. role of parties.
3. The KLA development approach as referred to in paragraph (1) letter c and paragraph (2) is further regulated by Mayor's Regulation.

#### Article 30

1. KLA development policy is directed at the fulfillment of children's rights which are divided into 5 (five) groups.
2. The fulfillment of children's rights as referred to in paragraph (1), consists of:
a. civil rights and freedoms;
b. family environment and alternative care;
c. basic health and welfare;
d. education and child-friendly schools, use of leisure time, and cultural activities; and
e. special protection.
3. The fulfillment of children's rights as referred to in paragraph (2), its mechanism and implementation are further regulated by Mayor's Regulation.

#### Article 31

1. In order to ensure the effectiveness of KLA policy implementation in the Region, a KLA Task Force is established.
2. The implementation of KLA policy as referred to in paragraph (1), is stipulated by Mayor's Decree.
3. The KLA Task Force as referred to in paragraph (1), has the main duties of:
a. coordinating the implementation of KLA policies and development;
b. determining the tasks of Task Force members;
c. conducting socialization, advocacy and communication of information and education on KLA policies;
d. collecting baseline data;
e. conducting needs analysis sourced from baseline data;
f. disseminating baseline data;
g. determining the focus and priority of programs in realizing KLA, which are adjusted to regional potential;
h. preparing a 5 (five) year regional action plan for KLA and work mechanisms; and
i. conducting monitoring, evaluation and reporting at least once (one) year.
4. Membership of the KLA Task Force is stipulated by Mayor's Decree.

#### Article 32

1. To assist the smooth implementation of the KLA Task Force's duties, a Secretariat is established.
2. The Secretariat as referred to in paragraph (1), is tasked with providing technical and administrative support to the KLA Task Force.
3. The KLA Task Force is domiciled in the SKPD that handles women's empowerment, family planning and child protection affairs.
4. The establishment and membership of the KLA Task Force is stipulated by Mayor's Decree.

# ELUCIDATION OF REGIONAL REGULATION OF BUKITTINGGI CITY

NUMBER 4 YEAR 2015 CONCERNING PROTECTION OF WOMEN AND CHILDREN

##### I. GENERAL

The protection of women and children, to this day, remains something that is widely discussed globally. There are still many, even in the most developed countries, violations of the rights of women and children that continue to this very moment. The understanding or view of some communities that see women and children as parties of lower status, or under the dominance of men, to this day still cannot be completely eliminated.

Recognizing this, in line with national development priorities, the Regional Government of Bukittinggi strives to produce local policies formulated within the framework of regional regulations, a guideline or reference for the empowerment and protection of women and children in a more institutionalized manner.

The objectives of formulating this Draft Regional Regulation are as follows:

1. To protect women and children from actions, decisions and policies that violate basic human rights.
2. To provide clear responsibility for the government, community, family, ninik mamak, and parents regarding the rights of children and women.
3. To provide clear boundaries on the role of mamak towards kemenakan and parents towards children.
4. To protect women and children from acts of violence, neglect, exploitation, and human trafficking as well as the rights of women in the event of divorce.
5. To contribute to the realization of gender equality and justice in various activities and to improve the welfare of women and children in the City of Bukittinggi.
6. To organize the structure and institutions of government and customary institutions in efforts to protect women and children.
7. As a guideline in making policies for the Regional Government and social institutions in matters relating to the protection of women and children in the City of Bukittinggi.
8. To protect women and children domiciled in the City of Bukittinggi from actions that violate their basic rights.
9. To provide guidance on the role of the Regional Government, community, parents and other parties involved with the interests of women and children.

With the presence of this Regional Regulation as part of the national legal system concerning the protection and empowerment of women and children, all forms of violations of their rights such as being neglected, exploited, discriminated against and even often experiencing domestic violence (KDRT), low quality of life, inability to contribute to the progress of development, can be institutionally eliminated and eradicated in our region.

It can be affirmed that this Regional Regulation will later lay the basic rules regarding the authority, rights and obligations of the government, community and elements involved with children and women, by making local wisdom, customary values and the Minangkabau cultural worldview towards women and children as the main philosophical foundation, which can simply be expressed through customary expressions such as "bundo kanduang limpapeh rumah nan gadang", and "anak dipangku kemenakan dibimbing".
