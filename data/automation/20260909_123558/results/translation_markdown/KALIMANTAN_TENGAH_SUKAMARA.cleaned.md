BY THE GRACE OF ALMIGHTY GOD

REGENT OF SUKAMARA,

Considering:
 
 
 
 
 
 
 
a. that every woman and child has equal rights to live a dignified life in accordance with the principles of humanity, equality and justice;
b. that to guarantee women's empowerment and child protection in accordance with talents and human dignity and to receive protection from violence, discrimination and other violations, women's empowerment and child protection must be carried out in an integrated and sustainable manner through accelerating the fulfillment and protection of the rights of women and children in their personal lives, family, community, nation and state;

# CHAPTER I

## GENERAL PROVISIONS

#### Article 1

In this Regional Regulation, the following terms shall mean:  
1. Region is Sukamara Regency.  
2. Regional Government is the Regent as an element of the Regional Government administrator who leads the implementation of governmental affairs that are the authority of the autonomous region.  
3. Regent is the Regent of Sukamara.  
4. Regional Apparatus hereinafter abbreviated as PD is the Regional Apparatus within the Sukamara Regency Government.  
5. Women's Empowerment is an effort to enable women to obtain access to and control over economic, political and cultural resources so that women can manage themselves and increase self-confidence to be able to play a role and participate actively in solving problems, so as to be able to build capacity and self-concept.  
6. Women's Protection is all efforts to guarantee and protect women's rights so that they can live healthily and participate optimally in accordance with human dignity and receive protection from neglect, violence and discrimination whether in the form of preventive, repressive or post-remedial protection (assistance and treatment).  
7. Child Protection is all activities to guarantee and protect children and their rights so that they can live, grow, develop and participate optimally in accordance with human dignity, and receive protection from neglect, violence and discrimination.  
8. Child is a person who has not reached the age of 18 (eighteen) years, including children who are still in the womb and are not married.  
9. Prevention is a direct effort carried out by the Regional Government, community and parents and/or family to prevent violations of the rights of women and children.  
10. Violence is any act that results in or may result in misery or suffering whether physical, sexual, economic, social, and psychological to the victim.  
11. Children's forum is a forum for children's participation formed with the aim of facilitating children's involvement in the decision-making process and enjoying changes relating to their lives either directly or indirectly, which is carried out with the consent and will of all children based on awareness and understanding, in accordance with age and level of maturity of thinking.  
12. Child-Friendly Regency hereinafter abbreviated as KLA is a Regency that has a child rights-based development system through the integration of commitments and resources of government, community and business world that are planned comprehensively and sustainably in policies, programs and activities to ensure the fulfillment of children's rights.  
13. Neglected child is a child whose needs are not properly fulfilled, whether physical, mental, spiritual or social.  
14. Social welfare is a condition of fulfillment of material, spiritual and social needs of citizens so that they can live decently and are able to develop themselves so that they can carry out their social functions.  
15. Gender Mainstreaming hereinafter abbreviated as PUG is a strategy built to integrate gender into an integral dimension of planning, formulation, implementation, monitoring and evaluation of national development program policies.  
16. Community is individuals, families, groups and social organizations and/or community organizations.  
17. Women victims of violence are women who experience actions that result in misery or suffering physically, sexually or psychologically including threats of certain actions, coercion or arbitrary deprivation of freedom whether occurring in the public sphere or in private life.  
18. Child victims of violence are children who experience actions that result in misery or suffering physically, mentally, sexually, psychologically or neglect that threatens life, body and degrades human dignity.  
19. Human trafficking is the act of recruiting, transporting, harboring, sending, transferring or receiving a person by threat of violence, use of violence, kidnapping, confinement, forgery, fraud, abuse of power or vulnerable position, debt bondage or giving payment or benefits, so as to obtain consent from the person holding control over that other person, whether carried out within the Country or outside the Country.  
20. Complaint Handling is a series of actions carried out by integrated service providers to follow up on reports of acts of violence against women and children, submitted by victims, families or the community.  
21. Social Rehabilitation is a service aimed at restoring and developing the ability of a person experiencing social dysfunction so that they can carry out their social functions properly.  
22. Law enforcement is the action of officials authorized by the state to implement statutory regulations.  
23. PUG focal point is an OPD operator who has the ability to carry out gender mainstreaming in their respective work units.  
24. Legal aid is legal services provided by legal assistants and advocates to carry out the process of assisting witnesses and/or victims of violence against women and children that is gender sensitive.  
25. Repatriation is an effort to return women and child victims of violence from abroad to the debarkation point/entry point or from the receiving Region to the region of origin.  
26. Social reintegration is an effort to reunite victims with family, substitute family or community that can provide protection and fulfillment of needs for victims.  
27. Safe house is a temporary shelter that aims to provide a sense of security to victims of violence.  
28. Shelter house is a forum for guidance and welfare services for street children so that they can grow and develop normally.  
29. Spiritual guidance is an effort to restore the psyche and systematic towards the differences between women and men in society accompanied by efforts to eliminate structural and cultural barriers in achieving gender equality.  
30. Gender Responsive is consistent and systematic attention to the differences between women and men in society accompanied by efforts to eliminate structural and cultural barriers in achieving gender equality.  
31. Gender Sensitivity or gender awareness is the ability to identify gaps in power relations between women and men in the family and in the community, the impact of gender-based division of labor on women and men and the experience that problems, needs, interests and aspirations of women and men are different.  
32. Public service provider is every state organizing institution, corporation, independent institution formed based on law for public service activities and other legal entities formed solely for public service activities.  
33. Integrated Service Center for Women's Empowerment and Children hereinafter abbreviated as P2TP2A is an integrated service center in efforts to empower women and children from various fields of development as well as protection of women and children from various types of discrimination and acts of violence, including human trafficking formed by the government or community-based, which includes: referral centers, business consultation centers, reproductive health consultation centers, integrated crisis centers (PKT), trauma recovery centers (trauma centers), women's crisis handling centers (women crisis centers), training centers, science and technology information centers (PIPTEK), safe houses (shelters), shelter houses, children's friend telephone (TESSA) 129 or other forms.  
34. Community organization is an organization established and formed by the community voluntarily based on common aspirations, will, needs, interests and goals to participate in development for the achievement of the goals of the Unitary State of the Republic of Indonesia based on Pancasila.  
35. Parents are biological father and/or mother or stepfather and/or stepmother or adoptive father and/or mother.  
36. Family is the smallest unit in society consisting of husband and wife or husband, wife and their children, or father and his children, or mother and her children, or blood relatives in a straight line up or down to the third degree.  
37. Customs are local wisdom values that apply and are still applied from generation to generation in the community environment.  
38. Regency Task Force is a coordination body at the regency level that coordinates policy efforts, programs and activities to realize a child-friendly regency.  
39. Action plan is the Regional Development of Child-Friendly Regency is a document containing policies, programs and activities to realize a Child-Friendly Regency.

#### Article 2

The Women's Empowerment and Child Protection System aims to:  
a. improve the quality of life of women, children, and the quality of families;  
b. enhance the institutional capacity of women's empowerment and child protection in Sukamara Regency, including the development of gender and child data systems; and  
c. provide protection of women's rights and fulfillment of children's rights including special protection for children from various forms of violence and other discriminatory treatment.

# CHAPTER II

## RIGHTS OF WOMEN AND CHILDREN

#### Article 3

Every woman and child has basic rights as a human being that must be protected, promoted, upheld and fulfilled in accordance with the provisions of laws and regulations.

#### Article 4

The rights of women as referred to in Article 2, include:  
a. living peacefully, safely, calmly, happily, prosperously both physically and mentally;  
b. enjoying a good and healthy living environment;  
c. living in a family within a legal marriage bond based on the provisions of laws and regulations;  
d. obtaining work according to ability, requirements and fair and just wages;  
e. specifically for women with disabilities having the right to obtain facilities and special treatment in public services;  
f. playing an active role in the political and governmental fields according to their potential;  
g. obtaining information and legal services;  
h. obtaining services to increase their income; and  
i. obtaining other rights according to their dignity and ability based on the provisions of laws and regulations;

#### Article 5

The rights of children as referred to in Article 2, include:  
a. obtaining education and teaching according to age level, physical and mental condition, intelligence and interests and talents;  
b. living, growing, developing and participating reasonably according to human dignity and receiving protection from acts of violence, exploitation, discrimination and neglect, cruelty, violence and persecution, injustice and mistreatment;  
c. having needs fulfilled by the family;  
d. receiving religious guidance;  
e. obtaining their identity;  
f. obtaining other rights according to the provisions of laws and regulations; and  
g. specifically for children with disabilities having the right to obtain facilities and special treatment in their services.

#### Article 6

In addition to the rights of women and children as referred to in Article 4 and Article 5, women and children victims of violence have the right to:  
a. have their dignity respected as human beings;  
b. the right to complaints and fast and affordable services;  
c. obtain information and legal certainty;  
d. confidentiality regarding the case being handled;  
e. compensation for the case being handled;  
f. obtain facilities in the judicial process;  
g. obtain assistance;  
h. obtain physical and psychological health recovery from the suffering experienced by the victim;  
i. determine their own decisions;  
j. obtain rehabilitation and social reintegration;  
k. obtain respect and services for survival, growth and development;  
l. obtain basic services;  
m. be free from negative stigma; and  
n. obtain freedom to determine choices and decide for themselves after their parents' divorce.

#### Article 7

The implementation of women's empowerment and child protection is based on the principles of:  
a. the best interests of women and children;  
b. gender justice and gender equality;  
c. local wisdom;  
d. law enforcement and legal certainty;  
e. non-discrimination;  
f. the right to life, survival and development; and  
g. respect for the views of children.

# CHAPTER III

## IMPROVING THE QUALITY OF LIFE OF WOMEN

## Part One

General

#### Article 8

1. In order to improve the quality of life of women, the regional government is responsible for:  
a. respecting and guaranteeing the rights of women without discrimination in accordance with human dignity;  
b. implementing gender mainstreaming;  
c. establishing a gender mainstreaming working group;  
d. enhancing women's empowerment in roles, quality and capacity in the fields of:
1\. politics;  
2\. law;  
3\. social; and
4\. economy.  
e. strengthening and developing women's empowerment service provider institutions in the Region.  
2. The responsibility of the Regional Government in efforts to improve the quality of life of women as referred to in paragraph (1) is carried out by the Regional Apparatus that administers governmental affairs in the field of women's empowerment and child protection.

## Part Two

Implementation of Gender Mainstreaming

#### Article 9

1. The Regent is responsible for the implementation of gender mainstreaming.  
2. The responsibility of the Regent as referred to in paragraph (1) may be delegated to the Vice Regent.

#### Article 10

In implementing Gender Mainstreaming as referred to in Article 8 paragraph (1) letter b, the Regent appoints the Regional Apparatus that administers governmental affairs in the field of women's empowerment and children as coordinator of the implementation of gender mainstreaming.

## Part Three

Establishment of Gender Mainstreaming Working Group

#### Article 11

1. In an effort to accelerate the institutionalization of Gender Mainstreaming, a Gender Mainstreaming working group is established in all Regional Apparatus.  
2. Members of the Gender Mainstreaming working group are all Heads of Regional Apparatus chaired by the Head of the Regional Apparatus that administers governmental affairs in the field of regional development planning as chair of the gender mainstreaming working group and the head of the Regional Apparatus that administers governmental affairs in the field of Women's Empowerment and Child Protection as head of the Secretariat of the Gender Mainstreaming Working Group.  
3. The establishment of the Gender Mainstreaming working group as referred to in paragraph (1) is stipulated by Regent's Decree.

#### Article 12

The Gender Mainstreaming Working Group as referred to in Article 11 has the duties to:  
a. promote and facilitate gender mainstreaming to each Regional Apparatus;  
b. carry out socialization and advocacy of gender mainstreaming to Sub-district Heads and Village Heads;  
c. prepare annual programs;  
d. encourage the realization of gender responsive planning and budgeting;  
e. prepare the work plan of the gender mainstreaming working group every year;  
f. be accountable to the Regent through the Vice Regent;  
g. formulate policy recommendations to the Regent;  
h. prepare the regional gender profile;  
i. conduct monitoring of gender mainstreaming in each agency;  
j. establish a technical team to conduct analysis of the regional budget;  
k. prepare a regional action plan for gender mainstreaming; and  
l. encourage the implementation of selection and appointment of gender mainstreaming focal points in each Regional Apparatus.

#### Article 13

1. The Technical Team as referred to in Article 12 letter j consists of apparatus who understand gender responsive budget analysis.  
2. The Regional Gender Mainstreaming Regional Action Plan as referred to in Article 12 letter k contains:  
a. gender mainstreaming in laws and regulations;  
b. gender mainstreaming in the development cycle;  
c. strengthening of gender mainstreaming institutions; and  
d. strengthening community participation.

#### Article 14

1. The gender mainstreaming focal point in each PD as referred to in Article 12 letter 1 consists of officials and/or staff in charge of planning and/or program duties.
2. The gender mainstreaming focal point as referred to in paragraph (1), has the following duties:
a. promoting gender mainstreaming in the work unit;
b. facilitating the preparation of work plans and budgeting of PD that are gender responsive;
c. conducting training, socialization, advocacy of gender mainstreaming to all officials and staff within the PD;
d. reporting the implementation of gender mainstreaming to the leadership of PD;
e. encouraging the implementation of gender analysis on policies, programs and activities in the work unit; and
f. facilitating the preparation of gender data in each PD.
3. The gender mainstreaming focal point as referred to in paragraph (1), is selected and determined by the PD.

## Part Four

Enhancement of Women's Role, Quality and Capability

### Paragraph 1

Political Field

#### Article 15

Efforts to enhance role, quality and capability in the political field as referred to in Article 7 paragraph (1) letter d number 1 include:
a. conducting socialization to increase women's politics in general elections;
b. implementing programs that encourage the level of women's involvement in political processes and political positions;
c. facilitating women's self-development in organizing, associating and assembling; and
d. involving women in decision-making concerning women's interests.

### Paragraph 2

Legal Field

#### Article 16

Efforts to enhance women's role, quality and capability in the legal field as referred to in Article 8 paragraph (1) letter d number 2 include:
a. conducting socialization to increase women's legal knowledge and awareness;
b. mapping and reviewing gender-biased policies; and
c. involving women's organizations in the process of drafting and discussing regional legal products related to women's participation.

### Paragraph 3

Social Field

#### Article 17

Efforts to enhance women's role, quality and capability in the social field as referred to in Article 7 paragraph (1) letter d number 3, include:
a. enhancement of knowledge and skills to encourage the fulfillment of educators in accordance with their potential;
b. enhancement and fulfillment of health quality for women;
c. enhancement of awareness and knowledge about independent, healthy and prosperous family planning including access to consultation services and marriage registration;
d. enhancement of women's awareness of the impact of early marriage;
e. facilitating and endeavoring the preservation of customs and the development of science, technology, arts and culture for the advancement of women;
f. enhancing women's awareness and knowledge of independent, healthy and prosperous family planning;
g. implementing programs to reduce maternal mortality rates; and
h. encouraging the formation of women's organizations.

### Paragraph 4

Economic Field

#### Article 18

Efforts to enhance women's role, quality and capability in the economic field as referred to in Article 8 paragraph (1) letter d number 4, include:
a. implementing training activity programs to enhance women's skills and expertise in order to realize the improvement of women's welfare;
b. facilitating the strengthening of business capital for women with weak economies;
c. marketing products from businesses of women with weak economies;
d. training to develop women's capabilities through economic empowerment of pre-prosperous families; and
e. comprehensive entrepreneurship training for women.

# CHAPTER IV

## PROTECTION OF WOMEN

## Part One

General

#### Article 19

1. The Regional Government in its efforts to protect women is responsible for:
a. guaranteeing women's rights without discrimination in accordance with laws and regulations;
b. establishing and implementing policy programs and activities for the protection of women's rights, including the provision of facilities and funding for the provision of services for women and child victims of violence;
c. cooperating with the community and/or Community Organizations in the implementation of women's protection in accordance with laws and regulations;
d. handling and empowering women who are victims of violence;
e. providing special protection to women victims of violence and human trafficking; and
f. taking preventive action in order to prevent violations of women's rights.
2. The responsibility of the Regional Government in efforts to protect women as referred to in paragraph (1) is carried out by PD that administers government affairs in the field of women's and children's empowerment and in its implementation may coordinate with relevant agencies.

## Part Two

Prevention of Violence Against Women

#### Article 20

In order to prevent the occurrence of violence against women, the regional government is obliged to:
a. conduct socialization regarding the prevention of violence against women by optimizing the role of customary, cultural, religious institutions and community organizations;
b. provide guidelines on handling violence against women;
c. involve community participation in recovery and reintegration;
d. develop an integrated database and information system on violence against women;
e. strengthen the function of community organizations engaged in women's and children's empowerment; and
f. provide public facilities that are comfortable and safe for women.

## Part Three

Effectiveness of Services for Women Victims of Violence

### Paragraph 1

General

#### Article 21

1. Protection of women victims of violence is carried out through integrated services.
2. The provision of integrated services to women victims of violence as referred to in paragraph (1) uses the principles of:
a. gender responsive;
b. non-discrimination;
c. equal and respectful relationships;
d. maintaining confidentiality;
e. providing a sense of security and comfort;
f. respecting individual differences;
g. non-judgmental;
h. respecting the choices and decisions of victims;
i. sensitive to the background and condition of victims and the use of appropriate language understood by victims;
j. fast and simple;
k. empathy; and
l. fulfillment of women's rights.

#### Article 22

Forms of integrated services for women victims of violence include:
a. complaint handling;
b. health rehabilitation;
c. social rehabilitation;
d. law enforcement and legal assistance; and
e. repatriation and social reintegration.

### Paragraph 2

Complaint Handling

#### Article 23

Integrated services for complaint handling as referred to in Article 22 letter a are carried out by:
a. hospitals;
b. community health centers;
c. women's service units;
d. trauma center shelters;
e. women crisis centers;
f. marriage counseling, guidance and preservation advisory boards;
g. police;
h. prosecutor's office;
i. courts;
j. ministry of religion, and/or
k. legal aid institutions.

#### Article 24

1. Complaints of acts of violence against women may be made by reporters consisting of victims, family, community and/or community organizations in the field of protection of women and children.
2. Reporting as referred to in paragraph (1) may lodge complaints:
a. directly;
b. by telephone; and/orc. by letter.
3. In addition to handling complaints submitted by reporters, officers at service institutions as referred to in Article 23, are also authorized to handle victims of acts of violence against women obtained through other information.
4. Further provisions regarding procedures for complaint handling services as referred to in paragraph (1) and paragraph (2) shall be regulated in a Regent Regulation.

### Paragraph 3

Health Rehabilitation

#### Article 25

1. Health Rehabilitation as referred to in Article 22 letter b, constitutes efforts that include aspects of:
a. promotive;
b. preventive;
c. curative; and
d. rehabilitative.
2. Health rehabilitation as referred to in paragraph (1) shall be conducted comprehensively, accessible 24 (twenty-four) hours at the Regional General Hospital and conducted in accordance with health service standards.
3. Women victims of acts of violence have the right to health services free of charge for both medical costs, medicolegal and visum.
4. Further provisions regarding procedures for implementation of health rehabilitation as referred to in paragraph (3) shall be regulated in a Regent Regulation.

### Paragraph 4

Social Rehabilitation

#### Article 26

1. Social rehabilitation as referred to in Article 22 letter c, is aimed at restoring and developing the capacity of women victims of acts of violence who experience social dysfunction to be able to carry out their social functions in society properly.
2. Social rehabilitation as referred to in paragraph (1) includes services:
a. counseling;
b. provision of safe houses; and
c. spiritual guidance.
3. Implementation of services as referred to in paragraph (2) shall be organized by:
a. Regional Apparatus that organizes government affairs in the field of social affairs;
b. Regional Apparatus that organizes government affairs in the field of women's empowerment and children, and may cooperate with integrated service centers women crisis center or community organizations in the social field that already exist; or.
c. religious institutions and/or organizations.

#### Article 27

1. Counseling services as referred to in article 26 paragraph (2) letter a, aim to:
a. assist women victims of acts of violence to recognize their problems and find the most effective way to overcome them themselves;
b. empower women victims of acts of violence to determine their future;
c. provide moral support to acts of violence in facing the process they are undergoing; and
d. make women victims of acts of violence feel accepted in the community environment.
2. Counseling services shall be conducted by gender-responsive social rehabilitation officers, consisting of:
a. counselors;
b. psychologists; and
c. psychiatrists.
3. Counseling services shall be conducted in places that guarantee a sense of security, comfort and confidentiality of information from women victims of acts of violence.
4. Stages of counseling services include:
a. initial counseling;
b. follow-up counseling;
c. case discussion; and
d. site visits.

#### Article 28

1. The Regional Government may provide safe houses for women victims of acts of violence.
2. Safe house provision services as referred to in Article 26 paragraph (2) letter b aim to protect women victims of acts of violence from intimidation by perpetrators or other parties.
3. The location of safe houses as referred to in paragraph (1) must be kept confidential.
4. Criteria for women victims of acts of violence who require safe houses include:
a. life-threatened;
b. receiving rejection from family or community;
c. requiring intensive services but their residence is relatively far; and/or
d. abandoned if not placed in a safe house.
5. Implementation of safe house services shall be conducted by social rehabilitation officers who have gender sensitivity or gender awareness, consisting of:
a. social workers/volunteers;
b. security officers; and/or
c. community leaders.
6. Further provisions regarding implementation of safe house services shall be regulated in a Regent Regulation.

#### Article 29

1. Spiritual guidance as referred to in Article 26 paragraph (2) letter c aims at psychological recovery.
2. Spiritual guidance as referred to in paragraph (1) shall be implemented based on the principles of:
a. not imposing any religion or belief; and
b. leaving the final decision in the hands of the victim.
3. Spiritual guidance shall be conducted by spiritual guidance officers.
4. Spiritual guidance officers as referred to in paragraph (3) consist of consultants of the Advisory, Development and Preservation of Marriage Board, Religious Affairs Office Officers, religious leaders, who have gender sensitivity or gender awareness.

### Paragraph 5

Law Enforcement and Legal Aid

#### Article 30

1. Law enforcement and legal aid services as referred to in article 22 letter d constitute actions by law enforcement officials to implement laws and regulations in order to handle cases of acts of violence against women especially to provide sanctions against perpetrators and provide protection for witnesses and/or victims.
2. Law enforcement and legal aid services as referred to in paragraph (1) shall be conducted by prioritizing the best interests of the victim.

#### Article 31

1. Law enforcement and legal aid services are aimed at providing assistance and protection to women victims of acts of violence and sanctions with a gender-responsive approach so that victims obtain justice and legal certainty.
2. Types of legal aid services include:
a. legal aid in the reporting process and preparation of examination reports;
b. legal aid in the prosecution process at the prosecutor's office;
c. legal aid in the examination process at court;
d. legal aid in the court hearing decision process until there is a final legal decision; and
e. legal aid in the mediation process.
3. The Regional Government shall provide legal aid to women victims of acts of violence based on the provisions of laws and regulations.
4. In providing legal aid as referred to in paragraph (3) the Regional Government may cooperate with legal aid institutions women crisis center, advocates and/or other legal assistants who are gender sensitive.

### Paragraph 6

Repatriation and Social Reintegration

#### Article 32

1. Repatriation is aimed at returning women victims of acts of violence to their region of origin.
2. Repatriation of women victims of acts of violence shall be accompanied by social reintegration efforts.

#### Article 33

1. Social reintegration as referred to in Article 32 paragraph (2) shall be conducted in order to reunite women victims of acts of violence with substitute family parties, or communities that can provide protection and fulfillment of needs for victims.
2. Social reintegration is aimed so that:
a. women victims of acts of violence can resume their social life; and
b. women victims of acts of violence can have a decent life.
3. Social reintegration as referred to in paragraph (1) shall be conducted after victims receive social rehabilitation services.

#### Article 34

The Regional Government shall implement repatriation and social reintegration of women victims of acts of violence in accordance with laws and regulations.

## Part Four

Employment Protection

#### Article 35

1. Employers who employ women must maintain decency, safety and security while in the workplace.
2. Employers must place female workers in places that are safe for women's reproductive health.
3. Employers must fulfill the rights of female workers to receive wages in accordance with the provisions of laws and regulations.
4. Employers must provide special breastfeeding facilities in their companies.

#### Article 36

1. Female workers/laborers who are less than 18 (eighteen) years old are prohibited from being employed between 23.00 WIB until 07.00 WIB.
2. Employers are prohibited from employing pregnant female workers/laborers who according to a doctor's statement are dangerous to the health and safety of their pregnancy.
3. Employers who employ workers/laborers between 23.00 WIB until 07.00 WIB, must:a. provide nutritious food and beverages; and
b. maintain decency and security while in the workplace.
4. Employers must provide shuttle transportation for female workers/laborers who depart and return between 23.00 WIB until 05.00 WIB.

# CHAPTER V

## IMPROVEMENT OF FAMILY QUALITY

#### Article 37

1. In order to improve family quality, the Regional Government is responsible for:
a. making efforts to improve family quality to realize gender equality and children's rights;
b. providing services for families in realizing gender equality and children's rights; and
c. strengthening and developing institutions providing family quality improvement services.
2. The responsibility of the Regional Government in efforts to improve family quality as referred to in paragraph (1) is carried out by PD in accordance with their duties and functions.

#### Article 38

The Regional Government makes efforts to improve family quality as referred to in Article 37 paragraph (1) letter a, including:
a. guidance;
b. counseling;
c. supervision;
d. facilitation of family development implementation; and
e. improving the health quality of pregnant and breastfeeding mothers.

#### Article 39

Guidance as referred to in article 38 letter a, includes:
a. improving child quality by providing access to information, education, counseling and services regarding care, nurturing and development of parenting patterns;
b. improving youth quality by providing access to information, education, counseling and services regarding family life through planned generation activities, youth information and counseling centers and youth family development;
c. improving the quality of life of the elderly through elderly development programs, elderly family development, elderly guidance and counseling; and

#### Article 40

Counseling as referred to in article 38 letter b, includes:
a. providing protection and assistance to develop themselves through improving family capabilities and skills;
b. conducting counseling on improving women's quality of life;
c. conducting counseling on improving gender equality in family and community life;
d. increasing access and opportunities to receive information and economic resources through efforts to increase prosperous family income and increase women's economic productivity; and

#### Article 41

#### Article 43

#### Article 44

3. Private parties who do not provide breastfeeding corner rooms as referred to in paragraph (2) are subject to administrative sanctions in the form of:
a. written warning;
b. limitation of business activities;
c. suspension of business activities;

# CHAPTER VII

## FULFILLMENT OF CHILDREN'S RIGHTS

## Part One

General

#### Article 48

1. The Regional Government in efforts to fulfill children's rights is responsible for:
a. establishing and implementing policies and programs/activities for fulfillment of children's rights;
b. respecting and guaranteeing the human rights of every child without distinguishing ethnicity, religion, race, class, gender, ethnicity, culture and language, legal status of the child, birth order of the child, and physical and/or mental condition;
c. strengthening and developing institutions providing family quality improvement services in realizing children's rights;
d. guaranteeing the protection, maintenance and welfare of children by taking into account the rights and obligations of parents, guardians or other persons who are legally responsible for the child;
e. guaranteeing the right of children to express opinions in accordance with the age and level of intelligence of the child;
f. conducting supervision and guidance in the context of implementing the fulfillment of children's rights; and
g. cooperating with private parties and/or NGOs in implementing child protection.
2. The responsibility of the Regional Government in efforts to protect children as referred to in paragraph (1) is carried out by PD that administers Government Affairs in the field of Women's Empowerment and Child Protection.

## Part Two

Birth Registration and Education

#### Article 49

1. Regional Government is obligated to organize birth registration of children with the issuance of birth certificates.  
2. Birth certificates as referred to in paragraph (1) shall be organized free of charge.

#### Article 50

Regional Government guarantees the implementation of a 12 (twelve) year compulsory education program for children aged 7 (seven) to 19 (nineteen) years.

#### Article 51

1. Every organizer of educational units is obligated to provide opportunities for children to obtain services without discrimination.  
2. Every organizer of educational units is prohibited from expelling children from educational institutions unless there is a guarantee for the continuity of the child's education.  
3. In the event that a child victim of violence experiences dropping out of school, the organizer of educational units is obligated to coordinate with relevant agencies.

#### Article 52

1. Children with disabilities and children with special needs are given equal opportunities to obtain formal, non-formal and informal education services, including inclusive education.  
2. Regional government provides teaching staff for inclusive education at every educational unit.

#### Article 53

Children in conflict with the law, children experiencing pregnancy outside of marriage and/or unwanted pregnancy as well as child victims of HIV/AIDS transmission are protected in their rights to obtain education.

## Part Three

Health and Social

#### Article 54

1. Regional Government is obligated to organize comprehensive health services for children, so that every child obtains optimal health status from the womb.  
2. Comprehensive health services as referred to in paragraph (1) include:  
a. promotive, preventive, curative and rehabilitative efforts for basic health services and care; and  
b. exemption from cost burden for malnourished children, children with disabilities, children with special needs, children infected with HIV/AIDS, child workers, child victims of narcotics abuse, alcohol, psychotropics, and other addictive substances, children who become victims of violence and child victims of human trafficking.  
3. Regional Government, community, family and parents are obligated to ensure that children who are born are protected from diseases that threaten survival and/or cause disability.

#### Article 55

1. Promotive efforts for basic health services as referred to in Article 54 paragraph (2) letter a are aimed at improving health status in a more optimal manner.  
2. Promotive efforts as referred to in paragraph (1) are carried out by providing socialization or counseling to the community regarding healthy lifestyle patterns which include:  
a. the importance of exclusive breastfeeding for infants;  
b. the importance of immunization for toddlers;  
c. clean living patterns;  
d. the importance of nutritious food; and  
e. the dangers of drugs and smoking.  
3. In addition to promotive efforts as referred to in paragraph (2), Regional Government may conduct other activities in the context of health organization.

#### Article 56

1. Preventive efforts for basic health services as referred to in Article 54 paragraph (2) letter a, are aimed at preventing the occurrence of disease or health problems.  
2. Efforts as referred to in paragraph (1) are carried out by:  
a. requiring mothers to provide exclusive breastfeeding to infants up to 6 (six) months of age;  
b. providing free immunization and vitamins to infants and toddlers;  
c. providing nutritious food to toddlers from underprivileged families;  
d. optimizing the role of posyandu for health services for mothers and toddlers;  
e. conducting periodic health examinations; and  
f. providing sanitation facilities and clean water at every school.

#### Article 57

1. Curative efforts for basic health services as referred to in Article 54 paragraph (2) letter a, are aimed at preventing disease from becoming more severe through treatment.  
2. Treatment as referred to in paragraph (1), shall be free of charge for children from underprivileged families.

#### Article 58

1. Rehabilitative efforts for basic health services as referred to in Article 54 paragraph (2) letter a, are aimed at maintaining and restoring conditions after illness.  
2. Rehabilitative efforts as referred to in paragraph (1), are carried out through periodic health examinations.

#### Article 59

1. Regional Government is obligated to organize social welfare for:  
a. children in conflict with the law;  
b. child victims of violence who are exploited economically and sexually, neglected children, child victims of kidnapping and child victims of human trafficking;  
c. children who do not have parents and are cared for by parties or families who are unable.  
d. child victims of natural disasters or social disasters; and  
e. children with disabilities.  
2. Social welfare as referred to in paragraph (1) may be in the form of provision of services:  
a. health;  
b. education;  
c. legal aid;  
d. assistance;  
e. social, mental and spiritual guidance;  
f. social rehabilitation; and/or  
g. reintegration.

## Part Four

Employment

#### Article 60

Regional government conducts periodic supervision of every person/entity that employs children in efforts to protect child workers.

#### Article 61

Every person/entity that employs children must observe the requirements:  
a. minimum age of 15 (fifteen) years;  
b. obtain written consent from the parent/guardian of the child worker;  
c. have a written work agreement between the employer and the parent/guardian of the worker and obtain validation from the authorized agency;  
d. not employ children at night;  
e. working time of maximum 3 (three) hours per day;  
f. not be employed in a place or environment that can disrupt the child's growth and development process, whether physically, mentally, morally and intellectually or the child's health;  
g. provide opportunities to obtain education in accordance with their talents and interests;  
h. employ for light types of work; and  
i. provide one day off per week.

#### Article 62

In the event that children are employed together with adult workers, the workplace for children must be separated from the workplace for adults.

#### Article 63

1. Every person/entity is prohibited from employing and involving children in the worst forms of work.  
2. The worst forms of work referred to in paragraph (1) include:  
a. all work in the form of slavery or the like;  
b. all work that utilizes, provides or offers children for prostitution, pornography production, pornographic performances, or gambling;  
c. all work that utilizes, provides or involves children for the production and trade of alcoholic beverages, narcotics, psychotropics, and other addictive substances; and/or  
d. all work that endangers the health, safety or morals of children.

## Part Five

Facilitation of the Juvenile Justice System

#### Article 64

1. Facilitation of the juvenile justice system includes criminal and civil justice.  
2. Facilitation of the criminal justice system as referred to in paragraph (1) includes:  
a. provision of companions or child volunteers during examination by investigators;  
b. provision of legal aid and psychologist services;  
c. involving members/investigators of the local police in efforts to prevent criminal acts committed by children, children as witnesses;  
d. realizing a restorative justice process for children in conflict with the law; and  
e. rehabilitation and social reintegration services.  
3. Fulfillment of children's rights in the civil justice system as referred to in paragraph (1) includes the provision and delivery of consultation and assistance services during the court process.  
4. In facilitating the juvenile justice system as referred to in paragraph (1) and paragraph (2), the authorized RG is the RG that organizes government affairs in the field of protection of women and children as well as social affairs.

## Part Six

Children's Forum

#### Article 65

1. In organizing the fulfillment of children's rights to express opinions and participate, the regional government facilitates the establishment of a children's forum.  
2. The children's forum as referred to in paragraph (1) represents children in the region, both representation of children's geographical domicile, components of children's socio-cultural groups and children's educational background.  
3. In every formulation of policies related to children, the regional government is obliged to consider and accommodate the opinions of children conveyed through the children's participation forum.  
4. Further provisions regarding the establishment of the children's forum as referred to in paragraph (1) through paragraph (3) shall be stipulated by Regent's Decree.

## Part Seven

Child-Friendly Regency

#### Article 66

1. In the context of fulfilling children's rights in an integrated, systematic and sustainable manner, the Regional Government establishes a Regional Action Plan for Child-Friendly Regency Development.  
2. The Regional Action Plan for Child-Friendly Regency Development as referred to in paragraph (1), contains:  
a. the concept of Child-Friendly Regency concerning objectives, strategies and roles of related parties;  
b. children's rights;  
c. the approach to developing a Child-Friendly Regency.

#### Article 67

1. The Regional Action Plan for Child-Friendly Regency Development is directed at fulfilling children's rights divided into 5 (five) target groups, comprising:  
a. civil rights and freedoms;  
b. family environment and alternative care;  
c. basic health and welfare;  
d. education, leisure time utilization, cultural activities; and  
e. special protection.  
2. Further provisions regarding the Regional Action Plan for Child-Friendly Regency Development as referred to in paragraph (1) shall be regulated in a Regent's Regulation.

#### Article 68

1. In order to ensure the effectiveness of implementing the Regional Action Plan for Child-Friendly Regency Development in the Region, a Child-Friendly Regency Task Force is established.  
2. The establishment and membership composition of the Child-Friendly Regency Task Force shall be stipulated by Regent's Decree.  
3. The Child-Friendly Regency Task Force is domiciled at the Regional Agency that administers governmental affairs in the field of women's empowerment and child protection.  
4. The Child-Friendly Regency Task Force as referred to in paragraph (1), has the principal tasks of:  
a. coordinating the implementation of policies and development of a Child-Friendly Regency;  
b. conducting socialization, advocacy and communication, information and education on Child-Friendly Regency policies;c. collecting baseline data for Child-Friendly Regency development;  
d. conducting needs analysis sourced from baseline data;  
e. disseminating baseline data;  
f. determining the focus and priority of programs in realizing a Child-Friendly Regency adjusted to regional potential;  
g. preparing a 5 (five) year Child-Friendly Regency regional action plan and work mechanism; and  
h. conducting monitoring, evaluation and reporting at least once (1) a year.

#### Article 69

In carrying out its duties, the Child-Friendly Regency Task Force may coordinate and cooperate with P2TP2A institutions or other institutions that provide child protection services.

# ELUCIDATION OF REGIONAL REGULATION OF SUKAMARA REGENCY

NUMBER 9 YEAR 2022 CONCERNING WOMEN'S EMPOWERMENT AND CHILD PROTECTION

##### I. GENERAL

The 1945 Constitution as a Constitutional foundation has explicitly regulated the importance of protection of Human Rights, including the rights of women and children, in addition to the guarantee from the 1945 Constitution of the Republic of Indonesia, the guarantee that every person must have the same dignity and degree, has also been recognized internationally. The Universal Declaration of Human Rights or UDHR of 1948 which underlies Human Rights in general has guaranteed that all people are born with freedom, have equal dignity and rights without exception.  

The 1945 Constitution of the Republic of Indonesia mandates the Regional Government to regulate and manage regional government affairs aimed at realizing community welfare, through improving services, protection and empowerment of the community. As a component of society that plays an important role in realizing a quality generation and as successors to the nation's struggle, women and children must receive guarantees for the fulfillment of children's rights.  

To ensure the implementation of women's empowerment and child protection in accordance with human dignity and in order to carry out mandatory affairs, the regional government needs to provide legal certainty in the implementation of women's empowerment and child protection through the formulation of a policy. The formulation of such policy is a form of support and concrete effort from various other parties in ensuring the fulfillment of the rights of women and children.  

The Implementation of Women's Empowerment and Child Protection is carried out based on the principles of:  
a. non-discrimination;  
b. the best interests of women and children;  
c. the right to life, survival and development;  
d. respect for children and legal certainty; and  
e. local wisdom and justice and gender equality.  

The formulation of this Regional Regulation on Women's Empowerment and Child Protection aims to:  
a. provide guidance in the planning and implementation of Women's Empowerment and Child Protection policies;  
b. increase efforts for Women's Empowerment and fulfillment of children's rights in order to realize improved quality of life for women and children in the Region;  
c. increase efforts to protect women and children from actions and policies that violate the rights of women and children;  
d. increase the value of local wisdom and the role of custom in Women's Empowerment and Child Protection, and  
e. increase the role of the Regional Government and other community institutions in efforts for Women's Empowerment and Child Protection.  

This Regional Regulation on Women and Child Protection regulates:  
a. Improving the quality of life of women which contains government responsibility, implementation of gender mainstreaming, efforts to increase the role, quality and capacity of women in the political, legal, social and economic fields;  
b. Protection of women which contains the responsibility of the regional government in the protection of women, prevention of violence against women, protection for women victims of violence, as well as complaint handling,  
c. Child Protection which contains the responsibility of the regional government, parents/family and community in the protection of children as well as efforts made by the regional government in the implementation of child protection. In addition, the need to establish a children's forum and Child-Friendly Regency as a form of appreciation in realizing protection and fulfillment of children's rights.
