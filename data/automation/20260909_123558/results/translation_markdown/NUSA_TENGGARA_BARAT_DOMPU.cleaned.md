REGENT OF DOMPU

WEST NUSA TENGGARA PROVINCE

NUMBER O2 OF 2024

CONCERNING

IMPLEMENTATION OF PROTECTION FOR WOMEN AND CHILDREN VICTIMS OF VIOLENCE

BY THE GRACE OF ALMIGHTY GOD

REGENT OF DOMPU,

Considering:




a. that women and children are very valuable assets to ensure the continuity of the nation's existence, therefore protection from all forms of violence and injustice is necessary;
b. that protection is carried out to guarantee constitutional rights and the fulfillment of the rights of women and children to be free from violence and treatment that degrades the dignity of women and children;
c. that to provide direction and foundation to all parties involved in the implementation of protection for women and children, a regulation is needed that can guarantee legal certainty effectively and efficiently;

# CHAPTER I

## GENERAL PROVISIONS

#### Article 1

In this Regional Regulation, what is meant by:  
1. Region is Dompu Regency.  
2. Regional Government is the Regent as an element of regional government administration who leads the implementation of government affairs that are the authority of Regional autonomy.  
3. Regent is the Regent of Dompu.  
4. Regional House of Representatives hereinafter abbreviated as DPRD is the Regional House of Representatives of Dompu Regency.  
5. Regional Apparatus is an auxiliary element of the Regent and DPRD in the administration of government affairs that are the authority of the region.  
6. Agency hereinafter referred to as PPPA Agency is the Agency for Women's Empowerment and Child Protection of Dompu Regency.  
7. Child is a person who is not yet 18 years old, including a child who is still in the womb.  
8. Woman is a person who is female in gender.  
9. Violence is any act unlawfully with or without the use of means against physical and psychological that causes danger to life, body or causes the deprivation of a person's freedom.  
10. Violence Against Women is any Action based on gender differences that results in or may result in misery or suffering of women physically, sexually, or psychologically, economic exploitation, social, including threats of certain Actions, coercion, or arbitrary deprivation of freedom, whether occurring in the public sphere or in private life.  
11. Violence Against Children is any act against a child that results in misery or suffering physically, sexually, psychologically, economic exploitation, social, including neglect and maltreatment that threatens bodily integrity and degrades the dignity of the child.  
12. Domestic violence is any Action or act against a person, especially women, that results in misery or neglect of the household including threats to commit acts of coercion or deprivation of freedom unlawfully within the scope of the household.  
13. Criminal Act of Sexual Violence hereinafter abbreviated as TPKS is any act that meets the elements of a criminal act as regulated in the Law on Criminal Acts of Sexual Violence and other sexual acts regulated in the Law as long as determined in the Law on Criminal Acts of Sexual Violence.  
14. Protection of Women is all activities aimed at providing a sense of security carried out by the Regional Government, police, prosecution, courts, social institutions, or other parties who know or hear that violence against women will or has occurred.  
15. Protection of Children is all activities to guarantee and protect children and their rights so that they can live, grow, develop and participate optimally in accordance with human dignity and receive protection from violence and discrimination.  
16. Service is an action taken as soon as possible to the victim when seeing, hearing and knowing that violence against the victim will, is or has occurred.  
17. Companion is a person or representative from an Institution who has the expertise to accompany victims to conduct counseling, therapy, advocacy, reintegration for the strengthening and recovery of victims of violence.  
18. Victim of violence is an individual or group of people who experience suffering either physical, mental or emotional, economic loss either physical suffering, mental or emotional, economic loss, or experience neglect, reduction or deprivation of their basic rights as a result of human rights violations or criminal acts as well as acts of violence committed either by state apparatus or by the state or regional government apparatus or individuals including the victim's heirs.  
19. Prevention is an effort made by the Regional Government to prevent acts of violence against women and children.
20. Outreach is an action to respond to reports of alleged problems of women and children that need to be verified and followed up.  
21. Social rehabilitation is a process of refunctionalization and development to enable a person to be able to carry out social functions properly in community life.  
22. Coordination is an effort made to realize integration and harmony in the context of services for women and children experiencing problems.
23. Task Force for Handling Women's and Children's Problems hereinafter abbreviated as Satgas PPA is a task force formed to handle women's and children's problems reported to women's and children's service organizations that have been established by the regional government.  
24. Regional Technical Implementation Unit for Women's and Children's Protection hereinafter abbreviated as UPTD PPA is a Regional Technical Implementation Unit established by the Regional Government in providing Services for women and children who experience violence, discrimination, special protection, and other problems.  
25. Regional Child Protection Commission hereinafter abbreviated as KPAD is an independent Institution established by the Regent to support the supervision of the implementation of child protection in the region.  
26. Community-Based Integrated Child Protection hereinafter abbreviated as PATBM is a movement of networks or groups of citizens at the community level who work in a coordinated manner to achieve the goals of child protection.  
27. Regional Long-Term Development Plan hereinafter referred to as RPJPD is a regional planning document for a period of 20 (twenty) years.  
28. Regional Medium-Term Development Plan hereinafter referred to as RPJMD is a regional planning document for a period of 5 (five) years.  
29. Task Force for Prevention and Handling of Criminal Acts of Human Trafficking is a coordinating institution tasked with coordinating efforts to prevent and handle criminal acts of human trafficking in Dompu Regency.  
30. Task Force for Prevention of Child Marriage is a coordinating institution tasked with coordinating efforts to prevent child marriage in Dompu Regency.  
31. Task Force for Protection of Women and Children is a coordinating institution tasked with coordinating efforts to implement the protection of women and children in Dompu Regency.  
32. Parents are biological father and/or mother, or step father and/or mother, or adoptive father and/or mother.  
33. Family is a person who has a blood relationship in a straight line upward or downward and collateral line up to the third degree, or who has a marital relationship, or a person who is a dependent of women and/or children.  
34. Community is individuals, families, groups, and social organizations and/or community organizations.  
35. Child-Friendly Regency is a regency that has a child rights-based development system through the integration of commitments and resources of government, community, business world, and media that are planned comprehensively and sustainably in policies, programs and activities to ensure the fulfillment of children's rights and special protection of children.  
36. Private Sector is Non-Governmental Institutions, Business World and Mass Media.

#### Article 2

The Implementation of Protection of Women and Children Victims of Violence is carried out based on the principles of:  
a. humanity;  
b. justice and gender equality;  
c. non-discrimination;  
d. order and legal certainty;  
e. openness;  
f. protection; and  
g. best interests of the victim.

#### Article 3

The objectives of organizing the Protection of Women and Children Victims of Acts of Violence are to:
a. prevent all forms of violence against women and children;
b. provide protection and services to women and children victims of gender-based violence;
c. provide a sense of security to women and children victims of violence;
d. restore the physical, psychological and economic condition of women and children victims of violence; and
e. take action against perpetrators of violence against women and children.

#### Article 4

The scope of the Organization of Protection of Women and Children Victims of Acts of Violence in this Regional Regulation covers forms of violence including:
a. the rights of Women and Children, obligations and responsibilities;
b. organization of protection;
c. special protection of Children;
d. guidance and supervision;
e. cooperation and partnership;
f. monitoring, evaluation and reporting; and
g. financing.

# CHAPTER II

## FORMS OF VIOLENCE

#### Article 5

Forms of Violence against Women and Children include:
a. Physical violence;
b. Psychological violence;
c. Sexual violence;
d. Domestic neglect;
e. Exploitation;
e. Trafficking in persons; and/or
f. Other violence.

#### Article 6

1. Physical violence as referred to in Article 5 letter a, constitutes an act that results in pain accompanied by injury, wounds or disability to the body, miscarriage, fainting and/or causing death of Women and/or Children.
2. Physical violence as referred to in paragraph (1) results in:
a. the victim suffering serious illness or injury;
b. the death of the victim; and/or
c. causing illness or impediment to carrying out work, position or livelihood or daily activities.

#### Article 7

Psychological violence as referred to in Article 5 letter b, constitutes an act that results in fear, loss of self-confidence, loss of ability to act, feelings of helplessness and/or psychological suffering to Women and/or Children.

#### Article 8

1. Sexual violence as referred to in Article 5 letter c, includes:
a. non-physical sexual harassment;
b. physical sexual harassment;
c. forced contraception;
d. forced sterilization;
e. forced marriage;
f. sexual torture;
g. sexual exploitation;
h. sexual slavery; and
i. electronic-based sexual violence.
2. In addition to sexual violence as referred to in paragraph (1), sexual violence also includes:
a. rape;
b. indecent acts;
c. intercourse with Children, indecent acts against Children, and/or sexual exploitation of Children;
d. acts violating decency that are contrary to the will of the Victim;
e. pornography involving Children or pornography that explicitly contains Violence and sexual exploitation;
f. forced prostitution;
g. criminal acts of trafficking in persons intended for sexual exploitation;
h. sexual violence within the scope of the household;
i. criminal acts of money laundering where the predicate offense constitutes TPKS; and
j. other criminal acts explicitly stated as TPKS as regulated in the provisions of laws and regulations.

#### Article 9

Neglect within the household as referred to in Article 5 letter d, includes:
a. acts of deliberately neglecting women within the scope of their household, whereas according to the law applicable to them or due to agreement or contract they are obliged to provide livelihood and care to such women;
b. acts that result in the failure to fulfill the needs of children properly, whether physical, mental, spiritual or social, committed by parents, guardians, or other parties responsible for their care;
c. acts of deliberately neglecting to care for or look after children as they should, committed by parents, guardians, or any other party responsible for their care; and
d. deliberate neglect of children who still require care by their biological mother to become Indonesian Migrant Workers abroad.

#### Article 10

1. Exploitation as referred to in Article 5 letter e, includes:
a. acts of economic or sexual exploitation with the intention of benefiting oneself or others;
b. acts with or without the consent of the victim which include but are not limited to prostitution, forced labor or services, slavery or similar practices, oppression, extortion, utilization of physical, sexual, reproductive organs, or unlawfully transferring or transplanting organs and/or body tissues or exploiting the labor or ability of a person by another party to obtain benefits both material and immaterial; and
c. acts of exploiting children in horse racing activities as jockeys by disregarding children's rights and/or with the intention of exploiting the labor or ability of children to obtain benefits both material and immaterial.
2. The involvement of Children in horse racing activities as jockeys as referred to in paragraph (1) letter c, shall be further regulated in a Regent Regulation.

#### Article 11

1. Other violence as referred to in Article 5 (1) letter g, constitutes threats of violence and coercion. Threats of violence as referred to in paragraph (1) include any unlawful act in the form of speech, writing, images, symbols, or body movements, whether with or without using means that create fear or restrict a person's fundamental freedom.
2. Coercion as referred to in paragraph (1) includes a situation where a person/victim is made to do something in such a way that the person does something contrary to their own will.

# CHAPTER III

## RIGHTS OF WOMEN AND CHILDREN VICTIMS OF ACTS OF VIOLENCE

#### Article 12

Women and Children Victims of Acts of Violence receive the following rights:
a. the right to have their dignity and worth as human beings respected;
b. the right to recovery;
c. the right to determine their own decisions;
d. the right to obtain information;
e. the right to confidentiality;
f. the right to social rehabilitation;
g. the right to appropriate, comfortable, and needs-based complaint handling;
h. the right of victims and their families to obtain facilities in the judicial process;
i. the right to assistance; and
j. to receive continuous handling up to the rehabilitation stage.

#### Article 13

Children Victims of Acts of Violence in addition to receiving the rights as referred to in Article 12 also receive the following special rights:
a. the right to survival, growth, and development;
b. the right to basic population services;
c. the right to equal protection;
d. the right to be free from various stigma; and
e. the right to obtain freedom.

#### Article 14

1. In the event that Victims of Acts of Violence as referred to in Article 13 require continuous handling, the victims may be placed in a safe house or alternative residence.
2. Continuous handling as referred to in paragraph (1) constitutes handling at every stage required.

#### Article 15

1. A safe house as referred to in Article 14 paragraph (1) provides a temporary safe place and/or shelter for victims of violence.
2. The organization and/or management of safe houses as referred to in paragraph (1) shall be carried out by the PPPA Office.
3. For the safety and at the request of victims of violence, the manager or organizer of safe houses as referred to in paragraph (2) may place victims of violence in a certain confidential location.
4. Provisions regarding the organizers of safe houses as referred to in paragraph (2) shall be further regulated in a Regent Regulation.

# CHAPTER IV

## OBLIGATIONS AND RESPONSIBILITIES

#### Article 16

The Implementation of Protection of Women and Children Victims of Violence is a joint obligation and responsibility of:  
a. Regional Government;  
b. Private Sector and Community Institutions;  
c. Community; and  
d. Family and/or Parents.

#### Article 17

1. The obligations and responsibilities of the Regional Government as referred to in Article 16 letter a include:  
a. establishing policies, programs, and activities for the protection of women and children from acts of violence;  
b. implementing policies for the protection of women and children from acts of violence established by the government;  
c. cooperating with law enforcement apparatus, government apparatus, as well as educational institutions, health institutions, hospitals, and various non-governmental organizations concerned with women and children from acts of violence;  
d. providing support for facilities and infrastructure for the implementation of protection of women and children from acts of violence;  
e. allocating budget for the implementation of protection of women and children, in accordance with regional financial capacity;  
f. fostering and supervising the implementation of protection of women and children; and  
g. realizing a Child-Friendly Regency.  
2. In order to carry out the obligations and responsibilities as referred to in paragraph (1), the Regent establishes programs and victim protection action activities in the Regional Action Plan and Main Performance Indicators in supporting the main regional programs as a basis for the Region in implementing the protection of women and children.  
3. The Regional Action Plan as referred to in paragraph (2), is part of the RPJMD.  
4. Further provisions regarding the Regional Action Plan (4) as referred to in paragraph (2) shall be regulated by Regent's Decree.

#### Article 18

The Regent in carrying out the obligations and responsibilities as referred to in Article 17 delegates to the relevant Regional Apparatus/Institutions.

#### Article 19

1. The obligations and responsibilities of the Private Sector and Community Institutions as well as the Community as referred to in Article 16 letter b, letter c, shall be implemented in the form of community participation.  
2. The form of community participation as referred to @) in paragraph (1), includes:   
a. preventing the occurrence of violence against women and children;  
b. providing emergency assistance to victims;  
c. providing information and/or reporting acts of violence against women and children to law enforcement or authorized parties;  
d. protecting victims; and €. participating in the handling of victims of acts of violence;  
3. The form of community participation as referred to in paragraph (2), shall be carried out responsibly in accordance with the provisions of laws and regulations.

#### Article 20

The Obligations of Family and/or Parents as referred to in Article 16 letter d, who legally have full responsibility to prevent all forms of violence and protect women and children as Family members.

# CHAPTER V

## IMPLEMENTATION OF PROTECTION

## Part One

Institutions

#### Article 21

1. The Regional Government conducts empowerment and awareness to families, parents, and the community.  
2. Empowerment and awareness as referred to in paragraph (1) shall be carried out by:  
a. providing materials on the prevention of acts of violence against women and children in both formal and informal education;  
b. providing accessibility to obtain education, training, funding, income enhancement and social services;  
c. creating a friendly and safe work environment atmosphere for women;  
d. building community participation and concern for the protection of women and children from acts of violence;  
e. building and providing an information system that is easily accessible;  
f. building networks and cooperation with law enforcement apparatus, government apparatus, universities and various non-governmental organizations that work and/or care about women and children; and  
g. providing information, guidance and/or counseling and realizing a Child-Friendly Regency.

#### Article 22

1. In order to implement services and protection for women and children from acts of violence.  
2. The Regional Government establishes UPTD PPA UPTD PPA as referred to in paragraph (1), functions to implement services:  
a. public complaints;  
b. victim outreach;  
c. case management;  
d. temporary shelter;
e. mediation; and  
f. victim assistance.

#### Article 23

1. In implementing protection for women and children from acts of violence, the Regional Government may also establish a task force.  
2. The task force as referred to in paragraph (1) includes:  
a. Task Force for the Prevention and Handling of Criminal Acts of Human Trafficking;  
b. Task Force for the Protection of Women and Children;  
c. Task Force for the Prevention of Child Marriage;
3. Further provisions regarding the position, organizational structure, duties and functions as well as working procedures of the task force as referred to in paragraph (2) shall be stipulated by Regent's Regulation.

#### Article 24

The Regional Government may establish KPAD in supervising the implementation of child protection in the region.  Further provisions regarding the position, organizational structure, duties and functions as well as working procedures of KPAD shall be regulated by Regent's Regulation.

#### Article 25

1. The Regional Government may establish Satgas PPA in an effort to coordinate the implementation of services for Women and Children victims of acts of violence.  
2. Satgas PPA as referred to in paragraph (1) aims to:  
a. coordinate and synchronize protection services for women and children carried out by the Regional Government, Regional Apparatus/Institutions and the community;  
b. improve cooperation and referral systems; and
c. collect, compile and present reports of violence.  
3. The establishment of Satgas PPA as referred to in paragraph (1) shall be stipulated by Regent's Decree.

#### Article 26

1. Village Government and/or Lurah as representatives of the Regional Government in the implementation of child protection at the village/kelurahan level may establish PATBM.  
2. PATBM functions as a network or community group that works in a coordinated manner in the implementation of child protection at the village/kelurahan level.  
3. The establishment of PATBM as referred to in paragraph (1) shall be stipulated by Village Head Decree.

## Part Two

Prevention

#### Article 27

1. Efforts to prevent violence against Women and Children shall be carried out in an integrated manner by Regional Apparatus whose duties and functions are in the fields of:  
a. PPPA;  
b. social affairs;  
c. health;  
d. education;  
e. manpower;  
f. population and civil registration;  
g. law;  
g. cooperatives and micro enterprises;  mental and spiritual; and  peace and order.  
2. Prevention efforts as referred to in paragraph (1) shall be implemented by:
a. building cooperation with law enforcement apparatus;  
b. law enforcement apparatus, government apparatus, as well as Educational Institutions, Health, hospitals, various NGOs concerned with women and children;  
c. building community and family participation and concern for the prevention and protection of women and children from acts of violence;  establishing a violence prevention system that provides a complete and easily accessible information system;  
d. conducting socialization on laws and regulations related to the protection of women and children victims of violence;  
e. providing critical education on the rights of women and children as victims of violence to the community; and  
f. opening complaint posts for the protection of women and children from acts of violence.  
3. Prevention of acts of violence by Regional Apparatus as referred to in paragraph (1), shall be carried out in an integrated and sustainable manner based on RPJPD and RPJMD.

## Part Three

Forms and Service Mechanisms

#### Article 28

Forms of services for acts of violence experienced by Women and Children include:  (1)
a. complaint services;  
b. health services;  
c. legal aid;  
d. submission of reports;  
e. repatriation and social reintegration, rehabilitation, and assistance services.  
2. In implementing the forms of services as referred to in paragraph (1), the Regional Government cooperates with related regional apparatus/institutions.

#### Article 29

Complaint services as referred to in Article 28 paragraph (1) letter a, include:  
a. identification or initial recording of victims; and  
b. informed consent.

#### Article 30

Health services as referred to in Article 28 paragraph (1) letter b, include:  
a. first aid to victims;  
b. treatment and recovery of physical injuries aimed at restoring the physical condition of victims carried out by medical personnel and paramedics; and  
c. referral to health services.  
d. medicolegal;  

psychological assistance.

#### Article 31

1. Legal aid services as referred to in Article 28 paragraph (1) letter c, aim to assist victims in undergoing the judicial process.  
2. Legal aid services as referred to in paragraph (1) are carried out by:  
a. providing legal consultation that includes information regarding victims' rights and the judicial process;  
b. accompanying victims at the reporting, investigation, prosecution, and examination levels in court hearings and helping victims to fully describe the violence they experienced; and  
c. coordinating with fellow law enforcers, volunteer assistants, and social workers so that the judicial process proceeds as it should.

#### Article 32

Report submission services as referred to in Article 28 paragraph (1) letter d, aim to accompany women and children as victims of acts of violence after mediation results are obtained from UPTD PPA.

#### Article 33

1. Repatriation and social reintegration services as referred to in Article 28 paragraph (1) letter e, aim to return victims to be accepted back into their social environment.  
2. Repatriation and social reintegration services for victims as referred to in paragraph (1) are carried out after socialization regarding the rights of women and children as victims of violence.  
3. Repatriation and social reintegration services as referred to in paragraph (2) are carried out by the Regional Government in coordination with related regional apparatus/institutions, and the community.

#### Article 34

1. Social rehabilitation services as referred to in Article 28 paragraph (1) letter f, include:  
a. motivation and psychosocial diagnosis;  
b. social assistance and aid;  
c. care and nurturing;  
d. vocational training and entrepreneurship development;
e. mental spiritual guidance;  
f. physical guidance;  
g. social guidance and psychosocial counseling;  
h. accessibility services;  
i. resocialization guidance;  
j. follow-up guidance; and/or  
k. referral.  
2. Forms of vocational training and entrepreneurship development as referred to in paragraph (1) letter c, include:  
a. skills training;  
b. field work practice;  
c. apprenticeship;  
d. entrepreneurial skills training;  
e. facilitation of joint business group formation;  
f. business capital in the form of business support tools; and  
g. business implementation assistance.

#### Article 35

Assistance Services as referred to in Article 28 paragraph (1) letter g, include:  
a. accompanying victims during the examination and health recovery process;  
accompanying victims during the medicolegal process;  
b. accompanying victims during the mediation process and/or examination at the Police, Prosecutor's Office, and Court;  
monitoring the interests and rights of victims in the examination process at the Police, Prosecutor's Office and Court;  
c. maintaining the privacy and confidentiality of victims from all parties who have no interest, including coverage by mass media;  
d. coordinating with other assistants; and  
providing continuous treatment up to the rehabilitation stage.

#### Article 36

1. The provision of services to Victims as referred to in Article 28 is carried out every day (including holidays), in a manner that is:  
a. fast;  
b. safe and comfortable;  
c. empathetic;  
d. non-discriminatory;  
e. easily accessible;  
f. free of charge; and  
g. guaranteed confidentiality.  
2. Service providers or managers are prohibited from charging any fees to victims of violence.

## Part Four

Assistance

#### Article 37

1. The Regional Government may provide assistance to women and children victims of acts of violence.  
2. Assistance as referred to in paragraph (1), includes all integrated efforts to restore and strengthen the condition of victims, carried out in health service units, education, police, prosecutor's office, courts, and social service institutions.  
3. Assistance as referred to in paragraph (2), is carried out by UPTD PPA assistants and other Assistants.

# CHAPTER VI

## SPECIAL PROTECTION FOR CHILDREN

#### Article 38

1. The Regional Government and other institutions are obligated and responsible for providing Special Protection to Children.  
2. Special Protection for Children as referred to in paragraph (1), is provided to:  
a. Children in Emergency Situations;  
b. Children in Conflict with the Law;  
c. Children from Minority and Isolated Groups;  
d. Children who are Exploited Economically and/or Sexually;  
e. Children who are Victims of Narcotics, Alcohol, Psychotropic, and other Addictive Substance Abuse,  
f. Children who are Victims of Pornography;  
g. Children with HIV and AIDS;  
h. Child Victims of Kidnapping, Sale, and/or Trafficking;  
i. Child Victims of Physical and/or Psychological Violence;  
j. Children with Disabilities, Child Victims of Sexual Crimes;  
k. Child Victims of Terrorism Networks;  
l. Children who are Victims of Stigmatization from Labeling;  
m. Child Victims of Mistreatment and Neglect;  
n. Children with Deviant Social Behavior, and Related to Their Parents' Condition.  
3. Special Protection for Children is carried out through efforts to:  
a. rapid handling, including treatment and/or physical, psychological, and social rehabilitation, as well as prevention of diseases and other health disorders;  
b. psychosocial assistance during treatment until recovery;  
c. provision of social assistance for Children from underprivileged families; and  
d. provision of protection and assistance in every judicial process.  
4. Special Protection for Children as referred to in paragraph (1) is provided at UPTD PPA that has been established with reference to established service standards.  
5. Special Protection for Children as referred to in paragraph (1) is implemented quickly, comprehensively and in an integrated manner.

#### Article 39

1. Special Protection for Children in Emergency Situations as referred to in Article 38 paragraph (2) letter a, shall be provided to:  
a. Children who become refugees;  
b. Children victims of riots;  
c. Children victims of natural disasters; and  
d. Children in armed conflict situations.  
2. In addition to children as referred to in paragraph (1), Special Protection for Children in Emergency Situations shall also be provided to:  
a. Children victims of social disasters;  
b. Children victims of non-natural disasters; and  
c. Children of female convicts/detainees.  
3. Special Protection as referred to in paragraph (1) shall be carried out through:
a. prevention so that children do not become victims in emergency situations;  
b. recording the number of children requiring Special Protection in emergency situations;  
c. mapping the basic and specific needs of children requiring Special Protection in emergency situations;  
d. security and safety guarantees for children in emergency situations;  
e. registration of children and their families for tracing and family reunification;  
f. priority emergency rescue, evacuation, and security measures;  
g. recovery of physical, psychological and social health of children in emergency situations;  
h. provision of legal aid, assistance, physical, psychological and social rehabilitation for children in emergency situations;  
i. care;  
j. improvement of facilities needed by children in emergency situations;  
k. fulfillment of basic and specific needs of children consisting of food, clothing, shelter, Education, provision of health services, learning and recreation, security guarantees, and equal treatment;  
l. fulfillment of special needs for children with disabilities and children experiencing psychosocial problems;  
m. exemption from Education fees whether conducted in formal or non-formal Educational Institutions during the emergency period;  
n. provision of services for fulfillment of children's identity rights and important documents lost due to emergency situations; and/or  
o. provision of social reintegration services.  
4. Special Protection for Children as referred to in paragraph (2) shall be provided in the form of care, nurturing, as well as fulfillment of basic needs and special needs of children in accordance with their age level and development.

#### Article 40

1. Special Protection for children in Conflict with the Law as referred to in Article 38 Paragraph (2) letter b, shall be carried out through:  
a. humane treatment taking into account needs according to their age, separation from adults;  
b. provision of legal aid and other assistance effectively;  
c. implementation of recreational activities;  
d. freedom from torture, punishment, or other treatment that is cruel, inhumane, and degrading to dignity and degree;  
e. avoidance of imposition of death penalty and/or life imprisonment;  
f. avoidance of arrest, detention, or imprisonment, except as a last resort and for the shortest period of time;  
g. provision of justice before a children's court that is objective, impartial, and in a hearing closed to the public;  
h. avoidance of publication of their identity;  
i. provision of accompaniment by parents/guardians and persons trusted by the child;  
j. provision of social advocacy;  
k. provision of private life;  
l. provision of accessibility, especially for children with disabilities;  
m. provision of Education;  
n. provision of health services; and  
o. provision of other rights in accordance with the provisions of laws and regulations.  
2. Special Protection for Children in Conflict with the Law as referred to in paragraph (1) shall be carried out through efforts of:  
a. prevention;  
b. administrative case resolution;  
c. rehabilitation; and  
d. social reintegration.

#### Article 41

Special Protection for Children from Minority and Isolated Groups as referred to in Article 38 Paragraph (2) letter c, shall be carried out through the provision of facilities and infrastructure to be able to enjoy their own culture, acknowledge and practice their own religion, and use their own language.

#### Article 42

1. Special Protection for Children Who Are Exploited Economically and/or Sexually as referred to in Article 38 paragraph (2) letter d, shall be carried out through:  
a. dissemination and/or socialization of provisions of laws and regulations related to the protection of Children Who Are Exploited Economically and/or Sexually;  
b. monitoring, reporting, and imposition of sanctions; and  c. involvement of various companies, labor unions, non-governmental organizations, and the community in the elimination of exploitation of children economically and/or sexually.  
2. In the event that Children Who Are Exploited Economically and/or sexually require recovery to their original condition, services must be provided in the form of:  
a. medical rehabilitation;  
b. social rehabilitation;  
c. legal aid and social assistance; and/or  
d. repatriation and social reintegration.

#### Article 43

Special Protection for Children who are Victims of Narcotics, Alcohol, Psychotropic, and Other Addictive Substances Abuse as referred to in Article 38 paragraph (2) letter e, shall be carried out through efforts of supervision, prevention, treatment, and rehabilitation.

#### Article 44

1. Special Protection for Children Who Are Victims of Pornography as referred to in Article 38 paragraph (2) letter f, shall be implemented through efforts of guidance, assistance, as well as social, physical and mental health recovery.  
2. Provisions regarding guidance, assistance, social recovery, physical and mental health as referred to in paragraph (1) shall be implemented in accordance with the provisions of laws and regulations.

#### Article 45

Special Protection for Children with HIV and AIDS as referred to in Article 38 paragraph (2) letter g, shall be carried out through efforts of supervision, prevention, treatment, care, and rehabilitation.

#### Article 46

Special Protection for Child Victims of Kidnapping, Sale, and/or Trafficking as referred to in Article 38 paragraph (2) letter h, shall be carried out through supervision, protection, prevention, care and rehabilitation.

#### Article 47

1. Special Protection for Child Victims of Physical and/or Psychological Violence as referred to in Article 38 paragraph (2) letter i, shall be carried out through efforts of:  
a. dissemination and socialization of provisions of laws and regulations that protect Child victims of acts of violence; and  
b. monitoring, reporting, and imposition of sanctions.  
2. Special Protection as referred to in paragraph (1) shall be carried out in the form of:  
a. prevention;  
b. assistance;  
c. medical rehabilitation; and  
d. social rehabilitation.  
3. Handling of Child Victims of Physical and/or Psychological Violence as referred to in paragraph (2) shall be implemented in accordance with the provisions of laws and regulations.

#### Article 48

Special Protection for Child Victims of Sexual Crimes as referred to in Article 38 paragraph (2) letter j, shall be carried out through:  
a. education about reproductive health, religious values, and moral values;  
b. social rehabilitation;  
c. psychosocial assistance during treatment until recovery; and  
d. provision of protection and assistance at every level of examination starting from investigation, prosecution, up to examination in court hearings.

#### Article 49

1. Special Protection for Child Victims of Terrorism Networks as referred to in Article 38 paragraph (2) letter k, shall be carried out through efforts of:  
a. education about education, ideology and nationalism values;  
b. social rehabilitation, and  
c. counseling about the dangers of terrorism;  
d. rehabilitation about the dangers of terrorism;  
e. social assistance.  
2. Special Protection for Child Victims of Terrorism Networks shall also be provided in the form of:  
a. fulfillment of the rights of Child Victims of Terrorism Networks to care and psychological health recovery;  
b. medical rehabilitation;  
c. re-education and social reintegration; and  
d. safety guarantees both physical, mental, and social for Child Victims of Terrorism Networks.

#### Article 50

Special Protection for Child Victims of Mistreatment and Child Victims of Neglect as referred to in Article 38 paragraph (2) letter m, shall be carried out through supervision, prevention, care, counseling, social rehabilitation and social assistance efforts.

#### Article 51

Special Protection for Children with Deviant Social Behavior as referred to in Article 38 paragraph (2) letter n, shall be carried out through supervision, prevention, care, counseling, social rehabilitation and social assistance efforts.

#### Article 52

Special Protection for Children who are Victims of Stigmatization and Labeling related to the Condition of Their Parents as referred to in Article 38 paragraph (2) letter o, shall be carried out through counseling, social rehabilitation, and social assistance.

#### Article 53

Further provisions regarding Special Protection for Children as referred to in Article 38 through Article 52 shall be regulated by Regent Regulation.

NUMBER 02 YEAR 2024 CONCERNING IMPLEMENTATION OF PROTECTION FOR WOMEN AND CHILDREN FROM ACTS OF VIOLENCE

##### I. GENERAL

Acts of violence against women and children are experiencing an increasing trend. Triggering factors such as economic, social, cultural and social environment are becoming increasingly complex along with the influence of technological developments. Concrete steps are needed to provide protection by all elements of Citizens as a unity of society, as well as the role of the Regional Government as a protector for its citizens with various programs to prevent acts of violence against women and children. One of the efforts to prevent violence against women is carried out through women's empowerment and optimization of previously possessed potential, so that it is expected to be able to improve and develop themselves to play a role and be protected from potential acts of violence. This condition will strengthen the form of appreciation and respect for women while maintaining their role and nature as a woman to play a role in educating and supporting the welfare and happiness of their family.

Efforts to prevent violence against children are a shared obligation of parents, families, communities and the private sector in a holistic and inseparable manner. Community service is the frontline when there is potential violence against children. The mechanism for preventing violence and protecting child victims of violence involves multiple sectors coordinated by the Regional Government. Such efforts are carried out with the direction of providing social rehabilitation for child victims of violence, reuniting child victims of violence with their families and/or environment, and increasing the empowerment of child victims of violence.

In connection with the above, to realize the provision of protection efforts for women and children victims of violence as well as for legal certainty in the implementation of women's empowerment and protection of child victims of violence in the Regional area, it is necessary to regulate it in a Regional Regulation.
