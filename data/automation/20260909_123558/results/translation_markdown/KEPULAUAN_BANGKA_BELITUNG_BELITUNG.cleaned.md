# CHAPTER I

## GENERAL PROVISIONS

#### Article 1

In this Regional Regulation, what is meant by:
1. Region is Belitung Regency.
2. Regional Government is the Regent as an element of the Regional Government administration who leads the implementation of government affairs that are the authority of the autonomous region.
3. Regent is the Regent of Belitung.
4. Regional Apparatus Organization hereinafter abbreviated as OPD is the Regional Apparatus of Belitung Regency.
5. Regional Apparatus Work Unit, hereinafter abbreviated as UKPD is the Regional Apparatus Work Unit of Belitung Regency.
6. Child is a person who has not yet reached the age of 18 (eighteen) years, including a child still in the womb.
7. Woman is a human being of female sex and a person who is recognized by law as a woman.
8. Violence is any act that results in or may result in misery or suffering whether physical, sexual, economic, psychological to the victim.
9. Victim is a woman and child who experiences misery and/or suffering either directly or indirectly as a result of violence and/or for and on behalf of another party who is their dependent.
10. Protection is all preventive, service and empowerment actions to protect and guarantee the rights of women and children from acts of violence.
11. Prevention is activities carried out to prevent the occurrence of acts of violence against women and children.
12. Service is action taken as soon as possible to the victim when seeing, hearing and knowing that violence against the victim will, is, or has occurred.
13. Empowerment is the strengthening of women victims of violence to be able to work and work independently after they are recovered and provided with health and social rehabilitation services.
14. Companion is a person who has the expertise to accompany victims to conduct counseling, therapy and advocacy for the strengthening and recovery of victims of violence.
15. Accompaniment is activities and actions carried out by companions to women and children.
16. Counseling is the provision of assistance by an expert/trained person in such a way that the psychological understanding and ability of the victim and/or perpetrator increases in solving the problems faced.
17. Integrated Service Center hereinafter abbreviated as PPT is a functional work unit that organizes integrated services for victims of violence.
18. Forum for the Protection of Victims of Violence hereinafter abbreviated as FPKK is a coordination forum for prevention, handling and empowerment of victims of violence against women and children whose implementation is carried out in a networked manner and is formed at the regency, sub-district and village/kelurahan levels.
19. Standard Operating Procedure hereinafter abbreviated as SOP is the standard steps that must be taken in protecting victims starting from complaints/identification, health rehabilitation, social rehabilitation, legal services to repatriation and social reintegration of witnesses and/or victims.
20. Family is the smallest unit in society consisting of husband and wife, or husband and wife and their children, or father and his children, or mother and her children.
21. Household is family members and relatives including grandchildren, nephews/nieces, older siblings, younger siblings, grandfathers, grandmothers, cousins and non-relatives including domestic helpers and drivers who live and eat from 1 (one) kitchen and reside in 1 (one) house.

# CHAPTER II

## SCOPE

#### Article 2

The scope of protection of women and children from acts of violence, is as follows:
a. principles and objectives;
b. forms of violence;
c. rights of women and children victims of violence;
d. obligations and responsibilities;
e. prevention of acts of violence;
f. special protection of children;
g. services and empowerment of victims of acts of violence;
h. institutions;
i. cooperation and partnerships;
j. reporting;
k. guidance and supervision; and
l. financing.

# CHAPTER III

## PRINCIPLES AND OBJECTIVES

#### Article 3

Protection of women and children from violence and discrimination is organized based on the principles of:
a. respect for human rights;
b. justice and gender equality;
c. non-discrimination;
d. victim protection;
e. empowerment; and
f. integration.

#### Article 4

The objectives of protecting women and children from acts of violence and discrimination are:  
a. to prevent acts of violence and discrimination against women and children including trafficking in persons;  
b. to eliminate all forms of violence, discrimination, and exploitation against women and children;  
c. to protect women and children from acts of violence and discrimination in obtaining their constitutional rights;  
d. to protect and provide security for women and children;  
e. to provide services and protection to women and children victims of acts of violence and discrimination, reporters, and witnesses; and  
f. to facilitate and conduct mediation on domestic disputes to realize the integrity of a harmonious and prosperous household.

# CHAPTER IV

## FORMS OF VIOLENCE

#### Article 5

Forms of violence against women and children include:  
a. physical violence;  
b. psychological violence;  
c. sexual violence;  
d. neglect;  
e. coercion/deprivation of liberty;  
f. exploitation;  
g. threats of certain actions; and/or  
h. other violence.

# CHAPTER V

## RIGHTS OF WOMEN AND CHILDREN VICTIMS OF ACTS OF VIOLENCE

#### Article 6

Women and children victims of acts of violence receive the following rights:  
a. the right to have their dignity respected as human beings;  
b. the right to health and psychological recovery from the suffering experienced by victims;  
c. the right to determine their own decisions;  
d. the right to obtain information;  
e. the right to confidentiality;  
f. the right to compensation;  
g. the right to social rehabilitation;  
h. the right to complaint handling;  
i. the right of victims and their families to obtain facilities in the judicial process; and  
j. the right to assistance.

#### Article 7

Child victims of acts of violence, in addition to receiving the rights as referred to in Article 6, also receive the following special rights:  
a. the right to respect and full use for survival, growth, and development;  
b. the right to basic services;  
c. the right to equal protection;  
d. the right to be free from various stigma; and  
e. the right to obtain freedom.

# CHAPTER VI

## OBLIGATIONS AND RESPONSIBILITIES

#### Article 8

The obligations and responsibilities in providing protection to women and children from acts of violence are the joint responsibility of:  
a. regional government;  
b. society;  
c. family; and  
d. parents.

#### Article 9

1. The obligations and responsibilities of the Regional Government as referred to in Article 8 letter a, include:  
a. implementing policies for the protection of women and children from acts of violence established by the government;  
b. establishing policies, programs, and activities for the protection of women and children from acts of violence;  
c. conducting cooperation in the implementation of protection of women and children from acts of violence;  
d. providing support for facilities and infrastructure for the implementation of protection of women and children from acts of violence;  
e. allocating budget for the implementation of protection of women and children from acts of violence according to regional financial capacity; and  
f. fostering and supervising the implementation of protection of women and children from acts of violence.  
2. In order to carry out the obligations and responsibilities as referred to in paragraph (1), the Regent establishes programs and action activities for the protection of women and children in one Regional Action Plan as a basis for OPD and  
3. The Regional Action Plan as referred to in paragraph (2), is set forth in the strategic plan of OPD.  
4. Further provisions regarding the Regional Action Plan as referred to in paragraph (2), are regulated by Regent Regulation.

#### Article 10

1. The obligations and responsibilities of society as referred to in Article 8 letter b, are implemented in the form of community participation.  
2. Forms of community participation as referred to in paragraph (1), include:  
a. preventing the occurrence of acts of violence against women and children;  
b. providing information and/or reporting acts of violence against women and children to law enforcement or authorized parties; and  
c. participating in handling victims of acts of violence. (3) Forms of community participation as referred to in paragraph (2) are implemented responsibly in accordance with the provisions of laws and regulations.

#### Article 11

The obligations of family and/or parents as referred to in Article 8 letter c and letter d, who legally have full responsibility to prevent all forms of violence and protect women and children as family members.

#### Article 12

Every person who hears, sees, or knows of the occurrence of violence against women and children is obliged to make efforts according to their capacity to:  
a. prevent and stop the continuation of acts of violence;  
b. provide protection to victims;  
c. provide emergency assistance; and/or  
d. assist in the process of submitting an application for protection determination.

# CHAPTER VII

## PREVENTION OF ACTS OF VIOLENCE

#### Article 13

1. To prevent the occurrence of acts of violence against women and children, the Regional Government conducts empowerment and awareness to families, parents and the community by providing information, guidance, and/or counseling.  
2. In addition to empowerment and awareness as referred to in paragraph (1), the Regional Government makes efforts as follows:  
a. increasing the number and quality of educational units both formal and non-formal;  
b. opening accessibility to obtain education, training, funding, income enhancement, and social services;  
c. opening employment opportunities for women;  
d. building community participation and concern for the prevention of protection of women and children from acts of violence;  
e. building and providing a complete and easily accessible information system;  
f. building networks and cooperation with law enforcement apparatus, government apparatus, universities, and various non-governmental organizations that work and/or care about women and children; and  
g. opening complaint posts for the protection of women and children from acts of violence.

# CHAPTER VIII

## SPECIAL PROTECTION OF CHILDREN

#### Article 15

1. Special protection of children is provided to:  
a. children in emergency situations;  
b. children in conflict with the law;  
c. children from minority and isolated groups;  
d. children who are exploited economically and/or sexually;  
e. children who are victims of narcotics, alcohol, psychotropic substances, and other addictive substances abuse;

# CHAPTER IX

## SERVICES AND EMPOWERMENT OF VICTIMS OF VIOLENCE

## Part One

Service Principles

#### Article 16

The provision of services to victims is implemented with the principles of:  
a. fast;  
b. safe and comfortable;  
c. empathy;  
d. non-discrimination;  
e. easily accessible;  
f. free of charge; and  
g. guaranteed confidentiality.

## Part Two

Forms of Services

a. complaint services;  
b. health services;  
c. legal aid;  
d. social rehabilitation and reintegration;  
e. repatriation;  
f. identification services; and  
g. psychological services.  
2. Services as referred to in paragraph (1) must be supported by facilities in the form of:  
a. special service rooms in police lines;  
b. expert and professional personnel;  
c. service centers and safe houses; and/or  
d. other facilities and infrastructure needed for victim recovery.

a. social affairs;  
b. health;  
c. women's empowerment and child protection; and  
d. mental and spiritual.  
4. In carrying out the duties and functions as referred to in paragraph (1), the Regional Government cooperates with government agencies, other regional governments, non-governmental organizations, and social institutions.  
5. Further provisions regarding the procedures for handling services for women and children from violence as referred to in paragraph (1), paragraph (2) and paragraph (3), shall be regulated by Regent Regulation.

#### Article 18

The Regional Government is obliged to provide complaint services for the protection of women and children from violence.

#### Article 19

The Regional Government and the community or social service institutions may establish recovery houses or safe houses.

#### Article 20

Further provisions regarding Complaint Services and Social Services as referred to in Article 17 and Article 18, shall be regulated by Regent Regulation.

# ELUCIDATION OF REGIONAL REGULATION OF BELITUNG REGENCY

NUMBER 2 OF 2018 CONCERNING PROTECTION OF THE RIGHTS OF WOMEN AND CHILDREN FROM ACTS OF VIOLENCE

##### I. GENERAL

Women and children are a serious concern, considering their vulnerable position as victims of violence both from family and their environment. Various acts of violence against women and children both within the household and outside the household will destroy the life of families, communities, government, nation, and State, therefore the Government of Belitung Regency is obliged to provide protection to women and children from acts of violence which is an implementation of several laws and regulations, including Law Number 23 of 2004 concerning the Elimination of Domestic Violence, Law Number 21 of 2007 concerning the Eradication of the Crime of Trafficking in Persons, Law Number 23 of 2002 concerning Child Protection as amended by Law Number 35 of 2014 concerning Amendments to Law Number 23 of 2002 concerning Child Protection.  

Protection for women and children from acts of violence is one of the obligations of the Regional Government as regulated in Law Number 23 of 2014 concerning Regional Government. On that basis, the Government of Belitung Regency has made various efforts to protect the rights of women and children from acts of violence, but has not been able to provide protection to women and children from various acts of violence, among others due to the still low understanding of the community regarding the rights of women and children, handling that has not been well coordinated, implementation that has not been sustainable, and so forth.

The Regional Regulation concerning the Protection of the Rights of Women and Children from Acts of Violence, gives responsibility to the Regional Government from the prevention of acts of violence to the handling of victims of acts of violence. In its implementation, the Regional Government cooperates with government agencies, other regional governments and the community. In addition, adequate funding support both from the Government, Regional Government and the participation of the business sector and the community, it is hoped that violence against women and children can be eliminated. Through this Regional Regulation it is hoped that the rights of children and women and their protection can be better implemented, so that it can be implemented more comprehensively, integrated and continuously by government elements, families and the community as well as other institutions related to child and women protection efforts.

#### Article 3

Letter a  
The principle of humanity becomes the foundation of the concept of protection of women and children from acts of violence, which is respect for human rights and the dignity of every citizen and resident of Indonesia proportionally (the second principle of Pancasila).  

Letter b  
The principle of justice and gender equality, that gender justice is a process to be fair to men and women. Letter c  
The principle of non-discrimination that in providing protection to women and children from acts of violence does not discriminate on the basis of age, sex, race, ethnicity, tribe, religion and between groups.  

Letter d  
Principle of Victim Protection The principle of protection is a principle that functions to provide protection to women and children in order to provide peace in family and community life.  

Letter e  
Principle of Empowerment The principle of the best interests of women and children, that all actions concerning women and children carried out by families, communities and Regional Governments to fulfill the rights of women and children as regulated in laws and regulations.

Principle of Integration

#### Article 4

Letter a  
What is meant by trafficking in persons is the act of recruitment, transportation, harboring, sending, transfer or receipt of a person by threat of violence, use of violence, kidnapping, confinement, falsification of documents or identity, fraud, abuse of power or vulnerable position, debt bondage or giving payment or benefits, so as to obtain consent from the person holding control or the other person, whether carried out within the country or between countries, for the purpose of exploitation or resulting in the person being exploited.  

Letter b  
What is meant by exploitation is an act with or without the consent of the victim which includes prostitution, forced labor or service, slavery or practices similar to slavery, oppression, extortion, utilization of physical sexual reproductive organs or unlawfully transferring/utilizing labor or transplanting organs and or body tissues or utilizing the labor or ability of a person by another party to obtain benefits both material and immaterial.  

Letter c  
Sufficiently clear.  

Letter d  
Sufficiently clear.  

Letter e  
What is meant by witness is a person who sees, hears and experiences events of violence and human rights violations.  

Letter f  
Mediation is carried out for cases of domestic violence that fall into the category of complaint offenses as regulated in Law Number 23 of 2004 concerning the Elimination of Domestic Violence.

#### Article 5

Letter a  
Physical violence is caused by acts that result in pain, injury, wounds or disability to a person's body, miscarriage, fainting and/or causing death.  

Letter b  
Psychological violence is caused by acts that result in fear, loss of self-confidence, loss of ability to act, feelings of helplessness and/or severe psychological suffering to a person.  

Letter c  
Sexual violence is caused by:  
1. acts in the form of sexual harassment;  
2. forced sexual intercourse;  
3. forced sexual intercourse in an unnatural or unwanted manner; and/or
4. forced sexual intercourse with others for commercial purposes and/or certain purposes.  

Letter d  
Neglect is caused by:  
1. acts that result in the unfulfillment of a child's needs properly, whether physical, mental, spiritual or social carried out by parents, guardians, or any other party responsible for their care;  
2. acts of deliberately neglecting to maintain, care for, or take care of a child as they should be carried out by parents, guardians, or other parties responsible for their care;  
3. acts of neglecting a person within their household, whereas according to the law applicable to them or because of an agreement or contract they must provide livelihood, care, or maintenance to that person; and/or
4. acts that result in economic dependence by limiting and/or prohibiting decent work inside or outside the home so that the victim is under the control of that person.  

Letter e  
Coercion/Deprivation of Freedom is a situation where a person/victim is ordered to do something in such a way that the person does something contrary to their own will and restricts their freedom as a human being who has the right to determine choices.  

Letter f  
Exploitation is caused by:  
1. acts that exploit economically or sexually with the intention of benefiting oneself or others;  
2. acts with or without the consent of the victim including prostitution, forced labor or service, slavery or similar practices, oppression, extortion, utilization of physical, sexual, reproductive organs, or unlawfully transferring or transplanting organs and/or body tissues or utilizing the labor or ability of a person by another party to obtain material or immaterial benefits; and/or
3. all forms of utilization of sexual organs or other body organs of the victim to obtain benefits, including but not limited to all activities of prostitution or indecency.  

Letter g  
Threat of certain actions is a form of intimidation to do or not do what is requested by the perpetrator with consequences that are not good for the victim.  

Letter h  
Other violence is caused among others by any unlawful act in the form of speech, writing, images, symbols, or body movements, whether with or without using means that cause fear.
