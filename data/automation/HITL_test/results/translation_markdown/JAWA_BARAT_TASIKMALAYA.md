# BY THE GRACE OF ALMIGHTY GOD

# REGENT OF TASIKMALAYA,

Considering:  
a. that every citizen has equal rights to live a dignified life in accordance with the principles of humanity, equality and justice;  
b. that women and children are groups of society vulnerable to acts of injustice and inequality as well as acts of violence that can harm their rights and dignity as human beings;  
c. that the rampant cases of violence against women and children in Tasikmalaya Regency require handling and concrete action from the Regional Government to eliminate all forms of violence against women and children;  
d. that based on the considerations as referred to in letter a, letter b, and letter c, it is necessary to establish a Regional Regulation on Women Empowerment and Child Protection;

# CHAPTER I

## GENERAL PROVISIONS

#### Article 1

In this Regional Regulation, what is meant by:

1. Regent is the Regent of Tasikmalaya.
2. Regency Area is the Regency Area of Tasikmalaya.
3. Regency Regional Government is the Regent as an element of regional government administration who leads the implementation of government affairs that are the authority of the autonomous region.
4. Regional House of Representatives hereinafter abbreviated as DPRD is a regional people's representative institution positioned as an element of regional government administration.
5. Regency Regional Apparatus is an auxiliary element of the Regent and Regency DPRD in the administration of government affairs that are the authority of the Regency area.
6. Women's Empowerment is every effort to improve physical, mental, spiritual, social capacity, knowledge, and skills so that women are ready to be utilized according to their respective abilities.
7. Protection is a series of fulfillment of the rights of women and children victims of violence and discrimination starting from prevention efforts, services and social rehabilitation.
8. Child is a person who has not reached the age of 18 (eighteen) years, including a child still in the womb.
9. Women's Protection is all efforts aimed at protecting women and providing a sense of security in the fulfillment of their rights by providing consistent and systematic attention aimed at achieving gender equality.
10. Child Protection is all activities to guarantee and protect children and their rights so that they can live, grow, develop and participate optimally in accordance with human dignity and receive protection from violence and discrimination.
11. Violence is any act that results in misery or suffering physically, psychologically, sexually, and/or neglect, including threats to commit acts, coercion, or unlawful deprivation of liberty.
12. Violence Against Women is any action based on gender differences that results in or may result in misery or suffering of women physically, sexually or psychologically, including threats of certain actions, coercion or arbitrary deprivation of liberty, whether occurring in the public sphere or in private life.
13. Violence Against Children is any act against a child that results in misery or suffering physically, mentally, sexually, psychologically, including neglect and maltreatment that threatens bodily integrity and degrades the dignity of the child.
14. Special Protection is a form of protection received by Children in certain situations and conditions to obtain a guarantee of security against threats that endanger themselves and their lives in their growth and development.
15. Domestic Violence hereinafter referred to as KDRT is any act against a person, especially women, that results in misery or suffering physically, sexually, psychologically, and/or household neglect including threats to commit acts of coercion, or unlawful deprivation of liberty within the scope of the household.
16. Parents are biological father and/or mother, or stepfather and/or stepmother, or adoptive father and/or adoptive mother.
17. Guardian is a person or body who in fact exercises custody as a Parent over a Child.
18. Family is the smallest unit in society consisting of husband and wife, or husband-wife and their children, or father and his children, or mother and her children, or blood relatives in a straight line upward or downward up to the third degree.
19. Community is individuals, families, groups, and/or community organizations.
20. Non-Governmental Organization, hereinafter referred to as LSM is an organization/institution formed by citizens of the Republic of Indonesia voluntarily of their own will and interested in and engaged in the field of social welfare business established by the organization/institution as a manifestation of community participation in efforts to improve community welfare that emphasizes voluntary service.
21. Victim is a woman and child who experiences misery and or suffering either directly or indirectly as a result of violence occurring in the Tasikmalaya Regency area.
22. Prevention is an effort to develop the capacity and mechanism of the Regional Government and community in creating conditions that can prevent the occurrence of violence, maltreatment, KDRT, exploitation and/or neglect.
23. Handling is action that includes identification, rescue, rehabilitation and reintegration of women and children who are victims of violence, maltreatment, KDRT, exploitation, maltreatment or neglect.
24. Repatriation is an effort to return women and child victims of violence from abroad to the debarkation point/entry point, or from the receiving area to the area of origin.
25. Service is action taken as soon as possible to victims when seeing, hearing and knowing that violence against victims will, is or has occurred.
26. Assistance is an effort made by a person or representative of an institution that has expertise in assisting victims to conduct counseling, therapy and advocacy for the strengthening and recovery of victims of violence.
27. Rehabilitation is recovery from disturbances to physical, psychological, and social conditions in order to be able to carry out their role again properly both in the family and in society.
28. Social Rehabilitation is a service aimed at restoring and developing the ability of a person experiencing social dysfunction to be able to carry out social functions properly.
29. Social Reintegration is an effort to reunite victims with family, substitute family, or community that can provide protection and fulfillment of needs for victims.
30. Regional Technical Implementation Unit for Women and Child Protection hereinafter abbreviated as UPTD PPPA is a technical implementer to carry out operational technical activities in its working area in providing services for women and children experiencing problems of violence, discrimination, special protection and other problems;
31. Legal Aid is legal services provided by legal assistants and advocates to conduct the process of assisting witnesses and/or victims of violence against women and children that is gender sensitive.
32. Integrated Service Center for Women's Empowerment and Child Protection, hereinafter abbreviated as P2TP2A, is an integrated service center in efforts to empower women in various fields of development, as well as protection of women and children from various types of discrimination and acts of violence, including trafficking in persons, established by the Regional Government or community-based, which includes: referral centers, business consultation centers, reproductive health consultation centers, legal consultation centers, Integrated Crisis Centers (PKT), Integrated Service Centers (PPT), trauma recovery centers (trauma centers), women's crisis handling centers (women crisis centers), training centers, Science and Technology Information Centers (PIPTEK), safe houses (shelters), transit houses, or other forms.
33. Women and Child Protection Task Force is a functional Work Unit that organizes protection services and handling of cases of violence against children and women in an integrated manner at the Sub-district level.
34. Women and Child Protection Task Unit is a functional Work Unit that organizes protection services and handling of cases of violence against children and women in an integrated manner at the Village level.
35. Child-Friendly Regency is a Regency that has a child rights-based development system through the integration of government, community and business world commitments and resources that are planned comprehensively and sustainably in policies, programs and activities to ensure the fulfillment of children's rights.
36. Child-Friendly Regency Task Force is a coordinative institution at the regency level that coordinates policy, program and activity efforts to realize a child-friendly regency.
37. Regional Action Plan for Child-Friendly Regency Development is a document containing policies, programs and activities to realize a child-friendly regency.

# CHAPTER II

## EMPOWERMENT OF WOMEN

#### Article 2

1. The Regional Government is obliged and responsible for the implementation of women's empowerment.  
2. Women's empowerment efforts as referred to in paragraph (1) include:  
a. determination of policies, budgets, programs, and activities;  
b. determination of implementation guidelines;  
c. provision of services; and  
d. coordination of policies, programs, and activities.

#### Article 3

Women's empowerment is directed at obtaining opportunities and rights as human beings so as to be able to play a role and participate in the fields of:  
a. economy;  
b. socio-culture;  
c. politics; and  
d. law.

#### Article 4

The implementation of women's empowerment in the economic field as referred to in Article 3 letter a is carried out through:

a. provision of skills and job training;  
b. facilitation of the formation, strengthening, and development of productive economic business groups;  
c. facilitation and capital assistance;  
d. facilitation of marketing network development; and  
e. Economic Empowerment of Women Heads of Households (PEKKA).

#### Article 5

Women's empowerment in the socio-cultural field as referred to in Article 3 letter b is carried out through:

a. improvement of knowledge, attitudes, and skills to encourage fulfillment of the right to education in stages according to potential to improve the social status of women;  
b. improvement of awareness, willingness, and ability to overcome health problems through promotional, preventive, curative, and rehabilitative efforts that are affordable and of quality, especially in the field of reproductive health;  
c. improvement of awareness and knowledge about independent family planning, healthy and prosperous, including access to consultation services and marriage registration; and  
d. facilitation and efforts to preserve customs and the development of science, technology, arts, and culture for the advancement of women.

#### Article 6

The implementation of women's empowerment in the political field as referred to in Article 3 letter c includes:

a. political education for women;  
b. involvement of women in decision-making at various levels of government;  
c. provision of opportunities for women to hold public office;  
d. participation in general elections; and  
e. self-development through organizations to associate, assemble, and express opinions.

#### Article 7

The implementation of women's empowerment in the legal field as referred to in Article 3 letter d includes:

a. improvement of knowledge and awareness in the legal field through communication, information, and education services; and  
b. facilitation of access and consultation services as well as free legal aid.

# CHAPTER III

## PROTECTION OF WOMEN

#### Article 8

1. Women have the right to obtain protection from acts of domestic violence, protection of women's rights in employment, protection of women in emergency situations and special conditions, as well as protection of women's rights from criminal acts of trafficking in persons.  
2. Protection of women's rights from acts of domestic violence is in the form of prevention of physical and psychological violence, sexual violence and neglect, and the handling, services, and empowerment of victims of domestic violence.  
3. Protection of women's rights in employment rights inside or outside the country obtains protection in the prevention and handling of cases of neglect of women's rights in terms of employment.  
4. Protection of women's rights in emergency conditions and special conditions includes protection of women in emergency situations, in conflict areas, disaster areas, protection of the rights of women with disabilities, and protection of the rights of elderly women with social problems.  
5. Protection of women's rights from trafficking in persons includes prevention and handling, services, and empowerment of victims of criminal acts of trafficking in persons.

#### Article 9

1. The Regional Government is obliged to provide protection to women so that they can realize and enjoy a decent standard of living.  
2. Provisions regarding Protection as referred to in paragraph (1) are regulated by a Regent Regulation.

# CHAPTER IV

## PROTECTION OF CHILDREN

## Part One

Protection of Children in General

#### Article 10

Protection of Children includes the fields of:

a. Protection of Children in the field of Religion and Belief;  
b. Protection of Children in the field of Civil and Freedom;  
c. Protection of Children in the field of Health;  
d. Protection of Children in the field of Education;  
e. Protection of Children in the field of Social; and  
f. Protection of Children in the field of Special Protection.

## Part Two

Protection of Children in the field of Religion and Belief

#### Article 11

1. Every child receives protection to worship according to their respective religion and belief.  
2. Before the child can determine their choice, the religion adopted by the child follows the religion of their parents.  
3. In the case of child adoption, prospective adoptive parents must have the same religion as the religion adopted by the prospective adopted child.  
4. If the origin of the child as referred to in paragraph (3) is not known, then the child's religion is adjusted to the religion of the majority of the local population.

## Part Three

Protection of Children in the Field of Civil and Freedom

#### Article 12

Protection of children in the Civil field as referred to in Article 10 letter b includes:

a. guarantee that every child has the right to obtain a name and self-identity from birth in the form of a birth certificate;  
b. making birth certificates is the responsibility of the Government;  
c. making birth certificates is not charged and is borne by the Regional Government; and  
d. provisions for making birth certificates are further regulated in a Regent Regulation.

#### Article 13

1. Protection of children in the civil and freedom field helps children so that children can:  
a. participate in all fields;  
b. freely express opinions and think according to conscience and their religion in accordance with the norms prevailing in the region;  
c. freely receive oral or written information in accordance with the age stage and development of the child;  
d. freely associate and assemble in accordance with noble norms and ethics;  
e. freely rest, play, recreate, and create arts and culture without neglecting obligations as a child; and  
f. obtain play facilities that meet health and safety requirements.  
2. Efforts as referred to in paragraph (1) are developed and adjusted to age, level of ability of the child, and their environment so as not to hinder and disturb the development of the child.

## Part Four

Protection of Children in the Field of Health

#### Article 14

Protection of children in the field of Health as referred to in Article 10 letter c includes:

a. providing facilities and organizing comprehensive health efforts for children, so that every child obtains an optimal degree of health from the womb carried out by the Regional Government;  
b. organizing comprehensive and free health efforts for all children including those from poor families;  
c. providing health insurance for child victims of violence, exploitation, neglect, and abuse;  
d. being responsible for maintaining health and caring for children from the womb carried out by family and parents; and  
e. ensuring that children born are protected from diseases that threaten survival and/or cause disability carried out by the Regional Government, community, family, and parents.

## Part Five

Protection of Children in the Field of Education

#### Article 15

The implementation of child protection in the field of education as referred to in Article 10 letter d, includes:
a. providing the widest possible opportunity for children to obtain education carried out by the Regional Government, family and parents;
b. every education provider is prohibited from expelling children from an Educational Institution without guarantees for the continuity of the child's education;  
c. implementation of a 12 (twelve) year compulsory education program, regulated in a separate Regional Regulation;  
d. providing equal opportunities and accessibility to obtain regular education and special education for children with disabilities; and
e. protecting their rights to obtain education for child victims of violence, exploitation, neglect and maltreatment.

## Part Six

Child Protection in the Social Field

#### Article 16

The implementation of child protection in the social field as referred to in Article 10 letter e is carried out in the form of:
a. Prevention services, including:
1. Making policies on child protection from all forms of violence, exploitation, neglect and maltreatment in all aspects of children's lives;  
2. Making policies on social welfare guarantees for children who experience violence, exploitation, neglect and maltreatment; and
3. Organizing training to increase capacity for family welfare consultation institutions.
b. Risk Reduction Services, including:
1. Conducting early identification, complaint services and assessment of children and families who are victims of violence, exploitation, neglect and maltreatment;  
2. Providing services for children and families who are victims of violence, exploitation, neglect and maltreatment including:
1. guidance and counseling on parenting patterns and behavior change; 
2) mediation to related parties;  
3. psychosocial services;  
4. economic assistance and life skills;  
5. temporary shelter;  
6. preparation of social reintegration for child victims of violence, exploitation, neglect and maltreatment; and
7. protecting the identity of victims to prevent negative stigma for children.
c. Case Handling Services, including:
1. Conducting immediate rescue efforts for victims in dangerous conditions for their safety;  
2. Providing referrals according to the needs of victims;  
3. Providing counseling and support from family; 
4) Providing rehabilitation and social reintegration services;  
5. Monitoring the condition of children in alternative care in the long term; and
6. Creating a database of alternative care facilities for children.

## Part Seven

Child Protection in the Field of Special Protection

#### Article 17

Child Protection in the field of Special Protection as referred to in Article 10 letter f, includes:
a. children in emergency situations;  
b. children in conflict with the law (ABH);  
c. children exploited economically and or sexually;  
d. children who are victims of narcotics, alcohol, psychotropic, and other addictive substance abuse (NAPZA);  
e. child victims of abduction, sale and trafficking;  
f. child victims of violence both physical and/or mental, and children with disabilities; and/or
g. child victims of maltreatment and neglect.

### Paragraph 1

Protection of Children in Emergency Situations

#### Article 18

1. Children in emergency situations as referred to in Article 17 letter a, consist of:
a. children who become refugees;  
b. child victims of riots;  
c. child victims of natural disasters; and
d. children in armed conflict situations.  
2. The implementation of child protection in emergency situations as referred to in paragraph (1) is carried out in the form of:
a. Prevention Services, including:
1. Making policies on child protection from all forms of violence, exploitation, neglect and maltreatment in emergency situations;  
2. Making policies on fulfillment of basic needs consisting of food, clothing, housing, education, health, learning and recreation, security guarantees, and equal treatment for children who become refugees, victims of violence, victims of natural disasters and children in armed conflict situations; and
3. Making policies and disaster management mechanisms that take into account the interests of children.
b. Risk Reduction Services, including:
1. Conducting early identification, complaint services and assessment of children and families who are victims of violence, exploitation, neglect and maltreatment.  
2. Providing services for children and families who are victims of violence, exploitation, neglect and maltreatment, which include:
a. legal consultation;  
b. mediation to related parties;  
c. psychosocial services;  
d. economic assistance and life skills;  
e. safe house; and
f. transit house.
c. Case Handling Services, including:
1. Conducting immediate rescue efforts for victims in dangerous conditions for their safety;  
2. Providing referrals according to the needs of victims;  
3. Providing counseling and support from family; 
4) Treating children in emergency situations humanely in accordance with the dignity and rights of children;  
5. Providing a sense of security and protection for children who become refugees, victims of violence, victims of natural disasters and children in armed conflict situations; and
6. Fulfilling special needs for children in emergency situations who experience psychosocial disorders.

### Paragraph 2

Protection for ABH

#### Article 19

1. Special protection for ABH as referred to in Article 17 letter b includes children in conflict with the law and child victims of criminal acts, constituting the obligation and responsibility of the Regional Government and society.  
2. The implementation of special protection for ABH as referred to in paragraph (1) shall be carried out in the form of:  
   a. Prevention Services, including:  
      1. Making policies regarding education guarantees for ABH;  
      2) Providing socialization and raising awareness of parents and/or society, the press, educators, government, law enforcement officials regarding the protection of the identity of ABH to avoid negative stigma towards children;  
      3. Providing socialization and raising awareness of parents/guardians and/or society, the press, educators, Regional Government and law enforcement officials regarding alternative forms of enforcing discipline without physical and psychological punishment;  
      4. Providing socialization to the community regarding understanding restorative justice or justice that restores;  
      5. Organizing training to increase the capacity of the community in conducting advocacy to law enforcement institutions to make distinctions in handling issues related to ABH;  
      6. Organizing training to increase the capacity of the community in conducting advocacy for the juvenile justice process to provide alternative punishments in the form of measures; and  
      7. Creating a complaint mechanism for handling issues related to ABH.  
   b. Risk Reduction Services, including:  
      1. Conducting early identification, complaint services and assessment of children and families at risk;  
      2. Providing services for children and families such as:  
         a. guidance and counseling on parenting patterns and behavior change;  
         b. legal consultation;  
         c. mediation to related parties;  
         d. psychosocial services; and  
         e. protecting the identity of victims.  
   c. Case Handling Services, including:  
      1. Providing counseling and the need for family support;  
      2) Treatment of children in a humane manner in accordance with the dignity and rights of children;  
      3. Providing guarantees of legal assistance/special assistance for children in conflict with the law;  
      4. Providing a sense of security and protection from reporting about the identity of ABH to avoid negative stigma towards children;  
      5) Providing special facilities and infrastructure for ABH;  
      6. Providing accessibility to obtain information regarding case developments;  
      7. Providing rehabilitation and social reintegration services; and  
      8. Conducting continuous monitoring and recording of the development of children in conflict with the law.

### Paragraph 3

Protection of Children Exploited Economically and/or Sexually

#### Article 20

1. Special protection for children who are exploited economically and/or sexually as referred to in Article 17 letter c, constitutes the obligation and responsibility of the Regional Government and society.  
2. The implementation of special protection for children who are exploited economically and or sexually as referred to in paragraph (1) shall be carried out in the form of:  
   a. Prevention Services, including:  
      1. Making policies for the protection of children from all forms of violence, exploitation, and maltreatment in all situations of children's lives;  
      2) Making policies for fulfilling the special needs of children with disabilities and children experiencing psychosocial disorders who are victims of economic and/or sexual exploitation;  
      3. Providing guarantees of protection for child victims of economic and/or sexual exploitation;  
      4. Providing guarantees of protection from all forms of violence, exploitation, neglect and maltreatment for children who are exploited economically and/or sexually;  
      5. Providing socialization and raising awareness of parents and/or society, the press, educators, by the government and law enforcement officials regarding the adverse effects of violence and economic and/or sexual exploitation;  
      6. Providing socialization and raising awareness of parents and/or society, the press, educators, by the government and law enforcement officials regarding the protection of the identity of child victims of economic and/or sexual exploitation; and  
      7. Providing socialization regarding the provisions of laws and regulations related to the protection of children who are exploited economically and/or sexually.  
   b. Risk Reduction Services, which include:  
      1. Conducting early identification, complaint services and assessment of children and families at risk of experiencing economic and/or sexual exploitation;  
      2. Providing services for children and families who are victims of violence, and economic and/or sexual exploitation including:  
         a. legal consultation;  
         b. legal assistance to victims of sexual violence;  
         c. mediation to related parties;  
         d. psychosocial services;  
         e. economic assistance and life skills;  
         f. temporary shelter; and  
         g. protecting the identity of victims.  
   c. Case Handling Services, including:  
      1. Conducting immediate rescue efforts for victims who are in dangerous conditions for their safety;  
      2. Providing referrals according to the needs of victims;  
      3. Providing counseling and support from the family;  
      4) Treating child victims of economic and/or sexual exploitation in a humane manner in accordance with the dignity and rights of children;  
      5) Providing a sense of security and protection for child victims of economic and/or sexual exploitation from reporting; and  
      6) Providing rehabilitation and social reintegration services.

### Paragraph 4

Protection of Children who are Victims of Narcotics, Alcohol, Psychotropic, and Other Addictive Substances (NAPZA) Abuse

#### Article 21

1. Supervision, prevention, treatment, and rehabilitation of children who are victims of narcotics, alcohol, psychotropic, and other addictive substances (NAPZA) abuse as referred to in Article 17 letter d and involved in their production and distribution, shall be the obligation and responsibility of the Regional Government and society.  
2. The implementation of protection for children who are victims of narcotics, alcohol, psychotropic, and other addictive substances (NAPZA) abuse as referred to in paragraph (1) shall be carried out in the form of:  
   a. Prevention Services, including:  
      1. Making policies for the protection of child victims of narcotics, alcohol, psychotropic and other addictive substances (NAPZA) abuse;  
      2. Making special protection policies for children who are victims of narcotics, alcohol, psychotropic, and other addictive substances (NAPZA) abuse; and  
      3. Providing socialization and raising awareness of parents and/or society and children regarding the adverse effects of narcotics, alcohol, psychotropic and other addictive substances (NAPZA) abuse.  
   b. Risk Reduction Services, which include:  
      1. Conducting early identification, complaint services and assessment of children and families at risk;  
      2. Providing services for children and families who are victims of narcotics, alcohol, psychotropic, and other addictive substances (NAPZA) abuse including:  
         a. guidance and counseling on parenting patterns and behavior change;  
         b. legal consultation; and  
         c. psychosocial services.  
   c. Case Handling Services, including:  
      1. Conducting immediate rescue efforts for victims who are in dangerous conditions for their safety;  
      2. Providing referrals according to the needs of victims of narcotics, alcohol, psychotropic and other addictive substances (NAPZA) abuse;  
      3. Conducting counseling and support from the family;  
      4. Treating child victims of narcotics, alcohol, psychotropic, and other addictive substances (NAPZA) abuse in a humane manner in accordance with the dignity and rights of children; and  
      5. Providing rehabilitation and social reintegration services.

### Paragraph 5

Protection of Children Victims of Kidnapping, Sale and Trafficking

#### Article 22

1. Special Protection for child victims of abduction, sale and trafficking as referred to in Article 17 letter e shall be carried out through supervision, protection, prevention, care and rehabilitation efforts by the Regional Government and community.  
2. The implementation of special protection for children who are victims of abduction, sale and trafficking as referred to in paragraph (1) shall be carried out in the form of:
   a. Prevention Services, including:
      1. Providing protection guarantees for child victims of abduction, sale and trafficking; and
      2. Providing complaint mechanisms and case handling.
   b. Risk Reduction Services, including:
      1. Conducting early identification, complaint services and assessment of children and families at risk; and
      2. Providing services for children and victims of abduction, sale and trafficking which include:
         a. guidance and counseling on parenting patterns and behavior change;  
         b. legal consultation;  
         c. mediation to related parties;  
         d. psychosocial services;  
         e. economic assistance and life skills;  
         f. temporary shelter; and
         g. protecting the identity of victims.
   c. Case Handling Services, including:
      1. Conducting immediate rescue efforts for victims in conditions dangerous to their safety;  
      2. Providing referrals according to the needs of victims;  
      3. Conducting counseling and providing support from family;  
      4. Treating child victims of abduction, sale and trafficking in a humane manner in accordance with the dignity and rights of children;  
      5. Providing accessibility to obtain information regarding case developments;  
      6. Providing rehabilitation and social reintegration services; and
      7. Creating a database of child victims of abduction, sale and trafficking.

### Paragraph 6

Protection of Child Victims of Physical and/or Mental Violence and Children with Disabilities

#### Article 23

1. Special Protection for child victims of physical and/or mental violence and children with disabilities as referred to in Article 17 letter f shall be carried out through efforts to:
   a. disseminate and socialize the provisions of laws and regulations that protect children from becoming victims of violence;  
   b. obtain equal treatment with other children to achieve full social integration as much as possible and individual development; and
   c. fulfill special needs.
2. The implementation of special protection for children who are victims of physical and/or mental violence and children with Disabilities as referred to in paragraph (1) shall be carried out in the form of:
   a. Prevention Services, which include:
      1. making policies for fulfilling special needs for children with disabilities and children experiencing psychosocial disorders;  
      2. providing education guarantees for child victims of physical and/or mental violence and children with disabilities;  
      3. providing protection guarantees from all forms of violence, exploitation, neglect and mistreatment for children with disabilities;  
      4. providing socialization and raising awareness of parents and/or the community, press, educators, by the government and law enforcement officials about the adverse effects of violence;  
      5. providing socialization and raising awareness of parents and/or the community, press, educators, by the government about protecting the identity of children who are victims of physical and/or mental violence and children with disabilities from bad stigma; and
      6. establishing complaint mechanisms for case handling.
   b. Risk Reduction Services, including:
      1. conducting early identification, complaint services and assessment of children and families at risk;  
      2. providing services for children and families who are victims of violence which include:
         a. guidance and counseling on parenting patterns and behavior change;  
         b. legal consultation;  
         c. mediation to related parties;  
         d. psychosocial services;  
         e. economic assistance and life skills;  
         f. temporary shelter; and
         g. protecting the identity of victims.
   c. Case Handling Services, which include:
      1. conducting immediate rescue efforts for victims in conditions dangerous to their safety;  
      2. providing referrals according to the needs of victims;  
      3. conducting counseling and support from family;  
      4. treatment of children in a humane manner in accordance with the dignity and rights of children;  
      5. providing a sense of security and protection from reporting to avoid bad stigma;  
      6. providing special facilities and infrastructure for children with disabilities;  
      7. providing rehabilitation and social reintegration services; and
      8. creating a database of child victims of physical and/or mental violence and children with disabilities.

### Paragraph 7

   a. Prevention Services, including:

   b. Risk Reduction Services, including:

         a. guidance and counseling on parenting patterns and behavior change;  
         b. legal consultation;

# CHAPTER V

## FORMS OF VIOLENCE AGAINST WOMEN AND CHILDREN

#### Article 25

Forms of violence against women and children, include:

a. physical violence;  
b. psychological violence;  
c. sexual violence;  
d. neglect;  
e. exploitation; and/or
f. other violence.

#### Article 26

1. Physical violence as referred to in Article 25 letter a constitutes an act that results in pain accompanied by injury, wounds or disability to the body, miscarriage, fainting and/or causes death of women and/or children.
2. Psychological violence as referred to in Article 25 letter b constitutes an act that results in fear, loss of self-confidence, loss of ability to act, feelings of helplessness and/or psychological suffering of women and/or children.
3. Sexual violence as referred to in Article 25 letter c, includes:
   a. acts in the form of sexual harassment, consisting of:
      1. physical harassment;
      2. verbal harassment;
      3. gestural harassment;
      4. written or pictorial harassment; and
      5. psychological/emotional harassment.
   b. forced sexual intercourse;
   c. forced sexual intercourse in an unnatural or unwanted manner; and/or
   d. forced sexual intercourse with others for commercial purposes and/or certain purposes.
4. Neglect as referred to in Article 25 letter d, includes:
   a. acts that result in the failure to fulfill the needs of children properly, whether physical, mental, spiritual or social, committed by parents, guardians, or any other party responsible for their care;
   b. acts of deliberately neglecting to maintain, care for, or look after children as appropriate, committed by parents, guardians, or any other party responsible for their care; and/or
   c. acts of deliberately neglecting women within the scope of their household, whereas according to the law applicable to them or due to an agreement or contract they are obliged to provide livelihood, care, or maintenance to such women.
5. Exploitation as referred to in Article 25 letter e, includes:
   a. acts of economic or sexual exploitation with the intention of benefiting oneself or others;
   b. acts with or without the consent of the victim which include but are not limited to prostitution, forced labor or services, slavery or similar practices, oppression, extortion, exploitation of physical, sexual, reproductive organs, or unlawfully removing or transplanting organs and/or body tissues or exploiting the labor or ability of a person by another party to obtain benefits both material and immaterial; and
   c. all forms of exploitation of sexual organs or other body organs of the victim to obtain benefits, but not limited to prostitution or indecency activities.
6. Other violence as referred to in Article 25 letter f constitutes threats of violence and coercion.
7. Threats of violence as referred to in paragraph (6) include any unlawful act in the form of speech, writing, images, symbols, or body movements, whether with or without using means that cause fear or restrict a person's fundamental freedom.
8. Coercion as referred to in paragraph (6) includes a situation where a person or victim is ordered to do something in such a way that the person does something contrary to their own will.

# CHAPTER VI

## RIGHTS OF VICTIMS OF VIOLENCE

#### Article 27

Women and children victims of violence receive the following rights:

a. the right to have their dignity and worth as human beings respected;
b. the right to health and psychological recovery from the suffering experienced by the victim;
c. the right to determine their own decisions;
d. the right to obtain information;
e. the right to confidentiality;
f. the right to compensation;
g. the right to social rehabilitation;
h. the right to complaint handling;
i. the right to assistance and legal aid;
j. the right to obtain visum et repertum free of charge; and
k. the right of victims and their families to obtain facilities in the judicial process.

#### Article 28

Child victims of violence, in addition to receiving the rights as referred to in Article 27, are granted special rights, as follows:

a. the right to respect and full enjoyment for survival, growth and development;
b. the right to basic services;
c. the right to equal protection;
d. the right to be free from various stigma;
e. the right to obtain freedom; and
f. the right to be tried based on the principle of restorative justice.

# CHAPTER VII

## OBLIGATIONS AND RESPONSIBILITIES

## Part One

General

#### Article 29

Obligations and responsibilities in the implementation of protection for women and children are the shared responsibility of:

a. Regional Government;
b. village government;
c. Community; and
d. parents, Guardians and/or Family.

## Part Two

Regional Government

#### Article 30

1. The Regional Government as referred to in Article 29 letter a, is obliged and responsible to:
   a. respect, protect and fulfill the rights of victims and the special rights of child victims of violence;
   b. formulate and implement programs and activities related to the protection of women and children;
   c. implement the protection of women and children from violence;
   d. supervise services to victims of violence based on minimum service standards in accordance with the provisions of laws and regulations;
   e. allocate budget for the protection of women and children from violence according to regional financial capacity and budgeted in each fiscal year; and
   f. realize a Child-Friendly Regency.
2. In order to formulate and implement effective women and children protection programs as referred to in paragraph (1) letter b, the Regional Government organizes:
   a. a disaggregated gender and child data system; and
   b. gender-responsive and child-friendly planning and budgeting.
3. The Regional Government may establish a Regional Technical Implementation Unit for Women's Empowerment and Child Protection to reach services in the Region.

## Part Three

Village Government

#### Article 31

1. The village government as referred to in Article 29 letter b, is obliged and responsible to:
   a. formulate and implement programs and activities related to the protection of women and children;
   b. implement the protection of women and children from violence;
   c. realize a Child-Friendly village; and
   d. allocate budget for the protection of women and children from violence according to village financial capacity in each fiscal year.
2. In order to carry out the obligations and responsibilities as referred to in paragraph (1), the village government formulates programs and activities for the protection of women and children in the village action plan which is part of the village medium-term development plan.

## Part Four

Community

#### Article 32

1. The community as referred to in Article 29 letter c is obliged and responsible for the protection of women and children through community participation activities in the protection of women and children.
2. Forms of community participation as referred to in paragraph (1), include:
   a. preventing the occurrence of acts of violence against women and children;
   b. providing information and/or reporting acts of violence against women and children to law enforcement or authorized parties; and
   c. participating in the handling of victims of acts of violence.

## Part Five

Parents, Guardians and/or Family

#### Article 33

1. Parents, guardians and/or family as referred to in Article 29 letter d legally have full responsibility to prevent all forms of violence and to report if violence occurs and to protect victims.
2. In the event that parents, guardians and/or family as referred to in paragraph (1) do not exist or their whereabouts are unknown or due to a certain reason cannot carry out their obligations and responsibilities, then the obligations and responsibilities may be transferred to the Regional Government and other family members which are implemented in accordance with the provisions of laws and regulations.

# CHAPTER VIII

## IMPLEMENTATION OF WOMEN AND CHILDREN PROTECTION

## Part One

General

#### Article 34

Regional Government implements activities for the administration of women's protection and child protection, which include:
a. prevention;  
b. services; and c. social rehabilitation.

## Part Two

Prevention

#### Article 35

1. Prevention as referred to in Article 34 letter a, is implemented by:a. forming a network in efforts to prevent violence; b. conducting coordination, integration, synchronization of violence prevention based on partnership patterns;  
c. forming a violence prevention system;  
d. conducting socialization regarding laws and regulations related to the protection of women and children; and e. providing knowledge about prevention and mechanisms for handling violence against women and children.  
2. Prevention as referred to in paragraph (1) is implemented in an integrated manner by Regional Apparatus of the Regency and related agencies that have principal tasks and functions in the administration of affairs: a. women's empowerment and child protection;  
b. education;  
c. health;  
d. social;  
e. manpower;  
f. youth and sports; and g. mental and spiritual.  
3. In addition to being implemented by Regional Apparatus of the Regency and related agencies as referred to in paragraph (2), prevention is also implemented by: a. family and/or closest relatives;  
b. community, NGOs, community organizations, social organizations and religious organizations; and c. business sector.

## Part Three

Services

#### Article 36

Services as referred to in Article 34 letter b, take into account: a. service principles; and
b. forms of services to victims.

#### Article 37

Service principles as referred to in Article 36 letter a, consist of: a. fast;  
b. safe and comfortable;  
c. sense of empathy;  
d. non-discrimination;  
e. easily accessible;  
f. not charged; and g. guaranteed confidentiality.

#### Article 38

Forms of services to victims as referred to in Article 36 letter b, consist of:
a. services to women and children victims of violence, include: 1. complaint, consultation and counseling services;  
2. assistance services;  
3. health services;  
4. legal aid services; and
5. repatriation and social reintegration services.  b. special services to women victims of domestic violence or trafficking in persons, include:
1. complaint services;  
2. pick-up services;  
3. medical and psychological referral services;  
4. shelter services;  
5. assistance and legal aid services; and 6. repatriation and social reintegration services.

#### Article 39

Complaint, consultation and counseling services as referred to in Article 38 letter a number 1, include:
a. identification or initial recording of victims; and b. informed consent.

#### Article 40

Assistance services as referred to in Article 38 letter a number 2 include:
a. accompanying victims during the examination and health recovery process; b. accompanying victims during the psychological recovery process;  
c. accompanying victims during the medicolegal process;  
d. accompanying victims during the examination process at the police, prosecutor's office and court;  
e. monitoring the interests and rights of victims in the examination process at the police, prosecutor's office and court;  
f. maintaining the privacy and confidentiality of victims from all parties who have no interest, including coverage by mass media;  
g. coordinating with other assistants; and h. providing continuous handling up to the rehabilitation stage.

#### Article 41

Health services as referred to in Article 38 letter a number 3 include:
a. first aid to victims by competent officers; b. treatment and recovery of physical injuries aimed at restoring the physical condition of victims carried out by medical and paramedical personnel; and c. referral to health services according to needs.

#### Article 42

1. Legal aid services as referred to in Article 38 letter a number 4 are to assist victims in undergoing the judicial process. (2) Legal aid services as referred to in paragraph (1) are carried out by:a. providing legal consultation that includes information regarding the rights of victims and the judicial process;  
b. accompanying victims at the investigation, prosecution, and examination levels in court hearings and helping victims to fully explain the violence they experienced; and c. coordinating with fellow law enforcers, volunteer assistants, and social workers so that the judicial process runs as it should.

#### Article 43

1. Repatriation and social reintegration services as referred to in Article 38 letter a number 5 are carried out to return victims to their families and social environment.  
2. Repatriation and social reintegration services as referred to in paragraph (1) are carried out by the Regional Government in coordination with: a. Regency/City Government within one Provincial area or outside the Province; and/or b. related agencies and institutions both Government and non-Government. (3) Forms of services as referred to in Article 38, are implemented in accordance with minimum service standards established by the Government/Regional Government and implemented by Regional Apparatus of the Regency that have tasks and functions in the administration of affairs: a. women's empowerment and child protection;  
b. social;  
c. manpower;  
d. health;  
e. education;  
f. security and order; and g. mental and spiritual.  
4. In carrying out tasks and functions as referred to in paragraph (1) the Regional Government cooperates with the Government, Provincial Government, other Regency/City Governments, and the community.

## Part Four

Social Rehabilitation

#### Article 44

1. Social rehabilitation as referred to in Article 34 letter c is a service provided by assistants to restore the traumatic condition of victims.  
2. Social rehabilitation as referred to in paragraph (1) is carried out by:a. providing guidance and counseling to victims and parents of victims;  
b. psychological recovery of victims;  
c. assistance to victims and parents of victims in the family and community environment; and d. providing safe houses for victims of violence who need continuous handling.  
3. Special social rehabilitation for women victims of domestic violence and trafficking in persons is carried out by:a. providing guidance and counseling;  
b. psychological recovery of victims; and c. assistance to victims in the family and community environment. (4) Social rehabilitation as referred to in paragraph (1) is implemented by Regional Apparatus of the Regency that have principal tasks and functions in the administration of affairs:a. social;  
b. women's empowerment and child protection;  
c. health; and d. mental and spiritual.  
5. In addition to being implemented by Regional Apparatus of the Regency as referred to in paragraph (4), social rehabilitation can also be implemented by the community or social service institutions.

Regional Children's Forum

District-Level Children's Forum

# CHAPTER X

## CHILD-FRIENDLY REGENCY

#### Article 56

1. The Regional Government is obligated to implement a Child-Friendly Regency implementation system in order to fulfill children's rights. (2) The development of a Child-Friendly Regency is carried out based on the following principles as follows:  
   a. good governance, namely transparency, accountability, participation, information disclosure, and rule of law;  
   b. non-discrimination, namely not differentiating ethnicity, race, religion, gender, language, political views, national origin, economic status, physical or psychological condition of the child, or other factors;  
   c. the best interests of the child, namely making what is best for the child the primary consideration in every policy, program, and activity;  
   d. the right to life, survival, and development of the child, namely guaranteeing the right to life, survival and development of the child to the maximum extent possible; and  
   e. respect for the views of the child, namely recognizing and ensuring that every child who has the ability to express their opinion, is given the opportunity to express their views freely on all matters affecting them.
2. The implementation instruments of the Child-Friendly Regency as referred to in paragraph (1) include:  
   a. development and public services system;  
   b. Child-Friendly Health Services;  
   c. Child-Friendly Family;  
   d. Child-Friendly School; and  
   e. Child-Friendly Environment;  
   f. Child-Friendly Islamic Boarding School.
3. The Regional Government in implementing the Child-Friendly Regency as referred to in paragraph (1) must pay attention to the Child-Friendly Regency Indicators which include:  
   a. institutions; and  
   b. clusters of children's rights.
4. Institutional strengthening as referred to in paragraph (3) letter a includes:  
   a. the existence of laws and regulations and policies for the fulfillment of children's rights;  
   b. percentage of budget for the fulfillment of children's rights, including budget for institutional strengthening;  
   c. number of laws and regulations, policies, programs and activities that receive input from Children's Forums and other children's groups;  
   d. availability of trained Human Resources (HR) in the Convention on the Rights of the Child (CRC) and capable of applying children's rights into policies, programs, and activities;  
   e. availability of disaggregated child data by gender, age, and district;  
   f. involvement of community institutions in the fulfillment of children's rights; and  
   g. involvement of the business sector in the fulfillment of children's rights.

#### Article 57

Clusters of children's rights as referred to in Article 56 paragraph (3) letter b which include:

a. Civil rights and freedoms which include:
1. children's right to identity;  
2. right to protection of identity;  
3. right to express and voice opinions;  
4. right to think, have conscience, religion, and belief;  
5) right to organize and assemble peacefully;  
6. right to protection of private life;  
7. right to access appropriate information; and
8. right to be free from torture and other cruel, inhuman, or degrading treatment or punishment.

b. Rights to family environment and alternative care which include:
1. parental guidance and responsibility;  
2. children separated from parents;  
3. reunification;  
4. illicit transfer of children;  
5. welfare support;  
6. children necessarily separated from the family environment;  
7. adoption of children;  
8. periodic review of placement; and
9. violence and neglect.

c. Children's rights to basic health and welfare which include:  
   a. children with disabilities receive access to health services and their welfare;  
   b. health and health services;  
   c. social security health services and facilities; and  
   d. standard of living;

d. Children's rights to education, leisure and cultural activities which include:
1. education;  
2. aims of education; and
3. leisure activities, cultural activities, and sports.

e. Protection for children in special protection which includes:
1) children in emergency situations;  
2. children in conflict with the law;  
3. children in situations of exploitation; and
4. children belonging to minority and isolated groups.

#### Article 58

1. To make the development of the Child-Friendly Regency effective, a Child-Friendly Regency Task Force is established as determined by a Regent's Decree.  
2. Membership of the Child-Friendly Regency Task Force as referred to in paragraph (1) may come from the Regional Government, children's representatives, business sector, religious leaders, community leaders and other parties as needed.  
3. In carrying out their main duties, members of the Child-Friendly Regency Task Force as referred to in paragraph (2) have detailed tasks including:  
   a. coordinating various efforts to develop the Child-Friendly Regency;  
   b. preparing the Regional Action Plan for the Development of the Child-Friendly Regency;  
   c. carrying out socialization, advocacy and communication for the development of the Child-Friendly Regency;  
   d. monitoring the implementation of policies, programs and activities in the Regional Action Plan for the Development of the Child-Friendly Regency;  
   e. conducting evaluations at the end of each year on the implementation of policies, programs and activities in the Regional Action Plan for the Development of the Child-Friendly Regency; and  
   f. submitting reports to the Regent.

#### Article 59

1. The Regional Action Plan as referred to in Article 58 paragraph (3) letter b is prepared for a period of 5 (five) years or according to needs integrated with the regional medium-term development plan, and the work plan of regional apparatus.  
2. The Regional Action Plan has an annual program focus that refers to the stages of achieving the Child-Friendly Regency indicators.  
3. The Regional Action Plan for the Child-Friendly Regency must be based on problems in the field and their comprehensive resolution. (4) The Regional Action Plan is socialized to all Regional Apparatus, child stakeholders, families, and the community.  
4. Further provisions regarding the Regional Action Plan for the Child-Friendly Regency are stipulated by Regent Regulation.

# CHAPTER XI

## MONITORING, EVALUATION, AND REPORTING

#### Article 60

Monitoring is carried out periodically and in an integrated manner every 6 (six) months by the Regional Apparatus that administers governmental affairs in the field of women's empowerment and child protection in coordination with Regional Apparatus or other units handling women's empowerment and child protection.

#### Article 61

Evaluation of the implementation of women's empowerment and child protection shall be conducted by the Regional Apparatus implementing governmental affairs in the field of women's empowerment and child protection at least 2 (two) times in 1 (one) year.

#### Article 62

1. Reporting on the implementation of women's empowerment and child protection shall be conducted by:a. P2TP2A to the Regent;  
   b. Task Force for the Protection of Women and Children at Sub-District Level to the Sub-District Head;  
   c. Task Unit for the Protection of Women and Children at Village Level to the Village Head; dand. Regional Indonesian Child Protection Commission to the Regent through the Head of Regional Apparatus implementing governmental affairs in the field of women's empowerment and child protection.  
2. Reporting as referred to in paragraph (1) shall be submitted in writing, covering:a. administration;  
   b. finance;  
   c. services; dand. performance.  
3. Submission of written reports as referred to in paragraph (2) shall be implemented at least every 6 (six) months.

# CHAPTER XII

## FUNDING

#### Article 63

Funding for women's empowerment and child protection shall be sourced from: a. regional revenue and expenditure budget;  
   b. village revenue and expenditure budget; danc. other lawful sources in accordance with the provisions of laws and regulations.

# CHAPTER XIII

## SANCTIONS

#### Article 64

1. Any person who intentionally violates the provisions of Article 10 or fails to provide protection against forms of violence against women and children as referred to in Article 25 and Article 26, and fails to provide the rights of women and children as referred to in Article 27 and Article 28, thereby causing violence to occur, allowing violence to occur, and/or failing to report and failing to provide protection to victims, shall be subject to sanctions as stipulated in laws and regulations.  
2. Regency Regional Apparatus having the duties and functions of implementing women's empowerment and child protection that fails to carry out its obligations and responsibilities shall be subject to administrative sanctions.  
3. Management of P2TP2A, Task Force for the Protection of Women and Children at Sub-District Level, Task Unit for the Protection of Women and Children at Village Level and other institutions or organizations in accordance with the provisions of laws and regulations implementing protection duties, if violating protection principles, may be subject to sanctions in accordance with the mechanisms applicable within the internal institution or respective organization.

# CHAPTER XIV

## CLOSING PROVISIONS

#### Article 65

This Regional Regulation shall come into force on the date of promulgation. In order that everyone may know of it, the promulgation of this Regional Regulation is ordered by its placement in the Regional Gazette of Tasikmalaya Regency.
