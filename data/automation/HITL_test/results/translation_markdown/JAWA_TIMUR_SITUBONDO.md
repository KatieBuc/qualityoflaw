## KBIENTUAN UMUM

#### Article 1

Regional Government is the Situbondo Regency Government.  

Regent is the Regent of Situbondo.  

Child is a person who has not yet reached 18 years of age, including those in the womb. Violence is any act unlawfully with or without the use of means against physical and psychological that causes danger to life, body or results in the deprivation of a person's freedom.  

Violence against women is any action that results in or may result in misery or suffering of women physically, sexually or psychologically, including threats of certain actions, coercion or arbitrary deprivation of freedom, whether occurring in the public sphere or in private life.  

Violence against children is any act against a child that results in misery or suffering physically, mentally, sexually, psychologically, including neglect and ill-treatment that threatens bodily integrity and degrades the dignity of the child committed by parties who should be responsible for the child or those who have power over the child, who should be trusted, such as parents, close family, teachers and assistants.  

Victim is a woman and child who experiences misery and/or suffering either directly or indirectly as a result of violence.  

Service is an action that must be immediately taken to the victim when seeing, hearing and knowing that violence against the victim will, is or has occurred.  

Assistant is a person or representative from an institution who has the expertise to accompany victims to conduct counseling, therapy and advocacy for the strengthening and recovery of victims of violence.  

10. Integrated Service Center (PPT) for Victims of Violence against Women and Children of Situbondo Regency, hereinafter referred to as PPT Situbondo Regency is an institution providing services to victims of violence, which is hospital-based, managed jointly in the form of medical services (including medico-legal), psychosocial and legal services.  

11. Safe house (shelter) is a temporary residence used to provide protection to victims in accordance with determined operational standards.  

12. Community is individuals, families, groups, social organizations and/or community organizations.  

13. Woman is a person who is female.  

14. Family is the smallest unit in society consisting of husband and wife, or husband and wife and their children, or father and his children, and mother and her children.  

15. Household is family members and relatives such as grandchildren, nieces/nephews, older siblings, younger siblings, grandfathers, grandmothers, husbands, wives, cousins and so on and non-relatives such as domestic workers, drivers and so on who live and reside in one house.  
16. Community and Women Empowerment Agency hereinafter abbreviated as BPMP is the Community and Women Empowerment Agency of Situbondo Regency.

# CHAPTER II

## PRINCIPLES AND OBJECTIVES

#### Article 2

The implementation of protection for women and children victims of violence is carried out based on the principles of: a. respect for human rights;  
b. gender equality and justice;  
c. protection of victims' rights;  
d. non-discriminatory; and  
e. best interests of the victim.

#### Article 3

1. The purpose of implementing protection for women and children victims of violence is to provide protection and services to victims of violence based on gender and the best interests of women and children that occur at home and/or in public places.  

(2) Violence as referred to in paragraph (1) may be in the form of:  
a. physical violence;  
b. psychological violence;  
c. sexual violence;  
d. economic neglect; and  
e. restriction of movement.

# CHAPTER III

## HAK.HAK KORBAN

#### Article 4

Every victim of violence has the right to obtain:  
a. protection and assistance;  
b. legal aid;  
c. health services according to medical needs;  
d. psychosocial services;  
e. information; and  
f. integrated services.

#### Article 5

1. In the event that a victim of violence as referred to in Article 4 requires ongoing treatment, they have the right to stay in a safe house or alternative residence.  

(2) Ongoing treatment as referred to in paragraph (1) is treatment at the rehabilitation stage carried out by individuals, groups or Government and non-Government institutions.

#### Article 6

Safe house as referred to in Article 5 (1) paragraph (1) provides a temporary safe place and/or shelter for victims of violence until their rights are restored.  

The operation and/or management of the safe house (2) as referred to in paragraph (1) is carried out by PPT.  

For the safety and at the request of the victim (3) of violence, the manager or operator of the safe house as referred to in paragraph (2) may place the victim of violence in a certain place that is kept confidential.

# CHAPTER IV

## IMPLEMENTATION OF PROTECTION

## Part One

Institutional

#### Article 7

The implementation of protection for victims (1) of violence is carried out in an integrated manner through the PPT Situbondo Regency forum.  

Provisions regarding PPT Situbondo Regency (2) as referred to in paragraph (1) are regulated by Regent Regulation.

## Part Two

Forms and Mechanisms of Service

#### Article 8

1. Forms of services to victims of violence organized by PPT include:a. medical services in the form of treatment and recovery of physical condition carried out by medical personnel and paramedics;  
b. medicolegal services are a form of medical service for evidentiary purposes in the legal field;  
c. legal aid services to assist victims of violence in undergoing the judicial process;  
d. first psychosocial assistance to victims conducted to identify the occurrence of violence and trauma experienced by victims;  
e. assistance to victims of violence carried out by competent persons or institutions who have the expertise to conduct counseling, therapy and advocacy for the strengthening and recovery of victims of violence and have cooperated with PPT; andf. economic independence services in the form of services for skills training and provision of economic access so that victims can be independent.  
2. Service mechanisms as referred to in paragraph (1) are organized according to Standard Operating Procedures (SOP) regulated by Regent Regulation.

## Part Three

Service Principles

#### Article 9

(1) The implementation of services to victims is carried out free of charge, quickly, safely, with empathy, non-discriminatory, easily accessible and with guaranteed confidentiality.  

(2) The organizer or manager of PPT or Safe House is prohibited from charging any fees to victims of violence.

# CHAPTER VI

SUPERVISION

#### Article 14

(1) Supervision of the implementation of protection of victims of violence shall be carried out by BPMP.

(2) In conducting supervision as referred to in paragraph (1), BPMP shall have the authority to:

a. place victims of violence in safe houses;

b. summon and present the family of victims of violence to be heard; and

c. order PPT to provide protection to a person who is a victim of violence.

(3) BPMP shall report periodically to the Regent regarding the implementation of protection of women and children victims of violence.

# CHAPTER VII

## FINANCING

#### Article 15

(1) The financing of the implementation of protection of women and children victims of violence shall be charged to the Regional Budget of Situbondo Regency and other lawful sources.

(2) The budget management for the implementation of protection of victims of violence shall be conducted with the principles of fairness, efficiency, transparency and accountability.

# CHAPTER VIII

## CRIMINAL PROVISIONS

#### Article 16

Any person who deliberately allows the occurrence of violence against women and children and/or does not report the occurrence of violence against women and children, shall be subject to sanctions as stipulated in the applicable laws and regulations.

#### Article 17

(1) Any person who deliberately violates the provisions as referred to in Article 6 paragraph (3), shall be punished with imprisonment of a maximum of 3 (three) months or a fine of a maximum of Rp. 50,000,000,- (fifty million rupiah).

(2) In the event that the act as referred to in paragraph (1) is committed by an official appointed to implement protection, shall be punished with imprisonment of a maximum of 6 (six) months or a fine of a maximum of Rp. 50,000,000,- (fifty million rupiah).

(3) The organizer or manager of PPT or Safe House who is proven to have violated the provisions as referred to in Article 9 paragraph (2), shall be punished with imprisonment of a maximum of 6 (six) months or a fine of a maximum of Rp. 50,000,000,- (fifty million rupiah).

##### VICTIMS OF VIOLENCE

##### I. GENERAL

The implementation of protection for victims of violence is conducted based on the principles of humanity, integration, and justice. Therefore, every person must obtain their right to protection for a sense of security and to be free from torture or treatment that degrades human dignity, which is contrary to what is aspired to in the Preamble of the 1945 Constitution of the Republic of Indonesia, namely the creation of a just and civilized humanity. To achieve integrated and comprehensive Protection Services for Women and Children Victims of Violence, an integrated form of service is required from various related institutions including medical services, medicolegal services, psychosocial services, legal aid, assistance, and economic independence (training). These services are carried out evenly across all levels of society so that every citizen has the right to obtain equal opportunities to receive security and legal protection.

In principle, the strategic target of implementing protection is to maintain the sustainability of protection through integrated, comprehensive services and a safe and comfortable house/shelter that is conducive for victims of violence. These strategic targets are pursued through interrelated stages, including planning, protection, services and financing, supervision, and sanctions.

There are four important phenomena that need to be observed in relation to the Implementation of Protection for Women and Children Victims of Violence. First, violence against women and children from year to year is increasing in number, with the mode of violence tending to be more sadistic (perpetrators who slit throats, pour hot water, throw [victims] into wells, to breaking parts of limbs and so on). Second, the many cases of violence against Domestic Workers (PRT), dominated by women, that have occurred recently require attention from all parties, especially the existence of legal protection, regulations, and policies that protect them. Third, the lack of facilities and infrastructure for safe and comfortable houses/shelters that are conducive for victims of violence. These three phenomena indicate the increasing complexity of violent behavior whose victims are dominated by women and children, both those who become domestic workers and others. Fourth, children are a trust and gift from God Almighty; inherent in them are dignity and worth as complete human beings. Children are the next generation of the nation's ideals who have a strategic role, and have special characteristics and traits that are expected to ensure the continuity of the nation and state's existence in the future. Children need to get the widest possible opportunity to grow and develop optimally, both physically, mentally, cognitively, spiritually, and socially.

Because of its nature, the growth and development of children must be carried out in an environment that protects [them] from all dangers in the form of optimal care.

Therefore, a legal umbrella is needed in the form of a Regional Regulation (Perda) that is able to accommodate all issues related to the implementation of protection for women and children victims of violence, able to provide integrated services holistically and comprehensively, and firmly give a mandate to institutions to coordinate policies and supervision, and a mandate to institutions to provide services. In addition, the Perda can also open the involvement of non-governmental institutions and the community to play a broad role.

The implementation of protection for women and children victims of violence in Situbondo Regency is carried out based on Situbondo Regent Regulation Number 6 of 2006 concerning the Implementation of Protection for Victims of Acts of Violence against Women and Children and Situbondo Regent Decree No. 188/237 1P.00 L.2/2007 concerning the Integrated Service Center (PPT) for Victims of Violence against Women and Children in Situbondo Regency. As with the phenomena described above, Regent Regulation Number 6 of 2006 has not been able to become a comprehensive legal umbrella in providing protection for women and children victims of violence. With this Regional Regulation, it is expected to anticipate and overcome the conditions of violence that occur in Situbondo Regency, so that it can provide protection and services to victims of violence in an integrated and comprehensive manner.

#### Article 2

Letter a What is meant by "the principle of respect for human rights" is that the implementation of protection for women and children victims of violence is to ensure the fulfillment of the rights of victims of violence as a manifestation of respect for human rights.  

Letter b What is meant by "the principle of gender equality and justice" is a process of equality and conditions for men and women to obtain opportunities and their rights as human beings, so that they are able to play a role and participate in political, economic, social, cultural, defense and national security activities and equality in enjoying the results of such development.  

Letter c What is meant by "the principle of protection of victims' rights" is the protection of personal self, family, honor, dignity, and property under their control, a sense of security and protection from threats of fear, freedom from torture or treatment that degrades human dignity.  

Letter d What is meant by "non-discriminatory principle" is an attitude and treatment towards victims by not making distinctions based on age, gender, race, ethnicity, religion and between groups.  

Letter e What is meant by "the principle of the best interests of the victim" is that all actions concerning victims carried out by the government, society, legislative bodies and judicial bodies, then the best interests of the victim must be the primary consideration.

#### Article 3

" Paragraph (1) What is meant by gender-based violence is an act based on gender relations that places women in a subordinate position to men.

Paragraph (2) Point a What is meant by "physical violence" is an act that results in pain, illness, or serious injury such as slapping, hitting, spitting, pulling hair/grabbing by the hair, kicking, burning with hot objects, hitting/injuring with weapons, and/or causing disability to a person's body, miscarriage, fainting and/or causing death.

Point b What is meant by "psychological violence" is an act that results in fear, loss of self-confidence, loss of ability to act, feelings of helplessness, and/or severe psychological suffering to a person. Psychological violence may take the form of emotional abuse such as insults, hurtful or demeaning comments, isolating the wife from the outside world, threatening or intimidating as a means of imposing one's will.

Point c What is meant by "sexual violence" is violence against sexuality which includes isolating (distancing) the wife from her emotional needs, forcing sexual intercourse, imposing one's own sexual preferences or not paying attention to the wife's satisfaction. Sexual violence may also take the form of forced sexual intercourse committed against a person residing within the household, forced sexual intercourse against one person within the household with another person for commercial purposes and/or certain purposes.

Point d What is meant by "economic neglect" is neglecting the economic needs of the wife or children or family members or relatives such as not providing for the wife, even spending the wife's money, not giving or withholding wages of domestic workers or drivers and includes acts of restricting and/or prohibiting family members from working in a proper manner.

Point e What is meant by "restriction of movement" is restricting the movement of the wife or children in developing their potential both for economic and social purposes such as prohibiting them from gathering and socializing in the community as well as restricting the movement of relatives such as preventing domestic workers or drivers from communicating or having contact with their families.
