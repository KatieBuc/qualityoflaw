REGENT OF PADANG PARIAMAN

PROVINCE OF WEST SUMATRA

REGIONAL REGULATION OF PADANG PARIAMAN REGENCY

NUMBER 6 OF 2017

CONCERNING

WOMEN'S EMPOWERMENT AND CHILD PROTECTION

BY THE GRACE OF ALMIGHTY GOD

REGENT OF PADANG PARIAMAN,

Considering:
a. that to guarantee Women's Empowerment and Child Protection in accordance with human dignity and to receive Protection from Violence, discrimination and other violations, legal certainty is necessary;
b. that to provide legal certainty in the implementation of Women's Empowerment and Child Protection as well as to carry out mandatory regional government affairs, regulation in the form of a Regional Regulation is necessary;
c. that based on the considerations as referred to in letter a, and letter b, it is necessary to establish a Regional Regulation concerning Women's Empowerment and Child Protection;

In View of:
1. Article 18 paragraph (6) of the Constitution of the Republic of Indonesia of 1945;

2. Law Number 12 of 1956 concerning the Establishment of Autonomous Regency Regions within the Central Sumatra Provincial Region (State Gazette of the Republic of Indonesia of 1956 Number 25);
3. Law Number 39 of 1999 concerning Human Rights (State Gazette of the Republic of Indonesia of 1999 Number 165, Supplement to the State Gazette Number 3886);
4. Law Number 23 of 2002 concerning Child Protection (State Gazette of the Republic of Indonesia of 2002 Number 109, Supplement to the State Gazette of the Republic of Indonesia Number 4235) as amended by Law Number 35 of 2014 concerning Amendment to Law Number 23 of 2002 concerning Child Protection (State Gazette of the Republic of Indonesia of 2014 Number 397, Supplement to the State Gazette of the Republic of Indonesia Number 5606);
5. Law .......
5. Law Number 23 of 2014 concerning Regional Government (State Gazette of the Republic of Indonesia of 2014 Number 244, Supplement to the State Gazette of the Republic of Indonesia Number 5587) as amended several times, most recently by Law Number 9 of 2015 (State Gazette of the Republic of Indonesia of 2015 Number 58, Supplement to the State Gazette of the Republic of Indonesia Number 5679);
6. Regulation of the State Minister for Women's Empowerment and Child Protection Number 1 of 2010 Concerning Minimum Service Standards for Integrated Services for Women and Children Victims of Violence;
7. Regulation of the State Minister for Women's Empowerment and Child Protection Number 11 of 2011 Concerning Policy for the Development of Child-Friendly Regencies;

With Mutual Approval of THE REGIONAL HOUSE OF REPRESENTATIVES OF PADANG PARIAMAN REGENCY and

REGENT OF PADANG PARIAMAN

HAS DECIDED:

To Establish: REGIONAL REGULATION CONCERNING WOMEN'S EMPOWERMENT AND CHILD PROTECTION.

CHAPTER I

GENERAL PROVISIONS

Article 1

In this Regional Regulation, what is meant by:

1. Region is Padang Pariaman Regency.
2. Regional Government is the Government of Padang Pariaman Regency.
3. Regent is the Regent of Padang Pariaman Regency.
4. Regional Work Unit hereinafter abbreviated as SKPD is the Regional Work Unit within the Government of Padang Pariaman Regency.
5. Women's Empowerment is an effort of Protection, strengthening of human rights, improving quality of life, and increasing women's participation in development.
6. Child Protection is all activities to guarantee and protect Children and their rights so that they can live, grow, develop, and participate optimally in accordance with human dignity and worth, and receive Protection from Violence and discrimination.
7. Child is a person who has not reached the age of 18 (eighteen) years, including a Child who is still in the womb and is not married.
8. Prevention is a direct effort carried out by the Regional Government, community and parents and/or family to prevent violations of the rights of women and Children.
9. Violence is any act that results in misery or suffering either physically, sexually, psychologically and/or domestic neglect including threats to commit acts, coercion, or deprivation of a person's freedom unlawfully.
10. Children's Forum is a forum for Child participation formed with the aim of facilitating the involvement of Children in the decision-making process and enjoying changes relating to their lives either directly or indirectly, which is carried out with the consent and will of all Children based on awareness and understanding, in accordance with age and level of maturity of thinking.
11. Child-Friendly Regency hereinafter abbreviated as KLA is a Regency that has a Child rights-based development system through the integration of commitment and resources of government, community and business world that is planned comprehensively and sustainably in policies, programs and activities to ensure the fulfillment of Children's rights.
12. Gender Mainstreaming is a strategy built to integrate gender into an integral dimension of planning, formulation, implementation, monitoring and evaluation of national development program policies.
13. Community is individuals, families, groups and social organizations and/or community organizations.
14. Women Victims of Violence are women who experience actions that result in misery or suffering physically, sexually or psychologically, including threats of certain actions, coercion or deprivation of freedom arbitrarily, whether occurring in the public sphere or in private life.
15. Child Victims of Violence are Children who experience actions that result in misery or suffering physically, mentally, sexually, psychologically or neglect that threatens life, body and degrades human dignity.
16. Trafficking in persons is the act of recruitment, transportation, harboring, sending, transfer, or receipt of a person by threat of Violence, use of Violence, kidnapping, confinement, forgery, fraud, abuse of power or vulnerable position, debt bondage or giving payment or benefits, so as to obtain consent from the person holding control over that other person, whether carried out within the Country or between Countries, for the purpose of exploitation or resulting in the person being exploited.
17. Complaint handling is a series of actions carried out by integrated service providers to follow up on reports of acts of Violence against women and Children, submitted by victims, families or the community.
18. Social rehabilitation is a service aimed at restoring and developing the ability of a person experiencing social dysfunction so that they can carry out their social functions properly.
19. Law enforcement is the action of apparatus authorized by the state to implement laws and regulations.
20. Legal aid is legal services provided by legal assistants and advocates to carry out the process of assisting witnesses and/or victims of Violence against women and Children that is gender sensitive.
21. Repatriation is an effort to return women and Child victims of Violence from abroad to the debarkation point/entry point, or from the receiving Region to the Region of origin.
22. Social reintegration is an effort to reunite victims with family, substitute family, or community that can provide Protection and fulfillment of needs for victims.
23. Safe house is a temporary shelter that aims to provide Protection and a sense of security to victims of Violence.
24. Shelter house is a forum for guidance and welfare services for street Children that carries out social assistance/guidance activities, maintenance, care, education, and health, skills guidance in order to ensure that Children do not carry out activities on the streets so that they can grow and develop normally.
25. Spiritual Guidance is an effort to restore the psyche through strengthening the religion adhered to by the victim.
26. Gender Mainstreaming is a strategy built to integrate gender into an integral dimension of planning, formulation, implementation, monitoring, and evaluation of policies, programs, and development activities in the Region.
27. Gender responsive is consistent and systematic attention to the differences between women and men in society accompanied by efforts to eliminate structural and cultural barriers in achieving gender equality.
28. Gender Sensitivity or Gender Awareness is the ability to identify gaps in power relations between women and men in the family and in the community, the impact of gender-based division of labor on women and men, and the experiences, problems, needs, interests and aspirations of women and men are different.
29. Public service providers are every state organizing institution, corporation, independent institution formed based on Law for public service activities, and other legal entities formed solely for public service activities.
30. Community Organization is an organization established and formed by the community voluntarily based on common aspirations, will, needs, interests, activities and goals to participate in development for the achievement of the goals of the Unitary State of the Republic of Indonesia based on Pancasila.
31. Parents are biological father and/or mother, or step father and/or mother, or adoptive father and/or mother.
32. Family is the smallest unit in society consisting of husband and wife or husband, wife and their Child, or father and his Child, or mother and her Child, or blood family in a straight line upward or downward up to the third degree.
33. Customs are eternal and hereditary conduct from one generation to another generation as a legacy, so that it is strongly integrated with community behavior patterns.

Article 2

The implementation of Women's Empowerment and Child Protection is based on the principles of:

a. best interests for women and Children;
b. gender justice and gender equality;
c. local wisdom;
d. law enforcement and legal certainty;
e. non-discrimination;
f. right to life, survival, and development;
g. respect for the opinion of Children; and
h. law enforcement and legal certainty.

Article 3

This Regional Regulation aims to:
a. guarantee the fulfillment of the rights of women and Children in the Region
b. serve as a guideline for the Region in planning, policies and strategies for Women's Empowerment and Child Protection;
c. protect and prevent women and Children from actions, decisions and policies that violate the rights of women and Children;
d. enhance the role and value of local wisdom and the role of customs in Women's Empowerment and Child Protection;

Article 4

The scope of regulation of Women's Empowerment and Child Protection includes:
a. improvement of women's quality of life;
b. Protection of women;
c. gender and Child data system;
d. fulfillment of Children's rights;
e. Child Protection;
f. community participation; and
g. guidance and supervision.

CHAPTER II

IMPROVEMENT OF WOMEN'S QUALITY OF LIFE

Part One

General

Article 5

1. In order to improve the quality of life of women, the regional government is responsible for:
a. respecting and guaranteeing the rights of women without discrimination in accordance with human dignity;
b. implementing Gender Mainstreaming in the Region;
c. establishing a Gender Mainstreaming working group in the Region;
d. increasing Women's Empowerment in roles, quality and capacity in the fields of;
1. politics;
2. law;
3. social; and
4. economy.
e. strengthening and developing service provider institutions for Women's Empowerment in the Region;
2. The responsibility of the Regional Government in efforts to improve the quality of life of women as referred to in paragraph (1) shall be implemented by SKPD in accordance with their duties and functions.

Part Two

Implementation of Gender Mainstreaming in the Region

Article 6

1. The Regent is responsible for the implementation of Gender Mainstreaming in the Region.
2. The responsibility of the Regent as referred to in paragraph (1) may be delegated to the Vice Regent.

Article 7

In implementing Gender Mainstreaming in the Region as referred to in Article 5 paragraph (1) letter b, the Regent shall designate the SKPD responsible for Women's Empowerment as the coordinator for the implementation of Gender Mainstreaming.

Part Three

Establishment of Gender Mainstreaming Working Group in the Region

Article 8

1. In an effort to accelerate the institutionalization of Gender Mainstreaming in all Regional SKPDs, a Gender Mainstreaming working group in the Region shall be established.
2. Members of the Gender Mainstreaming working group are all heads/leaders of SKPDs.
3. The Regent shall appoint the head of Bappeda as the chairperson of the Regional Gender Mainstreaming working group and the head of the SKPD responsible for Women's Empowerment as the head of the secretariat of the Regional Gender Mainstreaming working group.
4. The establishment of the Regional Gender Mainstreaming working group shall be determined by a Regent's decree.

Article 9

The Regional Gender Mainstreaming working group as referred to in Article 14 has the following duties:
a. promoting and facilitating Gender Mainstreaming to each SKPD;
b. conducting socialization and advocacy of Gender Mainstreaming to sub-district heads and wali nagari;
c. preparing an annual work program;
d. encouraging the realization of gender-Responsive planning and budgeting;
e. preparing the annual work plan of the Gender Mainstreaming working group;
f. being accountable to the Regent through the Vice Regent;
g. formulating policy recommendations to the Regent;
h. preparing a Regional Gender profile;
i. monitoring the implementation of Gender Mainstreaming in each agency;
j. establishing a technical team to conduct analysis of the Regional budget;
k. preparing a regional action plan for Regional Gender Mainstreaming; and
l. encouraging the carrying out of the selection and appointment of focal points in each SKPD.

Article 10

1. The Technical Team as referred to in Article 9 letter j shall consist of officials who understand gender responsive budget analysis.
2. The regional action plan for Regional Gender Mainstreaming as referred to in Article 9 letter k shall contain:
a. Gender Mainstreaming in regional legislation;
b. Gender Mainstreaming in the development cycle in the Region;
c. strengthening of Gender Mainstreaming institutions in the Region; and
d. strengthening community participation in the Region.

Article 11

1. The Gender Mainstreaming Focal Point in each SKPD shall consist of officials and/or staff responsible for planning and/or programs.
2. The Gender Mainstreaming Focal Point as referred to in paragraph (1) has the following duties:
a. promoting Gender Mainstreaming in the work unit;
b. facilitating the preparation of gender-responsive SKPD work plans and budgeting;
c. conducting training, socialization, advocacy of Gender Mainstreaming to all officials and staff within the SKPD;
d. reporting the implementation of Gender Mainstreaming to the SKPD leadership;
e. encouraging the implementation of gender analysis of policies, programs, and activities in the work unit; and
f. facilitating the preparation of gender data in each SKPD;
3. The Gender Mainstreaming Focal Point as referred to in paragraph (1) shall be selected and appointed by the SKPD leadership.

Part Four

Improvement of Women's Role, Quality, and Capacity in the Political Field

Article 12

Efforts to improve the role, quality and capacity of women in the political field as referred to in Article 5 paragraph (1) letter d number 1 include:
a. conducting socialization to increase women's political participation in general elections;
b. implementing programs that encourage the level of women's involvement in political processes and public positions;
c. facilitating women's self-development in organizing, associating and assembling;
d. involving women in decision-making concerning women's interests;

Part Five

Improvement of Women's Role, Quality, and Capacity in the Legal Field

Article 13

Efforts to improve the role, quality and capacity of women in the legal field as referred to in Article 5 paragraph (1) letter d number 2 include:
a. conducting socialization to increase women's legal knowledge and awareness;
b. mapping and reviewing gender-biased policies;
c. involving women's organizations in the process of drafting and discussing Regional legal products related to women's participation;

Part Six

Improvement of Women's Role, Quality, and Capacity in the Social Field

Article 14

Efforts to improve the role, quality and capacity of women in the social field as referred to in Article 5 paragraph (1) letter d number 3 include:
a. increasing knowledge and skills to encourage the fulfillment of education in accordance with their potential;
b. improving and fulfilling the quality of health for women;
c. increasing awareness and knowledge about planning an independent, healthy and prosperous family including access to consultation services and marriage registration;
d. increasing women's awareness of the impact of early marriage;
e. facilitating and promoting the preservation of customs and the development of science, technology, arts and culture for the advancement of women;
f. increasing women's awareness and knowledge of planning an independent, healthy and prosperous family;
g. implementing programs to reduce maternal mortality rates;
h. encouraging the formation of women's organizations in the Region;

Part Seven

Improvement of Women's Role, Quality, and Capacity in the Economic Field

Article 15

Efforts to increase the role, quality and capability of women in the economic field as referred to in Article 5 paragraph (1) letter d number 4 include:
a. implementing programs, activities, training to improve the skills and expertise of women in order to realize the improvement of women's welfare;
b. facilitation of business capital strengthening for women with weak economy;
c. marketing of products from businesses of women with weak economy;
d. training on developing women's capabilities through economic empowerment of pre-prosperous families;
e. comprehensive entrepreneurship training for women.

CHAPTER III

PROTECTION OF WOMEN

Part One

General

Article 16

1. The Regional Government in efforts to Protect women is responsible for:
a. establishing and implementing policies, programs and activities for the Protection of women;
b. making efforts to prevent acts of Violence against women including criminal acts of trafficking in persons;
c. increasing the effectiveness of services for women victims of acts of Violence;
d. increasing efforts to Protect female workers;
e. strengthening and developing institutions providing Protection services for women;
f. conducting cooperation with the Government, other Regional Governments, the private sector, business world and/or NGOs in efforts to prevent acts of Violence against women;
g. involving the active role of families, ninik mamak, bundo kanduang, customary leaders, religious leaders, the community, and institutions in the field of Protection of women;
2. The responsibility of the Regional Government in efforts to Protect women as referred to in paragraph (1) is implemented by SKPD in accordance with their duties and functions and may coordinate with related agencies.

Part Two

Prevention of Acts of Violence against Women

Article 17

In order to prevent acts of Violence against women, the Regional Government:
a. conducts socialization regarding the prevention of Violence against women by optimizing the role of customary, cultural, religious institutions and community organizations;
b. builds networks and cooperation with law enforcement apparatus, government apparatus, universities and various non-governmental organizations that work and/or care for women and children victims of Violence;
c. provides public facilities that are comfortable and safe for women;

Part Three

Effectiveness of Services for Women Victims of Acts of Violence

Paragraph 1

General

Article 18

1. Protection of women victims of acts of Violence is implemented through integrated services.
2. The provision of integrated services for women victims of acts of Violence as referred to in paragraph (1) uses the principles of:
a. gender responsiveness;
b. non-discrimination;
c. equal and respectful relationships;
d. maintaining confidentiality;
e. providing a sense of security and comfort;
f. respecting individual differences;
g. non-judgmental;

h. respecting the choices and decisions of victims;
i. sensitivity to the background and condition of victims and the use of language that is appropriate and understood by victims;
j. fast and simple;
k. empathy; and
l. fulfillment of women's rights.

Article 19

Forms of integrated services for women victims of acts of Violence include:
a. complaint handling;
b. health rehabilitation;
c. social rehabilitation;
d. law enforcement and legal assistance; and
e. repatriation and social reintegration.

Paragraph 2

Complaint Handling

Article 20

Integrated services for complaint handling as referred to in Article 19 letter a are implemented by:
a. hospitals;
b. community health centers;
c. women and children service units;
d. trauma center protection houses;
e. women crisis centres;
f. advisory board for marriage guidance and preservation;
g. police;
h. prosecutor's office;
i. courts;
j. ministry of religious affairs; and/or
k. legal aid institutions;

Article 21

1. Complaints of acts of Violence against women may be made by reporters consisting of:
a. victims;
b. family;
c. community; and/or
d. community organizations in the field of Protection of women and Children, such as: P2TP2A, Safe Houses etc.
2. Reporters as referred to in paragraph (1) may make complaints:
a. directly;
b. by telephone; and/or
c. by letter.
3. In addition to handling complaints submitted by reporters, officers at service institutions as referred to in Article 20, are also authorized to handle victims of acts of Violence against women obtained through other information.
4. Further provisions regarding the procedures for complaint handling services as referred to in paragraph (1) and paragraph (2) are regulated by Regent Regulation.

Paragraph 3

Health Rehabilitation

Article 22

1. Health rehabilitation as referred to in Article 19 letter b constitutes efforts that include aspects of:
a. promotive;
b. preventive;
c. curative; and
d. rehabilitative.
2. Health rehabilitation as referred to in paragraph (1) is carried out comprehensively, can be accessed 24 (twenty-four) hours, is of quality and is carried out in accordance with health service standards.
3. Women victims of acts of Violence have the right to health services free of charge, including:
a. medical services;
b. medicolegal services;
c. psychosocial counseling; and
d. visum.
4. Further provisions regarding the procedures for implementing health services as referred to in paragraph (3) are regulated by Regent Regulation.

Paragraph 4

Social Rehabilitation

Article 23

1. Social rehabilitation as referred to in Article 19 letter c is aimed at restoring and developing the capabilities of women victims of acts of Violence who experience social dysfunction to be able to carry out their social functions in society properly.
2. Social rehabilitation as referred to in paragraph (1) includes services for:
a. counseling;
b. provision of safe houses; and
c. spiritual guidance.
3. The provision of services as referred to in paragraph (2) is organized by:
a. SKPD that has duties and functions in the social field;
b. SKPD that oversees Women's Empowerment, and may cooperate with integrated service centers, women crisis centres or community organizations in the social field that already exist; or
c. ministry of religious affairs.

Article 24

1. Counseling services as referred to in Article 23 paragraph (2) letter a aim to:
a. help women victims of acts of Violence recognize their problems and find effective ways to overcome them themselves;
b. empower women victims of acts of Violence to determine their future;
c. provide moral support to women victims of acts of Violence in facing the process they are going through; and
d. make women victims of acts of Violence feel accepted in the community environment.
2. Counseling services are carried out by gender-responsive social rehabilitation officers, consisting of:
a. counselors;
b. psychologists; and
c. psychiatrists.
3. Counseling services are carried out in a place that guarantees a sense of security, comfort and confidentiality of information from women victims of acts of Violence.
4. Stages of counseling services include:
a. initial counseling;
b. follow-up counseling;
c. case discussion; and
d. site visits.

Article 25

1. The Regional Government may provide safe houses for women victims of Violence.
2. The provision of safe house services as referred to in Article 23 paragraph (2) letter b aims to protect women victims of Violence from intimidation by perpetrators or other parties.
3. The location of safe houses as referred to in paragraph (1) must be kept confidential.
4. The criteria for women victims of Violence who require safe houses include:
a. their life is threatened;
b. receiving rejection from family or community;
c. requiring intensive services but their residence is relatively far; and/or
d. abandoned if not placed in a safe house.
5. The provision of safe house services is carried out by social rehabilitation officers who have gender sensitivity or gender awareness, consisting of:
a. social workers/volunteers;
b. security officers; and/or
c. community leaders.
6. Further provisions regarding the provision of safe house services shall be regulated by Regent Regulation.

Article 26

1. Spiritual guidance as referred to in Article 23 paragraph (2) letter c aims for psychological recovery.
2. Spiritual guidance as referred to in paragraph (1) is implemented based on the principles of:
a. not imposing any religion or belief; and
b. leaving the final decision in the hands of the victim (self determination).
3. Spiritual guidance is conducted by spiritual guidance officers.
4. Spiritual guidance officers as referred to in paragraph (3) consist of consultants from the Marriage Advisory, Development and Preservation Agency (BP4), Religious Affairs Office Officers, Ustads/Ulamas, Priests, Pastors and other religious leaders who have gender sensitivity or gender awareness.

Paragraph 5

Law Enforcement and Legal Aid

Article 27

1. Law enforcement and legal aid services as referred to in Article 19 letter d constitute actions by law enforcement officials to implement laws and regulations in handling cases of Violence against women, especially to impose sanctions on perpetrators and provide Protection for witnesses and/or victims.
2. Law enforcement and legal aid services as referred to in paragraph (1) are carried out by prioritizing the best interests of the victim.

Article 28

1. Law enforcement and legal aid services are intended to provide assistance and Protection to women victims of Violence and witnesses with a gender-responsive approach so that victims obtain justice and legal certainty.
2. Types of legal aid services include:
a. legal aid in the reporting process and preparation of examination reports;
b. legal aid in the prosecution process at the prosecutor's office;
c. legal aid in the examination process at court;
d. legal aid in the court hearing decision process until there is a final legal decision; and
e. legal aid in the mediation process.
3. The Regional Government provides legal aid to women victims of Violence based on the provisions of laws and regulations.
4. In providing legal aid as referred to in paragraph (3), the Regional Government may cooperate with legal aid institutions, women crisis centers, advocates and/or other legal assistants who are gender sensitive.

Paragraph 6

Repatriation and Social Reintegration

Article 29

1. Repatriation is intended to return women victims of Violence to their Region of origin.
2. Repatriation of women victims of Violence is accompanied by social reintegration efforts.

Article 30

1. Social reintegration is carried out in order to reunite women victims of Violence with family, substitute family, or community that can provide Protection and fulfillment of needs for victims.
2. Social reintegration is intended so that:

a. women victims of Violence can resume their social life; and

b. women victims of Violence can have a decent life.
3. Social reintegration as referred to in paragraph (1) is carried out after the victim receives social rehabilitation services.

Article 31

The Regional Government implements repatriation and social reintegration of women victims of Violence in accordance with laws and regulations.

Part Four

Employment Protection

Article 32

1. Employers who employ women must maintain decency, safety and security while at the workplace.
2. Employers must place female workers in places that are safe for women's reproductive health.
3. Employers must fulfill the rights of female workers to receive wages in accordance with the provisions of laws and regulations.
4. Employers must provide special breastfeeding facilities at their companies.

Article 33

1. Female workers/laborers under 18 (eighteen) years of age are prohibited from being employed between 23.00 WIB until 07.00 WIB.
2. Employers are prohibited from employing pregnant female workers/laborers who according to a doctor's statement are dangerous to the health and safety of their pregnancy.
3. Employers who employ female workers/laborers between 23.00 WIB until 07.00 WIB, must:
a. provide nutritious food and drinks; and
b. maintain decency and security while at the workplace.
4. Employers must provide shuttle transportation for female workers/laborers who depart and return from work between 23.00 WIB until 05.00 WIB.

CHAPTER V

IMPROVING FAMILY QUALITY

Article 34

1. In order to improve family quality, the Regional Government is responsible for:
a. making efforts to improve family quality to realize gender equality and Children's rights;
b. providing services for families in realizing gender equality and Children's rights.
c. strengthening and developing family quality improvement service provider institutions in the Region; and
2. The responsibility of the Regional Government in efforts to improve family quality as referred to in paragraph (1) is implemented by SKPD in accordance with their duties and functions.

Article 35

The Regional Government makes efforts to improve family quality as referred to in Article 34 paragraph (1) letter a including:

a. development;
b. guidance;
c. supervision;
d. facilitation of family development implementation;
e. improving the health quality of pregnant and breastfeeding mothers;

Article 36

Development as referred to in Article 35 letter a, includes:

a. growth and development of toddlers;
b. Protection of adolescent reproductive health;
c. Maturation of marriage age;
d. Birth regulation; and
e. Population management.

Article 37

Guidance as referred to in Article 35 letter b includes:

a. conducting counseling on improving women's quality of life;
b. conducting premarital counseling;

Article 38.

Supervision as referred to in Article 35 letter c includes:

a. Conducting evaluation of toddler growth and development data;
b. Conducting evaluation of post-marriage data;

Article 39

Provision of services for families as referred to in Article 34 paragraph (1) letter b includes:

a. communication, information and education;
b. contraceptive devices and drugs;
c. recording and reporting of family planning services;
d. adequate health service facilities and infrastructure;
e. breastfeeding corner rooms in every public facility.

Article 40

Communication, information and education as referred to in Article 39 letter a constitute communication activities to increase knowledge and improve the attitudes and behavior of families and communities in the population and family planning Program.

Article 41

1. In addition to the Regional Government, private parties (private parties include, among others, limited liability companies (PT) that provide public services) are obligated to provide breastfeeding corner rooms. (2) Breastfeeding corner rooms as referred to in paragraph (1) are proper and adequate rooms.
3. Private parties that do not provide breastfeeding corner rooms as referred to in paragraph (2) shall be subject to administrative sanctions in the form of:a. written warning;
b. limitation of business activities;
c. suspension of business activities;
d. administrative fine; and/ore. revocation of license.

CHAPTER VI

FULFILLMENT OF CHILDREN'S RIGHTS

Part One

General

Article 42

1. The Regional Government, in efforts to fulfill Children's rights, is responsible for:  a. establishing and implementing policies and programs/activities for the fulfillment of Children's rights;
b. respecting and guaranteeing the human rights of every Child without distinguishing ethnicity, religion, race, class, gender, ethnic group, culture and language, the legal status of the Child, the birth order of the Child, and physical and/or mental condition;
c. strengthening and developing service provider institutions for improving family quality in realizing Children's rights;
d. guaranteeing the Protection, care, and welfare of Children by taking into account the rights and obligations of parents, guardians, or other persons legally responsible for the Child;
e. providing facilities and infrastructure in the implementation of the fulfillment of Children's rights;
f. guaranteeing the right of Children to express opinions in accordance with the age and level of intelligence of the Child;
g. conducting supervision and guidance in the context of implementing the fulfillment of Children's rights;

h. cooperating with private parties and/or NGOs in the implementation of Child Protection;

2. The responsibility of the Regional Government in efforts for Child Protection as referred to in paragraph (1) is carried out by SKPD in accordance with their duties and functions.

Part Two

Birth Registration

Article 43

1. The Regional Government is obligated to administer birth registration of Children by issuing birth certificates.
2. Birth certificates as referred to in paragraph (1) are administered free of charge.

Part Three

Education

Article 44

The Regional Government guarantees the implementation of a 9 (nine) year compulsory education program for Children aged 7 (seven) to 15 (fifteen) years.

Article 45

1. Every organizer of an education unit is obligated to provide opportunities for Children to obtain educational services without discrimination. (2) Every organizer of an education unit is prohibited from expelling Children from educational institutions unless there is a guarantee for the continuity of the Child's education.
3. In the event that a Child victim of Violence experiences dropping out of school, the organizer of the education unit is obligated to coordinate with relevant agencies.

Article 46

1. Every Child has the right to obtain education and teaching in the context of developing their personality and level of intelligence in accordance with their interests and talents.
2. Every Child has the right to receive Protection in education units from sexual crimes and Violence committed by educators, education personnel, fellow students, and/or other parties.

Article 47

1. Children with disabilities and Children with special needs are given equal opportunities to obtain formal, non-formal and informal educational services, including inclusive education programs.
2. The Regional Government provides teaching staff for inclusive education at every education unit.

Part Four

Health and Social

Article 48

1. The Regional Government is obligated to provide comprehensive health services for Children, so that every Child obtains optimal health status from the womb.
2. Comprehensive health services as referred to in paragraph (1) include:a. promotive, preventive, curative and rehabilitative efforts for basic health services;
b. exemption from cost burdens for malnourished Children, Children with disabilities, Children with special needs, Children infected with HIV/AIDS, child workers, Children victims of abuse of narcotics, alcohol, psychotropic substances and other addictive substances, Children who are victims of Violence, and Children victims of human trafficking; and (3) The Regional Government, the community, families and parents are obligated to endeavor so that Children who are born avoid diseases that threaten survival and/or cause disability.

Article 49

1. Promotive efforts for basic health services as referred to in Article 46 paragraph (2) letter a are aimed at improving health status more optimally.
2. Promotive efforts as referred to in paragraph (1) are carried out by providing socialization or counseling to the community regarding healthy lifestyles which include:a. the importance of exclusive breastfeeding for infants;
b. the importance of immunization for toddlers;
c. clean living patterns;
d. the importance of nutritious food;
e. the dangers of drugs and smoking; and (3) In addition to promotive efforts as referred to in paragraph (2), the Regional Government may carry out other activities in the context of health administration.

Article 50

1. Preventive efforts for basic health services as referred to in Article 46 paragraph (2) letter a are aimed at preventing the occurrence of disease or health problems.
2. Efforts as referred to in paragraph (1) are carried out by:  a. requiring mothers to provide exclusive breastfeeding to infants up to the age of 6 (six) months;
b. providing free immunization and vitamins to infants and toddlers;
c. providing nutritious food to toddlers from underprivileged families;
d. optimizing the role of posyandu for health services for mothers and toddlers;
e. conducting periodic health examinations; andf. providing sanitation facilities and clean water at every school.

Article 51

1. Curative efforts for basic health services as referred to in Article 46 paragraph (2) letter a are aimed at preventing disease from becoming more severe through treatment.
2. Treatment as referred to in paragraph (1) is carried out free of charge for Children from underprivileged families.

Article 52

1. Rehabilitative efforts for basic health services as referred to in Article 46 paragraph (2) letter a are aimed at maintaining and restoring conditions after illness.
2. Rehabilitative efforts as referred to in paragraph (1) are carried out through periodic health examinations.

Article 53

1. The Regional Government is obligated to administer Social Welfare for:  a. Children in conflict with the law;
b. Children victims of Violence who are exploited economically and sexually, neglected Children, Children victims of kidnapping and Children victims of human trafficking;
c. Children who do not have parents and are cared for by parties or families who are unable;
d. Children victims of natural disasters or social disasters; ande. Children with disabilities;
2. Social welfare as referred to in paragraph (1) may be in the form of providing services:a. health;
b. education;
c. legal aid;
d. assistance;
e. social, mental and spiritual guidance;
f. social rehabilitation; and/org. reintegration.

Part Five

Employment

Article 54

The Regional Government conducts periodic supervision of every person/entity that employs Children in efforts for Protection of child workers.

Article 55

Every person/entity that employs Children must pay attention to the requirements:
a. minimum age of 15 (fifteen) years;
b. obtain written consent from the parent/guardian of the child worker;
c. have a written employment agreement between the employer and the parent/guardian of the child worker and obtain validation from the authorized agency;
d. not employ Children at night;
e. maximum working time of 3 (three) hours per day;

Article 56

In the event that Children are employed together with adult workers, then the workplace of Children must be separated from the workplace of adult workers.

Article 57

1. Every person/entity is prohibited from employing and involving Children in the worst forms of work.
2. The worst forms of work referred to in paragraph (1) include:
a. all work in the form of slavery or similar practices;
b. all work that exploits, provides, or offers Children for prostitution, pornography production, pornographic performances, or gambling;
c. all work that exploits, provides, or involves Children for the production and trade of alcoholic beverages, narcotics, psychotropic substances, and other addictive substances; and/or
d. all work that endangers the health, safety, or morals of Children.

Part Six

Facilitation of the Child Justice System

Article 58

1. Facilitation of the Child justice system includes criminal and civil justice.
2. Facilitation of the criminal justice system as referred to in paragraph (1) includes:
a. provision of companions or Child volunteers during examination by investigators;
b. provision of legal and psychological assistance services;
c. involving local police members/investigators in efforts to prevent criminal acts committed by Children as well as Children as victims;
d. realizing restorative justice processes for Children in conflict with the law; and
e. rehabilitation and social reintegration services;
3. Fulfillment of Children's rights in the civil justice system as referred to in paragraph (1) includes the provision and delivery of consultation and legal assistance services during the judicial process.
4. In facilitating the Child justice system as referred to in paragraphs (1) and (2), the authorized SKPD is the SKPD responsible for the Protection of Women and Children

Part Seven

Children's Forum

Article 59

1. In organizing the fulfillment of Children's rights to express opinions and participate, the Regional Government facilitates the establishment of a Children's Forum.
2. The Children's Forum as referred to in paragraph (1) represents Children in the Region, both representing the geographical domicile of Children, components of Children's socio-cultural groups and Children's educational backgrounds.
3. In every formulation of policies related to Children, the Regional Government must consider and accommodate the opinions of Children submitted through the Children's participation Forum.
4. Further provisions regarding the establishment of the Children's Forum as referred to in paragraphs (1) through (3) shall be stipulated by Regent's Decree.

Part Eight

Child-Friendly Regency

Article 60

1. In the context of fulfilling Children's rights in an integrated, systematic and sustainable manner, the Regional Government establishes a KLA Development policy.
2. The KLA Development policy contains:
a. the KLA concept concerning objectives, strategies and roles of related parties;
b. Children's rights; and
c. the KLA development approach.

Article 61

1. The KLA development policy is directed at fulfilling Children's rights which are divided into 5 (five) groups, including:
a. civil rights and freedoms;
b. family environment and alternative care;
c. basic health and welfare;
d. education, leisure time utilization, and cultural activities; and
e. Special Protection.
2. Further provisions regarding the KLA development policy as referred to in paragraph (1) shall be regulated by Regent's Regulation.

Article 62

1. In order to ensure the effectiveness of implementing the KLA policy in the Region, a KLA Task Force is established.
2. The establishment and membership composition of the KLA Task Force shall be stipulated by Regent's Decree.
3. The KLA Task Force is domiciled at the Office of Women's Empowerment and Family Planning.
4. The KLA Task Force as referred to in paragraph (1) has the principal tasks of:
a. coordinating the implementation of KLA policies and development;
b. conducting socialization, advocacy and communication, information and education of KLA policies;
c. collecting basic data for KLA development;
d. conducting needs analysis sourced from basic data;
e. disseminating basic data;
f. determining the focus and priority of programs in realizing KLA, adjusted to regional potential;
g. preparing a 5 (five) year Child-Friendly City regional action plan and work mechanisms; and
h. conducting monitoring, evaluation and reporting at least once (1) a year.

Article 63

In carrying out its duties, the KLA Task Force may coordinate and cooperate with PPT institutions and other institutions that provide Child Protection services.

CHAPTER VII

CHILD PROTECTION

Article 64

The Regional Government, in order to provide protection to Children:
a. implements and supports national policies in organizing Child Protection in the Region;
b. guarantees Children to exercise their rights in expressing opinions in accordance with the age and intelligence level of Children;
c. conducts prevention of Violence against Children by optimizing the role of customary institutions;
d. conducts socialization, seminars and counseling regarding Protection and rights of Children;
e. develops child-friendly school models;
f. provides public facilities that are friendly and safe for Children
g. encourages public awareness of the importance of Protection for Children;

Article 65

Service efforts carried out by the Regional Government for child victims of acts of Violence as referred to in Articles 42 through 64 apply mutatis mutandis to service efforts for child victims of acts of Violence.

CHAPTER VIII

PARTICIPATION OF THE COMMUNITY, AND PARENTS AND/OR FAMILY

Part One

Community

Article 66

1. Community participation in efforts to Protect women and Children:
a. participate in prevention and provide support for the development of prevention programs for violations of the rights of women and Children;
b. provide information and/or report violations of the rights of women and Children to the competent authorities; and
c. supervise the law enforcement process regarding cases of violations of the rights of women and Children.
2. Ninik mamak, alim ulama and cadiak pandai as well as bundo kanduang are responsible for protecting and preventing violations of the rights of women and Children.
3. Community participation as referred to in paragraphs (1) and (2) is based on laws and regulations and the values of local wisdom of the local community.

Article 67

1. In the context of preventing violations of women's rights, the community:
a. monitors various conditions occurring in their environment that may give rise to violations of women's rights;

b. increases awareness of violations of women's rights;
c. strengthens the role of community, religious and family environment organizations;

d. supervises the implementation of Women's Empowerment and Child Protection organized by the Regional Government through mechanisms for conveying aspirations to the Regent or regional people's representative council;

2. In the context of preventing violations of Children's rights, the community:
a. monitors various conditions occurring in their environment that give rise to violations of Children's rights;

b. increases awareness of violations of Children's rights;
c. strengthens the role of community, religious and family environment organizations; and prevents early marriage.

Part Two

Parents and/or Family

Article 68

The family in efforts to Protect women's rights plays a role in:
a. protecting women from conflict and Violence in the household that degrades the dignity of women;
b. providing equal opportunities for women to develop themselves in the fields of education, social, economic, political and cultural matters; and
c. preventing violations of women's rights in the household.

Article 69

1. Parents in the effort to Protect the rights of Children have the role of:  a. caring for, maintaining, educating, and protecting Children;
b. developing Children according to their abilities, talents, and interests;
c. providing formal and non-formal education;
d. providing character education and instilling moral values in Children;
e. guiding Children to behave in accordance with religion, ethics and morals as well as customs; andf. preventing the occurrence of marriage at an early age.
2. In the event that parents do not exist, or their whereabouts are unknown, or due to a certain reason, cannot carry out their obligations and responsibilities, then the obligations and responsibilities as referred to in paragraph (1) may be transferred to the family.

CHAPTER IX

GUIDANCE AND SUPERVISION

Article 70

1. The Regional Government is obligated to conduct guidance and supervision of the implementation of Protection of women and Children victims of Violence. (2) Guidance as referred to in paragraph (1), includes:  a. guidelines and fulfillment standards;
b. technical guidance and training;
c. provision of facilities;
d. monitoring; ande. evaluation.
3. Guidance as referred to in paragraph (2) is in order to realize the objectives of Protection of women and Children victims of Violence in accordance with minimum service standards.

Article 71

1. Supervision of the implementation of Child Protection as regulated in this Regional Regulation is carried out by the Regional Government Supervisory Agency of Padang Pariaman Regency and P2TP2A;
2. In carrying out supervision P2TP2A has the authority to recommend cases to law enforcement officials, government agencies and other community institutions as regulated in the Law (3) Recommendations submitted by P2TP2A must be followed up by law enforcement officials, government agencies, community institutions and other related individuals.

CHAPTER X

FINANCING

Article 72

Financing for the implementation of programs and activities for Women's Empowerment and Child Protection is sourced from:
a. regional revenue and expenditure budget; andb. other lawful sources in accordance with the provisions of laws and regulations.

CHAPTER XI

CLOSING PROVISIONS

Article 73

Implementing regulations of this Regional Regulation shall be stipulated no later than 6 (six) months from the promulgation of this Regional Regulation.

Article 74

This Regional Regulation shall come into force on the date of promulgation, so that everyone may know of it, orders the promulgation of this Regional Regulation by placing it in the Regional Gazette of Padang Pariaman Regency.
