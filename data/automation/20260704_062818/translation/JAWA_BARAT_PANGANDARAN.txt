EMPOWERMENT AND PROTECTION OF WOMEN
BY THE GRACE OF GOD ALMIGHTY
REGENT OF PANGANDARAN,
Considering : a. that in order to improve the position, role and quality of women is an effort to realize gender equality and justice in family, community, national and state life;
b. that all forms of violence against women constitute a violation of human rights so that women need to be protected in their dignity and honor and guaranteed their right to life in accordance with their nature and destiny without discrimination and empowered so that they can actualize their potential optimally;
c. that to protect the community and realize equity and justice, as an obligation of the regional government to provide protection and welfare for women, a legal foundation is needed in the implementation of empowerment and protection of women;
d. that based on the considerations as referred to in letter a, letter b and letter c, it is necessary to establish a Regional Regulation concerning Empowerment and Protection of Women.
In View of : 1. Article 18 paragraph (6) of the 1945 Constitution of the Republic of Indonesia;

CHAPTER I
GENERAL PROVISIONS
Article 1
In this Regional Regulation, what is meant by: 1. Regency Area is Pangandaran Regency. 2. Regent is the Regent of Pangandaran.
3. Regency Regional Government is the Regent as an element of regional government administration who leads the implementation of government affairs that are the authority of the autonomous region.
4. Regional Apparatus is an auxiliary element of the Regent and Regional House of Representatives in the administration of government affairs that are the authority of the Region.
5. Women are adult humans of female sex and persons recognized by law as Women.
6. Prevention is activities carried out to prevent the occurrence of acts of violence against Women. 7. Women's Empowerment is every effort to improve physical, mental spiritual, social capabilities, knowledge and skills so that Women are ready to be utilized according to their respective capabilities. 8. Protection is all efforts aimed at providing a sense of security to victims carried out by family, advocates, social institutions, police, prosecutors, courts, or other parties either temporarily. 9. Protection of Women is all activities aimed at providing a sense of security carried out by the Regional Government, community, and/or other parties who know or hear that violence against Women will or has occurred.
10. Victim is a Woman who experiences misery and/or suffering either directly or indirectly as a result of violence and life vulnerability.
11. Violence is any act that results in or can result in misery or suffering whether physical, sexual, economic, social and psychological to the victim.
12. Victim of Violence is an individual or group of people who experience physical, mental or emotional suffering, economic loss, or experience neglect, reduction or deprivation of their basic rights, as a result of human rights violations or criminal acts as well as acts of Violence committed either by state apparatus, Regional Government apparatus, or individuals.
13. Violence Against Women is any action that results in or may result in misery or suffering to Women physically, sexually, economically, socially, psychologically including threats of jdih.pangandarankab.go.id 3 certain actions, coercion or deprivation of freedom, whether occurring in public or private life. 14. Physical Violence is any act that causes pain, injury, wounds or disability to a person's body, miscarriage or fainting and/or causes death.
15. Psychological Violence is an act that results in fear, loss of self-confidence, loss of ability to act, distrust or severe psychological suffering to a person.
16. Sexual Violence is any act in the form of sexual harassment, forced sexual intercourse, whether unnatural or unwanted with another person for commercial purposes and/or certain purposes.
17. Domestic Neglect is a person who neglects a person within their household scope, whereas according to the law applicable to them or because of an agreement or contract they are obliged to provide life, care, or maintenance to that person. In addition, neglect also applies to every person who causes economic dependence by means of restricting and/or prohibiting decent work inside or outside the home so that the victim is under the control of that person.
18. Trafficking in Persons is the act of recruitment, transportation, harboring, sending, transfer or receipt of a person by threat of violence, use of violence, kidnapping, confinement, forgery, fraud, abuse of power or vulnerable position, debt bondage or giving payment or benefits, so as to obtain consent from the person holding control over that other person, whether carried out within the country or between countries, for the purpose of exploitation or resulting in the person being exploited.
19. Exploitation is an act that exploits economically or sexually with the intention of benefiting oneself or others, acts with or without the victim's consent which include but are not limited to prostitution, forced labor or service, slavery or similar practices, oppression, extortion, utilization of physical, sexual, reproductive organs, or unlawfully removing or transplanting organs and/or body tissues or utilizing the labor or ability of a person by another party to obtain material or immaterial benefits and/or any form of utilization of sexual organs or other body organs of the victim to obtain benefits, including but not limited to all activities of prostitution or sexual abuse.
jdih.pangandarankab.go.id 4
20. Domestic Violence is any act against a Woman that results in the emergence of misery or suffering physically, sexually, psychologically and/or Domestic Neglect including threats to commit acts, coercion or deprivation of freedom unlawfully within the domestic environment.
21. Family is a person who has a blood relationship in a straight line upward or downward and collateral line up to the third degree, or who has a marital relationship, or a person who is a dependent of the Woman.
22. Parents are biological father and/or mother, or stepfather and/or stepmother, or adoptive father and/or mother. 23. Discrimination Against Women is any distinction, exclusion or restriction made on the basis of sex which has the effect or purpose of reducing or eliminating the recognition, enjoyment or exercise of human rights and fundamental freedoms in the political, economic, social, cultural, civil or any other field by Women, regardless of their marital status on the basis of equality between men and Women.
24. All Forms of Discrimination Against Women are all forms of discrimination that include territorial dimensions, age dimensions, and special dimensions.
25. Gender is a concept that refers to the differentiation of roles and responsibilities of men and Women that occur as a result of and can be changed by the social and cultural circumstances of society.
26. Gender Equality is the equality of conditions for men and Women to obtain opportunities and rights as human beings so as to be able to play a role and participate in political, economic, socio-cultural, defense and security activities, and equality in enjoying the results of development.
27. Gender Justice is a process to be fair to men and Women.
28. Health Rehabilitation is an effort to restore the Victim's condition including physical, psychological, and sexual health so that the Victim can carry out their role again properly and naturally both in the family and in society.
29. Social Rehabilitation is a service aimed at restoring and developing the ability of a person experiencing social dysfunction so that they can carry out their social functions naturally.
30. Repatriation is an effort to return Victims of Violence to their area of origin.
jdih.pangandarankab.go.id 5
31. Social Reintegration is an effort to reunite Victims with family, substitute family, or community that can provide Protection and fulfillment of needs for Victims.
32. Integrated Service Center for Women and Children Empowerment hereinafter referred to as P2TP2A is an integrated service unit established by the Regional Government to carry out the functions and main tasks of integrated services for Women and child victims of violence, including complaint services, Health Rehabilitation, Social Rehabilitation, Repatriation, Social Reintegration, and legal assistance.
33. Minimum Service Standards hereinafter abbreviated as SPM are performance benchmarks for integrated service units in providing services for handling reports, complaints, health services, Social Rehabilitation, law enforcement and legal assistance, as well as Repatriation and Social Reintegration for Women Victims of Violence.

CHAPTER II
WOMEN'S RIGHTS
Part One
General

Article 2
Every Woman has the right:
a. to life;
b. to form a family and continue descendants;
c. to self-development;
d. to obtain justice;
e. to personal freedom;
f. to security;
g. to welfare; and
h. to participate in government.

Second Part
Rights of Women with Disabilities

Article 3
Every Woman with disabilities has the right to obtain:
a. education at all units, pathways, types and levels of education;
b. decent work and livelihood in accordance with the type and degree of disability, education and ability;
c. equal treatment to play a role in development and enjoy its results;
d. accessibility in the context of independence;
jdih.pangandarankab.go.id 6
e. rehabilitation, social assistance and maintenance of social welfare standards; and
f. equal rights to develop talents, abilities and social life.

Third Part
Rights of Women Victims of Violence

Article 4
Every female victim of violence has the right: a. to have her dignity and worth as a human being respected; b. health rehabilitation;
c. to determine her own decisions;
d. to obtain information;
e. to confidentiality;
f. to compensation;
g. to social rehabilitation;
h. to complaint handling;
i. to obtain facilitation in the judicial process; and j. to assistance.

CHAPTER III
WOMEN'S EMPOWERMENT
Article 5
Women's empowerment is directed to obtain opportunities and rights as human beings in order to be able to play a role and participate in the fields of:
a. economy;
b. social culture;
c. politics and government;
d. law;
e. education;
f. health;
g. employment; and
h. social security.

First Part
Economic Sector

Article 6
Empowerment of women in the economic field is implemented through:
a. provision of skills and job training;
b. facilitation of the formation of productive economic business groups;
c. facilitation of strengthening and development of productive economic business groups;
d. facilitation and capital assistance; and
e. facilitation of marketing network development.
jdih.pangandarankab.go.id 7

Second Part
Social and Cultural Sector

Article 7
Empowerment of women in the social and cultural field is implemented through;
a. improvement of knowledge, attitudes and skills to encourage the fulfillment of tiered education in accordance with potential to improve social status;
b. improvement of awareness, willingness and ability to overcome health problems through quality promotive, preventive, curative, and rehabilitative efforts primarily in the field of reproductive health;
c. improvement of awareness and knowledge about independent, healthy and prosperous family planning including access to consultation services and marriage registration; and
d. facilitation and efforts to preserve customs and traditions and the development of science, technology, arts and culture for the advancement of women.

Third Part
Political and Governmental Sector

Article 8
(1) Empowerment of women in the field of politics and government includes:
a. involvement of women in decision-making at various levels;
b. provision of opportunities for women to hold public office;
c. participation in general elections; and
d. self-development through organizations to associate, assemble and express opinions.
(2) Empowerment of women in the field of politics and government as referred to in paragraph (1) shall be implemented in accordance with laws and regulations.
(3) The Regional Government provides guarantees to women to have the right to vote and/or be elected in general elections, regional head elections, village head elections and/or other political office elections based on equal rights through direct, general, free, secret, honest and fair voting according to laws and regulations.
(4) The Regional Government provides opportunities for women to be appointed as regional government officials and occupy strategic positions in regional government; and jdih.pangandarankab.go.id 8 (5) The Regional Government and/or Political Parties are responsible for providing political education for women.

Fourth Part
Legal Field

Article 9
(1) Empowerment of women in the field of law includes: a. increasing awareness and knowledge in the field of law through communication, information and education services; and
b. facilitation of access and legal consultation services. (2) Empowerment of women in the field of law as referred to in paragraph (1) shall be implemented in accordance with laws and regulations.

Fifth Part
Education Sector

Article 10
Empowerment of women in the field of education through: a. provision of formal and nonformal education for women in the Region.
b. nonformal education aimed at improving women's capabilities.
c. nonformal education as referred to in letter b, includes women's empowerment education, skills education and work training.
d. formal and nonformal education as referred to in letter a, is regulated by a separate Regional Regulation.

Sixth Part
Health Sector

Article 11
(1) Women have the right to obtain health services in order to undergo reproductive life and sexual life that is healthy, safe, and free from coercion and/or violence.
(2) Health services as referred to in paragraph (1) include:
a. health services for the period before pregnancy, childbirth and after giving birth;
b. pregnancy regulation, contraceptive services and sexual health; and
c. reproductive health services.
d. health services implemented through promotive, preventive, curative and rehabilitative approaches.
jdih.pangandarankab.go.id 9

Seventh Part
Employment Sector

Article 12
(1) Women have equal opportunity without discrimination to obtain employment.
(2) Employers who employ female workers, are obligated to provide protection that includes welfare, safety and health.

Eighth Part
Social Security Sector

Article 13
(1) The Regional Government is responsible for providing guidance facilities for women with social problems.
(2) The Regional Government provides the right to social security for women with social problems.
(3) The responsibility for providing guidance facilities for women with social problems is under the regional apparatus organization that handles women's empowerment affairs.

CHAPTER IV
PROTECTION OF WOMEN
Part One
General

Article 14
Implementation of protection for women is provided to: a. women workers/laborers;
b. elderly women;
c. women with disabilities;
d. homeless women;
e. domestic women workers;
f. women as family economic supporters;
g. women former inmates of correctional institutions;
h. women victims of disasters;
i. women commercial sex workers; and
j. women victims of violence.

First Part
Female Workers/Laborers

Article 15
(1) Implementation of protection for female workers/laborers to provide protection from acts of economic exploitation and cruel, inhumane treatment and to pursue proper appreciation for work.
jdih.pangandarankab.go.id 10 (2) Female workers/laborers as referred to in paragraph (1) receive protection for:
a. recognition of rights, wages and decent working conditions;
b. health insurance and social security for employment;
c. access to information and legal consultation services;
d. opportunities to obtain knowledge and skills to improve status.

Second Part
Elderly Women

Article 16
(1) The implementation of protection for elderly women in order to extend life expectancy so that they can carry out their social functions and play an active role naturally in their social life.
(2) Elderly women as referred to in paragraph (1) receive the right to Protection for:
a. guarantee of continuity and personal self-development;
b. participation in the fields of education, health, employment, politics, arts and culture as well as the utilization of information and communication technology;
c. accessibility to information, legal assistance, mobility, social services; and
d. early intervention efforts including treatment and rehabilitation to improve their functions and capacities.

Third Part
Women with Disabilities

Article 17
(1) Implementation of Protection for women with disabilities in order to provide protection and prevention against forms of discrimination including freedom from cruel treatment, inhuman treatment, degrading human dignity and freedom from exploitation;
(2) Women with disabilities as referred to in paragraph (1) shall receive the right to protection for:
a. guarantee of continuity and personal self-development;
b. participation in the fields of education, health, employment, politics, sports, arts and culture as well as utilization of information and communication technology;
c. accessibility of information, legal aid, mobility, social services; and
d. early intervention efforts including treatment and rehabilitation to improve their function and capacity.
jdih.pangandarankab.go.id 11

Fourth Part
Homeless Women

Article 18
(1) The implementation of protection for homeless women is in the context of providing protection and prevention against forms of violence caused by the absence of shelter or permanent residence.
(2) Homeless women as referred to in paragraph (1) shall receive the right to protection for:
a. decent housing;
b. health and social security;
c. population administration registration;
d. opportunities to obtain skills to improve economic status; and
e. access to information and legal consultation services.

Part Five
Female Domestic Workers

Article 19
(1) The implementation of Protection for female domestic workers is in order to provide protection and prevention against forms of discrimination, cruel treatment, inhumane treatment, degradation of human dignity, and exploitation in employment relations.
(2) Female domestic workers as referred to in paragraph (1) shall receive protection for:
a. recognition of rights, wages, and decent working conditions;
b. health insurance and social security;
c. access to information and legal consultation services; and
d. opportunities to obtain knowledge and skills to improve economic status.

Sixth Part
Women as the Economic Backbone of the Family

Article 20
(1) The implementation of protection for women as family economic pillars is in the framework of providing protection and prevention against forms of discrimination, negative stigma and economic empowerment for women as family economic pillars.
(2) Women as family economic pillars as referred to in paragraph (1) shall receive the right to protection against:
a. discriminatory actions and efforts to weaken the function of women as family economic pillars;

b. strengthening of capacity and recognition as family economic pillars in their environment; and
c. accessibility to economic and financial institutions.

Seventh Part
Women Former Inmates of Correctional Institutions

Article 21
(1) The implementation of protection for women former inmates of Correctional Institutions is in order to provide protection from obstacles to adjusting themselves in community life, so that they can carry out life normally.
(2) Women former inmates of Correctional Institutions as referred to in paragraph (1) receive protection for:
a. social rehabilitation and reintegration; and
b. opportunities to obtain knowledge and skills to improve economic status.

Part Eight
Women Victims of Disaster

Article 22
(1) Protection Implementers for women victims of disaster in order to provide protection during disaster and post-disaster.
(2) Women victims of disaster as referred to in paragraph (1) receive protection for:
a. access to proper housing;
b. health services;
c. information access services and legal consultation; and d. post-disaster recovery.

Ninth Section

(2) Protection for female commercial sex workers as referred to in paragraph (1) includes:
a. providing life skills capabilities;
b. facilitating reproductive health services; and c. preventing the occurrence of human trafficking.

CHAPTER V
OBLIGATIONS AND RESPONSIBILITIES
Part One
General

Article 25
The empowerment and protection of women is a joint obligation and responsibility of:
a. Regional Government;
b. community,
c. family; and
d. parents.

Part Two
Obligations and Responsibilities of Regional Government

Article 26
(1) The Regional Government has the obligation and responsibility for the empowerment and protection of women. (2) The efforts for empowerment and protection of women as referred to in paragraph (1), include:
a. determination of policies, programs, and activities;
b. determination of implementation guidelines;
c. provision of services; and
d. coordination of policies, programs and activities.
jdih.pangandarankab.go.id 14 (3) The obligations and responsibilities of the Regional Government as referred to in Article 25 letter a include: a. implementing policies for the Protection of Women from all forms of discrimination and acts of violence; b. determining policies, programs, and activities for the Empowerment and Protection of Women that have been established;

d. conducting education on matters related to the Protection of Women Victims of Violence and Discrimination;

CHAPTER VI

CHAPTER VII
PREVENTION, PROTECTION SERVICES, AND RECOVERY OF VICTIMS OF VIOLENCE AGAINST WOMEN

Part One
Prevention of Violence Against Women

Article 33
Regional Government in preventing acts of violence against women:
a. conducts preparation and socialization regarding matters related to violence against women;
b. conducts socialization on women's rights;
c. endeavors to improve education for women;
d. opens skills courses for women to support family economic needs;
e. creates employment opportunities for women;
f. provides counseling related to violence against women;
g. empowers social community organizations and women's organizations at regional, sub-district, and village levels to increase women's knowledge and insight;
h. establishes complaint posts for women's protection; and
i. forms networks in efforts to prevent violence against women.
jdih.pangandarankab.go.id 18

Second Part
Protection Services

Article 34
(1) Protection services to women are provided in the form of:
a. protection of the victim's personal security from physical and mental threats;
b. health services according to medical needs; c. medical services for evidentiary purposes in the legal field;
d. maintaining the confidentiality of the victim's identity;
e. providing testimony during court hearings without facing the suspect; f. spiritual guidance services; and/or
g. protection for Women who work as laborers or workers in accordance with the provisions of laws and regulations.
(2) Protection of the victim's personal security from physical and mental threats as referred to in paragraph (1) letter a is carried out by family, law enforcement officials, social institutions, and other parties either temporarily or based on a Protection order issued by the court.

Article 35
(1) In providing protection services as referred to in Article 34 paragraph (1), the Regional Government:
a. provides services and handling as quickly as possible to witnesses and/or victims;
b. provides convenience, comfort, safety, and free of charge for witnesses and/or victims;
c. maintains confidentiality of witnesses and/or victims;
d. guarantees justice and legal certainty for witnesses and/or victims;
e. provision of apparatus, health workers, social workers, and spiritual counselors;
f. creation and development of a system and mechanism for cooperation of service programs involving parties that are easily accessible to victims;
g. provides protection for companions, witnesses, families, and friends of victims;
h. provides decent work and livelihood in accordance with the type and degree of disability, education, and ability; and
i. provides protection for female workers or laborers in accordance with the provisions of protection for laborers or workers in accordance with statutory provisions.
jdih.pangandarankab.go.id 19 (2) The Regional Government in providing protection services as referred to in paragraph (1) may cooperate with the central government, provincial government, other district/city governments, the community, and/or other social institutions.
(3) Cooperation as referred to in paragraph (2) shall be set forth in a cooperation agreement.

Article 36
(1) The provision of services for the Protection of Women as referred to in Article 35 consists of identification of Victims, legal aid, medical rehabilitation, psychosocial rehabilitation, Social Reintegration, repatriation assistance, or other forms.
(2) The provision of services for the Protection of Women as referred to in paragraph (1) is provided for Women Victims of Violence including Victims of Human Trafficking, Women in disaster-prone areas, Women workers, elderly Women, Women with disabilities, Women Victims of sexual exploitation, Women heads of households, and Women from other vulnerable groups.

Article 37
Every person related to the implementation of Women's Protection who knows of the occurrence of acts of Violence against Women but allows it, does not provide Protection to Victims, and/or does not report to the relevant agency may be subject to administrative sanctions in the form of:
a. written warning;
b. temporary suspension from activities;
c. termination of cooperation;
d. revocation of registration certificate;
e. revocation of operational permit;
f. withdrawal of facilities; and
g. administrative fine.

Third Part
Recovery

Article 38
(1) For the purpose of recovery, victims have the right to obtain: a. health services;
b. victim assistance;
c. counseling;
d. spiritual guidance; and
e. resocialization.
jdih.pangandarankab.go.id 20 (2) For the purpose of recovery as referred to in paragraph (1), the Regional Government is obligated to provide: a. expert and professional personnel;
b. service centers and safe houses; and
c. other facilities and infrastructure necessary for victim recovery.
(3) Expert and professional personnel as referred to in paragraph (2) letter b consist of:
a. health workers;
b. social workers;
c. volunteer assistants; and/or
d. spiritual counselors.
(4) Health workers as referred to in paragraph (3) letter a are obligated to examine victims in accordance with their professional standards, restore, and rehabilitate the health of victims if the victims require treatment.
(5) Social workers, volunteer assistants, and spiritual counselors as referred to in paragraph (3) letter b, letter c, and letter d are obligated to provide services to victims in the form of providing counseling to strengthen and/or provide a sense of security for victims.
(6) Violations of the provisions as referred to in paragraph (4) and paragraph (5) may be subject to administrative sanctions in the form of:
a. written warning;
b. temporary suspension from activities;
c. termination of cooperation;
d. revocation of registered certificate;
e. revocation of operational permit;
f. withdrawal of facilities; and
g. administrative fine.
(7) Provisions regarding the procedures for implementing administrative sanctions as referred to in paragraph (6) are regulated in a Regent Regulation.

CONCERNING
EMPOWERMENT AND PROTECTION OF WOMEN

I. GENERAL
Fundamentally, Regions are formed to improve public services and realize community welfare, therefore in the implementation of autonomy, regions have obligations including protecting the community, maintaining national unity, integrity and harmony.

The unequal distribution of opportunities in all fields of life including in the fields of economy, social culture, politics and governance, law, education, health, employment and social security, which can give rise to various social problems primarily towards women. The reality shows that in the Region there are still acts of violence both physical, psychological, sexual and this constitutes a violation of human rights. Violence against women is a violation of human rights so that their dignity and honor need to be protected and their right to life is guaranteed in accordance with their nature and destiny without discrimination. The fate of women victims of violence must be given attention by regional governments. The high number of cases of violence against women are often caused by factors that develop in society, for example low levels of economy, education, environments located in the industrial sector. Therefore, victims of violence such as this need to receive protection in accordance with the principles of justice, truth, legal certainty, equality and upholding human rights.

Regional Governments are obligated and responsible to implement efforts to protect women victims of violence through: implementing policies for the protection of women victims of violence applied by the government, establishing policies, programs, and activities for the protection of women victims of violence, establishment of P2TP2A, guaranteeing the implementation of ease of service to victims, seeking effectiveness and efficiency for the victim recovery process, and seeking the creation of cooperation and coordination in victim recovery efforts.
