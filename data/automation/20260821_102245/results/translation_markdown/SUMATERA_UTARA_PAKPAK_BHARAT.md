# CHAPTER I

## GENERAL PROVISIONS

#### Article 1

In this Regional Regulation, the following terms shall mean:

1. Region is Pakpak Bharat Regency.
2. Regional Government is the Regional Head as an element of regional government administration who leads the implementation of government affairs that are the authority of the autonomous region.
3. Regent is the Regent of Pakpak Bharat.
4. Regional House of Representatives hereinafter abbreviated as DPRD is a regional people's representative institution positioned as an element of Regional Government administration.
5. Social Service is the Social Service of Pakpak Bharat Regency.
6. Discrimination is any distinction, harassment or exclusion, whether direct or indirect, based on human differentiation on the grounds of religion, ethnicity, race, ethnic group, social status, economic status, gender, language, political beliefs resulting in the reduction, deviation or elimination of recognition, implementation or use of human rights and fundamental freedoms in life both individually and collectively in the political, economic, legal, social, cultural and other aspects of life.
7. Trafficking in persons is the act of recruitment, transportation, harboring, sending, transfer, or receipt of a person by threat of violence, use of violence, abduction, confinement, forgery, fraud and abuse of power or position of vulnerability, debt bondage or giving payment or benefits, so as to obtain consent from the person holding control over that other person, whether conducted within the country or between countries, for the purpose of exploitation or resulting in the person being exploited.
8. Child is a person who has not reached 18 years of age including those in the womb.
9. Violence is any act unlawfully with or without the use of means against physical and psychological that causes danger to life, body or results in the deprivation of a person's freedom.
10. Violence against women is any act that results in misery or suffering of women physically, sexually or psychologically, including threats of certain actions, coercion or deprivation of freedom arbitrarily, whether occurring in the public sphere or in private life.
11. Violence against children is any act against a child that results in misery or suffering physically, mentally, sexually, psychologically, including neglect and maltreatment that threatens bodily integrity and degrades the dignity of the child committed by parties who should be responsible for the child or those who have power over the child, who should be trusted, such as parents, close family, teachers and companions.
12. Victim is a woman and child who experiences misery and/or suffering either directly or indirectly as a result of violence.
13. Health Rehabilitation is an effort to restore the victim's condition including physical, psychological, and sexual health so that the victim can perform their role again properly and naturally both in the family and in society.
14. Social Rehabilitation is a service aimed at restoring and developing the ability of a person experiencing social dysfunction so that they can perform social functions normally.
15. Repatriation is an effort to return victims of violence from a regional area to their area of origin.
16. Social Reintegration is an effort to reunite victims with family, substitute family, or community that can provide protection and fulfillment of needs for victims.
17. Prevention is a direct effort undertaken by the regional government to prevent acts of violence against women and children.
18. Companion is a person or representative from an institution who has expertise in accompanying victims to conduct counseling, therapy and advocacy for strengthening and recovery of victims of violence.
19. Integrated Service Center hereinafter abbreviated as PPT is a functional work unit that organizes integrated services for witnesses and/or victims of violence.
20. Integrated Service Center for Women and Children Empowerment hereinafter abbreviated as P2TP2A, is an Integrated Service Unit established by the regional government to provide services to women and children victims of violence comprehensively including information services, assistance and legal aid, counseling services, medical services and safe houses through referral.
21. Safe house is a temporary residence used to provide protection to victims in accordance with determined operational standards.
22. Community is individuals, families, groups, social organizations and/or community organizations.
23. Family is the smallest unit in society consisting of husband and wife, or husband-wife and their children, or father and his children and mother and her children.
24. Household is family members and relatives such as grandchildren, nieces/nephews, older siblings, younger siblings, grandfathers, grandmothers, husbands, wives, cousins and so on and non-relatives such as domestic workers, drivers and so on who live and reside in one house.
25. Local wisdom (local wisdom) is truth that has become traditional or established in an area, local wisdom is a combination of sacred religious values and various existing values.

# CHAPTER II

## PRINCIPLES AND OBJECTIVES

#### Article 2

Protection of women and children victims of violence is organized based on the principles of:
a. respect for human rights;
b. gender equality and justice;
c. non-discriminatory;
d. protection of victims' rights;
e. legal certainty.

#### Article 3

1. The purpose of organizing protection of women and children victims of violence is to:
   a. prevent acts of violence against women and children including trafficking in persons;
   b. eliminate all forms of violence and exploitation against women and children;
   c. protect, provide a sense of security for women and children;
   d. provide services to women and children victims of acts of violence, reporters and witnesses;
   e. facilitate and mediate household disputes to realize harmonious and prosperous household integrity.
2. Violence as referred to in paragraph (1) may take the form of:
   a. physical violence;
   b. psychological violence;
   c. sexual violence;
   d. economic neglect;
   e. restriction of movement.

# CHAPTER III

## VICTIMS' RIGHTS

#### Article 4

Every woman and child victim of violence has the right to receive:
a. respect for dignity as a human being;
b. protection from family, community, Regional Government and/or other parties either temporarily or based on a protection order from the court;
c. health services according to medical needs;
d. special handling related to victim confidentiality;
e. assistance by social workers and legal aid at every level of the examination process in accordance with the provisions of laws and regulations;
f. spiritual guidance services;
g. to determine their own decisions.

# CHAPTER IV

## OBLIGATIONS AND RESPONSIBILITIES

#### Article 5

Obligations and responsibilities in providing protection to women and children victims of violence are the shared responsibility of:
a. regional government;
b. community;
c. family;
d. parents.

#### Article 6

1. The Regional Government is obligated and responsible for providing protection to women and children victims of violence.  
2. The obligations and responsibilities of the regional government as referred to in Article 5 letter a include:
   a. implementing policies for the protection of women and children victims of violence based on laws and regulations;  
   b. establishing policies, programs, and activities for the protection of women and children victims of violence;  
   c. providing support for facilities and infrastructure for the implementation of protection of women and children victims of violence;  
   d. facilitating assistance, legal aid and legal services according to the needs of victims;  
   e. allocating budget for the implementation of protection of women and children from victims of violence according to regional financial capacity; and;  
   f. fostering and supervising the implementation of protection of women and children victims of violence.  
3. In order to carry out the obligations and responsibilities as referred to in paragraph (1), the Regent establishes programs and action activities for the protection of women and children in one Regional Action Plan as a basis for OPD in implementing the protection of women and children from acts of violence.  
4. The Regional Action Plan as referred to in paragraph (3), is part of the Regional Medium-Term Development Plan (RPJMD).

#### Article 7

1. The obligations and responsibilities of the community as referred to in Article 5 letter b, are carried out in the form of community participation. (2) Forms of community participation as referred to in paragraph (1), include:
   a. preventing the occurrence of acts of violence against women and children;  
   b. providing information and/or reporting acts of violence against women and children to law enforcement or authorized parties;  
   c. participating in handling victims of acts of violence;  
   d. providing protection to victims; and  
   e. providing emergency assistance.  

#### Article 8

The obligations of family and/or parents as referred to in Article 5 letters c and d are to, as a matter of law, have full responsibility to prevent all forms of violence and to protect women and children as family members.

# CHAPTER V

## PREVENTION OF ACTS OF VIOLENCE

#### Article 9

1. To prevent the occurrence of acts of violence against women and children, the regional government conducts empowerment and awareness to families, parents, and the community by providing information, guidance and/or counseling.  
2. In addition to empowerment and awareness as referred to in paragraph (1), the regional government undertakes efforts as follows:
   a. increasing the quantity and quality of education both formal and non-formal and informal;  
   b. opening accessibility to obtain education, training, funding, income enhancement and social services;  
   c. opening employment opportunities for women;  
   d. building community participation and concern for the prevention of protection of women and children from acts of violence;  
   e. building and providing a complete and easily accessible information system;  
   f. building networks and cooperation with law enforcement apparatus, government apparatus, universities and various non-governmental organizations that work and/or care about women and children; and  
   g. opening complaint posts for the protection of women and children from acts of violence.

#### Article 10

1. Prevention of the occurrence of acts of violence against women and children as referred to in Article 9, is carried out by OPD whose duties and functions are in the fields of:
   a. health;  
   b. education;  
   c. social affairs;  
   d. women's empowerment and child protection;  
   e. peace and order.  
2. Prevention of acts of violence by OPD as referred to in paragraph (1), is carried out in an integrated and continuous manner based on the regional action plan.

# CHAPTER VI

## INSTITUTIONAL ARRANGEMENTS

#### Article 11

1. In the case of implementing protection for women and children victims of violence, the regional government may establish an Integrated Service Center (PPT).  
2. The establishment and development of PPT is adjusted to the development of priority needs, and regional financial capacity as well as the institutional capacity of personnel in the region.  
3. PPT as referred to in paragraph (1) may accommodate institutions/work units that provide services for victims of violence such as P2TP2A, Safe Houses, Child Social Protection Houses, Trauma Center Houses, Women's Social Protection Houses, Shelters and other service activities in accordance with applicable laws and regulations.  
4. Forms of services implemented by PPT, include:
   a. complaints;  
   b. health rehabilitation;  
   c. social rehabilitation;  
   d. legal aid;  
   e. repatriation;  
   f. social reintegration.  
5. In providing protection to women and children victims of violence, PPT is obligated to:
   a. provide services as quickly as possible and free of charge to victims;  
   b. organize protection and fulfillment of victims' rights to health rehabilitation, social rehabilitation, repatriation, social reintegration and legal aid;  
   c. cooperate with certain institutions in providing translators and volunteer assistants needed for victims;  
   d. network with government or private hospitals for treatment and recovery of victims' health and cooperate with witness and victim protection institutions, social protection houses or trauma centers owned by the government, community or other institutions for victims' health recovery;  
   e. provide convenience, comfort, and safety for victims;  
   f. maintain confidentiality of victims;  
   g. provide fulfillment of legal aid for victims;  
   h. in the case that integrated service implementation is carried out in a networked manner, PPT remains responsible for the entire referral process of services needed by victims.  
6. Provision of integrated services as referred to in paragraph (3) is implemented in accordance with SPM.

# CHAPTER VII

## MINIMUM SERVICE STANDARDS

#### Article 12

SPM in the field of integrated services for women and children victims of violence, includes services:

a. handling complaints/reports of victims of violence against women and children;  
b. health services for women and children victims of violence;  
c. social rehabilitation for women and children victims of violence;  
d. law enforcement and legal aid for women and children victims of violence;  
e. repatriation and social reintegration for women and children victims of violence.  

#### Article 13

SPM as referred to in Article 12 has performance indicators including:

a. coverage of women and children victims of violence who receive complaint handling, health services, social rehabilitation services, spiritual guidance services conducted by trained officers within integrated service units;  
b. coverage of law enforcement from the investigation level to court decisions on cases of violence against women and children;  
c. coverage of women and children victims of violence who receive legal aid services;  
d. coverage of repatriation services for women and children victims of violence; and  
e. coverage of social reintegration services for women and children victims of violence.

# CHAPTER VIII

## MONITORING AND EVALUATION

#### Article 14

1. To ensure synergy, continuity, and effectiveness of integrated measures in the implementation of policies, programs, and activities for the protection of women and children victims of violence, the Regional Government shall conduct monitoring.  
2. Monitoring as referred to in paragraph (1) is to determine the progress and obstacles in the implementation of policies, programs, and activities for the protection of women and children victims of violence in the region.  
3. Monitoring shall be conducted periodically through coordination and direct monitoring of regional apparatus units that implement policies, programs, and activities for the protection of women and children victims of violence.  
4. Monitoring shall be conducted from the planning stage through the implementation of policies, programs, and activities for the protection of women and children victims of violence for the current year.

#### Article 15

1. Evaluation of the implementation of policies, programs and activities for the protection of women and children shall be conducted every evaluation of the implementation of policies, programs and activities for the protection of women and children shall be conducted every end of the fiscal year or if necessary according to needs.  
2. The results of the evaluation of the implementation of policies, programs and activities for the protection of women and children shall be used as input for the formulation of policies, programs, and activities for the protection of women and children for the following year.  
3. Evaluation as referred to in paragraph (1) shall be implemented in accordance with the provisions of laws and regulations.

# CHAPTER XI

# CHAPTER XII

## COMMUNITY PARTICIPATION

#### Article 19

In implementing protection for women and children victims of violence, the community may:

a. establish family partners at the village level by the community; b. conduct independent socialization of the rights of women and children;  
c. provide first aid to victims;  
d. participate with the competent authorities when violence against victims occurs in their environment.  e. community participation shall be carried out by individuals, social community institutions, non-governmental organizations, educational institutions, religious institutions, the private sector, and mass media.

PROTECTION OF WOMEN AND CHILDREN FROM ACTS OF VIOLENCE I. GENERAL The national objectives of the Unitary State of the Republic of Indonesia, as stipulated in the Preamble of the 1945 Constitution in the fourth paragraph, include: (1) protecting the entire Indonesian nation and all Indonesian territory, (2) advancing public welfare, (3) developing the intellectual life of the nation and (4) participating in the implementation of world order based on (a) independence, (b) lasting peace, and (c) social justice.   The Republic of Indonesia is a State of Law (rechtstaat) and not a state of power (machstaat), so that all actions of its citizens, including those in the form of violence, committed by both men and women, are also regulated by law.  

The rights of women and children are recognized as Human Rights (HAM), which must be upheld and respected by all parties, however in reality there are still many women and children who become victims of violence. The commitment of the Central Government to protect children and women is reflected in the legislation below it.  

Various acts of violence against both women and children within the household and outside the household continue to increase from time to time. This condition, if without serious handling, will destroy the life of households, society, nation and state. Therefore, the government of Pakpak Bharat Regency is obliged to provide protection to women and children from acts of violence which is an implementation of various laws and regulations, including Law Number 39 of 1999 concerning Human Rights, Law Number 23 of 2002 concerning Child Protection, Law Number 23 of 2004 concerning the Elimination of Domestic Violence, Law Number 21 of 2007 concerning the Eradication of the Criminal Act of Human Trafficking.  

Protection of women and children from acts of violence is the obligation of the Regional Government as stipulated in Law Number 23 of 2014 concerning Regional Government which states that: "in carrying out regional autonomy, it has the obligation to protect the community, maintain unity, integrity and national harmony as well as the integrity of the Unitary State of the Republic of Indonesia." Based on this, the Government of Pakpak Bharat Regency has taken steps as an effort to provide protection to women and children from acts of violence, but has not been able to provide maximum and adequate protection against acts of violence that occur in the social life of the community, especially to women and children jdih.pakpakbharatkab.go.id - 13 - due to the low understanding of the community regarding the rights of women and children, handling has not been well coordinated, implementation has not been sustainable and so on.  Therefore, a regulation in the form of a Regional Regulation concerning the protection of women and children from acts of violence is needed to answer while providing legal certainty in the protection of women and children from acts of violence that occur.  

With the enactment of the Regional Regulation concerning the Protection of Women and Children from Acts of Violence, in Pakpak Bharat Regency in particular, the handling, rehabilitation and reintegration can be carried out properly and more smoothly in the process of preventing and handling violence against women and children which in its implementation the Regional Government cooperates with government agencies, other regional governments, the community and also the participation of the business world in preventing violence against women and children from the beloved Pakpak Bharat Regency.

#### Article 2

Letter a The principle of humanity becomes the foundation of the concept of protection of women and children from acts of violence, constitutes respect for human rights and the dignity of every citizen and resident of Indonesia proportionally (the second principle of Pancasila)

Letter b The principle of gender equality and justice, that gender justice is a process to be fair to men and women. Gender equality is the equality of conditions for men and women to obtain opportunities and their rights as human beings so that they are able to play a role and participate in political, economic, socio-cultural, governmental activities and equality in enjoying the results of development.  

Letter c The principle of non-discrimination, that in providing protection to women and children from acts of violence does not discriminate on the basis of age, gender, race, ethnicity, tribe, religion and between groups.  

Letter d The principle of protection of the rights of victims, that all actions concerning women and children carried out by families, communities and Regional Governments to fulfill the rights of women and children victims of acts of violence as regulated in laws and regulations.  

Letter e The principle of legal certainty is the best interests of women and children, which is a principle specific to women and children themselves, for example, how the interests of child victims of violence to undergo sanctions imposed jdih.pakpakbharatkab.go.id - 14 - on them, or how children will face perpetrators in court, must be stated in accordance with the interests of child victims of violence.  

Likewise for women victims of violence who want to be given freedom in accordance with their interests, women want to forget the perpetrator who is in fact a person they once loved, and so on. So we as a community or as law enforcement officials need to give opportunities to children and women to express their interests regarding everything related to the perpetrator as well as how to overcome the impact of violence that befalls them.

#### Article 3

Paragraph (1) Sufficiently clear

Paragraph (2)

a. What is meant by "physical violence" is an act that results in pain, illness, serious injury such as slapping, hitting, spitting on, pulling hair/tugging hair, kicking, burning with hot objects, hitting/injuring with weapons and or resulting in bodily disability, miscarriage, fainting and or causing death.

b. What is meant by "psychological violence" is an act that results in fear, loss of self-confidence, loss of ability to act, feelings of helplessness and or severe psychological suffering in a person. Psychological violence may take the form of emotional abuse, hurtful comments that even demean self-worth, isolating the wife from the outside world, threatening/intimidating as a means of imposing one's will.

c. What is meant by "sexual violence" is violence against sexuality which includes isolating (distancing) the wife from her inner needs, forcing sexual intercourse, imposing one's own sexual preferences or not paying attention to the wife's satisfaction.

Sexual violence may also take the form of forced sexual intercourse committed against a person residing within the scope of the household, forced sexual intercourse against a person within the scope of one's household with another person for commercial purposes and or certain purposes.

d. What is meant by "Economic Neglect" is neglecting the economic needs of the wife and children or family or relatives such as not providing maintenance to the wife or spending the wife's money, not giving or withholding the wages of domestic workers or drivers including acts of restricting or prohibiting family members from doing decent work.

e. What is meant by "Restriction of movement" is restricting the movement of the wife or children to develop their potential either for economic jdih.pakpakbharatkab.go.id - 15 - or social purposes such as prohibiting them from gathering or socializing with the community as well as restricting the movement of relatives such as preventing domestic workers and drivers from communicating or having contact with their families.
