# CHAPTER I

# GENERAL PROVISIONS

## Part One

Definitions, Meanings and Terminology

Article 1

In this Regional Regulation, what is meant by:

1. Regency is Musi Banyuasin Regency.  
2. Regency Government is the Government of Musi Banyuasin Regency.  
3. Regent is the Regent of Musi Banyuasin.  
4. Regional Apparatus hereinafter abbreviated as PD is the Regional Apparatus within the Government of Musi Banyuasin Regency.  
5. Office of Women's Empowerment and Child Protection hereinafter abbreviated as DPPPA is the Regional Apparatus that administers the affairs of Women's Empowerment and Child Protection in Musi Banyuasin Regency.  
6. Protection of Women is all efforts to guarantee and protect the rights of Women so that they can live healthily and participate optimally in accordance with human dignity and obtain protection from neglect, violence and discrimination, whether in the form of preventive, repressive or post-remedial protection (assistance and treatment).  
7. Child Protection is all activities to guarantee and protect Children and their rights so that they can live, grow, develop, and participate optimally in accordance with human dignity, and receive protection from violence and discrimination.  
8. Child is a person who has not reached the age of 18 (eighteen) years, including a Child who is still in the womb.  
9. Child with Disability is a Child who has physical, mental, intellectual, or sensory limitations for a long period of time who in interacting with the environment and community attitudes may encounter obstacles that make it difficult to participate fully and effectively based on equal rights.  
10. Child with Excellence is a Child who has extraordinary intelligence or has potential and/or special talents not limited to intellectual abilities, but also in other fields.  
11. Prevention is direct efforts carried out by the Regency Government, the community and parents and/or family to prevent violations of Women's rights and Children's Rights.  
12. Children's Forum is a forum for Children's participation formed with the aim of facilitating Children's involvement in decision-making processes and enjoying changes relating to their lives either directly or indirectly, which is carried out with the consent and will of all Children based on awareness and understanding, in accordance with age and level of maturity of thinking.  
13. Child-Friendly Regency hereinafter abbreviated as KLA is a Regency/City that has a Child rights-based development system through the integration of government, community and business commitments and resources that are planned comprehensively and sustainably in policies, programs and activities to ensure the fulfillment of Children's rights.  
14. Neglected Child is a Child whose needs are not properly fulfilled, whether physical, mental, spiritual, or social.  
15. Women victims of violence are Women who experience actions that result in misery or suffering physically, sexually or psychologically, including threats of certain actions, coercion or arbitrary deprivation of liberty, whether occurring in the public sphere or in private life.  
16. Child victims of violence are Children who experience actions that result in misery or suffering physically, mentally, sexually, psychologically or neglect that threatens life, body and degrades human dignity.  
17. Trafficking in persons is the act of recruiting, transporting, harboring, sending, transferring, or receiving a person by threat of violence, use of violence, kidnapping, confinement, forgery, fraud, abuse of power or vulnerable position, debt bondage or giving payment or benefits, so as to obtain consent from the person holding control over that other person, whether carried out within the Country or between Countries, for the purpose of exploitation or resulting in the person being exploited.  
18. Complaint handling is a series of actions carried out by integrated service providers to follow up on reports of acts of violence against Women and Children, submitted by victims, families or the community.  
19. Social rehabilitation is a service aimed at restoring and developing the ability of a person experiencing social dysfunction so that they can carry out their social functions properly.  
20. Law enforcement is the action of officials authorized by the state to implement laws and regulations.  
21. Legal aid is legal services provided by legal assistants and advocates to carry out the process of assisting witnesses and/or victims of violence against Women and Children that is gender-sensitive.  
22. Repatriation is an effort to return Women and Child victims of violence from abroad to the debarkation point/entry point, or from the receiving area to the area of origin.  
23. Social reintegration is an effort to reunite victims with family, substitute family, or community that can provide protection and fulfillment of needs for victims.  
24. Safe house is a temporary shelter that aims to provide protection and a sense of security to victims of violence according to established standards.  
25. Transit house is a forum for guidance and welfare services for street Children that carries out social assistance/guidance activities, maintenance, care, education, and health, skills guidance in order to ensure that Children do not carry out activities on the streets so that they can grow and develop properly.  
26. Spiritual guidance is an effort to restore the psyche through strengthening the religion adhered to by the victim.  
27. Gender responsive is consistent and systematic attention to the differences between Women and men in society accompanied by efforts to eliminate structural and cultural barriers in achieving gender equality.  
28. Gender Sensitivity or Gender Awareness is the ability to recognize power relationship gaps between Women and men in the family and in the community, the impact of gender-based division of labor on Women and men, and the experiences, problems, needs, interests and aspirations of Women and men are different.  
29. Public service providers are every state organizing institution, corporation, independent institution formed based on Law for public service activities, and other legal entities formed solely for public service activities.  
30. Community Organization is an organization established and formed by the community voluntarily based on common aspirations, will, needs, interests, activities and goals to participate in development for the achievement of the goals of the Unitary State of the Republic of Indonesia based on Pancasila.  
31. Parents are biological father and/or mother, or stepfather and/or stepmother, or adoptive father and/or mother.  
32. Family is the smallest unit in society consisting of husband and wife or husband, wife and their Child, or father and his Child, or mother and her Child, or blood relatives in a straight line upward or downward up to the third degree.  
33. Integrated Service Center for Women's Empowerment and Children, hereinafter abbreviated as P2TP2A is an integrated service center in efforts to empower Women in various fields of development, as well as protection of Women and Children from various types of discrimination and acts of violence, including trafficking in persons, which is established by the government or community-based.


## Part Two

Definitions, Meanings and Terminology Article 2 (1) The implementation of Protection of Women is based on the principles of:
a. the best interests of Women;  
b. gender justice and gender equality;  
c. local wisdom; and

d. legal certainty.  
2. The implementation of Protection of Children is based on the principles of:
a. non-discrimination;  
b. the best interests of the Child;  
c. the right to life, survival, and development;
d. respect for the views of the Child; and

e. legal certainty.  

Article 3 The regulation of Protection of Women and Children aims to:
a. serve as a guideline for the Regency Government in planning, policies and strategies for the Protection of Women and Children;  
b. protect Women and Children from actions, decisions and policies that violate the rights of Women and Children;  
c. prevent violations of the Rights of Women and Children;
d. enhance empowerment of Women and Children;
e. enhance the role and value of local wisdom in the Protection of Women and Children; and

f. enhance the role of Regional Apparatus and non-Government entities in the Regency in efforts to Protect Women and Children.  

Article 4 The scope of regulation of Protection of Women and Children includes:
a. prevention;  
b. implementation of Protection of the Rights of Women and Children;
c. handling;  
d. empowerment; and

e. guidance and supervision.

### Chapter II

### RIGHTS OF WOMEN AND RIGHTS OF CHILDREN

## Part One

Rights of Women Article 5 Every Woman has the right to:
a. obtain protection from acts of discrimination;  
b. obtain protection from economic exploitation and sexual exploitation;
c. obtain protection from physical, psychological, sexual violence, and neglect;  
d. obtain decent work in accordance with her abilities;
e. obtain work leave while receiving wages/salary in accordance with statutory regulations;  
f. obtain education and teaching in all types, levels and pathways of education;  
g. receive special protection in the performance of work or profession against matters that may threaten safety and/or health in connection with the reproductive function of Women; and

h. ownership and management of joint property in marriage in accordance with the provisions of Statutory Regulations.

## Part Two

Rights of Children Article 6 (1)Every Child has the right to:
a. live, grow, develop, and participate properly in accordance with human dignity and human worth, and receive protection from violence and discrimination;  
b. receive a name as personal identity and citizenship status;
c. worship according to his/her religion, think, and express in accordance with the level of intelligence and age under the guidance of Parents or Guardians;
d. obtain education and teaching in the context of personal development and level of intelligence in accordance with interests and talents and receive protection in educational units from sexual crimes and Violence committed by educators, education personnel, fellow students, and/or other parties;  
e. obtain protection from being the target of persecution, torture, or imposition of inhuman punishment;  
f. obtain freedom in accordance with the law and arrest, detention, or criminal imprisonment of Children shall only be carried out if in accordance with applicable law and can only be carried out as a last resort;  
g. be cared for by his/her own Parents, unless there are reasons and/or valid legal rules showing that such separation is in the best interests of the Child and is a last consideration;  
h. obtain protection from abuse in political activities, involvement in armed conflict, involvement in social unrest, involvement in events containing elements of Violence, involvement in warfare and sexual crimes;  
i. participate, express opinions and think in accordance with conscience and religion;  

J. receive oral or written information in accordance with the age stage and development of the Child;  
k. associate and assemble; and
1. rest, play, recreate, create, and engage in cultural arts, obtain play facilities that meet health and safety requirements and are adapted to the age of the Child, the level of ability of the Child, and the environment so as not to hinder and disturb.

(2)Every child who is deprived of liberty has the right to:

a. be treated humanely;  

b. be placed separately from adults;  

c. obtain legal assistance or other assistance effectively at every stage of applicable legal remedies; and

d. defend himself/herself and obtain justice before an objective and impartial Children's court in a closed session to the public.

(3)Every child who is a victim or perpetrator of sexual violence or who is in conflict with the law has the right to confidentiality.  

4. Every Child with Disabilities in addition to receiving the rights as referred to in paragraph (1) is also entitled to obtain:

a. special education/special education;  

b. rehabilitation;  
c. social assistance; and

d. maintenance of social welfare standards.  

5. Every Child who has excellence has the right to receive special education.  

### Chapter III

### OBLIGATIONS AND RESPONSIBILITIES

## Part One

Regency Government

#### Article 7

1. The Regency Government in efforts to protect Women is obliged and responsible to:
   a. guarantee the Rights of Women without discrimination in accordance with the provisions of Laws and Regulations;  
   b. establish and implement policies, programs and activities for the protection of Women;  
   c. cooperate with the community and/or Community Organizations in the implementation of Women's Protection in accordance with the provisions of Laws and Regulations;  
   d. carry out rehabilitation and empowerment of Women who are victims of violence;  
   e. provide special protection to Women victims of violence and human trafficking; and  
   f. take preventive action in order to prevent violations of the rights of Women.  
2. The responsibility of the Regency Government in efforts to protect the Rights of Women as referred to in paragraph (1) is implemented by DPPPA.  

#### Article 8

(1) The Regency Government in efforts to protect Children is obliged and responsible to:
   a. respect the fulfillment of the rights of Children without distinguishing ethnicity, religion, race, class, gender, ethnicity, culture and language, legal status, birth order, and physical and/or mental condition;  
   b. provide support for facilities, infrastructure, and availability of human resources in the implementation of Child Protection;  
   c. guarantee the protection, care, and welfare of Children with due regard to the rights and obligations of Parents, Guardians, or other persons who are legally responsible for the Child;  
   d. guarantee Children to exercise their right to express opinions in accordance with the age and level of intelligence of the Child;  
   e. establish and implement policies and programs/activities for the protection of the rights of Children;  
   f. cooperate with the community and/or Community Organizations in the implementation of Child protection in accordance with the provisions of Laws and Regulations;  
   g. carry out rehabilitation of Children who are victims of violence;  
   h. provide special protection to Children:
      1. in emergency situations, victims of disasters and conflicts;  
      2. in conflict with the law;  
      3. victims of violence, neglect and human trafficking;  
      4. from minority and isolated groups;  
      5. who are exploited economically and/or sexually/victims of pornography;  
      6. who are victims of narcotics, alcohol, psychotropic abuse; and
      7. identified with HIV/AIDS;  
   i. organize inclusive education for Children with special needs;  
   j. take preventive action in order to prevent violations against Children; and  
   k. implement and support National policies in the implementation of Child Protection in the Regency carried out through efforts to realize Child-Friendly Regency, Child-Friendly Sub-district and Child-Friendly Village;  
2. The responsibility of the Regency Government in efforts to protect Children as referred to in paragraph (1) is implemented by DPPPA.  

#### Article 9

(1) The Regency Government in carrying out its responsibilities, establishes programs and activities for the protection of Women and Children, with the Regional Action Plan as the basis for Regional Apparatus in implementing the protection of Women and Children.  

2. Further provisions regarding the Regional Action Plan as referred to in paragraph (1) shall be regulated by Regent Regulation.

## Part Two

#### Article 10

(1) The community is obliged and responsible in implementing the protection of Women and Children including:
   a. participation in prevention and providing support for the development of prevention programs for violations of the rights of Women and Children;  
   b. providing information and/or reporting violations of the rights of Women and Children to the competent authorities; and  
   c. supervising the law enforcement process against cases of violations of the rights of Women and Children.  
2. The responsibility of the community as referred to in paragraph (1) is in accordance with the provisions of laws and regulations and the values of local wisdom of the local community.

## Part Three

Parents and/or Family

### Paragraph 1

#### Article 11

Responsibility for the Protection of Women's Rights The responsibility of the family in efforts to protect women includes:
   a. protecting women from conflict and domestic violence that degrades the dignity of women;  
   b. providing equal opportunities for women to develop themselves in the fields of:
      1. education;  
      2. social;  
      3. economy; and
      4. politics and culture.  
   c. preventing violations of women's rights in the household.  

### Paragraph 2

#### Article 12

(1) Parents in efforts to protect Children are responsible for:
   a. caring for, maintaining, educating, and protecting Children;  
   b. developing Children according to their abilities, talents, and interests;  
   c. providing formal and non-formal education;  
   d. guiding Children to behave in accordance with religion, ethics and morals as well as customs; and  
   e. preventing marriage at the age of Children.  
2. In the event that parents do not exist, or their whereabouts are unknown, or due to some reason, cannot carry out their obligations and responsibilities, then the obligations and responsibilities as referred to in paragraph (1) may be transferred to the family.

#### CHAPTER IV

#### PREVENTION

## Part One

#### Article 13

(1) Efforts made by the Regency Government in order to prevent violations of the rights of Women include:
   a. organizing socialization regarding the prevention of violence against Women by optimizing the role of customary, cultural, religious institutions and community organizations;  
   b. providing guidelines on handling violence against Women;  
   c. involving community participation in recovery and reintegration;  
   d. developing an integrated database and information system on violence against Women;  
   e. developing family empowerment programs;  
   f. strengthening the function of community organizations engaged in Women's empowerment; and  
   g. providing public facilities that are comfortable and safe for Women.  
2. Efforts made by the Regency Government in order to prevent violations of the rights of Children include:
   a. prevention of violence against Children by optimizing the role of customary, cultural, religious institutions and community organizations;  
   b. developing child-friendly schools;  
   c. encouraging increased child participation through the establishment of Children's Forums;  
   d. providing guidelines on handling violence against Children;  
   e. involving community participation in recovery and reintegration services for child victims of violence;  
   f. developing an integrated database and information system on violence against Children;  
   g. strengthening the function of community organizations engaged in Child protection; and  
   h. encouraging the establishment of Child-Friendly Regency.

## Part Two

#### Article 14

(1) Efforts made by the community in order to prevent violations of the rights of Women include:
   a. monitoring various conditions occurring in their environment that may cause violations of the rights of Women;  
   b. strengthening the role of community, religious organizations and the family environment; and  
   c. prevention of unregistered marriage/"siri" marriage.  
(2) Efforts made in order to prevent violations of the rights of Children include:
   a. monitoring various conditions occurring in their environment that cause violations of the rights of Children;  
   b. strengthening the role of community, religious organizations and the family environment; and  
   c. preventing marriage at the age of Children.

## Part Three

Parents and/or Family Article 15 (1) Efforts made by the family in order to prevent violations of the rights of Women include:

a. protection of women from various violations of rights in the household; and  
b. encouraging the formation of harmonious, educative and democratic families;  

2. Efforts made by parents in order to prevent violations of the rights of Children include:

a. providing good examples to children in accordance with religious and customary teachings;  
b. supervision of children in activities inside or outside the home;  
c. providing opportunities for children to engage in dialogue and express opinions democratically; and  
d. protection of children from various acts of violation of the rights of Children in the household.

### CHAPTER V

### IMPLEMENTATION OF PROTECTION OF WOMEN AND CHILDREN

## Part One

Implementation of Protection of Women

### Paragraph 1

General Article 16 The Regency Government implements the Protection of Women by involving:

a. family;  
b. community leaders;  
c. religious leaders;  
d. community;  
e. community organizations in the field of protection of Women.  

Paragraph 2 Marriage Article 17 During the marriage bond or after divorce, a Woman has the same rights as the man who is her husband/former husband over all matters relating to the consequences of marriage and/or divorce.  

Paragraph 3 Health Article 18 (1) The Regency Government provides adequate health and maternity facilities for pregnant and nursing mothers.  

2. Public service providers must provide special facilities for nursing mothers.  

Paragraph 4 Employment Article 19 (1) Employers who employ Women must maintain decency, safety and security while in the workplace.  

2. Employers must place female workers in places that are safe for Women's reproductive health.  
3. Employers must fulfill the rights of female workers to receive wages in accordance with the provisions of laws and regulations.  
4. Employers provide special facilities for nursing mothers in their companies.  

Article 20 (1) Female workers/laborers aged at most 18 (eighteen) years are prohibited from being employed between 23.00 WIBsampai dengan 07.00 WIB.  

2. Employers are prohibited from employing pregnant female workers/laborers who according to a doctor's statement are dangerous to the health and safety of their pregnancy or themselves during working hours from 23.00 WIB sampai dengan 07.00 WIB.  

3. Employers who employ female workers/laborers between 23.00 WIBsampai dengan 07.00 WIB,wajib:

a. provide nutritious food and drinks; and  
b. maintain decency and security while in the workplace.  

(4) Employers must provide shuttle transportation for female workers/laborers who depart and return from work between 23.00 WIB sampai dengan 05.00 WIB.  

Paragraph 5 Education

#### Article 21

Women are guaranteed the right to obtain education at every level of formal and non-formal education.  

Paragraph 6 Social Article 22 (1) The Regency Government implements empowerment of Women experiencing socio-economic vulnerability through productive economic business activities.  

2. Community Organizations may carry out empowerment for Women experiencing socio-economic vulnerability as referred to in paragraph (1) in accordance with the provisions of Laws and Regulations. !

## Part Two

Implementation of Protection of the Rights of Children

### Paragraph 1

Health Article 23 (1) Efforts of the Regency Government to provide protection of Children in the health sector include: a. free health services for Children from poor families;  
b. implementation of optimal health for Children to obtain good health status from the womb;  
c. ensuring that infants receive exclusive breastfeeding from their mothers properly and appropriately;  
d. ensuring Children receive adequate nutrition;  
e. ensuring newborn Children undergo screening; and  
f. ensuring the provision of monitoring of growth and development of toddlers and pre-school age children.  
2. The Regency Government, family, and parents protect children from attempts to transplant their body organs to other parties.  

Paragraph 2 Education Article 24 Children in formal and non-formal educational institutions must be protected from acts of violence.  

Article 25 Every Child aged 7 (seven) to 18 (eighteen) years who has not completed education, exceeding 3 (three) years beyond the age limit of formal education may pursue education through non-formal education units:

a. Package A equivalency program equivalent to Elementary School and Madrasah Ibtidaiyah;  
b. Package B equivalency program equivalent to Junior High School and Madrasah Tsanawiyah; and  
c. Package C equivalency program equivalent to Senior High School, Madrasah Aliyah and Vocational High School.  

Article 26 (1) Children with special needs and/or who have potential intelligence and/or special talents are given equal opportunities and facilities to obtain regular education or inclusive education.  

2. Inclusive education as referred to in paragraph (1) is organized by the Regency Government and the community.  

Article 27 Every organizer of formal education is prohibited from expelling Children without guaranteeing the continuity of the Child's education.  

Article 28 (1) The Regency Government provides scholarships and/or educational assistance for:

a. children from poor families;  
b. neglected children; and  
c. children residing in remote areas.  
2. Implementation of scholarship programs and/or educational assistance as referred to in paragraph (1) is supported by community participation and community organizations.  
3. Further provisions regarding the provision of scholarships and or educational assistance as referred to in paragraph (1) shall be regulated by Regent Regulation.  

Article 29 Children in conflict with the law, Children victims of violence and sexual abuse and Children victims of HIV/AIDS transmission are protected in their rights to obtain education.

#### Article 30

The Regency Government and the community shall organize Early Childhood Education (PAUD).

Paragraph 3 Employment Article 31 Employers are prohibited from employing Children.

#### Article 32

(1) The provisions as referred to in Article 31 may be exempted for Children aged between 13 (thirteen) years to 15 (fifteen) years, to perform light work as long as it does not interfere with physical, mental, and social development and health.

(2) Employers who employ Children in light work as referred to in paragraph (1) must fulfill requirements including:

a. written permission from parents or guardian;

b. work agreement between employer and parents or guardian;

c. maximum working time of 3 (three) hours;

d. performed during the day and does not interfere with school time;

e. occupational safety and health;

f. clear employment relationship; and

g. receive wages in accordance with the provisions of laws and regulations.

(3) The provisions as referred to in paragraph (2) letter a, letter b, letter f, and letter g are exempted for Children who work in their family business.

#### Article 33

In the event that a company employs Children as referred to in Article 32, then the workplace of Children must be separated from the workplace of adult workers.

#### Article 34

(1) Children may perform work in a workplace that is part of an educational or training curriculum authorized by the competent official.

(2) Children as referred to in paragraph (1) must be at least 14 (fourteen) years old.

#### Article 35

(1) Every person is prohibited from employing and involving Children in the worst forms of work.

(2) The worst forms of work as referred to in paragraph (1) include:

a. all work in the form of slavery or similar practices;

b. all work that exploits, provides, or offers Children for:

1. prostitution;

2. pornography production;

3. pornographic performances;

4. gambling;

5. production and trade of alcoholic beverages;

6. narcotics;

7. psychotropic substances; and

8. other addictive substances; and/or

c. all work that endangers the health, safety, or morals of Children.

Paragraph 4 Social Article 36

(1) The Regency Government is obliged to provide care for neglected Children.

(2) Care as referred to in paragraph (1) may be carried out by Community Organizations in the social field.

(3) In the event that a Child is neglected because for some reason the parents neglect their obligations, then the Family or Regency Government or Community Organizations as referred to in paragraph (2) may submit an application to the court to determine the Child as a neglected Child.

(4) The court determination as referred to in paragraph (3) simultaneously determines the shelter and care for the neglected Child concerned.

#### Article 37

(1) The Regency Government shall organize skills training for street Children and neglected Children.

(2) Skills training as referred to in paragraph (1) shall be organized by Regional Apparatus whose duties and functions are in the social and employment fields and may cooperate with Community Organizations.

#### Article 38

(1) The Regency Government shall provide shelters to foster street Children and social institutions for neglected Children.

(2) Shelters as referred to in paragraph (1) may be organized in cooperation with Community Organizations.

(3) Further provisions regarding shelters as referred to in paragraph (1) shall be regulated by Regent Regulation.

#### Article 39

The implementation of protection of Women and Children shall be carried out by Regional Apparatus whose duties and functions are related to the protection of Women and Children.

##### CHAPTER VI

##### HANDLING

## Part One

Handling of Women and Children Victims of Violence Article 40 The implementation of handling of Women and Children victims of violence includes:

a. handling of complaints from victims of violence;

b. health services;

c. social rehabilitation;

d. law enforcement and legal assistance; and

e. repatriation and social reintegration.

#### Article 41

(1) The implementation of handling of Women and Children victims of violence as referred to in Article 40 shall be carried out through integrated services.

(2) Integrated services for Women and Children victims of violence as referred to in paragraph (1) use the principles of:

a. gender responsive;

b. non-discrimination;

c. equal and respectful relationships;

d. maintaining confidentiality;

e. providing a sense of security and comfort;

f. respecting individual differences;

g. non-judgmental;

h. respecting the choices and decisions of victims;

i. sensitive to the background and condition of victims and the use of language that is appropriate and understood by victims;

j. fast and simple;

k. empathy;

l. fulfillment of the rights of Women and Children; and

m. free of charge.

#### Article 42

Services for handling complaints of victims of violence against Women and Children shall be carried out by:

a. hospitals;

b. community health centers;

c. P2TP2A;

d. Women and Children Service Unit (UPPA);

e. Trauma Center Shelter (RPTC);

f. Children's Social Shelter (RPSA);

g. Women Crisis Centre (WCC);

h. Advisory Board for Marriage Guidance and Preservation (BP4);

i. Police;

j. Prosecutor's Office;

k. Court;

l. Legal Aid Institute (LBH); and

m. Child Protection Institute (LPA).

#### Article 43

(1) Complaints of acts of violence against Women and Children as referred to in Article 42 may be made by reporters consisting of:

a. Victims;

b. Family;

c. Community; and/or

d. community organizations in the field of protection of Women and Children.

(2) Reporters as referred to in paragraph (1) may make complaints:

a. directly;

b. by telephone; and

c. by letter.

(3) In addition to handling complaints submitted by reporters, officers at service institutions as referred to in Article 42 are authorized to handle victims of violence against Women and Children obtained through other information.

(4) Further provisions regarding the procedures for handling complaint services as referred to in paragraph (1) and paragraph (2) shall be regulated by Regent Regulation.

## Part Two

Health Services for Women and Children Victims of Violence I Article 44

(1) Health services for Women and Children victims of violence include aspects of:

a. Promotive;

b. Preventive;

c. Curative;

d. rehabilitative.

(2) Health services for Women and Children victims of violence shall be carried out:

a. comprehensively;

b. accessible 24 (twenty-four) hours;

c. with quality; and

d. in accordance with health service standards.

(3) Health services specifically for Children victims of violence must prioritize the best interests of the Child.

(4) Women and Children victims of violence are entitled to health services, including:

a. medical services;

b. medicolegal services; and

c. psychosocial counseling.

(5) Further provisions regarding the procedures for implementing health services as referred to in paragraph (4) shall be regulated by Regent Regulation.

## Part Three

Social Rehabilitation Services for Women and Children Victims of Violence

Article 45

(1) Social rehabilitation services for Women and Children victims of violence are aimed at restoring and developing the abilities of victims who experience social dysfunction to be able to carry out their social functions in society properly.

2. Social rehabilitation services as referred to in paragraph (1) include:
   a. counseling services;
   b. provision of safe houses;
   c. spiritual guidance.

3. The provision of services as referred to in paragraph (2) is organized by:
   a. Regional Apparatus having duties and functions in the social field;
   b. DPPPA;
   c. P2TP2A;
   d. Women Crisis Centre (WeC);
   e. social community organizations; and/or
   f. religious institutions.

Article 46

(1) Counseling services as referred to in Article 45 paragraph (2) letter a aim to:
   a. help victims recognize their problems and find effective ways to overcome them themselves;
   b. empower victims to determine their future;
   c. provide moral support for victims in facing the process they are undergoing; and
   d. make victims feel accepted in the community environment.

(2) Counseling services are carried out by gender-responsive social rehabilitation officers, consisting of:
   a. counselors;
   b. psychologists; and
   c. psychiatrists.

3. Counseling services are carried out in a place that guarantees a sense of security, comfort and confidentiality of information from victims.

4. Stages of counseling services include:
   a. initial counseling;
   b. follow-up counseling;
   c. case conference; and
   d. home visit.

Article 47

(1) The Regency Government may provide a safe house for Women and Children victims of violence.

2. The safe house as referred to in paragraph (1) aims to protect Women and Children victims of violence from intimidation by perpetrators or other parties.

3. The location of the safe house as referred to in paragraph (1) must be kept confidential.

4. Criteria for Women and Children victims of violence who require a safe house include:
   a. their life is threatened;
   b. receiving rejection from family or community;
   c. requiring intensive services but their residence is relatively far; and/or
   d. abandoned if not placed in a safe house.

5. The provision of safe house services is carried out by social rehabilitation officers who have gender sensitivity or gender awareness, consisting of:
   a. social workers/volunteers;
   b. security officers; and
   c. community leaders.

6. Further provisions regarding the provision of safe house services are regulated by Regent Regulation.

Article 48

(1) Every Woman and Child victim of violence is provided with spiritual guidance aimed at psychological recovery.

2. Spiritual guidance as referred to in paragraph (1) is implemented based on the principles of:
   a. not imposing any religion or belief; and
   b. leaving the final decision in the hands of the victim (self determination).

(3) Spiritual guidance as referred to in paragraph (2) is carried out by spiritual guidance officers.

4. Spiritual guidance officers as referred to in paragraph (3) consist of:
   a. consultants of the Marriage Advisory, Development and Preservation Agency (BP4);
   b. Officers of the Office of Religious Affairs (KUA);
   c. Ustad or Ulama;
   d. Pastor;
   e. Minister; and
   f. other religious leaders who have gender sensitivity or gender awareness.

## Part Four

Law Enforcement and Legal Aid Services I,

Article 49

(1) Law enforcement and legal aid services constitute actions of law enforcement officers provided to Women and Children victims of violence and/or witnesses.

2. Law enforcement and legal aid services as referred to in paragraph (1) are carried out by prioritizing the best interests of victims.

Article 50

(1) Law enforcement and legal aid services as referred to in article 49 are aimed at providing assistance and protection to Women and Children victims of violence and witnesses with a gender-responsive approach so that victims obtain justice and legal certainty.

2. Types of legal aid services as referred to in paragraph (1) include:
   a. legal aid in the reporting process and preparation of BAP;
   b. legal aid in the prosecution process at the prosecutor's office;
   c. legal aid in the examination process at court;
   d. legal aid in the court hearing decision process until there is a final legal decision; and
   e. legal aid in the mediation process.

3. The Regency Government provides legal aid to Children and Women victims of violence free of charge.

4. In providing legal aid as referred to in paragraph (3) the Regency Government may cooperate with:
   a. Legal Aid Institute (LBH);
   b. Women Crisis Center (WCC);
   c. Advocates; and/or
   d. other legal assistants who are gender sensitive.

## Part Five

Repatriation and Social Reintegration Services

Article 51

(1) Repatriation of victims of violence is aimed at returning Women and Children victims of violence to their area of origin.

2. Repatriation of Women and Children victims of violence as referred to in paragraph (1) is accompanied by social reintegration efforts.

3. The Regency Government implements repatriation and social reintegration of Women and Children victims of violence in accordance with the provisions of Laws and Regulations.

### CHAPTER VII

### WOMEN'S EMPOWERMENT

Article 52

(1) The Regency Government guarantees the implementation of empowerment for Women.

2. The implementation of Women's empowerment as referred to in paragraph (1) is supported by community participation and Community Organizations.

Article 53

(1) The Regency Government provides skills training to increase Women's capacity.

2. In providing training as referred to in paragraph (1) the Regency Government may cooperate with training institutions organized by community organizations.

3. The Regency Government expands access and opportunities for Women in obtaining skills and employment.

Article 54

(1) The Regency Government facilitates:
   a. provision of business capital assistance for Women with weak economies;
   b. marketing of products resulting from Women's businesses;
   c. organization of community capacity development training through economic empowerment of pre-prosperous families;
   d. expansion of access, information and providing knowledge to Women regarding marketing efforts for products produced; and
   e. providing entrepreneurship training to Women comprehensively.

2. The Regency Government in facilitating activities as referred to in paragraph (1) cooperates with the community and/or community organizations.

# CHAPTER VIII

## CHILDREN'S PARTICIPATION

Article 55

(1) Every Child has the right to participate in expressing opinions, ideas, and thoughts related to life based on awareness and understanding, in accordance with age and level of thinking maturity.

2. Children's participation as referred to in paragraph (1) may be carried out through Children's Forums starting from the village level, sub-district within the regency.

3. Children's Forums as referred to in paragraph (2) represent Children, both geographical domicile representation, socio-cultural groups and Children's educational backgrounds.

4. The establishment of Children's Forums as referred to in paragraph (2) is facilitated by the Regency Government.

5. The establishment of Children's Forums as referred to in paragraph (4) is stipulated by Regent Decree.

Article 56

(1) In every formulation of policies related to Children, the Regency Government pays attention to and accommodates Children's Opinions submitted through Children's Forums.

2. The Regency Government includes representatives of Children's Forums in the Regency Development Planning Deliberation.

## CHAPTER IX

## CHILD-FRIENDLY REGENCY

Article 57 (1) The Regency Government conducts guidance to the District Government in the development of a Child-Friendly Regency.  

2. The guidance as referred to in paragraph (1) includes: a. coordination;  
b. facilitation;  
c. guidance;  
d. supervision;  
e. consultation; and f. education and training.  
3. Provisions regarding the Child-Friendly Regency shall be further regulated by Regent Regulation.

## CHAPTER X

## GUIDANCE AND SUPERVISION

Article 58 (1) The Regent conducts guidance and supervision of the implementation of Protection of Women and Children.  

2. The guidance as referred to in paragraph (1) may be conducted in the form of: a. training for government officials and law enforcement officers on prevention and handling of violence;  
b. training for religious leaders, community leaders, customary leaders, educators, journalists and media managers in order to prevent violence against Women and Children;  
c. training for health workers who provide health services to Women and Children victims of violence at community health centers, integrated service units, and general hospitals;  
d. training for social rehabilitation officers and spiritual guidance officers in order to protect Women and Children victims of violence; and f. improvement of professionalism of officers, managers of violence case data against Women and Children at community health centers, integrated service units and general hospitals.  
3. The Regent in conducting guidance and supervision as referred to in paragraph (1) may delegate to the Head of the Service/Department.  

Article 59 (1) The Head of DPPPA is obliged to submit a report on the implementation of the Women and Children protection program to the Regent.  

2. The report as referred to in paragraph (1) shall be submitted periodically 1 (one) time every 3 (three) months.  

Article 60 (1) The Regent conducts supervision and evaluation of the implementation of Protection of Women and Children periodically 1 (one) time every 3 (three) months.  

2. The results of the evaluation as referred to in paragraph (1) constitute evaluation material for the policy of the Women and Children protection program in the Regency.

## CHAPTER XI

FINANCING.  

Article 61 Financing for the implementation of protection of Women and Children is sourced from:
a. Regional Revenue and Expenditure Budget;  
b. State Revenue and Expenditure Budget;  
c. other lawful sources in accordance with the provisions of Laws and Regulations.

# CHAPTER XII

## CLOSING PROVISIONS

Article 62 Implementing regulations of this Regional Regulation shall be stipulated at the latest 1 (one) year from the promulgation of this Regional Regulation.
