# CONCERNING

WOMEN'S EMPOWERMENT AND CHILD PROTECTION BY THE GRACE OF GOD ALMIGHTY

# THE REGENT OF OGAN KOMERING ULU,

Considering:  
a. that every woman and child has the right to survival, growth, and proper development and has the right to protection from violence and discrimination as mandated in the 1945 Constitution of the Republic of Indonesia;  
b. that discriminatory treatment and violence against women and children will have an impact on the quality of human resources in the future;  
c. that in order to realize the vision and mission of development in the field of women's empowerment and child protection, respect, protection, fulfillment, enforcement, and advancement of the rights of women and children, it is deemed necessary to establish policies that can guarantee the implementation of women's empowerment and child protection;  
d. that based on the considerations as referred to in letter a, letter b, and letter c, it is necessary to establish a Regional Regulation on Women's Empowerment and Child Protection;

# CHAPTER I

## GENERAL PROVISIONS

#### Article 1

In this Regional Regulation, what is meant by:

1. Regency is Ogan Komering Ulu Regency.  
2. Regency Government is the Government of Ogan Komering Ulu Regency.  
3. Regent is the Regent of Ogan Komering Ulu.  
4. Regional Apparatus hereinafter abbreviated as PD is the Regional Apparatus within the Government of Ogan Komering Ulu Regency.  
5. Gender is a concept that refers to the differentiation of roles and responsibilities of men and women that occur as a result of and can be changed by the social and cultural conditions of society.  
6. Gender Equality hereinafter abbreviated as KG is the equality of conditions for men and women to obtain opportunities and their rights as human beings, so as to be able to play a role and participate in political, economic, social, cultural, defense and security activities and equality in enjoying the results of development.  
7. Gender Analysis is a process built systematically to identify and understand the division of labor or roles of men and women, access to and control over development resources, participation in the development process and the benefits they enjoy.  
8. Gender Mainstreaming hereinafter abbreviated as PUG is a strategy built to integrate gender into an integral dimension -3- of planning, formulation, implementation, monitoring, and evaluation of policies, programs and development activities in the region.  
9. Gender responsive is a policy, program, activity, and budgeting that reduces differences, needs, experiences, and aspirations of men and women.  
10. Gender Responsive Budget hereinafter abbreviated as ARG is a budget that is responsive to the needs of women and men with the aim of realizing gender equality and justice.  
11. Women's empowerment is an effort by women to obtain access and control over resources, economic, political, social, cultural, so that women can organize themselves and increase self-confidence to be able to play a role and participate actively in solving problems, so as to be able to build capacity and self-concept.  
12. Protection of women is all efforts aimed at protecting women and providing a sense of security in the fulfillment of their rights by providing consistent and systematic attention aimed at achieving gender equality.  
13. Child is a person who is not yet 18 (eighteen) years old, including a child who is still in the womb.  
14. Child Protection is all activities to guarantee and protect Children and their rights so that they can live, grow, develop, and participate optimally in accordance with human dignity, and receive protection from violence and discrimination.  
15. Special Protection is a form of protection received by Children in certain situations and conditions to obtain a guarantee of security against threats that endanger themselves and their lives in their growth and development.  
16. Violence is any unlawful act that results in suffering or suffering physically, sexually, mentally, psychologically, including neglect and ill-treatment that threatens and/or endangers life and body and degrades dignity.  
17. Special Protection for Children is a form of protection received by children in certain situations and conditions to obtain a guarantee of security against threats that endanger themselves and their lives in their growth and development.  
18. Children in Emergency Situations are Children who are in environmental situations that threaten and disrupt the life and livelihood of Children that -4- are caused by natural, non-natural, and/or social factors.  
19. Children in Conflict with the Law are Children who are in conflict with the law, Children who are victims of criminal acts, and Children who are witnesses to criminal acts.  
20. Children from Minority and Isolated Groups are Children who are left behind, foremost, outermost in an environment with different cultures, traditions, tribes, races, religions from other children whose numbers are far fewer than Children of other groups.  
21. Children Who Are Economically Exploited are Children who are victims of acts of prostitution, forced labor or service, slavery or practices similar to slavery, oppression, extortion, exploitation of physical, sexual, reproductive organs, or unlawfully transferring or transplanting organs and/or body tissues or exploiting the labor or abilities of Children by other parties or other similar actions to obtain material benefits.  
22. Children Who Are Sexually Exploited are Children who are exploited to obtain benefits through sexual organs or other body organs of Children, including but not limited to all prostitution and indecency activities.  
23. Children Who Are Victims of Narcotics, Alcohol, Psychotropic, and Other Addictive Substances Abuse are Children who are persuaded, deceived, tricked, forced, and/or threatened to use narcotics, alcohol, psychotropics, and other addictive substances.  
24. Children Who Are Victims of Pornography are Children who experience trauma or suffering as a result of pornography crimes.  
25. Human Immunodeficiency Virus hereinafter abbreviated as HIV is a virus that attacks the immune system and if not treated can reduce the immune system of humans until the condition of Acquired Immuno Deficiency Syndrome occurs.  
26. Acquired Immuno Deficiencg Syndrome hereinafter abbreviated as AIDS is a collection of symptoms of reduced self-defense ability caused by the entry of the HIV virus into a person's body.  
27. Children with HIV and AIDS are Children who are infected with HIV and/or AIDS either transmitted from parents or from other risk factors.  
28. Child Victims of Kidnapping are Children who are taken by someone unlawfully with the intention of placing the Child under their power or the power of another person or to place the Child in a helpless state.  
5-

29. Child Victims of Sale are Children who are transferred by a person or group of people to another party for compensation or other reasons.  
30. Child Victims of Trafficking are Children who experience psychological, mental, physical, sexual, economic, and/or social suffering resulting from acts of recruitment, transportation, harboring, sending, transfer, or receipt with threats of violence, use of violence, kidnapping, confinement, forgery, fraud, abuse of power or vulnerable position, debt bondage or giving payment or benefits, so as to obtain consent from the person who has control over the other person, whether carried out within the country or between countries, for the purpose of exploitation or resulting in the exploitation of Children.  
31. Child Victims of Physical Violence are Children who experience violence that causes pain, illness, or serious injury.  
32. Child Victims of Psychological Violence are Children who experience fear, loss of self-confidence, loss of ability to act, feelings of helplessness, and/or severe psychological suffering.  
33. Child Victims of Sexual Crimes are Children who experience forced sexual intercourse, forced sexual intercourse in an unnatural and/or unwanted manner, and forced sexual intercourse with others for commercial purposes and/or certain purposes.  
34. Child Victims of Terrorism Networks are Children who experience physical, mental suffering, and/or economic loss caused by terrorism crimes either as Child victims, Child perpetrators, Children of perpetrators, and Child witnesses.  
35. Children with Disabilities are Children who experience physical, intellectual, mental, and/or sensory limitations for a long period of time who in interacting with the environment may experience obstacles and difficulties to participate fully and effectively based on equal rights.  
36. Child Victims of Maltreatment are Children who are threatened physically and non-physically due to acts of violence, mistreated or improperly treated in the family environment or their closest social environment, so that their basic needs are not properly met physically, spiritually, or socially.  
37. Child Victims of Neglect are Children who do not receive fulfillment of needs such as but not limited to health, safety, and welfare needs from parents or other persons who have legal responsibility to care for the Child resulting in harm in the growth and development process of the Child.  
-6-

38. Children with Deviant Social Behavior are Children who have attitudes and behaviors that do not consider the judgment and existence of other people in general around them, show irresponsible attitudes and lack of remorse regarding their mistakes, and often violate the rights and norms that exist in society.  
39. Children Who Are Victims of Stigmatization from Labeling related to the Condition of Their Parents are Children who are given negative social labels based on prejudice and aimed at separating, differentiating, discrediting, and ostracizing Children with a bad stamp or view from labeling related to the condition of their parents.  
40. Substitute Family is foster parents, adoptive parents, and guardians who carry out the role and responsibility to provide alternative care for Children.  
41. Educational Institution is an educational unit as referred to in laws and regulations concerning the national education system.  
42. Counseling is a process carried out in the form of interviews to help Children understand themselves better, so that Children can overcome difficulties in adjusting themselves to various roles and relationships and find appropriate problem solving.  
43. Social Assistance is a dynamic interaction between social workers and Children who need Special Protection to jointly face and solve the social problems they face.  
44. Social Rehabilitation is a process of refunctionalization and development to enable Children to be able to carry out their social functions properly in community life.  
45. Social Reintegration is a process of preparing Children who need Special Protection to be able to return to the family and community environment.  
46. Fulfillment of Children's Rights hereinafter abbreviated as PHA is a child protection strategy by integrating children's rights into every development activity from the preparation of planning, budgeting, implementation, monitoring, and evaluation of various laws and regulations, policies, programs, and activities by applying the principle of the best interests of the child.  
47. Discrimination is any restriction, harassment, or exclusion, which directly or indirectly -7- is based on differentiation in humans on the basis of religion, ethnicity, race, class, social status, economic status, gender, language, political beliefs, legal status of children, and physical and/or mental conditions that result in reduction, deviation, or elimination, recognition, implementation or use of human rights and fundamental freedoms in life both individually and collectively in the political, economic, legal, socio-cultural fields and other aspects of life.  
48. Exploitation is an act with or without the consent of the victim which includes but is not limited to prostitution, forced labor or service, slavery or practices similar to slavery, oppression, extortion, exploitation of physical sexual, reproductive organs or unlawfully transferring or transplanting organs and/or body tissues or exploiting the labor or abilities of someone by other parties to obtain benefits both material and immaterial.  
49. Parents are biological father and/or mother, or stepfather and/or stepmother, or adoptive father and/or mother.  
50. Guardian is a person or body who in fact exercises parental authority as a parent over a child.  
51. Family is the smallest unit in society consisting of husband, wife and their children, or father and his children, or mother and her children, or blood relatives in a straight line up or down to the third degree.  
52. Community is individuals, families, groups and social organizations and/or community organizations, business world.

# CHAPTER II

## WOMEN'S EMPOWERMENT

## Part One

General

#### Article 2

( 1 ) Women's empowerment in the Regency is implemented through the protection of women's rights.  

( 2 ) Empowerment as referred to in paragraph (1) is implemented in order to fulfill women's rights in improving the quality of women's lives.  

( 3 ) Women's rights as referred to in paragraph (2) must be guaranteed by the Regency Government.  
8- ( 4 ) Women's rights as referred to in paragraph (3) include the fields of education, health, employment, politics, law, economy, culture, and social.

## Part Two

Obligations of the Regency Government

#### Article 3

( 1 ) The Regency Government is obligated to guarantee the protection of women's rights.  

( 2 ) Protection of women's rights as referred to in paragraph (1) is carried out by:
a. formulation of gender-responsive regional policies;  
b. institutionalization of PUG within the Regency Government;  
c. women's empowerment in the fields of education, health, employment, politics, law, economy, culture, and social in community organizations at the Regency level; and  
d. strengthening and development of institutions providing women's empowerment services at the regency level.

#### Article 4

1. Formulation of gender-responsive Regency policies as referred to in Article 3 paragraph (2) letter a serves as a guideline in the preparation of regional development plans.  
2. Preparation of development plans as referred to in paragraph (1) includes long-term plans, medium-term plans, and regional government work plans.  
3. Long-term development plans, medium-term plans, and regional government work plans as referred to in paragraph (2) must serve as a guideline for PD in the preparation of strategic plans and work plans within their respective working environments.  
4. Preparation of development plans as referred to in paragraph (3) is carried out through gender-responsive planning through gender analysis and ARG that contains a priority scale for the implementation of women's empowerment.  
5. Preparation of the priority scale for the implementation of women's empowerment as referred to in paragraph (4) is guided by the national level priority scale.  
9- (6) The national level priority scale as referred to in paragraph (5) becomes an inseparable part of the regional development plan.

#### Article 5

In order to ensure the realization of the formulation of gender-responsive policies as referred to in Article 3 paragraph (2) letter a, PD that carry out governmental affairs in the field of regional development planning are obligated to:
a. involve PD that administer governmental affairs in the field of women's empowerment in the formulation of regional development plans; and  
b. evaluate programs and activities at the PD level that are not gender-responsive.

#### Article 6

1. Institutionalization of PUG as referred to in Article 3 paragraph (2) letter b is implemented in all PD within the Regency Government.  
2. To accelerate the institutionalization of PUG as referred to in paragraph (1), the Regent may establish a PUG Working Group at the Regency level.  
3. Members of the PUG Working Group as referred to in paragraph (2) are all Heads/Leaders of PD.  
4. Further provisions regarding the duties and procedures for membership of the PUG Working Group are regulated by Regent Regulation.

#### Article 7

( 1 ) In order to strengthen and develop PUG, the Regent may establish a PUG Focal Point.  

( 2 ) The PUG Focal Point as referred to in paragraph (1) is a PD apparatus that has the ability to carry out gender mainstreaming in their respective work units.  

( 3 ) The Focal Point as referred to in paragraph (1) is established in each PD.  

( 4 ) Further provisions regarding the duties, membership composition and procedures for establishing the Focal Point are implemented in accordance with the provisions of laws and regulations.  
10-

#### Article 8

Women's empowerment in the fields of education, health, employment, politics, law, economy, culture, and social in community organizations at the Regency level as referred to in Article 3 paragraph (2) letter c constitutes part of temporary special policies provided by the Regency Government.

#### Article 9

Women's empowerment in the field of education is implemented by means including:
a. facilitating women to obtain education and teaching in all types, levels and pathways of education in accordance with predetermined requirements;  
b. education policies that encourage equity, improvement and quality relevance of education for all genders; and  
c. equitable learning opportunities for women and men.

#### Article 10

Women's empowerment in the field of health is implemented by means including:
a. facilitating women's health through health program activities implemented by the government such as:
1. Maternal mortality reduction program;  
2. Maternal and child health (KIA) program;  
3. Healthy environment, water sanitation and sanitation program;  
4. Malaria eradication program;  
5. Pulmonary tuberculosis eradication program;  
6. HIV/AIDS prevention program;  
7. Community nutrition improvement program;  
b. Women are given the right to obtain special protection in the implementation of their work or profession against matters that may threaten their safety and/or health;  
c. Women are given special rights inherent to women due to their reproductive function, guaranteed and protected by law.  
11-

#### Article 11

Women's empowerment in the field of employment is implemented by means including:
a. equal wages for male and female workers for the same work;  
b. prohibition of discrimination against female workers;  
c. prohibition of termination of employment for female workers who marry, become pregnant, and give birth;  
d. guidelines for employing female workers at night;  
e. providing flexibility for female workers who breastfeed children;  
f. prohibition of discrimination against female workers in company regulations; and  
g. regarding malnutrition in female workers.

#### Article 12

Women's empowerment in the field of politics is implemented by means including:
a. facilitating the improvement of human resources for prospective legislative candidates at the regency level, and members of political parties/women's community organizations in order to fulfill women's representation in legislative institutions in accordance with laws and regulations;  
b. facilitating increased political participation of women in legislative institutions;  
c. providing facilities and infrastructure for women's community organizations or community organizations whose vision and mission is to fight for the fulfillment of rights and protection of women in the Regency in accordance with Regional Financial capacity;  
d. implementing socialization in order to increase awareness of women's rights and women's participation in the field of politics;  
e. encouraging increased involvement of women in community organizations and decision-making activities within the Regency Government; and  
f. encouraging the establishment of women's organizations/forums.

#### Article 13

Women's empowerment in the field of law is implemented by means including:
a. facilitating the improvement of legal knowledge and awareness for women; and  
b. encouraging the establishment of organizations that provide free legal services for women.  
12-

#### Article 14

1. Women's empowerment in the field of economy is implemented by means including:
a. facilitating women in efforts to improve family economy;  
b. may provide capital assistance to women, especially migrant workers, women in remote areas, women as heads of households, and women victims of domestic violence; and  
c. implementing household industry and non-agricultural industry development programs.  
2. Procedures for providing capital assistance as referred to in paragraph (1) letter b are regulated by Regent Regulation.

#### Article 15

Empowerment of women in the field of culture is implemented by means including:
a. facilitating women in preserving original regional culture;  
b. encouraging women's activities in various cultural arts activities.

#### Article 16

Empowerment of women in the social field is implemented by means including:
a. facilitating women in increasing social activities in the community environment; and  
b. encouraging women's activities as agents of change in society.

## Part Three

Scope of Women's Empowerment

### Paragraph 1

General

#### Article 17

Protection of Women is carried out by means of:
a. prevention of violence against women;  
b. provision of services and advanced referral handling; and  
c. strengthening and development of institutions providing women's protection services.  
13-

### Paragraph 2

Prevention

#### Article 18

( 1 ) Prevention of violence against women as referred to in Article 17 letter a is implemented by means of increasing family and community awareness.  

( 2 ) Increasing family and community awareness as referred to in paragraph (1) is carried out in an integrated and continuous manner in the form of socialization, guidance and training, and/or counseling.  

( 3 ) Socialization, guidance and training, and/or counseling as referred to in paragraph (2) is implemented by technical PD in charge, institutions, organizations, mass media, business world, academics, law enforcers, community and Regional Technical Implementation Units in accordance with their duties and functions.

#### Article 19

1. In addition to prevention of violence against women as referred to in Article 18, efforts to prevent violence against women are carried out by increasing women's insight, knowledge, skills, and independence.  
2. Prevention of violence against women as referred to in paragraph (1) is carried out by means of:
a. increasing informal education;  
b. opening accessibility to obtain education, training, funding, income enhancement, and social services; and  
c. providing access to informal employment for women.

### Paragraph 3

Provision of Services and Advanced Referral Handling

#### Article 20

Provision of services and advanced referral handling as referred to in Article 17 letter b is intended for:
a. women victims of domestic violence;  
b. women victims of criminal acts of trafficking in persons;  
c. women in emergency situations and special conditions; and  
d. women victims of other acts of violence requiring coordination at the Regency level.  
14-

#### Article 21

( 1 ) In addition to the provision of services and referral service handling as referred to in Article 20, the Regency Government is obliged to provide special services for:
a. women who are included in vulnerable and/or minority groups;  
b. elderly women; and  
c. women with special needs.  
( 2 ) Special services as referred to in paragraph (1) are provided in the form of:
a. ease in obtaining and enjoying their rights in accordance with the provisions of laws and regulations;  
b. ease in access to economic resources and natural resources, social security, and basic public services; and  
c. provision of specific needs of women in emergency situations and special conditions.  
( 3 ) Further provisions regarding special services as referred to in paragraph (2) are regulated by Regent Regulation.

#### Article 22

1. In the framework of providing services and advanced referral handling for women as referred to in Article 20, the Regency Government may establish:
a. Women and Children Protection (PPA) unit at Regency level;  
b. task force; and/or  
c. task unit.  
2. PPA unit, task force, and/or task unit as referred to in paragraph (1) are established by Regent Regulation.  
3. Further provisions regarding duties, membership, procedures and/or working procedures of the PPA unit as referred to in paragraph (2) are regulated by Regent Regulation.  
15-

### Paragraph 4

Strengthening and Development of Institutions Providing Women's Protection Services

#### Article 23

Strengthening of institutions providing women's protection services is carried out by:
a. establishment of coordination forum or other forums in order to support handling of victims of violence against women;  
b. provision of service units and legal protection assistance for women victims of violence who conduct data collection related to violence against women;  
c. provision of information education communication, advocacy, socialization, and technical guidance;  
d. procurement of facilities and infrastructure for protection and services for women in disaster and evacuation areas;  
e. enhancement of cross-sectoral coordination;  
f. establishment of working groups;  
g. formulation of regional action plans; and  
h. information education communication both through electronic and print media and simulation regarding women's protection.

# CHAPTER III

## IMPLEMENTATION OF CHILD PROTECTION

## Part One

General

#### Article 24

1. In the framework of implementing Child Protection, the Regency Government is obliged to carry out activities:
a. formulation of policies, programs, and regional development activities with child rights perspective which are set forth in regional development planning documents;  
b. formulation of norms, standard procedures and assessment criteria for fulfillment and protection of children's rights which refer to norms, standards, procedures and criteria established by the Government;  
c. coordination of child-friendly Regency development is implemented in accordance with the provisions of laws and regulations;  
d. facilitation of the implementation of regional children's forum;  
16-
e. enhancement of community participation in the implementation and protection of children's rights;  
f. coordination and cooperation in the fulfillment and protection of Children;  
g. monitoring and evaluation of the implementation of programs for fulfillment and protection of Children;  
h. guidance of child protection institutions; and  
i. providing special protection for Children.  
2. In addition to the activities as referred to in paragraph (1), the Regency Government may conduct supervision and facilitation of child advocacy institutions in the Regency involved in assistance to Children requiring special handling.  
3. Supervision and facilitation as referred to in paragraph (2) are implemented in accordance with the provisions of laws and regulations.  
4. Provision of facilitation as referred to in paragraph (3) is adjusted to regional financial capacity.

#### Article 25

1. Coordination of child-friendly regency development as referred to in Article 24 paragraph (1) letter c is implemented to encourage acceleration of the realization of Child-Friendly Regency.  
2. Coordination as referred to in paragraph (1) is implemented by relevant agencies in accordance with their main duties and functions.  
3. Further provisions regarding the form and procedures for coordination as referred to in paragraph (1) are regulated in Regent Regulation.

#### Article 26

1. Guidance of Child protection institutions as referred to in Article 24 paragraph (1) letter h is implemented in order to increase the effectiveness of implementation of fulfillment of children's rights.  
2. Guidance of Child protection institutions as referred to in paragraph (1) is carried out by the Head of PD whose duties and functions are to administer government affairs in the field of child protection.  
3. Further provisions regarding guidance and institutional development as referred to in paragraph (1) are regulated in Regent Regulation.  
17-

#### Article 27

Activities in the Implementation of child protection in the Regency include:
a. fulfillment of children's rights; and  
b. special protection of children.

## Part Two

Fulfillment of Children's Rights

#### Article 28

Fulfillment of children's rights is the obligation of the Regency Government, Parents, and community.

#### Article 29

( 1 ) The Regency Government is obliged to fulfill children's rights in the fields of:
a. civil rights and freedoms;  
b. family environment and alternative care;  
c. basic health and welfare;  
d. education, use of leisure time, and arts and cultural activities.  

( 2 ) In fulfilling children's rights as referred to in paragraph (1) letter a, the Regency Government is obliged to:
a. endeavor for children to obtain birth certificates free of charge;  
b. provide infrastructure and facilities needed by children to express themselves, think, and create;  
c. provide infrastructure and facilities so that children can obtain the knowledge they need.  

3. In fulfilling children's rights as referred to in paragraph (1) letter b, the Regency Government makes efforts to:
a. provide guidance to families or substitute families to fulfill the rights of children needed during their care;  
b. provide family consultation institutions for child caregivers;  
c. provide child social welfare institutions as an alternative place for child caregivers in the event that the family or substitute family cannot provide proper care;  
d. provide guidance to children as a vulnerable group.  

4. In fulfilling children's rights as referred to in paragraph (1) letter c, the Regency Government makes efforts to:
-18-
a. reduce infant mortality rates;  
b. reduce malnutrition rates for children;  
c. provide breast milk for infants;  
d. provide immunization for children;  
e. provide reproductive health services;  
f. increase access to welfare improvement for poor families;  
g. increase access to clean water for households;  
h. create smoke-free zones;  
i. child-friendly community health center.  

5. In fulfilling children's rights as referred to in paragraph (1) letter d, the Regency Government makes efforts to:
a. provide early childhood education;  
b. implement 12 years of compulsory education;  
c. establish child-friendly schools;  
d. establish safe routes to/from school;  
e. provide child-friendly creative and recreational activity facilities;  

6. Further provisions regarding the form and procedures for fulfilling children's rights as referred to in paragraph (1) shall be regulated in a Regent Regulation.

#### Article 30

( 1 ) Parents are obliged and responsible to:
a. care for, nurture, educate, and protect children;  
b. develop children according to their abilities, talents, and interests;  
c. prevent child marriage.  

( 2 ) In the event that parents are absent, or their whereabouts are unknown, or due to a certain reason, cannot carry out their obligations and responsibilities, then the obligations and responsibilities as referred to in paragraph (1) may be transferred to the family, which shall be implemented in accordance with the provisions of laws and regulations.

#### Article 31

( 1 ) The Regency Government is obliged and responsible to carry out prevention and handling of cases of child victims of neglect, violence, discrimination, exploitation, child trafficking, maltreatment, narcotics, psychotropics, and other addictive substances, and/or pornography.  
19- ( 2 ) The responsibilities as referred to in paragraph (1) include:
a. respecting, fulfilling, and protecting children's rights;  
b. conducting socialization about the adverse effects of neglect, violence, discrimination, exploitation, child trafficking, maltreatment, narcotics, psychotropics, and other addictive substances, and/or pornography;  
c. making policies and/or policy changes regarding child protection;  
d. providing assistance;  
e. conducting training on handling cases of neglect, violence, discrimination, exploitation, child trafficking, maltreatment, narcotics, psychotropics, and other addictive substances, and/or pornography against children for stakeholders;  
f. assisting the social rehabilitation and social reintegration process;  
g. providing temporary shelters for children;  
h. conducting coordination and cooperation with stakeholders related to handling child cases.

Special Protection for Children

b. children in conflict with the law;  
c. children from minority and isolated groups;  
d. children who are economically and/or sexually exploited;  
e. children who are victims of narcotics, alcohol, psychotropics, and addictive substance abuse;  
f. children who are victims of pornography;  
g. children with HIV/AIDS;  
h. child victims of kidnapping, sale, and/or trafficking;  
i. child victims of physical and/or mental violence;  
j. child victims of sexual crimes;  
k. child victims of terrorist networks;

### Paragraph 13

Children Victims of Maltreatment and Neglect

#### Article 102

Special Protection for Children Victims of Maltreatment and Children Victims of Neglect as referred to in Article 32 paragraph (1) letter m shall be carried out through efforts of supervision, prevention, care, Counseling, Social Rehabilitation, and Social Assistance.

#### Article 103

Supervision as referred to in Article 102 shall be carried out by way of:

a. mapping of Children who are vulnerable to maltreatment and neglect; and
b. dissemination and advocacy of laws and regulations.

#### Article 104

a. providing guidance to parents regarding the rights of Children not to be maltreated and neglected;  
b. providing basic needs services;  
c. providing access to education; and
d. providing skills training or independent work.

#### Article 105

Care as referred to in Article 102 shall be carried out in the form of:

a. medical rehabilitation; and/or
b. family care or Substitute Family for optimal growth and development.

The provisions regarding Social Rehabilitation as referred to in Article 83 shall apply mutatis mutandis to the form of Social Rehabilitation as referred to in Article 102.

Special Protection for Children Victims of Maltreatment and Neglect as referred to in Article 102 shall be carried out by the Regency Government in accordance with its duties, functions, and authorities.

Children With Deviant Social Behavior

c. understanding not to commit violence and destruction; and

### Paragraph 15

Children Who Are Victims of Stigmatization from Labeling Related to the Condition of Their Parents

#### Article 113

Special Protection for Children Who Are Victims of Stigmatization from Labeling related to the Condition of Their Parents as referred to in Article 32 paragraph (1) letter o shall be carried out through Counseling, Social Rehabilitation, and Social Assistance.

#### Article 114

The provisions regarding Social Rehabilitation as referred to in Article 83 shall apply mutatis mutandis to the form of Social Rehabilitation as referred to in Article 113.

#### Article 115

1. Children who have received Special Protection as referred to in Article 113 shall be returned to their family or the Community.  
2. Before being returned to the family or the Community as referred to in paragraph (1), the steps that must be taken are:

a. preparing the psychological condition of Children Who Are Victims of Stigmatization from Labeling related to the Condition of Their Parents to be willing to be returned to the family or the Community;  
b. ensuring the family, Substitute Family, or the Community to accept and no longer label and discriminate against Children Who Are Victims of Stigmatization from Labeling related to the Condition of Their Parents;  
c. tracing another Substitute Family if there is rejection from the family or the Community;  
d. ensuring Children Who Are Victims of Stigmatization from Labeling related to the Condition of Their Parents are in a safe, comfortable condition, and their needs are met; and

e. conducting monitoring and evaluation of the development of Children Who Are Victims of Stigmatization from Labeling related to the Condition of Their Parents who have been returned to the family, Substitute Family, or the Community.

#### Article 116

The steps for monitoring and evaluation of the development of Children Who Are Victims of Stigmatization from Labeling related to the Condition of Their Parents who have been returned to the family, Substitute Family, or the Community as referred to in Article 115 paragraph (2) letter e shall be implemented by the Regency Government in accordance with its duties, functions, and authorities.

#### Article 117

Counseling, Social Rehabilitation, and Social Assistance as referred to in Article 113 shall be carried out by the Regency Government in accordance with its duties, functions, and authorities.

#### Article 118

To prevent the occurrence of stigmatization from labeling of Children related to the condition of their parents, the Regency Government shall:

a. provide education to the Community and play an active role in eliminating stigma against Children who are labeled related to the condition of their parents;  

b. provide space for Children who are labeled related to the condition of their parents to obtain recreational activities; and
c. coordinate with service units that handle the protection of Children who are labeled related to the condition of their parents in the event of potential violence and discrimination against Children in the Regency.

# CHAPTER IV

## IMPROVING FAMILY QUALITY

#### Article 121

Improving family quality shall be implemented in order to realize gender equality and children's rights.

#### Article 122

Improving family quality shall be carried out through:

a. prevention of child marriage;  
b. fulfillment of children's rights to exclusive breastfeeding;  
c. bringing mothers closer to health services;  
d. increasing family resilience.

#### Article 123

Prevention of child marriage as referred to in Article 122 letter a aims to:

a. increase the productivity of girls;  
b. develop the potential of girls;  
c. prevent pregnancy at too young an age;  
d. reduce maternal and infant mortality rates;  
e. reduce poverty rates; and
f. prevent the occurrence of domestic violence.

#### Article 124

The fulfillment of children's rights to exclusive breastfeeding as referred to in Article 123 letter b aims to:
a. guarantee the right of every child to obtain exclusive breastfeeding;  
b. guarantee the health of children and optimal growth and development, especially during the golden age; and  
c. prevent malnutrition.

#### Article 125

Bringing mothers closer to health services as referred to in Article 122 letter c aims to:
a. guarantee the health of mothers and children before, during, and after childbirth; and  
b. increase mothers' insight and knowledge about their own health and that of their families.

#### Article 126

Family resilience as referred to in Article 122 letter d aims to:
a. fulfill the rights of family members;  
b. improve the quality of life of family members; and  
c. improve family welfare.

#### Article 127

Further provisions regarding the form of activities and procedures for implementing family quality improvement as referred to in Article 122 shall be regulated by Regent Regulation.

# CHAPTER VII

## COMMUNITY PARTICIPATION

#### Article 131

1. The community may participate in organizing the protection of women and the protection of children.

3. The role of the community in organizing child protection as referred to in paragraph (1) is carried out by:

c. reporting to the competent authorities if violations of children's rights occur;  
d. playing an active role in the rehabilitation and social reintegration process for children;  
e. conducting monitoring, supervision and being jointly responsible for the organization of child protection;

g. forming a Regency area coordination forum;  
h. playing an active role in eliminating negative labeling of Children; and  
i. providing space for children to participate and express their opinions.

5. The role of mass media as referred to in paragraph (2) is carried out through the dissemination of information and educational materials that are beneficial from the social, cultural, educational, religious and health aspects of children with due regard to the best interests of the Child.

a. child-perspective corporate policies;
