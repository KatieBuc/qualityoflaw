# CHAPTER I

## GENERAL PROVISIONS

#### Article 1 In this Regional Regulation what is meant by: 1. Regency is the Regency of Penukal Abab Lematang Ilir. 2. Regency Government is the Regent as an element of regency government administration who leads the implementation of government affairs that are the authority of the autonomous region.

3. Regent is the Regent of Penukal Abab Lematang Ilir. 4. Regency Regional Apparatus is an auxiliary element of the Regent and the Regency Regional House of Representatives in the administration of government affairs that are the authority of the regency region.
5. Child is a person who has not reached the age of 18 (eighteen) years, including a child still in the womb. 6. Woman is a human being of female sex and a person who is legally recognized as a woman. 7. Fulfillment of Children's Rights is all activities to guarantee and protect children and their rights so that they can live, grow, develop and participate optimally in accordance with human dignity and receive protection from violence and discrimination. 8. Violence is any act that results in misery or suffering whether physical, sexual, psychological, and/or domestic neglect including threats to commit acts, coercion, or unlawful deprivation of a person's freedom. 9. Victim is a woman and/or child who experiences misery and/or suffering either directly or indirectly as a result of violence occurring in the area of Penukal Abab Lematang Ilir Regency. 10. Protection of women is all activities aimed at providing a sense of security carried out by the police, prosecutor's office, courts, social institutions, or other parties who know or hear that violence against women will or has occurred.
11. Child protection is all activities to guarantee and protect children and their rights so that they can live, grow, develop and participate optimally in accordance with human dignity and receive protection from violence and discrimination.
12. Prevention is activities carried out to prevent the occurrence of acts of violence against women and children. 13. Service is action taken as soon as possible to victims when seeing, hearing and knowing that violence against victims will, is or has occurred.
14. Empowerment is the strengthening of women victims of violence to be able to work and work independently after they are recovered and provided with health and social rehabilitation services.
15. Health Rehabilitation is an effort to restore the condition of victims including physical, psychological and social health so that victims can carry out their roles again properly and naturally both in the family and in society.
16. Social rehabilitation is a service aimed at restoring and developing the ability of a person experiencing social dysfunction so that they can carry out their social functions properly.
17. Repatriation is an effort to return victims of violence from the regional area to their area of origin.
18. Social reintegration is an effort to reunite victims with family, substitute family or community that can provide protection and fulfillment of needs for victims.
19. Integrated Service Center hereinafter abbreviated as PPT, is a unit that organizes integrated services for victims of violence and is integrated with PPT that organizes integrated services for witnesses and/or victims of criminal acts of human trafficking.

#### Article 2 The Implementation of Protection of Women and Children is carried out based on the principles of:

   - a. respect and protection of human rights; b. justice and gender equality;
   - c. non-discrimination;
   - d. best interests of the Child;
   - e. right to life, survival, and development; f. Empowerment; and
   - g. legal certainty.

#### Article 3 Protection of Women and Children aims to: a. ensure the fulfillment of the rights of Women and Children; b. maintain the integrity of the household so that a harmonious family is realized;

   - c. prevent violence against Women and Children; d. protect and provide a sense of security for Women and Children;
   - e. provide services to Women and Children victims of acts of violence;
   - f. provide protection to reporters and witnesses; and g. carry out empowerment of Women victims of violence.

# CHAPTER II

## PROTECTION OF WOMEN AND CHILDREN

## Part One

Rights of Women Article 4 Every Woman has the right:

   - a. to live, maintain life and her existence; b. to form a family and continue descendants through legal marriage;
   - c. to develop herself through the fulfillment of her basic needs; d. to obtain education and benefit from science and technology, arts and culture, in order to improve the quality of her life and for the welfare of mankind;
   - e. to advance herself in fighting for her rights collectively to build society, nation and state;
   - f. to recognition, guarantee, protection and fair legal certainty as well as equal treatment before the law;
   - g. to work and receive fair and proper remuneration and treatment in employment relations;
   - h. to obtain equal opportunities in government;
      - i. to freedom of association, assembly and expression of opinion;
   - j. to protection of her person, family, honor, dignity and property under her control, and the right to a sense of security and protection from threats of fear to do or not do something which constitutes a basic right;
   - k. to be free from torture or treatment that degrades human dignity and the right to obtain political asylum from other countries;
   - l. to live in physical and spiritual prosperity, to reside and obtain a good and healthy living environment, and the right to obtain health services;
   - m. to social security that enables her full development as a dignified human being;
   - n. to equality of position in law and government;
   - o. to work and a livelihood worthy of humanity;
   - p. in efforts to defend the state;
   - q. to communicate and obtain information to develop her personal and social environment, and the right to seek, obtain, possess, store, process, and convey information using all types of available channels;
   - r. to embrace her respective religion and to worship according to her religion and belief; and
   - s. to obtain education.

Second Part Fulfillment of Children's Rights

### Paragraph 1

Rights and Obligations of Children Article 5 Every Child has the right to:

   - a. birth registration;
   - b. obtain health;
   - c. obtain education; and
   - d. social welfare.

#### Article 6 Every Child has the obligation to:

   - a. respect parents, guardians and teachers;
   - b. love family, community and care for friends;
   - c. love the homeland, nation and state;
   - d. perform worship in accordance with their religion and belief;
   - e. study and develop themselves in accordance with their abilities and talents, interests; and
   - f. have noble character.

### Paragraph 2 Birth Registration

#### Article 7

For the registration of the birth of a child as referred to in Article 5 letter a, the Regency Government is obligated to issue a birth certificate in accordance with the provisions of Laws and Regulations.

### Paragraph 3

Health Article 8 (1) The Implementation of the Fulfillment of Children's Rights in the field of health as referred to in Article 5 letter b, the Regency Government is obligated to provide facilities and organize comprehensive health services for children.

2. Comprehensive health services as referred to in paragraph (1) include:
   - a. promotive, preventive, curative and rehabilitative efforts for basic health services; and
   - b. exemption from cost burden for malnourished children, Children with disabilities, children with special needs, street children, children infected with HIV/AIDS, child workers, child victims of narcotics abuse, alcohol, psychotropic substances and other addictive substances (NAPZA) Children, who become victims of violence, such as neglect, economically exploited, sexually and victims of human trafficking.
3. Exemption from cost burden for Children as referred to in paragraph (2) letter b constitutes Children from poor families unless regulated differently in accordance with the health financing program established by the government.

#### Article 9 The Regency Government together with the community, family and parents are obligated to ensure that Children who are born are protected from diseases that threaten the continuity of life and/or cause disability.

### Paragraph 4

Education Article 10 (1) The Implementation of Fulfillment of Children's Rights in the field of education as referred to in Article 5 letter c, the Regency Government guarantees the implementation of a 9 (nine) year compulsory education program.

2. Every organizer of an education unit is obligated to provide opportunities for Children to obtain educational services without discrimination.

#### Article 11 Every organizer of an education unit is prohibited from expelling Children from educational institutions unless there is a guarantee for the continuity of the Child's education.

#### Article 12 Every organizer of an education unit is obligated to coordinate with relevant child protection agencies if they find Children dropping out of school due to being victims of acts of violence.

#### Article 13 Children with disabilities and Children with special needs are given equal opportunities to obtain educational services:

   - a. formal;
   - b. nonformal;
   - c. informal;
   - d. special education; and
   - e. inclusion.

#### Article 14 Early Childhood Education for ages 0 (zero) to 3 (three) years and 4 (four) to 6 (six) years can be organized by:

   - a. Posyandu institutions;
   - b. Posyandu gardens;
   - c. Early Childhood Education Posts; and
   - d. similar Early Childhood Education unit institutions.

### Paragraph 5

Social Welfare Article 15 (1) The implementation of Child Protection and Empowerment in the field of social welfare as referred to in Article 5 letter d includes:

   - a. children in conflict with the law;
   - b. child victims of violence who are exploited economically and sexually;
   - c. neglected children;
   - d. child victims of kidnapping and child victims of human trafficking;
   - e. child victims of narcotics, alcohol, psychotropic and other addictive substance abuse (NAPZA);
   - f. children infected with HIV/AIDS;
   - g. children who do not have parents and are cared for by other parties or families who are unable;
   - h. street children;
      - i. child victims of natural disasters or social disasters; j. children with physical and mental disabilities;
   - k. children of migrant worker families;
   - l. children living in or around prostitution locations; and
   - m. child victims of other maltreatment.
2. Social welfare as referred to in paragraph (1) may be in the form of ease of access to services: a. health;
   - b. education;
   - c. legal aid;
   - d. assistance;
   - e. social, mental and spiritual guidance;
   - f. social rehabilitation;
   - g. empowerment;
   - h. social assistance; and/or
      - i. reintegration.
3. Special infrastructure for persons with disabilities in public places.

#### Article 16

1. The Regency Government is obligated to protect the fulfillment of the rights of child workers in the informal sector.
2. Child workers in the informal sector as referred to in paragraph (1) include:
   - a. domestic workers;
   - b. shoe shiners;
   - c. street vendors;
   - d. scavengers;
   - e. parking attendants;
   - f. children who work in the field of agriculture, animal husbandry and plantations;
   - g. children who work in the field of mining; and h. other informal sector work that employs Children.
3. Every person who employs Children in the informal sector is obligated to observe the requirements:
   - a. minimum age of 15 (fifteen) years, except for the category of Worst Forms of Child Labor minimum 18 (eighteen) years;
   - b. obtain written consent from the parents/guardian of the child worker;
   - c. have a written employment agreement between the employer and the parents/guardian of the child worker and obtain validation from the authorized agency;
   - d. not employed at night;
   - e. working time of at most 3 (three) hours per day; f. not employed in a place or environment that can disrupt the child's growth and development process, whether physical, mental, moral and intellectual or the Child's health;
   - g. provide opportunities to obtain education in accordance with their talents and interests;
   - h. employ for light types of work; and i. provide one day off per week.

#### Article 17 The fulfillment of the rights of child workers in the informal sector as referred to in Article 16 paragraph (1) includes:

   - a. counseling to stakeholders about the rights of Children;
   - b. psychological, medical and legal service assistance and socio-economic reintegration;
   - c. empowering families through providing training, business capital stimulus and assistance;
   - d. scholarships to continue formal education to higher levels; and
   - e. non-formal education and skills training for those who do not pursue formal education.

## Part Three Forms of Violence Article 18 Forms of violence against Women and Children include: a. physical violence;

   - b. psychological violence;
   - c. sexual violence;
   - d. neglect;
   - e. exploitation; and/or
   - f. other violence.

## Part Four Prevention of Violence against Women and Children Article 19 (1) Efforts to prevent violence against Women and Children are carried out in an integrated manner by the Regency Government and coordinated by the technical Regional Apparatus.

2. Prevention efforts as referred to in paragraph (1) are implemented by means of:
   - a. conducting guidance towards harmonious household integrity;
   - b. forming work networks in efforts to prevent violence;
   - c. conducting coordination, integration, synchronization of violence prevention based on partnership patterns;
   - d. establishing a violence prevention system;
   - e. conducting socialization regarding laws and regulations related to the protection of Women and Children victims of violence; and
   - f. providing critical education regarding the rights of Women and Children for the community.

#### Article 20 Efforts to prevent violence against Women and Children as referred to in Article 19 paragraph (1) in addition to the Regency Government are also implemented by:

   - a. family and/or closest relatives;
   - b. community;
   - c. educational institutions; and
   - d. Non-Governmental Organizations.

## Part Five

Handling of Women and Children Victims of Violence Article 21 The implementation of handling of Women and Children victims of violence shall be carried out with:

   - a. speed;
   - b. safety and comfort;
   - c. a sense of empathy;
   - d. non-discrimination;
   - e. easy accessibility;
   - f. no charges imposed; and
   - g. guaranteed confidentiality.

#### Article 22 Forms of handling of Women and Children victims of violence as referred to in Article 21 include services for:

   - a. complaints, consultation, and counseling;
   - b. assistance;
   - c. medical rehabilitation;
   - d. social rehabilitation;
   - e. legal matters; and
   - f. social reintegration.

## Part Six

Financing Article 23 The financing required for the implementation of the Protection of Women and Children shall be charged to:

   - a. Regional Revenue and Expenditure Budget; and
   - b. Other lawful and non-binding sources of funds in accordance with the provisions of applicable Laws and Regulations.

# CHAPTER III

## INTEGRATED SERVICE CENTER (PPT)

#### Article 24 (1) The provision of services to Women and Children victims of violence is carried out in an integrated manner by the PPT. (2) The PPT as referred to in paragraph (1) is an integrated part and constitutes one unity with the PPT that provides integrated services for witnesses and/or victims of criminal acts of human trafficking. (3) The provision of services to Women and Children victims of violence as referred to in paragraph (1) includes:

   - a. providing fast and free services to victims; b. providing protection and fulfillment of victims' rights to health rehabilitation, social rehabilitation, repatriation, social reintegration and legal assistance; c. cooperating with certain institutions in providing translators and volunteer assistants needed for victims;
   - d. networking with government or private hospitals for treatment and health recovery of victims as well as cooperating with witness and victim protection institutions, social protection houses or trauma centers owned by the government, community or other institutions for health recovery of victims;
   - e. providing convenience, comfort and safety for victims;
   - f. maintaining the confidentiality of victims; and
   - g. providing fulfillment of legal assistance for victims. (4) Further provisions regarding the procedures and mechanisms for the implementation of PPT are regulated by Regent Regulation.

# CHAPTER IV

## RIGHTS OF WOMEN AND CHILDREN VICTIMS OF VIOLENCE

#### Article 25

Every Woman and Child victim of violence has the right to:

   - a. complaint handling;
   - b. have their dignity and honor as human beings respected;
   - c. obtain protection from family, community, regional government and/or other parties either temporarily or based on a protection order from the court;
   - d. health and psychological recovery from the suffering experienced by the victim;
   - e. determine their own decisions;
   - f. special handling related to the confidentiality of the victim;
   - g. obtain information;
   - h. confidentiality of their identity;
      - i. social rehabilitation;
   - j. assistance by social workers and legal aid at every level of the examination process in accordance with the provisions of Laws and Regulations; and
   - k. spiritual guidance services.

#### Article 26

Women and children victims of violence in addition to having rights as referred to in Article 25, also receive special rights as follows:

   - a. right to survival;
   - b. grow and develop;
   - c. obtain equal protection; and
   - d. participate in community life.

# CHAPTER V

## MONITORING AND EVALUATION

#### Article 27 (1) The Regency Government conducts monitoring to ensure synergy, balance and effectiveness of integrated measures in the implementation of policies, programs, activities, Protection of Women and Children.

2. Monitoring as referred to in paragraph (1) shall be carried out by Regional Apparatus and PPT.
3. Monitoring as referred to in paragraph (1) is to determine the progress and obstacles in the implementation of policies, programs and activities of Protection of Women and Children.
4. Monitoring shall be conducted periodically through coordination and direct monitoring of Regional Apparatus implementing policies, activities of Protection of Women and Children.
5. Monitoring stages as referred to in paragraph (1) include:
   - a. planning
   - b. policy implementation;
   - c. program implementation; and
   - d. Protection of Women and Children activities every year. Article 28 (1) Evaluation of the implementation of policies, programs and activities of Protection of Women and Children shall be conducted at the end of each Budget year or if necessary according to needs.
2. The results of evaluation of the implementation of policies, programs and activities of Protection of Women and Children as referred to in paragraph (1) shall be used as input material for the formulation of policies, programs and activities for the following year.

   - a. provision of implementation instructions;
   - b. guidance;
   - c. supervision;

   - b. conducting socialization of women's and children's rights independently;
   - c. providing first aid to victims; and d. reporting to the competent authority if violence against victims occurs in their environment. (2) Community participation as referred to in paragraph (1) is carried out by:
   - a. individuals;

   - d. educational institutions;

# CHAPTER VIII

INVESTIGATION PROVISIONS Article 31 (1) Certain Civil Servant Officials within the Regency Government who are given special authority as Investigators to conduct investigations of criminal acts as regulated in the provisions of laws and regulations.

2. Investigators as referred to in paragraph (1) are certain Civil Servants within the Regency Government who are appointed by authorized officials in accordance with the provisions of laws and regulations.
3. The authority of Investigators as referred to in paragraph (1) includes:
   - a. receiving reports or complaints from a person regarding the existence of a criminal act;
   - b. taking first action at that time at the scene and conducting examination;
   - c. ordering a suspect to stop and examining the suspect's identity card;
   - d. seizing objects or documents;
   - e. taking fingerprints and photographing a person;
   - f. summoning persons to be heard and examined as suspects or witnesses;
   - g. bringing in expert witnesses needed in connection with the examination of the case;
   - h. conducting termination of investigation after receiving instruction from the Investigator that there is insufficient evidence or the incident does not constitute a criminal act and subsequently through the investigator notifying this matter to the Public Prosecutor, the suspect or their family;
      - i. conducting other actions according to law that can be accounted for.
4. Investigators as referred to in paragraph (1) notify the commencement of investigation and submit the results of their investigation to the Public Prosecutor through the Investigator Official of the Police of the Republic of Indonesia, in accordance with the provisions stipulated in the Criminal Procedure Code.

##### THE IMPLEMENTATION OF PROTECTION FOR WOMEN AND CHILDREN

##### I. GENERAL

Violence against women and children is a violation of human rights, therefore their dignity and honor need to be protected and their right to life guaranteed in accordance with their nature and disposition without discrimination. The fate of women and children victims of violence must be given attention by the regional government. The high number of cases of violence against women and children is often caused by factors that develop in society, for example low economic levels, education, environments located in the industrial sector. Therefore, victims of violence such as this need to receive protection in accordance with the principles of justice, truth, legal certainty, equality and upholding human rights.

For the empowerment of victims of violence, the district government establishes an Integrated Service Center (PPT) which is a generic term that can be used by various institutions that provide services for victims of violence such as the Integrated Service Center for Women and Children Empowerment (P2TP2A), Safe Houses, Child Social Protection Houses (RPSA), Trauma Center Protection Houses (RPTC), Women's Social Protection Houses (RPSW), Shelters, etc.

The District Government is obligated and responsible to implement efforts to protect women and children through implementing protection policies for women and children applied by the government, establishing policies, programs, and protection activities for women and children, establishing PPT, guaranteeing the implementation of service facilities for victims, seeking effectiveness and efficiency for the victim recovery process, and seeking the creation of cooperation and coordination in efforts to protect women and children.

#### Article 18 Point a What is meant by "physical violence" is an act that results in pain, injury, wounds or disability to a person's body, miscarriage, unconsciousness and/or causes death.

Point b What is meant by "psychological violence" is an act that results in fear, loss of self-confidence, loss of ability to act, feelings of helplessness and/or severe psychological suffering to a person.

Point c What is meant by "sexual violence" is an act in the form of sexual harassment, forced sexual intercourse, forced sexual intercourse in an unnatural or unwanted manner and/or forced sexual intercourse with another person for commercial purposes and/or certain purposes.

Point d What is meant by "neglect" is an act that results in the failure to fulfill a child's needs properly, whether physical, mental, spiritual or social, committed by parents, guardians, or any other party responsible for their care; the act of deliberately neglecting to maintain, care for, or look after a child as appropriate, committed by parents, guardians, or other parties responsible for their care; the act of neglecting a person within the household scope, whereas according to the law applicable to them or due to agreement or contract they are obliged to provide livelihood, care, or maintenance to that person; and/or an act that results in economic dependence by limiting and/or prohibiting proper work inside or outside the home so that the victim is under the control of that person.

Point e What is meant by "exploitation" is an act that exploits economically or sexually with the intention of benefiting oneself or others; an act with or without the victim's consent including prostitution, forced labor or services, slavery or similar practices, oppression, extortion, utilization of physical, sexual, reproductive organs, or unlawfully transferring or transplanting organs and/or body tissues or utilizing the labor or ability of a person by another party to obtain material or immaterial benefits; and/or any form of utilization of sexual organs or other body organs of the victim to obtain benefits, including but not limited to all activities of prostitution or indecency.

Point f What is meant by "other violence" is the threat of violence including: any unlawful act in the form of speech, writing, images, symbols, or body movements, whether with or without using means that cause fear or restrict a person's fundamental freedom; and coercion, including: a situation where a person/victim is ordered to do something in such a way that the person does something contrary to their own will.

#### Article 21 Point a What is meant by "quick" is immediate action taken without complications or simplified procedures.

Point b What is meant by "safe and comfortable" is a guarantee of service protection that feels comfortable, undisturbed, and served in a friendly, respectful and appreciative manner.

Point c What is meant by "empathy" is an act of appreciating, respecting, loving, befriending, and making happy that aims to please and calm the victim's heart.

Point d What is meant by "non-discrimination" is handling of victims provided to all victims without distinguishing tribe, religion, race, class, gender, ethnicity, culture and language, legal status and physical or mental condition.

Point e What is meant by "guarantee of confidentiality" is an effort to guarantee certainty for victims that their identity, medical treatment and legal handling will not be disseminated. Point f What is meant by "easily accessible" is the provision of services and assistance for all people regardless of their social status, so that such services are affordable for the underprivileged or relatively sufficient for the privileged.

Point g What is meant by "not charged" is that the activities of providing services and assistance carried out by PPT are not charged to the victim.

#### Article 22 Point a What is meant by "complaint, consultation, and counseling services" includes initial identification or recording of victims; case identification; and consent for action to be taken.

Point b What is meant by "assistance services" includes accompanying victims during the examination and health recovery process; accompanying victims during the examination process at the police, prosecutor's office and court; monitoring the interests and rights of victims in the examination process at the police, prosecutor's office and court; protecting the privacy and confidentiality of victims from all parties who have no interest, including reporting by mass media; providing a sense of security to victims; and providing continuous handling up to the rehabilitation stage.

Point c What is meant by "medical rehabilitation services" includes first aid to victims; treatment and recovery of physical injuries aimed at restoring the physical condition of victims carried out by medical personnel and paramedics; and referral to health services.

point d What is meant by "social rehabilitation services" is services provided in order to restore the traumatic condition of victims. Forms of services are provided through social support through spiritual guidance to victims; and psychological recovery of victims. Point e What is meant by "legal services" is to assist victims in undergoing the judicial process by providing legal consultation that includes information regarding victims' rights and the judicial process; accompanying victims at the investigation, prosecution, and examination levels in court hearings and helping victims to fully describe the violence they experienced; and coordinating with fellow law enforcers, volunteer assistants, and social workers so that the judicial process proceeds as it should.

Point f What is meant by "social reintegration services" is aimed at returning victims to their families and social environment. Social reintegration services are carried out by the Regency Government by coordinating with relevant agencies and institutions, both government and non-government; and village community institutions and/or local community leaders.
