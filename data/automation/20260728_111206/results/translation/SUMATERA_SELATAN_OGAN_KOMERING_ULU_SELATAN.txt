PROVINCE OF SOUTH SUMATRA
REGIONAL REGULATION OF OGAN KOMERING ULU SELATAN REGENCY NUMBER 7 OF 2020

CONCERNING
PROTECTION OF WOMEN AND CHILDREN
BY THE GRACE OF ALMIGHTY GOD
THE REGENT OF OGAN KOMERING ULU SELATAN,

Considering : a. that the government has the obligation to provide protection, advancement, enforcement and fulfillment of Human Rights for every citizen including the Rights of Women and Children;
b. that Women and Children are vulnerable groups who tend to experience violence and discrimination and therefore need to receive optimal protection;
c. that the fulfillment of constitutional rights of Women and Children as well as the improvement of the quality of life of Women and Children, is one of the mandatory affairs which is the duty, authority and responsibility of the Regency Government;
d. that based on the considerations as referred to in letter a, letter b and letter c, it is necessary to establish a Regional Regulation concerning Protection of Women and Children;

In View of : 1. Article 18 paragraph (6) of the 1945 Constitution of the Republic of Indonesia;

CHAPTER I
GENERAL PROVISIONS
Part One
Definitions, Meanings and Terms

Article 1
In this Regional Regulation what is meant by:
1. Regency is the Regency of Ogan Komering Ulu Selatan.
2. Regency Government is the Government of Ogan Komering Ulu Selatan Regency.
3. Regent is the Regent of Ogan Komering Ulu Selatan.
4. Regional Apparatus hereinafter abbreviated as PD is the Regional Apparatus within the Government of Ogan Komering Ulu Selatan Regency.
- 4 -
5. Office of Population Control Family Planning Women's Empowerment and Child Protection hereinafter abbreviated as DPPKBPP-PA is the Regional Apparatus that administers the affairs of Women's Empowerment and Child Protection in Ogan Komering Ulu Selatan.
6. Protection of Women is all efforts to guarantee and protect the rights of Women so that they can live healthily and participate optimally in accordance with human dignity and obtain protection from neglect, violence and discrimination, whether in the form of preventive, repressive or postremedial protection (assistance and treatment).
7. Child Protection is all activities to guarantee and protect Children and their rights so that they can live, grow, develop, and participate, optimally in accordance with human dignity, and receive protection from violence and discrimination.
8. Child is a person who has not yet reached the age of 18 (eighteen) years, including a Child who is still in the womb.
9. Child with Disability is a Child who has physical, mental, intellectual, or sensory limitations for a long period of time who in interacting with the environment and community attitudes may encounter obstacles that make it difficult to participate fully and effectively based on equal rights.
10. Child with Excellence is a Child who has extraordinary intelligence or has special potential and/or talent not limited to intellectual ability, but also in other fields.
11. Prevention is direct efforts carried out by the Regency Government, community and parents and/or family to prevent violations of Women's rights and Children's Rights.
12. Children's Forum is a forum for Children's participation formed with the aim of facilitating Children's involvement in decision-making processes and enjoying changes relating to their lives either directly or indirectly, which is implemented with the consent and will of all Children based on awareness and understanding, in accordance with age and level of thinking maturity.
13. Child-Friendly Regency hereinafter abbreviated as KLA is a Regency that has a development system based on Children's rights through - 5 - integration of commitment and resources of government, community and business world that are planned comprehensively and sustainably in policies, programs and activities to ensure the fulfillment of Children's rights.
14. Neglected Child is a Child whose needs are not properly fulfilled, whether physical, mental, spiritual, or social.
15. Women victims of violence are Women who experience actions that result in misery or suffering physically, sexually or psychologically, including threats of certain actions, coercion or arbitrary deprivation of freedom, whether occurring in the public sphere or in private life.
16. Child victims of violence are Children who experience actions that result in misery or suffering physically, mentally, sexually, psychologically or neglect that threatens life, body and degrades human dignity.
17. Trafficking in persons is the act of recruitment, transportation, harboring, sending, transfer, or receipt of a person by threat of violence, use of violence, abduction, confinement, forgery, fraud, abuse of power or position of vulnerability, debt bondage or giving payment or benefits, so as to obtain consent from the person holding control over that other person, whether carried out within the Country or between Countries, for the purpose of exploitation or resulting in the person being exploited.
18. Complaint handling is a series of actions carried out by integrated service providers to follow up on reports of acts of violence against Women and Children, submitted by victims, families or the community.
19. Social rehabilitation is services aimed at restoring and developing the ability of a person experiencing social dysfunction so that they can carry out their social functions properly.
20. Law enforcement is action by officials authorized by the state to implement laws and regulations.
21. Legal aid is legal services provided by legal assistants and advocates to carry out the process of accompanying witnesses and/or victims of violence against Women and Children that is gender sensitive.
22. Repatriation is an effort to return Women and Child victims of violence from abroad to the debarkation/entry point, or from the receiving area to the area of origin.
23. Social reintegration is an effort to reunite victims with family, substitute family, or community that can provide protection and fulfillment of needs for victims.
24. Safe house is a temporary shelter that aims to provide protection and a sense of security to victims of violence according to established standards.
25. Shelter house is a forum for guidance and welfare services for street Children that carries out mentoring/social guidance activities, maintenance, care, education, and health, skills guidance in order to ensure that Children do not carry out activities on the streets so that they can grow and develop properly.
26. Spiritual guidance is an effort to restore the psyche through strengthening the religion adhered to by the victim.
27. Gender responsive is consistent and systematic attention to the differences between Women and men in society accompanied by efforts to eliminate structural and cultural barriers in achieving gender equality.
28. Gender Sensitivity or Gender Awareness is the ability to identify gaps in power relations between Women and men in the family and in the community, the impact of gender-based division of labor on Women and men, and the experiences, problems, needs, interests and aspirations of Women and men are different.
29. Public service providers are every state organizing institution, corporation, independent institution formed based on Law for public service activities, and other legal entities formed solely for public service activities.
30. Community Organization is an organization established and formed by the community voluntarily based on common aspirations, will, needs, interests, activities and goals to participate in development for the achievement of the goals of the Unitary State of the Republic of Indonesia based on Pancasila.
31. Parents are biological father and/or mother, or step father and/or mother, or adoptive father and/or mother.
32. Family is the smallest unit in society consisting of husband and wife or husband, wife and their Child, or father and his Child, or mother and - 7 - her Child, or blood relatives in a straight line upward or downward up to the third degree.
33. Integrated Service Center for Women's Empowerment and Children, hereinafter abbreviated as P2TP2A is an integrated service center in efforts to empower Women in various fields of development, as well as protection of Women and Children from various types of discrimination and acts of violence, including trafficking in persons, which is formed by the government or community-based.
34. Human Immunodeficiency Virus hereinafter abbreviated as HIV is a virus that attacks the immune system and if not treated can reduce human immunity until the condition of Acquired Immuno Deficiency Syndrome (AIDS) occurs.

Second Part
Principles, Objectives and Scope

Article 2
(1) The implementation of Protection of Women is based on the principles of:
a. the best interests of Women;
b. gender justice and gender equality;
c. local wisdom; and
d. legal certainty.
(2) The implementation of Protection of Children is based on the principles of:
a. non-discrimination;
b. the best interests of the Child;
c. the right to life, survival, and development;
d. respect for the views of the Child; and
e. legal certainty.

Article 3
The Regulation on the Protection of Women and Children aims to: a. serve as a guideline for the Regency Government in planning, policies and strategies for the Protection of Women and Children;
b. protect Women and Children from actions, decisions and policies that violate the rights of Women and Children;
c. prevent violations of the Rights of Women and Children;
d. enhance empowerment of Women and Children;
e. enhance the role and value of local wisdom in the Protection of Women and Children; and
f. enhance the role of Regional Apparatus and non - 8 - Government entities in the Regency in efforts to Protect Women and Children.

Article 4
The scope of regulation for the Protection of Women and Children includes:
a. prevention;
b. implementation of Protection of the Rights of Women and Children;
c. handling;
d. empowerment; and
e. guidance and supervision.

CHAPTER II
WOMEN'S RIGHTS AND CHILDREN'S RIGHTS
Part One
Women's Rights

Article 5
Every Woman has the right to:
a. obtain protection from discriminatory actions;
b. obtain protection from economic exploitation and sexual exploitation;
c. obtain protection from physical, psychological, sexual violence, and neglect;
d. obtain decent work in accordance with her capabilities;
e. obtain work leave while receiving wages/salary in accordance with statutory regulations;
f. obtain education and teaching in all types, levels and pathways of education;
g. obtain special protection in the implementation of work or profession against matters that may threaten safety and/or health in relation to Women's reproductive function;
h. the right to choose, be chosen, be appointed in work, positions, and professions in accordance with requirements and statutory regulations; and
i. ownership and management of joint assets in marriage in accordance with the provisions of Statutory Regulations.
- 9 -

Second Part
Rights of the Child

Article 6
(1) Every Child has the right to:
a. every child has the right to protection by parents, family, community, and the state;
b. the rights of the child are human rights and for their interests the rights of the child are recognized and protected by law even from the womb;
c. every child from the womb, has the right to live, survive, and improve their standard of living;
d. every child from birth, has the right to a name and citizenship status;
e. every child with physical and/or mental disabilities has the right to obtain care, education, training, and special assistance at state expense, to ensure their life in accordance with human dignity, enhance self-confidence, and the ability to participate in community, national, and state life;
f. every child has the right to worship according to their religion, think, express themselves in accordance with their level of intellectuality and age under the guidance of parents and/or guardians;
g. every child has the right to know who their parents are, to be raised, and cared for by their own parents;
h. in the event that the child's parents are unable to raise and care for their child properly, then the child may be cared for or adopted as a child by another person in accordance with the provisions of laws and regulations;
i. every child has the right to be raised, maintained, cared for, educated, directed, and guided in their life by parents or guardians until adulthood in accordance with the provisions of laws and regulations;
j. every child has the right to obtain adoptive parents or a guardian based on a court decision if both parents have died or due to a legitimate reason cannot carry out their obligations as parents;
k. adoptive parents or guardians as referred to in letter j must carry out the obligations as actual parents;
- 10 -
l. every child has the right to obtain legal protection from all forms of physical or mental violence, neglect, maltreatment, and sexual abuse while in the care of parents or guardians, or other parties responsible for the care of the child;
m. in the event that parents, guardians, or child caregivers commit any form of physical or mental abuse, neglect, maltreatment, and sexual abuse including rape, and or murder of a child who should be protected, then aggravated punishment must be imposed;
n. every child has the right not to be separated from their parents against the child's own will, unless there are legitimate legal reasons and rules showing that the separation is in the best interests of the child;
o. in circumstances as referred to in letter n, the child's right to continue to meet directly and maintain personal contact regularly with their parents remains guaranteed by law;
p. every child has the right to obtain education and teaching in the context of personal development in accordance with interests, talents, and level of intelligence;
q. every child has the right to seek, receive, and provide information in accordance with their level of intellectuality and age for self-development as long as it is in accordance with moral values and propriety;
r. every child has the right to rest, associate with peers, play, recreate, and create in accordance with interests, talents, and level of intelligence for self-development;
s. every child has the right to obtain health services and social security appropriately, in accordance with their physical and spiritual mental needs;
t. every child has the right not to be involved in warfare events, armed conflicts, social unrest, and other events containing elements of violence;
u. every child has the right to obtain protection from economic exploitation activities and any work that endangers themselves, so as to disrupt education, physical health, morals, social life, and spiritual mentality;
- 11 -
v. every child has the right to obtain protection from exploitation activities and sexual abuse, kidnapping, child trafficking, as well as from various forms of narcotics, psychotropic, and other addictive substance abuse;
w. every child has the right not to be subjected to persecution, torture, or imposition of inhumane punishment;
x. every child has the right not to be deprived of freedom unlawfully;
y. arrest, detention, or imprisonment of a child may only be carried out in accordance with applicable law and can only be implemented as a last resort;
z. every child who is deprived of freedom has the right to receive humane treatment and with attention to the needs of personal development in accordance with their age and must be separated from adults, unless in their best interests;
aa. every child who is deprived of freedom has the right to obtain legal assistance or other assistance effectively at every stage of applicable legal remedies;
bb. every child who is deprived of freedom has the right to defend themselves and obtain justice before an objective and impartial Juvenile Court in a closed hearing to the public.
(2) Every child who becomes a victim or perpetrator of sexual violence or who is in conflict with the law has the right to confidentiality.
(3) Every Child with Disabilities in addition to obtaining rights as referred to in paragraph (1) is also entitled to obtain:
a. special education/special education;
b. rehabilitation;
c. social assistance; and
d. maintenance of social welfare standards.
(4) Every Child who has excellence has the right to receive special education.

CHAPTER III
OBLIGATIONS AND RESPONSIBILITIES
Part One
Regency Government - 12 -

Article 7
(1) The Regency Government in efforts to protect Women is obligated and responsible to:
a. guarantee Women's Rights without discrimination in accordance with the provisions of Laws and Regulations;
b. establish and implement policy programs and activities for the protection of Women;
c. cooperate with the community and/or Community Organizations in the implementation of Women's Protection in accordance with the provisions of Laws and Regulations;
d. conduct rehabilitation and empowerment of Women who are victims of acts of violence;
e. provide special protection to Women victims of acts of violence and human trafficking; and
f. take preventive actions in order to prevent violations of Women's rights.
(2) The responsibility of the Regency Government in efforts to protect Women's Rights as referred to in paragraph (1) shall be implemented by DPPKBP-PPPA.

Article 8
(1) The Regency Government in efforts to protect Children is obligated and responsible to:
a. respect the fulfillment of Children's rights without distinguishing ethnicity, religion, race, group, gender, ethnic group, culture and language, legal status, birth order, and physical and/or mental condition;
b. provide support for facilities, infrastructure, and availability of human resources in the implementation of Child Protection;
c. guarantee the protection, care, and welfare of Children by taking into account the rights and obligations of Parents, Guardians, or other persons who are legally responsible for the Child;
d. guarantee Children to convey their right to express opinions in accordance with the age and intelligence level of the Child;
e. establish and implement policies and programs/activities for the protection of Children's rights;
f. conduct cooperation with the community and/or Community Organizations in the implementation of Child Protection in accordance with the provisions of Laws and Regulations;
g. conduct rehabilitation for Children who are victims of acts of violence;
- 13 -
h. provide special protection to Children:
1. in emergency situations;
2. in conflict with the law;
3. from minority and isolated groups;
4. who are exploited economically and/or sexually;
5. who are victims of narcotics, alcohol, psychotropic, and other addictive substance abuse;
6. who are victims of pornography;
7. with HIV/AIDS;
8. victims of kidnapping, sale, and/or trafficking;
9. victims of physical and/or psychological violence;
10. victims of sexual crimes;
11. victims of terrorism networks;
12. with disabilities;
13. victims of maltreatment and neglect;
14. with deviant social behavior; and
15. who are victims of stigmatization from labeling related to their parents' condition.
i. organize inclusive education for Children with special needs;
j. take preventive action in order to prevent violations against Children; and
k. implement and support National policies in the implementation of Child Protection in the Regency conducted through efforts to realize Child-Friendly Regency, Child-Friendly Sub-district and Child-Friendly Village.
(2) The responsibility of the Regency Government in efforts to protect Children as referred to in paragraph (1) is implemented by DPPKBP-PPPA.

Article 9
(1) The Regency Government in the framework of carrying out its responsibilities, establishes programs and activities for the protection of Women and Children with a Regional Action Plan as the basis for Regional Apparatus in implementing the protection of Women and Children.
(2) Further provisions regarding the Regional Action Plan as referred to in paragraph (1) shall be regulated by Regent Regulation.
- 14 -

Second Part
Society

Article 10
(1) The community is obligated and responsible in implementing the protection of Women and Children including:
a. participation in prevention and providing support for the development of prevention programs against violations of the rights of Women and Children;
b. providing information and/or reporting violations of the rights of Women and Children to the authorized parties; and
c. supervising the law enforcement process regarding cases of violations of the rights of Women and Children.
(2) The responsibility of the community as referred to in paragraph (1) is in accordance with the provisions of laws and regulations and the values of local wisdom of the local community.

Third Part
Parents and/or Family

Paragraph 1
Responsibility Towards the Protection of Women's Rights Article 11 The family's responsibility in efforts to protect women includes:
a. protecting women from conflict and domestic violence that degrades the dignity of women;
b. providing equal opportunities to women to develop themselves in the fields of:
1. education;
2. social;
3. economic; and
4. political and cultural.
c. preventing violations of women's rights within the household.

Paragraph 2
Responsibility Towards the Protection of Children's Rights Article 12 (1) Parents in the effort to protect Children are responsible for:
a. nurturing, caring for, educating, and protecting Children;
- 15 -
b. developing Children according to their abilities, talents, and interests;
c. providing formal and non-formal education; d. guiding Children to behave in accordance with religion, ethics and morals as well as customs; and e. preventing marriage at the age of Children. (2) In the event that parents do not exist, or their whereabouts are unknown, or due to a certain reason, cannot carry out their obligations and responsibilities, then the obligations and responsibilities as referred to in paragraph (1) may be transferred to the family.

CHAPTER IV
PREVENTION
Part One
Regency Government

Article 13
(1) Efforts undertaken by the Regency Government in order to prevent violations against the rights of Women include:
a. conducting socialization regarding the prevention of violence against Women by optimizing the role of customary, cultural, religious institutions and community organizations;
b. providing guidelines on handling acts of violence against Women;
c. involving community participation in recovery and reintegration;
d. developing an integrated database and information system on acts of violence against Women;
e. developing family empowerment programs;
f. strengthening the function of community organizations working in the field of Women's empowerment; and
g. providing public facilities that are comfortable and safe for Women.
(2) Efforts undertaken by the Regency Government in order to prevent violations of the rights of Children include:
a. preventing acts of violence against Children by optimizing the role of customary, cultural, religious institutions and community organizations;
b. developing child-friendly schools;
c. encouraging increased participation of children through the establishment of Children's Forums;
d. providing guidelines on handling acts of violence against Children;
- 16 -
e. involving community participation in recovery and reintegration services for child victims of violence;
f. developing an integrated database and information system on acts of violence against Children;
g. strengthening the function of community organizations working in the field of Child protection; and
h. encouraging the establishment of Child-Friendly Regencies.

Second Part
Society

Article 14
(1) Efforts made by the community in order to prevent violations of Women's rights include:
a. monitoring various conditions occurring in their environment that may give rise to violations of Women's rights;
b. strengthening the role of community organizations, religious organizations and the family environment; and
c. prevention of unregistered marriages/siri marriages.
(2) Efforts made in order to prevent violations of Children's rights include:
a. monitoring various conditions occurring in their environment that give rise to violations of Children's rights;
b. strengthening the role of community organizations, religious organizations and the family environment; and
c. prevention of marriage at the age of Children.

Third Part
Parents and/or Family

Article 15
(1) Efforts made by the family in order to prevent violations of Women's rights include:
a. protection of women from various violations of rights in the household; and
b. encouraging the formation of harmonious, educative and democratic families;
(2) Efforts made by parents in order to prevent violations of Children's rights include:
a. providing good examples to children in accordance with religious and customary teachings;
b. supervision of children in their activities inside - 17 - or outside the home;
c. providing opportunities for children to dialogue and express opinions democratically; and d. protection of children from various acts of violation of Children's rights in the household.

CHAPTER V
IMPLEMENTATION OF PROTECTION
OF WOMEN AND CHILDREN
Part One
Implementation of Protection of Women

Paragraph 1
General

Article 16
The Regency Government implements Protection of Women by involving:
a. family;
b. community leaders;
c. religious leaders;
d. teachers and/or academics;
e. the community; and
f. community organizations in the field of protection of Women.

Paragraph 2
Marriage

Article 17
During the marriage bond or after divorce, a Woman has equal rights with the man who is her husband/former husband over all matters related to the consequences of marriage and/or divorce.

Paragraph 3
Health

Article 18
(1) The Regency Government shall provide adequate health and childbirth facilities for pregnant and breastfeeding mothers.
(2) Public service providers are obligated to provide special facilities for breastfeeding mothers.
- 18 -

Paragraph 4
Employment

Article 19
(1) Employers who employ Women are obligated to maintain decency, safety and security while in the workplace.
(2) Employers are obligated to place Women workers in places that are safe for Women's reproductive health.
(3) Employers are obligated to fulfill the rights of Women workers to receive wages in accordance with the provisions of laws and regulations.
(4) Employers provide special facilities for breastfeeding mothers in their companies.

Article 20
(1) Female workers/laborers who are at most 18 (eighteen) years old are prohibited from being employed between 23.00 WIB until 07.00 WIB.
(2) Employers are prohibited from employing pregnant female workers/laborers who according to a doctor's statement would endanger the health and safety of their pregnancy as well as themselves during working hours from 23.00 WIB until 07.00 WIB.
(3) Employers who employ female workers/laborers between 23.00 WIB until 07.00 WIB, are obligated to:
a. provide nutritious food and beverages; and b. maintain decency and security while at the workplace.
(4) Employers are obligated to provide shuttle transportation for female workers/laborers who depart for and return from work between 23.00 WIB until 05.00 WIB.

Paragraph 5
Education

Article 21
Women are guaranteed the right to obtain education at every level of formal and non-formal education.
- 19 -

Paragraph 6
Social

Article 22
(1) The Regency Government shall organize empowerment of Women experiencing socio-economic vulnerability through productive economic business activities.
(2) Community Organizations may conduct empowerment for Women experiencing socio-economic vulnerability as referred to in paragraph (1) in accordance with the provisions of Laws and Regulations.

Second Part
Implementation of Protection of Children's Rights

Paragraph 1
Health

Article 23
(1) The District Government's efforts to provide protection for Children in the health sector include:
a. free health services for Children from poor families;
b. provision of optimal health care for Children to obtain good health status from the womb;
c. ensuring that infants receive exclusive breastfeeding from their mothers properly and naturally;
d. ensuring Children receive adequate nutrition;
e. ensuring newborn Children undergo screening; and
f. ensuring the provision of growth and development monitoring for toddlers and pre-school age children.
(2) The District Government, families, and parents protect children from attempts to transplant their body organs to other parties.

Paragraph 2
Education

Article 24
Children who are in the environment of formal and non-formal educational institutions must be protected from acts of violence.
- 20 -

Article 25
Every Child aged 7 (seven) to 18 (eighteen) years who has not completed education, exceeding 3 (three) years beyond the age limit of formal education may pursue education through non-formal education units:
a. Equivalency program package A equivalent to Elementary School and Madrasah Ibtidaiyah;
b. Equivalency program package B equivalent to Junior High School and Madrasah Tsanawiyah; and
c. Equivalency program package C equivalent to Senior High School, Madrasah Aliyah and Vocational High School.

Article 26
(1) Children with special needs and/or who have potential intelligence and/or exceptional talents are given equal opportunities and facilities to obtain regular education as well as inclusive education.
(2) Inclusive education as referred to in paragraph (1) is organized by the Regency Government and the community.

Article 27
Every organizer of formal education is prohibited from expelling a Child without a guarantee for the continuity of the Child's education.

Article 28
(1) The Regency Government provides scholarships and/or educational assistance for:
a. children from poor families;
b. abandoned children; and
c. children residing in remote areas.
(2) The implementation of scholarship programs and/or educational assistance as referred to in paragraph (1) is supported by community participation and community organizations.
(3) Further provisions regarding the provision of scholarships and/or educational assistance as referred to in paragraph (1) shall be regulated by Regent Regulation.

Article 29
Children in conflict with the law, Child victims of violence and sexual abuse and Child victims of HIV/AIDS transmission are protected in their right to obtain education.
- 21 -

Article 30
The Regency Government and the community shall organize Early Childhood Education (PAUD).

Paragraph 3
Employment

Article 31
Employers are prohibited from employing Children.

Article 32
(1) The provisions as referred to in Article 31 may be exempted for Children aged between 13 (thirteen) years up to 15 (fifteen) years, to perform light work as long as it does not interfere with physical, mental, and social development and health.
(2) Employers who employ Children in light work as referred to in paragraph (1) must fulfill requirements including:
a. written permission from parents or guardian;
b. work agreement between the employer and parents or guardian;
c. maximum working time of 3 (three) hours;
d. conducted during the day and does not interfere with school time;
e. occupational safety and health;
f. the existence of a clear employment relationship; and
g. receiving wages in accordance with the provisions of laws and regulations.
(3) The provisions as referred to in paragraph (2) letter a, letter b, letter f, and letter g are exempted for Children who work in family businesses.

Article 33
In the event that a company employs a Child as referred to in Article 32, then the workplace of the Child must be separated from the workplace of adult workers.

Article 34
(1) Children may perform work in the workplace that is part of an education or training curriculum authorized by the competent official.
(2) Children as referred to in paragraph (1) shall be at least 14 (fourteen) years of age.
- 22 -

Article 35
(1) Every person is prohibited from employing and involving Children in the worst forms of work.
(2) The worst forms of work as referred to in paragraph (1) include:
a. all work in the form of slavery or similar practices;
b. all work that exploits, provides, or offers Children for: 1. prostitution;
2. production of pornography;
3. pornographic performances;
4. gambling;
5. production and trade of alcoholic beverages; 6. narcotics;
7. psychotropic substances; and
8. other addictive substances; and/or
c. all work that endangers the health, safety, or morals of Children.

Paragraph 4
Social

Article 36
(1) The Regency Government is obligated to provide care for neglected Children.
(2) The care as referred to in paragraph (1) may be carried out by Social Community Organizations.
(3) In the event that a Child is neglected due to any reason where the parents neglect their obligations, then the Family or the Regency Government or Community Organizations as referred to in paragraph (2) may submit an application to the court to determine the Child as a neglected Child.
(4) The court determination as referred to in paragraph (3) shall simultaneously determine the shelter and care for the neglected Child concerned.

Article 37
(1) The Regency Government shall organize skills training for street children and neglected children.
(2) Skills training as referred to in paragraph (1) shall be organized by Regional Apparatus whose duties and functions are in the social and manpower sectors and may cooperate with Community Organizations.
- 23 -

Article 38
(1) The Regency Government provides shelter homes to foster street Children and social institutions for abandoned Children.
(2) Shelter homes as referred to in paragraph (1) may be organized in cooperation with Community Organizations.
(3) Further provisions regarding shelter homes as referred to in paragraph (1) shall be regulated by Regent Regulation.

Article 39
The implementation of protection of Women and Children is carried out by Regional Apparatus whose duties and functions are related to the protection of Women and Children.

CHAPTER VI
HANDLING
Part One
Handling of Women and Children Victims of Violence Article 40 The implementation of handling of Women and Children victims of violence includes:
a. handling of complaints from victims of violence; b. health services;
c. social rehabilitation;
d. law enforcement and legal assistance; and
e. repatriation and social reintegration.

Article 41
(1) The implementation of handling of Women and Children victims of violence as referred to in Article 40 shall be carried out through integrated services.
(2) Integrated services for Women and Children victims of violence as referred to in paragraph (1) shall use the principles of:
a. gender responsive;
b. non-discrimination;
c. equal and respectful relationships;
d. maintaining confidentiality;
e. providing a sense of security and comfort;
f. respecting individual differences;
g. non-judgmental;
h. respecting the choices and decisions of victims; i. sensitive to the background and conditions of victims and the use of appropriate language that is understood - 24 - by victims;
j. fast and simple;
k. empathy;
l. fulfillment of the rights of Women and Children; and m. free of charge.

Article 42
Services for handling complaints of victims of violence against Women and Children are implemented by:
a. hospitals;
b. community health centers;
c. P2TP2A;
d. Women and Children Service Units (UPPA);
e. Trauma Center Shelter Houses (RPTC);
f. Children's Social Shelter Houses (RPSA);
g. Women Crisis Centre (WCC)
h. Advisory Board for Marriage Guidance and Preservation (BP4);
i. Police;
j. Prosecutor's Office;
k. Courts;
l. Legal Aid Institutions (LBH);
m. Child Protection Institutions (LPA);
n. Social Welfare Protection Institutions; and
o. Shelters.

Article 43
(1) Complaints of acts of violence against Women and Children as referred to in Article 42 may be made by reporters consisting of:
a. Victims;
b. Family;
c. Community; and/or
d. community organizations in the field of protection of Women and Children.
(2) Reporters as referred to in paragraph (1) may file complaints:
a. directly;
b. by telephone; and
c. by letter.
(3) In addition to handling complaints submitted by reporters, officers at service institutions as referred to in Article 42 are authorized to handle victims of violence against Women and Children obtained through other information.
(4) Further provisions regarding procedures for complaint handling services as referred to in paragraph (1) and paragraph (2) shall be regulated by Regional Regulation.
- 25 -

Second Part
Health Services for Women and Children Victims of Violence

Article 44
(1) Health services for Women and Children victims of violence include aspects of:
a. Promotive;
b. Preventive;
c. Curative;
d. rehabilitative.
(2) Health services for Women and Children victims of violence are conducted in a manner that is:
a. Comprehensive;
b. accessible 24 (twenty-four) hours;
c. quality; and
d. in accordance with health service standards.
(3) Special health services for Children victims of violence must prioritize the best interests of the Child.
(4) women and Children victims of violence are entitled to health services, including:
a. medical services;
b. medicolegal services; and
c. psychosocial counseling.
(5) Further provisions regarding the procedures for implementing health services as referred to in paragraph (4) shall be regulated by Regional Regulation.

Third Part
Rehabilitation Services for Women and Children Victims of Violence

Article 45
(1) Social rehabilitation services for Women and Children victims of violence are aimed at restoring and developing the abilities of victims who experience social dysfunction to be able to carry out their social functions in society properly.
(2) Social rehabilitation services as referred to in paragraph (1) include:
a. counseling services;
b. provision of safe houses;
c. spiritual guidance.
(3) The implementation of services as referred to in paragraph (2) is carried out by:
a. Regional Apparatus that has duties and functions in the social field;
- 26 -
b. DPPKBPP-PA;
c. P2TP2A;
d. Women Crisis Centre (WeC);
e. social community organizations; and/or
f. religious institutions.

Article 46
(1) Counseling services as referred to in Article 45 paragraph (2) letter a aim to:
a. assist victims in recognizing their problems and finding effective ways to overcome them independently;
b. empower victims to determine their future;
c. provide moral support for victims in facing the process they are undergoing; and
d. make victims feel accepted in the community environment.
(2) Counseling services are conducted by gender-responsive social rehabilitation officers, consisting of:
a. counselors;
b. psychologists; and
c. psychiatrists.
(3) Counseling services are conducted in a place that guarantees a sense of security, comfort and confidentiality of information from victims.
(4) Stages of counseling services include:
a. initial counseling;
b. follow-up counseling;
c. case conference; and
d. home visit.

Article 47
(1) The Regency Government may provide a Safe House for Women and Children victims of violence.
(2) The Safe House as referred to in paragraph (1) aims to protect Women and Children victims of violence from intimidation by perpetrators or other parties.
(3) The location of the Safe House as referred to in paragraph (1) must be kept confidential.
(4) The criteria for Women and Children victims of violence who require a Safe House include:
a. their life is threatened;
b. receiving rejection from family or community;
c. requiring intensive services but their residence is relatively far; and/or - 27 -
d. abandoned if not placed in a safe house.
(5) The implementation of Safe House services is carried out by social rehabilitation officers who have gender sensitivity or gender awareness, consisting of:
a. social workers/volunteers;
b. security officers; and
c. community leaders.
(6) Further provisions regarding the implementation of Safe House services shall be regulated by Regional Regulation.

Article 48
(1) Every Woman and Child victim of violence shall be provided with spiritual guidance aimed at psychological recovery.
(2) Spiritual guidance as referred to in paragraph (1) shall be implemented based on the principles of:
a. not imposing any religion or belief; and
b. leaving the final decision in the hands of the victim (self determination).
(3) Spiritual guidance as referred to in paragraph (2) shall be conducted by spiritual guidance officers.
(4) Spiritual guidance officers as referred to in paragraph (3) shall consist of:
a. consultants of the Marriage Advisory, Development and Preservation Board (BP4);
b. Officers of the Office of Religious Affairs (KUA);
c. Ustad or Ulama;
d. Pastor;
e. Minister; and
f. other religious leaders who have gender sensitivity or gender awareness.

Fourth Part
Enforcement Services and Legal Assistance

Article 49
(1) Law enforcement and legal aid services constitute actions by law enforcement officials provided to Women and Children victims of violence and/or witnesses.
(2) Law enforcement and legal aid services as referred to in paragraph (1) shall be carried out by prioritizing the best interests of the victims.
- 28 -

Article 50
(1) Law enforcement and legal aid services as referred to in Article 49 are intended to provide assistance and protection to Women and Children victims of violence and witnesses with a gender-responsive approach so that victims obtain justice and legal certainty.
(2) Types of legal aid services as referred to in paragraph (1) include:
a. legal aid in the reporting process and preparation of BAP;
b. legal aid in the prosecution process at the prosecutor's office;
c. legal aid in the examination process at the court;
d. legal aid in the court hearing decision process until there is a final legal decision; and
e. legal aid in the mediation process.
(3) The Regency Government provides legal aid to Children and Women victims of violence free of charge.
(4) In providing legal aid as referred to in paragraph (3) the Regency Government may cooperate with:
a. Legal Aid Institute (LBH);
b. Women Crisis Center (WCC);
c. Advocates; and/or
d. other legal assistants who are gender sensitive.

Part Five
Repatriation and Social Reintegration Services Article 51 (1) Repatriation of victims of violence is intended to  return Women and Children victims of  violence to their area of origin.
(2) Repatriation of Women and Children victims of violence  as referred to in paragraph (1) shall be accompanied by social  reintegration efforts.
(3) The Regency Government shall implement repatriation and  social reintegration of Women and Children victims of  violence in accordance with the provisions of Laws and  Regulations.
- 29 -

CHAPTER VII
WOMEN'S EMPOWERMENT
Article 52
(1) The Regency Government guarantees the implementation of empowerment for Women.
(2) The implementation of Women's empowerment as referred to in paragraph (1) is supported by community participation and Community Organizations.

Article 53
(1) The Regency Government provides skills training for capacity building of Women. (2) In providing training as referred to in paragraph (1), the Regency Government may cooperate with training institutions organized by community organizations. (3) The Regency Government expands access and opportunities for Women in obtaining skills and employment.

Article 54
(1) The Regency Government facilitates:
a. provision of business capital assistance for Women with weak economy;
b. marketing of products resulting from Women's businesses;
c. implementation of community capacity development training through economic empowerment of pre-prosperous families;
d. expansion of access, information and provision of knowledge to Women regarding efforts to market the products produced; and
e. provision of comprehensive entrepreneurship training to Women.
(2) The Regency Government in facilitating activities as referred to in paragraph (1) cooperates with the community and/or community organizations.

CHAPTER VIII
CHILD PARTICIPATION
Article 55
(1) Every Child has the right to participate in expressing opinions, ideas, and thoughts related to life based on awareness and understanding, in accordance with age and level of thinking maturity.
- 30 - (2) Child Participation as referred to in paragraph (1) may be conducted through Child Forums starting from the village level, sub-district level within the regency.
(3) Child Forums as referred to in paragraph (2) constitute representation of Children, both geographical domicile representation, socio-cultural groups and educational background of Children.
(4) The establishment of Child Forums as referred to in paragraph (2) shall be facilitated by the Regency Government.
(5) The establishment of Child Forums as referred to in paragraph (4) shall be stipulated by Regent's Decree.

Article 56
(1) In every formulation of policies related to Children, the Regency Government shall pay attention to and accommodate the Opinions of Children conveyed through the Children's Forum.
(2) The Regency Government shall include representatives of the Children's Forum in the Regency Development Planning Deliberation.

CHAPTER IX
CHILD-FRIENDLY REGENCY
Article 57
(1) The Regency Government conducts guidance to the Sub-District Government in the development of Child-Friendly Regency.
(2) The guidance as referred to in paragraph (1) includes:
a. coordination;
b. facilitation;
c. mentoring;
d. supervision;
e. consultation; and
f. education and training.
(3) Provisions regarding Child-Friendly Regency shall be further regulated by Regional Regulation.

CHAPTER X
GUIDANCE AND SUPERVISION
Article 58
(1) The Regent conducts guidance and supervision of the implementation of Protection of Women and Children.
(2) Guidance as referred to in paragraph (1) may be conducted in the form of:
a. training for government officials and law enforcers regarding prevention and handling of violence;
- 31 -
b. training for religious leaders, community leaders, customary leaders, educators, journalists and media managers in order to prevent violence against Women and Children;
c. training for health workers who provide health services to Women and Children victims of violence at community health centers, integrated service units, and general hospitals;
d. training for social rehabilitation officers and spiritual guidance officers in order to protect Women and Children victims of violence; and
e. improvement of professionalism of officers, managers of violence case data against Women and Children at community health centers, integrated service units and hospitals.
(3) The Regent in conducting guidance and supervision as referred to in paragraph (1) may delegate to the Head of DPPKBPP-PA.

Article 59
(1) The Head of DPPKBPP-PA is obligated to submit a report on the implementation of the Women and Children protection program to the Regent.
(2) The report as referred to in paragraph (1) shall be submitted periodically 1 (one) time every 3 (three) months.

Article 60
(1) The Regent conducts supervision and evaluation of the implementation of Women and Children Protection periodically 1 (one) time every 3 (three) months.
(2) The evaluation results as referred to in paragraph (1) constitute evaluation material for the Women and Children protection program policy in the Regency.

CHAPTER XI
FINANCING
Article 61
The financing required for the implementation of Child-Friendly Regency activities shall be charged to:
a. Regional Revenue and Expenditure Budget of the Regency; b. State Revenue and Expenditure Budget; and c. other legal and non-binding sources.

CLOSING PROVISIONS
Article 62
This Regent Regulation shall come into force on the date of promulgation.
