CHAPTER I
GENERAL PROVISIONS
Article 1
In this Regional Regulation, what is meant by:
1. Regency is Muara Enim Regency.
2. Regency Government is Muara Enim Regency.
3. Regent is the Regent of Muara Enim.
4. Regional Apparatus is the Regional Apparatus within the Muara Enim Regency Government.
5. Women's Empowerment is the strengthening of human rights, enhancement of Protection efforts, quality of life, and increased participation of women in development.
(4)
6. Women's Protection is all activities aimed at providing a sense of security carried out by the Regional Government, police, prosecutor's office, courts, social institutions, or other parties who know or hear that violence against women will occur or has occurred;
7. Child Protection is all activities to guarantee and protect Children and their rights so they can live, grow, develop, and participate optimally in accordance with human dignity and worth, and receive Protection from Violence and discrimination.
7. Child is a person who has not yet reached the age of 18 (eighteen) years, including a Child still in the womb and not yet married.
8. Prevention is direct efforts carried out by the Regional Government, community and parents and/or family to prevent violations of the rights of women and Children.
9. Violence is any act that results in misery or suffering whether physical, sexual, psychological and/or domestic neglect including threats to commit acts, coercion, or deprivation of a person's freedom unlawfully.
10. Children's Forum is a forum for Child participation formed with the aim of facilitating the involvement of Children in decision-making processes and enjoying changes relating to their lives either directly or indirectly, which is implemented with the consent and will of all Children based on awareness and understanding, in accordance with age and level of thinking maturity.
11. Child-Friendly Regency hereinafter abbreviated as KLA is a Regency that has a Child rights-based development system through the integration of government commitments and resources, community and business world that is planned comprehensively and sustainably in policies, programs and activities to ensure the fulfillment of Children's rights.
12. Gender Mainstreaming is a strategy built to integrate gender into an integral dimension of planning, formulation, implementation, monitoring and evaluation of national development program policies.
(5)
13. Community is individuals, families, groups and social organizations and/or community organizations.
14. Women Victims of Violence are women who experience actions that result in misery or suffering physically, sexually or psychologically, including threats of certain actions, coercion or deprivation of freedom arbitrarily, whether occurring in the public sphere or in private life.
15. Child Victims of Violence are Children who experience actions that result in misery or suffering physically, mentally, sexually, psychologically or neglect that threatens life, body and degrades human dignity.
16. Human trafficking is the act of recruitment, transportation, harboring, sending, transfer, or receipt of a person by threat of Violence, use of Violence, kidnapping, confinement, forgery, fraud, abuse of power or vulnerable position, debt bondage or giving payment or benefits, so as to obtain consent from the person holding control over that other person, whether carried out within the Country or between Countries, for the purpose of exploitation or resulting in a person being exploited.
17. Complaint handling is a series of actions carried out by integrated service providers to follow up on reports of acts of Violence against women and Children, submitted by victims, families or the community.
18. Social rehabilitation is services aimed at restoring and developing the abilities of a person experiencing social dysfunction so they can carry out social functions normally.
19. Law enforcement is action by officials authorized by the state to implement laws and regulations.
20. Legal aid is legal services provided by legal assistants and advocates to carry out the process of assisting witnesses and/or victims of Violence against women and Children that is gender sensitive.
(6)
21. Repatriation is an effort to return women and Child victims of Violence from abroad to the debarkation point/entry point, or from the receiving Region to the Region of origin.
22. Social reintegration is an effort to reunite victims with family, substitute family, or community that can provide Protection and fulfillment of needs for victims.
23. Safe house is a temporary shelter that aims to provide Protection and a sense of security to victims of Violence.
24. Shelter home is a forum for guidance and welfare services for street children that carries out social assistance/guidance activities, maintenance, care, education, and health, skills guidance in order to ensure that Children do not carry out activities on the streets so they can grow and develop normally.
25. Spiritual Guidance is an effort to restore the psyche through strengthening the religion adhered to by the victim.
26. Gender Mainstreaming is a strategy built to integrate gender into an integral dimension of planning, formulation, implementation, monitoring, and evaluation of development policies, programs, and activities in the Region.
27. Gender responsive is consistent and systematic attention to the differences between women and men in society accompanied by efforts to eliminate structural and cultural barriers in achieving gender equality.
28. Gender Sensitivity or Gender Awareness is the ability to identify gaps in power relations between women and men in the family and in the community, the impact of gender-based division of labor on women and men, and that the experiences, problems, needs, interests and aspirations of women and men are different.
(7)
29. Public service provider is every state organizing institution, corporation, independent institution formed based on Law for public service activities, and other legal entities formed solely for public service activities.
30. Community Organization is an organization established and formed by the community voluntarily based on common aspirations, will, needs, interests, activities and goals to participate in development for the achievement of the goals of the Unitary State of the Republic of Indonesia based on Pancasila.
31. Parents are biological father and/or mother, or step father and/or mother, or adoptive father and/or mother.
32. Family is the smallest unit in society consisting of husband and wife or husband, wife and their Child, or father and his Child, or mother and her Child, or blood relatives in direct line upward or downward up to the third degree.
33. Customs are eternal conduct patterns passed down from one generation to another generation as heritage, so that it is strongly integrated with community behavior patterns.
32. Best interests are the best interests for women and children.

Article 2
The Implementation of Protection of Women and Children is based on the principles of:
a. the best interests of women and Children;
b. gender justice and gender equality;
c. local wisdom;
d. law enforcement and legal certainty.
e. non-discrimination;
f. the right to life, survival, and development; g. respect for the opinion of Children; and
h. law enforcement and legal certainty.

Article 3
This Regional Regulation aims to:
a. guarantee the fulfillment of the rights of women and Children in the Regency;
(8)
b. serve as a guideline for the Region in planning, policies and strategies for the Protection of Women and Children;
c. protect and prevent women and Children from actions, decisions and policies that violate the rights of women and Children;
d. enhance the role and value of local wisdom as well as the role of customs in the Protection of Women and Children;
e. serve as a legal umbrella for regional apparatus that handle the protection of women and children in the context of providing assistance and support to women and child victims.

Article 4
The scope of regulation for the Protection of Women and Children includes:
a. protection of women;
b. protection of Children;
c. fulfillment of Children's rights;
d. improvement of women's quality of life;
e. gender and Children data systems, disaggregated data on women and children, and strengthening of Gender Mainstreaming institutions through Gender Responsive Planning and Budgeting;
f. community participation; and
g. guidance, supervision, assistance.

CHAPTER II
IMPROVEMENT OF WOMEN'S QUALITY OF LIFE
Part One
General

Article 5
(1) In order to improve the quality of life of women, the regional government is responsible for:
a. respecting and guaranteeing the rights of women without discrimination in accordance with human dignity and worth;
b. implementing Gender Mainstreaming in the Regency; c. establishing a Gender Mainstreaming working group in the Region; d. enhancing Women's Empowerment in terms of role, quality and capacity in the fields of (9)
1. politics;
2. law;
3. social;
4. economy; and
5. culture.
e. strengthening and developing institutions providing Women's Empowerment services in the Regency.
(2) The responsibility of the Regency Government in efforts to improve the quality of life of women as referred to in paragraph (1) shall be implemented by regional apparatus in accordance with their duties and functions.

Second Part
Enhancement of the Role, Quality and Capability of Women in the Political Field

Article 6
Efforts to increase the role, quality and capability of women in the political field as referred to in Article 5 paragraph (1) letter d number 1 include:
a. implementing socialization to increase women's political participation in general elections;
b. implementing programs that encourage the level of women's involvement in political processes and public positions;
c. facilitating the self-development of women in organizing, associating and assembling; and
d. involving women in decision-making concerning women's interests.

Third Part
Enhancement of the Role, Quality, and Capability of Women in the Legal Field

Article 7
Efforts to increase the role, quality and capability of women in the field of law as referred to in Article 5 paragraph (1) letter d number 2 include:
a. conducting socialization to increase women's legal knowledge and awareness;
(10)
b. conducting mapping and reviewing gender-biased policies; and c. involving women's organizations in the process of drafting and discussing regional legal products related to women's participation.

Part Four
Enhancement of the Role, Quality, and Capability of Women in the Social Sector

Article 8
Efforts to increase the role, quality and capability of women in the social field as referred to in Article 5 paragraph (1) letter cl number 3 include:
a. increasing knowledge and skills to encourage the fulfillment of education in accordance with existing potential;
b. increasing and fulfilling health quality for women;
c. increasing awareness and knowledge about independent, healthy and prosperous family planning including access to consultation services and marriage registration;
d. increasing women's awareness of the impact of early marriage;
e. facilitating and endeavoring to preserve customs and traditions and the development of science, technology, arts and culture for the advancement of women;
f. increasing women's awareness and knowledge of independent, healthy and prosperous family planning;
g. implementing programs to reduce maternal mortality rates; and
h. encouraging the establishment of women's organizations in the Regency.

Part Five
Enhancement of the Role, Quality, and Capability of Women in the Economic Field

Article 9
Efforts to increase the role, quality and capability of women in the economic field as referred to in Article 5 paragraph (1) letter d number 4 include: (11)
a. implementing programs, activities, training for improving the skills and expertise of women in order to realize the improvement of women's welfare;
b. facilitation of business capital strengthening for women with weak economic capacity;
c. marketing of products resulting from businesses of women with weak economic capacity;
d. training for developing women's capabilities through economic empowerment of pre-prosperous families; and
e. comprehensive entrepreneurship training for women.

CHAPTER III
PROTECTION OF WOMEN
Part One
General

Article 10
(1) The Regency Government in efforts to Protect women is responsible for:
a. establishing and implementing policies, programs and activities for the protection of women;
b. undertaking prevention efforts against acts of Violence against women including criminal acts of human trafficking;
c. increasing the effectiveness of services for women victims of acts of Violence;
d. increasing protection efforts for female workers;
e. strengthening and developing institutions providing women's protection services;
f. conducting cooperation with the Government, other Regional Governments, private parties, business sectors and/or Non-Governmental Organizations in efforts to prevent acts of Violence against women;
g. involving the active role of families, customary leaders, religious leaders, communities, and institutions in the field of women's protection.
(12) (2) The responsibility of the Regency Government in efforts to protect women as referred to in paragraph (1) shall be implemented by regional apparatus in accordance with their duties and functions and may coordinate with relevant agencies.

Part Two
Prevention of Acts of Violence Against Women

Article 11
In order to prevent acts of Violence against women, the Regency Government:
a. conducts socialization regarding the prevention of violence against women by optimizing the role of customary, cultural, religious institutions and community organizations;
b. builds networks and cooperation with law enforcement apparatus, government apparatus, universities and various Non-Governmental Organizations that work and/or care about women and children victims of Violence;
c. provides public facilities that are comfortable and safe for women;
d. establishes a Regional Technical Service Unit for the Protection of Women and Children and provides a secretariat for the Regional Technical Service Unit for the Protection of Women and Children.

Third Part
Effectiveness of Services for Women Victims of Acts of Violence

Paragraph 1
General

Article 12
(1) Protection of women victims of acts of Violence is implemented through integrated services.
(2) The provision of integrated services to women victims of acts of Violence as referred to in paragraph (1) uses the principles of:
a. gender responsive;
b. non-discrimination;
(13)
c. equal and respectful relationships;
d. maintaining confidentiality;
e. providing a sense of security and comfort;
f. respecting individual differences;
g. non-judgmental;
h. respecting the choices and decisions of victims;
i. sensitive to the background and conditions of victims and the use of language that is appropriate and understood by victims;
j. fast and simple;
k. empathy; and
l. fulfillment of women's rights.

Article 13
The form of integrated services for women victims of violence includes:
a. complaint handling;
b. health services;
c. social rehabilitation;
d. victim assistance, enforcement and legal aid; and e. repatriation and social reintegration.

Paragraph 2
Handling of Complaints

Article 14
Integrated complaint handling services as referred to in Article 13 letter a shall be implemented by:
a. Hospitals, Community Health Centers and other health service facilities;
b. Technical Implementation Unit for the Protection of Women and Children;
c. Trauma Center Shelter,
d. Women crisis centre;
e. Advisory Board for Marriage Development and Preservation;
f. Police;
g. Prosecutor's Office;
h. Court;
i. Ministry of Religious Affairs; and/or J. Legal Aid Institution.
{14)

Article 15
(1) Complaints of acts of violence against women can be made by reporters consisting of:
a. victims;
b. family;
c. community; and/or
d. community organizations in the field of protection of women and children.
(2) Reporters as referred to in paragraph (1) may file complaints:
a. directly;
b. by telephone; and/or
c. by letter.
(3) In addition to handling complaints submitted by reporters, the office in charge of Protection of Women and Children, officers at service institutions as referred to in Article 13, are also authorized to provide assistance in handling victims of acts of violence against women obtained through other information.
(4) Further provisions regarding procedures for complaint handling services as referred to in paragraph (1) and paragraph (2) shall be regulated by Regent Regulation.

Paragraph 3
Health Rehabilitation

Article 16
(1) Health rehabilitation as referred to in Article 13 letter b constitutes efforts that include aspects of:
a. promotive;
b. preventive;
c. curative; and
d. rehabilitative.
(2) Health rehabilitation as referred to in paragraph (1) shall be conducted comprehensively, accessible 24 (twenty-four) hours, with quality and conducted in accordance with health service standards.
(15) (3) Women and children victims of acts of violence and children in conflict with the law have the right to free health services, including:
a. medical services;
b. medicolegal services;
c. psychosocial counseling; and
d. visum.
(4) Further provisions regarding the procedures for implementation of health services as referred to in paragraph (3) shall be regulated by Regent Regulation.

Paragraph 4
Social Rehabilitation

Article 17
(1) Social rehabilitation as referred to in Article 13 letter c is intended to restore and develop the capabilities of women victims of acts of Violence who experience social dysfunction so that they can carry out their social functions in society properly.
(2) Social rehabilitation as referred to in paragraph (1) includes services:
a. motivation and psychosocial diagnosis;
b. care and nurturing;
c. vocational training and entrepreneurship development;
d. spiritual mental guidance;
e. physical guidance;
f. social guidance and psychosocial counseling;
g. accessibility services;
h. social assistance and aid;
i. resocialization guidance;
j. follow-up guidance; and/or
k. referral.
(3) The implementation of services as referred to in paragraph (2) is carried out by:
a. regional apparatus that has the duties and functions in the social field;
(16)
b. regional apparatus that oversees Women's Empowerment, and may cooperate with integrated service centers, women crisis centres or social community organizations that already exist; or
c. Ministry of Religious Affairs.

Article 18
(1) Counseling services as referred to in Article 17 paragraph (2) letter a aim to:
a. assist female victims of violence in recognizing their problems and finding effective ways to overcome them independently;
b. empower female victims of violence to determine their future;
c. provide moral support to female victims of violence in facing the process they are undergoing; and
d. make female victims of violence feel accepted in the community environment.
(2) Counseling services are conducted by gender-responsive social rehabilitation officers, consisting of:
a. counselors;
b. psychologists; and
c. psychiatrists.
(3) Counseling services are conducted in a place that guarantees a sense of security, comfort and confidentiality of information from female victims of violence.
(4) Stages of counseling services include:
a. initial counseling;
b. follow-up counseling;
c. case discussion; and
d. site visits.

Article 19
(1) The Regency Government may provide safe houses for women victims of acts of violence.
(17) (2) The provision of safe house services as referred to in Article 17 paragraph (2) letter b aims to protect women victims of acts of violence from intimidation by perpetrators or other parties.
(3) The location of safe houses as referred to in paragraph (1) must be kept confidential.
(4) The criteria for women victims of acts of violence who require safe houses include:
a. their life is threatened;
b. receiving rejection from family or community;
c. requiring intensive services but their residence is relatively far; and/or
d. abandoned if not placed in a safe house.
(5) The implementation of safe house services is carried out by social rehabilitation officers who have gender sensitivity or gender awareness, consisting of:
a. social workers/volunteers;
b. security officers; and/or
c. community leaders.
(6) Further provisions regarding the implementation of safe house services are regulated in a Regent Regulation.

Article 20
(1) Spiritual guidance as referred to in Article 17 paragraph (2) letter d aims for psychological recovery.
(2) Spiritual guidance as referred to in paragraph (1) is implemented based on the principles of:
a. not imposing any religion or belief; and b. leaving the final decision in the hands of the victim (self determination).
(3) Spiritual guidance is conducted by spiritual guidance officers. (4) Spiritual guidance officers as referred to in paragraph (3) consist of consultants from the Marriage Advisory, Development and Preservation Agency, Officers of the Religious Affairs Office, Ustad/Ulama, Priests, Pastors and other religious leaders who have gender sensitivity or gender awareness.
(18)

Paragraph 5
Victim Assistance, Enforcement and Legal Aid Article 21 (1) Victim assistance services, enforcement and legal aid as referred to in Article 13 letter d constitute actions by law enforcement officials to implement laws and regulations in the context of handling cases of violence against women, especially to impose sanctions on perpetrators and provide protection for witnesses and/or victims.
(2) Victim assistance services, enforcement and legal aid as referred to in paragraph (1) shall be carried out by prioritizing the best interests of the victim.

Article 22
(1) Victim assistance services, enforcement and legal aid are intended to provide assistance and protection to women victims of violence and witnesses with a gender-responsive approach so that victims obtain justice and legal certainty.
(2) Types of legal aid services include:
a. legal aid in the reporting process and preparation of official examination reports;
b. legal aid in the prosecution process at the prosecutor's office; c. legal aid in the examination process at court; d. legal aid in the court hearing decision process until there is a final legal decision; and
e. legal aid in the mediation process.
(3) The Regional Government provides legal aid to women victims of violence based on the provisions of laws and regulations.
(4) In providing legal aid as referred to in paragraph (3) the Regional Government may cooperate with legal aid institutions, women crisis centers, advocates and/or other gender-sensitive legal assistants.
(19)

Paragraph 6
Repatriation and Social Reintegration

Article 23
(1) Repatriation is intended to return women victims of acts of violence to their region of origin.
(2) Repatriation of women victims of acts of violence is accompanied by social reintegration efforts.

Article 24
(1) Social reintegration is carried out in the context of reuniting female victims of acts of Violence with family, substitute family, or community that can provide Protection and fulfillment of needs for victims.
(2) Social reintegration is aimed so that:
a. female victims of acts of violence can resume their social life; and
b. female victims of acts of violence can have a decent life.
(3) Social reintegration as referred to in paragraph (1) is carried out after victims receive social rehabilitation services.

Article 25
The Regency Government shall implement the repatriation and social reintegration of women victims of violence in accordance with the laws and regulations.

Fourth Part
Employment Protection

Article 26
(1) Employers who employ women are obligated to maintain decency, safety and security while in the workplace. (2) Employers are obligated to place female workers in places that are safe for women's reproductive health.
(20) (3) Employers are obligated to fulfill the rights of female workers to receive wages in accordance with the provisions of laws and regulations.
(4) Employers are obligated to provide special breastfeeding facilities at their company.
Article 27 (1) Female workers/laborers who are less than 18 (eighteen) years of age are prohibited from being employed between 23.00 WIB until 07.00 WIB.
(2) Employers are prohibited from employing pregnant female workers/laborers who according to a doctor's statement are dangerous to the health and safety of their pregnancy.
(3) Employers who employ female workers/laborers between 23.00 WIB until 07.00 WIB, are obligated to: a. provide nutritious food and drinks; and b. provide shuttle transportation for female workers/laborers who depart for and return from work between 23.00 WIB until 05.00 WIB.

CHAPTER IV
IMPROVEMENT OF FAMILY QUALITY
Article 28
(1) In order to improve the quality of family life, the Regency Government is responsible for:
a. conducting efforts to improve family quality to realize gender equality and children's rights;
b. providing services for families in realizing gender equality and children's rights; and
c. strengthening and developing institutions providing family quality improvement services in the Regency.
(2) The responsibility of the Regency Government in efforts to improve family quality as referred to in paragraph (1) shall be implemented by regional apparatus in accordance with their duties and functions.
(21)

Article 29
Regional Government undertakes efforts to improve family quality as referred to in Article 28 paragraph (1) letter a including:
a. coaching;
b. guidance;
c. supervision;
d. facilitation of family development implementation; and
e. improving the health quality of pregnant and breastfeeding mothers.

Article 30
Guidance as referred to in Article 29 letter a, includes:
a. growth and development of children under five years of age;
b. protection of adolescent reproductive health;
c. maturation of marriage age;
d. birth regulation; and
e. population management.

Article 31
Guidance as referred to in Article 28 letter b includes: a. conducting counseling on improving the quality of life of women; and
b. conducting premarital counseling.

Article 32
Supervision as referred to in Article 28 letter c includes: a. conducting evaluation of growth and development data of children under five years old; and b. conducting evaluation of post-marriage data.

Article 33
Provision of services for families as referred to in Article 28 paragraph (1) letter b includes:
a. communication, information and education;
b. contraceptive devices and medicines;
c. recording and reporting of family planning services;
d. adequate health service facilities and infrastructure;
e. lactation rooms in every public facility.
(22)

Article 34
Communication, information and education as referred to in Article 33 letter a constitutes communication activities to increase knowledge and improve the attitudes and behavior of families and communities in population and family planning programs.

Article 35
(1) In addition to the Regency Government, private parties (private parties include companies that provide public services) are obligated to provide lactation rooms.
(2) Lactation rooms as referred to in paragraph (1) shall be proper and adequate rooms.
(3) Private parties that do not provide lactation rooms as referred to in paragraph (2) shall be subject to administrative sanctions in the form of:
a. written warning;
b. restriction of business activities;
c. suspension of business activities;
d. administrative fine; and/or
e. revocation of permit.

CHAPTER V
FULFILLMENT OF CHILDREN'S RIGHTS
Part One
General

Article 36
(1) The Regency Government in efforts to fulfill children's rights is responsible for:
a. establishing and implementing policies and programs/activities for the fulfillment of children's rights;
b. respecting and guaranteeing the human rights of every child without distinguishing ethnicity, religion, race, class, gender, ethnic group, culture and language, legal status of the child, birth order of the child, and physical and/or mental condition;
(23)
c. strengthening and developing service provider institutions for improving family quality in realizing children's rights;
d. guaranteeing the protection, care, and welfare of children by taking into account the rights and obligations of parents, guardians, or other persons who are legally responsible for the child;
e. providing facilities and infrastructure in the implementation of the fulfillment of children's rights;
f. guaranteeing the right of children to express opinions in accordance with the age and level of intelligence of the child;
g. conducting supervision and guidance in the framework of implementation; and
h. conducting cooperation with the private sector and/or Non-Governmental Organizations in the implementation of child protection.
(2) The responsibility of the Regency Government in efforts to protect children as referred to in paragraph (1) shall be implemented by regional apparatus in accordance with their duties and functions.

Second Part
Birth Registration

Article 37
(1) The Regency Government is obligated to administer the registration of child births with the issuance of birth certificates.
(2) Birth certificates as referred to in paragraph (1) shall be administered free of charge.

Third Part
Education

Article 38
The Regency Government guarantees the implementation of the 9 (nine) year compulsory education program for children aged 7 (seven) to 15 (fifteen) years.
(24)

Article 39
(1) Every education unit organizer must provide opportunities for children to obtain educational services without discrimination.
(2) Every education unit organizer is prohibited from expelling children from educational institutions unless there is a guarantee for the continuity of the child's education.
(3) In the event that a child victim of acts of Violence experiences dropping out of school, the education unit organizer must coordinate with related agencies.

Article 40
(1) Every child has the right to obtain education and teaching in the context of developing their personality and level of intelligence in accordance with their interests and talents.
(2) Every child has the right to receive protection in educational units from sexual crimes and violence committed by educators, educational personnel, fellow students, and/or other parties.

Article 41
(1) Children with disabilities and children with special needs are given equal opportunities to obtain formal, non-formal and informal education services, including inclusive education programs.
(2) The Regency Government provides educators for inclusive education at every education unit.

Fourth Part
Health and Social

Article 42
(1) The Regency Government is obligated to provide comprehensive health services for children, so that every Child obtains an optimal degree of health from the time they are in the womb.
(2) Comprehensive health services as referred to in paragraph (1) include: (25)
a. promotive, preventive, curative and rehabilitative efforts for basic health services;
b. exemption from cost burden for malnourished Children, children with disabilities, children with special needs, children infected with HIV/AIDS, child workers, child victims of narcotics abuse, alcohol, psychotropic substances and other addictive substances, children who are victims of violence, and child victims of human trafficking; and (3) The Regency Government, community, family and parents are obligated to ensure that children who are born are protected from diseases that threaten survival and/or cause disability.

Article 43
(1) Promotive efforts for basic health services as referred to in Article 42 paragraph (2) letter a are aimed at improving health status more optimally.
(2) Promotive efforts as referred to in paragraph (1) are carried out by providing socialization or counseling to the community regarding healthy lifestyle patterns which include:
a. the importance of exclusive breastfeeding for infants;
b. the importance of immunization for toddlers;
c. clean living patterns;
d. the importance of nutritious food; and
e. the dangers of narcotics and smoking.
(3) In addition to promotive efforts as referred to in paragraph (2), the Regional Government may conduct other activities in the context of health service provision.

Article 44
(1) Preventive efforts for basic health services as referred to in Article 42 paragraph (2) letter a are aimed at preventing the occurrence of disease or health problems.
(2) Efforts as referred to in paragraph (1) are carried out by: a. obligating mothers to provide exclusive breastfeeding to infants up to 6 (six) months of age;
(26)
b. provision of free immunization and vitamins to infants and toddlers;
c. provision of nutritious food to toddlers from underprivileged families;
d. optimizing the role of posyandu for health services for mothers and toddlers;
e. conducting periodic health examinations; and f. providing sanitation facilities and clean water in every school.
Article 45 (1) Curative efforts for basic health services as referred to in Article 42 paragraph (2) letter a are aimed at preventing disease from becoming more severe through treatment.
(2) Treatment as referred to in paragraph (1) is implemented free of charge for Children from underprivileged families.

Article 46
(1) Rehabilitative efforts for basic health services as referred to in Article 42 paragraph (2) letter a are intended to maintain and restore conditions after illness.
(2) Rehabilitative efforts as referred to in paragraph (1) are carried out through periodic health examinations.

Article 47
(1) The implementation of social welfare is directed to: a. individuals;
b. families;
c. groups; and/or
d. communities.
(2) The implementation of social welfare as referred to in paragraph (1) is prioritized for those who have lives that are not decent from a humanitarian perspective and have social problem criteria: (27)
a. poverty;
b. abandonment;
c. disability;
d. remoteness;
e. social dysfunction and behavioral deviation;
f. disaster victims; and/or
g. victims of violence, exploitation and discrimination. (3) Social welfare as referred to in paragraph (1) may be in the form of provision of services:
a. health;
b. education;
c. legal aid;
d. assistance;
e. social, mental and spiritual guidance;
f. social rehabilitation; and/or
g. reintegration.

Fifth Part
Employment

Article 48
The Regency Government conducts periodic supervision of every person/entity that employs children in efforts to protect child workers.

Article 49
Every person/entity that employs children must observe the requirements:
a. minimum age of 15 (fifteen) years;
b. obtain written consent from the parent/guardian of the child worker;
c. have a written employment agreement between the employer and the parent/guardian of the child worker and obtain validation from the authorized agency;
d. not employ children at night;
e. maximum working time of 3 (three) hours per day.
(28)

Article 50
In the event that a child is employed together with adult workers, then the workplace of the child must be separated from the workplace of the adult workers.

Article 51
(1) Every person/entity is prohibited from employing and involving children in the worst forms of work.
(2) The worst forms of work referred to in paragraph (1) include:
a. all work in the form of slavery or similar practices;
b. all work that exploits, provides, or offers children for prostitution, production of pornography, pornographic performances, or gambling;
c. all work that exploits, provides, or involves children for the production and trade of alcoholic beverages, narcotics, psychotropic substances, and other addictive substances; and/or
d. all work that endangers the health, safety, or morals of children.

Sixth Part
Facilitation of the Juvenile Justice System

Article 52
(1) Facilitation of the child justice system includes criminal and civil justice.
(2) Facilitation of the criminal justice system as referred to in paragraph (1) includes:
a. provision of companions or child volunteers during examination by investigators;
b. provision of legal and psychological assistance services;
c. involving members/investigators of the local police in efforts to prevent criminal acts committed by children as well as children as victims;
(29)
d. realizing restorative justice processes for children in conflict with the law; and
e. rehabilitation and social reintegration services.
(3) Fulfillment of children's rights in the civil justice system as referred to in paragraph (1) includes the provision and delivery of consultation and legal assistance services during the judicial process.
(4) In facilitating the child justice system as referred to in paragraph (1) and paragraph (2), the authorized regional apparatus is the regional apparatus in charge of Women's and Children's Protection and the Integrated Service Center for Women's and Children's Empowerment which has the obligation to accompany child victims and perpetrators, in police examinations, prosecution and in court.

Seventh Part
Children's Forum

Article 53
(1) In organizing the fulfillment of children's rights to express opinions and participate, the Regency Government facilitates the establishment of a Children's Forum.
(2) The Children's Forum as referred to in paragraph (1) represents children in the Region, both representation of children's geographical domicile, components of children's socio-cultural groups and children's educational background.
(3) In every formulation of policies related to children, the Regional Government is obligated to consider and accommodate the opinions of children conveyed through the children's participation forum.
(4) Further provisions regarding the establishment of the Children's Forum as referred to in paragraph (1) through paragraph (3) shall be stipulated by Regent's Decree.
(30)

Eighth Part
Child-Friendly Regency

Article 54
(1) In order to fulfill children's rights in an integrated, systematic and sustainable manner, the Regency Government shall establish a policy for the Development of KLA.
(2) The policy for the Development of KLA shall contain:
a. the concept of KLA concerning objectives, strategies and the roles of related parties;
b. children's rights; and
c. the approach to KLA development.
Article 55 (1) The policy for KLA development shall be directed towards the fulfillment of children's rights which are divided into 5 (five) groups, comprising:
a. civil rights and freedoms;
b. family environment and alternative care;
c. basic health and welfare;
d. education, leisure time utilization, and cultural activities; and
e. special protection.
(2) Further provisions regarding the policy for KLA development as referred to in paragraph (1) shall be regulated by Regent Regulation.
Article 56 (1) In order to ensure the effectiveness of the implementation of KLA policy in the Regency, a KLA Task Force shall be established.
(2) The establishment and membership composition of the KLA Task Force shall be determined by Regent Decree.
(3) The KLA Task Force shall be domiciled at the Office of the Women's Empowerment and Child Protection Agency.
(4) The KLA Task Force as referred to in paragraph (1), shall have the following principal duties:
a. coordinating the implementation of policies and the development of KLA;
b. conducting socialization, advocacy and communication, information and education on KLA policy;
(31)
c. collecting basic data for KLA development;
d. conducting needs analysis based on basic data;
e. conducting dissemination of basic data;
f. determining the focus and priority of programs in realizing KLA, which shall be adjusted to regional potential;
g. preparing a 5 (five) year regional action plan for KLA and work mechanisms; and
h. conducting monitoring, evaluation and reporting at least once every 1 (one) year.

Article 57
In carrying out its duties, the KLA Task Force may coordinate and cooperate with PPT institutions and other institutions that provide child protection services.

CHAPTER VI
CHILD PROTECTION
Article 58
The Regency Government, in order to provide protection for children:
a. implements and supports national policy in the administration of child protection in the Regency;
b. guarantees children to exercise their rights in expressing opinions in accordance with the age and intelligence level of the child;
c. conducts prevention of violence against children by optimizing the role of customary institutions;
d. conducts socialization, seminars and counseling regarding child protection and rights;
e. develops child-friendly school models;
f. provides public facilities that are friendly and safe for children; and
g. encourages public awareness of the importance of child protection.
(32)

Article 59
Service efforts carried out by the Regency Government towards child victims of acts of violence as referred to in Article 36 through Article 58 shall apply mutatis mutandis to service efforts towards child victims of acts of violence.

CHAPTER VII
SPECIAL PROTECTION OF CHILDREN
Article 60
(1) Special protection of children is provided to: a. children in emergency situations;
b. children in conflict with the law;
c. children from minority and isolated groups;
d. children who are exploited economically and/or sexually;
e. children who are victims of narcotics, alcohol, psychotropic, and other addictive substance abuse;
f. children who are victims of pornography;
g. children with HIV/AIDS;
h. children victims of kidnapping, sale, and/or trafficking;
i. children victims of physical and/or psychological violence;
j. children victims of sexual crimes;
k. children victims of terrorist networks;
l. children with disabilities;
m. children victims of maltreatment and neglect; n. children with deviant social behavior; and o. children who are victims of stigmatization from labeling related to their parents' condition.
(2) The Regency Government in implementing the handling of special protection of children as referred to in paragraph (1) shall be in accordance with the provisions of Laws and Regulations.

Article 61
Children in emergency situations as referred to in Article 60 paragraph (1) letter a consist of: (33)
a. children who become refugees;
b. children victims of riots;
c. children victims of natural disasters; and
d. children in armed conflict situations.

Article 62
Special protection for children who become refugees as referred to in Article 61 paragraph (1) letter a shall be implemented in accordance with the provisions of laws and regulations.

Article 63
Special protection for child victims of riots, disaster victims, and children in armed conflict as referred to in Article 61 paragraph (1) letter b, letter c, and letter d, shall be implemented through:
a. fulfillment of basic needs consisting of food, clothing, shelter, education, health, learning and recreation, security guarantees, and equal treatment; and
b. fulfillment of special needs for children with disabilities and children experiencing psychosocial disorders.

Article 64
Special Protection for children in conflict with the law as referred to in Article 60 paragraph (1) letter b is carried out through:
a. humane treatment with attention to needs according to their age;
b. separation from adults;
c. provision of legal assistance and other assistance effectively;
d. implementation of recreational activities;
e. freedom from torture, punishment, or other treatment that is cruel, inhumane and degrading to dignity and status;
f. avoidance of imposition of the death penalty and/or life imprisonment;
(34)
g. avoidance of arrest, detention or imprisonment, except as a last resort and for the shortest period of time;
h. provision of justice before a juvenile court that is objective, impartial, and in a hearing closed to the public;
i. avoidance of publication of their identity;
j. provision of assistance from Parents/Guardians and persons trusted by the child;
k. provision of social advocacy;
l. provision of private life;
m. provision of accessibility, especially for Children with Disabilities;
n. provision of education;
o. provision of health services; and
p. provision of other rights in accordance with the provisions of Laws and Regulations.

Article 65
Special Protection for Children from minority and isolated groups as referred to in Article 60 paragraph (1) letter c is carried out through the provision of infrastructure and facilities to be able to enjoy their own culture, acknowledge and practice their own religious teachings, and use their own language.

Article 66
Special Protection for children who are exploited economically and/or sexually as referred to in Article 60 paragraph (1) letter d shall be carried out through:
a. dissemination and/or socialization of statutory provisions related to the protection of children who are exploited economically and/or sexually.
b. involvement of various companies, labor unions, Non-Governmental Organizations, and the community in the elimination of economic and/or sexual exploitation of children.
(35)

Article 67
Special protection for Children who are victims of narcotics, alcohol, psychotropic substances, and other addictive substances abuse as referred to in Article 60 paragraph (1) letter e and children involved in their production and distribution shall be carried out through supervision, prevention, treatment, and rehabilitation efforts.

Article 68
(1) Every Person is obligated to protect children from the influence of pornography and prevent children's access to information containing elements of pornography.
(2) Special Protection for children who become victims of pornography as referred to in Article 60 paragraph (1) letter f is implemented through efforts of guidance, assistance, as well as social, physical health and mental recovery.
(3) Guidance, assistance, as well as social, physical health and mental recovery as referred to in paragraph (1) is implemented in accordance with the provisions of Laws and Regulations.

Article 69
Special protection for Children with HIV/AIDS as referred to in Article 60 paragraph (1) letter f shall be carried out through supervision, prevention, treatment, care, and rehabilitation efforts.

Article 70
Special protection for child victims of abduction, sale, and/or trafficking as referred to in Article 60 paragraph (1) letter h shall be carried out through supervision, protection, prevention, care, and rehabilitation efforts.

Article 71
Special protection for child victims of physical and/or psychological violence as referred to in Article 60 paragraph (1) letter i is carried out through efforts: (36)
a. dissemination and socialization of provisions of laws and regulations that protect child victims of acts of violence; and
b. monitoring, reporting, and imposition of sanctions.

Article 72
Special Protection for child victims of sexual crimes as referred to in Article 60 paragraph (1) letter j shall be carried out through efforts to:
a. provide education on reproductive health, religious values, and moral values;
b. social rehabilitation;
c. psychosocial assistance during treatment until recovery; and
d. provide protection and assistance at every level of examination starting from investigation, prosecution, up to examination in Court proceedings.

Article 73
Special Protection for child victims of terrorism networks as referred to in Article 60 paragraph (1) letter k shall be carried out through efforts to:
a. provide education on education, ideology, and nationalism values;
b. provide counseling on the dangers of terrorism;
c. provide social rehabilitation; and
d. provide social assistance.

Article 74
Special protection for children with disabilities as referred to in Article 60 paragraph (1) letter 1 is carried out through efforts to:
a. treat children humanely in accordance with the dignity and rights of the child;
b. fulfill special needs;
c. provide equal treatment with other children to achieve social integration as fully as possible and individual development;
d. provide social assistance.
(37)

Article 75
Special Protection for child victims of mistreatment and neglect as referred to in Article 60 paragraph (1) letter m shall be carried out through supervision, prevention, care, counseling, social rehabilitation, and social assistance efforts.

Article 76
Special Protection for children with deviant social behavior as referred to in Article 60 paragraph (1) letter n shall be carried out through guidance on religious values and social values, counseling, social rehabilitation, and social assistance.

Article 77
Special Protection for children who become victims of stigmatization from labeling related to their parents' condition as referred to in Article 60 paragraph (1) letter o shall be carried out through counseling, social rehabilitation, and social assistance.

Article 78
(1) Every child who becomes a victim as referred to in Article 60 paragraph (1) letter b, letter d, letter f, letter h, letter i and letter j has the right to submit to the Court in the form of the right to restitution which is the responsibility of the perpetrator of the crime.
(2) Further provisions regarding the implementation of restitution as referred to in paragraph (1) shall be regulated by Regent Regulation.

Article 79
Further provisions regarding Special Protection for Children as referred to in Article 60 shall be regulated by Regent Regulation.
(38)

CHAPTER VIII
PARTICIPATION OF THE COMMUNITY, AND PARENTS
AND/OR FAMILY
Part One
Community

Article 80
(1) Community participation in efforts to protect women and children:
a. participate in prevention and provide support for the development of programs to prevent violations of the rights of women and children;
b. provide information and/or report violations of the rights of women and children to the competent authorities; and
c. monitor the law enforcement process regarding cases of violations of the rights of women and children.
(2) Community participation as referred to in paragraph (1) is based on laws and regulations and the values of local wisdom of the local community.

Article 81
(1) In the context of preventing violations of women's rights, the community:
a. monitors various conditions occurring in their environment that may give rise to violations of women's rights;
b. increases awareness of violations of women's rights;
c. strengthens the role of community organizations, religious organizations and the family environment;
d. conducts supervision of the implementation of Protection of Women and Children carried out by the Regional Government through a mechanism for conveying aspirations to the Regent or Regional House of Representatives.
(39) (2) In the context of preventing violations of children's rights, the community:
a. monitors various conditions occurring in their environment that give rise to violations of children's rights;
b. increases awareness of violations of children's rights;
c. strengthens the role of community organizations, religious organizations and the family environment; and
d. prevents the occurrence of marriage at an early age.

Second Part
Parents and/or Family

Article 82
The family in efforts to protect the rights of women has the role to:
a. protect women from conflict and violence in the household that degrades the dignity of women;
b. provide equal opportunities to women to develop themselves in the fields of education, social, economic, political and cultural; and
c. prevent the occurrence of violations against the rights of women in the household.

Article 83
(1) Parents in the effort to protect children's rights have the role to:
a. care for, maintain, educate, and protect children;
b. develop children according to their abilities, talents, and interests;
c. provide formal and non-formal education;
d. provide character education and instill moral values in children;
e. guide children to behave in accordance with religion, ethics and morals as well as customs; and
f. prevent the occurrence of marriage at an early age.
(40) (2) In the event that parents do not exist, or their whereabouts are unknown, or due to a certain reason, cannot carry out their obligations and responsibilities, then the obligations and responsibilities as referred to in paragraph (1) may be transferred to the family.

CHAPTER IX
GUIDANCE AND SUPERVISION
Article 84
(1) The Regency Government is obligated to conduct guidance and supervision of the implementation of protection for women and children victims of violence.
(2) Guidance and supervision as referred to in paragraph (1), includes:
a. guidelines and fulfillment standards;
b. technical guidance and training;
c. provision of facilities;
d. monitoring; and
e. evaluation.
(3) Guidance as referred to in paragraph (2) is in order to realize the objectives of protection for women and children victims of violence in accordance with minimum service standards implemented by the agency responsible for Women and Children Protection.
(4) The agency responsible for Women and Children Protection is obligated to report periodically to the Regent regarding the implementation of protection for women and children victims of violence.

CHAPTER X
FINANCING
Article 85 Financing for the implementation of programs and activities for the Protection of Women and Children is sourced from: a. regional revenue and expenditure budget; and b. other lawful sources in accordance with the provisions of laws and regulations.
(41)

CHAPTER XI
CLOSING PROVISIONS
Article 86
The implementing regulations of this Regional Regulation shall be stipulated no later than 6 (six) months calculated from the promulgation of this Regional Regulation.

Article 87
This Regional Regulation shall come into force on the date of promulgation.
