# PROTECTION OF WOMEN AND CHILDREN

REGENT OF PESAWARAN

LAMPUNG PROVINCE

# REGIONAL REGULATION OF PESAWARAN REGENCY

NUMBER 1 OF 2019

CONCERNING

PROTECTION OF WOMEN AND CHILDREN

BY THE GRACE OF GOD ALMIGHTY

REGENT OF PESAWARAN,

Considering:
 
 
 
 
 
 
 
a. that the Republic of Indonesia is a State that upholds the dignity and worth of every human being, provides guarantees for the rights and security of women and children and freedom from all forms of violence, mistreatment, exploitation, trafficking and neglect;
b. that in order for efforts to protect children and children to obtain optimal results, there needs to be concrete action from the regional government and it is necessary to increase the broad participation of the community;
c. that based on Article 12 paragraph (2) letter b of Law Number 23 of 2014 concerning Regional Government as has been amended several times most recently by Law Number 9 of 2015 concerning the Second Amendment to Law Number 23 of 2014 concerning Regional Government, women's empowerment and child protection are governmental affairs that must be implemented by the Regional Government;
d. that based on the considerations as referred to in letter a, letter b and letter c above, it is necessary to establish a Regional Regulation concerning the Protection of Women and Children;

In View of:
 
 
 
 
 
 
 
1. Article 18 paragraph (6) of the 1945 Constitution of the Republic of Indonesia;

# CHAPTER I

## GENERAL PROVISIONS

#### Article 1

In this Regional Regulation, the following terms shall mean:  
1. Region is Pesawaran Regency.  
2. Regional Government is the Regent as an element of the Regional Government organizer who leads the implementation of government which is the authority of the autonomous region.  
3. Regent is the Regent of Pesawaran.  
4. Regional House of Representatives hereinafter referred to as DPRD is the Regional House of Representatives of Pesawaran Regency.  
5. Sub-district is a part of the Regency area led by a sub-district head.  
6. Village is a part of the Regency area led by a Village Head;  
7. Village Government is the village government in Pesawaran Regency.  
8. Community is individuals, families, groups, and social organizations and/or community organizations.  
9. Family is the smallest unit in society consisting of husband, wife and children, or father and children or mother and children or blood relatives in direct line upward or downward laterally to the third degree.  
10. Parents are biological father and/or mother or step father and/or mother, or adoptive father and/or mother.  
11. Guardian is a person or body who in reality exercises parental authority as a parent over a child.  
12. Woman is a human being who can menstruate, become pregnant, give birth and breastfeed.  
13. Child is a person who has not reached the age of 18 (eighteen) years, including a baby still in the womb.  
14. Neglected child is a child whose needs are not properly fulfilled, whether physical, mental, spiritual, or social.  
15. Protection is any effort aimed at providing a sense of security to victims carried out by family, advocates, social institutions, police, prosecutors, courts, or other parties either temporarily or based on court determination.  
16. Protection of women is any effort made to ensure the fulfillment of women's rights as a form of respect for human rights.  
17. Empowerment of women is any effort to improve physical, mental, spiritual, social capacity, knowledge, and skills so that women are ready to be utilized according to their respective abilities.  
18. Child protection is all activities to guarantee and protect children and their rights so they can live, grow, develop and participate optimally in accordance with human dignity and receive protection from violence and discrimination.  
19. Child with Disability is a Child who has physical, mental, intellectual, or sensory limitations for a long period of time who in interacting with the environment and community attitudes may encounter obstacles that make it difficult to participate fully and effectively based on equal rights.  
20. Child with Excellence is a Child who has extraordinary intelligence or has special potential and/or talent not limited to intellectual ability, but also in other fields.  
21. Adopted Child is a Child whose rights are transferred from the environment of the authority of the Family of Parents, legal Guardian, or other person responsible for the care, education, and raising of the Child into the environment of the adoptive Parents' Family based on a court decision or determination.  
22. Foster Child is a Child who is cared for by a person or institution to be provided with guidance, maintenance, care, education, and health because the Parents or one of the Parents is unable to guarantee the proper growth and development of the Child.  
23. Custody is the power of Parents to care for, educate, maintain, foster, protect, and develop the Child in accordance with the religion professed and in accordance with the ability, talent, and interest.  
24. Child's Rights are part of human rights that must be guaranteed, protected, and fulfilled by Parents, Family, community, state, government, and regional government.  
25. Companion is a social worker who has professional competence in their field.  
26. Violence is any act unlawfully, which results in suffering or pain physically, psychologically, sexually, and/or neglect, including threats to commit acts, coercion, or deprivation of a person's freedom.  
27. Violence against women is any act based on gender differences that results in or may result in suffering or pain to women physically, sexually or psychologically, including threats of certain actions, coercion or arbitrary deprivation of freedom, whether occurring in the public sphere or in private life.  
28. Violence against children is any act against a child that results in suffering or pain physically, mentally, sexually, psychologically, including neglect and ill-treatment that threatens bodily integrity and degrades the dignity of the child.  
29. Child-Friendly Regency is a Regency that has a Development and Public Service system from the Regency Government with support from parents, families, communities, private sector and Children's forums for the fulfillment of children's rights through the integration of commitments and resources that are planned comprehensively and sustainably through policies, programs, activities and budgeting for the welfare of children.  
30. Integrated Service Center hereinafter abbreviated as PPT is a cooperative network that organizes integrated social service functions.  
31. Integrated Service Center for the Protection of Women and Children hereinafter abbreviated as P2TP2A is a cooperative network that organizes integrated service functions for the protection of women and children.  
32. Community Social Institution is a Non-Governmental Organization (NGO) and/or other community organizations.  
33. Family Welfare Consultation Institution hereinafter abbreviated as LK3 is an integrated social service unit that handles family psychosocial problems to realize family resilience.  
34. Regional Child Protection Commission hereinafter abbreviated as KPAD is an integrated social service unit that provides child protection services to realize the fulfillment of children's rights.  
35. Children's Forum is a Social Organization that represents the voice and participation of children that is independent and not affiliated with political organizations where the establishment and preparation of its activities are determined in a Regent Regulation.  
36. Gender mainstreaming is a strategy built to integrate gender into an integral dimension of planning, formulation, implementation, monitoring, and evaluation of national development program policies.  
37. Discrimination is any restriction, harassment, or exclusion that is directly or indirectly based on human differentiation on the basis of religion, ethnicity, race, ethnic group, group, class, social status, economic status, gender, language, political beliefs, which results in the reduction, deviation or elimination of recognition, implementation or use of human rights and fundamental freedoms in life both individually and collectively in the political, economic, legal, social, cultural fields and other aspects of life.  
38. Safe House (Shelter) is a shelter for women and children including families and witnesses of victims of violence.

# CHAPTER II

## SCOPE

#### Article 2

The Scope of this Regional Regulation consists of:  
a. implementation of protection of women;  
b. implementation of protection of children;  
c. obligations and responsibilities;  
d. institutions;  
e. child-friendly regency;  
f. child-friendly regency task force;  
g. special development planning deliberation for women and children;  
h. financing.

# CHAPTER III

## PRINCIPLES, PRINCIPLES AND OBJECTIVES

#### Article 3

The implementation of the protection of women is based on Pancasila and the 1945 Constitution of the Republic of Indonesia covering:  
a. respect for human rights;  
b. gender justice and equality;  
c. non-discrimination;  
d. protection of victims;  
e. the best interests of women;  
f. respect for the opinions of women;  
g. legal certainty; and  
h. empowerment of women.

#### Article 4

The basic principles of the convention on the rights of the child include:  
a. non-discrimination;  
b. the best interests of the child;  
c. the right to life, survival, and development; and  
d. respect for the views of the child.

#### Article 5

The objectives of implementing the protection of women and children are:  
a. to ensure the fulfillment of the rights of women and children;  
b. to protect and provide a sense of security for women and children;  
c. to maintain the integrity of the household in order to realize a harmonious and prosperous family;  
d. to ensure the fulfillment of the rights of women and children;  
e. to provide services to women and children victims of violence;  
f. to provide protection to reporters and witnesses;  
g. to realize quality children with noble character and prosperity;  
h. to empower women victims of violence.

# CHAPTER IV

## IMPLEMENTATION OF WOMEN'S PROTECTION

## Part One

Rights of Women

#### Article 6

The rights of women are as follows:  
a. to live, maintain life and improve their standard of living;  
b. to live peacefully, safely, peacefully, happily, prosperous physically and mentally;  
c. to enjoy a good and healthy living environment;  
d. to live in a family and continue offspring in a legal marriage bond based on the provisions of laws and regulations;  
e. to obtain work according to ability, conditions and decent and fair wages;  
f. specifically for women with disabilities, the right to obtain facilities and special treatment in public services;  
g. to play an active role in the political and governmental fields in accordance with their potential and capabilities;  
h. to obtain information and legal services;  
i. to obtain services to increase their income; and  
j. to obtain other rights in accordance with their human dignity and based on the provisions of laws and regulations.

## Part Two

Empowerment of Women

#### Article 7

1. In order to improve the position, role, and quality of women, as well as efforts to realize gender equality and justice in family, community, national, and state life, women must be empowered.  
2. The empowerment of women as referred to in paragraph (1) is directed to obtain opportunities and their rights as human beings, so that they are able to play a role and participate in activities:  
a. economic;  
b. socio-cultural;  
c. political; and  
d. legal.

#### Article 8

The implementation of women's empowerment in the economic field as referred to in Article 7 paragraph (2) letter a, is carried out through:  
a. provision of skills and job training;  
b. facilitation of the formation of productive economic business groups;  
c. facilitation of strengthening and development of productive economic business groups;  
d. facilitation of capital assistance; and  
e. facilitation of marketing network development.

#### Article 9

The empowerment of women in the socio-cultural field as referred to in Article 7 paragraph (2) letter b, is carried out through:  
a. increasing knowledge, attitudes and skills to encourage the fulfillment of tiered education according to potential to improve social status;  
b. increasing awareness, willingness and ability to overcome health problems through quality promotive, preventive, curative, and rehabilitative efforts, especially in the field of reproductive health;  
c. increasing awareness and knowledge about independent, healthy and prosperous family planning including access to consultation services and marriage registration; and  
d. facilitation and efforts to preserve customs and the development of science, technology, arts and culture for the advancement of women.

#### Article 10

1. The implementation of women's empowerment in the political field as referred to in Article 7 paragraph (2) letter c, includes:  
a. involvement of women in decision-making at various levels;  
b. providing opportunities for women to hold public office;  
c. participation in general elections; and  
d. self-development through organizations to associate, assemble and express opinions.  
2. The implementation of women's empowerment in the political field as referred to in paragraph (1) is carried out in accordance with the provisions of laws and regulations.

#### Article 11

1. The implementation of women's empowerment in the legal field as referred to in Article 7 paragraph (2) letter d, includes:a. increasing awareness and knowledge in the legal field through communication, information and education services; and  
b. facilitation of access and legal consultation services.  
2. The implementation of women's empowerment in the legal field as referred to in paragraph (1) is carried out in accordance with the provisions of laws and regulations.

#### Article 12

1. The Regional Government is obliged and responsible for efforts to empower women.  
2. Efforts to empower women as referred to in paragraph (1) are carried out in an integrated manner in accordance with the provisions of laws and regulations.

#### Article 13

1. To implement the empowerment of women as referred to in Article 12 paragraph (1), the Regional Government organizes gender mainstreaming including:  
a. establishing the implementation of gender mainstreaming;  
b. coordinating, facilitating, and mediating gender mainstreaming;  
c. facilitating institutional strengthening and development of gender mainstreaming mechanisms in government institutions, women's study centers, research and development institutions, non-governmental institutions;  
d. implementing gender mainstreaming related to the fields of development, education, health, economy, law, human rights and politics, environment and socio-culture;  
e. improving the quality of life of women related to the fields of development, education, health, economy, law, human rights and politics, environment and socio-culture;  
f. integrating efforts to improve the quality of life of women related to the fields of development, education, health, economy, law, human rights and politics, environment and socio-culture;  
g. coordinating the implementation of women related to the fields of health, economy, law, rights, environment and socio-culture; improving the quality of life of development, education, human rights and politics;  
h. organizing the protection of women especially protection against victims of violence, women workers, elderly women, and women with disabilities, in conflict areas and disaster-affected areas;  
i. facilitating the integration of women's protection especially against victims of violence, women workers, elderly women and women with disabilities, in conflict areas and disaster-affected areas;  
j. coordinating the protection of women especially against victims of violence, women workers, elderly women and women with disabilities;  
k. facilitating the strengthening and development of networks of community institutions or organizations and the business world for the implementation of gender mainstreaming;  
l. developing gender-based information systems;  
m. providing and organizing integrated or non-integrated services for women victims of violence, women workers, elderly women, and women with disabilities.  
2. The forms of women's empowerment and gender mainstreaming mechanisms as referred to in paragraph (1), are implemented in accordance with laws and regulations.

## Part Three

Prevention of Violence and Participation in Prevention

### Paragraph 1

Prevention of Violence

#### Article 14

1. Efforts to prevent violence against women are carried out in an integrated manner by the Regional Government coordinated by the agency having the duties and functions of coordination in the field of women's empowerment and child protection.  
2. Prevention efforts as referred to in paragraph (1) are implemented through the following means:  
a. establishing a network in efforts to prevent violence;  
b. conducting coordination, integration, synchronization of violence prevention based on partnership work patterns between Regional Government institutions and the community;  
c. building an integrated security system in areas prone to cases of violence against women;  
d. building complaint and service units regarding violence against women at the sub-district, urban village and/or Village levels; and  
e. awareness campaigns, socialization and education to the community regarding the protection and empowerment of women.

### Paragraph 2

Participation in Prevention

#### Article 15

Participation in prevention efforts as referred to in Article 14, the Regional Government is obliged to involve:  
a. families;  
b. the community; and  
c. women's advocacy institutions.

## Part Four

Protection of Women

#### Article 16

1. Protection of women includes:  
a. protection of the personal security of victims or witnesses;  
b. physical and psychological threats;  
c. confidentiality of the identity of victims and witnesses;  
d. provision of testimony during the ongoing legal process regulated through a protection mechanism.  
2. Protection of women victims also includes the provision of safe houses as temporary shelters to obtain a sense of security during the judicial process and/or outside the judicial process.  
3. The implementation of protection and safe houses is carried out by the Regional Government.  
4. Further provisions on the mechanism for the protection of women as referred to in paragraph (2), and the implementation of safe houses as referred to in paragraph (3), shall be regulated in a Regent Regulation.

# CHAPTER V

## IMPLEMENTATION OF CHILD PROTECTION

## Part One

Rights and Obligations of Children

#### Article 17

Every child has the right to live, grow, develop, and participate reasonably in accordance with human dignity, and to receive protection from violence and discrimination.

#### Article 18

Every child has the right to a name as personal identity and citizenship status.

#### Article 19

Every Child has the right to worship according to his/her religion, to think, and to express himself/herself in accordance with his/her level of intelligence and age under the guidance of Parents or Guardians."

#### Article 20

1. Every child has the right to know his/her parents, to be raised, and to be cared for by his/her own parents.  
2. In the event that for any reason the parents cannot guarantee the child's growth and development, or the child is in a state of neglect, then the child has the right to be cared for or adopted as a foster child or adopted child by another person in accordance with the provisions of applicable laws and regulations.

#### Article 21

Every child has the right to obtain health services and social security in accordance with physical, mental, spiritual, and social needs.

#### Article 22

1. Every Child has the right to obtain education and teaching in the context of developing his/her personality and level of intelligence in accordance with his/her interests and talents.  
2. Every Child has the right to receive protection in educational units from sexual crimes and Violence committed by educators, education personnel, fellow students, and/or other parties.  
3. In addition to obtaining Children's Rights as referred to in paragraph (1) and paragraph (2), Children with Disabilities have the right to obtain special education and Children who have excellence have the right to receive special education.

#### Article 23

Every child has the right to express and be heard in his/her opinion, to receive, seek, and provide information in accordance with his/her level of intelligence and age for his/her self-development in accordance with the values of decency and propriety.

#### Article 24

Every child has the right to rest and utilize leisure time, to associate with peers, to play, to recreate, and to create in accordance with his/her interests, talents, and level of intelligence for self-development.

#### Article 25

Every Child with Disabilities has the right to obtain rehabilitation, social assistance, and maintenance of social welfare standards.

#### Article 26

1. Every child while in the care of parents, guardians, or any other party responsible for care, has the right to receive protection from treatment of:  
a. discrimination;  
b. exploitation, whether economic or sexual;  
c. neglect;  
d. cruelty, violence, and persecution;  
e. injustice; and  
f. other mistreatment.  
2. In the event that parents, guardians or child caregivers commit any form of treatment as referred to in paragraph (1), the perpetrator shall be subject to aggravated punishment.

#### Article 27

1. Every Child has the right to be cared for by his/her own Parents, unless there are valid legal reasons and/or rules showing that the separation is in the best interests of the Child and is a last resort.  
2. In the event of separation as referred to in paragraph (1), the Child still has the right to:  
a.meet directly and maintain personal relations regularly with both Parents;  
b.receive care, maintenance, education and protection for the growth and development process from both Parents in accordance with abilities, talents and interests;  
c. obtain living expenses from both Parents; and  
d.obtain other Children's Rights.

#### Article 28

Every child has the right to obtain protection from:  
a. abuse in political activities;  
b. involvement in armed conflict;  
c. involvement in social unrest;  
d. involvement in events containing elements of violence; and  
e. involvement in warfare.

#### Article 29

1. Every child has the right to obtain protection from being the target of persecution, torture, or imposition of inhuman punishment.  
2. Every child has the right to obtain freedom in accordance with the law.  
3. Arrest, detention, or criminal imprisonment of a child shall only be carried out if in accordance with applicable law and can only be carried out as a last resort.

#### Article 30

1. Every child who is deprived of his/her freedom has the right to:  
a. receive humane treatment and placement separated from adults;  
b. obtain legal assistance or other assistance effectively at every stage of applicable legal remedies; and  
c. defend himself/herself and obtain justice before an objective and impartial juvenile court in a closed hearing to the public.  
2. Every child who becomes a victim or perpetrator of sexual violence or who is in conflict with the law has the right to confidentiality.

#### Article 31

Every child who becomes a victim or perpetrator of a criminal act has the right to receive legal assistance and other assistance.

#### Article 32

Every child is obliged to:  
a. respect parents, guardians, and teachers;  
b. love family, community, and care for friends;  
c. love the homeland, nation, and state;  
d. perform worship in accordance with the teachings of his/her religion; and  
e. practice noble ethics and morals.

#### Article 33

The regional government, families, and parents are obliged to ensure that children who are born are protected from diseases that threaten the continuity of life and/or cause disability.

#### Article 34

Every Person is prohibited from performing abortion on a Child still in the womb, except for reasons and procedures justified in accordance with the provisions of laws and regulations.

#### Article 35

1. The Regional Government, Community, and Parents are obliged to protect Children from acts that interfere with the health and growth and development of Children.  
2. In carrying out their obligations as referred to in paragraph (1), the Regional Government, Community, and Parents must carry out activities that protect Children.

## Part Two

Prevention

#### Article 36

1. Efforts to prevent violence against children shall be carried out in an integrated manner by the Regional Government coordinated by the agency having the duties and functions of coordination in the field of women's empowerment and child protection.  
2. Prevention efforts as referred to in paragraph (1) shall be implemented through the following means:  
a. forming a network in efforts to prevent violence;  
b. conducting coordination, integration, synchronization of violence prevention based on partnership work patterns among regional government institutions and the community;  
c. building an integrated security system in areas prone to cases of violence against children;  
d. incorporating local content material on children's rights and the elimination of violence against children as teaching material in teaching and learning activities in early childhood education institutions up to Senior High School level;  
e. establishing complaint and service units regarding violence against children at the sub-district, urban village and/or village levels, as well as schools;  
f. awareness campaigns, socialization and education to the community regarding child protection.

## Part Three

Child Protection

#### Article 37

1. Every child shall receive protection from violent treatment, exploitation, trafficking, discrimination, maltreatment and neglect.  
2. Protection as referred to in paragraph (1) is elaborated in various areas of life, among others:  
a. in the field of health;  
b. in the field of religion;  
c. in the field of education;  
d. in the social field; and  
e. special protection.

#### Article 38

1. Protection in the field of health as referred to in Article 37 paragraph (2) letter a, shall be carried out by the government through the provision of health facilities and efforts to provide comprehensive health services for children, so that every child obtains the optimal degree of health from the womb.  
2. Comprehensive health services as referred to in paragraph (1) include promotive, preventive, curative, and rehabilitative efforts, both for basic health services and referrals.  
3. Comprehensive health service efforts as referred to in paragraph (2) shall be implemented by the Regional Government free of charge for families who are unable to afford it.

#### Article 39

1. Parents and families shall provide protection to maintain the health of children starting from examination of the child's growth and development from the womb.  
2. In the event that parents and families are unable to implement protection as referred to in paragraph (1), the Regional Government shall be obligated to fulfill it.  
3. The Regional Government shall provide free assistance and services for communities who are unable to afford it as referred to in paragraph (2), and its implementation shall be carried out in accordance with the provisions of laws and regulations.

#### Article 40

1. Every Child has the right to be cared for by his/her own Parents, unless there are valid legal reasons and/or rules showing that such separation is in the best interests of the Child and is a last resort.  
2. In the event of separation as referred to in paragraph (1), the Child shall still have the right to:  
a. meet directly and maintain regular personal relations with both Parents;  
b. receive care, maintenance, education and protection for the growth and development process from both Parents in accordance with abilities, talents, and interests;  
c. obtain living expenses from both Parents; and  
d. obtain other Children's Rights."

#### Article 41

1. Every child shall receive protection in the field of religion as referred to in Article 37 paragraph (2) letter b, to worship according to his/her religion.  
2. Protection of children in embracing their religion as referred to in paragraph (1) includes guidance, mentoring, and good practice of religious teachings for children.

#### Article 42

1. Every child shall receive protection in the field of education as referred to in Article 37 paragraph (2) letter c, to obtain education.  
2. The Regional Government, parents, and families must provide the widest possible opportunity for children to obtain education from an early age.  
3. The Regional Government shall provide adequate facilities and infrastructure to support the teaching and learning process for children.  
4. Non-governmental institutions may provide teaching and learning facilities and infrastructure for children, in accordance with the provisions of laws and regulations.

#### Article 43

Education as referred to in Article 42 paragraph (1), is intended and directed towards:  
a. development of personality abilities, mental, talents, and physical abilities of children to the fullest extent possible;  
b. development of respect for parents and fellow human beings;  
c. development of understanding of customary and cultural values in the place where children grow and develop;  
d. preparation of children for a responsible life; and  
e. development of respect and love for the nation and state.

#### Article 44

1. The Regional Government shall provide protection for children with disabilities, to obtain opportunities and accessibility to obtain inclusive education and/or special education.  
2. Children who have excellence shall be given the opportunity to receive special education.

#### Article 45

The Regional Government shall provide education costs and/or free assistance or special services for children from families who are unable to afford it, neglected children, and children in remote areas.

#### Article 46

1. Protection in the field of education as referred to in Article 37 letter c shall apply within and in the environment of education units.  
2. Protection as referred to in paragraph (1) shall be in the form of protection from acts of physical violence, psychological violence, sexual crimes, and other crimes.  
3. Protection within and outside education units may be carried out by educators, education personnel, regional government officials, and/or the community.

#### Article 47

Special services as referred to in Article 33, in addition to assistance in the form of money, school equipment, may also be in the form of provision of school shuttle buses, the procurement of which shall be in accordance with regional financial capacity.

#### Article 48

1. Child protection in the Social field as referred to in Article 37 paragraph (2) letter d, includes maintenance and care of neglected children.  
2. Maintenance and care as referred to in paragraph (1), includes:  
a. fulfillment of basic needs consisting of clothing, food, shelter, education, health, security guarantees, freedom to worship according to their religion and equal treatment with other children to achieve optimal social integration; and  
b. fulfillment of special needs for children with disabilities.  
3. Maintenance of neglected children as referred to in paragraph (1), may also be carried out by individuals and non-governmental institutions or community institutions.  
4. Regional government institutions, and/or individuals, and/or community institutions in providing services as referred to in paragraph (2), may cooperate with various related service units.  
5. Supervision of the implementation of maintenance and care as referred to in paragraph (1), shall be carried out by regional apparatus organizations (OPD) that administer affairs in the social field in accordance with the provisions of laws and regulations.

#### Article 49

Special protection as referred to in Article 37 paragraph (2) letter e, is given specifically to:  
a. children in conflict with the law;  
b. children from minority and isolated groups;  
c. children who are exploited economically and/or sexually;  
d. children who are victims of narcotics, alcohol, psychotropic, and other addictive substance abuse;  
e. children who are victims of pornography;  
f. children with HIV/AIDS;  
g. children victims of kidnapping, sale, and/or trafficking;  
h. children victims of physical and/or psychological violence;  
i. children victims of sexual crimes;  
j. children victims of terrorism networks;  
k. children with disabilities;  
l. children victims of maltreatment and neglect;  
m. children with deviant social behavior; and  
n. children who are victims of stigmatization from labeling related to their parents' condition.

#### Article 50

1. Children in emergency situations as referred to in Article 37 letter a, consist of:  
a. children who become refugees;  
b. children victims of riots;  
c. children victims of natural disasters; and  
d. children in armed conflict situations.  
2. Special protection for children in emergency situations as referred to in paragraph (1), is implemented in accordance with the provisions of laws and regulations.

#### Article 51

1. Regional government and society provide special protection for children in conflict with the law as referred to in Article 49 letter a, which includes children in conflict with the law and children victims of criminal acts.  
2. Protection as referred to in paragraph (1) is carried out through:  
a. humane treatment with attention to needs according to their age;  
b. separation from adults;  
c. provision of legal assistance and other assistance effectively;  
d. implementation of recreational activities;  
e. freedom from torture, punishment, or other treatment that is cruel, inhumane and degrading to dignity and degree;  
f. avoidance of imposition of death penalty and/or life imprisonment;  
g. avoidance of arrest, detention or imprisonment, except as a last resort and for the shortest period of time;  
h. provision of justice before a Children's court that is objective, impartial, and in a closed hearing to the public;  
i. avoidance of publication of their identity.  
j. provision of assistance from Parents/Guardians and persons trusted by the Child;  
k. provision of social advocacy;  
l. provision of private life;  
m.provision of accessibility, especially for Children with Disabilities;  
n. provision of education;  
o. provision of health services; and  
p. provision of other rights in accordance with the provisions of laws and regulations.

#### Article 52

Special protection for children from minority and isolated groups as referred to in Article 49 letter b, is carried out through the provision of facilities and infrastructure to be able to enjoy their own culture, acknowledge and practice their own religion, and use their own language.

#### Article 53

Special Protection for Children who are exploited economically and/or sexually as referred to in Article 51 paragraph (2) letter d is carried out through:  
a. dissemination and/or socialization of provisions of laws and regulations related to the Protection of Children who are exploited economically and/or sexually;  
b. monitoring, reporting, and imposition of sanctions; and  
c. involvement of various companies, labor unions, non-governmental organizations, and the Community in the elimination of exploitation of Children economically and/or sexually.

#### Article 54

Special protection for Children who are victims of narcotics, alcohol, psychotropic, and other addictive substance abuse as referred to in Article 49 letter d and Children involved in their production and distribution is carried out through efforts of supervision, prevention, treatment, and rehabilitation.

#### Article 55

Every Person is obliged to protect Children from the influence of pornography and prevent Children's access to information containing pornographic elements.

#### Article 56

1. Special Protection for Children who are victims of pornography as referred to in Article 49 letter e is implemented through efforts of guidance, assistance, as well as social, physical and mental health recovery.  
2. Guidance, assistance, as well as social, physical and mental health recovery as referred to in paragraph (1) is implemented in accordance with the provisions of laws and regulations.

#### Article 57

Special Protection for Children with HIV/AIDS as referred to in Article 49 letter f is carried out through efforts of supervision, prevention, treatment, care, and rehabilitation.

#### Article 58

Regional Government provides special protection for children victims of trafficking as referred to in Article 37 letter e and children victims of kidnapping as referred to in Article 49 letter g which is carried out through efforts of:  
a. supervision;  
b. protection;  
c. prevention;  
d. care; and  
e. rehabilitation.

#### Article 59

Special Protection for Children with Disabilities as referred to in Article 49 letter k is carried out through efforts of:  
a. treatment of Children in a humane manner in accordance with the dignity and Rights of the Child;  
b. fulfillment of special needs;  
c. equal treatment with other Children to achieve full social integration as much as possible and individual development; and  
d. social assistance.

#### Article 60

Special Protection for Children victims of maltreatment and neglect as referred to in Article 49 letter l is carried out through efforts of supervision, prevention, care, counseling, social rehabilitation, and social assistance.

#### Article 61

Special Protection for Children with deviant social behavior as referred to in Article 49 letter m is carried out through guidance on religious values and social values, counseling, social rehabilitation, and social assistance.

#### Article 62

Special protection for Children who are victims of stigmatization from labeling related to their Parents' condition as referred to in Article 49 letter n is carried out through counseling, social rehabilitation, and social assistance.

#### Article 63

Further provisions regarding special protection for children as referred to in Article 37 through Article 62 are implemented in accordance with laws and regulations.

# CHAPTER VI

## OBLIGATIONS AND RESPONSIBILITIES

## Part One

General

#### Article 64

Obligations and responsibilities in providing protection to women and children from acts of violence and discrimination are the joint responsibility of:  
a. Regional Government;  
b. Village Government;  
c. Community; and  
d. Parents and Family.

## Part Two

Regional Government

#### Article 65

Obligations and responsibilities of Regional Government as referred to in Article 64 letter a, include:  
a. establishing policies, programs, and activities for the protection of women and children from acts of violence, exploitation, trafficking and discrimination;  
b. implementing policies for the protection of women and children from acts of violence established by the government;  
c. conducting cooperation with other parties in the implementation of protection of women and children from acts of violence exploitation, trafficking and discrimination;  
d. providing support for facilities and infrastructure for the implementation of protection of women and children from acts of violence;  
e. allocating budget for the implementation of protection of women and children from acts of violence according to regional financial capacity;  
f. fostering and supervising the implementation of protection of women and children from acts of violence, exploitation, trafficking and discrimination;  
g. providing recovery services and social reintegration;  
h. encouraging and increasing community participation; and  
i. providing human resources in the implementation of protection of women and children victims of acts of violence, exploitation, trafficking and discrimination.

#### Article 66

1. In order to carry out the obligations and responsibilities as referred to in Article 65 letter a, the Regent shall establish programs and action activities for the protection of women and children in the regional action plan as a basis for regional apparatus in implementing the protection of women and children from acts of violence, exploitation, trafficking and discrimination.
2. The regional action plan as referred to in paragraph (1), constitutes part of the Regional Medium-Term Development Plan (RPJMD).
3. Further provisions regarding the Regional Action Plan as referred to in paragraph (2) shall be regulated by Regent Regulation.

## Part Three

Village Government

#### Article 67

The obligations and responsibilities of the Village Government as referred to in Article 64 letter b, include:
a. implementing policies for the protection of women and children from acts of violence, exploitation, trafficking and discrimination established by the Regional Government;
b. conducting cooperation with other parties in the implementation of protection of women and children from acts of violence, exploitation, trafficking and discrimination;
c. providing support for facilities and infrastructure for the implementation of protection of women and children from acts of violence, exploitation, trafficking and discrimination;
d. allocating budget for the implementation of protection of women and children from acts of violence, exploitation, trafficking and discrimination in accordance with village financial capacity;
e. fostering and supervising the implementation of protection of women and children from acts of violence, exploitation, trafficking and discrimination; and
f. encouraging and increasing community participation.

## Part Four

Community

#### Article 68

1. The obligations and responsibilities of the community as referred to in Article 64 letter c, shall be carried out by participating in the protection of women and children victims of violence, exploitation, trafficking and discrimination, either individually or in groups.
2. The role of the community as referred to in paragraph (1) shall be carried out by individuals, women and child protection institutions, social welfare institutions, community organizations, educational institutions, mass media, and the business sector.
3. The role of the community in the implementation of protection of women and children as referred to in paragraph (1) shall be carried out by:
a. providing information through socialization and education regarding the protection of women and children from acts of violence, exploitation, trafficking and discrimination;
b. providing input in the formulation of policies related to the protection of women and children from acts of violence, exploitation, trafficking and discrimination;
c. reporting to the competent authorities if violations of acts of violence, exploitation, trafficking and discrimination against women and children occur;
d. playing an active role in the rehabilitation and social reintegration process for women and children victims of acts of violence, exploitation, trafficking and discrimination;
e. conducting monitoring and supervision of the Protection of Women and Children from acts of violence, exploitation, trafficking and discrimination;
f. providing facilities and infrastructure in order to support the protection of women and children from acts of violence, exploitation, trafficking and discrimination;
g. playing an active role in eliminating negative labeling of women and children victims of acts of violence, exploitation, trafficking and discrimination; and
h. providing space for women and children to participate and express opinions.

## Part Five

Parents and Family

#### Article 69

1. The obligations and responsibilities of parents and family as referred to in Article 45 letter d, are to safeguard and prevent the occurrence of all forms of violence, exploitation, trafficking and discrimination both against women and against children.
2. The obligations and responsibilities of parents towards children as referred to in paragraph (1), are to:
a. care for, maintain, educate, and protect children;
b. develop children in accordance with their abilities, talents, and interests;
c. prevent the occurrence of marriage at child age; and
d. provide character education and instillation of moral values in children.
3. Guardians and/or foster parents appointed as referred to in paragraph (3) must have the same religion as that professed by the Child.
4. Guardians and/or foster parents as referred to in paragraph (3) shall be responsible for the child and must manage the property of the child concerned for the best interests of the child.
5. Guardians and/or foster parents as referred to in paragraph (3), may represent the child to perform legal acts, both inside and outside the court for the best interests of the child.

# CHAPTER VII

## INSTITUTIONS

## Part One

#### Article 70

1. In the context of protection of women and children, the Regional Government may establish PPT.
2. The establishment and development of PPT shall be adjusted to priority needs, regional financial capacity as well as institutional and personnel capacity available in the region.
3. PPT as referred to in paragraph (1), may take the form of:
a. P2TP2A;
b. LK3;
c. KPAD; and
d. other institutions according to needs and in accordance with the provisions of laws and regulations.

#### Article 71

1. P2TP2A as referred to in Article 70 paragraph (3) letter a, shall be positioned at the regional level and sub-district level.
2. P2TP2A shall be under the coordination of OPD or other units that handle women's empowerment and child protection.
3. P2TP2A as referred to in paragraph (1) does not have the authority to formulate policies on women's empowerment and child protection.
4. P2TP2A is one form of integrated service unit, which functions as:
a. an information center for women and children;
b. a service center for women and children victims of violence; and
c. an empowerment center for women and children.

#### Article 72

1. The establishment of P2TP2A shall be stipulated by Regent Decree for the regional level and sub-district head decree for the sub-district level.
2. Membership of P2TP2A may come from elements of the Regional Government, professional organizations, academics, community leaders and other elements as needed.
3. Further provisions regarding P2TP2A as referred to in paragraph (1) shall be regulated in a Regent Regulation.

#### Article 73

1. In the context of handling psychosocial problems of victims, recovery of psychosocial conditions of victims and strengthening family resilience, LK3 may be established in the region.
2. The procedures for establishment, main duties and functions as well as service mechanisms of LK3 shall be carried out in accordance with the provisions of laws and regulations.

#### Article 74

1. In the context of providing protection and fulfillment of children's rights, KPAD may be established in the region.
2. The procedures for establishment, main duties and functions as well as service mechanisms of KPAD as referred to in paragraph (1), shall be carried out in accordance with the provisions of laws and regulations.

## Part Two

Children's Forum

#### Article 75

1. The Regional Government shall facilitate the establishment of a children's forum.
2. The children's forum as referred to in paragraph (1) constitutes a representation of children in Pesawaran Regency, both representation of children's geographical domicile, components of children's socio-cultural groups and children's educational background.
3. In every formulation of policies related to children, the Regional Government must consider and accommodate the opinions of children conveyed through the children's forum.
4. The establishment of the children's forum as referred to in paragraph (1) shall be stipulated by Regional Head Decree.
5. Sources of financing for the implementation of children's forum activities may come from:
a. contributions from members of the children's forum;
b. donations from the community/private parties that are non-binding in nature;
c. assistance from the Regional Government; and/ord. other sources of financing in accordance with applicable laws and regulations.

# CHAPTER VIII

## CHILD-FRIENDLY REGENCY

#### Article 76

1. To ensure the fulfillment of children's rights to live, grow, develop, and participate optimally in accordance with human dignity and to receive protection from violence and discrimination, concrete integrated efforts are needed among the regional government, community and business sector through the development of CFA.  
2. The implementation of Child-Friendly Regency by the Regency Government is intended to:  
a. Ensure the fulfillment of children's rights to live, grow, develop and participate optimally in accordance with human dignity, for the realization of quality, virtuous and prosperous children;  
b. Ensure the fulfillment of children's rights in creating a safe, friendly, and amicable atmosphere;  
c. Protect children from the threat of social problems in their lives;  
d. Develop children's potential, talents and creativity;  
e. Optimize the role and function of the family as the primary educational foundation for children; and  
f. Build Regency facilities and infrastructure capable of meeting children's basic needs to grow and develop optimally.

#### Article 77

The Child-Friendly Regency implemented by the Regency Government is carried out based on principles that include:  
a. Good governance, namely transparency, accountability, participation, information disclosure and rule of law.  
b. Non-discrimination, namely not differentiating ethnicity, race, religion, gender, language, political views, national origin, economic status, physical, mental or psychological condition of the child.  
c. Culture and local wisdom;  
d. Best interests of the child, namely making what is best for the child the primary consideration in every policy, program and activity; and  
e. Respect for the views of the child, namely recognizing and ensuring that every child who has the ability to express their opinion is given the opportunity to freely express their views on all matters affecting them.

## Part One

Child-Friendly

#### Article 78

1. The physical condition of an area in which there are facilities and infrastructure managed in such a way as to meet minimum requirements for the healthy and proper growth and development of children, not containing elements that endanger children.  
2. Suitability as referred to in paragraph (1) includes infrastructure such as highways, bridges, sidewalks, transportation facilities, regency parks, green environment and the availability of supporting legal instruments.  
3. Suitability as referred to in paragraph (2) is implemented at the village, sub-district and Regency levels as well as in the provision of public services, including in the education and health sectors.

## Part Two

Child-Friendly

#### Article 79

1. The non-physical condition of an area in which there are cultural values, ethics, attitudes, and community behavior that are consciously practiced or used and developed in such a way as to provide a sense of pleasure, comfort, and joy to children.  
2. Friendliness as referred to in paragraph (1) includes:  
a. The manner in which adults face and treat children such as greeting, giving salutations;  
b. Choosing and using wise words for children;  
c. The habit of praising children;  
d. Saying thank you;  
e. Being patient and not imposing one's willf. Listening to children's opinions carefully; and g. Setting examples of good and positive things.  
3. Friendliness as referred to in paragraph (2) is implemented in the Rukun Warga, Rukun Tetangga, family environment and in the provision of public services including in the education and health sectors.  
4. In realizing child-friendly conditions and situations, a social movement of child-caring communities is carried out in the form of child-friendly RW, child-friendly RT, child-friendly families, child-friendly schools, child-friendly community health centers, child-friendly polyclinics, child-friendly midwives and others.

## Part Three

Implementation of Child-Friendly Regency

#### Article 80

1. The implementation of child-friendly regency includes:  
a. Fulfillment of basic health and welfare rights;  
b. Fulfillment of the right to survival;  
c. Fulfillment of rights to growth and development;  
d. Fulfillment of the right to participate; and  
e. Fulfillment of the right to receive protection from violence and discrimination.  
2. Every person is obliged to support the Child-Friendly Regency program as referred to in paragraph (1).

# CHAPTER IX

## CHILD-FRIENDLY REGENCY TASK FORCE

#### Article 81

1. In order to ensure the effectiveness of implementing Child-Friendly Regency policies in the region, a Child-Friendly Regency Task Force is established whose membership includes Regional Apparatus Organizations, child representatives, and may involve legislative, judicial institutions, business sector, religious/community/customary leaders and the community.  
2. The Child-Friendly Regency Task Force as referred to in paragraph (1) has the principal duties of:  
a. coordinating the implementation of policies and development of Child-Friendly Regency;  
b. determining the tasks of Task Force members;  
c. conducting socialization, advocacy and communication, information and education on Child-Friendly Regency policies;  
d. collecting baseline data;  
e. conducting needs analysis based on baseline data;  
f. disseminating baseline data;  
g.determining the focus and priority of programs in realizing Child-Friendly Regency, adjusted to regional potential;  
h.preparing a 5 (five) year Regional Action Plan for Child-Friendly Regency and work mechanisms;  
i. conducting monitoring, evaluation and reporting at least once (1) a year.  
3. Membership of the Child-Friendly Regency Task Force is appointed and dismissed by the Regent.

#### Article 82

1. To assist the smooth implementation of the duties of the Child-Friendly Regency Task Force, a Secretariat is established.  
2. The Secretariat as referred to in paragraph (1) is tasked with providing technical and administrative support to the Child-Friendly Regency Task Force.  
3. The Secretariat of the Child-Friendly Regency Task Force is located at the office of the Women's Empowerment and Child Protection Agency.  
4. The establishment of the secretariat and appointment of secretariat personnel of the Child-Friendly Regency Task Force is determined by the Regional Head.

# CHAPTER X

## SPECIAL DEVELOPMENT PLANNING FORUM FOR WOMEN AND CHILDREN

#### Article 83

1. The special development planning forum for women and children is a form of annual meeting or gathering held specifically for women and children to explore various ideas and input for the fulfillment of women's and children's rights in regional development program activities.  
2. The special development planning forum for women and children is implemented before the implementation of the development planning forum.  
3. Further provisions regarding the implementation mechanism of the special development planning forum for women and children as referred to in paragraph (1), are regulated by Regent Regulation.

# CHAPTER XI

## FINANCING

#### Article 84

The costs of implementing the protection of women and children are sourced from:  
a. Regional Revenue and Expenditure Budget;  
b. Village Revenue and Expenditure Budget; and  
c. Other legitimate and non-binding sources in accordance with the provisions of laws and regulations.

# CHAPTER XII

## GUIDANCE AND SUPERVISION

#### Article 85

1. The Regional Head has the authority to conduct guidance and supervision over the Protection of Women and Children.  
2. The form of guidance as referred to in paragraph (1) may include:  
a. providing socialization to the community and children's groups regarding the concept of Child-Friendly Regency and children's rights;  
b. providing books, leaflets, brochures regarding child protection, reproductive health, dangers of Sexually Transmitted Diseases and Narcotics and other Addictive Substances (NAPZA) and distributing them to the community;  
c. providing training related to child care/education, counseling principles, basic psychology to communities participating in efforts to organize early childhood education, development of Toddler Family Development Groups (BKB), organizing integrated child protection services and other similar activities related to fulfilling children's needs;  
d. facilitating the organization of children's forums and components of children's socio-cultural groups;  
e. facilitating the growth and development of centers or platforms for information and counseling services for adolescents;  
f. providing awards to the community, both individuals and groups or community organizations deemed to have carried out child protection efforts well.  
3. Supervision as referred to in paragraph (1) may include monitoring and evaluation activities on the Protection of Women and Children implemented by child protection organizers.  
4. The authority as referred to in paragraph (1) may be delegated to the Head of Women's Empowerment and Family Planning and/or other officials within the Regional Government in accordance with their respective duties and functions.

# ELUCIDATION OF REGIONAL REGULATION OF PESAWARAN REGENCY

NUMBER 1 OF 2019 CONCERNING THE PROTECTION OF WOMEN AND CHILDREN

##### I. GENERAL

That human beings are endowed by God Almighty with reason and conscience which gives them the ability to distinguish between good and bad which will guide and direct attitudes and behavior in living their lives. With their reason and conscience, humans have the freedom to decide for themselves their behavior or actions. In addition, to balance that freedom, humans have the ability to be responsible for all actions they take.  

Those basic freedoms and basic rights are what are called human rights which are inherently attached to humans naturally as a gift from God Almighty. These rights cannot be denied. Denial of these rights means denying human dignity. Therefore, the state, government, or any organization bears the obligation to recognize and protect human rights for every human being without exception. This means that human rights must always be the starting point and goal in the administration of community, national and state life.  

The State upholds human rights, including Children's rights, which is marked by the guarantee of protection and fulfillment of Children's Rights in the 1945 Constitution of the Republic of Indonesia and several provisions of laws and regulations both national and international in nature. This guarantee is strengthened through the ratification of international conventions on Children's Rights, namely the ratification of the Convention on the Rights of the Child through Presidential Decree Number 36 of 1990 concerning Ratification of the Convention On The Rights Of The Child.  

The State, Government, Regional Government, Community, Family and Parents are obliged to provide protection and ensure the fulfillment of Children's rights in accordance with their duties and responsibilities. Protection of Children carried out so far has not provided guarantees for Children to receive treatment and opportunities in accordance with their needs in various fields of life, so that in implementing efforts to protect Children's Rights by the Government must be based on the principles of human rights, namely respect, fulfillment, and protection of Children's Rights.
