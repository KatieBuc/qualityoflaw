# CHAPTER I

GENERAL PROVISIONS

#### Article 1

In this Regional Regulation, what is meant by:

1. Region is the Region of East Barito Regency.

2. Regional Government is the Regent and Regional Apparatus as elements of Regional Government Administration.

3. Regent is the Regent of East Barito.

4. Regional House of Representatives hereinafter abbreviated as DPRD is the Regional House of Representatives Institution which is positioned as an element of Government administration.

5. Regional Apparatus of East Barito Regency is an auxiliary element of the Regent and Regional House of Representatives of East Barito Regency in the Administration of government affairs which are the authority of East Barito Regency.

6. Agency is a Regional Apparatus that manages government affairs in the field of women and children.

7. Technical Service Unit is a Regional Technical Service Unit that manages affairs of protection of women and children.

8. Child is a person who has not reached 18 years of age, including a child who is still in the womb.

9. Woman is an adult human being of female sex and a person who is recognized by law as a woman.

10. Prevention is an activity carried out to prevent the occurrence of acts of violence against women and children.

11. Protection is all service actions to guarantee and protect the rights of victims of acts of violence organized by the Integrated Service Center.

12. Violence is any act that results in or causes misery and suffering whether physical, sexual, psychological including neglect, threats of certain actions, coercion or arbitrary deprivation of freedom, whether occurring in public or in private life.

13. Physical violence is any act that results in pain, injury, wounds or disability to a person's body, miscarriage or fainting and/or causes death.

14. Psychological violence is an act that results in fear, loss of self-confidence, loss of ability to act, feelings of helplessness, and/or severe psychological suffering to a person.

15. Sexual violence is any act in the form of sexual harassment, forced sexual intercourse, whether unnatural or unwanted with another person for commercial purposes and/or certain purposes.

16. Victim is a woman and child who experiences misery and or suffering either directly or indirectly as a result of violence.

17. Victim of acts of violence is an individual or group of people who experience physical, mental or emotional suffering, economic loss, or experience neglect, reduction or deprivation of their basic rights, as a result of violations of human rights (HAM), or criminal acts as well as acts of violence committed either by state apparatus or by the state or regional government apparatus or by individuals.

18. Service is immediate activities and actions carried out by Professional personnel in accordance with their respective professions in the form of counseling, therapy and advocacy for the strengthening and recovery of victims of violence.

19. Assistance is activities and actions carried out by assistants during the service process.

20. Assistant is a person or representative from an institution who has the expertise to provide assistance.

21. Repatriation is an effort to return victims of acts of violence to their family, substitute family, or community that can provide protection and fulfillment of their needs.

22. Rehabilitation is the recovery of victims from psychosocial disorders and the restoration of normal social functioning, both in the family and in society.

23. Social Reintegration is an effort to reunite victims with family, community, institutions, or other social environments that can provide protection.

24. Institution is an agency/service/body/office within the scope of regional government and/or non-governmental organization (NGO) that provides assistance.

25. Regional Action Plan hereinafter abbreviated as RAD is a foundation and guideline for related agencies, vertical agencies, and the community, in order to carry out activities for the implementation of prevention and protection of women and children victims of acts of violence.

26. Safe House is a temporary residence, provided to give protection to victims in accordance with predetermined standards.

27. Community is individuals, families, groups, social organizations and/or community organizations.

28. Family is a person who has a blood relationship in a straight line upward or downward and collateral line up to the third degree, or who has a marital relationship, or a person who is a dependent of a woman and/or child.

29. Parents are biological father and/or mother, or stepfather and/or stepmother, or adoptive father and/or mother.

30. Regional Revenue and Expenditure Budget hereinafter abbreviated as APBD is the annual financial plan of the Regional Government which is discussed and approved jointly by the Regional Government and DPRD and established by Regional Regulation.

31. Gender Equality is the view that all people should receive equal treatment and not be discriminated against based on their gender identity.

32. Medicolegal Service is a form of health service carried out by medical personnel using medical science and technology based on the authority possessed for legal purposes and to implement applicable regulations.

# CHAPTER II

PRINCIPLES AND OBJECTIVES

#### Article 2

(1) The implementation of protection of women and children is carried out based on the principles of:

a. respect for the rights of women and children;

b. justice and gender equality;

c. non-discrimination in the empowerment of women and children;

d. the best interests of women and children; and

e. restoration of social and economic rights for women and children who experience acts of violence or neglect.

(2) The objectives of implementing protection of women and children include:

a. preventing acts of violence or neglect against women and children including trafficking in persons;

b. eliminating all forms of violence and exploitation against women and children:

c. protecting and providing a sense of security for women and children;

d. providing services to women and children victims of acts of violence, reporters, and witnesses;

e. facilitating and mediating domestic disputes to realize the integrity of a harmonious and prosperous household; and

f. seeking compensation/compensation for losses suffered by victims from perpetrators of violence.

# CHAPTER III

SCOPE

#### Article 3

(1) Protection of women and children in the region includes prevention, services, and empowerment of victims of physical violence, psychological violence, sexual violence, exploitation, trafficking in persons, and domestic neglect including child deviant behavior.

(2) The implementation of protection of women and children from acts of violence is a joint obligation and responsibility between the Regional Government, community, family, and parents.

# CHAPTER IV

RIGHTS OF WOMEN AND CHILDREN VICTIMS OF VIOLENCE

#### Article 4

(1) Every woman and child who becomes a victim of physical violence, psychological violence, exploitation or trafficking in persons is entitled to receive protection and services.

(2) Protection, assistance, and services to victims as referred to in paragraph (1) include:

a. to have their dignity respected as human beings;

b. complaints or requests for protection from the person concerned and/or other parties;

c. confidential handling or placement of victims in safe locations and houses whose whereabouts are kept secret to avoid intimidation and threats;

d. medical services/health treatment in the form of care and recovery of injuries or physical condition of victims by medical personnel and paramedics;

e. medicolegal services for legal evidence;

f. protection by law enforcement officers;

g. provision of information regarding legal rights and obligations to victims or guardians of victims as well as identification of incidents;

h. continuous handling up to the rehabilitation stage;

i. recovery of physical, psychological and sexual health according to the suffering experienced by victims of violence;

j. psychological assistance to restore the traumatic condition of victims and restore the self-confidence of victims;

k. assistance for legal processes and obtaining compensation or compensation rights;

l. spiritual guidance services;

m. determining their own decisions;

n. assistance by social workers and legal aid at every level of the examination process in accordance with the provisions of laws and regulations;

i. guarantee of rights related to status as a family member and community member (social reintegration); and

j. economic independence services in the form of skills training and providing economic access so that victims can be independent.

(3) In the event that the victim is a child, in addition to obtaining the rights as referred to in paragraph (2), they also receive special rights including:

a. the right to respect and full use for survival, growth and development;

b. the right to basic services;

c. the right to equal protection;

d. the right to be free from various stigma;

e. the right to obtain freedom; and

f. the right to obtain compensation from the perpetrator.

# CHAPTER V

OBLIGATIONS AND RESPONSIBILITIES

#### Article 5

Obligations and responsibilities in providing protection to women and children from acts of violence are the shared responsibility of:

a. regional government;

b. community;

c. institutions;

d. family; and

e. parents.

#### Article 6

The obligations and responsibilities of the Regional Government as referred to in Article 5 letter a, include:

a. implementing the obligations of protection of women and children from acts of violence established by the government;

b. establishing policies, programs and activities for the protection of women and children from acts of violence;

c. conducting cooperation in the implementation of protection of women and children from acts of violence;

d. providing support for facilities and infrastructure for the implementation of protection of women and children from acts of violence;

e. providing Safe Houses for women and children victims of violence;

f. allocating budget for the implementation of protection of women and children from acts of violence according to regional financial capacity; and

g. fostering and supervising the implementation of protection of women and children from acts of violence;

h. establishing regional technical implementing units for the protection of women and children based on the provisions of laws and regulations.

#### Article 7

(1) the obligations and responsibilities of the community as referred to in Article 5 letter b, are implemented in the form of community participation.

(2) Forms of community participation as referred to in paragraph (1), include:

a. preventing the occurrence of acts of violence against women and children;

b. providing protection to victims;

c. providing emergency assistance;

d. providing information and/or reporting acts of violence against women and children to law enforcement or authorized parties, and/or related Agencies through UPTD PPA; and

e. participating in the handling of victims of acts of violence.

(3) Forms of community participation as referred to in paragraph (2), are implemented responsibly in accordance with the provisions of Laws and Regulations.

#### Article 8

The obligations of family and/or parents as referred to in Article 5 letter c and letter d, who legally have full responsibility to prevent all forms of violence and protect women and children as family members.

# CHAPTER VI

INSTITUTIONAL ARRANGEMENTS

#### Article 9

(1) The Regional Government is obliged to establish a Technical Service Unit for the protection of women and children.

(2) The Technical Service Unit as referred to in paragraph (1) is established based on the provisions of laws and regulations.

(3) The Technical Service Unit as referred to in paragraph (1) is established after this Regional Regulation comes into force and is regulated by Regent's regulation.

# CHAPTER VII

PREVENTION, SERVICES AND EMPOWERMENT

## Part One

General

#### Article 10

The Regional Government through the Agency or technical service unit and PD, implements prevention and service efforts for victims of violence, exploitation and neglect of Children and Women up to the empowerment of victims to be able to resume their lives in accordance with the dignity they possess.

## Part Two

Prevention of Acts of Violence, Exploitation and Neglect of Women and Children

#### Article 11

(1) Forms of prevention as referred to in Article 10 are carried out through:

a. socialization activities of laws and regulations related to the protection of Children and Women to the wider community in the region;

b. dissemination activities of information on the protection of women and children to be known by everyone in the region through the distribution of brochures, leaflets, pamphlets or notice boards so that incidents of violence, exploitation and neglect can be reported as soon as possible and can be handled before reaching the worst condition;

c. training for members who are able to disseminate norms of prohibitions and sanctions to the community;

d. opening of public complaint channels by the Regional Government or Agency through the Technical Service Unit in each area through community networks;

e. strengthening coordination channels with Sub-district, Village/Sub-district Government;

f. immediate handling of community complaints throughout the region and securing subjects of acts of violence, exploitation, trafficking in persons or neglect; and/or

g. counseling/socialization in the form of raising awareness of the wider community down to the smallest community groups (families), especially parents to protect women and children.

(2) The Regional Government through the Agency/UPTD conducts data collection on areas of violence based on the incidence rate and conducts research and studies on the level of vulnerability of areas to find solutions for reduction up to cessation of acts of violence, exploitation and neglect in vulnerable point areas.

## Part Three

Services for Women and Children Who Experience Acts of Violence, Exploitation and Neglect

#### Article 12

(1) Forms of services provided to women and children victims of acts of violence, exploitation, trafficking in persons or neglect are implemented in accordance with the rights of victims as referred to in Article 4 paragraph (2) and paragraph (3).

(2) To support the operations of UPTD, the Regent orders the Agency related to services as referred to in paragraph (1) in accordance with minimum service standards established by the government.

#### Article 13

The Regional Government through the Agency/UPTD is obliged to prepare standard operating procedures for Assistance to victims of violence, exploitation, trafficking or neglect at every stage of fulfilling the rights of victims.

#### Article 14

The provision of services and assistance to victims shall be carried out with the principles of:

a. non-discrimination;

b. fast, safe, and empathetic;

c. coordination between government agencies;

d. guarantee of confidentiality;

e. easily accessible; and

f. free of charge.

#### Article 15

Forms of services for the rights of women and children from acts of violence include:

a. complaint, consultation, and counseling services;

b. assistance services;

c. health services;

d. social rehabilitation services;

e. legal services and/or legal aid; and

f. repatriation and social reintegration services.

## Part Four

Empowerment of Victims of Violence

#### Article 16

(1) The Regional Government through the Agency/UPTD is obliged to carry out economic empowerment for victims of violence, exploitation and/or trafficking or neglect.

(2) Forms of empowerment of victims of violence as referred to in paragraph (1), include:

a. procuring the necessary needs for entrepreneurship training for victims of violence, in order to increase business knowledge, attitudes, and skills;

b. facilitating the implementation of various job training and skills training;

c. providing assistance in developing productive economic enterprises;

d. exploring cooperation with small, medium and large enterprises, as well as financial institutions to develop the businesses of victims of violence;

e. seeking the provision of capital for victims of violence; and

f. expanding access to information and promoting the products of victims of violence.

## CHAPTER VIII

COOPERATION AND PARTNERSHIP

## Part One

Cooperation

#### Article 17

(1) Cooperation is carried out to strengthen the role of the Agency/UPTD in achieving the objectives of protection of women and children in the regional area.

(2) Cooperation is carried out with:

a. the government;

b. the provincial government;

c. other regency/city governments;

d. non-governmental institutions; and

e. international institutions whose existence is recognized.

(3) Cooperation as referred to in paragraph (2), includes:

a. exchange of data and information;

b. rehabilitation of victims of violence.

c. repatriation and social reintegration; and

d. provision of evidence and witnesses, and followed up based on the provisions of laws and regulations.

(4) Cooperation as referred to in paragraph (1) and paragraph (2), shall be set forth in the form of a joint agreement.

## Part Two

Partnership

#### Article 18

(1) The Regional Government establishes partnerships with the business community to support the implementation of protection for women and children in the regional area.

(2) Partnership as referred to in paragraph (1), is carried out through:

a. notification of information on employment opportunities for women victims of violence;

b. education and training for women victims of violence;

c. educational assistance for women and children victims of violence who have been withdrawn from their education; and

d. fostering and increasing the economic independence of women victims of violence.

(3) Partnership as referred to in paragraph (1) and paragraph (2), shall be set forth in the form of a cooperation agreement.

## CHAPTER IX

COMMUNITY PARTICIPATION

#### Article 19

(1) In providing protection for women and children, the community may:

a. form family partners at the sub-district/village level by the community;

b. form women and children protection units within community organizations;

c. independently conduct socialization of the rights of women and children;

d. provide first aid to victims; and;

e. report to the competent authority if violence against victims occurs in their environment.

(2) Community participation as referred to in paragraph (1) shall be carried out by individuals, social community institutions, non-governmental organizations, educational institutions, religious institutions, the private sector, and mass media.

## CHAPTER X

REGIONAL ACTION PLAN

#### Article 20

(1) In order to carry out obligations and responsibilities, the regional government establishes action programs and activities for the protection of women and children in 1 (one) RAD as the basis for implementing protection of women and children from acts of violence.

(2) The RAD as referred to in paragraph (1), is part of the Regional Medium-Term Development Plan (RPJMD).

(3) Further provisions regarding the RAD as referred to in paragraph (1), shall be regulated by Regent Regulation.

## CHAPTER XI

GUIDANCE AND SUPERVISION

#### Article 21

(1) The Regent shall conduct guidance and supervision of the implementation of protection of women and children from acts of violence.

(2) The Regent may appoint the Head of the Agency to conduct guidance and supervision.

#### Article 22

The community may conduct supervision over the implementation of protection of women and children in the regional area through a mechanism for conveying aspirations to the Regent and/or DPRD.

## CHAPTER XII

INVESTIGATION

#### Article 23

(1) In addition to general investigation officials, investigation of criminal acts of violence against women and children in this Regional Regulation may also be carried out by Civil Servant Investigators whose appointment is in accordance with laws and regulations.

(2) Civil Servant Investigators as referred to in paragraph (1), are authorized to:

a. receive reports or complaints from someone about the existence of criminal acts of violence against women and children;

b. take first action at that time at the scene and conduct an examination;

c. interrogate a suspect and examine the suspect's identification;

d. seize objects and/or documents;

e. take fingerprints and photograph a person;

f. summon persons to be heard and examined as suspects or witnesses;

g. bring in experts needed in connection with the examination of the case;

h. terminate the investigation after receiving instructions from the General Investigator that there is insufficient evidence, or the incident does not constitute a criminal act and subsequently through the General Investigator notify this matter to the public prosecutor, the suspect or their family; and/or

i. take other actions necessary for the smooth investigation of criminal acts of violence against women and children, which according to law can be accounted for.

(3) The Investigator as referred to in paragraph (2), shall notify the commencement of the investigation and submit the results of the investigation to the public prosecutor through the Investigator Official of the Police of the Republic of Indonesia, in accordance with the provisions stipulated in the Criminal Procedure Code.

# CHAPTER XIII

SANCTION PROVISIONS

#### Article 24

(1) Any person who intentionally does not provide protection for women and children so as to cause acts of violence, allows acts of violence to occur, and/or does not report and does not provide protection to victims, shall be subject to sanctions as stipulated in the Laws and Regulations.

(2) If an official appointed to provide protection does not carry out their obligations and responsibilities, they shall be subject to sanctions in accordance with the provisions of Laws and Regulations.

(3) Officials who carry out service and protection duties for Children and Women from acts of violence, who violate service principles, may be subject to sanctions in accordance with the internal organizational mechanism.

# CHAPTER XIV

OTHER PROVISIONS

#### Article 25

(1) The handling of legal cases in the investigation process by law enforcement officials must be handled by special investigators who have been appointed according to their competence by law enforcement institutions for the field of protection of women and children.

(2) Special investigators as referred to in paragraph (1) are:

a. in cases of investigation for women or girls who are victims of violence must be handled by female investigators;

b. the implementation process as referred to in point a must be in a closed room attended only by the victim's parents or guardian along with the advocacy and psychology team from P3A who have the same gender;

c. in cases of investigation for boys who are victims of violence may be conducted by male or female investigators, preferably of the same gender and implemented as referred to in point b; and

d. The trial process is not open to the public.

#### Article 26

In the case of trial proceedings related to cases of violence against children, the presence of the child does not necessarily have to be presented at the trial, except in circumstances that are very compelling while still separating the meeting between the child and the perpetrator in trial processes at different times.

#### Article 27

In the case that the perpetrator of a criminal act is a child, the following matters apply:

a. the legal process is carried out humanely by viewing the child as still having hope for their future;

b. The investigator must be a person who has competence in special investigation for child handling cases;

c. The investigation is conducted in a closed room and attended only by the advocacy team and the child's parents/guardian;

d. in the investigation process, the child is not placed in general detention, but rather entrusted to their family with supervision and custody;

e. the trial process is carried out specifically in a juvenile court room; and

f. the sentencing process for children is placed in a special juvenile correctional institution, with a transition period that may be accompanied by their parents.

#### Article 28

News media are not permitted to expose news coverage for cases concerning child victims of sexual violence in order to protect the child's identity and their hope for life in the future unless the victim as a result of such violence loses their life.

# CHAPTER XV

FUNDING

#### Article 29

Budget allocation for protection of women and children who are victims of violence carried out by the Regional Government is sourced from the Regional Revenue and Expenditure Budget, and/or other lawful sources in accordance with the provisions of laws and regulations.

#### I. GENERAL

The role of women is very important because they are the mothers of the nation who are the pillars of the family to build the future of human resources in various fields including family economic empowerment, likewise with children who are the nation's generation who will play a role in filling development in the future.

Children and Women must receive protection from violent treatment, exploitation, human trafficking and neglect by responsible parties. Protection is intended so that a peaceful and prosperous atmosphere is created for all levels of society in fostering life and togetherness within the regional territory.

Violence cannot be avoided just like that, but must be prevented as early as possible from spreading to a level that is detrimental to all parties. Protection efforts are prioritized on prevention efforts and minimizing incidents.

Acts of violence against Children and Women constitute violations of human rights so that their dignity and honor need to be protected and their right to life guaranteed in accordance with their nature and disposition without discrimination. So far, laws and regulations governing the protection of Children and Women who are victims of violence have not regulated protection efforts in the Region so that institutional and regulatory support is needed that can guarantee its implementation. Regional Regulations regulate protection efforts for victims, particularly in terms of prevention, services and empowerment of Children and Women who are victims of violence in Kotabaru Regency. As one of the efforts to protect Children and Women, it is carried out through guidance, training and assistance to victims and families of victims of violence. Economic empowerment is also carried out for the prevention of women as victims, which can be done by growing and developing micro and small enterprises in accordance with local potential. To grow micro and small enterprises, it is necessary to increase the capacity and capability of women through assistance and guidance so that women are motivated to start and develop their businesses.

#### II. ARTICLE BY ARTICLE

Article 1

Sufficiently clear.

Article 2 Paragraph (1)

Letter a

Respect for the rights of Children and Women constitutes respect for the fulfillment of basic rights for women and children.

Letter b

What is meant by "justice and gender equality" is a condition where every person, both men and women, is treated equally and obtains equal opportunities to gain access, as well as welfare; Gender equality is the equality of rights, opportunities, benefits and decision-making between women and men including in entering employment opportunities both in the formal and informal sectors.

Letter c

What is meant by "non-discrimination" is an attitude and treatment toward victims by not making distinctions on the basis of age, sex, race, ethnicity, religion and between groups.

Letter d

What is meant by "the best interests of the victim" is all best actions concerning victims carried out by the Government, society, legislative bodies and judicial bodies, then the best interests of the victim must be the primary consideration.

Letter e

What is meant by socio-economic rights recovery is increasing understanding, awareness, responsibility, commitment, participation, capability and independence for the purpose of economic improvement and quality of women or target groups so as to avoid problems.

Paragraph (2)

Sufficiently clear.

Article 3

Paragraph (1)

What is meant by "physical violence" is any act that results in pain, injury, wounds or disability to a person's body, miscarriage, fainting and or causes death

What is meant by "psychological violence" is an act that results in fear, loss of self-confidence, loss of ability to act, feelings of helplessness and or severe psychological suffering to a person

What is meant by "sexual violence" is any act in the form of sexual harassment, forced sexual intercourse, forced sexual intercourse in an unnatural or unwanted manner, forced sexual intercourse with others for commercial purposes and or certain purposes

What is meant by "exploitation" includes:

a. actions that exploit children economically or sexually with the intention of benefiting oneself or others;

b. actions with or without the victim's consent which include but are not limited to prostitution, forced labor or services, slavery or similar practices, oppression, extortion, exploitation of physical, sexual, reproductive organs, or unlawfully removing or transplanting organs and/or body tissues or exploiting the labor or ability of a person by another party to obtain benefits both material and immaterial.

c. all forms of exploitation of sexual organs or other body organs of victims to obtain benefits, including but not limited to all prostitution or indecency activities.

What is meant by "trafficking in persons" is the act of recruitment, transportation, harboring, sending, transfer, or receipt of a person by threat of violence, use of violence, abduction, confinement, forgery, fraud, abuse of power or vulnerable position, debt bondage or giving payment or benefits, so as to obtain consent from the person holding control over that other person, whether carried out within the country or between countries, for the purpose of exploitation or resulting in the person being exploited.

What is meant by "Household neglect" is:

a. actions that result in the unfulfillment of children's needs properly, whether physical, mental, spiritual or social;

b. actions of deliberately neglecting to maintain, care for, or look after children as they should be;

c. actions that neglect people within their household scope, whereas according to the law applicable to them or because of an agreement or contract they are obliged to provide livelihood, care, or maintenance to that person;

d. actions that result in economic dependence by limiting and/or prohibiting proper work inside or outside the home so that the victim is under the control of that person.
