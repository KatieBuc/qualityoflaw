# AN ACT

AN ACT

Adding a new Title 53 of the Pohnpei Code relative to Domestic Issues and inserting a new Chapter 1 therein relating to Domestic Violence; amending 59 PC 4- 101 of the Pohnpei Code to provide consistency therewith; and for other purposes.

BE IT ENACTED BY THE POHNPEI LEGISLATURE:

### Section 1

The Pohnpei Code is hereby amended by adding a new 2 Title 53 and inserting a new Chapter 1 therein to read as follows:

"TITLE 53

DOMESTIC ISSUES

## CHAPTER 1

DOMESTIC VIOLENCE

#### §1

101. Public policy. The government of the state of Pohnpei recognizes that families form the core of our existence as a people, and they serve as the foundation of the structures of our communities. Acceptance of family values and recognition of the obligations of family members to honor and respect each other: lay at the very heart of our Constitution and within the very soul of our traditions. It is therefore declared to be the public policy of the state of Pohnpei to preserve and promote harmonious relationships in domestic affairs and to prevent the perpetration of acts of violence within the families of Pohnpei State. In support of this public policy this chapter has been enacted and shall be known and may be cited as the "Domestic Violence Act of 2017".

#### §1

102. Definitions. As used in this chapter:

(1) "Attempt" has the same meaning as that term is utilized by the courts under Title 61 of the Code, as amended.
(2) "Bodily injury" has the same meaning as that utilized by the courts under Title 61 of the Code, as amended; PROVIDED that for purposes of this chapter the term "bodily

injury" shall include "bodily harm" as that term is utilized by the courts under Title 61 of the Code, as amended.

(3) "Discipline" means training that corrects, molds, or perfects mental faculties and/or moral character. For purposes of this chapter, discipline shall be that which is acceptable for family discipline purposes within the customs and traditions of Pohnpei State and in accordance with Article 5 of the Pohnpei Constitution.

(4) "Diversion" means the judicial process by which a person is diverted from the criminal proceedings and referred for an education and treatment program directed specifically to the violent conduct of the defendant.

(5) "Family or household members" include:

(a) Adults or minors who are current or former spouses:

(b) Adults or minors who live together or who have lived together within the past year;

(c) Adults or minors who are related by blood or adoption:

(d) Persons who have a child in common; and
(e) Minor children of a person in a relationship

described in Paragraphs (a) through (d) above.

(6) "Serious psychological injury" means

psychological or emotional damage that requires protracted psychological treatment or is characterized by extreme

behavioral changes or severe physical symptoms.

(7) "Domestic violence" means any offense listed in Chapter 5 of Title 61 of this Code, captioned "Offenses Against the Person" or Chapter 7 of Title 61 of this Code, captioned "Offenses Against the Family", committed by a person against a family or household member as defined by this section, or the occurrence of an offense of one or more of the following acts by a person against a family or household member as defined by this section, but does not include discipline as described by Subsection (3) of this section or acts of self-defense or defense of others:

(a) Psychological abuse of a family or household member;
(b) Harassment of a family or household member;
(c) Intimidation of a family or household member;
(d) Stalking of a family or household member;
(e) Restriction of movement or confinement by force of a family or household member; and

(f) Deprivation of economic benefits of a spouse.
(8) "Harassment" means the systematic and/or continued unwanted and annoying actions of a person, including threats and demands upon a family or household member.

(9) "Intimidation" means the act of a person to make another person fearful or to put into fear. Generally, proof of actual fear is not required in order to establish intimidation. It

may be inferred from conduct, words, or circumstances reasonably calculated to produce fear.

(10) "Police officer" means a law enforcement officer within the Department of Public Safety, or of any local jurisdiction of the state of Pohnpei, which local jurisdiction is acting under a valid law enforcement agreement with the state of Pohnpei for the enforcement of state laws.

(11) "Prosecuting attorney" as used in this chapter means the Attorney General of Pohnpei and those persons employed by the Attorney General's Office specifically designated by the Attorney General.

(12) "Psychological abuse" means, for purposes of this chapter, a course of conduct on the part of one member of the family or household toward another member of the family or household that can endanger the mental and physical health and efficiency of the other person to such an extent as to render continuance of the family or household relation intolerable.

(13) "Restriction of movement or confinement by force" means the act of a person who knowingly or intentionally:

(a) Confines another person without the other person's consent; or

(b) Removes another person, by fraud, enticement, force, or threat of force, from one place to another.

(4) "Stalking" means the act of a person who intentionally and repeatedly follows or harasses another person

and who makes a credible threat, either expressed or implied, with the intent to place that person in reasonable fear of death or serious bodily harm.

(15) "Victim" means any natural person against whom a crime, as defined under the laws of Pohnpei, has been committed or attempted to be committed.

(16) "Witness" means any natural person:

(a) Having knowledge of the existence or nonexistence of facts relating to any crime; or

(b) Whose declaration under oath is received or has been received as evidence for any purpose; or

(c) Who has reported any crime to any police officer; or

(d) Who has been served with a subpoena issued under the authority of the Pohnpei Supreme Court; or

(e) Who would be believed by any reasonable person to be an individual described in Paragraphs (a) through (d), above, inclusive.

(\S 1 - 103) . Domestic violence offenses.

(1) Any offense listed in Chapter 5 of Title 61 of this Code, captioned "Offenses Against the Person" and any offense listed in Chapter 7 of Title 61 of the Code, captioned "Offenses Against the Family" committed by a person against a family or household member as defined by (\S 1 - 102) of this chapter of the Code shall be deemed to be an act of domestic violence and

shall be subject to:

(a) The special provisions pertaining to enforcement, prosecution, sentencing, suspension and probation;

(b) The special provisions pertaining to judicial procedures and protective orders;

(c) The entitlement provisions for victim's and witness' rights and privileges; and

(d) The eligibility provisions for defendant diversions, education programs and counseling set forth in this chapter.

(2) In addition to the domestic violence offenses referred to in Subsection (1) of this section, any person who intentionally, knowingly, or recklessly commits an act of domestic violence, specifically listed in Paragraphs (a) through (f) of §1-102(8) of this chapter of the Code, is guilty of a misdemeanor offense of domestic violence and upon conviction thereof shall be imprisoned for not more than one year, or fined not more than $1000, or both such fine and imprisonment.

(3) Any person who intentionally, knowingly, or recklessly commits three or more acts of domestic violence under this section, inclusive of Title 61, Chapter 5 and Title 61, Chapter 7 offenses referred to in Subsection (1) of this section and offenses referred to in Subsection (2) of this section, within sever years of the first conviction under this section must, upon conviction thereof, be imprisoned for the third or subsequent

offense, and may also be fined for that offense.

(\S 1 - 104) Powers and duties of police officers.

(1) If a police officer has reasonable cause to believe that a person has committed a crime involving domestic violence, the police officer shall arrest the person.

(2) If a police officer receives complaints of domestic violence from two or more opposing persons, the officer shall evaluate each complaint separately to determine who was the primary aggressor. If the officer determines that one person was the primary aggressor, the officer need not arrest the other person believed to have committed domestic violence but the police officer shall document to the best of his or her ability the evidence concerning the actions of each participant in the incident.

(3) In determining whether a person is the primary aggressor, the officer shall consider:

(a) Prior complaints of domestic violence;
(b) The relative severity of the injuries inflicted on each person;

(c) The likelihood of future injury to each person;
(d) Whether one of the persons acted in self-defense or defense of the others;

(e) The use or threatened use of a weapon; and
(f) The use or threatened use of physical force.

(4) A police officer shall not:

(a) Threaten, suggest, or otherwise indicate the possible arrest of all parties to discourage requests for intervention by police officers by any party; or

(b) Base the decision to arrest or not to arrest on:

(i) The specific consent or request of the victim; or (ii) The officer's perception of the willingness of a victim of or witness to the domestic violence to testify or otherwise participate in a judicial proceeding.

(5) In addition to any other report required, a police officer who does not make an arrest after investigating a complaint of domestic violence or who arrests two or more persons for a crime involving domestic violence must submit a written report setting forth the grounds for not arresting or for arresting both parties.

#### §1

105. Authority of police officer to seize weapons. For a crime involving domestic violence, a police officer:

(1) Shall, incident to an arrest, seize all weapons that are alleged to have been involved or threatened to be used in the commission of a crime.

(2) May seize a weapon that is in the plain view of the officer or was discovered pursuant to consensual search, as necessary for the protection of the officer or other persons.

#### §1

106. Charge considerations.

(1) In determining whether any felony charge filed

pursuant to this chapter should be reduced to a misdemeanor, the court shall consider the following factors, among others:

(a) The extent or seriousness of the victim's injuries;
(b) The defendant's history of violence against the

same victim whether charged or uncharged;

(c) The use of a gun or other weapon by the defendant;

(d) The defendant's prior criminal history;

(e) The victim's attitude and conduct regarding the

incident;

(f) The involvement of alcohol or other controlled substance, and the defendant's history of substance abuse as reflected in the defendant's criminal history and other sources; and

(g) The defendant's history of and amenability to counseling.

(2) If the court, after hearing, finds substantial evidence that a victim suffered serious bodily injury, no felony charge filed under this section shall be reduced to a misdemeanor unless the court finds that due to unusual circumstances a reduction of the charge is manifestly in the interest of justice.

(3) The fact that an alleged criminal act involved domestic violence as defined in §1-102 of this chapter of the Code shall not preclude the prosecuting attorney from charging and prosecuting the defendant for any other violations of law.

#### §1

107. Spousal privileges inapplicable. Notwithstanding any

other provision of law, the following evidentiary privileges do not apply in any criminal proceeding in which a spouse or other family or household member is the victim of an alleged crime involving domestic violence perpetrated by the other spouse:

(1) The privilege not to testify against one's spouse; and
(2) The privilege for confidential marital communication.

#### §1

108. Probation; suspension of sentence.

(1) In any case in which a person is convicted of violating this chapter and probation is granted, the court shall require participation in an education and treatment program if one should be available.

(2) Subject to the provisions of Subsection (3) of this section, if a person has previously been convicted of a violation of this chapter within seven years of the date of the conviction for the violation of this chapter that is presently before the court for sentencing, it shall be a condition of any probation or suspended sentence for the conviction then before the court that he or she be punished by imprisonment for not less than ten days, and that he or she participates in an education and treatment program, for no less than one year, and that he or she successfully completes said education and treatment program, as designated by the court, if one should be available. However, except as provided in §1-103(3) of this chapter of the Code, the court, upon a showing of good cause, may find that the minimum imprisonment, or the participation in an education

and treatment program, or both the minimum imprisonment and participation in an education and treatment program, as required by this subsection, shall not be imposed and may grant probation or the suspension of the imposition of a sentence.

(3) If a person has previously been convicted of two or more violations of this chapter within seven years of the date of the conviction for the violation of this chapter that is presently before the court for sentencing, it shall be a condition of any probation or suspended sentence for the conviction of the violation presently before the court for sentencing that he or she be punished by imprisonment for not less than 30 days and that he or she participates in an education and treatment program for no less than one year, and that he or she successfully completes said education and treatment program, as designated by the court, if one should be available. However, except as provided in §1-103(3) of this chapter of the Code, the court, upon a showing of good cause, may find that the minimum imprisonment, or the participation in an education and treatment program, or both the minimum imprisonment and participation in an education and treatment program, as required by this subsection, shall not be imposed and may grant probation or the suspension of the imposition of a sentence.

#### §1

109. Protective orders: eligible petitioners; relief.

(1) A person who is or has been a victim of a crime involving domestic violence may file a petition in the court for

a protective order against a family or household member. A parent, guardian, or other representative appointed by the court under this section may file a petition for a protective order on behalf of a minor. The court may appoint a guardian ad litem or attorney to represent the minor.

(2) When a petition for a protective order is filed, the court shall schedule a hearing and provide at least 5 days' notice to the respondent of the hearing and of the respondent's right to appear and be heard, either in person or by an attorney.

(3) Proceedings for a protective order shall be conducted in camera and shall not be open to the public.

(4) If the court finds by a preponderance of evidence that the respondent has committed a crime involving domestic violence against the petitioner, regardless of whether the respondent appears at the hearing, the court may order any relief available under Subsection (5) of this section. The provisions of a protective order issued under this section are effective for one year unless earlier dissolved by court order.

(5) A protective order under this section may:

(a) Prohibit the respondent from threatening to commit or committing domestic violence;

(b) Prohibit the respondent from telephoning, emailing, texting, contacting, or otherwise communicating directly or indirectly with the petitioner;

(c) Remove and exclude the respondent from the

residence of the petitioner; PROVIDED that if the ownership of the residence is the respondent's, the petitioner shall be allowed entry to gather belongings;

(d) Direct the respondent to stay away from the residence, school, or place of employment of the petitioner or any specified place frequented by the petitioner or any designated household member;

(e) Prohibit the respondent from entering a propelled vehicle in the possession of or occupied by the petitioner;

(f) Prohibit the respondent from using or possessing a deadly weapon if the court finds the respondent was in the actual possession of or used a weapon during the commission of domestic violence;

(g) Direct the respondent to surrender any firearm owned or possessed by the respondent if the court finds that the respondent was in the actual possession of or used a firearm during the commission of the domestic violence;

(h) Request a police officer to accompany the petitioner to the petitioner's residence to ensure that the petitioner:

(i) Safely obtains possession of the petitioner's residence, vehicle, or personal items; and

(ii) Is able to safely remove a vehicle or personal items from the petitioner's residence;

(i) Award temporary custody of a minor child to the petitioner and may arrange for visitation with a minor child if

the safety of the child and the petitioner can be protected;

(i) Give the petitioner possession and use of a vehicle and other essential personal items, regardless of ownership of the items;

(k) Prohibit the respondent from consuming controlled substances;

(1) Require the respondent to pay support for the petitioner or a minor child in the care of the petitioner if there is an independent legal obligation of the respondent to support the petitioner or child;

(m) Require the respondent to reimburse the petitioner or other person for expenses associated with the domestic violence, including medical expenses, counseling, shelter, and repair or replacement of damaged property;

(n) Require the respondent to pay costs and fees incurred by the petitioner in bringing the action under this chapter;

(o) Order the respondent, at the respondent's expense, to participate in a program for the rehabilitation of perpetrators of domestic violence under this chapter; and

(p) Order other relief the court determines necessary to protect the petitioner or any family or household member.

(6) If the court issues a protective order under this section, it shall:

(a) Make reasonable efforts to ensure that the order is understood by the petitioner and by the respondent, if

present; and

(b) Have the order delivered to the appropriate local law enforcement agency for expedited service of process.

(7) A court may not deny a petition for a protective order under this section solely because of a lapse of time between an act of domestic violence and the filing of the petition.

#### §1

110. Ex parte and emergency protective orders.

(1) A person who is a victim of a crime involving domestic violence may file a petition under §1-109 of this chapter of the Code and request an ex parte protective order. If the court finds that the petition establishes probable cause that a crime involving domestic violence has occurred, it is necessary to protect the petitioner from violence, and if the petitioner has certified to the court in writing the efforts, if any, that have been made to provide notice to the respondent, the court shall ex parte and without notice to the respondent issue a protective order. An ex parte protective order may grant the protection provided by §1-109 of this chapter of the Code. An ex parte protective order expires 20 days after it is issued unless dissolved earlier by the court at the request of either the petitioner or the respondent and after notice and, if requested, a hearing. If a court issues an ex parte protective order, the court shall have the order delivered to the appropriate local law enforcement agency for expedited service of process.

(2) A court may not deny a petition for an ex parte

protective order filed under Subsection (1) of this section solely because of a lapse of time between an act of domestic violence and the filing of the petition.

(3) A police officer, on behalf of and with the consent of a victim of a crime involving domestic violence, may request an emergency protective order from a judicial officer. The request may be made orally or in writing based upon the sworn statement of a police officer, and in person or by telephone. If the court finds probable cause to believe that the victim is in immediate danger of domestic violence based on an allegation of the recent commission of a crime involving domestic violence, the court shall ex parte issue an emergency protective order. In an emergency protective order, the court may grant the protection provided by §1-109 of this chapter of the Code. An emergency protective order expires 72 hours after it is issued unless dissolved earlier by the court at the request of the petitioner.

(4) A police officer who obtains an emergency protective order under Subsection (3) of this section shall:

(a) Place the provisions of an oral order in writing on a form provided by the court and file the written order with the issuing court by the end of the judicial day after it was issued:

(b) Provide a copy of the order to the petitioner; and
(c) Serve a copy of the order on the respondent.

#### §1

111. Modification of protective orders.

(1) Either the petitioner or the respondent may request

modification of a protective order. If a request is made for modification of:

(a) An ex parte protective order under §1-110(1) of this chapter of the Code, the court shall schedule a hearing on three days' notice or on shorter notice as the court may prescribe; the court shall hear and rule on the request in an expeditious manner; or

(b) A protective order after notice and hearing under §1-109 of this chapter of the Code, the court shall schedule a hearing within 20 days after the date the request is made, except that if the court finds that the request is meritless on its face, the court may deny the request without further hearing.

(2) If a request for a modification is made under this section and the respondent raises an issue not raised by the petitioner, the court may allow the petitioner additional time to respond.

(3) If the court modifies a protective order under this section, it shall issue a modified order and shall:

(a) Make reasonable efforts to ensure that the order is understood by the petitioner and by the respondent, if present at the hearing; and

(b) Have the order delivered to the appropriate local law enforcement agency for expedited service of process.

#### §1

112. Specifics of protective orders.

(1) If a respondent in a protective order issued under this

chapter is prohibited from communicating with the petitioner, excluded from the residence of the petitioner, or ordered to stay away from the petitioner as provided in §1- 109 and §1- 110 of this chapter of the Code, an invitation by the petitioner to communicate, enter the residence or vehicle, or have other prohibited contact with the petitioner does not waive or nullify any provision in a protective order.

(2) A court may not grant protective orders against the petitioner and the respondent in the same action under this chapter.

(3) In addition to other required information contained in a protective order, the order must include in bold face type the following statements in both Pohnpeian and English:

(a) "Violation of this order may be a misdemeanor. punishable by up to one year of incarceration or a fine of not more than \$1,000, or both such fine and imprisonment.";

(b) "If you are ordered to have no contact with the petitioner or to stay away from the petitioner's residence, vehicle, or other place designated by the court, an invitation by the petitioner to have the prohibited contact or to be present at or enter the residence, vehicle, or other place does not in any way invalidate or nullify the order."

(4) A protective order issued under this chapter is in addition to and not in place of any other civil or criminal remedy.

#### §1

113. Protective orders of other states. [RESERVED]

#### §1

114. Forms for petitions and orders; fees.

(1) The forms for petitions for protective orders under this chapter shall be as prescribed in the rules of procedure issued by the Pohnpei Supreme Court pursuant to 4 PC 2-102 of the Code.

(2) Filing fees may not be charged in any action seeking only the relief provided in this chapter.

#### §1

115. Notification of law enforcement agencies. When a court issues or accepts for filing a protective order under this chapter, it shall send a copy of the order to the appropriate local law enforcement agency. Each law enforcement agency shall establish procedures to inform police officers of protective orders. Police officers shall use every reasonable means to enforce a protective order issued or filed under this chapter.

#### §1

116. Other matters pertaining to protective orders.

(1) The Department of Public Safety shall maintain a complete and systematic record of all protective orders with respect to domestic violence incidents, including orders which have not yet been served and proofs of service in effect. This shall be used to inform police officers responding to domestic violence calls of the existence, terms, and effective dates of protective orders in effect.

(2) The terms and conditions of the protective orders remain enforceable, notwithstanding the acts of the parties, and may be changed only by order of the court.

(3) Upon request, the Department of Public Safety shall serve the court orders specified in §1-109 and §1-110 of this chapter of the Code upon the party to be restrained at the scene of a domestic violence incident or at any time the party is in custody.

#### §1

117. Violation of a protective court order.

(1) Any knowing violation of a protective court order issued under the authority of §1-109 or §1-110 of this chapter of the Code shall be a misdemeanor punishable by imprisonment for not more than one year or by a fine of not more than $1,000, or both such fine and imprisonment.

(2) In the event of a conviction for a violation of Subsection (1) of this section which results in bodily injury, the defendant shall be imprisoned for at least 48 hours.

(3) In the event of a conviction for a violation under Subsection (2) of this section, occurring within one year of a conviction of either Subsection (1) or Subsection (2) of this section, committed against the same victim, the defendant shall be imprisoned for no less than 30 days.

(4) When a police officer has probable cause to believe that a person has violated an order of the court specified in Subsection (1) of this section and verifies the existence of the order, the police officer shall arrest the person.

(5) An admission by the defendant that he or she had knowledge of the court order shall be admissible in court

notwithstanding the corpus delicti rule.

(\S 1 - 118) . Diversion eligibility. Except as otherwise provided in this section and notwithstanding any other provision of law, and upon the determination of the judge, this section shall apply whenever a case is before the court upon an accusatory pleading for any criminal act identified in (\S 1 - 103) of this chapter of the Code against a family or household member as defined in (\S 1 - 102(6)) of this chapter of the Code.

(1) The following persons are ineligible for the diversion process:

(a) A defendant who has a felony conviction for any offense involving violence within seven years prior to the alleged commission of the charged offense;

(b) A defendant who has been diverted pursuant to this section within five years prior to the alleged commission of the charged offense;

(c) A defendant who has been sentenced for a violation of (\S 1 - 103) of this chapter of the Code within one year prior to the alleged commission of the charged offense; or

(d) A defendant whose current charge involves serious bodily injury, or sexual assault, unless the court finds that due to unusual circumstances diversion of the criminal proceedings is manifestly in the interest of justice.

(2) The fact that a defendant is not made ineligible by Subsection (1) of this section does not automatically entitle a

defendant to the diversion process.

(3) The prosecuting attorney shall determine whether the defendant is ineligible for diversion by reason of any of the factors set forth in Subsection (1) of this section. If the prosecutor finds that the person is eligible, and the prosecutor will agree to diversion, the prosecutor shall notify the defendant.

(4) If the prosecutor finds that the defendant is ineligible, or if the prosecutor will not agree to diversion although the defendant is not excluded by reason of Subsection (1) of this section, the prosecutor shall notify the defendant.

(5) Any defendant who is not specifically ineligible for the diversion process pursuant to Subsection (1) of this section may apply to the court, by noticed motion for an order granting diversion. The prosecuting attorney may oppose this application.

#### §1

119. Diversion hearing.

(1) Upon noticed motion, the court shall hold a hearing and, after consideration of any and all information the court believes to be relevant to its decision, the court shall determine if the defendant consents to further proceedings under this section and waives his or her right to a speedy trial, and if the defendant should be diverted from the criminal proceedings and referred for an education and treatment program directed specifically to the violent conduct of the defendant, if one is

available. The court, in determining the defendant's eligibility for diversion, shall consider the nature and extent of the injury inflicted upon the victim, any prior incidents of domestic violence by the defendant, and any factors which would adversely influence the likelihood of successful completion of the diversion process. If the court does not deem the defendant a person who would be benefited by diversion, or if the defendant does not consent to participate, the criminal proceedings shall continue as in any other case. If the court orders a defendant to be diverted, the court shall make inquiry into the financial condition of the defendant and upon a finding that the defendant is able in whole or part to pay the expense of such counseling the court may order him or her to pay for all or part of such expense. Nothing in this subsection shall prohibit the placement of a defendant in another appropriate counseling program if the court determines that there is no available education and treatment program.

(2) At such time that the defendant's case is diverted, any bail bond or undertaking, or deposit in lieu thereof, on file by or on behalf of him or her shall be exonerated, and the court shall enter an order so directing.

(3) The period during which further criminal proceedings against a person may be diverted pursuant to this section shall be no less than one year, and no more than three years if a misdemeanor is charged, and not less than one year, and no

more than five years if a felony is charged.

(4) The court shall set forth in writing or state on the record its reason for granting or denying diversion. The court's decision in such a matter shall be final and shall not constitute an appealable order.

#### §1

120. Reinstatement of criminal proceedings; dismissal. If it appears to the prosecuting attorney, the court or the probation officer that the divierte under this chapter is performing unsatisfactorily in the assigned program, or that the divierte is not benefiting from education and treatment programs, or that he or she has been convicted of any offense involving violence, after notice to the divierte, and upon motion by the prosecuting attorney or on the court's own motion, the court shall hold a hearing to determine whether the criminal proceedings should be reinstated. If the court finds by substantial evidence that the divierte is not performing satisfactorily in the assigned program, or that the divierte is not benefiting from diversion, or the court finds that the divierte has been convicted of a crime as set out above, the criminal case shall be referred back to the court for resumption of the criminal proceedings. If the divierte has performed satisfactorily during the period of diversion, at the end of the period of diversion, the criminal charges shall be dismissed upon motion or application of the defendant.

#### §1

121. Use of arrest record. Any records filed with the Pohnpei Department of Public Safety and the Office of the

Attorney General shall set out the disposition of those cases. diverted pursuant to this chapter. The divertee may indicate in response to any question concerning his or her prior criminal record that he or she was not arrested or diverted for such offense. A record pertaining to an arrest resulting in successful completion of the diversion process shall not, without the divertee's consent, be used in any way which could result in the denial of any employment, benefit, license, or certificate.

#### §1

122. Admissibility of statement or information. Any statement or any information procured from the arrest record as set forth in §1- 121 of this chapter of the Code, with respect to the specific offense with which the defendant is charged, which is made to any probation officer or counselor during the process of determining the defendant's eligibility for diversion or subsequent to the granting of diversion, shall be inadmissible.

#### §1

123. Counseling and education and treatment programs.

(1) If a person is ordered to complete a counseling program or education and treatment program as a result of being in diversion, entering a deferred plea of guilty to domestic violence, or is adjudged guilty of domestic violence, he or she shall be ordered to pay a fee to the Pohnpei Supreme Court for such service.

(2) The fee shall be set by order of the presiding judge of the Trial Division of the Pohnpei Supreme Court. The fee shall not exceed \$10 per session or per class.

(3) The fee shall be paid into a revolving fund of the Pohnpei Treasury hereby established and maintained apart from other funds of the Pohnpei Supreme Court. The Clerk of the Pohnpei Supreme Court shall be the certifying officer for the fund. The revolving fund shall be expended by the Pohnpei Supreme Court without necessity of appropriation to hire, as independent contractors, licensed individual and family counselors who shall conduct either group sessions or individual sessions for the perpetrators of domestic violence, victims of domestic violence or children who have witnessed domestic violence.

(\S 1 - 124) . Training courses. To the extent that sufficient monies are budgeted or are otherwise available through special grants or donations and to the extent practicable, the Department of Public Safety shall implement a course or courses of instruction for the training of police officers in Pohnpei in the handling of domestic violence complaints and also shall develop guidelines for law enforcement response to domestic violence. The course or courses of instruction and the guidelines shall stress enforcement of criminal laws in domestic violence situations, availability of civil remedies and community resources, and protection of the victim."

### Section 2

Section 4- 101 of Title 59 of the Pohnpei Code is amended to read as follows:

"§4- 101. Spouses. Except as otherwise provided by Pohnpei

1 law, neither husband nor wife shall be compelled to testify against the other in the trial of an information, complaint, citation or other criminal proceeding."

### Section 3

This act shall take effect upon its approval by the Governor, or upon its becoming law without such approval; PROVIDED that Section 1- 109 through Section 1- 117 of Chapter 1 of Title 53 of the Pohnpei Code, as added to the Code by this act, shall not take effect until these sections of the Code are amended to provide for an effective date for said sections; PROVIDED FURTHER that not later than 30 days following the effective date of this act the Governor shall establish a task force to review and study the concept of utilizing protective orders in the context of domestic violence situations in this state; PROVIDED FURTHER that the task force shall submit its recommendations to the Governor and the Legislature no later than six months following the effective date of this act.
