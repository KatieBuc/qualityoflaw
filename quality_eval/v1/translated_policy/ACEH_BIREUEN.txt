THE REGENT OF BIREUEN,

Considering: a. that women, as noble and dignified beings, need to have their capacities empowered and are entitled to protection in accordance with Islamic Sharia;
b. that based on Article 231 of Law Number 11 of 2006 concerning the Governance of Aceh, the Regency Government is obliged to advance and protect the rights of women and children and to undertake dignified empowerment efforts;
c. that based on the considerations referred to in letters a and b, it is necessary to establish a Qanun concerning Women's Protection;

2. Law Number 39 of 1999 concerning Human Rights (State Gazette of the Republic of Indonesia of 1999 Number 165, Supplement to the State Gazette of the Republic of Indonesia Number 3886);

DECIDES:

To Establish: QANUN CONCERNING WOMEN'S PROTECTION.

CHAPTER I
GENERAL PROVISIONS

Article 1

In this Qanun, the following terms shall mean:
1. Region means Bireuen Regency.
2. Regency means Bireuen Regency, which is part of the Province of Aceh, led by a Regent.
3. Regency Government means the Government of Bireuen Regency as the administration of affairs carried out by the regency government and the Regional People's Representative Council of Bireuen Regency in accordance with their respective functions and authorities.
4. The Regency Government means the element of administration of the Regency consisting of the Regent and regency apparatus.
5. Regent means the Head of the Bireuen Regency Government elected through a democratic process based on the principles of direct, general, free, secret, honest and fair elections in accordance with prevailing laws and regulations.
6. The Regional People's Representative Council of the Regency, hereinafter abbreviated as DPRK, means the element of administration of Bireuen Regency whose members are elected through general elections in accordance with prevailing laws and regulations.
7. Women means persons (human beings) aged 15 years and above who menstruate, become pregnant, give birth, and breastfeed.
8. Women's Protection means all efforts aimed at protecting women and providing a sense of security in the fulfillment of their rights by giving consistent and systematic attention aimed at achieving a fair and equal relationship with men.
9. Victim means a woman who experiences the threat of violence and/or violence within and/or outside the household and/or becomes an object of trafficking/exploitation, and/or discrimination in any form and aspect.
10. Threat of Violence means any act of non-physical abuse, whether using means or not, that is unlawful and may cause harm to the body, life, freedom and human rights of women.
11. Violence means any act against a person, particularly a woman, that results in misery or suffering physically, psychologically, sexually and/or domestic neglect, including the threat to commit acts, coercion, or unlawful deprivation of liberty, whether within or outside the household.
12. Discrimination Against Women means any distinction, exclusion or restriction made on the basis of sex which has the effect or purpose of impairing or nullifying the recognition, enjoyment or exercise by women, irrespective of their marital status, of human rights and fundamental freedoms in the political, economic, social, cultural, civil or any other field, on a basis of equality between men and women.
13. Women's Rights means all rights held by every woman that form part of human rights.
14. Special Protection means protection provided to women and children in emergency situations, women and children in conflict with the law, women and children from minority and isolated groups, women and children who are economically and/or sexually exploited, women and children who are trafficked, women and children who become victims of abduction, sale, trafficking, and women and children who are victims of physical and/or mental violence.
15. Community means individuals, families, groups and social organizations and/or community organizations.
16. Family means the smallest unit in society consisting of husband and wife, or husband and wife and their children, or a father and his children, or a mother and her children, or blood relatives in a direct line upward or downward up to the third degree.
17. Manpower means every person capable of performing work to produce goods and/or services, whether to meet their own needs or those of society.
18. Workers/Laborers means every person who works and receives wages or other forms of remuneration.
19. Persons with Disabilities means every person who experiences physical, intellectual, mental, and/or sensory limitations over a long period of time, who in interacting with their environment may experience obstacles and difficulties to participate fully and effectively with other citizens based on equal rights.
20. Service means action taken as quickly as possible for a victim upon seeing, hearing, or becoming aware that violence against the victim is occurring, has occurred, or is about to occur.
21. Rehabilitation means a series of actions carried out by the competent authority in its field to restore the physical, mental and psychosocial condition of a woman to its original state.

CHAPTER II
PRINCIPLES AND OBJECTIVES

Article 2

Women's protection shall be implemented based on the principles of:
a. respect for the values of Islamic Sharia and Acehnese customs that are responsive to women;
b. legal certainty;
c. public interest;
d. respect for human rights;
e. justice and gender equality;
f. non-discrimination;
g. professionalism, accountability and transparency; and
h. protection and fulfillment of victims' rights.

Article 3

Women's protection has the objectives of:
a. strengthening regional policy and budgetary support in order to optimize the implementation of women's protection;
b. reinforcing the commitment of policymakers at the regency, district, and village levels within the framework of enhancing efforts and guarantees of protection for women;
c. improving coordination, integration, synchronization, and synergy among Regional Work Units (SKPK) and all stakeholders, including religious organizations, professional organizations, academics, the private sector and the wider community, in efforts to improve the quality of women's protection;
d. building public knowledge and awareness to support efforts to protect women;
e. increasing the institutional and human resource capacity of the UPTD PPA (Regional Technical Implementation Unit for the Protection of Women and Children);
f. providing guarantees to women so that they can fulfill their rights as human beings in all aspects of life;
g. providing a sense of security to women in all aspects of life; and
h. reducing the rate of violence against women.

CHAPTER III
SCOPE

Article 4

The scope of regulation in this Qanun includes:
a. principles and objectives;
b. women's rights;
c. women's protection;
d. obligations and responsibilities of the Regency Government;
e. support from the village government;
f. strategy and mechanism for the implementation of women's protection;
g. community participation; and
h. awards.

CHAPTER IV
WOMEN'S RIGHTS

Part One
General

Article 5

(1) Every woman has the right to:
a. live and maintain her life, and improve her standard of welfare in her life;
b. practice her religion in accordance with Islamic Sharia;
c. obtain justice, a sense of security, and freedom to express opinions without discrimination;
d. obtain protection of her person, family, honor, dignity and property;
e. fulfill her basic needs to grow and develop properly, to form a family and continue her lineage;
f. obtain fair and equal treatment in carrying out her roles and functions both in family life, in society, and to participate fully in every process of formulating, determining and implementing development policy;
g. obtain education and instruction at all types, levels, and pathways of education, without any obstruction or pressure whatsoever, in order to improve her skills and quality of life;
h. obtain information and knowledge about health, especially reproductive health, in full; and
i. obtain special protection in the performance of her work or profession against matters that threaten her safety and/or health related to reproductive function.
(2) Every woman in her capacity as a wife, for as long as she remains within the bond of marriage, has the same rights and responsibilities as her husband over all matters relating to their married life, relations with their children, and the ownership and management of joint property in accordance with prevailing laws and regulations.
(3) In addition to the rights referred to in paragraph (2), a woman as a wife has the right to receive material and spiritual support from her husband.
(4) After the dissolution of a marriage, a woman has rights and responsibilities from her former husband regarding all matters concerning the children, with due regard for the best interests of the child.

Part Two
Rights of Women with Disabilities

Article 6

Every woman with a disability has the right to obtain:
a. education at all units, pathways, types, and levels of education;
b. decent work and livelihood in accordance with the type and degree of her disability, education, and abilities;
c. equal treatment to participate in development and enjoy its results;
d. accessibility in order to achieve her independence;
e. rehabilitation, social assistance, and the maintenance of social welfare standards;
f. equal rights to develop her talents, abilities, and social life; and
g. other rights as regulated under prevailing laws and regulations.

Part Three
Rights of Women Victims of Violence

Article 7

Every woman who is a victim of violence has the right to obtain:
a. respect for her dignity and worth as a human being;
b. restoration of health and psychological recovery from the suffering experienced by the victim;
c. determine for herself decisions regarding the handling of the violence case she experienced;
d. obtain information;
e. obtain a guarantee of confidentiality;
f. social rehabilitation;
g. complaint handling;
h. obtain ease in the judicial process;
i. companionship/advocacy; and
j. obtain empowerment after her case has been resolved.

Part Four
Special Treatment

Article 8

(1) Every woman who is in a condition of disability, advanced age, and/or pregnancy, has the right to obtain ease and special treatment related to access to public services.
(2) Every elderly woman, woman with a physical disability and/or mental disability, has the right to obtain care, education, training, and special assistance at the expense of the Regency Government to ensure a decent life in accordance with human dignity.
(3) Every woman entangled with narcotics, whether as a perpetrator or a victim, has the right to fair treatment both in the law enforcement process and the rehabilitation (recovery) process.

CHAPTER V
WOMEN'S PROTECTION

Part One
Implementation of Women's Protection

Article 9

The implementation of women's protection shall be provided to:
a. women as housewives;
b. women as heads of household;
c. women domestic workers;
d. women workers/laborers;
e. women with disabilities;
f. homeless women;
g. women who are former inmates of correctional institutions;
h. women who are victims of violence;
i. women who are victims of conflict; and
j. elderly women.

Paragraph 1
Women as Housewives

Article 10

The implementation of protection for women as housewives as referred to in Article 9 letter a, shall be as follows:
a. material and spiritual support;
b. the head of household shall provide special protection to the housewife during pregnancy; and
c. a housewife who has given birth must receive protection from the head of household to reduce her burden as a housewife.

Article 11

A housewife who performs work outside the home must receive protection from the head of household in the form of granting permission and the provision of domestic help where possible.

Paragraph 2
Women as Heads of Household

Article 12

(1) A woman as head of household as referred to in Article 9 letter b, is a widow whose husband has died or as a result of divorce, and is entitled to protection.
(2) The implementation of protection for women as heads of household as referred to in paragraph (1) is aimed at providing protection and prevention against forms of discrimination, negative stigma, and economic empowerment for women heads of household.
(3) Women as heads of household as referred to in paragraph (2) obtain the right to protection against:
a. acts of discrimination and efforts to weaken the function of women as the family's economic mainstay;
b. strengthening of capacity and recognition as head of household within their environment; and
c. accessibility to economic and financial institutions.
(4) The implementation of protection for women as heads of household who are widowed as referred to in paragraph (1) includes:
a. a woman as head of household shall receive 1/8 (one-eighth) of the total remaining estate of her husband if she has children; and
b. a woman as head of household shall receive 1/4 (one-quarter) of the total remaining estate of her husband if she has no children.
(5) The implementation of protection for women as heads of household as a result of divorce as referred to in paragraph (1), during the iddah period, the former husband is responsible for providing housing and maintenance.

Paragraph 3
Women Domestic Workers

Article 13

(1) The implementation of protection for women domestic workers as referred to in Article 9 letter c, is aimed at providing protection and prevention against forms of discrimination, cruel treatment, inhumane treatment, degradation of human dignity, and exploitation in employment relationships.
(2) Women domestic workers as referred to in paragraph (1) shall obtain protection from their employer in the form of:
a. recognition of rights, wages and decent working conditions;
b. health and social insurance; and
c. a guarantee against discrimination, inhumane treatment, degradation of human dignity, and exploitation.

Paragraph 4
Women Workers/Laborers

Article 14

(1) The implementation of protection for women workers/laborers as referred to in Article 9 letter d, is to provide protection from economic exploitation and cruel, inhumane treatment, and to secure decent appreciation for their work.
(2) Women workers/laborers as referred to in paragraph (1) shall obtain protection from their employer in the form of:
a. recognition of rights, wages and decent working conditions;
b. health and social insurance; and
c. a guarantee against discrimination, inhumane treatment, degradation of human dignity, and exploitation.

Paragraph 5
Women with Disabilities

Article 15

(1) The implementation of protection for women with disabilities as referred to in Article 9 letter e, is aimed at providing protection and prevention against forms of discrimination, including freedom from cruel and inhumane treatment, degradation of human dignity, and freedom from exploitation.
(2) Women with disabilities as referred to in paragraph (1) obtain the right to protection over:
a. guarantee of the continuity and development of their person;
b. accessibility to information, legal assistance, mobility, and social services; and
c. early intervention measures including treatment and rehabilitation to improve their function and capacity.

Paragraph 6
Homeless Women

Article 16

(1) The implementation of protection for homeless women as referred to in Article 9 letter f, is aimed at providing protection and prevention against forms of violence caused by the absence of shelter or permanent residence.
(2) Homeless women as referred to in paragraph (1) obtain the right to protection over:
a. decent housing;
b. health and social insurance;
c. civil registration administration;
d. opportunities to obtain education and skills to improve economic status; and
e. access to information and legal consultation services.

Paragraph 7
Women Former Inmates of Correctional Institutions

Article 17

(1) The implementation of protection for women who are former inmates of Correctional Institutions as referred to in Article 9 letter g, is aimed at providing protection from obstacles to adjusting to community life, so that they can live a normal life.
(2) Women who are former inmates of Correctional Institutions as referred to in paragraph (1) obtain protection over:
a. rehabilitation and social reintegration; and
b. opportunities to obtain knowledge and skills to improve economic status.

Paragraph 8
Women Victims of Violence

Article 18

(1) The implementation of protection for women victims of violence as referred to in Article 9 letter h, is aimed at providing legal protection and/or health restoration.
(2) Protection for women victims of violence as referred to in paragraph (1) includes:
a. legal assistance;
b. companionship/advocacy following an act of violence;
c. rehabilitation and social reintegration; and
d. health services.

Article 19

(1) Every victim as referred to in Article 18 has the right to obtain protection, information, integrated services, continued handling up to the rehabilitation stage, and confidential handling by individuals, groups or institutions, whether of the Regency Government or the community.
(2) In the event of violence, every victim has the right to obtain health services and companionship, both psychologically and legally, as well as guarantees regarding her rights related to her status as a woman.

Paragraph 9
Women Victims of Conflict

Article 20

The implementation of protection for women who are victims of conflict as referred to in Article 9 letter i, shall be through:
a. providing a sense of security;
b. fulfilling the basic needs of women victims of conflict;
c. eliminating trauma; and
d. providing services needed by victims.

Paragraph 10
Elderly Women

Article 21

(1) The implementation of protection for elderly women as referred to in Article 9 letter j, is aimed at enabling them to carry out their social function and to play an active role appropriately in their social life.
(2) Protection of elderly women as referred to in paragraph (1) shall be primarily prioritized as the protection of their children and heirs.
(3) If an elderly woman does not receive care from her children and heirs and/or does not have children and other heirs as referred to in paragraph (2), then the protection of the elderly woman becomes the responsibility of the Regency Government.
(4) Elderly women as referred to in paragraph (1) obtain the right to protection over:
a. basic health services to maintain and improve health status, including gerontological/geriatric services;
b. use of public facilities and accessibility of infrastructure; and
c. government administrative services.

CHAPTER VII
OBLIGATIONS AND RESPONSIBILITIES
OF THE REGENCY GOVERNMENT

Part One
General

Article 22

(1) The Regency Government is obliged and responsible for the protection of women.
(2) Efforts to protect women as referred to in paragraph (1) include:
a. establishment of policies, programs, and activities;
b. establishment of implementation guidelines;
c. provision of services; and
d. coordination of policies, programs and activities.
(3) Women's protection as referred to in paragraph (2) shall be carried out in an integrated manner in accordance with prevailing laws and regulations.

Article 23

(1) In implementing policies, programs, and activities for women's protection and empowerment for women victims, the Regency Government may cooperate with the community/community institutions.
(2) Cooperation as referred to in paragraph (1) shall be implemented in accordance with the provisions of prevailing laws and regulations.

Article 24

The Regent, in implementing policies, programs, and activities for women's protection, shall undertake efforts to:
a. coordinate the implementation of policies, programs, and activities for women's protection among regency apparatus;
b. cooperate with Regencies/Cities within the same Province, and cooperate between Regencies/Cities in other Provinces, in the implementation of policies, programs, and activities for women's protection in accordance with prevailing laws and regulations;
d. strengthen institutional capacity for the implementation of policies, programs and activities for women's protection;
e. facilitate women's protection services;
f. provide women's protection services;
g. implement affirmative action for women's protection; and
h. develop a data system for women's protection.

Article 25

(1) The Regency Government is obliged and responsible for implementing efforts to prevent violence against women in the form of:
a. collecting data and information on victims of violence as well as laws and regulations;
b. providing education on the values of anti-violence against women;
c. socializing laws and regulations related to the provision of protection for victims of violence;
d. fostering the support of religious leaders and customary leaders to build public awareness of the importance of protecting women;
e. monitoring and evaluating the provision of protection for victims of violence; and
f. preparing reports on the provision of protection for women victims of violence.
(2) To anticipate the occurrence of acts of violence against women, the Regency Government is obliged to provide and organize services for victims in the form of:
a. establishing institutions and facilitating the organization of integrated services for victims involving community elements; and
b. encouraging community concern for the importance of protecting victims.
(3) To prevent the occurrence of acts of violence against women, the Regency Government shall undertake the following efforts:
a. conducting socialization regarding regulations and policies concerning violence against women for village officials, customary leaders and religious leaders;
b. providing counseling on the importance of promptly reporting to local authorities in the event of violence against women;
c. establishing a network in efforts to prevent violence; and
d. other efforts deemed strategic to prevent the occurrence of violence against women.

Article 26

(1) The regency government is obliged to provide social assistance for women experiencing social problems and disaster victims.
(2) The regency government is obliged to provide the right to social security for poor women and women with limited capacity.

Part Two
Women's Protection Institution

Article 27

(1) The Regency Government shall establish a women's protection institution.
(2) The women's protection institution as referred to in paragraph (1) shall also handle the protection of children.
(3) The women's protection institution as referred to in paragraph (2) shall be implemented by establishing the UPTD PPA.
(4) The Regency Government must guarantee the availability of adequate facilities and infrastructure, human resources, and budget to realize the operation of the UPTD PPA.
(5) Further provisions concerning the UPTD PPA shall be regulated by Regent's Regulation.

CHAPTER VIII
VILLAGE GOVERNMENT SUPPORT

Article 28

(1) The Village Government shall support efforts for women's protection by:
a. issuing village-level regulations on women's protection;
b. socializing regulations related to the empowerment and protection of women to the community;
c. encouraging the realization of a village environment that is friendly to women;
d. building community awareness, including among customary and religious leaders at the village level, to care about issues of violence against women;
e. allocating budget to support women's empowerment and protection programs; and
f. building a system for handling violence against women at the village level.
(2) The efforts referred to in paragraph (1) letter e, above may be developed in accordance with the village's financial capacity, problems and the needs of women.

CHAPTER IX
STRATEGY AND MECHANISM FOR WOMEN'S PROTECTION

Part One
General

Article 29

(1) The Regent shall integrate policies, programs, and activities for women's protection into regency development planning.
(2) The integration of policies, programs, and activities for women's protection must be incorporated into planning and budget documents, including the Long-Term Regional Development Plan (RPJPD), the Medium-Term Regional Development Plan (RPJMD), the Regency Work Unit Strategic Plan (Renstra-SKPK), the Regency Government Work Plan (RKPK), and the Regency Work Unit Work and Budget Plan (RKA-SKPK).
(3) The Regency Work Unit whose duties and functions handle women's empowerment affairs shall facilitate the integration of policies, programs, and activities for women's protection into the preparation of the planning and budget documents referred to in paragraph (2).
(4) In carrying out the duties and functions referred to in paragraph (3), the work unit handling women's empowerment in the region shall coordinate with the Regional Development Planning Agency (BAPPEDA).

Part Two
Strategy

Article 30

(1) Women's protection shall be implemented by integrating Gender Mainstreaming (PUG) with disaggregated data, gender data and violence data in an integrated manner in accordance with prevailing laws and regulations.
(2) The Regency Work Unit handling women's empowerment and protection shall coordinate with other Regional Apparatus Organizations in order to realize the integrated implementation of women's protection.
(3) Coordination as referred to in paragraph (2) shall be carried out within the framework of strengthening synergy in policy formulation, program and activity support, as well as monitoring and evaluation.

Part Three
Mechanism

Article 31

The mechanism for organizing women's protection shall be carried out through the stages of:
a. formulation of policies for planning programs and activities;
b. budgeting of programs and activities;
c. implementation of programs and activities; and
d. monitoring, evaluation and reporting.

Article 32

(1) The formulation of policies for planning programs and activities as referred to in Article 31 letter a, shall be carried out through:
a. mapping, collection, processing and presentation of disaggregated data;
b. coordination and synchronization of the preparation of program and activity plans with relevant SKPK; and
c. establishment of programs and activities in planning documents.
(2) The formulation of policies for planning programs and activities shall be further regulated in service standards and standard operating procedures.
(3) The budgeting of programs and activities as referred to in Article 31 letter b, shall be carried out through general budget policy as set out in the Regency Budget (APBK).
(4) The implementation of programs and activities as referred to in Article 31 letter c, shall be carried out through:
a. implementation of programs and activities set out in the SKPK budget document; and
b. coordination and synchronization of the implementation of programs and activities with relevant SKPK.
(5) The implementation of monitoring, evaluation and reporting as referred to in Article 31 letter d, shall be carried out through:
a. periodic monitoring and evaluation of the implementation of programs and activities that have been carried out; and
b. preparation of periodic annual reports submitted to the Regent.

CHAPTER VIII [as numbered in original]
COMMUNITY PARTICIPATION

Article 33

(1) The community may participate in women's protection.
(2) Community participation as referred to in paragraph (1) may be carried out by individuals and/or organizations.
(3) Participation as referred to in paragraphs (1) and (2) may be carried out by:
a. providing input and suggestions to the Regency Government related to policies, programs and activities for women's protection;
b. monitoring Regency Government policies related to women's protection;
c. initiating or proposing new policies that need to be established by the Regency Government in efforts to optimize women's protection;
d. assisting village officials in implementing efforts for women's protection as regulated in this Qanun;
e. conducting socialization on the elimination of violence against women;
f. providing information and/or reporting the occurrence of violence against women to law enforcement institutions or relevant authorities; and
g. monitoring the performance of law enforcement officers in handling cases of violence against women.

Article 34

To ensure the best service for women victims of violence, the community, whether as individuals, groups or institutions, may coordinate with the UPTD PPA.

Article 35

(1) The Regency Government encourages the business sector to participate in protecting women from acts of violence.
(2) This participation may be carried out both internally and externally.
(3) Internal participation as referred to in paragraph (2) may take the form of:
a. socialization to employees within the company environment regarding the dangers of acts of violence against women;
b. building an internal system for women to handle issues of violence against women; and
c. providing social assistance as a form of corporate social responsibility.
(4) External participation as referred to in paragraph (2) may take the form of:
a. providing special opportunities for women victims of violence to work at the company;
b. creating special education and training for women victims of violence;
c. educational assistance for women victims of violence who have been deprived of education; and
d. fostering and improving the economic independence of women victims of violence.

CHAPTER IX [as numbered in original]
AWARDS

Article 36

(1) The Regency Government shall give awards to State Apparatus who are assessed as having commitment and success in carrying out the task of women's protection.
(2) The Regency Government shall give awards to Community Organizations, the Private Sector and Individuals who are assessed as having carried out innovation and success in supporting efforts for women's protection.
(3) Awards shall also be given to Village Governments that are assessed as having commitment and success in supporting efforts for women's protection.
(4) Further regulation concerning the granting of awards as referred to in paragraphs (1) and (2) shall be regulated by Regent's Regulation.

CHAPTER X
FUNDING

Article 37

All costs for the implementation of planning policies, programs and activities related to women's protection shall be sourced from:
a. the Regency Revenue and Expenditure Budget; and
b. other legitimate and non-binding sources.

CHAPTER XI
GUIDANCE, MONITORING AND EVALUATION

Article 38

(1) The Regent, through the Regional Apparatus responsible for women's empowerment affairs, shall provide guidance and oversight of women's protection.
(2) The forms of guidance referred to in paragraph (1) are as follows:
a. providing socialization to the community and the business sector regarding women's protection policy;
b. providing books, leaflets, brochures on women's protection and other related issues and disseminating them to the community;
c. facilitating capacity building of institutions implementing women's protection;
d. facilitating participation forums for women, women's organizations and organizations of persons with disabilities;
e. facilitating rehabilitation and reintegration services for women victims of disaster and victims of violence;
f. coordinating the participation of the business sector and other institutions, including international assistance, for women's protection; and
g. granting awards to the community and business sector, whether individually or institutionally, that have carried out efforts for women's protection.

Article 39

(1) To ensure synergy, continuity, and effectiveness of integrated measures in the implementation of policies, programs, and activities for women's protection, the Regency Government shall conduct monitoring.
(2) Monitoring as referred to in paragraph (1) is intended to determine the progress and obstacles in the implementation of policies, programs, and activities for women's protection.
(3) Monitoring shall be carried out periodically through coordination and direct monitoring of regional apparatus implementing policies, programs and activities for women's protection.
(4) Monitoring shall be carried out from the planning stage through to the implementation of policies, programs and activities.

Article 40

(1) Evaluation of the implementation of policies, programs, and activities for women's protection shall be carried out at the end of each budget year.
(2) The results of the evaluation of the implementation of policies, programs, and activities for women's protection shall be used as input for the preparation of policies, programs, and activities for women's protection for the following year.
(3) The evaluation referred to in paragraph (1) shall be carried out in accordance with the provisions of prevailing laws and regulations.

CHAPTER XII
CLOSING PROVISIONS

Article 41

Implementing regulations of this Qanun in the form of Regent's Regulations shall be established no later than six (6) months from the date this Qanun is enacted.

Article 42

This Qanun shall come into force on the date of its enactment.

So that everyone may be aware of it, the enactment of this Qanun is ordered to be placed in the Bireuen Regency Gazette.

BIREUEN REGENCY QANUN
NUMBER 3 OF 2022
CONCERNING
WOMEN'S PROTECTION

I. GENERAL

1. Islamic Sharia accords a high and noble position to women. Women hold a position equal to men as servants of Allah and dignified beings. Neither the Qur'an nor the Hadith of the Prophet Muhammad (peace be upon him) contains any teaching or practice leading to the discrimination or exploitation of women. Indeed, one of the missions of the Prophet (peace be upon him) in this world was to elevate the dignity and worth of women from a condition of great lowliness and disgrace.
2. Law Number 44 of 1999 concerning the Implementation of the Special Status of Aceh and Law Number 11 of 2006 concerning the Governance of Aceh have provided a foundation for the empowerment and protection of women, which is an important undertaking given that the condition of women has long been quite concerning, whether in terms of education, economics, health, employment, law, or even threats of violence, exploitation, discrimination, pornography and indecent acts, with women frequently being the target.
3. The Bireuen Regency Government considers it necessary to have a Bireuen Regency Qanun concerning the Empowerment and Protection of Women, so that every woman may be empowered and obtain protection without discrimination, and so that the Integrated Service Center for the Empowerment of Women and Children (P2TP2A), which already exists in Bireuen Regency, has a legal basis as one of the organizers of women's empowerment and protection in Bireuen Regency.
