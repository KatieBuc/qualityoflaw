REGENT OF NORTH CENTRAL TIMOR
PROVINCE OF EAST NUSA TENGGARA
REGIONAL REGULATION OF NORTH CENTRAL TIMOR REGENCY NUMBER 3 OF 2020
CONCERNING
THE IMPLEMENTATION OF WOMEN'S EMPOWERMENT

BY THE GRACE OF GOD ALMIGHTY

THE REGENT OF NORTH CENTRAL TIMOR,

Considering:
a. that every person has equal dignity to live in society, nation, and state, and is entitled to protection of human rights and fundamental human freedoms without discrimination, as an effort to realize gender equality and justice in national and regional development;
b. that in order to realize gender equality and justice in development in North Central Timor Regency, it is necessary to increase the effectiveness and optimization of the implementation of the sub-affairs of women's empowerment as an inseparable part of the functional activities of governmental and non-governmental institutions in order to improve the position, role, and quality of women in development in North Central Timor Regency;
c. that in order to guarantee legal certainty and to fill legal vacuums for the realization of gender equality and justice in development in North Central Timor Regency, it is necessary to have regulation in a Regional Regulation concerning the implementation of women's empowerment;
d. that based on the considerations referred to in letters a, b, and c, it is necessary to establish a Regional Regulation concerning the Implementation of Women's Empowerment;

In view of:
1. Article 18 paragraph (6) of the 1945 Constitution of the Republic of Indonesia;
2. Law Number 69 of 1958 concerning the Establishment of Level II Regions within the Level I Regions of Bali, West Nusa Tenggara, and East Nusa Tenggara (State Gazette of the Republic of Indonesia of 1958 Number 122, Supplement to the State Gazette of the Republic of Indonesia Number 1655);
3. Law Number 23 of 2014 concerning Regional Government (State Gazette of the Republic of Indonesia of 2014 Number 244, Supplement to the State Gazette of the Republic of Indonesia Number 5587), as amended several times, most recently by Law Number 9 of 2015 concerning the Second Amendment to Law Number 23 of 2014 concerning Regional Government (State Gazette of the Republic of Indonesia of 2015 Number 58, Supplement to the State Gazette of the Republic of Indonesia Number 5679);

With the Joint Approval of:
THE REGIONAL PEOPLE'S REPRESENTATIVE COUNCIL OF NORTH CENTRAL TIMOR REGENCY and
THE REGENT OF NORTH CENTRAL TIMOR

HAVE DECIDED:

To Establish: REGIONAL REGULATION CONCERNING THE IMPLEMENTATION OF WOMEN'S EMPOWERMENT.

CHAPTER I
GENERAL PROVISIONS

Article 1

In this Regional Regulation, the following terms shall mean:
1. Region means North Central Timor Regency.
2. Regional Government means the Government of North Central Timor Regency.
3. Regent means the Regent of North Central Timor.
4. Regional People's Representative Council, hereinafter abbreviated as DPRD, means the Regional People's Representative Council of North Central Timor Regency.
5. Regional Secretary means the Regional Secretary of North Central Timor Regency.
6. Office (Dinas) means the Regional Apparatus that administers government affairs in the field of women's empowerment and child protection.
7. Head of Office means the Head of the Regional Apparatus that administers government affairs in the field of women's empowerment.
8. Regional Apparatus, hereinafter abbreviated as PD, means a Regional Apparatus in North Central Timor Regency.
9. Head of Regional Apparatus means the Head of a Regional Apparatus in North Central Timor Regency.
10. Agency (Badan) means the Regional Apparatus that administers government affairs in the field of development planning and regional research.
11. Head of Agency means the Regional Apparatus that administers government affairs in the field of planning.
12. Women's Empowerment means every effort to improve physical, mental-spiritual, social, knowledge, and skills capabilities so that women are ready to be utilized according to their respective abilities.
13. Gender Mainstreaming, hereinafter abbreviated as PUG, means a strategy built to integrate gender as an integral dimension of the planning, implementation, monitoring, and evaluation of regional development policies and programs.
14. Gender means a concept that refers to the roles and responsibilities of men and women that arise as a result of social construction and may change due to the social and cultural conditions of society.
15. Gender Equality means equal conditions for men and women to obtain opportunities and rights as human beings so as to be able to play a role and participate in the development process.
16. Gender Justice means a process of becoming fair to men and women.
17. Gender Mainstreaming Working Group, hereinafter referred to as Pokja PUG, means a consultative forum for implementers and drivers of gender mainstreaming from various regional agencies/institutions.
18. Gender Mainstreaming Focal Point, hereinafter referred to as Focal Point PUG, means a PD official who has the capability to carry out gender mainstreaming in their respective work units.
19. Regional Medium-Term Development Plan, hereinafter abbreviated as RPJMD, means the elaboration of the vision, mission, and program of the Regent, the preparation of which is guided by the Regional Long-Term Development Plan, containing the regional development strategy, regional general policy, regional programs, and a macroeconomic framework covering an overall economic outlook including fiscal policy direction in a work plan in the form of an indicative regulatory and funding framework.
20. Gender Analysis means a systematically built process to identify and understand the division of labor or roles of men and women, access to and control over development resources, participation in development, and the benefits they enjoy.
21. Gender-Responsive Budget means a performance-based budget allocation for certain programs or activities whose input, output, and outcome indicators can guarantee the fulfillment of the aspirations and needs of women and men fairly.
22. Communication, Information, and Education, hereinafter abbreviated as KIE, means the delivery of messages in the form of information, ideas, or facts related to gender mainstreaming that need to be known by the public, directly or indirectly, through communication channels to recipients of the message in order to achieve an effect, as a process of behavioral change toward gender responsiveness.
23. Village/Sub-village Development Planning Deliberation, hereinafter referred to as Musrembangkel, means an annual village/sub-village development planning deliberation forum involving development actors at the village/sub-village level.
24. Sub-district Development Planning Deliberation, hereinafter referred to as Musrembangcam, means a deliberation forum among development actors to discuss and agree on steps for handling priority program activities listed in the list of proposed village development planning activities, integrated with regional development priorities of North Central Timor Regency at the sub-district level, coordinated by the Regional Development Planning Agency and implemented by the Sub-district Head.
25. Development planning deliberation for the preparation of the Regional Medium-Term Development Plan, hereinafter referred to as Musrembang RPJMD, means a deliberation forum among stakeholders to discuss, refine, and agree on the draft Regional Medium-Term Development Plan.
26. Strategic Plan, hereinafter referred to as Renstra, means a five-year planning document containing the vision, mission, objectives, strategies, policies, programs, and development activities in accordance with the organization's duties and functions as an elaboration of the RPJMD.
27. Work Plan, hereinafter referred to as Renja, means a one-year planning document containing policies, programs, and development activities, whether planned directly by the Regional Apparatus or pursued by encouraging community participation, as an elaboration of the Renstra.
28. Regional Government Work Plan, hereinafter abbreviated as RKPD, means a one-year planning document containing policies, programs, and development activities, whether planned directly by the Regional Government or pursued by encouraging community participation, as an elaboration of the Renstra.
29. Regional Action Plan, hereinafter abbreviated as RAD, means an activity planning document prepared by a technical team to be implemented within a certain period/time.
30. Gender Analysis Pathway, hereinafter abbreviated as GAP, means a gender analysis tool that can be used to assist planners in carrying out gender mainstreaming in the planning of development policies/programs/activities with the aim of identifying gender gaps and gender issues, as well as formulating development policy/program/activity plans aimed at reducing or eliminating such gender gaps.
31. Technical Team means a team formed by Pokja PUG with the task of conducting gender-perspective budget analysis and preparing the RAD PUG.

CHAPTER II
SCOPE

Article 2

The scope of the implementation of Women's Empowerment includes:
a. quality of life of women;
b. protection of women;
c. quality of family; and
d. gender data system.

CHAPTER III
QUALITY OF LIFE OF WOMEN

Part One
General

Article 3

The Quality of Life of Women includes:
a. institutionalization of PUG;
b. Women's Empowerment in the fields of politics, law, social affairs, and economics within community organizations; and
c. strengthening and development of institutions providing women's empowerment services.

Part Two
Institutionalization of PUG

Sub-part 1
General

Article 4

The institutionalization of PUG includes:
a. determination of the PUG coordinator;
b. Pokja PUG;
c. Focal Point PUG;
d. budgeting;
e. gender-based development planning;
f. monitoring and evaluation;
g. reporting; and
h. supervision.

Sub-part 2
PUG Coordinator

Article 5

(1) The Regent has the authority to appoint the Head of the PD that administers government affairs in the field of Women's Empowerment as the coordinator for the implementation of PUG.
(2) The appointment referred to in paragraph (1) shall be made by Regent's Decision.

Sub-part 3
Pokja PUG

Article 6

(1) The Regent has the authority to establish the Pokja PUG.
(2) The establishment of the Pokja PUG referred to in paragraph (1) shall be determined by Regent's Decision.
(3) The Regent's Decision referred to in paragraph (2) shall be valid for 5 (five) years.
(4) The membership of the Pokja PUG consists of:
a. chairperson held by the Head of the Agency;
b. secretariat held by the Head of the Office;
c. members consisting of:
1. heads of PD;
2. a technical team consisting of:
a) officials who have undergone training or education in gender-perspective budget analysis, at most 5 (five) persons;
b) representatives from higher education institutions, at most 5 (five) persons;
c) representatives of the press, at most 5 (five) persons; and
d) representatives of non-governmental organizations with a scope of orientation in the field of gender, at most 5 (five) persons.

Article 7

The duties of the Pokja PUG include:
a. promoting and facilitating dialogue between work units within the regional government;
b. developing networks in accordance with the duties, functions, and authority granted by leadership in efforts to realize gender equality and justice;
c. assigning the technical team to prepare the RAD PUG in order to realize gender equality and justice in accordance with the RPJMD periodization;
d. elaborating and following up on policies and implementation programs to be gender responsive as implied and stated in the RPJMD;
e. coordinating the formation of Focal Point PUG in each PD or work unit;
f. promoting PUG in each PD;
g. strengthening the capacity of Focal Point PUG members in preparing gender-responsive budgets through the technical team;
h. overseeing the preparation of gender-responsive planning and budgeting in each PD;
i. incorporating gender indicators into the guidelines for preparing the regional budget (APBD);
j. ensuring that gender-responsive budgeting is taken into consideration in the discussion and determination of the APBD together with the DPRD;
k. holding coordination meetings every 6 (six) months to ensure the implementation of PUG in the Region;
l. preparing and submitting reports on the implementation of PUG to the Regent every 6 (six) months; and
m. holding coordination meetings prior to the preparation of the RKPD.

Sub-part 4
Focal Point PUG

Article 8

(1) The Regional Secretary has the authority to establish the Focal Point PUG within the Regional Secretariat.
(2) The establishment of the Focal Point PUG referred to in paragraph (1) shall be determined by Decision of the Regional Secretary.
(3) The membership of the Focal Point PUG referred to in paragraph (1) consists of:
a. person in charge held by the Regional Secretary;
b. coordinator held by an Echelon IIB official in charge of government affairs in the field of law;
c. deputy coordinator held by an Echelon IIIA official of each bureau within the Regional Secretariat, except for the Echelon IIB official referred to in letter b; and
d. members from representative officials and/or staff from all bureaus within the Regional Secretariat.

Article 9

(1) Heads of PD are required to establish a Focal Point PUG for their PD.
(2) The establishment of the Focal Point PUG referred to in paragraph (1) shall be by Decision of the Head of PD.
(3) The membership of the Focal Point PUG referred to in paragraph (1) consists of:
a. person in charge held by the Head of PD;
b. coordinator held by an Echelon IIIA official in charge of planning at the PD;
c. deputy coordinator held by an Echelon IIIB official with competence in PUG; and
d. members from representative officials or staff from divisions and the secretariat at the PD.

Article 10

(1) Hospital Directors have the authority to establish a Hospital Focal Point PUG.
(2) The establishment of the Focal Point PUG referred to in paragraph (1) shall be determined by Decision of the Hospital Director.
(3) The membership of the Focal Point PUG referred to in paragraph (1) consists of:
a. person in charge held by the hospital director;
b. coordinator held by an Echelon IIIB official in charge of hospital planning;
c. deputy coordinator held by an Echelon IVA official with competence in PUG; and
d. members from representative officials or staff in divisions or sections within the hospital.

Article 11

(1) Heads of Community Health Centers have the authority to establish a Community Health Center Focal Point PUG.
(2) The establishment of the Focal Point PUG referred to in paragraph (1) shall be determined by Decision of the Head of the Community Health Center.
(3) The membership of the Community Health Center Focal Point PUG consists of:
a. person in charge held by the Head of the Community Health Center;
b. coordinator held by an Echelon IVB official in charge of Community Health Center planning; and
c. members consisting of Community Health Center staff.

Article 12

The duties of the Focal Point PUG include:
a. assisting policy makers within the work unit's scope of duties, principal tasks, and functions to take comprehensive planned steps whenever gender gaps exist;
b. encouraging and assisting the work unit in evaluating and improving mandates, policies, programs, projects, activities, and budgets to be more gender responsive;
c. facilitating the implementation of gender sensitivity training, gender analysis training, and developing gender networks with agencies, institutions, or organizations and other work units, both governmental and non-governmental;
d. pursuing the implementation of gender analysis as one stage in every development process, starting from the planning, implementation, monitoring, and evaluation stages;
e. elaborating and following up on development policies and implementation programs to be gender responsive as implied in the PD's Renstra, PD's Renja, and the RKPD;
f. participating in various activities organized by the Pokja PUG and/or the national PUG working group; and
g. preparing periodic activity reports to be submitted to the Regent through the chairperson of the Pokja PUG.

Sub-part 5
Gender-Based Regional Development Planning

Article 13

(1) The Regional Government is responsible for preparing the planning of policies, programs, and development activities with a gender perspective.
(2) The responsibility referred to in paragraph (1) shall be coordinated by the PD that administers government affairs in the field of development planning, regional research, and development.
(3) The planning referred to in paragraph (1) includes the following stages:
a. Musrenbangdus;
b. Musrembangdes/kel;
c. Musrembangcam;
d. Regional-level Musrenbang;
e. preparation of the RPJMD;
f. preparation of the PD Renstra;
g. preparation of the PD Renja; and
h. RKPD;
i. preparation of the draft regional budget (RAPBD).
(4) The planning referred to in paragraph (1) shall be carried out through Gender Analysis using the Gender Analysis Pathway method or other analytical methods.
(5) The implementation of Gender Analysis on the RPJMD and PD Renstra may be carried out in cooperation with higher education institutions or other parties with relevant capabilities.

Article 14

(1) The Agency is responsible for preparing guidelines for gender-based development planning.
(2) The guidelines referred to in paragraph (1) shall be used in facilitating:
a. Musrenbangdus;
b. Musrembangdes/kel;
c. Musrembangcam;
d. Regional-level Musrenbang;
e. preparation of the RPJMD;
f. preparation of the PD Renstra; and
g. preparation of the PD Renja,
that are gender responsive.
(3) The preparation of the guidelines referred to in paragraph (1) may be carried out in cooperation with the Pokja PUG Technical Team.

Article 15

(1) The Gender Analysis Pathway method referred to in Article 13 paragraph (4) is used as a method for preparing plans in each PD.
(2) The method referred to in paragraph (1) includes:
a. Stage I, analysis of gender-responsive policy;
b. Stage II, formulation of gender-responsive policy;
c. Stage III, gender-responsive action plan;
d. Stage IV, implementation of the activities that have been prepared; and
e. Stage V, monitoring and evaluation of each stage and step taken.

Sub-part 6
Budgeting

Article 16

(1) The Regional Government is required to allocate a budget for the implementation of PUG in the Region.
(2) The implementation budget referred to in paragraph (1) shall come from:
a. the APBD; and
b. non-binding assistance from other parties, in accordance with applicable laws and regulations.
(3) The budget referred to in paragraphs (1) and (2) shall be used to finance the activities of the Pokja PUG and the PD Focal Point PUG in integrating Gender at the level of planning, preparation, implementation, monitoring, and evaluation of policy and development in the Region.

Sub-part 7
Monitoring and Evaluation

Article 17

(1) The Regent has the authority to monitor and evaluate the implementation of PUG.
(2) The Regent may delegate the implementation of monitoring and evaluation to the Pokja PUG.
(3) Monitoring and evaluation shall be carried out on each PD and/or other institutions implementing PUG efforts.
(4) Monitoring and evaluation shall be carried out at least once every 6 (six) months.

Article 18

Monitoring of the implementation of PUG shall be carried out through:
a. monitoring of the preparation of gender-responsive development planning documents at both the PD level and the regional level;
b. monitoring of the RAD PUG; and
c. verification of reports on the implementation of the RAD PUG.

Article 19

Evaluation of the implementation of PUG shall be carried out through:
a. evaluation of the achievements and challenges of implementing the RAD PUG;
b. preparation of the RAD PUG for the next period;
c. evaluation of the achievements and challenges of implementing PUG every 6 (six) months; and
d. preparation of the plan for implementing PUG for the following 6 (six) months.

Sub-part 8
PUG Reporting

Article 20

(1) The Pokja PUG is responsible for preparing a PUG Implementation Report every 6 (six) months and a five-year RAD PUG Implementation Report.
(2) The reporting framework referred to in paragraph (1) shall be guided by the reporting framework as referred to in the laws and regulations concerning the General Guidelines for the Implementation of PUG in the Regions.
(3) The Pokja PUG report referred to in paragraph (1) shall be reported to the Regent.
(4) The Regent is responsible for examining and approving the Pokja PUG Report referred to in paragraph (1).
(5) The Regent is responsible for submitting the Pokja PUG report referred to in paragraph (1) to the Minister who administers government affairs in Home Affairs.

Sub-part 9
Supervision

Article 21

(1) The Regent is responsible for supervising the implementation of PUG.
(2) The supervision referred to in paragraph (1) shall take the form of:
a. gender-responsive planning and budgeting carried out by the regional government budget team;
b. coordination between the Pokja PUG and the Gender Focal Point; and
c. annual progress in achieving the RAD PUG.
(3) The responsibility referred to in paragraph (1) may be technically and operationally carried out by the Chairperson of the Pokja PUG and the Head of the Pokja PUG Secretariat.
(4) The implementation of the responsibility referred to in paragraph (3) shall be reported to the Regent.

Part Three
Women's Empowerment in the Fields of Politics, Law, Socio-Culture, and Economy within Community Organizations

Sub-part 1
General

Article 22

Efforts for Women's Empowerment in the fields of politics, law, socio-culture, and economy within community organizations include the following stages:
a. planning;
b. implementation; and
c. reporting and evaluation.

Sub-part 2
Planning

Article 23

(1) The Agency is responsible for facilitating the planning of Women's Empowerment in the fields of politics, law, socio-culture, and economy within community organizations.
(2) The planning referred to in paragraph (1) shall be carried out in an integrated manner within development planning deliberations based on the gender analysis pathway instrument.

Sub-part 3
Implementation

Article 24

The implementation of Women's Empowerment within community organizations shall be carried out by the PD in accordance with its principal duties and functions and according to regional financial capacity.

Article 25

(1) The PD that administers government affairs in the field of national unity and politics is responsible for facilitating women's empowerment in the field of politics.
(2) The empowerment referred to in paragraph (1) includes:
a. conducting education and training for women to enhance their competence, particularly in the field of politics; and
b. encouraging women's representation in the leadership of every community organization.

Article 26

(1) The PD that administers government affairs in the field of law is responsible for facilitating Women's Empowerment in the legal field.
(2) The implementation of Women's Empowerment in the legal field includes:
a. raising awareness and knowledge in the legal field through communication, information, and education services; and
b. facilitating access to legal consultation services.

Article 27

(1) The PD that administers government affairs in the field of social affairs is responsible for facilitating Women's Empowerment in the field of socio-culture.
(2) Women's Empowerment in the field of socio-culture shall be carried out through:
a. increasing knowledge, attitudes, and skills to encourage the fulfillment of education at all levels according to potential, in order to improve social status;
b. raising awareness, willingness, and ability to address health problems through quality promotive, preventive, curative, and rehabilitative efforts, particularly in the field of reproductive health;
c. raising awareness and knowledge about independent, healthy, and prosperous family planning, including access to consultation services and marriage registration; and
d. facilitation and efforts to preserve customs and develop science, technology, art, and culture for the advancement of women.

Article 28

(1) The PD that administers government affairs in the field of cooperatives, small and medium enterprises, and industry and trade is responsible for facilitating Women's Empowerment in the economic field.
(2) The implementation of Women's Empowerment in the economic field shall be carried out through:
a. provision of skills and job training;
b. facilitating the formation of productive economic business groups;
c. facilitating the strengthening and development of productive economic business groups;
d. facilitation and capital assistance; and
e. facilitating the development of marketing networks.

Sub-part 4
Reporting and Evaluation

Article 29

(1) Every PD is responsible for preparing a report on the implementation of Women's Empowerment activities that have been carried out, to be submitted to the Regent through the Office.
(2) After receiving the report submitted by the PD, the Office shall evaluate the implementation of the activities.
(3) The results of the evaluation of the implementation of activities referred to in paragraph (2) shall be reported to the Regent.
(4) The results of the evaluation referred to in paragraph (3) shall be used for budget planning for the following year.

CHAPTER IV
PROTECTION OF WOMEN

Part One
General

Article 30

Protection of Women includes:
a. strengthening and development of institutions providing women's protection services;
b. prevention of violence against women; and
c. strengthening and development of institutions providing protection services for women victims of violence.

Part Two
Strengthening and Development of Institutions Providing Women's Protection Services

Sub-part 1
Regional Technical Implementation Unit for Women's and Children's Protection

Article 31

(1) The PD that administers government affairs in the field of institutional and organizational matters is responsible for facilitating the establishment of a regional technical implementation unit for the protection of women and children.
(2) The establishment of the UPTD P2TP2A referred to in paragraph (1) shall be regulated by Regent's Regulation.
(3) The establishment of the UPTD P2TP2A referred to in paragraph (2) shall be carried out no later than 2 (two) years after this Regional Regulation is enacted.

Sub-part 2
Regional Action Plan

Article 32

(1) The preparation of the RAD referred to in Article 7 letter c shall be carried out through the following mechanism:
a. determination of the drafting team;
b. holding a meeting to form working groups based on indicators;
c. each working group identifying objectives, targets, and indicators;
d. determining priority programs and activities related to the achievement of each respective target; and
e. determining the budget allocation for each program activity.
(2) The RAD shall at least contain:
a. introduction;
b. policy direction and achievement strategy;
c. monitoring and evaluation;
d. closing; and
e. an appendix containing a matrix of RAD implementation.
(3) The preparation of the RAD shall be carried out with due regard to:
a. the RPJMN;
b. the RPJMD; and
c. other related documents.
(4) Further provisions concerning the implementation of the RAD shall be regulated by Regent's Regulation.

Sub-part 3
Monitoring and Evaluation

Article 33

(1) The Regent shall monitor the implementation of policies, programs, and activities for the protection of women.
(2) The monitoring referred to in paragraph (1) is intended to determine the progress and obstacles in the implementation of policies, programs, and activities for the protection of women in the Region.
(3) The monitoring referred to in paragraph (2) shall be carried out periodically on the PD through the Office.
(4) Monitoring shall be carried out from the planning stage through to the implementation of policies, programs, and activities for the protection of women for the current year.

Article 34

(1) Evaluation of the implementation of policies, programs, and activities for the Protection of Women shall be conducted at the end of every fiscal year.
(2) The results of the evaluation of the implementation of policies, programs, and activities for the protection of women shall be used as input for the preparation of policies, programs, and activities for the Protection of Women for the following year.
(3) The evaluation referred to in paragraph (1) shall be carried out in accordance with applicable laws and regulations.

Part Three
Prevention of Violence Against Women

Article 35

(1) The Office is responsible for carrying out efforts to prevent the occurrence of Violence against Women.
(2) The prevention referred to in paragraph (1) shall be carried out in the form of:
a. conducting outreach and socialization regarding matters relating to violence against women;
b. conducting socialization on women's rights;
c. seeking to improve education for women;
d. providing outreach to promptly report to local authorities in the event of Violence against women;
e. empowering social and community organizations and women's organizations to enhance women's knowledge and insight; and
f. forming a network in an effort to prevent violence against women.

Part Four
Strengthening and Development of Institutions Providing Protection Services for Women Victims of Violence

Article 36

(1) The Office may provide assistance to Service Institutions in the Region in the form of:
a. strengthening of Service Institutions; and
b. development of Service Institutions.
(2) Strengthening of service institutions referred to in paragraph (1) letter a shall be carried out through:
a. enhancing the competence of service institution members; and/or
b. facilitating paralegal training on a regular basis.
(3) Development of Service Institutions referred to in paragraph (1) letter b shall be carried out through:
a. providing additional facilities and infrastructure;
b. providing information technology-based service approaches; and/or
c. recruiting village cadres as assistant paralegals.

CHAPTER V
FAMILY QUALITY

Part One
General

Article 37

Family Quality includes:
a. improvement of family quality in realizing gender equality; and
b. strengthening and development of institutions providing services to improve family quality in realizing gender equality.

Part Two
Improving Family Quality in Realizing Gender Equality

Article 38

(1) The Office is responsible for facilitating activities to improve family quality in the Region.
(2) The improvement of family quality referred to in paragraph (1) shall be carried out through:
a. facilitating the formation of service-providing institutions;
b. in a coordinated manner with the PD and/or related institutions having the principal duties and functions to carry out activities of:
1. enhancing entrepreneurial capability for women; or
2. developing job training for women.

Part Three
Strengthening and Development of Institutions Providing Services to Improve Family Quality in Realizing Gender Equality

Article 39

(1) The facilitation of the formation of service-providing institutions referred to in Article 36 paragraph (2) letter a shall be carried out by establishing a family learning center.
(2) The stages of establishing a family learning center include:
a. preparing regulations;
b. preparing human resources;
c. preparing facilities and infrastructure; and
d. establishment of the family learning center.
(3) The family learning center functions:
a. as a service institution for prevention and counseling;
b. as a referral liaison point for solutions to problems concerning children and families;
c. to strengthen family capacity in caring for and protecting children;
d. as a place for family learning through education for parents; and
e. as a child-rights-based, holistic-integrative one-stop family service.

CHAPTER VI
GENDER DATA SYSTEM

Part One
General

Article 40

The Gender Data System includes:
a. data collection;
b. data processing; and
c. gender data analysis and presentation.

Part Two
Data Collection

Article 41

(1) The PD carrying out Focal Point PUG duties is responsible for reporting Gender data and PUG activities carried out to the Regent through the Office.
(2) The report on Gender data and PUG activities referred to in paragraph (1) shall be made every 6 (six) months.

Part Three
Data Processing

Article 42

(1) After receiving the report on Gender data and PUG activities from each Gender focal point in the Region, the Office shall process the data.
(2) The data processing referred to in paragraph (1) shall be carried out to sort Gender data and PUG activities with good grouping that is easy to analyze.

Part Four
Gender Data Analysis and Presentation

Article 43

(1) The Office shall conduct an analysis of the data that has been processed.
(2) The analysis referred to in paragraph (1) shall be carried out comprehensively based on adequate mapping so that it is easy to understand.
(3) The adequate analysis referred to in paragraph (2) shall be presented as regional Gender data, either manually and/or electronically.

CHAPTER VII
SANCTION PROVISIONS

Article 44

Any PD that fails to establish a Focal Point PUG as referred to in Articles 8, 9, 10, and 11 shall be subject to administrative sanctions in the form of:
a. verbal warning;
b. written warning; or
c. a statement of dissatisfaction.

CHAPTER VIII
TRANSITIONAL PROVISIONS

Article 45

At the time this Regional Regulation comes into effect, existing institutions providing women's protection services shall continue to carry out their duties until the establishment of the regional technical implementation unit for the protection of women and children in accordance with the provisions of this Regional Regulation.

CHAPTER IX
CLOSING PROVISIONS

Article 46

This Regional Regulation shall come into effect on the date of its promulgation.

So that everyone may be aware of it, it is ordered that this Regional Regulation be promulgated by placing it in the Regional Gazette of North Central Timor Regency.

THE IMPLEMENTATION OF WOMEN'S EMPOWERMENT

I. GENERAL

The advent of Law Number 23 of 2014 concerning Regional Government, as amended several times, most recently by Law Number 9 of 2015 concerning the Second Amendment to Law Number 23 of 2014 concerning Regional Government, and Law Number 33 of 2004 concerning Fiscal Balance Between the Central Government and Regional Governments, constitutes the gateway for Women's Empowerment in regional development. Women's empowerment is the appropriate development strategy for realizing gender equality and justice through development policies and programs that are gender sensitive and responsive in the future, particularly in North Central Timor Regency.

In North Central Timor Regency there are several conditions that need to be addressed through the establishment of a Regional Regulation on Women's Empowerment. This is because there is considerable discrimination against women in developing themselves and in opportunities to fulfill their rights. Women are regarded merely as second-class citizens, whose duty is to manage the household and who need not work. This is what must be opposed; this stigma constitutes a problem that must be resolved through empowerment programs.

The paradigm above is reflected in data on the presence of women working in Government; the data shows that women are not in strategic positions to participate in decision-making or policy-making, particularly in government affairs, as evidenced by civil servant (ASN) data by gender available in the field.

Based on this reasoning, it is now time for North Central Timor Regency to establish a Regional Regulation on Women's Empowerment in North Central Timor Regency. This Regional Regulation shall regulate the implementation of gender mainstreaming clearly, firmly, in a balanced manner, and comprehensively, in order to realize gender equality and justice, as well as to provide understanding of the importance of women's empowerment as a gender analysis strategy in the planning, preparation, implementation, monitoring, and evaluation of development policies and programs.
